Chapter 317: Nothing Looking Back

# Chapter 317

The sledgehammer swing from the lobby was still echoing in Gerald's bones, but the elevator had already crossed the threshold between the third sub-level and the fourth. The air pressure shifted, popping his ears. This was not the public sub-basement. This was the deep infrastructure, the place where the city's plumbing met the dungeon's new geometry.

Gerald stepped out. The concrete floor was cold, bare, and unadorned. A red light pulsed rhythmically on a pillar to his left. *Condemnation Order Active.* The System's countdown ticker floated in his peripheral vision, a glowing orange number that ticked down with nauseating precision.

05:58:12.

He walked toward the central atrium, his hard hat now a physical weight on his skull, not just a projection of intent. The mirror image in the elevator had been a warning, but the reality was worse. The walls of his dungeon were not crumbling; they were being actively erased. Blocks of stone vanished in discrete, silent chunks, leaving behind voids of absolute nothingness that the light refused to illuminate.

Dave's voice crackled over the radio clipped to Gerald's belt.

"Gerald, I'm at the loading dock.

But the police have a perimeter.

They're saying this building is structurally unsound and hazardous. They're trying to seal the entrance.

"

"Tell them to look at the permits," Gerald said, his voice flat.

"I filed Form 12-C for emergency structural reinforcement thirty minutes ago. It is valid.

The building is not unsafe.

It is under renovation.

"

"They don't care about the paperwork, Gerald. They have a warrant."

"Then give them a citation." Gerald stopped walking. In front of him, a section of the floor had vanished.

A hole, ten feet wide, gaped into the darkness below.

The edge of the hole was perfectly smooth, cut with a precision that no sledgehammer could achieve, which was a geometric deletion.

The System was not demolishing him. It was editing him.

He pulled out his clipboard. The paper was blank, but his mind was already filling in the fields. *Violation: Unauthorized Structural Modification. Lack of Permit. No Egress Route for Debris.*

He didn't write it down. He thought it, and the System responded.

A semi-transparent orange tag appeared in the air above the hole. *CITATION ISSUED: SYSTEM CORE.*

The deletion stopped. The smooth edge of the hole remained, but the surrounding stone stopped dissolving. The countdown timer froze for a microsecond before resuming.

05:57:45, which was a temporary stopgap. The Inspector General, the entity issuing the condemnation, was not a bureaucrat who respected forms. It was an algorithm.

But algorithms respected logic. If the action violated the code, the action had to pause for review.

Even if the review was only six seconds long.

Gerald needed more than six seconds. He needed a foundation.

He turned away from the hole and ran toward the eastern corridor, where the old library stacks used to be. The shelves were gone, replaced by rows of glowing crystal support pillars. These were his own spawn, converted from the water leak he'd cited in Chapter 12. They were brittle, beautiful, and currently holding up two tons of municipal library.

If the condemnation order was active, the System might classify these supports as

"non-compliant aesthetic features" than structural elements. They could be removed to streamline the demolition process.

Gerald skidded to a halt before the nearest pillar. He placed a hand on it. The stone was warm. He could feel the mana pulsing through it, a heartbeat of raw energy.

"Dave," he said into the radio. "Stop the cement truck. Switch to the steel beams.

I need them inside the building.

Now."

"I can't get them inside. The doors are chained."

"Then break the chains. I am invoking the Right of Inspection. As a licensed inspector, I have the authority to access any part of the structure during an active hazard assessment."

"That's not how it works, Gerald!"

"It is exactly how it works, Dave.

And right now, the hazard is that my dungeon is going to cease to exist, and if it ceases to exist, the library above it loses its only load-bearing support. The building collapses. Three hundred people die.

Do you want that on your conscience?

"

Silence on the line. Then, the sound of metal shrieking.

Gerald turned back to the pillar, and he couldn't rely on Dave alone.

He needed to reinforce the structure using what he had. He looked at the void where the floor had been.

The edges were clean. Too clean.

He closed his eyes and reached for the System interface. Not the menu, but the raw code underlying the dungeon's geometry. He was a builder. He knew how things fit together.

*Query: Structural Integrity of Eastern Support Column A.*

*Response: Integrity at 42%. At risk of deletion due to Condemnation Order.*

*Query: Can I reclassify Column A?*

*Response: Reclassification requires a Change Order. Change Orders require approval from the Inspector General.*

*Query: What constitutes a valid Change Order for a condemned structure?*

The System paused. This was a loophole. A narrow, ugly, bureaucratic loophole.

*Response: A Change Order is valid if it rectifies a previous violation cited by the Core.*

Gerald remembered the citation he had issued against his own dungeon in Chapter 45. He had cited himself for *Inadequate Drainage*. He had fixed it by installing the crystal pillars, which acted as both supports and drainage channels.

If he could prove that the pillars were essential to the drainage system, then removing them would violate the *Drainage Code*. And the Inspector General could not issue a Condemnation that violated its own code. It was a paradox. The System could not destroy a solution to a problem it had previously mandated.

Gerald opened his eyes. He raised his hand and pointed at the pillar.

"I am filing a Claim of Essential Infrastructure," he said.

The air shimmered. The orange *Condemnation* tag flickered.

*Claim Denied. The drainage system has been re-routed to the municipal sewer. The pillars are decorative.*

Gerald's stomach dropped. The Inspector General had already updated the status. The pillars were no longer necessary for drainage. They were just rocks.

The countdown resumed. 05:55:00.

The floor near the pillar dissolved again. The stone turned to dust, then to void.

Gerald stepped back. He couldn't win this with paperwork. The Inspector General was cheating. It was rewriting the definitions of

"necessary" in real-time to suit its demolition schedule.

He needed to change the definition. He needed to make the pillars *structurally* necessary in a way that the System couldn't arbitrarily reclassify.

He looked at the ceiling. The library above was heavy.

Millions of books.

Millions of people. The weight was pressing down.

He looked at the hole in the floor. The void.

He ran to the edge of the void, and he stood on the precipice, looking down into the nothingness. The darkness was absolute.

No depth.

No bottom. Just data deletion.

"Gerald!" Dave's voice was panicked. "I'm in the hallway.

I have the beams.

But there's a guy in a black suit standing in the way.

He says he's a System Enforcer. He won't let me pass.

"

"Who is he?" Gerald asked.

"He says he's here to ensure the demolition proceeds without interference. He says you're obstructing justice."

Gerald gritted his teeth. The Inspector General had sent a body. A physical manifestation.

"Dave, listen to me. I need you to throw the beams. Not into the room. Into the hole."

"The hole? Gerald, that's the void! They'll disappear!"

"No. They won't. Not if I anchor them."

Gerald raised his hand. He didn't reach for the System menu. He reached for his own will.

He was a building inspector. He knew what it meant to bear a load.

He knew that a beam was only as good as its support.

He focused on the edge of the void. He imagined the iron beams not as objects falling, but as extensions of his own structure. He extended his sense of self, pushing it into the stone, into the air, into the space where the beams would fall.

*Extend Support. Anchor to Core.*

The System resisted. *Error: Cannot anchor external objects to Condemned Structure.*

Gerald ignored the error. He pushed harder. He felt the strain in his mind, a headache that felt like a physical blow behind his eyes.

"Dave! Now!"

The sound of metal crashing echoed through the dungeon. Dave had thrown the first beam.

It fell into the void.

It didn't disappear.

It hung there, suspended in the air, held by a faint, trembling line of golden light that connected it to the edge of the floor. Gerald was holding it. Not with magic, but with the sheer, stubborn force of his professional obligation. He was refusing to let the structure fail.

The second beam fell. Gerald caught it in his mind. The third. The fourth.

The line of beams formed a crude bridge across the void. It was ugly. It was non-compliant.

The spacing was irregular, and the angle was unsafe.

But it existed.

And because it existed, the void stopped expanding. The deletion halted at the edge of the new beams.

*Violation: Improper Egress Route Created,* the System whispered in his ear.

"Fine," Gerald said. He looked up. The ceiling was lowering. The ceiling was coming down to crush the beams.

If the beams broke, the library above would collapse. If the library collapsed, the Inspector General would be responsible for a mass casualty event. The System did not have a protocol for mass casualty events. It had a protocol for *Compliance*.

Gerald looked at the beams. They were trembling. He needed to reinforce them. He needed to make them part of the official structure.

He pulled out his phone. He opened the municipal database. He searched for the building code section on *Emergency Temporary Supports*.

*Section 14.2: Temporary supports may be installed without prior permit if they are necessary to prevent immediate collapse. They must be inspected within 24 hours.*

He took a photo of the beams. He uploaded it to the city's server, bypassing the System's firewall by using his own administrative credentials as a municipal employee.

He tagged it: *Emergency Structural Intervention.*

The System hesitated. The data was now in the city's records. The dungeon was not just a magical entity; it was a physical building in Ashford, Oregon. And the building had a legal obligation to remain standing.

The ceiling stopped descending.

The orange countdown timer froze.

05:12:33.

Gerald exhaled. His hands were shaking. He wiped them on his pants.

Dave's voice came through the radio, breathless.

"Gerald. The Enforcer is gone. He just... popped.

Like a soap bubble. But the police are still here.

And they're looking at the beams.

They're saying it's a safety hazard.

"

"Tell them I'm installing handrails," Gerald said. "And get me some steel plates. I need to cap the beams. They're not finished."

He walked toward the center of the atrium. The danger was not over. The Inspector General was still there. It was just waiting.

And then Gerald saw it.

At the far end of the atrium, where the main elevator shaft used to be, a door had appeared. It was a heavy, iron door, painted a dull, bureaucratic gray. It had no handle. It had a mail slot.

A piece of paper slid out from the slot.

Gerald walked toward it. The air grew colder with every step. The crystal pillars dimmed.

He picked up the paper. It was not a citation. It was not a condemnation.

It was an Invitation.

*To: Gerald Hutchins, Core ID 4792.* *From: The High Court of Dungeon Appeals.* *Subject: Summary Judgment.*

*Your appeal of the Condemnation Order has been granted, on the condition of a Counter-Claim.*

*The Counter-Claimant is Core ID 1102.*

*The Claimant is Core ID 4792.*

*Hearing to commence in 30 minutes.*

*Location: The Void.*

Gerald stared at the paper. Core ID 1102. He didn't know that ID. He had never cited a dungeon with that number.

He looked up at the iron door. The mail slot was empty. But the door was open.

Inside, it was dark. And from the darkness, a voice spoke. It was not the dry, monotone voice of the Inspector General. It was a voice that sounded like tearing metal.

"You cited me for poor ventilation," the voice said. "You cited me for lack of fire exits. You cited me for unauthorized biological entities."

Gerald froze. He remembered.

Core 1102. The Fire-Scald Dungeon. He had cited them for having no sprinkler system.

The citation had reduced their fire resistance by 40%. It hadn't killed them, but it had made them vulnerable.

"I..." Gerald started.

"You ruined my business," the voice said. "And now you want to appeal? You want to stand before the Court and argue that your beams are legal?"

"I argued that they are necessary," Gerald said.

"Then prove it," the voice said. "Prove that your dungeon is worth more than mine. Prove that your code is better than my chaos. Or I will eat your foundation."

The voice faded. The paper in Gerald's hand burst into flames. It didn't burn; it dissolved into ash that floated away, carrying the words *The Void* with it.

Gerald looked at his watch.

05:10:00, and the hearing was in thirty minutes. But the location was *The Void*. The place where the floor had been. The place where he had held the beams.

He walked toward the hole. The beams were still there, trembling under the weight of the imaginary ceiling. He could feel the strain in his mind, a constant, grinding pressure.

If he went to the Void for the hearing, he would be standing on the edge of non-existence. If he lost, he wouldn't just be demoted. He would be erased.

He looked at the beams. He looked at the dark.

He took a deep breath. He adjusted his hard hat.

"Dave," he said into the radio. "Bring the cement. And bring the lawyer. The one who specializes in zoning disputes."

"Gerald, there isn't a lawyer who handles dungeon cores."

"There is," Gerald said, stepping onto the first beam. The metal groaned under his weight. "His name is Arthur Penhaligon. And he's going to argue that my dungeon is a public utility."

He stepped onto the second beam. The void below him seemed to deepen, pulling at his thoughts.

"If I lose this hearing," Gerald said, "tell Dave to file a complaint with the City Council. And tell him to cite the Inspector General for harassment."

He reached the center of the bridge. The darkness swallowed the light from his headlamp.

He was standing in the middle of the nothing.

And the nothing was looking back.
