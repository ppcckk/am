Chapter 359: The Rat Files Back

# Chapter 359

The static cleared with the violence of a snapped tendon.

Gerald blinked.

The knight was gone.

So was the laboratory, and he stood on damp concrete, surrounded by the familiar scent of mildew and old paper. The sub-basement. His dungeon. The water leak in Sector 4 was still dripping, a steady, rhythmic tap against the tile floor that Gerald had been meaning to address for six chapters.

His hand was empty.

The tablet was gone.

The knight's cable-sword was gone.

But the pages remained.

Hundreds of them, and they lay scattered across the wet floor, their edges curled and darkened by the moisture. The paper was not digital. It was not System-generated parchment. It was standard bond, three-hole punched, printed with a laser printer that had missed its calibration marks.

Gerald lowered himself to his knees.

His joints popped.

He picked up the nearest sheet. The ink was smeared, but the header was legible.

**FORM 89-ALPHA: PROTOTYPE INTEGRATION LOG.** **SUBJECT: G. HUTCHINS.** **STATUS: REJECTED.** **NOTES: Excessive rigidity prevents organic adaptation. Core fails to evolve beyond initial parameters. Recommend termination.**

Gerald read the line twice.

*Excessive rigidity.*

He did not feel insulted. He felt validated.

Rigidity was the only thing that kept a wall standing.

Rigidity was the only thing that kept a roof from collapsing under snow load. If they wanted flexibility, they should have hired a gymnast, not an inspector.

He picked up another page.

**SUBJECT: M. JONES.** **STATUS: TERMINATED.** **CAUSE OF DEATH: Structural collapse during stress test. Core failed to reinforce primary support beams.**

Gerald's mouth tightened into a thin, bloodless line, and she hadn't failed because she was weak. She had failed because she didn't inspect the load-bearing columns.

He moved through the papers, sorting them by date. The pile was massive. Hundreds of failed cores. Hundreds of bodies that had dissolved into dungeon footprints, only to be discarded by the architects who had spawned them.

Arthur Pendragon's page was different, which was thick. Heavy stock.

**SUBJECT: A.

PENDRAGON.** **STATUS: ACTIVE.** **TRAIT: Adaptive Chaos.** **NOTES: Subject demonstrates ability to bypass code logic. Can mutate dungeon geometry in real-time.

High potential.

High risk. Monitor for deviation.**

Adaptive Chaos.

Gerald hated the phrase.

It sounded like an excuse for poor craftsmanship.

A chime sounded.

Not the soft, polite *ping* of a routine update. This was a harsh, grinding buzz that vibrated in his teeth.

**SYSTEM ALERT: UNAUTHORIZED DATA LEAK DETECTED.** **SOURCE: EXTERNAL TERMINAL (OFF-LINE).** **ACTION: PURGE CACHE?** **YES/NO**

Gerald stared at the prompt floating in his peripheral vision, and he knew what the System wanted. It wanted to delete the evidence. It wanted to maintain the narrative that dungeons were natural phenomena, born of magic or malice, not manufactured products with serial numbers.

His finger hovered over *YES*.

If he deleted it, he remained safe. The System would continue to audit him.

He would continue to cite other cores for their sloppy ventilation shafts and non-compliant handrails.

He could keep doing what he did best.

Enforcing the rules.

But the rules were a lie.

If the dungeons were built, then the System was not a god, and the System was a contractor.

And contractors were liable for negligence.

Gerald looked at the paper in his hand. He looked at the *NO* button.

He selected *NO*.

The prompt vanished.

The papers remained.

A new notification appeared, smaller, tucked into the corner of his vision. It wasn't a warning. It was an invitation.

**NEW MECHANIC UNLOCKED: CHAIN OF CUSTODY.** **DESCRIPTION: Cores that possess physical evidence of external manipulation may petition the Inspector General for a Preliminary Hearing.** **REQUIREMENT: Physical proof must be presented to the IG's proxy within one hour.** **PENALTY FOR FAILURE TO APPEAR: Immediate Core Dissolution.**

Gerald felt a cold weight settle in his stomach.

*Immediate Core Dissolution.*

This wasn't a citation. This wasn't a fine.

This was an eviction notice, and he stood up. His knees ached. He gathered the papers, piling them into a neat stack.

He did not run. Running triggered the System's panic protocols, which could misinterpret his movement as a hostile spawn attempt.

He walked.

He walked with the steady, measured pace of a man who knows that if he steps off the curb, he might slip.

He needed to move the evidence.

The library sub-basement was not public property. It was his dungeon. The System treated his physical footprint as his body. the papers were inside his body.

But the Inspector General's proxy was not inside his body, which was in the lobby.

Gerald moved toward the central corridor. The water leak hissed as he passed it. He ignored it. He had bigger problems.

As he walked, the air grew colder.

The lights in the hallway flickered.

Then they died.

Total darkness swallowed the corridor.

For a normal core, this would be a tactical disadvantage. For Gerald, it was an opportunity.

He didn't need light to measure distance. He needed light to see faces. And right now, he needed to see who was coming.

A sound echoed from the far end of the hall.

Footsteps.

Heavy ones.

Metal boots on concrete.

Gerald stopped, and he stood in the center of the corridor, arms at his sides. He did not summon a spawn.

He did not raise a wall.

He waited.

The figure emerged from the darkness.

It was not a knight, which was a humanoid shape, wrapped in gray robes. Its face was a smooth, featureless mask of white plastic. In its hands, it carried a clipboard.

**IG PROXY: UNIT 001.** **FUNCTION: AUDIT AND ENFORCEMENT.** **STATUS: AWAITING SUBMISSION.**

Gerald looked at the mask.

"You're early," Gerald said.

The mask tilted.

"Violation detected," the proxy said. Its voice was synthesized, stripped of inflection.

"Core 47-Alpha.

You have accessed restricted memory sectors.

You are holding non-System artifacts.

"

Gerald held up the stack of papers.

"Are these artifacts?"

The proxy stepped closer. The lights did not return. The darkness pressed in, but the proxy seemed immune to it.

"They are contraband," the proxy said. "They represent deviation from the intended Core trajectory. Surrender them for disposal."

Gerald looked at the clipboard.

"Form 11-B," Gerald said.

The proxy paused.

"Unlicensed Biological Entity," Gerald continued. "That's what you are. You are not a Core.

You are not an adventurer. You are a construct operating within my jurisdiction without a permit.

"

The proxy raised the clipboard.

"Your logic is flawed. The IG is not subject to local dungeon codes."

"Wrong," Gerald said.

He reached into his pocket and pulled out a pen. It was a cheap ballpoint, the kind he used for grocery lists.

"The System operates on a hierarchy of rules.

The Inspector General is the highest authority in the Dungeon Compliance Bureau.

But the Bureau is part of the City of Ashford's municipal framework. I checked the bylaws.

Page 4, Section 2, Paragraph C.

"

He held up a piece of paper from the stack.

"It states that any enforcement agent operating within a registered Core's footprint must be registered as a Municipal Inspector or a Licensed Auditor. If you are not licensed, you are trespassing."

The proxy's mask remained blank, but its posture shifted. A slight tilt of the head. Uncertainty.

"I am a construct of the System," the proxy said.

"You're a worker," Gerald said. "And you're working without a badge."

He stepped forward.

"Serve this."

Gerald thrust a form at the proxy, which was a blank Form 12-C: *Citation for Unauthorized Presence on Private Property.*

He had filled it out in his head while walking. The date was today. The location was Sector 1, Corridor A. The violation was *Failure to Display Credentials.*

The proxy reached out.

Its hand was cold. Dry. Like paper.

It took the form.

The System chimed.

**CITATION ISSUED.** **TARGET: IG PROXY UNIT 001.** **VIOLATION: UNLICENSED OPERATION.** **PENALTY: SUSPENSION OF AUTHORITY FOR 10 MINUTES.**

The proxy froze.

The clipboard clattered to the floor.

The gray robes slumped. The figure fell to its knees, not in defeat, but in a system reboot. Its head snapped back, eyes rolling up into white voids.

Gerald stepped over the kneeling figure, and he walked to the end of the corridor.

The library lobby was ahead.

But the door was not open.

It was sealed.

A barrier of black ice stretched from floor to ceiling.

Gerald approached it. He touched it with his finger. It was cold, but it hummed with energy.

A message appeared on the ice, written in frost.

**SYSTEM OVERRIDE: PROTOCOL BLACKOUT.** **REASON: CORE INSTABILITY DETECTED.** **ACTION: CONTAINMENT.**

Gerald stepped back, and he looked at the kneeling proxy behind him.

The suspension was temporary. Ten minutes.

He had ten minutes to get the papers out.

He turned around and ran back to his spawn point, and the rat was there. The rat looked at him. The rat squeaked, confused.

Gerald ignored it.

He went to the water leak.

He placed his hand on the wall.

"Open," he said.

He wasn't asking the System. He was asking the stone.

He focused on the structural integrity of the wall. He visualized the mortar joints. He visualized the hairline cracks he had noted in Chapter 4. He found the weak point.

He pushed, and the wall did not open.

The System blocked it. *No unauthorized egress.*

Gerald gritted his teeth.

He pulled the papers from his pocket. He tore one sheet in half.

He held the torn paper up.

"Form 15-Z: Emergency Egress."

He wrote on the paper with his pen.

*Condition: Fire.* *Condition: Flood.* *Condition: Hostile Occupant.*

He threw the paper at the wall.

The System scanned it.

*Violation: False Alarm.*

*Penalty: Debuff -10% Strength.*

Gerald felt his muscles weaken. His legs trembled.

But the paper stuck to the ice.

And the ice cracked.

Not because the System accepted the lie. But because the System recognized the *form*.

The form was valid. The logic was sound. If there was a fire, egress was mandatory. The System could not forbid egress during a declared emergency, even a false one, without violating the fundamental Right to Survival clause.

A crack spiderwebbed across the black ice.

Then another.

Gerald kicked the ice.

It shattered, and he stumbled out into the library lobby.

The lobby was empty.

The shelves were intact. The circulation desk was quiet.

But the front doors were locked.

Chained.

And standing in front of the doors was Arthur Pendragon, and the hero was wearing his armor, but it was damaged. Dented. Scratched.

He looked at Gerald.

He did not look angry.

He looked tired.

"You broke the containment field," Arthur said.

"I filed a form," Gerald corrected.

Arthur sighed. He reached into his belt and pulled out a small, black device.

"They're coming, Gerald. The IG isn't just auditing you anymore. They're auditing *me*."

Arthur held up the device, which was a tracker.

"I have to go," Arthur said. "If I stay, they'll cite me for aiding a rogue core. If I go, they'll cite me for abandonment."

"Which is worse?" Gerald asked.

"Abandonment," Arthur said. "Because if I'm not there when they find the papers, they'll say I planted them. They'll say I'm the one who built you."

Gerald looked at the papers in his hands.

"If they built me," Gerald said, "then who owns the mortgage?"

Arthur smiled. It was a sad, crooked smile.

"That's the question you're supposed to ask."

He pressed the button on the device.

The air rippled.

Arthur vanished.

And the lights in the library turned on.

Every single bulb.

Blinding, white, sterile light.

Gerald squinted.

In the center of the lobby, a new door opened, which was not a door in the wall.

It was a door in the air.

It was framed in gold.

And walking through it was a woman.

She wore a suit that looked like it was made of code. Her eyes were scanning lines. Her hair was silver, floating around her head like static.

She looked at Gerald, and she looked at the papers.

"Gerald Hutchins," she said. Her voice sounded like a gavel strike.

"I'm the Inspector General."

Gerald straightened his tie.

"I have a complaint," Gerald said.

The woman tilted her head.

"Everyone has a complaint," she said. "Most of them are just whining about spawn rates."

"This isn't a complaint," Gerald said. "It's an audit."

He held out the stack of papers.

"You want to inspect me?"

The woman stepped forward.

"Show me."

Gerald handed her the first page, and she read it.

Her eyes widened.

For the first time, the Inspector General looked afraid.

"Where did you get this?" she whispered.

"I found it," Gerald said.

"No," she said. "You didn't find it. You *summoned* it."

She looked at the floor.

Where the water leak was.

Where the rat was.

Where the broken tablet had been.

"You're not a Core," she said.

Gerald waited.

"You're a Glitch," she said. "And the System is trying to patch you."

The gold door behind her shrinked.

The walls of the library dissolved.

Wood turned to wireframe. Carpet turned to pixels.

"Leave now," she said. "Or you get deleted."

Gerald looked at the dissolving room, and he looked at the papers.

He looked at the Inspector General.

"I have a citation," Gerald said.

He pulled a final form from his pocket.

**FORM 99-Omega: NOTICE OF TERMINATION FOR MALICIOUS CODE.**

He held it up.

"Serve this," Gerald said.

The Inspector General's eyes narrowed.

"You can't cite me."

"Watch me," Gerald said.

He threw the paper.

It sailed through the air, landing on the chest of the woman.

It stuck.

The System froze.

The dissolving walls stopped, and the silence was absolute.

Then, a single word appeared in the air, written in red fire.

**ERROR.**

**INVALID CITATION.**

**SOURCE: UNAUTHORIZED USER.**

**ACTION: PURGE USER.**

Gerald looked at the word *Purge*, and he looked at his hands.

They were becoming transparent.

He looked at the Inspector General.

She wasn't looking at him.

She was looking at the rat, and the rat was standing on the circulation desk.

And it was holding a pen.

Gerald realized then that he had forgotten to file a Form 11-B for the rat.

And now, the rat had filed one for him.
