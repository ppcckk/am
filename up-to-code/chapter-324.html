Chapter 324: Hunger Wakes

# Chapter 324

The hammer did not hit.

It stopped three inches from Gerald's forehead, suspended in a lattice of glowing amber light. The sound of the impact never came. Instead, a low hum vibrated through the wet pavement of the library parking lot, shaking the puddles around Gerald's shoes.

Gerald did not breathe. His clipboard was still clutched to his chest, the metal clip digging into his palm.

*Hearing Initiated.* *Case ID: G-Hutchins vs. System Inspector General.* *Charge: Abuse of Process.* *Time Limit: 00:14:59.*

The Auditor stood frozen. The Demolitionist froze mid-swing, its stone muscles locked in a grotesque tableau of violence. Rain continued to fall, but it passed through them like ghosts, hitting the asphalt with a rhythmic hiss that seemed to come from everywhere and nowhere.

Gerald looked at the amber cage. He looked at the briefcase lying open on the ground.

Inside, the mirror remained. The tomb remained.

He did not panic. Panic was inefficient.

Panic required energy, and energy was currently being drained from his Core at a rate of five points per second.

Gerald checked his internal status screen. His HP was dropping. His Stamina was gone.

But his Mana pool was full, hovering at maximum capacity because the System had paused all active spellcasting, which was the loophole.

Gerald had spent thirty-one years knowing that laws were not walls. Laws were grids. If you moved a grid line one inch to the left, a building that was illegal became legal. If you moved the definition of

"habitable" to include "sub-basement," a condemned zone became a rental unit.

He looked at the Auditor's blank face.

"Objection," Gerald said.

His voice cracked. He cleared his throat. The sound was dry, like dead leaves.

"Objection," he repeated, louder. "The citation was served. The hearing was initiated. The verdict was not rendered."

The Auditor's head tilted. The paper-tearing voice returned, softer now, almost curious.

"The verdict was delivered, Mr. Hutchins. You saw the tomb."

"I saw a projection," Gerald said. He stepped forward.

The amber light resisted him, burning his skin where it touched his shirt, but he did not stop.

"A projection is not a judgment. A judgment requires a signature. A signature requires consent or authority.

You have no authority over a Core that has filed a Form 99-Z.

"

The Auditor paused. The rain seemed to hesitate in the air.

"Form 99-Z is for emergency displacement of unregistered entities," the Auditor said. "You are a registered Core."

"I am," Gerald agreed.

He walked past the frozen Demolitionist.

The giant stone construct smelled of wet chalk and old blood. Gerald did not look at it.

He looked at the briefcase.

"However," Gerald continued, his mind racing through the mental library of every municipal code he had ever memorized, "I am also a citizen of Ashford. And as a citizen, I am entitled to due process under the Municipal Charter, which is incorporated into the System's foundational laws via the Integration Act of 2024."

Gerald reached into his pocket. He pulled out a crumpled piece of paper.

It was not a system document, which was a real-world receipt from the library coffee shop, printed twenty minutes ago. He held it up.

"This receipt," Gerald said, "proves I was present in the public sector during the incident. Therefore, I am protected by the Public Servant Immunity Clause."

The Auditor's blank face remained expressionless. But the amber light flickered.

*Processing.* *Checking Integration Act Section 4, Paragraph B.* *Match Found.* *Recalculating Verdict.*

The cage around the Demolitionist dissolved.

The stone giant stumbled forward, its hammer crashing into the ground. The impact sent a shockwave through the parking lot, cracking the asphalt. The Auditor stepped back, his suit rippling like water.

Gerald's knees buckled. He caught himself on the edge of the library's concrete planter. His HP was at 12%. If the System had not paused the damage, he would have been dust.

*Verdict Overturned.* *Penalty: None.* *Cost: 500 Mana Points.* *New Status: Probationary Core.*

Gerald stared at the notification. 500 Mana. That was half his reserve. He had spent a fortune just to prove he existed.

The Auditor closed his briefcase. The snap echoed like a gunshot.

"You are clever, Mr. Hutchins," the Auditor said. "But cleverness is a resource. It depletes."

"I have paper," Gerald said. He pointed to the clipboard. "I have forms. I have the code."

"The Inspector General does not care about paper," the Auditor said. "The Inspector General cares about balance."

The Auditor pointed a long, grey finger at the Demolitionist.

"This entity is a Class 5 Threat.

You have neutralized it, yes. But you have not cleaned it up.

The debris is a hazard.

The violation of the Noise Ordinance during combat is still outstanding. And you have failed to file a Post-Combat Safety Audit.

"

Gerald looked at the shattered stone of the Demolitionist. Chunks of granite lay scattered across the parking lot. Some pieces were large enough to crush a car.

"I will file it," Gerald said.

"You will file it," the Auditor corrected, "within six hours. If you fail to submit Form 77-C (Structural Hazard Remediation) before the sun sets, your Probationary status will be revoked.

Your Core will be terminated.

Not for abuse of process.

For negligence.

"

The Auditor walked away. His feet made no sound on the wet asphalt.

"Wait," Gerald said.

The Auditor stopped. He did not turn.

"Who authorized the Demolitionist?" Gerald asked. "It was not a random spawn. It was targeted.

It knew my layout. It knew where my support pillars were.

"

The Auditor turned slowly. The blank oval of his face reflected the streetlights.

"That is not for you to know, Mr. Hutchins. That is for the file."

He walked into the rain and vanished.

Gerald stood alone in the parking lot. The Demolitionist's hammer lay at his feet. It was huge, easily four feet long. It was covered in runes that pulsed with a dull, dying light.

Gerald looked at the runes. He recognized them. They were standard load-bearing enchantments, the kind used in high-rise foundations.

He picked up the clipboard. His hand was shaking. He forced it to stop.

*Task: Clean up.* *Time: 05:58:12.*

Gerald looked at the rubble.

There were tons of it. He could not move it with his bare hands. He could not spawn workers; his mana was too low.

He could not cite the rubble for trespassing because it was not an entity.

He needed a way to reduce the physical mass of the debris without violating the Environmental Code.

He opened his Core interface. He scrolled past the spawn menu. He scrolled past the construction menu. He found the *Hazardous Materials* tab.

*Item: Stone Debris (Class 5).* *Disposal Method: Crushing, Recycling, or Burial.* *Requirement: Licensed Recycling Facility or Permit Z-12.*

Gerald did not have a facility. He did not have a permit.

But he had a loophole, and he looked at the library entrance behind him. The main doors were boarded up, but the service entrance on the side was unlocked. Inside, the sub-basement was his dungeon.

The Demolitionist had punched through the foundation wall. The hole was jagged, dangerous. According to the code, any hole larger than four inches in a load-bearing wall required immediate reinforcement.

If Gerald reinforced the hole, he would be performing construction.

Construction generated debris.

If he generated debris, he could classify the Demolitionist's remains as

"construction waste" than "hazardous combat debris."

Construction waste could be stored on-site for up to forty-eight hours before requiring disposal, provided it was contained in a designated skip.

Gerald did not have a skip.

But the library had dumpsters.

Gerald ran to the side entrance. He burst into the library. The air smelled of mold and damp paper. His dungeon's ambient humidity was too high; he could feel it on his skin, a clammy film that made his hair stick to his forehead.

He moved through the dark corridors. His vision was poor, but he knew this place. He had inspected it every month for three decades. He knew every crack, every loose brick.

He reached the sub-basement. The hole in the wall was gaping. Through it, he could see the street, the rain, and the massive chunks of stone from the Demolitionist.

Gerald opened his inventory. He had one item left from his previous adventure: a *Bag of Holding (Small)*. It was rated for up to fifty pounds of organic material. It could not hold stone.

He needed to break the stone into pieces small enough to fit.

He could use a hammer. But swinging a hammer in a confined space was an OSHA violation. It required a safety harness and a spotter. He had no spotter.

He looked at his dungeon walls. The wood was rotting. The support beams were sagging.

*Structural Integrity: 34%.*

If he collapsed a section of the ceiling above the hole, the weight of the falling timber would crush the stone debris.

It was a terrible idea. It would lower his Compliance Rating further. It might kill him if the roof collapsed entirely.

But it was legal.

Section 8.4 of the Municipal Demolition Code allowed for

"controlled implosion" of unstable structures in the event of imminent failure.

Gerald ran back to the parking lot. He grabbed the Demolitionist's hammer. It was heavy, nearly two hundred pounds. He dragged it back to the hole in the wall.

He dragged it across the wet pavement, leaving a streak of red mud and gray dust.

He dragged it into the library, and he dragged it to the hole.

He placed the hammer on top of a large slab of granite that had fallen from the Demolitionist's leg.

Then he climbed onto a rotting beam above the hole.

He checked his wristwatch. 4:45 PM. The sun would set at 6:30. He had ninety minutes.

He needed to break the slab, and he needed to hit the slab with the beam.

He needed to do it in a way that looked like an accident.

Gerald took a breath. He closed his eyes. He counted to ten.

He opened his eyes, and he kicked the beam.

It cracked. It snapped. The beam fell, swinging down like a pendulum.

It struck the hammer. The hammer struck the slab.

The slab shattered.

Dust filled the air. The noise was deafening, a thunderclap in the enclosed space.

Gerald coughed, covering his mouth with his sleeve. He waited for the dust to settle.

When he looked, the slab was broken into chunks the size of footballs.

He looked at the *Bag of Holding*. It was still too small.

He looked at the dumpster outside, and he dragged the chunks of stone to the service door. He dragged them to the alley. He threw them into the library's official recycling bin.

*Action: Disposal.* *Method: Municipal Recycling.* *Status: Compliant.*

Gerald panted. His mana was gone. His HP was at 5%.

He looked at the clock. 5:10 PM, and he had done it. He had cleaned up. He had filed the intent.

But then he heard a sound.

Not from the alley. Not from the street.

From the dumpster.

Something rustled in the trash.

Gerald froze.

*Entity Detected.* *Type: Scavenger.* *Level: 1.* *Affiliation: Unknown.*

A hand emerged from the trash. It was pale, thin, and covered in sores. It grabbed the edge of the bin.

Then another hand.

A creature pulled itself up. It looked like a goblin, but it wore a tattered business suit. It had no eyes. Just empty sockets.

It looked at Gerald.

"You cited me," the creature whispered. Its voice was like gravel.

Gerald stepped back.

"I cited the rubble."

"The rubble is mine," the creature said. It climbed out of the bin.

It stood on wobbly legs.

"I am the Keeper of Waste. The Auditor sent me."

Gerald gripped his clipboard.

"You are not a registered entity," Gerald said. "You are trespassing on municipal property."

"I am the consequence," the creature said. "You broke the law of conservation.

You did not destroy the threat.

You recycled it.

You gave it new life.

"

The creature raised its hands. The trash around it swirled. Cans, plastic, glass, and stone flew into the air, forming a shield.

"Your Compliance Rating," the creature said, "has dropped to 20. You are now ineligible for System protection. You are now fair game."

Gerald looked at the swirling debris. He looked at his empty mana bar. He looked at the sunset, bleeding orange across the sky.

*Time Remaining: 01:15:00.*

The creature smiled. It had too many teeth.

"The Inspector General is watching, Mr. Hutchins. He wants to see if you will appeal the citation."

Gerald looked at the creature. He looked at the sky.

He did not raise his clipboard, and he reached into his pocket and pulled out his pen.

He clicked it.

The sound was small, but it echoed in the silence.

"Form 88-A," Gerald said. "Request for Asylum."

The creature's smile faltered.

"There is no such form," the creature said.

"There is," Gerald said. "It's buried in the Appendix. I memorized it in 1998. It allows a Core to claim sanctuary within a registered municipal building during a hostile audit."

Gerald pointed at the library.

"This is a municipal building."

The creature hissed. The debris shield lowered slightly.

"You cannot stay here," the creature said. "The library is condemned."

"It is not condemned," Gerald said. "It is under review. I filed the paperwork. You have the copy."

Gerald held up the coffee shop receipt.

"Read it."

The creature looked at the paper. It did not have eyes, but it leaned in.

As it leaned, Gerald moved, and he did not attack. He stepped sideways, into the shadow of the doorway.

He clicked the pen again.

*System Alert: Sanctuary Request Accepted.* *Location: Ashford Municipal Library.* *Duration: Until Sunset.*

A wall of light erupted between Gerald and the creature. It was thin, fragile, but it held.

The creature slammed against it. The light flared, then dimmed.

*Sanctuary Failed.* *Reason: Building Condemned.*

Gerald's heart stopped, and the Auditor had lied. The library was condemned.

The creature pushed through the light. It lunged.

Gerald had one second.

He looked at the floor. He looked at the hole he had made in the foundation.

He looked at the Demolitionist's hammer, still lying in the alley.

He looked at the creature.

"Objection," Gerald said.

He slammed his clipboard against the creature's knee, which was not a attack. It was a citation.

*Violation: Aggressive Posture in Public Easement.* *Penalty: Immediate Repulsion.*

The System, confused by the sudden legal maneuver in a combat zone, hesitated.

For a fraction of a second, the creature was repelled.

Gerald grabbed the hammer, and he did not swing it at the creature.

He swung it at the support beam above the door.

The beam snapped.

The ceiling came down.

Wood, brick, and plaster crashed onto the creature, pinning it to the ground.

Dust choked the air.

Gerald stood in the wreckage, bleeding from a cut on his forehead. His clipboard was broken. His pen was gone.

He looked at the clock.

5:59 PM, and the sun was setting.

And in the darkness of the collapsed library, something else waked up.

The stone beneath Gerald's feet hummed.

Not with power.

With hunger.
