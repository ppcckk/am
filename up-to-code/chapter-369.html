Chapter 369: Clipboard from the Hole

# Chapter 369

Gerald Hutchins did not like the smell of wet rot. It was unprofessional. It implied a failure of drainage infrastructure that should have been rectified during the initial zoning approval, not discovered forty-eight hours before a deletion protocol was triggered.

The wrench in his hand was heavy, cold, and useless without a reference point. He gripped it with both hands, knuckles white, and stared at the crumbling concrete floor of what used to be the primary filtration basin. The task prompt pulsed in his peripheral vision, a steady red rhythm that matched the pounding in his temples.

**CITATION WINDOW: CLOSED.**

He could not issue citations. He could not appeal. He could only build.

"Move the debris," the rat commanded.

The rat, whose name tag read *Auditor 7*, scurried across the slick floor on all fours, its tiny leather vest stretching over its ribs. It did not help. It supervised. It held a clipboard that seemed to materialize out of thin air, a piece of parchment that rustled aggressively whenever Gerald paused.

Gerald set the wrench down. He did not swing it.

He picked up a rusted pipe fitting instead, and he examined the joint where it connected to the wall. A hairline fracture ran through the metal.

"Non-compliant," Gerald muttered.

He tapped the fracture with a fingernail. The pipe groaned.

Auditor 7 scribbled on its clipboard.

"Structural integrity check failed. Deduction: five points from Efficiency Rating.

Current Rating: 92/100.

You are trending toward failure.

"

"I need a solvent," Gerald said. His voice was flat, stripped of panic.

Panic was inefficient.

Panic wasted oxygen.

"This corrosion is not surface-level. It is deep.

Chemical treatment is required to restore load-bearing capacity.

"

"The budget is zero," Auditor 7 said. It did not look up.

"Then we use mechanical agitation," Gerald said. He picked up the wrench again.

Not to hit the pipe, but to rotate it, and he found a loose bolt on the adjacent support beam. It was seized. He applied torque, slowly, feeling the resistance in his forearms.

The bolt did not move.

He tried again. The metal shrieked.

"Stop," Auditor 7 said. "You are damaging the substrate. Further damage to existing infrastructure will result in immediate citation for Negligent Repair."

Gerald froze. His hands hovered over the bolt.

"Explain," Gerald said.

"Rule 44-C," the rat said, quoting from memory. "Preservation of Historical Context.

In ruins, removal of original fixtures requires a Heritage Waiver.

You do not have a Waiver. Therefore, you must reinforce around the decay, not remove it.

"

Gerald blinked. He looked at the rotting wall. He looked at the rat.

"You want me to build new supports *inside* the old, failing structure?" Gerald asked.

"Yes."

"That is unsafe."

"It is compliant with the Heritage Act," Auditor 7 said. "And you have forty-seven hours and twelve minutes left. Move."

Gerald did not move. He closed his eyes.

He visualized the library sub-basement.

He visualized the handrails he had installed there, the precise angle of the brackets, the way the light hit the egress signage.

He had spent thirty-one years ensuring that buildings stood up. He knew that if you built around a cancer, you only delayed the collapse.

But the System did not care about collapse. The System cared about the paperwork.

Gerald opened his eyes. He looked at the pile of debris in the corner. Broken brick.

Rebar. Shards of glass.

"Inventory," Gerald said.

Auditor 7 paused.

"Explain."

"I need to know the weight limits of the remaining floor slab. If I am building supports, I need to know where the load can be placed without punching through the foundation."

Auditor 7 tilted its head.

"Scan initiated."

A blue grid overlay appeared in Gerald's vision. It washed over the room, highlighting stress fractures in red. Solid ground was green. Hollow voids were black.

The center of the room was black.

Gerald walked to the edge of the black zone. He dropped a piece of rebar. It fell silently into the darkness. No echo.

"Void," Gerald said.

"Historical sinkhole," Auditor 7 confirmed. "Common in municipal ruins. Do not fall in. Falling in is a safety violation."

Gerald looked at the wrench, and he looked at the black void. An idea formed, cold and sharp in his gut. It was a loophole.

He knew this System.

He had learned its syntax. It was literal.

It was pedantic.

And it was obsessed with definitions.

"Define 'reinforcement'," Gerald said.

"Structural additions that increase the load-bearing capacity of the existing assembly," Auditor 7 recited.

"Does 'reinforcement' include the containment of loose debris that could act as a counter-weight?" Gerald asked.

Auditor 7's tail twitched.

"Query logged. Answer: Yes, provided the debris is secured in a manner consistent with municipal waste containment standards."

Gerald smiled. It was a small, tight expression, barely visible.

"Then we do not build supports," Gerald said. "We build a cage."

He moved to the debris pile. He sorted.

Brick here.

Glass there.

Metal here.

Auditor 7 watched, confused.

"You are sorting garbage."

"I am prepping aggregate," Gerald corrected. He picked up a large shard of concrete. He carried it to the edge of the void.

He did not throw it in. He placed it on the lip of the hole.

Then he picked up another, and he stacked.

Not high. Wide. A pyramid of refuse, built with the precision of a mason.

"What are you doing?" Auditor 7 asked.

"Creating a temporary buttress," Gerald said. "I am using the weight of the rubble to stabilize the rim of the sinkhole. This prevents further collapse of the surrounding slab."

"That is... unorthodox."

"It is effective," Gerald said. He kept stacking. Brick.

Stone.

A rusted chair leg. He wove them together, using wire found in the pipes to bind them.

He worked for two hours.

His back ached. His knees popped. He did not stop.

The red timer in his vision ticked down. **45:00:00.**

Auditor 7 stopped writing. It stared at the pile.

"Efficiency Rating: 88," the rat said. "The structure is unstable."

"Test it," Gerald said. He stepped back.

Auditor 7 kicked the base of the rubble pile. It shifted. Dust fell. But it did not topple.

"Passable," Auditor 7 conceded.

"But the walls are still compromised.

The ceiling is low. Ventilation is nonexistent.

You have not addressed the air quality.

"

"I am addressing it," Gerald said. He pointed to the large, cracked vent in the ceiling. "That is a Class 4 ventilation failure. I cannot fix it."

"Then you will fail the air quality inspection."

"I will not fail," Gerald said. He walked to the corner of the room, where a large, rusted industrial fan lay on its side. The blades were bent. The motor was burned out.

Gerald picked up the wrench. He smashed the motor housing.

Sparks flew, and he ignored them. He removed the blades. He kept the casing.

"What are you doing?" Auditor 7 shrieked. "That is destruction of property!"

"I am repurposing," Gerald said.

He dragged the fan casing to the cracked vent. He wedged it into the opening.

It did not fit perfectly.

It rattled, and he then dragged a sheet of corrugated metal from the debris pile. He cut it with a jagged piece of rebar, slicing his glove.

Blood welled up.

He ignored the pain.

He fitted the metal sheet over the fan casing, creating a makeshift duct.

It was ugly. It was temporary. It was a violation of every aesthetic guideline in the municipal code.

But it created a tunnel.

"The air flows," Gerald said.

Auditor 7 sniffed the air.

"It is still damp."

"Less damp," Gerald said. "The draft will reduce humidity by 15% within the hour. That brings us into the 'Acceptable' range for non-residential storage."

Auditor 7 looked at the timer. **42:00:00.**

"You are gambling," the rat said.

"I am calculating," Gerald said.

A sound echoed from the shadows of the plant. A deep, wet gurgle. Not from the pipes. From the walls.

Auditor 7 froze.

"What was that?"

Gerald listened. It sounded like breathing. Massive, wet breathing.

"Biological entity?" Gerald asked.

"Unregistered," Auditor 7 said. Its voice trembled. "If a hostile entity enters the zone before inspection is complete, the audit is terminated. You will be deleted for Failure to Secure."

Gerald looked at the shadows. The darkness in the corner of the room was shifting. Something was moving in the sludge.

"You have not secured the perimeter," Auditor 7 said.

Gerald looked at his hands. They were covered in grime and blood. He looked at the wrench. It was clean.

He had no weapons. He had no guards. He had no walls.

He had forms.

Gerald closed his eyes. He reached for the System interface. It was greyed out, locked behind the citation ban.

But he remembered the fine print. The loophole he had used in Chapter 12.

The one that allowed a Core to issue a *Temporary Stop Work Order* if a hazard was identified and not immediately remedied by the responsible party.

The hazard was the entity in the corner. The responsible party was... himself.

He could not cite himself.

But he could cite the *environment*.

**ACTION: FILE SAFETY HAZARD NOTICE.**

The text appeared in his mind. He focused on it.

**HAZARD TYPE: UNCONTAINED BIOLOGICAL MATERIAL.**

**LOCATION: SECTOR 4, CORNER UNIT.**

**STATUS: UNREMEDIED.**

The System flickered. A red box appeared.

**STOP WORK ORDER ISSUED.**

The timer stopped.

**TIME REMAINING: PAUSED.**

Auditor 7 gasped. The rat dropped its clipboard.

"What have you done?" the rat whispered.

"I have declared the room unsafe," Gerald said. "Work cannot proceed until the hazard is removed."

"You paused the clock," the rat said. "But you did not remove the hazard."

"No," Gerald said. "I made the hazard my responsibility."

The shadows coalesced. A shape emerged. It was a mass of sludge, shaped like a man, but too tall.

It had no face, only a mouth that hung open, dripping black fluid.

It looked at Gerald.

It roared. A sound like tearing metal.

**HAZARD CONFIRMED.**

**PENALTY FOR NON-REMEDY: DELETION.**

The timer restarted. But it was different.

**COUNTER:**

**TIME TO REMEDY: 10 MINUTES.**

Gerald looked at the monster. He looked at the rat.

"You have ten minutes to kill it," Gerald said.

Auditor 7 shook its head.

"I cannot. I am an observer."

"Then you are negligent," Gerald said. "And negligence is a violation."

Gerald looked at the monster. It was advancing. The floor vibrated under its weight.

Gerald had no weapon. He had a wrench. He had a fan casing. He had a pile of rubble.

He had the Stop Work Order.

The System enforced safety codes. It forced compliance.

If the hazard was not removed, the dungeon was deleted.

But what if the hazard *was* the dungeon's compliance issue?

Gerald looked at the monster. He looked at the ventilation duct he had built.

He looked at the black void in the floor, and he picked up the wrench.

"Hey," Gerald said.

The monster paused.

Gerald pointed to the void.

"You are in violation of Code 12-A," Gerald said. "Failure to observe designated walkways. This is a tripping hazard."

The monster tilted its head. Sludge dripped from its nose.

Gerald did not wait for a response. He threw the wrench.

It did not hit the monster. It hit the fan casing.

The casing wobbled. It fell.

It crashed into the floor, blocking the path between the monster and the exit.

The monster roared again. It charged.

Gerald did not run. He stood his ground. He watched the monster come.

He watched the timer.

**09:58... 09:57...**

The monster reached the fan casing. It stepped over it.

Gerald raised his hand.

"Objection," he said.

The monster ignored him.

Gerald focused on the System. He pulled the Stop Work Order. He extended the scope.

**HAZARD UPDATED: OBSTRUCTION OF EGRESS.**

**TARGET: BIOLOGICAL ENTITY.**

The System pinged.

**HAZARD: OBSTRUCTION CONFIRMED.**

**PENALTY: REMOVAL OF OBSTRUCTION BY FORCE.**

The air pressure in the room dropped. The walls groaned. The ceiling cracked.

The System was not killing the monster. The System was trying to clear the path.

The ceiling above the monster collapsed.

Gerald dove.

Debris rained down. Bricks. Concrete. Glass.

The monster was buried.

Silence returned to the room.

Auditor 7 stood in the corner, untouched. A single brick had landed on its head, but the rat shook it off and straightened its vest.

Gerald pulled himself out from under a slab of concrete. He coughed. Dust coated his tongue.

He checked the timer.

**COUNTER: RESET.**

**TIME TO REMEDY: REMAINING.**

But the Stop Work Order was gone.

Auditor 7 picked up its clipboard. It looked at the pile of rubble that was now the monster.

"Remedy accepted," the rat said. "The hazard has been removed."

Gerald stood up. He brushed dust from his shirt.

"I did not remove it," Gerald said. "The System did."

"Result is the same," Auditor 7 said.

"You have forty-two hours. The walls are still compromised. The ceiling is missing.

And now, there is a hole in your floor.

"

The rat pointed to the spot where the ceiling had collapsed. It had punched through the floor, creating a larger hole.

"Structural Integrity: 60/100," the rat said. "You are failing."

Gerald looked at the new hole. It went deeper. Into darkness.

From the darkness, a sound came. Not a roar.

A click.

Then another.

Then the sound of footsteps. Hundreds of them. Small, precise, clicking footsteps.

Auditor 7's eyes widened.

"That sound..."

"What is it?" Gerald asked.

"Inspectors," the rat whispered. "Real ones."

A hand reached out of the hole. It was human. It wore a white glove.

And it held a clipboard.
