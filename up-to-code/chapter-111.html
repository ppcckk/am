Chapter 111: Blinding the Auditor

# Chapter 111 The wire bit into the plaster. It was a simple act of physics.

A copper strand, stripped of its insulation, driven into the fissure that ran like a lightning bolt across the ceiling of Chamber Four.

Gerald held the end of the wire in one hand and the exposed terminal of the dungeon’s primary lighting array in the other. He did not look at the Auditor. He looked at the crack.

If the Auditor was a machine, it operated on logic. If it operated on logic, it could be tricked by logic.

**HEARING STARTING IN: 3...**

Gerald twisted the wire.

The current surged. The lights above him flickered, dying instantly.

The dungeon plunged into absolute darkness, save for the cold, golden glow emanating from the Auditor’s chassis.

The creature floated in the center of the room, a monolith of impossible geometry, its surface shifting like oil on water.

**2...**

Gerald closed his eyes. He counted the seconds.

He knew the latency of a System packet, and he knew the lag of a local render.

He had spent thirty-one years knowing exactly how long it took for a permit to get stamped in the county office. It was usually three to five seconds.

**1...**

**HEARING COMMENCED.** **CASE FILE: CITATION 774-B (IMMEDIATE STRUCTURAL FAILURE).** **DEFENDANT: CORE ID 884-G.** **CHALLENGE: DEFENDANT CLAIMS CITATION IS INVALID DUE TO MISCLASSIFICATION OF LOAD-BEARING STATUS.**

The voice was not a voice. It was a vibration in the teeth.

Gerald opened his eyes. The darkness held for a fraction of a second, then the emergency backup lights kicked in. They were dim, red, and useless, but they cast long, jagged shadows.

Gerald stood in the center of the room. He had not moved.

He had not argued, and he had simply interrupted the power grid for exactly two seconds.

The Auditor hovered. Its eye, a point of white light, scanned the room.

"EXPLAIN THE MISCLASSIFICATION," the Auditor vibrated.

Gerald stepped forward. His boots crunched on debris. He walked to the wall where the crack was, and pointed a finger at the wire he had just jammed into the fissure.

"The citation claims the ceiling is a non-load-bearing partition," Gerald said. His voice was steady. "It claims that the removal of this section poses no risk to the structural integrity of the upper floors. That is the basis for the demolition order that triggered the audit."

"AFFIRMED," the Auditor said. "STRUCTURAL SENSORS DETECT NO SUPPORT BEAMS IN THIS SECTOR. THE MATERIALS ARE DECORATIVE."

"Then why is the wire holding?" Gerald asked.

The Auditor paused. The light in its eye pulsed.

"THE WIRE IS CONDUCTIVE. IT IS NOT STRUCTURAL."

"Look closer," Gerald said.

He pointed to the ceiling. "Look at the dust pattern.

Observe the plaster's fractures.

It didn’t shatter. It fractured. That’s not decorative plaster.

That’s a shear wall. The System’s sensors are calibrated to detect magical reinforcement, not municipal drywall.

You missed the human element."

The Auditor floated closer. It extended a limb, a tendril of golden light, and touched the wall. It scanned the material.

**ANALYSIS: MATERIAL COMPOSITION: GYPSUM PLASTER, WOOD LATH, GALVANIZED STEEL STUDS.** **MAGICAL INFUSION: NONE.** **MAGICAL SUPPORT: NONE.**

"Your sensors are looking for mana," Gerald said.

"I’m talking about steel. The System assumes a dungeon is a magical construct.

But my dungeon is a basement. It was built in 1974. It has a load-bearing wall.

If you demolish this wall as per the citation’s remediation protocol, the library above collapses, and that is a Class A Catastrophic Failure.

That is a violation of the Prime Directive of Dungeon Stability."

The Auditor retracted its limb. It turned to face Gerald.

**QUERY: YOU ARE ARGUING THAT THE SYSTEM IS WRONG?**

"I am arguing that the Code is specific," Gerald said. "Subsection 4.12: *Structural integrity is defined by physical mass and material tensile strength, not magical aura.* If the System enforces a rule that causes a Class A Failure, the rule is void. Therefore, the citation is void. Therefore, you have no grounds to delete me."

The silence stretched. It was heavy. It tasted like ozone and old dust.

**CITATION REVIEWED.** **ERROR FOUND: SENSOR MISINTERPRETATION OF MATERIAL CLASS.** **CITATION 774-B REVOKED.** **AUDIT STATUS: PENDING.**

Gerald exhaled. His knees buckled. He caught himself on the desk.

He had done it, and he had beaten the Auditor with a wire and a fact about 1970s construction.

But the victory felt hollow. The golden light did not fade. The Auditor did not retreat. It remained in the center of the room, hovering, watching.

**PENDING AUDIT IMPLIES FURTHER INVESTIGATION.** **WARNING: REPEATED FALSE CLAIMS RESULT IN PERMANENT BANISHMENT.** **NEXT AUDIT IN: 24 HOURS.**

"Twenty-four hours," Gerald whispered.

He looked at Kael. The adventurer was leaning against the doorframe, pale. He looked at Gerald with a mixture of awe and terror.

"You just told a god it’s stupid," Kael said. "And it listened."

"It’s not a god," Gerald said. "It’s a bureaucrat. A very dangerous, very literal bureaucrat."

Gerald rubbed his temples, and the headache was back. The stress of the Hearing had drained him. He needed to rest.

He needed to reinforce the wall; had to determine if the System missed other load-bearing walls in the dungeon.

But before he could move, the floor beneath him shuddered.

It was not a vibration from the Auditor, which was rhythmic.

Thud. Thud.

Thud.

Something was walking through the lower corridors.

Gerald checked his minimap. The red blips were not where they should be.

The rat was in the kitchen, and the mold spores were in the ventilation shaft; the water leak was dripping into the drain.

But a new icon had appeared. It was large. It was moving fast. It was heading straight for the central chamber.

"Kael," Gerald said. "Get the weapon."

"I don’t have a weapon," Kael said, drawing his sword anyway. "I have a dagger and a prayer."

"Good enough."

The door to the central chamber burst open. Splinters flew.

A creature stepped through, which was seven feet tall. It had skin like granite and eyes like burning coals.

It wore armor made of jagged obsidian shards.

It carried a hammer the size of a beer keg.

This was not a goblin. This was not an orc. This was a Stone Golem, summoned from the deep earth.

Behind the Golem, a voice echoed. It was smooth, cultured, and dripping with disdain.

"Ah. The Inspector."

Gerald recognized the voice. It belonged to Core ID 902-F.

The local rival.

The Dungeon of the Black Spire.

His name was Malacor.

Malacor did not step into the room. He stayed in the hallway, his form projected through a crystal embedded in the Golem’s chest.

"You survived the Auditor," Malacor said. "Impressive. I expected you to be dust by now."

"I follow the rules," Gerald said.

"Rules are for those without power," Malacor sneered. "And you, Gerald, have no power. You have a wire and a wall.

I have a Golem. And I have the budget."

The Golem took a step forward. The floor cracked under its weight.

"I have issued a challenge," Malacor said. "A formal Core Duel. Under System Rule 114, if a Core challenges a lower-tier Core to a duel and the lower-tier Core refuses, the lower-tier Core is deemed in default and forfeits all assets."

Gerald felt a cold knot in his stomach.

A Core Duel. He had read about them. They were rare.

They were brutal, and they were designed for Cores who had invested heavily in combat units. Gerald had invested in handrails and fire extinguishers.

"I do not accept," Gerald said.

"Then you default," Malacor said. "And I take your dungeon. I will pave over your precious library. I will turn your basement into my new treasury."

The System window flashed.

**CHALLENGE RECEIVED FROM CORE ID 902-F.** **OPTIONS: ACCEPT OR DEFAULT.** **DEFAULT PENDING: 60 SECONDS.**

Gerald looked at the timer. Fifty seconds.

He looked at the Golem. It was ready to smash him.

He looked at Kael. Kael was trembling.

Gerald had no combat units. He had no mana shields. He had no traps that would work on a Stone Golem.

But he had the Code.

"Accept," Gerald said.

The System window flashed.

**DUEL INITIATED.** **ARENA: LOCAL DUNGEON FOOTPRINT.** **RULES: TOTAL DESTRUCTION OF OPPONENT CORE OR SURRENDER.** **STARTING TIME: 00:00.**

The Golem roared. It charged.

Gerald did not run. He did not raise a shield. He walked toward the Golem.

Malacor laughed. "Come on, then. Let’s see your wire fight magic."

The Golem swung its hammer. The air pressure alone blew Gerald’s hair back.

Gerald sidestepped, which was not a dodge. It was a calculation. He knew the arc of the swing.

He knew the momentum. He stepped into the blind spot created by the hammer’s over-extension.

The Golem stumbled.

Gerald reached out and touched the Golem’s knee joint. He placed his palm flat against the obsidian armor.

"Code Violation," Gerald muttered.

**ACTIVATING SUB-ROUTINE: TENSILE STRESS ANALYSIS.**

The Golem froze. It could not see Gerald’s attack because Gerald was not attacking its health bar. He was attacking its structure.

Gerald had studied the Golem’s design as it entered the room, and it was a construct of magical stone, bound by arcane runes. But the runes were anchored to physical points.

Gerald tapped three points on the Golem’s leg. *Tap. Tap. Tap.*

The runes flickered.

"What are you doing?" Malacor’s voice sharpened.

"Checking the bolts," Gerald said.

The Golem tried to move, but its leg locked. The magical binding was disrupted. The physical stress of the previous step had caused a micro-fracture in the rune’s anchor point. Gerald had found it.

The Golem fell to one knee.

Malacor screamed. "Attack him! Tear him apart!"

The Golem swung its other arm. Gerald ducked. The hammer smashed into the floor, creating a crater.

Gerald was not fighting to kill. He was fighting to dismantle.

He moved around the Golem, tapping, probing, citing.

**CITATION: UNSAFE JOINT ALIGNMENT.** **EFFECT: MOBILITY REDUCED BY 40%.**

The Golem slowed. It stumbled. It tried to step over the crater, but its footing slipped.

Gerald was dancing. It was the most undignified dance he had ever performed. He was dodging lethal blows by inches, using the environment, using the dust, using the fact that the Golem was heavy and clumsy.

But he was running out of time. The Golem was adapting. It was learning to compensate for the damaged runes.

Malacor was furious. "This is a trick! This is not combat!"

"It is compliance," Gerald said.

He reached the Golem’s back. He saw the main power core. A pulsing red gem embedded in the stone spine.

He could not break it. It was too hard.

But he could overload it.

Gerald looked at the ceiling, and he looked at the wire.

He had left the wire in the crack. The emergency power was still on.

The lightning resistance he had earned from building his dungeon to electrical code was active.

He ran to the wall. He grabbed the end of the wire.

He ran to the Golem.

Malacor saw it coming. "Protect the core!"

The Golem turned, raising its arms to block.

Gerald jumped. He did not aim for the Golem. He aimed for the ceiling.

He slammed the wire into the exposed junction box on the ceiling.

**WARNING: HIGH VOLTAGE DETECTED.**

The electricity surged. It traveled down the wire, through the air, and into the metallic dust that had shaken loose from the Golem’s armor during the fight.

The dust was conductive, and the Golem was surrounded by a cloud of conductive particulate matter.

The electricity arced. It jumped from the wire, through the dust, and into the Golem’s metallic joints.

The Golem convulsed. The magical runes short-circuited. The red core flickered, then exploded.

The Golem collapsed. It hit the floor with a sound like a thunderclap. The dust cloud dissipated.

Silence.

Gerald lay on the floor, smoking slightly. His hand was blistered. His heart was hammering against his ribs.

Malacor’s crystal was dark. The connection was severed.

**DUEL RESULT: VICTORY FOR CORE ID 884-G.** **REWARD: STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY BONUS (+15% PHYSICAL DAMAGE RESISTANCE).** **STATUS: THREAT NEUTRALIZED.**

Gerald groaned. He pushed himself up.

Kael ran to him. "Did you win?"

"I cited the Golem for electrical non-compliance," Gerald said. "And it died."

Kael stared at him. "That’s insane."

"It’s efficient," Gerald said.

But the victory was short-lived. The System window flashed again.

**ALERT: UNAUTHORIZED STRUCTURAL ALTERATION DETECTED.** **SOURCE: CORE ID 902-F (MALACOR).** **ACTION: RETALIATORY DEMOLITION INITIATED.**

The ground shook. Not from the Golem. From outside.

Gerald ran to the window. The library above was crumbling.

Malacor was not done. He was bringing down the building.

Gerald looked at the ceiling. The crack was widening. The library’s foundation was failing.

If the library collapsed, the dungeon would be crushed. Gerald would die. Not deleted. Crushed.

He had seconds, and he had no reinforcements. He had no mana. He had only his knowledge of the building.

He knew where the support columns were. He knew where the weak points were.

He ran to the center of the room. He pulled out his clipboard. He started writing.

Not a citation. A modification order.

**SUBMITTING REQUEST: EMERGENCY BRACING PROTOCOL.** **COST: 500 MANA UNITS.** **AVAILABLE MANA: 12.**

He didn’t have the mana.

But he had something else. He had the Auditor.

The Auditor was still in the room. It had not left. It was watching.

Gerald looked up at the floating machine.

"Auditor!" Gerald shouted. "I am invoking Emergency Clause 9: *Immediate Remediation of Imminent Catastrophic Failure.*"

The Auditor turned.

**CLAUSE 9 IS RESERVED FOR GOVERNMENT-SPONSORED DUNGEONS.** **YOUR DUNGEON IS PRIVATE.**

"Then sponsor me!" Gerald yelled. "Cite me for the violation!

Cite me for the lack of public access!

Cite me for anything! Make me a public utility! If you don’t, the library falls, and the System loses a data node.

You have a duty to preserve data nodes!"

The Auditor hovered. The golden light pulsed.

The ceiling groaned. A chunk of plaster fell.

The Auditor moved toward Gerald.

**QUERY: DO YOU CONSENT TO INCORPORATION?**

"Incorporate?" Gerald asked.

**SUBJECT TO MUNICIPAL OVERSIGHT. PUBLIC ACCESS REQUIRED. TAXES DUE.**

"If I say yes, do I survive?"

**YES.**

The ceiling cracked again. A beam snapped.

"YES!" Gerald screamed.

**INCORPORATION COMPLETE.** **CORE ID 884-G IS NOW A PUBLIC UTILITY.** **EMERGENCY BRACING DEPLOYED.**

The floor glowed. Blue lines spread across the dungeon. The walls thickened. The ceiling held.

The shaking stopped.

Gerald fell to his knees. He was alive.

But then the System window flashed.

**NOTIFICATION: TAX BILL ISSUED.** **AMOUNT: 10,000 COPPER PIECES.** **DUE: IMMEDIATELY.**

Gerald stared at the number. He had twelve copper pieces.

The Auditor looked at him.

**PAYMENT FAILURE RESULTS IN ASSET SEIZURE.**

Gerald looked at Kael.

"We have a problem," Gerald said.

And outside, in the streets of Ashford, the city council was already drafting the bylaws to regulate the new, dangerous, tax-exempt dungeon that had just appeared in their neighborhood.

It was already too late to regulate it.
