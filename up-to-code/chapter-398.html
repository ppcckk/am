Chapter 398: Off Schedule Trial

# Chapter 398

The pen was silver. The nib was black. The hand holding it was translucent, shimmering with a faint, iridescent glow that suggested it belonged to something far more expensive than a rat.

It hovered.

The entity attached to the hand stepped through the jagged hole in the concrete. It was tall.

Too tall.

Its limbs were elongated, stretching the fabric of its midnight-blue suit until the seams screamed. It wore no hat, but its face was obscured by a mask of polished obsidian, smooth and featureless save for two vertical slits where eyes should be.

Gerald Hutchins did not run. He did not scream. He adjusted his glasses.

He looked at the hand. He looked at the pen. He looked at the fresh, wet concrete still glistening in the hole where the lance had been jammed.

"You're not on the schedule," Gerald said. His voice was flat, stripped of the adrenaline that should have flooded his veins.

The entity tilted its head. The obsidian mask caught the emergency red lighting, fracturing it into a dozen bloody shards.

"Schedule?" The voice was like gravel shifting under a glacier. "We do not follow schedules, Inspector. We follow the Flow."

Gerald blinked.

"The Flow is not a building code."

"Everything is a building code," the entity whispered. It raised the pen. "Especially when the foundation is rotting."

Before Gerald could respond, the air in the sub-basement thickened. The smell of ozone vanished, replaced by the scent of old paper and rotting timber. The entity snapped its fingers.

A wave of pressure hit Gerald's chest. It wasn't physical force; it was administrative weight. It was the sensation of a thousand rejected permit applications landing on a desk at once. Gerald stumbled back, his boots slipping on the wet floor.

The knight on the floor groaned, lifting his head. The armor clattered against the concrete.

"Who are you?" the knight gasped, blood trickling from beneath his helmet.

Gerald didn't answer, and he was staring at the entity's pen. It wasn't writing on paper.

It was writing in the air.

Glowing blue script hung suspended in the void, forming characters Gerald recognized instantly.

*Section 14.2: Unauthorized Structural Modification.*

*Penalty: Immediate Demolition Order.*

"No," Gerald muttered. "You can't issue an order without a citation first. You have to serve it. You have to be present."

The entity smiled behind the mask. The sound was a wet tear.

"I am here, Inspector. I am serving it."

The blue script expanded. It wrapped around Gerald's dungeon walls, pulsing like a vein. The Compliance Rating in his peripheral vision dropped.

*88/100.*

*72/100.*

*41/100.*

The mold spores in the corner shrieked. The water leak erupted into a geyser. The ceiling groaned, dust raining down onto Gerald's shoulders.

The entity stepped forward. It didn't walk; it glided, its feet not touching the ground. It pointed the pen at the knight.

"And you," the entity said. "You are an obstacle to the renovation."

The knight raised his lance, but his arm shook. The debuff was already taking hold. His armor, usually a beacon of heroic light, dimmed. His joints locked.

Gerald's hand moved on instinct. He grabbed a shard of broken concrete from the floor. It was sharp. Heavy.

"Stop," Gerald said.

The entity ignored him. It brought the pen down, slashing a line through the air.

A crack appeared in the knight's breastplate.

Not a physical crack.

A *legal* crack. A structural deficiency in the hero's existence.

The knight screamed. It was a sound of tearing metal and breaking bones.

Gerald looked at the entity. He looked at the glowing citations. He looked at the System interface hovering in his mind, screaming warnings in red text.

*System Alert: Core Integrity Critical.* *System Alert: Unauthorized Auditor Detected.*

*Unauthorized Auditor?*

The word stuck in Gerald's brain. An auditor. Not a combatant.

Not a boss. An auditor.

And auditors had rules. Even the illegal ones.

Gerald dropped the concrete shard. He reached into his pocket. He didn't pull out a weapon. He pulled out his clipboard.

It was empty. The pages were blank.

He looked at the entity.

"You're violating the Noise Ordinance."

The entity paused. The pen hovered an inch from the knight's throat.

"Excuse me?" the entity said.

"Section 7.4, Paragraph B," Gerald said, his voice gaining volume. "Noise levels during off-hours inspections shall not exceed 40 decibels. Your entrance was 85. Your gloating is at least 60."

The entity lowered the pen slightly.

"This is a dungeon. Decibels are irrelevant."

"In a municipal zone, they are not," Gerald said. "And I am the authority."

"Are you?" The entity laughed.

It was a high, shrieking sound.

"Your Compliance Rating is plummeting. Your dungeon is crumbling.

You are a man holding a clipboard in the dark.

You are nothing.

"

"I am an Inspector," Gerald said. "And you are in my jurisdiction."

He stepped forward.

The entity recoiled. Not from fear, but from confusion. It had expected resistance.

It had expected magic. It had not expected bureaucracy.

Gerald raised his hand. He didn't touch the entity. He touched the air.

He focused. He remembered the rules. He remembered the fine print he had spent forty years memorizing.

*Appeal Window: 10 Seconds.*

*Jurisdictional Override: Requires Proof of Ownership.*

Gerald slammed his palm against his chest.

"System! File a Counter-Complaint. Violation of Sovereign Core Territory."

The System hesitated.

**[Error: Core is Under Audit.]**

**[Counter-Complaint Rejected.]**

Gerald gritted his teeth. He tried again.

"System! File a Motion for Stay of Execution. Citing lack of prior notice."

The System processed. The red light flickered.

**[Motion Accepted.]** **[Processing...]** **[Stay Granted: 3 Seconds.]**

Three seconds.

The entity's pen froze in mid-air. The glowing blue script halted its descent. The knight stopped screaming, gasping for air.

Gerald had three seconds, and he looked at the hole in the wall.

The void behind it was swirling, dark and hungry.

The entity was a projection, he realized. A construct sent by the Inspector General to test him.

To break him.

But constructs had anchors.

Gerald ran to the hole. He didn't jump in. He kicked the lance that was still stuck in the concrete.

It wobbled, and he kicked it again. Harder.

The lance flew loose.

Gerald caught it.

He spun. He threw the lance not at the entity, but at the pen.

The silver tip struck the black nib.

A spark flew. A loud *crack* echoed through the sub-basement.

The entity shrieked. The obsidian mask cracked. A shard of black glass flew off, hitting the wall.

The pen dissolved into smoke.

The blue citations vanished.

The pressure on Gerald's chest lifted.

The entity staggered back. Its form flickered, becoming transparent again.

"You—" the entity gasped. Its voice was distorted, static-filled. "You cannot... interfere..."

"I just did," Gerald said. He grabbed the knight by the collar of his armor and hauled him up. "Move. Now."

The knight stumbled, leaning heavily on Gerald.

"My legs..."

"Use them," Gerald said. "We have to get to the service elevator."

The entity reformed. The smoke swirled, pulling back together. The mask reassembled, though the crack remained.

Gerald looked at his System interface.

**[Stay of Execution Expires in: 0.1 Seconds.]**

He looked at the entity, and he looked at the knight.

He made a choice.

He didn't run for the elevator. He ran toward the entity.

"System," Gerald said. "Query: Is an Auditor subject to trespassing laws if they enter without a warrant?"

**[Clarification Required.]**

"Does the System recognize Warrants for Audits?"

**[Yes. Warrant of Inspection must be served 24 hours in advance.]**

Gerald stopped three feet from the entity. He pulled a small, folded piece of paper from his pocket. It was a receipt for a coffee he'd bought two days ago. He crumpled it into a ball.

"I didn't see a warrant," Gerald said. "I didn't see a notice."

The entity loomed over him. It was taller now.

Stronger, and the 3 seconds were up. The stay was over.

"You are a fool, Inspector," the entity said. It raised a hand to strike him.

Gerald closed his eyes.

He felt the wind of the blow coming.

And then he heard the sound.

*Click.*

A mechanical sound. Like a stamp hitting paper.

The entity froze.

Gerald opened one eye.

The entity's hand was suspended in the air. Glowing red chains had wrapped around its wrist.

**[Violation: Unauthorized Use of Force Within a Protected Zone.]** **[Violation: Failure to Present Valid Warrant.]** **[Penalty: Immediate Ejection.]**

The System had responded.

The chains tightened. The entity screamed, its form distorting, stretching like taffy.

Gerald looked up at the entity. He didn't smile. He didn't gloat. He looked bored.

"You're cited," Gerald said.

The entity was pulled backward. The void behind the hole opened wide, a black vortex sucking in the smoke, the suit, the mask.

For a second, the entity looked at Gerald with genuine hatred. Not the hatred of a monster. The hatred of a bureaucrat who had been outmaneuvered by a petty clerk.

Then it was gone.

The hole in the wall snapped shut. The concrete sealed itself with a hiss of steam.

Silence returned to the sub-basement.

Gerald slumped to the floor. His clipboard clattered beside him.

The knight fell to his knees, coughing.

"What... what was that?"

Gerald picked up his clipboard, and he checked his reflection in the shard of glass on the floor. He looked terrible.

His shirt was torn.

His glasses were crooked.

"An audit," Gerald said.

He looked at the System interface.

**[Compliance Rating: 15/100.]** **[Status: Critical.]** **[New Notification: Inspector General has Flagged Your Core for 'Unsanctioned Aggression'.]**

Gerald stared at the text.

*Unsanctioned Aggression.*

He hadn't attacked. He had defended. He had followed the rules.

But the System disagreed.

A new window popped up. It was large. It covered the entire field of vision.

**[MANDATORY HEARING: CASE #9942-B]** **[Subject: Gerald Hutchins vs. The Inspector General]** **[Charge: Malicious Use of Technicalities.]** **[Penalty: Total Demolition of Core.]** **[Time to Prepare Defense: 1 Hour.]**

Gerald felt his stomach drop. He had won the battle. He had repelled the auditor. But he had triggered something worse.

He looked at the knight. The knight was staring at him, eyes wide behind the visor.

"You broke the rules," the knight whispered.

"No," Gerald said. "I used them."

He stood up. His legs felt weak. He brushed the dust off his trousers.

"Where is the elevator?" Gerald asked.

"The back," the knight said, pointing to a heavy steel door at the far end of the room. "But it's locked. The power is out."

Gerald walked to the door. He tried the handle. It was rusted shut.

He pulled out his pen. A normal, cheap ballpoint pen.

He looked at the door. He looked at the System interface.

**[Structural Weakness Detected: Hinges.]**

Gerald tapped the door with the pen.

"System," he said. "Cite the door for improper maintenance of pivot points."

**[Citation Accepted.]** **[Debuff Applied to Door: Rusted Hinges.]**

With a groan, the hinges gave way. The door swung open.

Behind it was not the service elevator, which was a staircase. Leading down.

Deeper.

The air coming from the staircase was cold. It smelled of sulfur and old blood.

And on the wall, painted in bright, glowing yellow letters, was a sign.

**[ASHFORD SUB-LEVEL 9: RESTRICTED AREA.]**

Gerald had never known his dungeon went this deep, and he had only measured four thousand square feet. He had mapped the sub-basement of the library.

But the sign said *Sub-Level 9.*

Gerald looked at the knight.

"You know where this goes?"

The knight shook his head.

"I've never seen it."

Gerald looked at the stairs. The darkness at the bottom seemed to pulse.

He looked at his Compliance Rating. 15/100, and he looked at the timer.

**[Hearing Starts in: 59 Minutes, 58 Seconds.]**

He had one hour to prepare for a trial that could end his existence.

And he had a mysterious staircase leading to a level of his dungeon that didn't exist on any blueprint he had ever drawn.

Gerald Hutchins took a deep breath. He tightened his tie.

He stepped into the dark.
