Chapter 199: Wrong Technicality

# Chapter 199

The floorboard cracked. It did not shatter. It split. A jagged fissure ran through the laminate, exposing the rebar beneath like exposed bone.

Gerald felt the vibration travel up through his core. The Emergency Protocol had activated. It was not a combat maneuver. It was a structural intervention.

The intruders froze. The leader, a man in black tactical gear holding a torch that burned with unnatural green flame, looked down at the fissure.

He frowned, and he did not attack. He assessed.

Gerald's mana reserves plummeted. The electrical overload from the previous minute had already cost him thirty percent of his capacity. The Protocol cost another ten. He was running on fumes and spite.

Above, the ceiling groaned. Debris dusted the floor.

The leader spoke.

"It's not a trap. It's a breach."

One of his companions kicked the fissure again. It widened. The structural integrity of Sub-Basement 4 was failing.

Gerald watched the fissure spread. He watched the compliance rating tick down in the corner of his vision.

**COMPLIANCE RATING: 41/100.** **PENALTY: STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY COMPROMISED. SPAWN HEALTH REDUCED BY 15%.**

He could not fight them with mana. He could not fight them with minions. He had only one weapon left.

Documentation.

Gerald reached into the void where his inventory should have been. He pulled out a Form 11-C: *Emergency Structural Remediation Order*. It glowed with a dull, bureaucratic blue.

He stamped it. *APPROVED. GERALD HUTCHINS, CORE-734.*

The System accepted it. A new window opened in his vision.

**NOTICE: EMERGENCY REMEDIATION INITIATED.** **REQUIRED: VERTICAL SUPPORT COLUMNS (3).** **TIME LIMIT: 30 SECONDS.** **FAILURE TO COMPLY WILL RESULT IN COLLAPSE AND TOTAL LOOT LOSS.**

Three seconds.

Gerald looked at the water leak. It was gone, reabsorbed into the plumbing matrix. Useless.

He looked at the mold spores. They were dormant. They were small.

He looked at the rat, and the rat was cowering behind a stack of shelving units that had not yet fallen. It squeaked. A high, terrified sound.

Gerald stared at the rat.

He needed columns. He needed vertical support. The rat was too small. The mold was too diffuse.

But the shelving units. They were metal. They were modular. They were rated for forty pounds per square foot.

If he repurposed them.

If he cited them for improper storage.

Gerald focused his will. He targeted the nearest shelving unit. It stood ten feet away, holding three boxes of damp books.

"System," Gerald said. His voice was flat. "File Citation: Improper Storage of Combustible Materials in Egress Path."

The System hesitated. Then it processed.

**CITATION ISSUED.** **TARGET: SHELVING UNIT A-4.** **VIOLATION: OBSTRUCTION OF EGRESS.** **PENALTY: IMMEDIATE DISASSEMBLEMENT AND RELOCATION.**

The shelving unit shuddered.

Its bolts sheared. The metal frame twisted.

It did not fall over.

It unfolded. The sides detached. The top plate separated.

The unit reconfigured itself into three separate, heavy-duty steel plates and four support rods.

It looked like a collapsed IKEA furniture nightmare. But it was code-compliant disassembly.

Gerald grabbed the mental control of the rods. He dragged them toward the fissure.

It hurt. It felt like dragging a car with his thoughts.

The first rod slammed into the concrete floor, perpendicular to the crack.

The second rod followed, and the third rod was slow. The intruder above leaned in. He saw the steel bars moving. He raised his sword.

"Blades," the leader said.

The blade struck the third rod. Sparks flew. The rod stopped.

Gerald gritted his teeth. He pushed.

The rod slid into the crack. It locked against the rebar.

**REMEDIATION COMPLETE.** **STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY RESTORED TO 60%.** **COMPLIANCE RATING: 43/100.**

The ceiling stopped groaning.

But the intruders were still there. They looked at the steel bars protruding from the floor. They looked at Gerald's central core, which was now visible through the gap in the shelving.

The leader smiled. It was a cold, professional smile.

"Interesting," he said. "You use the System against itself."

He sheathed his sword. He pulled out a black tablet. He tapped it.

**SYSTEM ALERT: UNAUTHORIZED INSPECTION REQUEST.** **SENDER: THE BLACK IRON INSPECTORS.** **STATUS: PENDING.**

Gerald's blood ran cold. He knew that name. He had read about them in the forums, back when he was still human and browsing Reddit for complaints about zoning laws.

They were not adventurers, and they were corporate auditors.

They worked for the largest dungeon conglomerate in the region. They did not fight.

They sued. They filed cease-and-desist orders. They bought your land and paved it into a parking lot.

And now they were here. In his basement.

The leader looked up at the fissure.

"We are filing a counter-claim. You have violated Section 4.2 of the Inter-Dungeon Conflict Resolution Act. Unauthorized structural modification of shared borders."

Gerald blinked.

"Shared borders?"

"Your dungeon expands into our territory," the leader said. He pointed at the wall to the east.

"That wall.

It's not yours. It's a retaining wall for the Ironspire complex. You just cracked it.

"

Gerald looked at the wall, and he had always assumed the walls were his. The System had said so. *Your Dungeon Footprint: 4,000 sq ft.*

He had never checked the survey maps. He had never verified the property lines.

He had been so busy installing handrails that he had missed the easement.

**SYSTEM ALERT: TITLE DISPUTE DETECTED.** **CLAIMANT: IRONSPIRE CORE-992.** **DEFENDANT: CORE-734 (Gerald Hutchins).** **HEARING SCHEDULED: IMMEDIATE.** **PENALTY FOR LOSS: SEIZURE OF 50% OF MANA RESERVES AND DELETION OF ALL SPAWN.**

Gerald felt the room spin, and he had one minute. Maybe two.

He looked at the rat. The rat was still hiding.

He looked at the mold. The mold was still dormant.

He looked at the intruders. They were waiting. They had time. They had lawyers.

Gerald had a pen. A broken pen.

He picked up the halves of the pen. He snapped them together. They did not hold.

He needed a witness, and he needed someone to testify that the wall was, in fact, his.

Or he needed to prove that the Ironspire Core had violated the code first.

Gerald closed his eyes. He scanned the System logs for the last hour. He looked for any interaction between his dungeon and the neighboring complex.

Nothing, and he looked for any interaction between the intruders and the wall.

There.

When the intruder had kicked the floorboard earlier, he had hit the wall.

The wall had not moved.

But the wall had hummed.

A low frequency vibration.

Gerald zoomed in on the System telemetry.

**WALL MATERIAL: REINFORCED CONCRETE.** **AGE: 12 YEARS.** **LAST INSPECTION: NONE.** **COMPLIANCE STATUS: UNKNOWN.**

If the wall had no inspection record, it was non-compliant.

If it was non-compliant, it could be cited.

But who cited it?

Only a Core could cite a Core.

Gerald was a Core. The Ironspire was a Core.

But the Ironspire wasn't here. The intruders were just its agents.

Gerald had to serve the Citation. To the Core. In person.

He couldn't leave his dungeon.

Unless.

Unless he summoned them.

**SYSTEM RULE: A CITATION CAN BE SERVED VIA SUMMONS IF THE TARGET IS WITHIN RANGE AND CONSENTS TO SERVICE.**

They wouldn't consent.

But there was a loophole.

**RULE 114: SERVICE BY PUBLICATION.**

If a Core issues a Citation to a public forum, and the target Core has an active profile in that forum, the Citation is considered served.

Gerald had a profile. He had posted a picture of his handrails on the *Dungeon Core Forum* yesterday.

Did the Ironspire have a profile?

Gerald checked the global registry.

**IRONSPIRE CORE-992.** **STATUS: ACTIVE.** **LAST POST: "New Loot Tables Available. Check Us Out!"** **LAST POST TIME: 10 MINUTES AGO.**

Gerald's hands shook, and he had to post a Citation. Right now. On the Forum.

He had to write it.

He had to make it perfect.

One typo would invalidate the legal standing.

He opened his mental interface to the Forum. His fingers trembled.

He typed.

**SUBJECT: CITATION NOTICE - CORE-734 TO CORE-992.** **VIOLATION: FAILURE TO MAINTAIN LOAD-BEARING STRUCTURAL ELEMENTS PER SECTION 8.1 OF THE MUNICIPAL BUILDING CODE (CROSS-APPLIED TO SYSTEM INFRASTRUCTURE).** **EVIDENCE: UNINSPECTED RETAINING WALL EXHIBIT A.** **SERVICE METHOD: PUBLICATION PER RULE 114.**

He hit post.

The System hung.

The intruders watched the wall.

One of them tapped his earpiece.

"Did you get it?"

The other nodded.

"It's live. It's on the board."

Gerald held his breath.

The System processed the post.

**POST ACCEPTED.** **CITATION SERVED.** **HEARING BEGINNING IN 60 SECONDS.** **LOCATION: MENTAL PLANE.** **PARTICIPANTS: CORE-734, CORE-992.**

The world around Gerald dissolved. The library basement vanished. The intruders vanished. The mold and the rat vanished.

He stood in a white void.

Across from him stood a man in a suit. The suit was made of black metal plates. The face was a shield.

"Ironspire," the man said. His voice sounded like grinding gears.

"Gerald Hutchins," Gerald said. He straightened his tie. It was invisible, but he felt it.

"You served me," Ironspire said. He did not sound angry. He sounded bored.

"I cited you," Gerald said.

"You cited me for a wall you touched," Ironspire said. "That wall is mine. You invaded my territory. You are the one in violation."

"The wall is uninspected," Gerald said.

"Private property," Ironspire said.

"The System applies code to all structures," Gerald said. "Even private ones. Especially private ones. Because you don't care about public safety."

Ironspire leaned forward.

"Prove it. Show me the violation."

Gerald pulled out the telemetry. The hum. The lack of inspection.

Ironspire waved a hand. The data vanished.

"I have an exemption," Ironspire said. "Grandfather Clause. The wall was built before the new code."

Gerald froze, and he knew this. Every inspector knew this. The Grandfather Clause.

If a structure existed before the new regulations, it did not have to meet the new regulations. Unless it was modified.

Gerald looked at the telemetry again.

He looked at the time of the kick, and the kick had caused a crack.

A crack was a modification.

A modification triggered a mandatory inspection.

If you modify a grandfathered structure, you must bring it up to current code.

Gerald's heart hammered against his ribs.

"You modified the wall," Gerald said.

"I did not," Ironspire said. "Your agent hit it."

"My agent did not touch the wall," Gerald said. "He kicked the floor."

"Same thing," Ironspire said. "Vibration transfer."

"Vibration transfer is not structural modification," Gerald said. "Structural modification requires material change. You have no material change."

Ironspire smiled. It was a metallic, grinding sound.

"You are losing, Inspector. The clause protects me."

Gerald looked at the System. The timer was at ten seconds.

He had one second left, and he needed a material change.

He needed to prove the wall had been modified.

He looked at the Intruder's torch. The green flame.

That flame was not normal fire. It was alchemical. It burned hot.

Did it burn the wall?

Gerald zoomed in on the wall's temperature log.

**TEMP: 40 DEGREES.** **TEMP: 41 DEGREES.**

No.

Gerald looked at the floor. The crack.

The crack went through the floor. It did not go through the wall.

Gerald was stuck, and he was going to lose.

50% of his mana. Deletion of his spawn.

He would be a single rat in a dark basement.

And then he would be deleted.

Gerald looked at Ironspire.

"Wait," Gerald said.

"I am ready to rule," Ironspire said.

"One more piece of evidence."

Ironspire sighed.

"Make it quick."

Gerald pulled up the floor plans. The original library plans. From 1994.

He zoomed in on the basement.

There was a note in the margin. Handwritten. Scanned by the System.

*Note: Do not install shelving in Sector D. Soil instability detected. Requires geotechnical survey.*

Gerald pointed at the note.

"Look at the date," Gerald said.

"It says 1994," Ironspire said.

"Soil instability," Gerald said. "That means the wall was not load-bearing. It was a retaining wall. Retaining walls are not subject to the same Grandfather Clause as load-bearing columns if they are found to be defective."

Ironspire's faceplate flickered.

"Defective?"

"Yes," Gerald said. "The soil is unstable. The wall is leaning. Look at the angle."

Gerald pulled up the laser scan from his inspection last week, and the wall was leaning at 3 degrees.

The code allowed 1 degree.

3 degrees was a violation.

And if the wall was defective, it was not grandfathered. It was unsafe.

And if it was unsafe, it had to be remediated.

And if it had to be remediated, and Ironspire had not remediated it, he was in violation.

Ironspire stared at the number.

3 degrees, and he checked his own logs.

He had no log.

He had never checked it.

Gerald watched Ironspire's face. The metal plates shifted. The shield face lowered.

"You missed that," Gerald said.

Ironspire did not speak.

The System flashed.

**HEARING CONCLUDED.** **RULING: DEFENDANT IN VIOLATION.** **PENALTY: CORE-992 FINED 10,000 MANA. STRUCTURAL REMEDIATION ORDER ISSUED.** **CITATION VALIDATED.**

The white void dissolved.

Gerald was back in the basement, and the intruders were gone.

The wall remained. But it was glowing. The System was repairing it.

Gerald slumped against his core, and he had won.

But then, a new window opened.

**SYSTEM ALERT: COUNTER-CITATION RECEIVED.** **SENDER: IRONSPIRE CORE-992.** **VIOLATION: FALSE PRETENSES IN LEGAL PROCEEDINGS.** **CLAIM: INSPECTOR HUTCHINS USED OUT-OF-DATE CODES TO SECURE RULING.** **STATUS: APPEAL FILED.** **HEARING SCHEDULED: IMMEDIATE.** **PENALTY FOR LOSS: DELETION OF CORE.**

Gerald stared at the screen.

He had used 1994 codes.

But the System had updated the codes in 2023, and he had won on a technicality.

But he had used the wrong technicality.

And now Ironspire was appealing.

And this time, they weren't going to argue about the wall.

They were going to argue about the law.

And Gerald had broken it.
