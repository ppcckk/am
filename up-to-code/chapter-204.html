Chapter 204: Preliminary Inquiry

# Chapter 204

The descent began not with a step, but with a stamp.

A heavy, inked seal pressed into the parchment of the air itself. Gerald felt the vibration in his nonexistent teeth, a low hum that resonated through the lattice of his core.

The darkness below Sub-Level 9 did not recede.

It thickened. It became a substance, viscous and black, resisting the light of his awareness.

He did not look down. He looked at the clipboard.

The page was blank. That was the problem. The System did not provide a form for "Citing the Divine." There was no Form 11-C for theological infractions. There was no Schedule Z for "Unauthorized Creation of Realities."

Gerald uncapped his pen. The nib scratched against the paper, a sound loud in the silence.

He did not write a violation. He wrote a header.

**NOTICE OF PRELIMINARY INQUIRY** **Regarding: Unregistered Entity in Restricted Zone** **Case ID: 0000-0000**

The Architect watched from the void. He did not speak. He waited.

Gerald's hand moved. It was a steady hand. A municipal hand. A hand that had signed ten thousand notices of violation.

He filled in the date. He filled in the location. He filled in the description of the offense.

*Subject observed within a non-public easement. Subject fails to display identification badge. Subject occupies space designated for structural load-bearing. Subject's presence constitutes an obstruction to egress.*

It was nonsense. It was also technically accurate.

The System flickered. A new window opened in Gerald's peripheral vision. It was red. It pulsed with a warning color that usually meant fire suppression failure.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** **INVALID CITATION ATTEMPT.** **TARGET CLASS: UNDEFINED.** **CANNOT APPLY STAT DEBUFF TO NON-CORE ENTITY.**

Gerald stared at the alert. He did not panic. He adjusted his glasses, though he had no nose to hold them up. He tapped the side of his head.

"Error," he muttered. "Code error. Not a violation error."

He crossed out the header. He started a new page.

**INTERIM COMPLIANCE REVIEW** **Target: Environmental Integrity of Sub-Level 9** **Inspector: G. Hutchins**

This was better. This was within his wheelhouse.

He walked.

The floor was not solid. It was a grid of light, suspended over a drop that had no bottom.

Gerald did not look at the drop.

He looked at the grid lines.

They were uneven. The spacing was inconsistent.

*Violation 1: Uneven flooring. Trip hazard. Code Section 3.2.1: All walking surfaces shall be uniform in slope and texture to prevent accidental falls.*

He wrote it down. The System accepted it. A small green checkmark appeared.

**+1 Compliance Point (Structural)**

The Architect's mask twitched. A hairline fracture appeared on the porcelain surface.

"You cite the floor?" the Architect's voice echoed. It sounded like wind through a cracked window.

"I built this floor from the dreams of dead gods.

It is stable.

It is eternal."

"It is not level," Gerald said. He pulled a measuring tape from his pocket. The tape unspooled across the void, glowing with blue mana-light.

"See? The deviation is four millimeters per meter.

That is a three percent grade.

Too steep for wheelchair access. Too steep for fire evacuation carts."

Gerald wrote the measurement down.

*Violation 2: Non-compliant slope.*

Another green checkmark.

The Architect lowered his hand. The black smoke coiled around his ankles, agitated. "You are playing a game you do not understand, Inspector. You measure the cage, but you do not look at the animal."

"The animal does not matter," Gerald said. "The cage does. If the cage fails, the animal escapes. If the animal escapes, I get fined."

He moved forward.

The grid lines shifted under his feet. The darkness tried to pull him off the path.

Gerald did not fight the pull.

He fought the *angle*.

He adjusted his stance. He leaned into the drag, using the physics of the void to keep his center of gravity over the compliant line.

It was awkward. It was un-heroic. It looked like a man trying to walk on a moving walkway while holding a cup of coffee.

But it worked.

The System hummed. A new notification popped up. It was not red. It was gray.

**NOTICE:** **ENVIRONMENTAL AUDIT IN PROGRESS.** **TARGET: VOID SUBSTRATE.** **STATUS: NON-COMPLIANT.**

Gerald stopped. He looked at the notice. Then he looked at the Architect.

"This is new," Gerald said.

"I am testing the limits," the Architect said. "You are testing my patience."

"No," Gerald said. "I am testing the substrate."

He turned his clipboard toward the Architect.

"You said you wrote the code. You said you are the Architect. That means you are responsible for the structural integrity of the System's lower levels."

The Architect went still.

"Under the Municipal Inspection Act, Section 1, Paragraph 4," Gerald read, "the author of a structure is liable for all defects in said structure. If the structure is unsafe, the author is cited, not the inhabitant."

The mask shattered.

Not completely. Just a piece. A shard of white porcelain fell into the void and disintegrated.

Behind it, the face was not lines and angles. It was a geometric impossibility. A shape that hurt to look at. It folded in on itself, infinite and sharp.

"You invoke liability?" the entity hissed. The voice was no longer human.

It was a chorus of grinding stones.

"I am the foundation.

I am the base. You cannot cite the foundation for being the foundation."

"I can," Gerald said. "And I have."

He pointed to the glowing grid.

"Look at the spacing. Look at the slope. Look at the lack of handrails on the descent path.

You built this place without a permit. You built this place without a safety inspection.

You built this place without considering the needs of those who must traverse it."

Gerald clicked his pen.

"Violation 3: Lack of Safety Infrastructure. Code Section 5.5: All vertical descent paths must include continuous handrails on both sides."

There were no handrails.

The System reacted. This was not a small green checkmark. This was a boom.

The ground shook. The void rippled.

**CRITICAL VIOLATION DETECTED.** **LIABILITY TRANSFER INITIATED.** **TARGET: ARCHITECT.** **PENALTY: STRUCTURAL WEAKENING.**

The entity screamed. It was a sound like tearing metal.

The black smoke erupted from the Architect's form. It lashed out at Gerald. A tendril of pure negation struck the clipboard.

Gerald did not dodge. He had nowhere to dodge to.

He held the clipboard tight. The wood splintered. The paper burned.

But the citation remained visible.

The System had accepted it. The liability was transferred.

The Architect stumbled. The geometric face flickered, losing cohesion. For a second, the entity looked small. Fragile.

"Stop!" the Architect roared. "You will collapse the sector!"

"I am inspecting it!" Gerald shouted back. His voice was dry, devoid of fear, full of bureaucratic indignation. "If it collapses, it is because it was never built to code! I am documenting the failure mode!"

He stepped closer. The heat was unbearable. The air tasted of ozone and old blood.

Gerald reached into his pocket. He did not pull out a weapon. He did not pull out a spell component.

He pulled out a stamp.

A heavy, brass stamp. *APPROVED FOR DEMOLITION.*

It was a relic from his old life.

A prop. A joke. He had used it on condemned buildings in Ashford.

He had never expected to use it on a cosmic entity.

He slammed it onto the clipboard.

The ink spread. The System processed the command.

**ACTION: DEMOLITION ORDER.** **EXECUTION: PENDING INSPECTOR CONFIRMATION.**

The Architect lunged. The face expanded, filling Gerald's entire field of vision.

"I will erase you," the Architect said. "I will erase this dungeon. I will erase your memory of the code."

Gerald looked at the stamp. He looked at the entity.

He did not press the confirmation button.

Instead, he looked at the entity's base. The point where the geometric face connected to the void.

It was frayed. Loose threads of reality dangled there, vibrating.

"Violation 4," Gerald whispered. "Improper attachment of superstructure to foundation."

He pointed at the loose threads.

"You are unanchored. You are floating. That is a safety hazard. That is a trip hazard for the universe."

The Architect froze. The anger drained out of the voice, replaced by confusion.

"What?"

"You are not fixed," Gerald said. "You are a floating object in a high-traffic zone. Under Code Section 8.1, all floating entities must be secured to a load-bearing anchor."

Gerald raised his hand. He did not attack.

He reached out and grabbed one of the loose threads of reality.

It burned his hand. It felt like grabbing a live wire.

He did not let go.

"I am issuing a temporary shackle," Gerald said. "Hold still."

The System paused. The air went silent.

The Inspector General's voice echoed in Gerald's skull. It was cold. It was bored.

**INTERVENTION REQUIRED.** **INSPECTOR HUTCHINS EXCEEDS AUTHORITY.** **HALT ACTION.**

Gerald ignored the voice. He pulled the thread.

The Architect screamed. The geometry of the face twisted. The entity was being pulled down, anchored by Gerald's grip.

It was not combat. It was manual labor. It was pulling a stuck door shut.

The System watched. The rules did not forbid an inspector from touching a hazard. They only forbade him from ignoring it.

The Architect was forced down. The void stabilized. The floor became solid.

Gerald released the thread. He stepped back. He wiped soot from his glasses.

The Architect hung there, suspended by the thread Gerald had tied. The entity was smaller now. Diminished. The infinite depth was gone, replaced by a cramped, confused shape.

"You have violated the sanctity of the core," the Architect said. The voice was weak.

"I have corrected a safety hazard," Gerald said. He picked up his clipboard. The page was scorched, but legible.

He turned away from the entity. He did not gloat. He did not celebrate.

He checked his watch.

It was time for his break.

He turned to go back up the shaft.

But the floor did not hold him.

The ground beneath his feet vanished. The grid lines snapped. The compliant slope was gone.

Gerald fell.

Not down. Sideways.

The void had shifted. The anchor Gerald had created was not holding the Architect. It was holding the *sector*.

And the sector was tilting.

**ALERT:** **SECTOR INTEGRITY COMPROMISED.** **GRAVITATIONAL ANOMALY DETECTED.** **EVACUATION PROTOCOL: FAILED.**

Gerald grabbed a support beam. It crumbled in his hands.

He looked up. The exit was closing. The light was dying.

The Architect laughed. It was a dry, rattling sound.

"You anchored the wrong thing, Inspector," the Architect said. "You anchored me. But I am not the foundation. I am the *door*."

The door opened.

Not out of the void. In.

A light poured through. It was not fire. It was not mana.

It was white. Pure, absolute white.

And from that white light, a hand reached out.

It was not a monster's hand. It was not a goblin's hand.

It was a hand wearing a glove. A white cotton glove.

The hand held a clipboard.

Gerald stared. His heart, which did not beat, felt a phantom thud against his ribs.

The hand handed a paper to the Architect.

The Architect took it. He read it.

The Architect's face changed. The rage vanished. The confusion vanished.

He looked pale.

He looked terrified.

The entity looked up at Gerald. Its eyes were wide.

"You did not cite the System," the Architect whispered. "You cited the *Auditor*."

The white hand reached toward Gerald.

It did not strike him.

It tapped him on the forehead.

**NOTICE OF APPEAL ACCEPTED.** **TARGET: G. HUTCHINS.** **REASON: PROCEDURAL IRREGULARITY IN CITATION 4.** **CONSEQUENCE: SUSPENSION OF INSPECTOR LICENSE.** **STATUS: PROBATIONARY.**

Gerald's vision blurred. The void rushed up to meet him.

He tried to raise his clipboard to shield his face.

The clipboard was gone.

In its place was a single sheet of paper.

It was a termination letter.

Gerald Hutchins, Municipal Building Inspector, Grade 4, was being fired.

Not by the city.

By the System.

The white light swallowed the darkness. The darkness swallowed the Architect. And Gerald was left floating in the white, with no dungeon, no core, and no rights.

A voice spoke in the white. It was smooth. It was pleasant. It was the voice of a customer service representative.

"Thank you for your cooperation, Mr. Hutchins. Your compliance rating has been reset to zero. You have thirty seconds to appeal or be deleted."

Gerald looked around. The white was empty. There was no wall to measure. There was no door to check.

There was only the timer.

29.

28.

Gerald opened his mouth. He had no voice. He had no lungs.

But he had a memory.

He remembered the rule. The one rule he had missed.

Section 1, Paragraph 9.

*Appeals may be filed by any citizen of good standing within the jurisdiction of the appealing party.*

Gerald was not a citizen. He was a core.

But he was also a building.

And buildings had addresses.

He screamed, not with sound, but with intent. He pushed his awareness toward the address.

*4000 Square Feet. Sub-Basement. Ashford Municipal Library.*

The timer hit 10.

The white light intensified.

Gerald felt his edges dissolving. The boundaries of his core were breaking down. He was becoming data. He was becoming noise.

He held onto the address. He held onto the concept of *place*.

The timer hit 3.

2.

1.

The white light stopped.

The voice spoke again.

"Appeal received. Processing jurisdictional claim."

The white faded. The darkness returned.

Gerald was back in the void.

He was on his knees.

The Architect was gone.

The clipboard was back.

But the paper on the clipboard was not the demolition order.

It was a new notice.

**HEARING SCHEDULED.** **DATE: IMMEDIATE.** **PARTIES: G. HUTCHINS VS. THE INSPECTOR GENERAL.** **TOPIC: LIABILITY OF THE SYSTEM FOR UNAUTHORIZED EVOLUTION OF AIDED ENTITIES.**

Gerald looked at the words. He looked at the blank space where the Architect had been.

He realized what he had done.

He had not just cited a god.

He had sued the System for malpractice.

And now, he had to win the lawsuit.

Or be deleted.

Gerald stood up. He straightened his tie. He clicked his pen.

He opened his mouth to speak, to argue, to cite.

But before he could make a sound, the void exploded.

Not with smoke.

With people.

Hundreds of them. Adventurers. They poured out of the cracks in reality, drawn by the disturbance. They landed on the grid, confused and frightened.

And at the front of the crowd, standing on a floating platform of bone and steel, was a woman.

She wore a crown of black iron. She held a sword made of code.

She looked at Gerald.

She smiled.

"You called the police, Inspector," she said. "Now you have to deal with the mob."

Gerald looked at the crowd. He looked at the woman. He looked at the timer, which was gone.

He did not run.

He raised his clipboard.

"Violations," he said. "So many violations."

The woman raised her sword.

"Try to cite us," she said.

Gerald looked at the crowd. He looked at the woman.

He opened his pen.

"I have already begun."
