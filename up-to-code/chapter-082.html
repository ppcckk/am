Chapter 82: Pre-Registration Precedent

# Chapter 82

Gerald had been inside his own dungeon for three hours and was losing the argument with himself.

The foundational corridor beneath the library's east wing was older than everything else in the structure. He could tell by the masonry.

Not the style, not the material, but the tolerances, and the joints were laid at a 3/16-inch gap, which meant the original code had specified a 3/16-inch gap. Current code required 1/8-inch. The difference was small.

The difference was everything.

He pressed his palm against the wall and felt the System's interface flicker. Not the usual holographic overlay he'd grown accustomed to.

Something older.

Fainter. Like a watermark visible only under certain light.

*Original Code Framework: Pre-Registration Standard, Revision 7.*

Gerald pulled his hand back. He looked at the wall again. Nothing visible to the naked eye. But the System had told him, and the System did not lie.

Revision 7. He'd never heard of it.

The current framework was Revision 14, which was seven generations of code evolution, which meant his dungeon predated the current System by a significant margin.

The physical impossibility argument wasn't just valid. It was the only honest description of what was happening.

He pulled out his phone. The Variance Request form was already drafted. He'd filled it out six times, each time adding more detail to the "basis for request" field.

The current version read: *The subject structure was constructed under Pre-Registration Standard, Revision 7, which is not the current governing code. Compliance measurement against Revision 14 constitutes a category error.*

He needed to attach evidence. He needed a Certified Code Analysis. The System's form required one, and he'd spent the last three hours trying to figure out how to generate one.

The answer was the wall. The wall was the evidence. He just had to document it properly.

Gerald opened the System's inspection interface, which was designed for Cores to monitor their own structures, not to perform forensic analysis. But Gerald had been performing forensic analysis since 1993. He knew how to write a code analysis report.

He knew what fields needed to be filled.

He knew the language.

He started typing.

*Subject: Structural element, east corridor foundation, masonry joint specification.* *Observation: Joint width measures 3/16 inch, consistent with Pre-Registration Standard, Revision 7, Section 4.2.1.* *Comparison: Current governing code, Revision 14, Section 4.2.1, specifies 1/8 inch maximum.* *Conclusion: The subject structure was not built to Revision 14 standards. Measurement against Revision 14 is invalid.*

He repeated the process for four more elements.

A load-bearing column with an older steel profile. A drainage channel sized for a different flow-rate calculation, and a ceiling height that matched Revision 7's minimum but violated Revision 14's minimum.

Each one a data point. Each one proof.

By the fifth element, the System had started responding. Not with the usual notification chime. With something lower. A hum that Gerald felt in his teeth.

*Structural Forensic Analysis recognized. Certified Code Analysis generated. Filed under Case Number 7741-C.*

Gerald stared at the notification. He hadn't filed anything. The System had generated the analysis from his observations and filed it automatically.

That shouldn't happen. Certified Code Analyses required a licensed inspector. Gerald wasn't a licensed inspector anymore. He was a dungeon Core.

But the System had accepted it, and the System had looked at what he was doing, recognized the methodology, and stamped it.

He thought about that for exactly two seconds, and then he attached the analysis to his Variance Request and hit submit.

The response was immediate.

*Variance Request 7741-C filed. Pending review. Estimated processing time: 48 hours.*

Forty-eight hours.

The IG would arrive in forty-eight hours.

The variance would be processed in forty-eight hours. It was exactly enough time.

Exactly.

Gerald allowed himself one breath. Then he went back to the wall.

More elements remained to be documented. The more evidence he had, the harder it would be for the IG to reject the variance.

He'd been writing code analysis reports his entire career, and he knew that the strength of a variance argument wasn't in the argument itself. It was in the volume of supporting documentation. A data point was an anomaly.

A dozen data points was a pattern.

A hundred was an irrefutable fact.

He moved to the west corridor.

The masonry here was different. Newer. He checked the joints. 1/8 inch.

Current code. This section had been built or renovated after the current System took over.

That was fine.

It just meant his dungeon was a hybrid.

Old bones, new skin. The variance would apply only to the original structure.

Which raised a question. If only part of his dungeon was governed by Revision 7, his Compliance Rating would be a blend. The old sections would be exempt.

The new sections would be measured normally. He'd need to calculate the proportional adjustment.

He pulled out his phone and started doing the math, and the east wing was roughly 40% of his total footprint. If the old sections were exempt, his effective Rating would be based only on the new 60%. His current Rating was 12.

If he removed the exempt sections, the Rating would rise.

Maybe to 30. Maybe higher, depending on how the new sections scored.

It wasn't enough. 30 was still below the demolition threshold.

Unless he could get more sections exempted.

Gerald looked at the ceiling.

The library above him had been renovated in 2007. The sub-basement had not. But the ceiling was shared.

If the ceiling was original to the sub-basement, it fell under Revision 7. If it had been replaced during the 2007 renovation, it fell under Revision 14.

He pressed his palm to the ceiling and felt for the watermark.

Nothing. No faint interface. No older code signature.

The ceiling had been replaced, and he tried the floor. Nothing. Replaced.

The east wall. The watermark was there. Faint, but there.

He tried the north wall. Nothing.

South wall. Nothing.

Only the east corridor. Only the foundation. Maybe 15% of his total structure.

Gerald did the math again. Fifteen percent exempt. Effective Rating: maybe 18. Still not enough.

He needed more. He needed to find something else that was original. Something that the System hadn't replaced when it inherited his structure.

He walked the perimeter of his dungeon, pressing his palm to every surface.

Floor, wall, ceiling, column, beam, and he did it methodically. As he had done for thirty-one years.

Surface by surface. Room by room. As a building inspector should.

In the southeast corner, behind the storage alcove where he kept his spare handrails, he found a support column. The watermark was there. Strong. Clear.

*Pre-Registration Standard, Revision 7, Section 9.1: Load-bearing support, minimum cross-section 14 inches.*

The column was 14 inches square. Current code required 16 inches.

So by current standards, the column was undersized.

A violation. But by Revision 7, it was compliant.

Gerald documented it.

Then he found another column in the northeast corner. Same watermark.

Same compliance.

Then a third.

Four columns. Four pieces of original structure. Each one a data point.

He was halfway through documenting the fourth when the System notification came. Not the usual chime. Not the hum. A sharp, percussive sound, like a stamp hitting paper.

*Counter-Complaint filed against Core 7741. Filed by Core 4419 (Thornwall). Basis: Administrative abuse under Section 12.7. Request: Immediate suspension of all active Citations issued by Core 7741 pending review of Core 7741's authority to issue Citations.*

Gerald read it twice.

Thornwall. The dungeon he'd cited in Chapter 34. The one that had lost three elite spawns to his fire-code Citation. The one that had appealed and lost.

Thornwall was filing a counter-complaint. Arguing that Gerald had no authority to issue Citations. That his Citations were themselves violations.

The System's language was precise. *Administrative abuse under Section 12.7.* Gerald knew Section 12.7. It covered regulatory overreach. It covered situations where an inspector issued Citations outside their jurisdiction or authority.

Thornwall was arguing that Gerald, as a Core, was not a licensed inspector. That his Citations were unauthorized. That every single one of them was void.

If the IG agreed, every Citation Gerald had ever issued would be reversed. Every debuff he'd placed on a rival dungeon would be lifted. Every dungeon he'd weakened would return to full strength.

Including the ones that had lost spawns to his Citations. Including the ones that had sworn revenge.

Gerald looked at the timestamp on the counter-complaint. Filed forty minutes ago.

While he was in the east corridor documenting masonry joints.

Thornwall had been watching.

Waiting. And had filed the complaint with the IG directly, bypassing Gerald entirely.

The IG would arrive in less than forty-eight hours. It would find Gerald's Variance Request pending. It would find Thornwall's counter-complaint pending.

Two cases. Same inspector.

Same timeframe.

And here was the problem. Gerald's Variance Request argued that his dungeon was governed by Revision 7. Revision 7 did not include Citation authority. The power to issue Citations had been added in Revision 9.

If Gerald won his Variance Request, he proved his dungeon was governed by Revision 7. And if his dungeon was governed by Revision 7, he had no authority to issue Citations. And Thornwall's counter-complaint would succeed.

Winning one case meant losing the other.

Gerald stood in the southeast corner, his hand still pressed against the column, and felt the trap close around him. The System had built this. Not the current System.

The original one.

Revision 7. The code that had created him.

It had given him the forensic methodology, which had recognized his analysis. It had filed his Variance Request automatically.

And it had left the Citation authority in Revision 9. A later revision. One he could not claim.

He pulled his hand off the column. He looked at his phone.

The Variance Request was still pending, and he could withdraw it.

If he withdrew the Variance Request, the IG would measure him against Revision 14. His Rating would stay at 12.

The demolition order would proceed.

If he kept it, his Rating might rise. But his Citations would fall.

Gerald sat down on the floor. He put his phone on his knee. He looked at the ceiling, at the replaced ceiling, the current-code ceiling, the one that had no watermark, no older signature, no escape.

He thought about the Ashford Street water tower. He thought about the 2019 variance. He thought about the restaurant owner who had argued physical impossibility and won.

The restaurant owner had not also been the health inspector.

Gerald opened a new document. He started typing.

*Supplemental Filing to Variance Request 7741-C. Basis: Request for partial variance application.

Subject structure's original elements are governed by Revision 7 for purposes of Compliance Rating measurement.

Subject structure's regulatory authority is governed by Revision 9 for purposes of Citation issuance. These are not contradictory. A structure can be measured under one code and regulated under another.

The measurement code establishes what the structure is.

The regulatory code establishes what the structure may do.*

He stared at the words. They were either brilliant or insane. He couldn't tell which.

He attached the supplemental filing to the Variance Request. He hit submit.

*Supplemental filing received. Case 7741-C updated. Pending review.*

The IG would have to decide. Could a Core be measured under one code and regulated under another? Could Gerald's dungeon be old enough to be exempt from current compliance standards but new enough to have Citation authority?

It was a question the System had never been asked before.

Gerald knew that. He'd checked the precedent database. There was nothing like it.

No Core had ever filed a dual-framework variance.

He'd never filed one either. Not in thirty-one years. Not in a inspection. Not once.

But he was writing the argument now. And the argument was either the finest piece of regulatory reasoning he'd ever produced, or it was the thing that would get him demolished.

His phone buzzed. A new notification.

*Inspector General confirmed. Arrival time: 14 hours. Case 7741-C and Case 4419-R scheduled for joint hearing.*

Fourteen hours. Not forty-eight. The IG had moved it up.

Gerald looked at his supplemental filing. He looked at the four columns in his southeast corner. He looked at the east corridor with its 3/16-inch joints.

He had fourteen hours to decide whether the argument he'd just made would save him or end him. And he had no way to know. No precedent. No precedent at all.

His phone buzzed again.

*Pre-hearing brief requested. Core 7741 must submit oral argument summary within 12 hours. Failure to submit will result in automatic default.*

Gerald put the phone down. He picked it back up. He typed the summary in eleven words and sent it.
