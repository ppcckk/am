Chapter 141: Measure Twice

# Chapter 141

The glass pulsed.

It wasn't a heartbeat. It was a rhythm, mechanical and wet, spreading through the foundation of the Ashford Municipal Library like a fungal infection. The janitor, Dale, scrambled backward, his mop bucket clattering across the linoleum. He pointed a shaking finger at the translucent tendrils creeping up the staircase railing.

"Stop it," Dale whispered. "Please. Just stop it."

Gerald Hutchins tried to move. He couldn't.

The containment field held his core tight against the sub-basement floor.

But he could still speak. The System allowed it. It wanted a performance.

"Mr.

Dale," Gerald's voice echoed through the vents. It sounded flat.

Administrative.

"Step away from the railing. That is a non-compliant egress path under current conditions."

The Inspector General adjusted his cuffs. His grey suit was immaculate, devoid of dust or sweat, despite standing in a dungeon that smelled of ozone and rot.

He held a clipboard.

Not a magical artifact.

A standard municipal clipboard with a metal clip and a stack of paper forms.

"Silence, citizen," the IG said. His voice didn't echo. It cut through the air like a scalpel.

"You are currently under audit. You do not have the standing to issue directives to non-core personnel."

"I have standing," Gerald said. "I am the Core."

"You are a violation," the IG corrected.

He tapped his pen against the clipboard. *Click. Click. Click.* "Section 9.

Unsanctioned Existence.

You expanded your footprint without a permit. You generated biomass without an environmental impact statement.

And now you are leaking Class-4 containment material into public infrastructure."

The IG stepped forward. He didn't look at the glass. He looked at Gerald's core, a swirling vortex of blue light trapped in the concrete.

"Where is the paperwork, Gerald?"

The question hung in the air. The glass pulses slowed. The janitor froze.

Gerald felt a cold sweat break out on his phantom skin. He had prepared for combat. He had prepared for the Hearing.

He had prepared to argue structural logic. He had not prepared for this.

"What paperwork?" Gerald asked.

"Form 11-B.

Unlicensed Biological Entity." The IG flipped a page.

"We issued that citation three hundred chapters ago. Or, in your case, thirty-one years ago.

The System has been waiting for you to fill it out."

"I don't have a form," Gerald said.

"You have a mind," the IG said. "The System is a bureaucracy, Gerald.

Bureaucracies require forms.

If you cannot produce the paperwork, the violation stands. And if the violation stands, the audit concludes. Conclusion means termination."

The glass surged upward. It hit the first step of the stairs. Dale screamed again, louder this time.

"Mr. Dale, stay calm," Gerald said. He focused on the IG. "What form do you require?"

The IG smiled. That thin, cruel smile again.

"Form 44-C," he said.

"Notice of Intent to Remediate Containment Breach. Signed by two witnesses.

One witness must be a sentient entity with a Credit Score above 500.

The other must be a municipal employee with a valid union card."

Gerald looked at the janitor. Dale was crying. He was holding his mop like a weapon.

"Dale," Gerald said. "Listen to me."

Dale looked up, eyes wide. "I don't want to touch it. It's moving."

"You are not touching it," Gerald said. "You are witnessing a legal proceeding. Do you have your union card?"

Dale blinked. He reached into his pocket, his hands shaking so hard his fingers slipped.

He pulled out a laminated card.

"I...

I have it."

"Good." Gerald turned his attention back to the IG. "I need a second witness. A sentient entity with a credit score."

The IG laughed. It was a dry, papery sound. "There are no sentient entities in your dungeon.

You have one rat. The rat has no credit score.

The rat is property."

"The rat is a spawn," Gerald said. "And I have a neighbor."

The IG raised an eyebrow. "The Goblin Core? He is currently cited for unauthorized noise pollution.

He is in a Hearing.

He cannot witness."

"I don't mean the Goblin," Gerald said. "I mean the party that came in an hour ago."

The IG paused.

He looked at the ceiling. "The Adventurer Party? They fled.

They triggered your defense mechanisms."

"They didn't flee," Gerald said.

"They retreated to the lobby.

I can feel them. They are arguing about loot distribution.

They are still within range."

"Range is irrelevant," the IG said. "The witness must be present."

"I can bring them in," Gerald said.

The IG checked his watch. A pocket watch, silver, ticking with unnatural precision.

"You have four minutes," the IG said. "The glass will breach the library floor in four minutes.

If you do not have two signed witnesses, the audit concludes.

You will be deleted. Your dungeon will be rezoned for commercial use."

"Four minutes," Gerald said.

He tried to move. The glass held him.

He pushed against the psychic weight.

It felt like trying to lift a car with his mind. He couldn't move his core, but he could move the dungeon.

He opened the lobby doors.

He unlocked the front entrance.

He deactivated the security wards.

"Dale," Gerald said. "Go to the lobby. Tell them the Core is ready to negotiate."

Dale looked at the glass. It was halfway up the stairs. He looked at Gerald's core. He looked at the IG.

"They hate you," Dale said. "You put spikes in the hallway."

"I put handrails in the hallway," Gerald corrected. "The spikes were a misunderstanding. Go."

Dale ran. His footsteps echoed up the stairs, faster than the creeping glass.

Gerald turned to the IG. "While he gets them, we discuss the violation."

The IG sat down on a crate. He pulled a pen from his pocket. "Proceed."

Gerald took a breath. He thought about the last thirty-one years.

The code books.

The zoning laws.

The endless, grinding machinery of municipal approval.

"The glass is not a containment breach," Gerald said. "It is a structural reinforcement."

The IG stopped writing. He looked up. "Explain."

"Section 402, Subsection D," Gerald said. "Mandatory Seismic Bracing. My dungeon is in a flood zone.

The library is in a flood zone. When the water table rises, as it does every spring in Ashford, the foundation shifts.

The glass is not an infection.

It is a polymer-based anchor system, self-repairing and chemically inert."

The IG tilted his head. "Chemically inert?"

"Mostly," Gerald said. "The System interprets rapid organic growth as a threat because it lacks context. I filed a permit for 'Structural Anchoring' in my head, but I never submitted it because I was a human inspector, not a Core. I didn't think I needed to file a permit for my own body."

The IG tapped his pen. "You didn't think you needed to file a permit for your own body."

"No," Gerald said. "I thought I was the inspector. I didn't think I was the building."

The IG nodded slowly. "A common oversight. New Cores often struggle with the distinction between the operator and the infrastructure. But you are claiming this is a permit issue."

"I am claiming it is a filing error," Gerald said.

"Not a violation of Section 9. Section 9 requires *malice* or *negligence*.

Negligence requires a duty of care.

Did I have a duty to file a permit for my own skeletal structure before I was registered as a Core?"

The IG frowned. He looked at his clipboard. He flipped pages. He found the one he was looking for.

"Rule 12-A," the IG read aloud. "Cores are responsible for all structural modifications from the moment of Registration."

"Gerald Hutchins was a municipal employee before Registration," Gerald said. "Under City Ordinance 44, employees are exempt from self-certification for personal residence modifications if the modifications are for safety compliance."

The IG stared at him. The silence stretched. The glass pulse grew louder, a wet *thrum-thrum-thrum*.

"That is a stretch," the IG said.

"It is a loophole," Gerald said. "And I have the receipt."

He projected a memory. Not a magical image, but a System log. A timestamp.

*09:00 AM, Tuesday. Gerald Hutchins inspects Library Sub-Basement.

Notes: Foundation shift detected.

Recommendation: Immediate bracing required.

Filed internally. No permit issued due to emergency safety exemption.*

The IG looked at the log. His eyes narrowed.

"That log exists," the IG said.

"It does," Gerald said.

"It is incomplete," the IG said. "It lacks a signature."

"I was the inspector," Gerald said. "I signed it. I just didn't sign it *as a Core*.

Because I wasn't one yet. I was a man worried about a crack in the wall."

The IG stood up. He walked toward the glass. He placed a hand on it. The glass hissed, reacting to the System Authority.

"You are arguing semantics, Gerald."

"I am arguing code," Gerald said.

"And you are the Inspector General. You do not break code. You enforce it.

If you delete me for a filing error in a pre-Registration safety check, you are enforcing an ex-post facto law.

That is a violation of the System Charter."

The IG's hand tightened on the glass. His knuckles whitened.

"Be careful," the IG said. "The Charter is not a suicide pact."

"It is a contract," Gerald said. "And I am holding you to it."

Dale burst back into the basement.

Behind him came three figures. They were covered in dust and minor burns. The leader was a large man with a shield.

The second was a woman with a staff. The third was a rogue, checking his daggers.

They stopped at the edge of the light.

"Who are you?" the man in armor asked. He looked at the IG. He looked at Gerald's core. He looked at the glass, which was now halfway up the stairs.

"I am the Inspector General," the IG said. He didn't turn around.

The adventurer party exchanged glances.

The rogue stepped forward.

"The IG? Here?

For a dungeon core?"

"For a citizen," the IG said.

The woman with the staff lowered her weapon. "Is the dungeon under attack?"

"No," Gerald said. "It is under audit."

The rogue laughed. "An audit? In a dungeon?"

"It is the highest form of combat," Gerald said. "The rogue. What is your name?"

"Brix," the rogue said.

"Brix," Gerald said. "You are a sentient entity. Do you have a credit score?"

Brix blinked. "I... I have a guild membership. That counts as a score, right?"

"It counts," Gerald said. "Will you witness a legal signing?"

Brix looked at the IG. He looked at the glass. He looked at Gerald. "Why should I?"

"Because if I am deleted, the glass spreads," Gerald said. "And if the glass spreads, the library collapses.

If the library collapses, the local economy takes a hit.

If the economy takes a hit, your quest rewards drop. Do you want to lose fifty percent of your loot table?"

Brix looked at the woman. She shrugged.

"I guess we help him," she said.

Brix walked forward. He pulled a scroll from his belt. "I need a signature."

Gerald projected the Form 44-C. It appeared in the air, a glowing document.

"Sign it," Gerald said. "Witness for Gerald Hutchins, Core ID 4992."

Brix signed. His name appeared in blue ink.

"Dale," Gerald said. "Sign it."

Dale stepped forward, still holding his mop. He signed with a shaking hand.

The IG looked at the two signatures. He looked at the timestamp. He looked at Gerald.

"Two witnesses," the IG said. "Credit scores verified. Union status verified."

"The violation is cured," Gerald said. "The glass is not an infection. It is a safety measure. The audit is invalid."

The IG stared at him. For a long moment, the only sound was the dripping of the water leak and the distant hum of the city above.

Then, the IG smiled. It wasn't cruel this time. It was impressed.

"Valid," the IG said. He stamped the clipboard. *Approved.*

The glass stopped.

It didn't retreat.

It remained. But the pulsing stopped.

It became solid.

Transparent. Stable.

The adventurers sighed in relief. Dale slumped against the wall.

Gerald felt the weight lift from his core. He could breathe. He could think.

"However," the IG said. He turned to face Gerald fully. "The audit is not closed."

Gerald's heart sank. "What?"

"The audit is not closed," the IG repeated. "The violation is cured. The citation is withdrawn. But the *investigation* continues."

The IG walked over to the glass wall. He ran his finger along the edge.

"You said this is a safety measure," the IG said. "A seismic brace."

"Yes," Gerald said.

"But look at the structure," the IG said. He pointed to the intricate lattice of the glass.

"It is not just bracing the foundation.

It is interlocking with the library's plumbing.

It is wrapping around the electrical mains. It is *controlling* the building's systems."

Gerald felt a cold dread. He hadn't noticed that. He had been so focused on survival, on the paperwork, on the IG's logic traps, that he hadn't looked at the *result*.

"I didn't design it that way," Gerald said.

"The System designs it that way," the IG said. "When you cited the neighboring goblin dungeon for noise, the System optimized your defenses. It used the available resources.

The glass. It used what was there.

But now, Gerald, you are not just protecting your dungeon.

You are seizing control of municipal infrastructure."

The IG turned to the adventurers. "Do you know what this is?"

The adventurers looked at the glass. It was shimmering, pulsing with faint light.

"It's cool," Brix said.

"It is illegal," the IG said. "You are now a Municipal Utility Provider. You are holding the library's power and water hostage. That is a Class-1 Felony under System Law."

Gerald looked at the glass. He looked at the IG. He looked at his own core.

"I can disconnect it," Gerald said.

"You cannot," the IG said. "It is load-bearing. If you remove it, the library collapses. If you leave it, you are in violation of Code 1001: Unlicensed Infrastructure Control."

The IG held out his hand. He produced a new form.

"Form 77-A," the IG said. "Application for Municipal Utility Integration."

Gerald stared at the form. It was complex. Hundreds of pages. It required technical schematics, environmental impact reports, and a bond payment of ten million gold coins.

"I don't have ten million gold," Gerald said.

"Then you are in default," the IG said. "And for default, the penalty is not deletion."

The IG pointed to the ceiling. To the library above. To the city beyond.

"The penalty is *Absorption*," the IG said.

"The System will integrate you. You will not be a Core.

You will be a building.

You will lose your consciousness. You will become a smart-building with no mind. Just code."

Gerald looked at the adventurers. They were backing away.

They knew what that meant.

A dungeon core with a mind was a quest.

A dungeon core without a mind was just a hazard.

"Give me a week," Gerald said.

"No," the IG said. "You have until the next inspection cycle. Which is now."

The IG snapped his fingers.

The glass surged. Not outward. *Inward*.

It wrapped around Gerald's core. Tight. Hot.

"I am initiating the integration," the IG said. "If you can prove you can manage the library's grid in the next five minutes, you keep your mind. If you fail, you become the building."

Gerald's vision blurred. He felt the library above him. The lights.

The heating. The water pressure.

It was all connected to him now.

It was too much. It was noise. It was chaos.

"Dale," Gerald said. His voice was straining. "Dale, get the master breaker box."

Dale nodded. He ran for the door.

"Gerald," Brix said. "How do we help?"

"Tell me," Gerald said, his mind splitting, trying to hold the library together while the glass crushed him. "Tell me what the lights are doing."

"They're flickering," the woman said.

"They are shorting," Gerald said. "Find the breaker. Flip it. Do not let them short."

The IG watched. He held up a stopwatch.

"Three minutes," the IG said.

Gerald closed his eyes.

He felt the code. The building code.

The wiring diagrams.

The load limits.

He had spent thirty-one years learning these rules.

He had spent one hundred and forty chapters surviving them.

He had to use them now.

He reached out with his mind, not to fight, but to *inspect*.

He found the fault. A loose connection in the second-floor fuse box.

"Dale!" Gerald screamed. "Second floor! North corridor!"

Dale was running up the stairs.

"Two minutes," the IG said.

The glass tightened. Gerald felt his thoughts slowing. The edges of his consciousness were fraying.

If he failed, he would be nothing. A structure. A thing.

But he was a builder.

And he knew the code.

He pushed.

The glass pulsed.

The lights in the library above flickered once, then steadied.

The IG stopped his stopwatch.

He looked at Gerald.

He looked at the steady lights.

He looked at the form in his hand.

"Insufficient data," the IG said.

He tore the form in half.

"Insufficient data means automatic rejection," the IG said. "You have failed the integration test."

The glass exploded.

Not outward.

It shattered into a million shards of light, and then reformed.

It formed a cage.

Around Gerald.

"I am sentencing you," the IG said. "To Probation."

Gerald looked up. The cage was tight. The IG was smiling.

"Probation," the IG said. "You will be assigned a Supervisor. And your Supervisor..."

The IG pointed to Brix. The rogue.

Brix looked confused. He pointed to himself. "Me?"

The IG nodded.

"Your Supervisor will be the adventurer party," the IG said. "They will report to me weekly. If they report that you are deviating from code, you will be deleted."

Gerald stared at the rogue. The rogue stared at Gerald.

"I don't know what I'm doing," Brix said.

"Neither did I," Gerald said.

The IG turned and walked up the stairs. He didn't look back.

"Good luck, Core," the IG called out. "Don't miss a deadline."

The door slammed.

Silence.

The glass cage hummed.

Brix walked up to the bars. He looked through them at Gerald.

"So," Brix said. "What's the first rule?"

Gerald looked at the library above. The lights were steady.

The water was running.

The building was his.

And his life was in the hands of a man who used a dagger to open cans of beans.

Gerald closed his eyes.

"Rule one," Gerald said. "Measure twice."

Brix shrugged. He pulled out a rope.

"I'll start with that," Brix said.

He tied a knot in the air.

It was a bad knot.

Gerald watched it unravel.

The IG was gone.

The audit was over.

The real work had just begun.
