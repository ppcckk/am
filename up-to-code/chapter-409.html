Chapter 409: The Inspector General

# Chapter 409

The beacon's light died before it reached the ceiling.

Kaelen watched the blue glow fade, leaving only the dripping dark. Her fingers trembled around the cold metal of the emergency flare.

She tried to activate her mana reserves.

The air remained still. No hum. No resistance.

The magic had been severed at the root.

Below, the water rose again.

A wet, slapping sound echoed from the drain. Then another. A rhythmic, squelching noise that suggested heavy limbs dragging through viscous fluid.

Kaelen pressed her back against the damp cavern wall. She counted her steps.

Twenty to the tunnel entrance.

Thirty to the ladder. Fifty to the surface. Her legs felt like lead.

The lack of mana sickness was immediate and debilitating. She was physically strong, but her magical reflexes were gone.

If something grabbed her now, she had only muscle and bone to defend herself.

The shape in the darkness moved, which was not human. The limbs were too long, the joints bending at impossible angles. The head was low, sweeping the water with a snout that smelled of rot and copper.

Vorax.

The parasite had taken the form of the host's deepest fear, or perhaps the host's most functional tool. Gerald had been an inspector. Vorax wore the shape of something that inspected things.

It stopped ten feet away. The blue interface light was gone. Gerald was blind down there.

Kaelen opened her mouth to scream, but no sound came out. The silence in the cavern was absolute. It was a vacuum of noise. The System had muted the world.

Then, a crack.

Not in the rock. In the air.

A jagged line of white text appeared in Kaelen's vision, burning against the darkness. It was not a spell. It was not a skill. It was a notification.

**[SYSTEM OFFLINE. LOCAL ENFORCEMENT PROTOCOLS ACTIVE.]** **[UNAUTHORIZED STRUCTURAL ALTERATION DETECTED.]** **[SOURCE: SUB-BASEMENT 7.]** **[STATUS: CRITICAL.]**

The text hovered for a second, then dissolved into dust.

Vorax paused. The creature tilted its head. It seemed to be listening to something Kaelen could not hear.

A second line appeared in the air, lower this time. It was not glowing blue. It was the dull, bureaucratic gray of a municipal warning label.

**[VIOLATION: UNLICENSED DEMOLITION.]** **[PERMIT NUMBER: NULL.]** **[PENALTY: IMMINENT.]**

The creature hissed. It looked up at Kaelen. Its eyes were black pits, void of any recognition. It raised a clawed hand, pointing at the ceiling where the library foundations lay.

Then, it dug.

Not into the floor. Into the air. It clawed at the space above its head, tearing invisible seams in reality.

Kaelen realized what was happening, and the System had blacked out because the foundation was failing. Gerald was not fighting a monster.

He was fighting a structural collapse. And he was losing.

Her hands went still. Fear was a luxury she could not afford.

If the library collapsed, the city above would fall into the sinkhole.

Hundreds of people. Children. Staff.

She had to go down.

Kaelen grabbed a loose piece of rebar from the rubble. It was heavy, rusted, and useless against magic. But it was a lever. She jammed it into a crack in the floor and pulled.

The rock shifted. Dust rained down.

She did not have mana. She had physics.

***

Gerald Hutchins did not see the darkness. He saw the load-bearing calculations.

They overlaid his vision in a translucent grid, even without the interface. The System was offline, but his mind was not. Thirty-one years of measuring door widths had wired his brain to perceive structural integrity as a physical sensation.

He was submerged in the black water. His core was exposed to the pressure of the surrounding rock. The cold was immense. It bit at his essence, trying to dissolve his code.

But he was not dissolving. He was anchoring.

Gerald gripped the iron pipe that Vorax had been chewing on. The metal was warm. Too warm. It was vibrating with the creature's attempts to sever the connection between the dungeon and the library.

The voice in his head was not his own. It was wet and gurgling, a mimicry of Gerald's own internal monologue.

*Cut it out. Cut it out. It is weak. It is rot.*

The voice was not an external enemy. It was a suggestion. A compliance error in the code itself. Vorax was trying to rewrite Gerald's core programming, convincing him that demolition was the only path to compliance.

Gerald ignored it. He focused on the pipe.

The wall thickness was three millimeters. Corrosion had reduced the effective strength by forty percent.

The calculation flashed in his mind, independent of the System.

**[STRESS RATIO: 1.4:1. FAILING.]**

He could not reinforce the pipe. He did not have the materials. He did not have the time.

But he had a citation.

Gerald tried to access his inventory. Nothing. He tried to open his spellbook. Empty.

He tried to issue a command.

*Cease.*

Nothing happened.

The water rushed into his core. A pain like white-hot iron flooded his senses. Gerald's essence flickered.

*He is not a Core anymore,* Gerald thought. *He is a leak.*

If he was a leak, he could not fight. He could only seal.

Gerald stopped trying to push the parasite out.

Instead, he reached out with his mind and felt the surrounding rock. The limestone.

The clay.

The sand, and he did not try to command it. He tried to audit it.

He scanned the material composition of the cavern walls. He identified the voids. The cracks. The places where the structural integrity was compromised.

There were twelve critical failures.

Gerald focused on Failure Nine. A void directly beneath Vorax's feet.

He did not attack the void. He acknowledged it.

**[NOTICE OF VIOLATION: SUBGRADE SUPPORT DEFICIENCY.]**

The words had no power. The System was off.

But the intent was clear. Gerald poured his remaining energy into the concept of *support*.

Not as a spell, but as a definition, and he defined the space as occupied.

He defined the void as a violation of occupancy load.

The water around him froze.

Not into ice. Into solid rock.

The fluidity of the leak rejected the new definition. The water pushed back, trying to reassert its state as liquid. Gerald held the definition.

*This is solid. This is load-bearing. This is up to code.*

The water resisted. It churned, violent and hot.

Gerald felt his core cracking. A hairline fracture in his essence.

If he held this definition for three more seconds, the water would turn to stone. Vorax would be trapped in stone. The leak would be sealed.

But the cost.

To change the state of matter in his own body required energy. Energy he did not have.

*Pay now,* the logic whispered. *Or pay later.*

Gerald did not hesitate. He spent it.

All of it.

The blue light in his core dimmed to a faint gray. The pain vanished. Numbness spread from his center outward.

The water solidified.

A groan echoed through the cavern. Not from Vorax. From the earth.

The stone that had been water was heavier than water. It settled. It compressed the rock around it.

The floor beneath Kaelen, three levels up, shuddered.

***

Kaelen felt the tremor through the soles of her boots.

The creature stopped clawing at the air. It looked down.

The water around it had turned gray. Stagnant. Hard.

Vorax struggled. It pulled at its leg, but the leg was fused to the stone. The parasite had become embedded in the foundation.

Kaelen saw the opening, and she did not attack the creature. She attacked the support.

She swung the rebar at a stalactite hanging above the trapped beast. Not to hit it. To break it.

The rock shattered. A shower of debris fell.

Vorax shrieked, shielding its face.

Kaelen ran, and she slid down the slope, boots slipping on the wet gravel. She reached the edge of the solidified pool.

Gerald was there.

He was not a glowing light. He was a dark, jagged shape in the stone. Unconscious.

Dormant. His core was visible, a dark sphere embedded in the rock, pulsing faintly.

Vorax was half-emerged from the stone, its claws still trying to dig free.

Kaelen raised the rebar, and she had one swing.

She aimed for the parasite's neck.

The blow connected. Stone crunched. Vorax howled, a sound like grinding teeth.

But it did not die.

The creature's other hand shot out, catching Kaelen by the throat.

She lifted off the ground. Her feet dangled. The air left her lungs.

Vorax pulled her closer. Its face was inches from hers. The black pits of its eyes reflected her terror.

*You are not the inspector,* the creature seemed to say, though it made no sound. *You are the debris.*

Kaelen kicked. Her boot caught the creature's knee. It stumbled, but kept her held.

She was suffocating.

Then, a voice, which was not from the creature. It was from the stone.

**[APPEAL FILED.]**

The text appeared in Kaelen's vision, bright and intrusive.

**[APPELLANT: CORE ID 44-ALPHA (GERALD HUTCHINS).]** **[REASON: EXCESSIVE USE OF FORCE BY UNLICENSED ENTITY.]** **[HEARING SCHEDULED: IMMEDIATE.]**

The System was back.

The blue light returned to the cavern, flooding the space with harsh, artificial daylight.

Kaelen gasped, air rushing into her lungs as Vorax dropped her.

The creature recoiled, hissing as the blue light burned its skin. It scrambled back into the cracks, retreating into the deep dark.

Gerald's core pulsed. Brighter. Stronger.

Kaelen scrambled to her knees. She looked at the interface.

Her health bar was full. Her mana pool was refilled.

But the notification window was open.

**[HEARING MINIGAME INITIATED.]** **[TOPIC: LIABILITY FOR STRUCTURAL DAMAGE CAUSED BY SEIZURE ACTION.]** **[ARGUMENT REQUIRED: DID THE CORE ACT WITHIN ITS JURISDICTION?]**

Gerald was not awake. He was still in the stone.

The System had activated the Hearing protocol because Gerald, in his dying moments, had filed an appeal against his own actions. He was arguing that seizing the water (by turning it to stone) was a defensive measure, not a violation of the sanctity of the dungeon environment.

Kaelen stared at the screen, and she had to argue for him.

She was not a Core. She had no voice in this proceeding.

Unless.

The text below the argument prompt glowed.

**[REPRESENTATIVE DETECTED: KAELEN VANCE.]** **[CREDENTIALS: GUILD LICENSE #99-402 (ADVENTURER).]** **[STATUS: AUTHORIZED ADVOCATE.]**

The System recognized adventurers as official witnesses to dungeon law.

Kaelen's hands shook. She could not lose this. If Gerald lost the hearing, the

"seizure" would be ruled an illegal destruction of the dungeon ecosystem. The penalty would be immediate dissolution of his core.

He would be wiped out. The library above would collapse.

She typed. Her fingers flew across the holographic keyboard.

*The action was necessary to prevent total structural failure. The water was a biohazard. Code Section 14-B: Sanitation.*

She hit submit.

The system processed.

**[ARGUMENT ACCEPTED.]** **[JUDGMENT: DEFENSIVE ACTION JUSTIFIED.]** **[PENALTY WAIVED.]** **[BONUS: REINFORCEMENT MANDATE ISSUED.]**

A surge of energy pulsed from Gerald's core. The stone around him cracked, revealing the dark sphere.

Gerald's light flared.

Then, the cavern changed, and the water that had not frozen was gone. Drained. The level dropped another ten feet, exposing a massive, rusted valve wheel on the wall behind the trapped Vorax.

The creature was gone, fled into the deeper tunnels.

But the valve remained.

And on the valve wheel, a new symbol was stamped.

It was not the System's logo. It was not the Guild's crest.

It was a red stamp. A municipal seal.

**[CITY OF ASHFORD. DEPARTMENT OF PUBLIC WORKS. APPROVED.]**

Gerald's voice echoed in Kaelen's head. Not a whisper. A command.

*Do not let them turn it.*

Kaelen looked at the valve. It controlled the flow of the main sewer line, the source of the toxic water.

If she closed it, the dungeon would flood from the other side.

If she opened it, the city above would receive a flood of black water.

The interface flickered. A new message appeared.

**[AUDIT INITIATED.]** **[INSPECTOR GENERAL: THE OBSERVER.]** **[LOCATION: CURRENT.]** **[STATUS: ARRIVING.]**

The air in the cavern grew cold.

From the tunnel entrance, footsteps echoed. Not the squelch of Vorax. The heavy, rhythmic clack of metal on stone.

Kaelen turned, and a figure stood in the light. It was tall, clad in armor that shifted like oil on water. Its face was a blank, white mask with a single, vertical eye slit.

It did not speak. It held a clipboard.

The Inspector General had arrived. And it was not here to save Gerald.

It was here to cite him.
