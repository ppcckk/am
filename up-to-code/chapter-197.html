Chapter 197: The Price of Time

# Chapter 197

The token lay in a perfect circle of rubble.

It was small, black, and cracked down the middle. A hairline fracture ran from the top seal to the bottom stamp. The air around it hummed with a low, grinding frequency, like a power transformer pushed past its limit.

Inspector Vane did not blink. His smile had not returned. It had simply been deleted, replaced by a look of cold, administrative confusion.

Gerald Hutchins looked at the rat.

The rat looked at Gerald. It was grooming its whiskers with the casual indifference of a creature that had just violated the fundamental laws of metaphysics.

Vane took a step forward. His boot crushed a shard of concrete.

"This is a forgery," Vane said. His voice was flat.

"A Core cannot summon an Inspector General's audit token.

The token is unique. It is bound to the Server Authority."

"Agreed," Gerald said.

He picked up his clipboard. The pen was still in his hand.

He uncapped it.

Click. "The token is bound. But the citation isn't."

Vane's eyes narrowed. "Explain."

Gerald pointed his pen at the crack in the token. "You served me a Citation for Fraudulent Employment Records. You claimed my start date was falsified. I admitted it."

"You confessed," Vane said. "Confession is final."

"I confessed to the *act*," Gerald corrected. "I did not confess to the *jurisdiction*."

Vane paused. His hand hovered over the control panel embedded in his chest plate. The extraction sequence was still running; Gerald could feel the tug in his core, a physical hook pulling at the center of his being.

"Jurisdiction?" Vane asked.

"Inspector Vane," Gerald said, speaking directly into the air, projecting his voice for the System's audio sensors. "You are an employee of the System's Internal Audit Division. You have authority to inspect Cores. You do not have authority to inspect the personnel files of a Municipal Employee."

"That is a semantic distinction," Vane said.

"It is a structural distinction," Gerald replied. He stepped toward the token.

The floor shuddered, but he did not stop.

"I am Gerald Hutchins.

I am a building inspector for the City of Ashford. I am not a Dungeon Core.

I am a citizen with a registered home."

Vane laughed. It was a dry, humorless sound.

"You are a Dungeon Core.

You have a footprint. You have spawns."

"I have a sub-basement," Gerald said. "And I have a rat. And now, I have a witness."

Gerald tapped the clipboard.

"Form 11-B, Amendment 4," he said. "Unlicensed Biological Entity acting as a proxy for a registered citizen. Under Municipal Ordinance 74-C, any entity acting within the property lines of a registered citizen is subject to the property owner's legal standing."

Sarah, who had been frozen against the elevator wall, finally moved. She stepped forward, her face pale. "Gerald, what are you doing?"

"Building a defense," Gerald said. He didn't look at her. He looked at Vane.

Vane's eyes flicked to the System interface floating in his vision. He was checking the rules. Gerald could see the faint blue glow reflecting in Vane's pupils.

"There is no such ordinance," Vane said.

"Check your local code," Gerald said.

"Ashford, Municipal Code Chapter 12, Section 4. Property rights extend to all entities within the perimeter of the dwelling.

The rat is inside the dwelling.

The rat found the token. The token was found on my property. Therefore, the token's presence constitutes a trespass by the finder."

Vane frowned. "That logic is circular."

"It's procedural," Gerald said. "You didn't have a warrant to enter the property.

You didn't have a warrant to serve me.

You entered a private residence without consent.

You are currently committing a trespass."

The humming noise grew louder. The crack in the token widened.

Vane looked at the token. Then he looked at Gerald.

"If the token is here," Vane said slowly, "then the Inspector General is already here."

"No," Gerald said. "The Inspector General sent the token. The token was lost.

The rat found it. The rat is now in possession of the authority."

"That is absurd."

"Read the code," Gerald said.

"Article 9, Section 2: 'A citation is valid only if served by an authorized agent upon a subject physically present and capable of receipt.' You attempted to serve me. But the citation was defective because it lacked jurisdiction over my employment status. A defective citation cannot trigger Core Revocation.

It must be voided."

Vane's hand tightened on his chest plate. "I can override the system."

"You can try," Gerald said. "But if you override a defective citation, you are falsifying a legal record. That is a Class A violation. The penalty for a Class A violation by an Auditor is immediate termination of the Auditor's contract."

Vane froze.

The silence in the room was absolute. The dust motes hung suspended in the light.

Sarah held her breath.

Vane looked at the token. He looked at the rat. He looked at Gerald.

"You're bluffing," Vane said.

"I don't bluff," Gerald said. "I measure."

He walked to the token. He didn't touch it.

He didn't need to.

The System was already parsing the argument. Gerald could feel the weight of the logic settling into the architecture of the world.

The hook in his core snapped.

The sensation was abrupt, like a rubber band breaking. The pulling stopped. The crumbling sensation in his chest eased, replaced by a dull ache of exhaustion.

Vane staggered back. His control panel flashed red.

**CITATION VOIDED.** **JURISDICTION ERROR.** **AUDITOR STATUS: UNDER REVIEW.**

Vane's face went gray. "No."

The air shimmered again. This time, it wasn't the rat.

A figure coalesced from the dust. It was not human. It was a geometric abstraction, a shifting prism of light and shadow that occupied the space where the token had been. It had no face, but it had a presence that pressed against the lungs.

The Inspector General.

It did not speak. It simply existed.

Vane dropped to his knees. Not out of respect, but because the gravity around the prism had increased.

Gerald straightened his back. He adjusted his tie. He picked up his clipboard.

"Inspector General," Gerald said. "I am filing a counter-citation."

The prism turned toward him. The light bent around it.

**SUBJECT: AUDITOR VANE.** **VIOLATION: PROCEDURAL OVERREACH.** **PENALTY: PENDING.**

Vane looked up, his eyes wide. "You can't. I am under the protection of the Audit Division."

"Protection is contingent on compliance," Gerald said. He looked at the prism.

"I request an immediate hearing.

I will present evidence of the fraudulent employment record, yes.

But I will also present evidence of the Auditor's failure to verify jurisdiction, failure to obtain a warrant for entry into a private dwelling, and failure to secure chain of custody for the audit token."

The prism pulsed.

**HEARING GRANTED.** **VENUE: LOCAL.** **TIME: IMMEDIATE.**

The prism dissolved into light, flowing into Vane's chest.

Vane screamed. It was a short, sharp sound, cut off by the System's enforcement. His body locked rigid. His eyes rolled back.

He collapsed.

The elevator doors slid shut.

Gerald exhaled. He looked at his hands. They were shaking.

He made a note to add a stress-relief room to the dungeon plans. It would need proper ventilation and non-slip flooring.

Sarah rushed to him. "Gerald! Are you okay?"

"I am compliant," Gerald said. He sounded tired. "Barely."

He looked at the unconscious body of Inspector Vane. The System had paused the extraction, but it hadn't canceled it. The red warning light on Vane's chest still pulsed.

**Hearing in progress. Auditor status: Frozen.**

Gerald looked at the rat.

"Good boy," Gerald said.

The rat chattered. It climbed back onto the elevator button panel.

Sarah looked at Vane, then at Gerald. "We can't just leave him here."

"We have to," Gerald said.

"The hearing isn't over. The prism is inside him. It's checking his logs.

If it finds anything else..."

"What else?"

"Anything," Gerald said. "Vane was looking for me.

He found me.

But why? I'm a small fish. A sub-basement with three mold spores.

Why send a high-level Auditor?"

Sarah frowned. "Maybe you're more of a threat than you think."

"I am a building inspector," Gerald said. "I am not a threat. I am a nuisance. There is a difference."

He walked over to the console on the wall.

He tapped the screen.

The dungeon map appeared. It was a simple blueprint of the library's sub-basement.

Four thousand square feet. A rectangle.

One of the walls was flickering.

Gerald zoomed in.

The wall was not just flickering. It was being rewritten.

New lines were appearing. Not handrails. Not support beams.

Tunnels.

They were extending from Gerald's dungeon footprint, drilling into the adjacent bedrock. They were moving fast. The System was generating new corridors, linking Gerald's dungeon to something else.

"Gerald," Sarah said. Her voice was tight. "Look at the energy signature."

Gerald looked. The energy was not green, the color of his own Core. It was purple.

Violet.

The color of a Void Core.

"Who is it?" Sarah asked.

Gerald stared at the new tunnels. They were connecting directly to the east wall. The east wall was the boundary between the library and the old abandoned subway station.

"The Metro," Gerald said. "The old tunnels."

"That dungeon is dormant," Sarah said. "It's been dead for ten years."

"It wasn't dead," Gerald said. "It was waiting."

The console beeped.

**NEW CONNECTION ESTABLISHED.** **LINKED CORE: VOID SENTINEL.** **STATUS: HOSTILE.** **ACTION: INCORPORATION.**

The floor shook again. But this time, it wasn't Vane's extraction. It was the ground itself.

From the new tunnel opening, a hand emerged.

It was large. It was made of shadow and jagged stone. It grasped the edge of the concrete floor.

Gerald stepped back. He raised his clipboard.

"Stop," Gerald said. "You are entering a private residence."

The hand kept coming. A second hand followed. Then a head. It had no face, just a smooth, black oval where a face should be.

The Void Sentinel pulled itself into Gerald's dungeon.

It stood seven feet tall. It radiated cold. The air around it turned to frost.

Gerald's spawn list updated.

**RAT: HIDDEN.** **MOLD SPORES: FROZEN.** **WATER LEAK: ICE.**

The Sentinel looked at Gerald. It did not speak. It simply raised a hand and pointed at the unconscious body of Inspector Vane.

Then it pointed at Gerald.

The System interface flashed.

**CORE COMBAT INITIATED.** **OPPONENT: VOID SENTINEL.** **DIFFICULTY: EXTREME.** **CODE VIOLATION DETECTED IN OPPONENT: UNAUTHORIZED STRUCTURAL MODIFICATION.**

Gerald looked at the violation.

It was the first time he had seen the System flag a violation *before* he filed it.

The Sentinel had built a tunnel. Without a permit. Without zoning approval. Without an environmental impact study.

Gerald smiled. It was a thin, tight smile.

He uncapped his pen.

"You didn't file for a variance," Gerald said.

The Sentinel tilted its head.

Gerald tapped the air.

**CITATION DRAFTED.** **VIOLATION: ILLEGAL EXCAVATION.** **PENALTY: STRUCTURAL COMPROMISE.**

The Sentinel roared. It was a sound like grinding tectonic plates. It lunged.

Gerald didn't dodge. He stepped forward.

He slammed his clipboard into the Sentinel's chest.

The clipboard didn't hurt. The *Citation* did.

The air around the Sentinel shattered. Cracks appeared in its shadowy form.

The System enforced the code.

The illegal tunnel collapsed. The Sentinel's connection to its home was severed.

It screamed, a silent, psychic shriek that made Gerald's teeth ache.

But it didn't fall.

The Sentinel stabilized. It looked at Gerald with something that felt like amusement.

It raised both hands. The ice on the floor cracked. Spikes of black crystal erupted from the ground, aiming at Gerald's core.

Gerald looked at Sarah.

"Run," he said.

"I'm not leaving you," Sarah said.

"You're not a Core," Gerald said.

"You're a witness. Go.

File a report with the City Council.

Tell them I need more manpower. Tell them I need a fire suppression system."

"Gerald—"

"Go!"

Sarah grabbed her bag and ran.

Gerald was alone.

The Sentinel advanced. The spikes grew taller.

Gerald looked at his dungeon map. He looked at the rat.

The rat was gone.

Gerald looked at the ceiling. The ventilation ducts.

He had never installed proper ventilation. The System had fined him for it.

**VENTILATION RATING: INADEQUATE.** **EFFECT: REDUCED REGEN.**

Gerald looked at the Sentinel.

The Sentinel was breathing. It needed air.

Gerald walked to the main air return. He didn't have tools. He had a pen.

He jammed the pen into the grate.

The System registered the action.

**MAINTENANCE REQUEST: BLOCKAGE DETECTED.** **AUTOCORRECT: ACTIVE.**

The fans spun up. The air rush changed.

But Gerald didn't stop. He pulled the pen out. He reached into his pocket and pulled out a small, folded piece of paper.

It was a Form 11-C. *Emergency Structural Repair.*

He held it up.

The Sentinel paused. It looked at the paper. It looked at Gerald.

Gerald smiled.

"You're not going to kill me," Gerald said. "You're going to have to pay for my repairs."

The Sentinel's hand raised higher.

Gerald closed his eyes.

He waited for the strike.

It never came.

Instead, the air behind him shimmered.

The Inspector General's prism reformed.

**HEARING RESUMED.** **NEW SUBJECT: VOID SENTINEL.** **CHARGE: UNLICENSED DUNGEON EXPANSION.**

The Sentinel froze.

The prism turned to Gerald.

**CITATION ACCEPTED.** **PENALTY: MANDATORY CONSTRUCTION.** **COST: 50% OF CURRENT MANA POOL.**

Gerald opened his eyes.

His mana bar plummeted. It hit zero.

He fell to his knees. The exhaustion was absolute.

The Sentinel looked at Gerald. It looked at the prism.

It lowered its hand.

The prism pulsed.

**AUDIT COMPLETE.** **SENTENCE: CO-EXISTENCE.**

The Sentinel turned and walked back into its tunnel.

The tunnel sealed itself.

Gerald sat on the floor. He picked up his clipboard.

Sarah's voice came over the intercom. "Gerald? Are you alive?"

Gerald looked at the empty spot where the Sentinel had been.

"I am compliant," he said.

He looked at the ceiling. The water leak had stopped. The mold spores were waking up. The rat was back in the corner, washing its face.

But the map was changed.

The tunnel was still there. It wasn't gone. It was just closed.

And on the edge of the map, a new name had appeared.

**NEIGHBOR: VOID SENTINEL.** **STATUS: ALLIED?** **NOTE: THE PRISM IS WATCHING.**

Gerald stood up. His legs were weak, but they held.

He looked at Vane's unconscious body.

He looked at the clock.

The hearing wasn't over. The prism was still inside Vane. And now, it was inside Gerald.

Gerald realized he hadn't won. He had just bought time.

And the price was his own core.

He looked at the ceiling. The System's red warning light was back.

**COMPLIANCE RATING: 15/100.**

Gerald sighed. He picked up his pen.

He had a lot of paperwork to do.
