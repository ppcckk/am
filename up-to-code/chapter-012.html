Chapter 12: Progressive Structural Failure

# Chapter 12

The corridor was still intact. That was the first thing Gerald noticed, and it made him stop walking.

Vorrath had collapsed the east-central chamber. The ceiling had come down in a sheet of compressedite and boneite, sealing the entrance with forty tons of rubble.

But the access corridor leading to it was undamaged.

No stress fractures. No spalling. No displacement of the load-bearing arches on either side.

A controlled demolition would have damaged the corridor. Any competent Core would have known that. The blast wave alone would have cracked the arches.

Vorrath hadn't done a controlled demolition. He'd triggered a localized structural failure and hoped the debris would fall straight down.

It hadn't. The rubble pile had angled three degrees west, which meant the chamber's eastern wall had already been compromised before the collapse. The failure was progressive. It had started somewhere in the foundation.

Gerald pressed his palm against the rubble.

The System fed him the composition data: boneite, compressedite, trace quantities of ironite. No explosives residue.

No magical displacement signature.

Just gravity doing what gravity does when a load path fails.

He pulled up the form. Not a citation. A demolition permit review.

Form 17-C. Required before any Core may alter or remove a structural element of its own dungeon.

Filed in advance.

Reviewed by the System's automated compliance layer.

If no permit was filed, the alteration was unauthorized.

Gerald searched the permit log for Vorrath's dungeon. Nothing. No demolition permit for the east-central chamber. No permit for any structural modification in the last ninety days.

The collapse was unauthorized. The rubble was, by the System's own definition, an unpermitted structure.

And unpermitted structures could be subject to a Stop Work Order.

Gerald opened Form 22-A. Stop Work Order. He filled in the target: the debris field itself.

Not the chamber. The rubble.

The pile of collapsed material that now occupied the space where Petra's shell Core had been.

The form required a description of the hazard. Gerald typed: "Unpermitted structural alteration. Debris field constitutes a temporary structure with no approved load path.

Risk of secondary collapse.

Risk of entrapment for any entity within the rubble volume."

He checked the scope.

A Stop Work Order on a debris field froze all spawning activity within the violation zone for a period determined by the System. The zone was defined by the physical extent of the unpermitted structure. In this case, the rubble pile.

Which meant the entire east-central sector of Vorrath's dungeon would go dark. No new spawns.

No movement of existing entities through the zone.

No magical reinforcement of the debris, which was, Gerald realized, the equivalent of taping off a condemned building. The Core couldn't touch the rubble.

Couldn't move it. Couldn't use it. Couldn't rebuild on top of it.

He signed the form.

The System accepted it. The boundary sensors around the east-central sector flickered and went amber. Spawning frozen. Vorrath would sense it like a builder sensing a padlock on his job site door.

But the form had a clause Gerald had never needed to use before. Clause 4. The Stop Work Order remained active only while the issuing inspector was physically present within the violation zone.

Gerald looked at the rubble pile. He looked at the corridor behind him.

He stepped into the rubble.

The boneite crunched under his feet. The air was thick with mineral dust.

His lungs burned, but the System didn't apply any debuff, and he wasn't inside a dungeon he was trying to shut down. He was inside a violation zone he was actively enforcing. The System recognized the distinction.

He started digging.

The rubble was heavy.

Each piece weighed between fifty and two hundred pounds. Gerald's Core body didn't have muscles, but it had a form, and the form had hands, and the hands had fingers that could grip.

He pulled chunks of compressedite aside, and he moved boneite slabs; worked methodically, top to bottom, left to right. The method he used on every job site for thirty-one years.

Twenty minutes. Thirty. The rubble was deeper than it looked. The chamber had been tall.

At forty minutes, his fingers struck something that wasn't stone, which was a cage. Small.

Maybe two feet across.

Made of ironite wire, twisted by hand.

Inside the cage, a light. Amber.

Flickering. Not the steady pulse of a healthy Core. The stuttering rhythm of something running out.

Petra.

Gerald lifted the cage. It was warm.

The wire was bent inward on one side, as if something had pressed against it.

Not crushed. Pressed. Gently.

Like a hand testing whether the thing inside was still alive.

He set the cage on a flat piece of boneite and opened the System interface, and pulled up Petra's Core record.

It was sparse.

Almost empty. No dungeon. No spawned entities.

No compliance history. A shell Core with a room that had been collapsed around her.

He checked her status. Dissolution: 73%. The System estimated she had about ninety minutes before the amber light went dark permanently.

Gerald opened a new form.

Core relocation request.

It required a destination. A dungeon with available capacity that would accept the Core as a protected structure.

He looked at his own dungeon. He had a room. A small one, near the entrance, that he'd built for storage.

It had ventilation. It had a fire-rated door.

It met code, and he filed the relocation request. The System processed it. Petra's amber light steadied slightly.

Not much.

Enough.

Then he noticed the citation.

It was filed against Petra's shell Core.

Not against Vorrath. Against her. Three days ago.

Before Gerald had found her. Before anyone had known she existed.

The citation was for unregistered operation; a Core without a registered dungeon was, under the System's building code, an unpermitted structure. The citation had been served. It had a signature.

Gerald read the signature. He did not recognize it.

The name was Keth.

No surname.

No Core designation. Just a name, in the signature block where a Core's registered title should be.

The citation had been served by a Core that wasn't registered.

And the citation was still active. It had never been appealed.

Which meant Petra had been carrying a debuff for three days.

Which meant her dissolution rate had been artificially elevated. Which meant she might have had more than ninety minutes if the citation hadn't been filed.

Gerald stared at the amber light, and he pulled up the citation's full text.

The violation was real. Petra was unregistered.

The citation was technically valid.

But the service method was wrong. A citation required physical presence. The serving Core had to be in the same space as the target.

Gerald checked the service log, and the citation had been served remotely.

That was impossible.

The System didn't allow remote service.

Gerald had never seen a citation served without physical presence. He'd built his entire professional career on that rule.

It was the one rule that made citations meaningful. You had to look the violator in the eye.

Unless the System had a loophole he'd never found, and he scrolled to the bottom of the citation record. He noticed a field he hadn't seen before.

Service Method.

It was a dropdown. The options were: In Person, By Mail, By Messenger.

There was no "Remote" option.

But the citation had been served remotely. The log confirmed it. The System had accepted it.

Gerald sat down on a chunk of boneite, and the rubble was cold. His Core body didn't feel cold, but the form felt heavy.

He'd never encountered a citation that broke the service rule.

Not in thirty-one years. Not in the System.

He opened a search. Citation service methods.

He typed the query into the System's reference layer, and the results came back.

Standard procedures. Standard forms.

Standard definitions.

Then a fourth result. Not a form. A note. A line, buried in the appendix of the Inspector General's procedural manual.

"Service by Proxy: A registered Core may authorize a non-registered entity to serve citations on its behalf. The proxy must be physically present at the target location. The proxy's identity is not recorded in the service log."

Gerald read it twice.

A proxy. Someone had sent a proxy into Vorrath's dungeon.

Into the east-central chamber.

Three days ago. The proxy had found Petra. Served the citation.

And left.

The proxy wasn't recorded. The proxy's identity was hidden.

But the proxy had been physically present. Which meant the proxy had been inside Vorrath's dungeon three days ago.

Which meant Vorrath had known.

Vorrath had known about Petra. Vorrath had known about the citation.

And Vorrath had collapsed the chamber anyway.

Gerald looked at the cage. At the bent wire. At the amber light.

He opened the System interface again. He pulled up his own dungeon's compliance record.

He needed to file an appeal on Petra's behalf, and he had the right.

He was now her relocation authority. The System recognized him as her proxy.

He filed the appeal. The Hearing window opened. Fourteen hours.

But he needed to know who Keth was. He needed to know who the proxy was. And he needed to know why Vorrath had collapsed the chamber instead of just killing Petra, which would have been easier.

Unless Vorrath hadn't collapsed the chamber to kill her. Unless he'd collapsed it to bury the evidence. To bury the proxy's trail. To bury whatever the proxy had left behind.

Gerald looked at the rubble. He'd dug to the cage. He'd stopped.

But the proxy had been here. The proxy had served the citation.

The proxy had been physically present.

And the System recorded physical presence. Every Core's boundary sensors logged every entity that entered. Every spawn.

Every visitor.

Every proxy.

Gerald pulled up Vorrath's boundary log, and he didn't have access to it; he had a Stop Work Order active in this sector. A Stop Work Order gave the inspector full access to the violation zone.

Including the zone's sensor logs.

He pulled the logs. Three days ago. East-central sector.

One entry.

Timestamp: 0347 hours.

Entity type: Unknown. Size: Humanoid.

Movement pattern: Entering, stationary for eleven minutes, exiting.

Eleven minutes. Enough time to find Petra. Serve the citation. Leave.

Gerald pulled up the entity's signature. The System logged every entity's Core signature when it crossed a boundary. Even unknowns. Even proxies.

The signature was there. A Core signature. Registered. Active.

Gerald read it, and he knew this one. He'd seen it before.

In his own dungeon.

Two weeks ago. A visitor. A Core that had walked into his front entrance, looked around, and left without speaking.

Gerald had assumed it was a scout.

He'd assumed it was routine.

The Core's name was Keth.

And it was registered.

Properly. With a full dungeon. A real one.

With spawns and compliance ratings and a building that met code.

Gerald pulled up Keth's dungeon location, which was close.

Six hundred meters northeast. Smaller than Vorrath's.

Newer. But fully operational.

He pulled up Keth's compliance history.

Twelve citations issued. All served in person. All valid. All against other Cores.

Twelve citations in two weeks.

Gerald had issued four in three months.

Keth was faster. Keth was more aggressive. Keth was,, the reason Petra was dying.

And Keth had walked into Gerald's dungeon. Had looked around. Had left.

Gerald looked at the amber light. Ninety minutes. Maybe less now, if the citation debuff was still active. He'd filed the appeal, but the Hearing was fourteen hours away.

He looked at the boundary log again. The timestamp. 0347 hours. Three days ago.

He checked the log for the last twenty-four hours. Keth's signature. Any Keth signature.

There it was. Two hours ago.

Keth had entered Vorrath's dungeon.

West entrance. Walked to the east-central sector.

The sector where the rubble was.

The sector where Gerald was standing right now.

The Stop Work Order was active. Keth couldn't enter. The System would have blocked the entry.

But the log showed Keth had entered.

Gerald checked the timestamp again. Two hours ago. Before Gerald had filed the Stop Work Order.

Keth had been here.

Two hours ago. While the rubble was still unpermitted.

While the citation was still active.

While Petra was still trapped.

Keth had been here, and Keth hadn't removed the citation. Keth hadn't appealed. Keth hadn't helped.

Keth had come and gone.

And the log showed one more entry. Not Keth.

A different signature.

Unknown.

Humanoid. Entered two hours ago.

Stationary for four minutes. Exited.

Four minutes. Not enough time to serve a citation. Not enough time to dig.

Enough time to look. To assess. To decide something.

Gerald looked at the cage. At the bent wire. At the amber light, flickering.

He looked at the rubble around him. The Stop Work Order kept Vorrath's spawns frozen. Kept the debris in place. Kept Gerald safe, for now.

But it also kept Gerald here. Trapped in the violation zone. Vulnerable. Alone.

And somewhere northeast, a Core named Keth was building something. Had been building it for two weeks. Had issued twelve citations. Had come to this room twice.

The second time, four minutes. Enough to look. Enough to decide.

Gerald pulled up Keth's dungeon layout. The System gave him access. A Stop Work Order inspector could view any structure within range.

Keth's dungeon was small. Six rooms.

A central chamber.

A spawning hall. A storage area.

And a room labeled "Evidence."

Gerald stared at the label.

Dungeons didn't have rooms labeled Evidence. Dungeons had rooms labeled for function.

Spawn chambers.

Traps. Lairs.

Keth had a room labeled Evidence.

Gerald zoomed in. The room's contents were listed. The System showed stored items.

Twelve items were present. One for each citation Keth had issued.

Each item was a fragment.

A piece of rubble.

A shard of boneite. A sample.

Physical evidence. From each violation.

Keth wasn't just citing dungeons. Keth was collecting. Building a case.

Against what? Against whom?

The twelfth item in the Evidence room wasn't a fragment, which was a Core. A shell Core. Amber.

Flickering.

Gerald looked down at the cage in his hands.

He looked at the System interface. The Evidence room. The twelfth item. The location tag.

It wasn't in Keth's dungeon. The location tag pointed here. To this rubble. To this cage.

The System was logging Petra as Keth's evidence. As if Keth had already claimed her. The citation appeared not to be a punishment.

It was a seizure.

Gerald's hands were shaking. The cage was warm. The light was steady now, but barely.

He opened a new form. He didn't know what form it was.

He knew what he needed, and he needed to unfile the citation. He needed to reverse the seizure. He needed to prove that Keth's service method was invalid.

But the System said the proxy rule existed. The proxy rule was legal. The service was valid.

Unless the proxy wasn't authorized.

Gerald pulled up the authorization record. A proxy needed written authorization from the sponsoring Core. The authorization had to be filed with the System.

He searched for Keth's proxy authorization.

None existed.

No proxy had been authorized. The service was invalid. The citation was void. Petra's debuff was illegitimate.

Gerald filed the motion to vacate. The System processed it.

The amber light brightened.

Just a fraction.

Just enough.

Then a new notification appeared. Not from the System's standard layer. From the Inspector General's audit queue.

Gerald's own dungeon was flagged. The audit was live. His Compliance Rating was being recalculated in real time.

And it was dropping.
