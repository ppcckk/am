Chapter 236: Pre-Inspection Self-Assessment

# Chapter 236

The clipboard snapped shut with the finality of a prison door.

Pendergast did not blink. His eyes, grey and flat as wet concrete, tracked the movement of Gerald's hand toward his pocket. The rat, Whiskers, skittered behind Gerald's left boot, its tiny claws clicking against the linoleum of the municipal hallway.

"Department of Internal Compliance," Pendergast repeated. He tapped the black card with a manicured fingernail.

"Section 4, Subsection C: Unsanctioned Expansion.

You are currently undergoing a live audit, Inspector. Do not resist."

Gerald kept his hand near his pocket. The pen felt heavy.

"I am not resisting," Gerald said. "I am clarifying jurisdiction."

Pendergast tilted his head. A faint smile touched his lips, cold and sharp.

"Jurisdiction is absolute. Your dungeon footprint has exceeded the registered baseline by three hundred square feet. You have constructed new egress routes without filing Form 88-Delta. You have spawned entities that are not on your approved roster."

The slime on Gerald's shirt dripped onto the floor. A small, green puddle formed near the drain.

Pendergast looked at the puddle. He wrinkled his nose.

"Biohazard violation," he noted.

He wrote something on his clipboard. The sound of the pen scratching was loud in the quiet hallway.

"That is another deduction.

Your Compliance Rating is currently thirty-two. If it drops below twenty, I am authorized to initiate Core Dissolution."

Gerald's stomach turned over. Not from fear. From the specific, nauseating dread of an unfinished form.

"You cannot dissolve a core during an active enforcement cycle," Gerald said. His voice was steady. It was the voice he used when telling a contractor that their electrical panel was a fire hazard.

"Watch me."

Pendergast raised his hand again. The air twisted, pulling at the fabric of Gerald's jacket.

Gerald did not step back. He stepped forward.

"Stop," Gerald said.

Pendergast paused. The twisting air halted.

"Why?"

Gerald pointed a finger at the black card.

"Look at the header. Top left corner. Small print."

Pendergast looked down. His brow furrowed.

"It says 'Conditional Audit.'"

Gerald nodded. He pulled the pen from his pocket. He did not attack. He uncapped it.

"Under System Statute 19, Paragraph 7," Gerald said, speaking rapidly, his eyes scanning the card as if reading a building permit, "an audit is conditional upon the provision of a Notice of Infraction. You have not provided one. You are attempting to serve a final judgment without a preliminary citation."

Pendergast's eyes narrowed. He looked at Gerald. Then at the card. Then back at Gerald.

"That is a technicality."

"It is the law," Gerald said. "And you are the Department of Internal Compliance. You are supposed to be the embodiment of the law. If you break procedure, you invalidate the audit."

Pendergast lowered his hand. The air stopped twisting.

"You are wasting my time, Hutchins."

"Then fill out the form." Gerald held out his pen. "Here.

I will help.

It is a standard Form 1-A.

Non-compliance with structural expansion. You fill in the date.

You fill in the square footage. You sign. Then you can begin the audit."

Pendergast stared at the pen. He did not take it.

"You think this will save you?"

"I think it will delay you," Gerald said. "And I have twenty-three hours. I intend to spend them fixing my code."

Pendergast laughed. It was a dry, brittle sound.

"You cannot fix a dungeon in twenty-three hours. The Maw is already moving.

It has breached the library's east wing.

Three heroes are dead. Four more are trapped in the ventilation shafts. Your dungeon is the only thing keeping the breach contained, and it is failing."

Gerald felt a cold weight settle in his chest.

"The ventilation shafts?"

"Yes."

Gerald's mind raced.

Ventilation. He knew ventilation.

He knew air exchange rates.

He knew static pressure.

"Show me," Gerald said.

Pendergast hesitated. Then he turned.

"Follow me. Do not deviate from the path."

They walked.

Gerald followed the man in the suit through the back corridors of the library. The lights flickered. The smell of ozone and rotting meat grew stronger. Whiskers rode on Gerald's shoulder now, chattering softly.

They reached the service entrance to the sub-basement. The heavy steel door was warped. The frame had splintered.

Gerald stopped. He ran a hand over the wood.

"Structural failure," he muttered. "Impact damage. High-velocity projectile?"

"The Maw," Pendergast said. "It threw a chunk of masonry. It was aiming for the core room."

"Why didn't it hit?"

"Your rat."

Gerald looked at Whiskers. The rat looked smug.

"Biological interference," Gerald said. "Improper storage of organic waste."

Pendergast opened the door. He gestured for Gerald to enter.

Inside, the air was thick. Dust motes danced in the beams of Pendergast's flashlight. Gerald clicked on his own headlamp.

The main chamber was a mess of broken stone and green slime. But it was the tunnels that caught Gerald's eye.

The tunnels were narrow. Too narrow.

Gerald walked to the nearest corridor. He measured it with his steps.

"Thirty inches," he said. "Code requires thirty-six for egress."

Pendergast stood by the door, checking his watch.

"You are irrelevant to the tactical situation, Hutchins. The Maw is coming.

It will arrive in six hours.

If you do not boost your defensive stats by then, you will be consumed.

Your dungeon will be deleted. The library will fall."

Gerald ignored him. He walked further in.

He found a group of adventurers. They were huddled near the central spire, their armor dented, their weapons drawn. They looked exhausted. One of them, a woman with a cracked breastplate, saw Gerald.

"Is that the core?" she asked. She pointed at Gerald.

Gerald straightened up.

"I am the Inspector," Gerald said.

"Where is the core?"

"I am the core."

The woman stared at him. Then she looked at Pendergast.

"Who is that?"

"Internal Compliance," Gerald said. "Do not engage with him. He is here to shut us down."

The adventurers looked confused. They looked at the broken walls. They looked at the slime.

"We can't fight the Maw like this," the woman said. "Our fire resistance is down. Our healing is down. Everything is down."

Gerald nodded. He pulled out his clipboard. He had not had his own clipboard, so he had taken a blank sheet of paper from his pocket.

"What is your current Compliance Rating?" Gerald asked.

The woman blinked.

"Fourteen."

"Low," Gerald said. "You are suffering from Ventilation Debuff. Your party's Health regeneration is reduced by forty percent. That is why your healer is dying."

The healer, a young man in robes, looked up. He was pale.

"Can you fix it?"

"I need to install a secondary air intake," Gerald said. He looked at the tunnel walls. "There is a crack in the north wall.

Four inches wide. It is a code violation, but it is currently serving as a natural ventilation port.

If I widen it to twelve inches and install a filtered grate, I can increase Air Exchange Rate by fifteen percent."

Pendergast stepped forward.

"You are modifying the dungeon structure. This requires a permit."

"Emergency Maintenance," Gerald said. "Under Clause 9. Hazardous Atmosphere. I am authorized to make immediate corrections to preserve life safety."

Pendergast's jaw tightened.

"Fine. Do it. But if you fail, I will cite you for Unauthorized Construction."

Gerald looked at the adventurers.

"Do you have a hammer? A chisel? Anything?"

The warrior handed Gerald a heavy warhammer.

Gerald took it. He walked to the north wall. He measured the crack.

Twelve inches.

He swung the hammer.

The stone cracked. Dust fell.

He swung again. And again.

Whiskers chattered, biting at the mortar.

The crack widened. Air rushed in. It was cold, but it was clean.

Gerald felt a surge in his chest. A warmth.

**System Notification: Ventilation Improved. Compliance Rating +5. Current Rating: 37.**

The adventurers gasped. Their wounds stopped bleeding faster. The healer's hands steadied.

"It works," the woman said.

Gerald nodded. He wiped dust from his face.

"It is a temporary patch," Gerald said. "It is not up to code. But it will hold for six hours."

Pendergast watched him. The man's expression was unreadable.

"You are buying time," Pendergast said. "The Maw will not care about your ventilation. It will care about your Core Integrity."

"I know," Gerald said.

He turned to the warriors.

"I need you to do something for me."

The woman looked at Gerald. Then at the broken wall. Then at the man in the suit.

"Tell us."

"Stand in a line. Five feet apart. Hold your shields up."

The warrior frowned. "Why?"

"Because you are blocking the main egress route," Gerald said. "Move."

The warriors looked at each other. They did not move.

Gerald looked at Pendergast.

"Tell them."

Pendergast sighed. He stepped forward.

"Move," Pendergast said. His voice carried a weight that was not human. It vibrated in the bones.

The warriors moved. They formed a line, five feet apart, holding their shields.

Gerald walked behind them. He pulled a small device from his pocket. A laser distance meter.

He measured the gap between the shields.

"Five feet," he said. "That is the minimum clearance for emergency evacuation. Good."

He looked at Pendergast.

"You said the Maw is coming."

"Six hours," Pendergast confirmed.

"Then we need to prepare the kill zone," Gerald said.

Pendergast raised an eyebrow.

"A kill zone?"

"The Maw is large. It will not fit through the narrow tunnels. It will have to break through the main chamber walls. Where will it enter?"

Pendergast looked at the map on his clipboard.

"The east wall. The library foundation is weak there. The Maw will punch through."

Gerald nodded. He walked to the east wall.

He measured the thickness of the stone.

"Eighteen inches," he said. "It will take three strikes to break it."

The woman approached him.

"We can't stop three strikes," she said.

"No," Gerald said. "But we can control the debris."

He looked at the line of warriors.

"Stand exactly here. When the wall breaks, do not attack. Block the gap with your shields. Hold the debris in place."

The warrior scoffed. "Hold up a wall?"

"Create a bottleneck," Gerald said. "The Maw will push through. It will try to enter.

But the gap will be only three feet wide.

It will get stuck."

Pendergast looked at Gerald.

"You are using the dungeon's own geometry against the enemy?"

"I am using the building code," Gerald said.

"Egress routes must be wide enough for evacuation. They must not be so wide that they allow uncontrolled access to hazardous materials. By restricting the breach, I create a code violation for the Maw.

Uncontrolled Entry."

Pendergast's eyes lit up.

"Citation," Pendergast whispered.

Gerald looked at him.

"Exactly."

Gerald raised his pen. He wrote on the air. The System responded. A blue text box appeared.

**Drafting Citation: Uncontrolled Entry.**

Pendergast watched, fascinated.

"You can do that?" Pendergast asked.

"I am a core," Gerald said. "And I am an inspector. I see a violation. I write a citation."

He looked at the Maw's predicted entry point.

"It will take three strikes. On the third strike, I will serve the citation. The Maw will be physically repelled by the System's enforcement protocol."

Pendergast smiled. It was a genuine smile this time.

"It will not repel a Maw," Pendergast said. "The penalty for Uncontrolled Entry is a stat debuff. Not a barrier."

Gerald looked at the pen.

"I am not just citing the Maw," Gerald said. "I am citing the hole."

Pendergast paused.

"The hole?"

"The breach is a structural deficiency," Gerald said. "If I cite the breach itself, the System will attempt to repair it. It will fill the hole. It will close the door."

Pendergast looked at the wall.

"Can you cite a hole?"

"I can cite anything that violates code," Gerald said. "A hole is a breach of security. A hole is a failure of containment."

He looked at Pendergast.

"I need you to stand there. When the wall breaks, I need you to serve the citation for me. You are Internal Compliance. You have the authority."

Pendergast looked at Gerald.

"You want me to be your hand?"

"I want you to be the law," Gerald said.

Pendergast straightened his jacket. He looked at the east wall.

"I am the law," he said.

The floor shook. Dust rained from the ceiling.

Boom.

The first strike.

The adventurers braced their shields.

Boom.

The second strike. Cracks spiderwebbed across the stone.

Gerald checked his watch.

"Two minutes," Gerald said.

Pendergast stepped forward. He held out his hand.

"Give me the pen."

Gerald handed it to him.

Pendergast held the pen. He looked at the cracking wall.

"If we fail," Pendergast said, "I will audit you for negligence."

"If we succeed," Gerald said, "I will file a complaint for harassment."

Boom.

The wall exploded inward.

Debris flew. The warriors screamed, holding their shields, blocking the chunks of stone.

A massive, chitinous limb punched through the gap. The Maw was trying to enter.

Pendergast raised the pen.

Gerald shouted.

"Serve it!"

Pendergast slammed the pen against the limb.

**Citation Issued: Uncontrolled Entry.**

The limb jerked. The Maw shrieked, a sound like grinding metal.

The limb pulled back. It tried to push forward again.

The System responded. The rubble around the gap glowed. It moved.

The stones slid together. The gap closed.

The Maw was locked out.

Silence fell over the chamber.

The adventurers lowered their shields. They stared at the sealed wall.

Pendergast lowered the pen. He looked at Gerald.

"It worked," Pendergast said.

Gerald exhaled. His hands were shaking.

"Temporarily," Gerald said. "The Maw will try to break it again. It will be faster next time."

Pendergast looked at the black card.

"I am canceling the audit," Pendergast said.

Gerald looked up.

"Why?"

"Because you are useful," Pendergast said. He put the card away. "And because the Inspector General is coming."

Gerald froze.

"The Inspector General?"

"Here. Now."

Pendergast pointed to the ceiling.

Above them, the library's floorboards glowed gold.

A voice echoed through the dungeon. It was not loud, but it was everywhere.

**"Hutchins, Gerald. Core ID 442. Prepare for inspection."**

Gerald looked at Pendergast.

Pendergast looked at the ceiling.

"You are not the only one who knows how to read the fine print, Inspector," Pendergast said. "You just cited a god."

The gold light intensified. The air grew heavy.

Gerald looked at his dungeon. It was messy. It was broken. It was barely holding together.

He looked at Pendergast.

"I need a form," Gerald said.

Pendergast pulled a fresh clipboard from his suit.

"Form 19-Omega," Pendergast said. "Pre-Inspection Self-Assessment."

Gerald took the pen.

He looked at the glowing ceiling.

He looked at the sealed wall.

He looked at Whiskers.

The rat chattered.

Gerald wrote.
