Chapter 189: Absorbing the Violation

# Chapter 189

The steel door swung inward with a sound like a gasp.

Gerald stepped forward. His boots crunched on material that was not stone, not dirt, and not wood. It felt like dried eggshells underfoot. The air smelled of ozone and old blood.

He did not look at the room. He looked at the clipboard.

The page had filled itself. The ink was wet.

**CASE FILE: 89-44-B** **TARGET: The Unregistered** **STATUS: Non-Compliant by Existence** **CITATION STATUS: Pending**

The words blurred. Gerald blinked.

The text stabilized.

He adjusted his glasses, pushing them up the bridge of his nose with a trembling thumb. The tremor stopped when he saw the margin notes.

*Violation 1: No Foundation Permit.* *Violation 2: No Zoning Approval.* *Violation 3: Unauthorized Use of Void Space.*

A low hum vibrated in his teeth. The black badge on his chest grew warm. It pulsed against the sternum, a rhythmic throb that matched the beating of his heart, or perhaps the pulse of the room itself.

Gerald moved the clipboard. He scanned the left wall.

The wall was not a wall.

It was a membrane. Translucent, veined with purple capillaries that pulsed with fluid.

Behind it, shapes moved.

Shadows with too many limbs. They pressed against the barrier, testing for weakness.

He turned his gaze to the right. A staircase spiraled upward into darkness. The steps were made of fused bone. The railing was missing.

"Gerald."

The voice came from the shadows of the staircase. Not from the room. From the mind.

The Inspector General's avatar was not here. The avatar had stayed at the door. But the presence remained, a cold weight in the parietal lobe.

*Observe,* the voice said. *Do not act until the form is complete.*

Gerald ignored the command. His hands moved. He pulled a measuring tape from his belt. The reel snapped out, extending six feet.

He walked to the bone staircase.

The first step was twelve inches high. The second was fourteen. The third was eleven.

Gerald wrote: *Non-Uniform Tread Height. Violation of Egress Safety Code 4.2.*

The membrane wall shuddered. A purple vein burst. Fluid sprayed across the floor, hissing as it touched the bone steps. The shadows behind the membrane recoiled.

They were reacting to the citation.

Gerald kept writing. He moved to the center of the room.

The floor was uneven.

Puddles of the purple fluid pooled in depressions that served no drainage purpose.

There were no drains. There was no slope.

*Standing water in high-traffic areas,* he muttered. *Mold hazard. Slip hazard.*

He wrote: *Failure to Provide Adequate Drainage. Violation of Sanitation Code 9.1.*

The room groaned. The floor beneath his feet softened. The eggshell texture turned to gel. Gerald's boots sank two inches into the muck.

He did not struggle. He planted his feet. He widened his stance. Stability was a matter of distribution.

A shape detached itself from the membrane.

It was humanoid, but elongated. Its skin was grey and slick, peeling away in strips. It had no eyes. Instead, it had three horizontal slits on its face, leaking purple fluid.

It slid toward him. The movement was wrong. It did not use legs. It used the floor, pulling itself along the wet surface.

Gerald did not back away. He held up the clipboard.

"Identify yourself," Gerald said. His voice was flat. Professional.

The creature tilted its head. The slits widened. A sound emerged, like wet leather tearing.

*Nameless,* the creature projected into Gerald's mind. *We are the Unregistered. We have no code. We have no limits.*

"Everyone has limits," Gerald said. "Limits are what keep you from collapsing into yourself."

He pointed to the creature's left arm. It was three feet longer than the right. The elbow joint bent in both directions.

"Structural imbalance," Gerald noted. "Center of gravity compromised. High risk of fall-related injury."

The creature lunged.

It was fast. Faster than a rat. Faster than a goblin. It crossed the room in two seconds, claws raking the air toward Gerald's throat.

Gerald did not dodge.

He sidestepped. One step to the left. The claws passed through the space where his head had been, scattering droplets of purple fluid onto the clipboard.

The creature stumbled. Its overlong arm caught on the bone railing of the staircase.

The railing snapped.

The creature crashed into the stairs. Bone shards flew. The creature shrieked, a high, thin sound that shattered the glass lenses of Gerald's glasses.

Gerald caught the glasses before they hit the ground. He replaced them on his nose.

"Failure to maintain handrails," he wrote. *Violation of Structural Integrity Code 1.1.*

The System responded.

A red line appeared in the air. It traced the broken railing. It highlighted the fracture points. Then, the red line turned black.

**CITATION ISSUED.** **SEVERITY: CRITICAL.** **PENALTY: IMMEDIATE DEGRADATION OF STRUCTURAL SUPPORT.**

The bone staircase crumbled. Not all of it. Just the parts cited.

The steps where the creature had fallen turned to dust. The railing fragments disintegrated into powder.

The creature screamed. It tried to climb back up, but its limbs were losing cohesion. The bones in its arms softened. The cartilage turned to jelly.

*You are killing us,* the creature projected. *We are free. We are outside your rules.*

"You are outside the code," Gerald corrected. "That means you are illegal. And illegal structures are condemned."

He looked at the black badge. It was hot now. Burning.

*Warning,* the System text appeared on the clipboard. *Auditor Efficiency Exceeding Parameters. Risk of Core Instability.*

Gerald ignored the warning.

He walked through the dissolving staircase. The ground held. His boots were heavy, lead-weighted by the System's mandate.

He was an anchor.

The creature slumped against the membrane wall. It was losing form. Its legs had merged with the floor. The membrane was absorbing it, pulling it back into the void.

But it did not die.

The purple fluid flowed over it, repairing the damage. The creature's form shifted, becoming more fluid, more amorphous. It was adapting.

Gerald stopped.

He looked at the clipboard. The citations were stacking up.

Three violations.

The penalty was being paid. The dungeon's structural integrity was failing at a rate of ten percent per minute.

But the creature was still moving.

*You cite the parts,* the creature projected. *But you cannot cite the whole.*

Gerald frowned. He tapped his pen against the clipboard.

If the system only targeted specific violations, the creature could shed the violated parts and survive. The core of the creature—the unregistered mass—remained untouched.

He needed a violation that covered the entire entity.

He looked at the ceiling.

There was no ceiling. The purple veins extended upward, branching into a dark void. There was no roof. No upper boundary.

The space was infinite. Or it pretended to be.

*Violation of Containment Zone,* Gerald wrote. *Uncontrolled Expansion of Spatial Boundary.*

He wrote: *Failure to Define Perimeter. Violation of Zoning Ordinance 7.3.*

The System paused.

The red line did not appear immediately.

Gerald held his breath. The pen hovered.

If the System accepted this citation, it would attack the boundary. The membrane wall.

The floor.

The air.

It would try to force the space to close.

If it rejected it, the Auditor would fail. And Gerald would lose his Core.

The pen touched the paper.

The ink sank in.

The System processed.

*Violation Detected.*

A black line appeared around the perimeter of the room. It traced the membrane. It traced the floor. It traced the ceiling void.

The line tightened.

The room shrank.

The creature shrieked. It was being compressed. The space around it was collapsing, forcing the matter inward.

Gerald felt the pressure. The air grew thick. The ozone smell intensified, becoming suffocating.

He stood firm. He did not let the compression push him. He was an Auditor. He defined the space.

The creature flailed. Its form was distorting, forced into a smaller and smaller volume. The purple veins snapped. The shadows were sucked into the core.

But the core did not vanish.

It resisted.

The membrane wall bulged outward. It pushed against the black line. The System's enforcement was fighting the entity's raw existence.

A crack appeared in the air. Not in the wall. In the fabric of the dungeon itself.

Spiderweb fractures spread from the center of the room. The black line flickered.

*Balance is not maintained,* the System warned. *Target mass exceeds containment parameters.*

Gerald looked at the black badge.

The badge was cracking.

A hairline fracture ran across the black metal.

He looked at the creature. It was a lump of grey flesh now. Small.

Dense. Pulsing.

It was still alive.

The citation was not enough. The violation was too broad. The penalty was too diffuse.

He needed a specific hook. A point of failure.

Gerald scanned the lump.

The creature had no eyes. No mouth. No limbs.

But it had a scar.

A jagged line across its surface. A place where the membrane had been torn. Where the fluid had leaked.

That was the wound. The point of entry.

Gerald walked toward the lump. The floor was buckling. Tiles cracked under his feet. The air was thinning.

He stopped three feet from the mass.

He looked at the scar.

*Violation of Bio-Mechanical Integrity,* he wrote. *Failure to Seal Perforation in Membrane.*

He wrote: *Target identified as Point of Failure.*

The black line shifted. It moved from the perimeter to the scar.

It focused.

The black line touched the scar.

The creature spasmed.

A sound like a gunshot echoed in the shrinking room.

The scar exploded.

Not outward. Inward.

The black line pulled the edges of the wound together.

It stitched them shut. But it pulled too hard. The tissue tore.

The core ruptured.

The grey flesh collapsed into a puddle. The purple fluid stopped flowing. The veins darkened, then faded.

The silence returned.

Gerald stood in the center of the room. The black line faded. The fractures in the air sealed.

The room was stable.

But it was empty.

No creature. No dungeon. Just the bone steps, the membrane wall, and the silence.

Gerald looked down at his clipboard.

The page was blank.

No.

Not blank.

A new line had appeared at the bottom.

**AUDIT COMPLETE.** **RATING: 100/100.** **BONUS UNLOCKED: VOID WALKER TRAIT.** **NEXT TARGET: THE LIBRARY.**

Gerald stared at the last line.

*The Library?*

He looked up.

The steel door was gone.

The room he had entered was gone.

He was standing in the Ashford Municipal Library sub-basement.

The familiar concrete walls were there. The smell of mold and old paper was there.

But the air was different.

It was cold. Colder than it had ever been.

And there was a sound.

Footsteps.

Not his own.

They were coming from the direction of the main stairwell. Heavy, deliberate steps.

Gerald turned.

The door to the library was open.

Standing in the doorway was a woman. She wore a grey robe, identical to the Inspector General's. But her face was not the avatar's.

It was his mother's face.

She had died in 2019.

Gerald's breath hitched. His hand tightened on the clipboard until the paper creased.

The woman stepped into the basement.

She did not look at him. She looked at the floor.

"Insufficient clearance for emergency egress," she said. Her voice was his mother's.

But the tone was not.

It was flat. Administrative.

She reached into her robe. She pulled out a citation.

It was not a piece of paper.

It was a bone.

Carved with symbols Gerald did not recognize.

She held it out to him.

"You have been cited," she said.

Gerald looked at the bone.

He looked at his mother's face.

He looked at the black badge on his chest. It was whole again.

But the fracture had moved. It was now on his left hand. The skin of his palm was grey. The veins were purple.

He had absorbed the violation.

He was becoming the dungeon.

The woman smiled. It was not a smile. It was a grimace of bureaucratic satisfaction.

"Sign here," she said.

She pointed to the bone.

Gerald raised his grey hand. He reached for the citation.

And the library lights went out.
