Chapter 395: Violation Begins

# Chapter 395

The first thing that hit Gerald was the smell. Not the rot, which he had expected, but the ozone. Sharp, metallic, and thick enough to coat the back of his throat like a bad penny.

Martha stopped beside him, her hammer raised. Her eyes darted left, then right.

The tunnel stretched ahead, a jagged throat of wet stone and dripping pipes.

To their left, a rusted ladder descended further. To their right, a narrow ledge hugged the wall.

Kaelen's dungeon was a mess. It always had been. It was a sprawling, chaotic sprawl of ad-hoc tunnels and hastily carved chambers, designed for ambush, not for occupancy. It was a violation waiting to happen, and Gerald had been waiting thirty-one years for someone to finally admit it.

"Pressure differential is wrong," Gerald said, his voice flat. He didn't raise it. He didn't need to. The System heard everything.

"What?" Martha asked. She didn't lower her hammer.

"The air.

It's being pulled toward the main chamber. Too fast.

See the condensation on the pipes?

It's moving upward against gravity. That means the intake fans in Kaelen's central hub are running at 110% capacity to compensate for a blockage downstream.

"

Gerald reached into his inventory pocket and pulled out a small, brass anemometer. It was a relic from his municipal days, useless in combat, essential in bureaucracy. He held it up to the damp air. The needle twitched, then pinned itself to the far right.

"Ventilation failure," Gerald noted. "Category 4. Imminent risk of suffocation for all biological entities within this sector."

Martha frowned.

"So we wait for Kaelen's monsters to pass out?"

"No," Gerald said. "We fix it. Then we cite him."

He stepped forward. The mud sucked at his boots.

A low growl echoed from the darkness ahead.

Something large and many-legged shifted in the shadows.

A Spider-Queen? A Broodmother?

The name didn't matter. What mattered was the airflow.

If the ventilation was failing, the dungeon's fire suppression system would be compromised. If the suppression was compromised, the structural integrity of the ceiling supports would be at risk from smoke damage. And if the structure was at risk, the entire sub-basement was a death trap.

Gerald walked toward the shadow. The creature hissed, a sound like tearing silk.

"Step back," Martha warned.

"I need to measure the ductwork," Gerald said.

The creature lunged. It was a Skitterling, fast and chitinous, with too many eyes. Martha swung her hammer, cracking its skull against the stone wall. It shrieked and scurried back into the dark.

"Careful," Martha said, breathing hard. "They're nesting near the main junction."

"Good," Gerald said. "That means the junction is occupied. Which means the fire exit is blocked. Which is a Class 3 violation under Ashford Municipal Code Section 7.14."

He pulled his clipboard from his inventory. The paper glowed with a faint blue light.

This was the Citation Form.

It required a physical presence to serve. It required a violation to exist. And it required Gerald to be within ten feet of the offending structure when he stamped it.

He walked faster. The tunnel narrowed. The air grew hotter, thick with the scent of sulfur and burning hair.

"There," Gerald said, pointing to a large circular vent set into the wall.

It was clogged with webs and debris.

"That's the blockage.

It's blocking the return air.

That's why the pressure is building. Kaelen is trying to force fresh air in, but he's not letting the stale air out. He's choking his own dungeon.

"

Martha looked at the vent.

"Can you clear it?"

"I can't clear it," Gerald said. "I can only document it. But I can make the System clear it for me."

He raised the anemometer again. He looked at the vent. He looked at the spider webs.

"Violation: Obstruction of mechanical ventilation system," he muttered. "Severity: Critical. Remedy: Immediate removal of obstruction."

He tapped the clipboard. The blue light pulsed.

A deep, resonant *click* echoed through the tunnel. Not from the System. From the wall.

The vent shuddered. The metal frame groaned.

The spider webs snapped, dissolving into dust as the System's automated enforcement protocols kicked in.

The obstruction wasn't just cleared; it was incinerated.

A burst of sterile, dry air rushed out of the vent, blasting Martha backward.

"What the hell?" Martha shouted, shielding her face.

"Code enforcement," Gerald said calmly. He adjusted his glasses. "The System doesn't like obstructions.

It punishes them. By destroying them.

And by extension, it punishes the Core that allowed them to exist.

"

A scream ripped through the tunnel. Not a monster's scream. A Core's scream.

It was a psychic shriek, a direct assault on the mind.

Kaelen was feeling the damage.

Gerald staggered. His nose bleeded. He wiped it away with his sleeve. His hand shook, just slightly.

"Stay close," he said. "The feedback loop is starting."

They moved deeper.

The tunnel opened into a larger chamber, a rough-hewn cavern that served as Kaelen's central hub. The ceiling was low, pressing down on them. The walls were slick with slime.

And in the center of the room, a massive stone pillar supported the weight of the library above.

The pillar was cracked.

Gerald froze. He stared at the fissure running down the center of the stone. It was thin, barely visible, but it was there. A hairline fracture, spreading like a vein of ice.

"Structural compromise," Gerald whispered.

Martha saw it too.

"Is that... is that from the pressure?"

"Exactly." Gerald pulled out his measuring tape.

He walked to the pillar, and he wrapped the tape around the circumference. He noted the measurement.

He noted the crack's width.

"The ventilation failure caused a pressure buildup in this chamber," Gerald said, his voice gaining speed. "The pressure exerted outward force on the walls.

The walls, being made of soft limestone, expanded.

The pillar, being rigid, did not. The differential created stress. The pillar cracked.

"

He looked up.

The ceiling above the pillar was sagging, just an inch. Maybe two.

It was enough, which was a ticking bomb.

"Kaelen didn't just fail ventilation," Gerald said. "He failed structural support. He's about to collapse the library."

Martha looked at the ceiling.

"We need to leave. Now."

"No," Gerald said. "We need to serve the Citation."

"We can't fight Kaelen here! If the ceiling comes down, we're buried!"

"We don't have to fight him," Gerald said. "We have to cite him. And to cite him, I have to be near the violation. The pillar is the violation."

He walked toward the pillar. The air was vibrating. A low hum filled the room, the sound of stone grinding against stone.

"Gerald, stop!" Martha yelled.

Gerald ignored her. He reached the base of the pillar.

He placed his hand on the cold stone, and he could feel the vibration.

The entire dungeon was shaking.

"Violation: Structural instability due to inadequate support systems," Gerald recited. "Severity: Catastrophic. Remedy: Immediate stabilization or closure of facility."

He raised the stamp.

The ground lurched. A chunk of ceiling fell, smashing into the floor ten feet away. Dust billowed.

Martha grabbed Gerald's arm.

"Gerald! Move!"

"I can't move until the stamp connects," Gerald said. "The System requires contact with the violation. The pillar is the violation."

"Then touch it and run!"

"I'm trying!" Gerald said. He slammed the stamp down.

Nothing happened, and he tried again. The red stamp hit the stone. No light. No *click*.

"What is it?" Martha screamed. The ceiling groaned. Another crack split the stone above them.

"The pillar is load-bearing," Gerald said, his eyes widening. "But it's not just a pillar. It's... it's anchored. Look."

He pointed to the base of the pillar. There were bolts. Huge, rusted iron bolts driven deep into the bedrock. They were holding the stone together.

"It's a temporary shoring," Gerald realized. "Someone bolted it. Recently. Within the last week."

"Who?" Martha asked.

"Doesn't matter. The bolt is the support. The violation is the lack of redundancy.

But if I cite the pillar, the System will try to 'fix' the violation. If the System tries to fix a load-bearing wall without a backup, it will pull the support.

And the ceiling will come down on us.

"

Gerald pulled his hand back. He stepped away from the pillar.

"We have to cite something else," he said. "Something that doesn't hold the roof up."

Martha looked around the room. The walls were slimy. The floor was uneven. There were no exits, save the tunnel they had entered.

"The exit," Martha said. "The only way out is that tunnel. And it's full of monsters."

"No," Gerald said. "The exit is the problem. But the *signage* is the violation."

He pointed to the wall above the tunnel entrance. There was a crude arrow painted in black. It pointed deeper into the dungeon.

"It points the wrong way," Gerald said. "Egress signage must point to the nearest safe exit. The nearest safe exit is back the way we came.

This sign leads to the monster nest.

It's misleading. It's a violation of fire code egress standards.

"

"That's minor," Martha said. "Compared to the ceiling!"

"In the System, violations are cumulative," Gerald said.

"But some violations trigger immediate enforcement. Misleading egress signage is a 'Life Safety' category. The System will correct it.

It will force the dungeon to reveal the true path.

"

Gerald raised the stamp. He aimed not at the pillar, but at the wall. At the painted arrow.

"Gerald, don't!" Martha shouted. The floor tilted. They slid sideways.

He stamped the air.

*Click.*

The blue light flared. The wall shimmered. The black arrow vanished, replaced by a glowing green exit sign. It pointed directly at them.

But that wasn't all.

The System, enforcing the correction, did more than change the sign.

It forced the dungeon to comply.

The tunnel entrance, which had been narrow and blocked by debris, widened. The rubble was swept away by a gust of wind.

The path was clear.

And then, the ceiling stopped groaning.

The pressure dropped. The ventilation, now corrected by the previous citation, stabilized. The structural stress relieved. The crack in the pillar stopped spreading.

It was a hold. A temporary reprieve.

Martha slumped against the wall, gasping.

"You did it. You fixed it."

"I cited it," Gerald corrected. "I didn't fix it. The System fixed it. I just pointed out where it was broken."

He looked at the glowing exit sign. It was bright, cheerful, and inappropriate for a dungeon.

"Kaelen is going to hate this," Gerald said.

"He's going to kill us," Martha said. She pointed to the entrance.

Shadows were pouring in. Not one Skitterling.

Dozens.

Hundreds. And at their front, a figure in black armor. Kaelen himself.

His eyes burned with violet light.

His hands were clenched into fists, wrapped in dark energy.

"You," Kaelen hissed.

His voice was like grinding gravel.

"You touch my walls. You touch my air.

You touch my stone.

"

Gerald straightened his glasses. He picked up his clipboard. He checked his watch.

"Twelve hours left on the citation," Gerald said. "You have twelve hours to file an appeal. Or twelve hours to comply."

Kaelen stepped forward. The ground shook with his steps.

"I don't comply with inspectors. I crush them."

"Then you're in violation of the Core-to-Core Combat Protocol," Gerald said. "Aggression without a formal challenge is a Class 1 breach of conduct. I'm adding it to the citation."

He tapped the clipboard again.

The System reacted instantly.

A beam of white light shot from the ceiling, striking Kaelen's chest. It wasn't damage. It was a restraint. A field of golden bars wrapped around him, locking his arms to his sides.

Kaelen roared, struggling against the bars. The dungeon shook. Monsters screamed and scattered.

"What did you do?" Martha asked, eyes wide.

"Invoked the Neutral Zone," Gerald said.

He was sweating now.

His hands were trembling.

"When a Core attacks an Inspector during an active citation, the System freezes the Core.

It gives them a chance to appeal. Or to surrender.

"

Kaelen strained against the golden bars.

"This won't hold."

"It holds for twenty-four hours," Gerald said. "Long enough for the Inspector General to arrive. Long enough for the audit to begin."

Martha looked at Kaelen.

"He's strong. If he breaks free..."

"He won't break free," Gerald said. "He's cited for five major violations. Ventilation, Egress, Structural, Signage, and Combat Protocol. His Compliance Rating has dropped to 12."

Martha looked at the golden bars. They were humming. Cracks were forming in the gold.

"12 is dead, Gerald," Martha said. "Cores with a Rating below 10 usually self-destruct. They go critical."

Gerald looked at Kaelen. The Core was sweating, dark fluid leaking from his pores. The golden bars were flickering.

"He's not going to self-destruct," Gerald said. "He's going to appeal."

Kaelen's eyes locked onto Gerald.

"I have a loophole," he sneered. "I have an appeal."

"You have twenty-three hours to file," Gerald said.

"I'm filing now."

A chime sounded. Loud. Resonant. It vibrated in Gerald's teeth.

The golden bars vanished.

Kaelen stood free. He smiled, a jagged, terrible expression.

"Appeal accepted," Kaelen said. "Hearing scheduled: Immediate. Location: Here. Participants: You and Me."

Gerald's face went pale. He looked at his clipboard.

The text had changed, and the citation was suspended. The clock had stopped.

"It's a Hearing," Gerald whispered. "He's invoking the Hearing mechanic."

Martha raised her hammer.

"Can you win a hearing?"

Gerald looked at Kaelen. The Core was no longer constrained. The dungeon was no longer stable. The ceiling was groaning again, louder than before.

"I can win anything if I have the code," Gerald said. "But Kaelen didn't just appeal. He invoked a Counter-Citation."

Gerald looked down at his own body. A red light was pulsing from his chest. A violation marker.

"What did you do?" Martha asked.

Kaelen laughed.

"I cited *you*, Inspector. For Unauthorized Entry.

For Trespassing.

And for..." He pointed to the ceiling. "For Causing Structural Damage to a Protected Site."

Gerald looked up. The crack in the pillar was widening. The System recognized the damage. It recognized that Gerald's actions had caused the stress.

It was his fault. By fixing the ventilation, he had changed the pressure. The pressure had cracked the pillar. The pillar was now damaged.

And Kaelen had cited Gerald for it.

"Your Citation Rating is dropping," Kaelen said. "You're becoming non-compliant. And when you're non-compliant, you're fair game."

The golden bars were gone. Kaelen raised his hand. Dark energy coiled around his fingers.

"Gerald," Martha said, stepping in front of him. "We have to run."

"No," Gerald said. He looked at the crack in the pillar. He looked at Kaelen. He looked at his own red pulsing chest.

He had made a mistake. He had applied a rule without considering the cascade effect. He had fixed the air, but broken the stone.

He was the one in violation now.

And the hearing had already begun.
