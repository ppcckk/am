Chapter 134: Unlicensed Puddle

# Chapter 134

The notification arrived before the lights returned.

It did not chime. It did not flash. It simply existed, a block of text superimposed over Gerald's consciousness like a stamp pressed onto wet ink.

**SYSTEM ALERT: INSPECTOR GENERAL AUDIT INITIATED.** **TARGET: CORE 47-B (FORMERLY GERALD HUTCHINS).** **STATUS: UNLICENSED.** **ACTION: IMMEDIATE SEIZURE OF ASSETS PENDING COMPLIANCE REVIEW.**

Gerald's puddle flattened itself against the linoleum. The cold from the library floor bit into his surface tension, but he held the shape.

He had to hold the shape.

If he lost coherence, he became just water. And water did not have standing to file an appeal.

Stanley Miller was still holding his phone. The screen was black. The call had disconnected, but the silence in the library was louder than any siren.

"Who are you talking to?" Stanley whispered. His voice trembled, not with fear, but with the specific, vibrating anxiety of a man who has just realized the rules of his world have changed.

Gerald rippled. He formed letters, jagged and urgent.

**CITE ME.**

Stanley stared at the water. "What?"

Gerald expanded the pool. He drew an arrow pointing to the ceiling, then to the floor, then to Stanley. He formed a new word, larger than the first.

**VIOLATION.**

The lights flickered back on, but they were dim, buzzing with a low, electric hum that set Gerald's teeth on edge.

In the corner of the room, near the circulation desk, the air shimmered. It wasn't a monster.

It wasn't a goblin.

It was a cursor. A glowing, blue arrowhead the size of a dinner plate, hovering in mid-air.

The System's interface had manifested physically.

The cursor moved. It clicked.

A window opened in the air. It was a spreadsheet.

A massive, scrolling grid of data.

Columns labeled *Ventilation*, *Egress*, *Fire Load*, *Occupancy*, *Liability*.

Rows filled with numbers that scrolled too fast to read.

Gerald felt his core contracting. The System was stripping him. Every second, his mana reserves drained, not to fuel spawns, but to pay the fine for existing without a permit.

He needed to pause the clock. He needed to open a case file.

But Gerald was not in his dungeon. He was in the Library. The Library belonged to the City of Ashford. The City of Ashford had jurisdiction over municipal buildings.

Jurisdiction.

That was the loophole.

Gerald didn't try to fight the cursor. He didn't try to attack the System. He attacked the premise. He attacked the premise by filing paperwork.

He focused his will. He pulled moisture from the air, from the carpet, from the sweat on Stanley's forehead. He didn't form letters this time. He formed a document.

It was a **Form 11-B: Application for Temporary Occupancy Permit (Emergency Conditions)**.

The water rose from the floor, defying gravity, solidifying into a rigid, translucent sheet. It hovered at eye level. On its surface, etched in droplets, was the header of the Ashford Department of Buildings.

Stanley backed away, tripping over a stack of encyclopedias. "Is that... is that legal?"

Gerald couldn't speak. He could only display.

The cursor hovered over the water-document. It paused. The System was processing. It was looking for a valid municipal authority to counter-sign.

Gerald had no authority. He was a rogue element. A self-cited hazard.

But Stanley Miller was not.

Stanley was the night manager of the Ashford Municipal Library. He had keys. He had a seal. He had thirty years of institutional memory.

Gerald rippled a new instruction into the water.

**SIGN IT.**

Stanley looked at the floating water. He looked at the giant blue cursor waiting for input. He looked at the dark corners of the library where the shadows seemed to be stretching toward him.

"I can't," Stanley said. "I don't have the authority to license a..." He gestured at the puddle. "A sentient structural anomaly."

Gerald shifted the form. He highlighted the clause at the bottom.

**SECTION 4, PARAGRAPH C: EXIGENT CIRCUMSTANCES.** **IF A STRUCTURAL HAZARD IS IMMEDIATELY LIFE-THREATENING, THE ON-SITE MANAGER MAY GRANT PROVISIONAL AUTHORIZATION TO MAINTAIN STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY UNTIL A FULL REVIEW CAN BE CONDUCTED.**

It was a technicality. A bureaucratic Hail Mary. If Gerald stayed unlicensed, the System would delete him. If he was licensed as a hazard, he gained temporary immunity from deletion, though he remained under probationary watch.

Stanley read the text. His eyes darted left and right. He was calculating the liability.

If he signed, he admitted the library was unsafe. If he didn't sign, the library might collapse, or he might be murdered by whatever was watching from the walls.

"You're a building inspector," Stanley said slowly. "I saw you in the basement. You were measuring the door frames."

Gerald formed a checkmark.

Stanley reached into his pocket. He pulled out a pen. His hand shook, but the pen touched the air, touching the surface of the water-form.

The ink didn't appear. Water repelled ink.

Gerald adjusted. He changed the form to **Digital Verification**. He pulled a stream of water into the shape of a signature box.

"Put your finger there," Gerald signaled with a ripple.

Stanley hesitated. The cursor was growing larger. The buzzing in the lights was getting louder. The floorboards groaned, not from weight, but from the System attempting to evict the tenant.

Stanley pressed his thumb against the water.

A digital chime sounded.

**ACCEPTED.** **USER: S. MILLER.** **ROLE: AUTHORIZED SIGNATORY.** **STATUS: PROVISIONAL LICENSE GRANTED.** **DURATION: 24 HOURS.** **CONDITIONS: NO NEW SPAWNS. NO EXTERNAL EXPANSION. NO UNAUTHORIZED MODIFICATIONS.**

The cursor vanished.

The lights stopped buzzing. They returned to their steady, warm glow.

Gerald collapsed. Not physically—he had no body—but metaphysically. The pressure in his mind released. He sank back into the cracks between the linoleum tiles, spreading thin to conserve energy.

He had bought a day.

But the System was not done.

A new notification popped up in Gerald's peripheral vision. It was not a warning. It was an invitation.

**CASE FILE OPENED: HUTCHINS VS. SYSTEM.** **PRELIMINARY HEARING SCHEDULED.** **LOCATION: VIRTUAL CONSTRUCT [THE ARCHIVE].** **PARTICIPANTS: CORE 47-B, INSPECTOR GENERAL, ADJUDICATOR.**

Gerald understood.

The Audit wasn't over. The license was provisional. Now came the trial.

The Hearing.

And this time, he wouldn't be arguing with a goblin. He would be arguing with the architecture of the world itself.

He had to prepare. He had to gather evidence.

But his dungeon was gone.

The basement beneath the library had been sealed off by the System's cleanup crew moments after Gerald's escape. The walls had been reinforced with concrete that burned to touch. The vents were welded shut.

He had no resources. No spawns. No traps.

He had only Stanley.

Stanley was still standing in the middle of the circulation desk, staring at his thumb. "I need a lawyer," he muttered. "I need a really good lawyer."

Gerald pulled himself together. He formed a small puddle near Stanley's shoe.

**NOT A LAWYER.** **A CODE COMPLIANCE OFFICER.**

Stanley looked down. "Do you know any?"

Gerald formed a single name.

**MARGARET HALE.**

Stanley's face went pale. "Margaret Hale?

She was fired.

She got cited for excessive nitpicking.

She lost her license. She's working at the DMV now."

Gerald formed a new word.

**CALL HER.**

"Are you insane?" Stanley hissed, looking around as if the shelves might be listening. "Hale is a menace. She cited the mayor's house for having a decorative gargoyle that violated setback ordinances. She got fired because she threatened to bulldoze the City Hall annex."

Gerald rippled.

**SHE IS THE ONLY PERSON WHO UNDERSTANDS THE SYSTEM'S LOVE FOR PAPERWORK.**

Stanley rubbed his temples. He looked at the darkened windows of the library. Outside, the streetlights were flickering.

Cars were slowing down. The System was bleeding into the city.

If Gerald didn't win this hearing, the city might be declared non-compliant.

And non-compliant cities were demolished.

Stanley picked up his phone. He dialed from memory.

It rang three times.

"Department of Motor Vehicles, this is Margaret. If you are here to renew your license, please have your insurance card and a utility bill. If you are here to complain, please leave a voicemail. I am currently in the process of appealing my termination."

Stanley looked at the water puddle. Gerald formed a single, glowing symbol: a building inspector's badge.

"It's not a complaint," Stanley said, his voice dropping to a whisper. "It's a referral. For a structural emergency."

There was a long pause on the line. The sound of a clock ticking was audible. Then, a sharp, dry voice.

"Define 'structural emergency.'"

Stanley looked at Gerald. Gerald formed the words: **SENTIENT HAZARD. UNLICENSED. UNDER AUDIT.**

Stanley repeated them.

Silence again. Longer this time. Then, the click of a stamp being removed from a holder.

"Hold the line," Margaret said.

"I have a precedent. Case 409-A. *Hutchins v.

The Void*.

I kept the file."

Gerald felt a jolt of surprise. *Hutchins v. The Void?* He had never filed a case against the Void. He had never even met the Void.

But Margaret Hale had.

Stanley held the line. Gerald sank deeper into the floor, conserving every drop of moisture. He listened to the static on the phone, waiting for Margaret to find the file.

When she spoke again, her tone was different. Colder. Professional.

"Mr. Miller, you need to bring the entity to the Municipal Building.

Basement Level 2.

The old records room.

It's shielded from the System's direct broadcast because it's technically a 'dead zone' for data retention. Bring it before 9:00 AM.

If it isn't there, I will file a motion to dismiss, and the System will wipe it."

"Where is the entity?" Stanley asked.

Gerald formed a map on the floor. A path from the library to the Municipal Building. It was six blocks.

Six blocks through a city that was starting to glitch.

Stanley looked at the map. Then he looked at the clock on the wall. 8:15 AM.

"How do I carry it?" Stanley asked.

Gerald formed a bucket. Or rather, he signaled for Stanley to find one.

Stanley ran. He grabbed a mop bucket from the janitorial closet.

He filled it with cold water.

He poured it onto the floor, collecting Gerald's essence. It was a humiliating process, being scooped up like a spill, but Gerald didn't resist.

He was data. He was property. And property had to be transported to the court.

Stanley carried the bucket to the door. He paused. "The exit door is alarmed. It triggers a police response."

Gerald formed a new plan.

**THE SERVICE ELEVATOR.** **IT HAS A 1998 VENDOR MAINTENANCE TAG.** **THE TAG IS EXPIRED.** **THE SYSTEM DOES NOT RESPECT EXPIRED TAGS.**

Stanley stared at the bucket. "You want me to break the law to save your soul?"

Gerald formed a single sentence.

**I WANT YOU TO FOLLOW THE CODE.**

Stanley walked to the service elevator.

He pressed the button. The doors opened.

The interior was dark, smelling of grease and rust.

He stepped in. He placed the bucket on the floor. He hit the button for Basement Level 2.

The elevator shuddered. It moved down.

Gerald floated in the bucket, watching the numbers change. 1. 0. -1. -2.

The doors opened.

The Municipal Building's basement was not like the library's. It was concrete.

Bare.

Cold.

And it was occupied.

Three figures stood in the center of the room. They were not people. They were avatars. Glowing outlines of men in suits, with briefcases made of light.

In the center stood a woman. Her avatar was sharper, more detailed. She wore glasses made of wireframe. She held a stack of papers that glowed red.

Margaret Hale.

Or rather, Margaret Hale's consciousness, projected into the System's hearing chamber via her DMV terminal.

She looked at the bucket. She looked at Stanley. She looked at the water inside.

"Mr. Hutchins," she said. Her voice echoed in the concrete room.

"You filed a Form 11-B. It was sloppy.

The margins were wrong.

The font size was 10 instead of 11. But..."

She smiled. It was a terrifying smile.

"But you cited yourself. And you found a signatory."

The air in the room changed. The shadows receded. A table appeared. Chairs appeared.

"Sit down," Margaret said. "The Inspector General is running late. But I have already prepared our opening statement."

Gerald formed a word in the bucket.

**WHAT IS THE CHARGE?**

Margaret opened her folder. The pages rustled like wind in a dry field.

"The charge is Unauthorized Existence," she said. "The defense is..."

She paused. She looked at Stanley.

"Mr.

Miller, you signed the form. You are now an accessory to a Class A Regulatory Breach.

If we lose, you go to prison.

Not in a dungeon. In a real one."

Stanley swallowed hard. "What's the defense?"

Margaret turned back to Gerald.

"We argue that you are not a dungeon core," she said. "We argue that you are a municipal employee who has suffered a severe occupational injury resulting in a transfer of consciousness into a static asset. We argue that you are not a hazard. You are an infrastructure upgrade."

Gerald froze.

That was insane. That was bureaucratic insanity. It might just work.

But before Margaret could speak again, the wall behind her exploded.

Not with fire. Not with force.

With data.

A tear in reality ripped open, jagged and purple. Out of the tear stepped a creature.

It was not a monster.

It was a bureaucrat.

A seven-foot-tall figure made of gray stone and stamped with red ink. Its face was a blank slate.

In its hand, it held a stamp the size of a car tire.

**CITATION ENFORCER.** **SUBORDINATE TO THE INSPECTOR GENERAL.**

It had arrived early.

And it was not here for a hearing.

It was here to serve the subpoena.

Margaret Hale's avatar flickered. "They're fast," she said, her voice tight. "Too fast. They didn't wait for the summons."

The Enforcer raised the stamp.

Gerald felt the weight of it. If that stamp hit him, he wouldn't just be deleted. He would be archived. Lost to the system forever.

"Stanley," Gerald formed, his letters shaking.

**RUN.**

Stanley didn't run. He looked at the bucket. He looked at the stamp. He looked at Margaret, who was frantically flipping through pages, looking for a counter-citation.

Stanley Miller, who had never broken a rule in his life, kicked the bucket.

Water splashed across the floor.

Gerald scattered.

He didn't try to reform. He didn't try to fight.

He flowed into the cracks in the concrete.

He flowed into the drain in the corner. He flowed into the municipal sewer system.

He was no longer in the building.

He was in the pipes.

And above him, he heard the Enforcer slam the stamp down on the empty bucket.

The impact shook the floor.

Gerald traveled fast in the dark, slick water. He knew these pipes. He had inspected them. He knew every elbow, every rusted joint.

But he wasn't safe.

Because as he flowed through the main sewer line, he felt a presence blocking the exit.

Not the Enforcer.

Something older.

Something that had been down there since before the library was built.

A core.

Not a new one. Not a rival.

The Original Core of the City of Ashford.

And it was angry.
