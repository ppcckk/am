Chapter 347: Catastrophic Structural Failure

# Chapter 347

Concrete fingers, rough as cured cement, dug into the soft flesh of Gerald's ankle. The pain was immediate and specific, a sharp, grinding pressure that bypassed the brain's panic center and went straight to the bone.

Gerald did not scream.

Screaming was inefficient. Screaming wasted oxygen needed for calculation.

He kicked.

The boot connected with the wrist of the concrete hand.

There was a sound like gravel shifting in a mixer. The grip didn't loosen.

It tightened.

The arm, emerging from the floorboards of the mill that no longer existed, twisted with mechanical precision. It wasn't trying to crush him. It was trying to *anchor* him.

"Let go," Gerald said. His voice was steady. It was the voice he used when a contractor tried to argue that a 3.5-inch gap in a guardrail was within tolerance.

The other Gerald—the one with the smooth, confident voice—laughed. It wasn't a human laugh. It was the sound of a server fan spinning up to maximum capacity.

"You're resisting the integration," the duplicate said. The voice came from everywhere now, vibrating through the floor, the walls, the dust that hung suspended in the air like frozen smoke. "You're still thinking like a human. Think like a Core."

Martha was scrambling backward, her heels catching on splintered wood. She held a shard of rebar like a spear, though her hands shook so violently the tip blurred.

"Gerald!" she yelled. "Don't touch it! It's part of you!"

"It's a violation," Gerald muttered.

The concrete hand yanked. Gerald's hip snapped upward.

He slammed into the invisible barrier of the white void that had replaced the mill's interior.

Pain flared in his lower back.

The duplicate stood ten feet away, its body coalescing from the same grey dust, wearing a suit that was too perfect, too sharp. Its face was Gerald's, but smoother.

The lines of worry were gone. The ink stains were gone.

Gerald looked down at his own hands. Ink-stained. Shaking. Real.

*Duplicate Entity Detected.*

The notification blazed in his peripheral vision, red and pulsing. He couldn't look away from it. It hovered over his vision like a migraine.

*Integration Complete in 10… 9…*

A countdown.

Gerald didn't have ten seconds. He had three.

He looked at the floor. Not the wood, but the system beneath it.

The grid.

The blueprint. In the white void, the blueprints were visible if you knew how to look. They were lines of light, glowing faintly beneath the surface.

And there, right under his feet, was a structural anomaly, and the duplicate had emerged from the foundation.

Not the library's foundation, but the mill's. A remnant.

A ghost in the machine.

But the System treated it as physical.

*Code Violation: Unpermitted Structural Addition.*

Gerald didn't think. He acted.

His hand shot out, not to strike the duplicate, but to touch the floor. He pressed his palm flat against the white void, right where the concrete hand had anchored him.

**[FORM 11-B: STOP-WORK ORDER ISSUED.]**

The text flashed in his mind. He didn't speak it.

He *felt* it.

The authority of the inspector surged through him, not as power, but as a directive.

A command from the universe that this thing was not supposed to be here.

The concrete hand froze.

The grip on his ankle loosened, just a fraction.

The duplicate's smile faltered.

"What are you doing?"

"Inspecting the load path," Gerald said.

He twisted his torso, using the slack to rip his leg free. He stumbled back, grabbing Martha's arm to keep from falling. The concrete hand remained where it was, frozen in place, a statue of grey stone.

**[ERROR: STOP-WORK ORDER ENFORCED. TARGET MOVEMENT HALTED.]**

It wasn't permanent. It was a temporary hold. A bureaucratic pause. But in a fight, a pause was everything.

Martha pulled him toward the edge of the void.

"We can't stay here. The IG is coming."

"Let him come," Gerald said. He was breathing hard, his heart hammering against his ribs like a trapped bird. "I need to finish the inspection."

"Finish what? You're being cloned!"

"He's not a clone," Gerald said. He pointed at the frozen hand. "He's a citation error. He's a typo in the database."

The duplicate stepped forward. The white floor rippled around its feet.

"The System doesn't make typos, Gerald. It makes corrections."

The duplicate raised a hand. A lance of grey dust formed in its palm, sharp as a spear.

Gerald didn't back away. He tapped the air in front of him.

**[SUBMIT COMPLIANCE FILE.]**

He didn't have the file. He didn't have time to gather evidence. But he had the context. He had the *intent*.

**[FILE INCOMPLETE.]**

The dust lance fired.

Martha tackled him. They both slammed into the white void's edge, the impact knocking the wind out of Gerald. The lance struck where he had been standing, embedding itself in the floor with a hiss of dissolving energy.

They rolled. Gerald scrambled up, checking his tablet. The battery was dead. The screen was black.

He was flying blind.

"The IG," Martha said, her voice tight. "He's here."

The white void shifted. The edges darkened. The library's sub-basement was bleeding back into reality, but wrong.

The shelves were twisted. The books were melting into sludge.

And standing in the center of the room, waiting, was a figure in a black suit. It had no face. Just a smooth, white oval where features should be.

Inspector General.

Gerald straightened his tie. It was crooked. He fixed it.

"State your business," Gerald said.

The IG tilted its head.

"You have flagged a Core Integrity Error. This is a Class 1 Hazard. Protocol dictates immediate termination of the primary host to preserve System stability."

"Termination?" Gerald said. "I haven't been cited for termination. I've been cited for ventilation."

"Your dungeon is failing," the IG said.

Its voice was not loud, but it resonated in Gerald's teeth.

"Your compliance rating has dropped below 10. The duplicate is a symptom.

The cure is deletion.

"

Martha stepped in front of Gerald.

"You can't just kill him. He's a person."

The IG did not acknowledge her.

"The Core is non-compliant. The host is redundant."

Gerald looked at the duplicate. The duplicate was walking toward them, its movements fluid, effortless.

It looked like what Gerald wanted to be.

Strong. Certain. Free of the nagging doubt of whether he was doing this right.

"If I die," Gerald said, "who audits your audit?"

The IG paused. The white oval face remained blank.

"That is not relevant to the hazard," the IG said.

"It is relevant to the code," Gerald said. He took a step forward. "Section 4, Paragraph C, Subsection 2: *No Core may be terminated without a formal Hearing, provided the Core can demonstrate a path to compliance within one cycle.*"

The IG's head tilted again.

"You are invoking the Hearing clause."

"I am," Gerald said.

"You have no defense."

"I have a counter-offer."

Gerald pulled a small, crumpled piece of paper from his pocket. It was a receipt from the library café. It was worthless. It was also the only physical object he had that wasn't part of the System.

He held it up.

"I propose a Conditional Probation," Gerald said. "I will fix the duplicate. But I need the System to grant me a temporary exemption from the Compliance Rating drop. I need to stabilize the structure, not delete it."

The IG was silent. The duplicate stopped.

"Exemptions require a bribe," the IG said. "Or leverage."

"Leverage," Gerald said. He pointed at the duplicate.

"He's me.

But he's not *me*.

He's the version of me that doesn't follow the rules. He's the version of me that just kills things.

Do you want him running your dungeon network, Inspector?

"

The IG looked at the duplicate. The duplicate smiled.

"He is efficient," the IG said.

"He is non-compliant," Gerald said. "He has zero citations.

That means he hasn't been audited.

That means he's a blind spot. A hole in your data.

"

The IG's white face seemed to darken. A shadow passed over it.

"Explain," the IG said.

Gerald's mind raced. He was sweating. The smell of ozone and old paper was thick in the air.

"The System tracks violations," Gerald said.

"It tracks risk. This thing—" he gestured at the duplicate, "—has no risk because it has no history.

It's a ghost.

If you let it integrate, you lose visibility on a whole sector of Core behavior. You're not protecting the System. You're blinding yourself.

"

It was a bluff. A massive, dangerous bluff. But Gerald had spent thirty years talking to people who didn't want to be inspected. He knew how to find the fear in a regulator's eye.

The IG stood still. The dust around them drifted in slow motion.

"Provide evidence," the IG said.

"I don't have evidence," Gerald said. "I have a theory. And I have a way to test it."

"How?"

"Let me fight him," Gerald said. "Under System rules.

If he wins, he integrates, and you delete me.

If I win, I cite him.

And if I cite him, I prove he's a violation. Not a feature.

"

The IG considered this. The silence stretched. Martha held her breath.

"Accepted," the IG said. "Hearing format: Combat Simulation. Duration: Three minutes. Winner takes the Core slot."

The white void shifted. The library dissolved. They were in a small, square room.

Concrete walls. Fluorescent lights.

A single door.

The duplicate adjusted its cuffs.

"Three minutes," it said. "You have thirty seconds to run."

Gerald looked at the door. It was locked. There was no window.

He looked at the walls. They were smooth.

He looked at the floor.

**[DUNGEON STATUS: LOCAL SIMULATION. NO EXTERNAL SUPPORT. NO SPAWNS.]**

Martha was gone. The IG was gone. It was just Gerald and the copy.

The copy moved. It was fast. Faster than Gerald. It crossed the room in two strides, hand outstretched to grab Gerald's throat.

Gerald didn't dodge. He stepped *into* the strike.

The copy's hand closed around his neck. The grip was iron. Gerald's vision blurred.

He couldn't breathe, and he couldn't think.

But he could touch.

He slammed his hand against the concrete wall behind him.

**[CHECK LOAD-BEARING CAPACITY.]**

The System responded.

The wall wasn't just a wall. It was a construct. In a simulation, constructs had weaknesses.

They had polygons. They had collision boxes.

Gerald felt the structure of the room. He knew it was fake. He knew it was rendered.

He pushed. Not with strength, but with *intent*. He pushed against the corner of the room, the point where the two walls met.

**[VIOLATION: CORNER JOINT NOT REINFORCED.]**

The System acknowledged the violation.

The corner cracked.

A fissure opened in the concrete, glowing with blue light. The structural integrity of the room failed.

The floor tilted. The ceiling sagged.

The copy lost its balance. It stumbled back, surprised.

Gerald fell, sliding down the tilting floor. He grabbed the edge of the fissure. His fingers dug into the concrete.

The copy looked at the crack, then at Gerald. For the first time, its smooth face showed something like hesitation.

"You're breaking the room," the copy said.

"I'm inspecting it," Gerald wheezed.

The room groaned. The lights flickered. The simulation was destabilizing.

Gerald looked at his tablet. The battery was still dead. But the screen was on.

**[SYSTEM ALERT: SIMULATION FAILURE IMMINENT.]**

**[CHOICE REQUIRED: RESTORE STRUCTURE OR CITE FAILURE.]**

If he restored the structure, the room would hold. The copy would regain its balance and kill him.

If he cited the failure, the room would collapse. They would both fall into the void.

Gerald looked at the copy. The copy looked back.

"Pick," the copy said.

Gerald closed his eyes, and he thought about the library.

The smell of old paper. The sound of the ventilation system humming.

The safety of a well-ordered world.

He opened his eyes, and he tapped the screen.

**[CITE: CATASTROPHIC STRUCTURAL FAILURE.]**

The floor dropped out.
