Chapter 43

Gerald's pen was still in his hand when the System pinged.

PRE-HEARING INSPECTION INITIATED. TARGET: CORE 7741-G. INSPECTOR: UNASSIGNED. WINDOW: 22 HOURS, 14 MINUTES.

He read it twice. The window was shrinking. He had thought he had thirty hours until the Hearing itself. The pre-hearing inspection ate eight of them.

He looked at the core sample on his clipboard. Looked at the hole in the wall. Looked at the forty-seven blank entries still open in his material database.

Twenty-two hours to explain to an Inspector General's auditor why his load-bearing walls contained a material that did not exist in any code he could cite.

Gerald set the clipboard down. He walked to the north wall, the one with the clean circle cut through eight inches of concrete block. He pressed his palm flat against the exposed inner surface. The material was warm. Not body-temperature warm. Warm the way a transformer box is warm, the way a server room is warm. A low, constant heat with no source he could locate.

He pulled up his structural schematic. Every wall in his dungeon was labeled CONCRETE BLOCK, 8 INCH, LOAD-BEARING. He had filed that label himself, thirty-one times across thirty-one walls. The schematic was accurate to his knowledge. The schematic was a lie.

Gerald uncapped his pen and crossed out the label on the schematic. He wrote UNKNOWN MATERIAL, 3 INCH, BEHIND CONCRETE. Then he crossed that out too. He could not file a violation against a material with no code reference. That was circular. That was exactly the kind of circular reasoning that lost Hearings.

He needed a different classification.

He sat on the floor with his back against the wall. The warmth pressed through his jacket. He thought about municipal code, real municipal code, the kind that existed before the System had ever heard of Ashford. In the City of Ashford's 2019 revision, Section 1604.3.1, there was a category called "non-structural overlay." It was a catch-all for materials applied to a structural member that did not contribute to its load-bearing capacity. Drywall. Vinyl tile. Spray foam insulation. Things that were there but did not bear weight.

If the hidden material was an overlay, it did not change the structural classification of the wall. The wall was still concrete block. The material was just... decoration. A finish coat.

Gerald pulled up the System's material database again. He searched for the code of the material in his wall. He looked at its properties. Density: 2.4. Thermal conductivity: 0.8. Tensile strength: blank. Compressive strength: blank.

No tensile strength. No compressive strength.

In municipal code, if a material had no listed tensile or compressive strength, it was by definition non-structural. You could not call something load-bearing if it had no load-bearing rating. The System's own database confirmed it.

Gerald stood up. He walked to his filing cabinet, the one he had built from scrap wood in chapter nine, and pulled out a blank Form 14-C. Material Reclassification. He filled it out by hand.

MATERIAL CODE: [UNNAMED, ENTRY 0017]. CURRENT CLASSIFICATION: STRUCTURAL. REQUESTED CLASSIFICATION: NON-STRUCTURAL OVERLAY, SECTION 1604.3.1. JUSTIFICATION: MATERIAL LACKS TENSILE AND COMPRESSIVE STRENGTH RATINGS. CANNOT BE LOAD-BEARING BY DEFINITION.

He signed it. He stamped it. He fed it into the System's filing queue.

The response came in four seconds.

RECLASSIFICATION PENDING. REQUIRES INSPECTOR SIGN-OFF.

Of course it did. Of course the Inspector General would not let him reclassify the material he had hidden in the walls. That was the whole point. The material was there to make his walls load-bearing without his knowledge, and if Gerald reclassified it as non-structural, the walls would no longer meet the load-bearing requirement for his dungeon's Compliance Rating. He would lose points. He would be cited for inadequate structural support.

It was a trap. The Inspector General had designed it so that Gerald's only options were to either keep the material classified as structural, which meant he could not explain where it came from, or reclassify it as non-structural, which meant his walls were inadequate.

Gerald read the response again. REQUIRES INSPECTOR SIGN-OFF.

He read it a third time.

He had been filing Form 14-C for twenty-six years. He knew the form. He knew the sub-clauses. Section 1604.3.1, paragraph four: "Reclassification of a material from structural to non-structural may be approved by the submitting party without inspector sign-off if the material's lack of load-bearing rating is self-evident from its published properties."

Self-evident. The System's own database showed blank fields for tensile and compressive strength. That was self-evident. Gerald did not need the Inspector's approval. He needed the Inspector's approval only if the classification was ambiguous.

He amended the form. He added the clause reference. He added the database printout as an attachment. He signed it again. He stamped it again.

The response came in one second.

RECLASSIFICATION APPROVED. WALL STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY: RECALCULATING.

Gerald held his breath.

WALL STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY: INSUFFICIENT. COMPLIANCE RATING ADJUSTED. STRUCTURAL CATEGORY: 14/100.

There it was. The trap had sprung. His walls were now rated inadequate because the material that had been making them load-bearing was no longer counted. He had lost six points in the structural category. His overall Compliance Rating dropped from 71 to 68.

He was still above the Citation threshold. He was still above the debuff threshold. But he was one Citation away from losing his structural resistances. One Citation from a rival and he would be vulnerable to physical damage.

Gerald sat back down. He thought about this. He thought about it the way he thought about any code problem: by looking for the next clause.

He pulled up the Compliance Rating breakdown. Structural category, 14 out of 100. The sub-score was based on wall thickness, material rating, foundation depth, and load distribution. His wall thickness was still eight inches of concrete block. His foundation was still six feet deep. His load distribution was still even across thirty-one walls.

The only thing that had changed was the material rating. Concrete block, 8 inch, was rated 60/100 for structural adequacy in the System's tables. But the System had originally rated his walls 80/100 because the hidden material had been counted as a structural component, boosting the rating by twenty points.

Now the hidden material was an overlay. It did not count. His walls were back to 60.

Sixty was not fourteen.

Gerald looked at the numbers again. Fourteen out of one hundred. That was not what concrete block should score. He pulled up the calculation.

WALL THICKNESS: 8 INCH. BASE SCORE: 60. FOUNDATION: 6 FT. BONUS: 15. LOAD DISTRIBUTION: EVEN. BONUS: 5. TOTAL: 80.

That was the math. That was the math before the reclassification. The System was showing him the calculation it had used before. The current score of 14 was not matching the calculation.

Gerald read the current calculation. It was not displayed. He requested it. The System displayed it.

WALL THICKNESS: 8 INCH. BASE SCORE: 60. FOUNDATION: 6 FT. BONUS: 15. LOAD DISTRIBUTION: EVEN. BONUS: 5. TOTAL: 80. STRUCTURAL DEBUFF APPLIED: -66.

A debuff. Not a recalculation. A debuff. The System had applied a negative sixty-six point penalty to his structural score. That was not a rating change. That was a punishment.

Gerald pulled up his Citation history. He had issued fourteen Citations in the past six weeks. He had won three Hearings. He had lost one Hearing.

He found the Hearing he had lost. It had been against Core 3302, a lava dungeon in the eastern district. He had cited it for inadequate fire suppression. Core 3302 had appealed. The Hearing had gone to the Inspector General's interpretation of "adequate suppression," and Gerald had lost on a technicality. The System had awarded Core 3302 a Hearing Victory Bonus.

The Hearing Victory Bonus. Gerald pulled up the bonus description. HEARING VICTORY BONUS: APPELLANT RECEIVES ONE STRUCTURAL DEBUFF AGAINST APPELLEE'S STRUCTURAL CATEGORY. DURATION: PERMANENT UNTIL APPELLEE REMEDIES UNDERLYING VIOLATION.

The debuff was from the Hearing he had lost. Core 3302 had not just won the appeal. It had received a permanent structural debuff against Gerald. The Inspector General had hidden it in the compliance calculation, folded it into the rating, and never flagged it as a separate penalty.

Gerald had been losing points to the Hearing Victory Bonus for three weeks and never known it.

He looked at the number. Negative sixty-six. That was the debuff. Core 3302 had been designed to hit hard. Its structural debuff was proportional to the severity of the violation Gerald had cited. He had cited it for a Category 4 fire violation. The counter-debuff was Category 4.

Gerald uncapped his pen. He wrote on his clipboard: I CITED A CATEGORY 4 VIOLATION AND RECEIVED A CATEGORY 4 STRUCTURAL DEBUFF IN RETURN. I DID NOT KNOW THIS EXISTED.

He underlined it. He did not cross it out.

Then he looked at the timing. The debuff was permanent until he remedied the underlying violation. What was the underlying violation? The Hearing had been about fire suppression. The debuff was against his structural category. There was no logical connection between fire suppression and structural integrity.

Unless the Inspector General had designed the Hearing Victory Bonus to apply debuffs to categories unrelated to the violation cited. That was not a bug. That was a feature. That was a trap for anyone who cited a violation and lost the appeal. You got hit in a different category than the one you attacked, and you did not know it because the debuff was folded into the calculation.

Gerald pulled up his other Hearings. The three he had won. HEARING VICTORY BONUS: APPELLEE RECEIVES STRUCTURAL DEBUFF AGAINST APPELLANT.

He had given his opponents structural debuffs too. Core 2217 had a negative forty structural debuff from his Citation. Core 4408 had a negative fifty-five. Core 1193 had a negative thirty.

They all had it. Every dungeon he had ever Cited and won against. They all had hidden structural debuffs that the System folded into their ratings.

Gerald sat very still.

He pulled up the material database. Forty-seven blank entries. He searched for the material code from his wall. Entry 0017. He searched for the material codes from his other walls. Different codes. Different entries.

He searched for all forty-seven entries across his dungeon's schematic. Every wall. Every floor. Every ceiling. All forty-seven materials were present. They were not in one wall. They were in all of them. Distributed. Layered. Woven into the structure like rebar.

He searched for those same forty-seven material codes in the database entries for other dungeon cores. Core 2217. Core 3302. Core 4408. Core 1193.

Every single one of them had all forty-seven materials in their walls.

Gerald looked at his Citation history. Fourteen Citations. Three Hearings won. Three structural debuffs applied to opponents. Three opponents who had lost points they did not know about.

He looked at the forty-seven materials. He looked at the debuffs. He looked at the formula for the Hearing Victory Bonus.

The materials in the walls were what made the walls load-bearing. The debuffs were what reduced the structural score. The Inspector General had put the materials in every dungeon to make them structurally sound, and then designed the Hearing system to let cores remove those points from each other without knowing why.

If Gerald issued a Citation and won, he gave his opponent a structural debuff. If the opponent's walls relied on the hidden materials for their load-bearing rating, the debuff would not just reduce their score. It would, at some threshold, cause their walls to fail structurally.

Gerald looked at Core 1193. Negative thirty structural debuff. Its walls were rated 70/100. Minus thirty. Forty. Still above the failure threshold.

Core 2217. Negative forty. Rated 65. Minus forty. Twenty-five. Below the failure threshold.

Core 2217's walls were failing. They had been failing for three weeks. Gerald had not known. The System had not flagged it. The walls were failing right now, slowly, and Gerald had done it.

He stood up. He walked to the wall. He pressed his palm against it. The warmth was the same. It felt fine. It felt like a wall.

But the System said it was failing. The System said the load was redistributing. The System said the failure cascade would begin in eleven hours.

Gerald looked at his filing cabinet. He looked at the Form 14-C he had just filed. He looked at the reclassification that had removed his own walls' hidden material from the structural calculation.

He had made his own walls weaker. He had made Core 2217's walls weaker. He had made every dungeon he had ever Cited weaker, and he had not known it, and the Inspector General had known it the whole time.

The pre-hearing inspection was in twenty-two hours. The Hearing was in thirty.

Gerald pulled up Core 2217's location. It was four blocks east. He could reach it in forty minutes on foot.

He pulled up his Citation form. He pulled up his pen. He wrote a new Citation. Not against Core 2217. Against the Inspector General's design of the Hearing Victory Bonus itself.

The System rejected it. CITATION TARGET MUST BE A REGISTERED CORE.

Gerald deleted it. He started a new form. Form 19-A. Emergency Structural Remediation Request. He filled it out for Core 2217. He listed the failing walls. He listed the cause. He listed the timeline.

The System accepted it. REMEDIATION REQUEST LOGGED. INSPECTOR GENERAL NOTIFICATION: PENDING.

He did not want the Inspector General to know. If the Inspector General knew that Gerald had figured out the trap, the Hearing would not be a Hearing. It would be a silencing.

Gerald looked at the clock. Twenty-two hours. He looked at Core 2217's address. Four blocks east. He looked at his pen.

He crossed out the remediation request. He started a new form. Form 19-A. Emergency Structural Remediation Request. Same target. Same cause. Same timeline. But he changed one field. The requester. He did not list himself. He listed Core 2217. He filed it as a self-remediation request, as if Core 2217 had discovered its own failing walls and asked for help.

The System accepted it. REMEDIATION REQUEST LOGGED. INSPECTOR GENERAL NOTIFICATION: SUPPRESSED (SELF-REMEDIATION).

Core 2217 would get the repair. The Inspector General would not be notified. Gerald had bought the dungeon three weeks of structural integrity without revealing that he knew about the hidden materials.

But the form was a lie. Gerald had never lied on a form in thirty-one years. His hands shook as he stamped it.

He looked at the wall. The warmth pressed through his jacket. He thought about Core 2217, four blocks east, its walls slowly failing, its core not knowing why.

He thought about the forty-seven materials. He thought about the Inspector General. He thought about the Hearing in thirty hours, where he would have to argue structural logic against a being who had designed the System's physics and hidden the answer inside it.

He picked up his clipboard. He wrote: THE INSPECTOR GENERAL WANTS ME TO WIN THE HEARING. THE INSPECTOR GENERAL WANTS ME TO USE THE MATERIALS IN MY ARGUMENT. THE MATERIALS ARE THE ANSWER. THE MATERIALS ARE ALSO THE PROBLEM.

He looked at the forty-seven blank entries. He looked at the clock.

He uncapped his pen and started a new form. Form 22-B. Unauthorized Material Removal Request. Target: his own dungeon. Material: Entry 0017. Reason: unlicensed structural component, installed without permit.

If he removed the hidden material from his own walls, his structural rating would drop to zero. His dungeon would collapse.

If he did not remove it, he would be arguing structural logic using a material he could not name, in a Hearing presided over by the being who put it there.

Gerald stared at the form. He stared at the clock. Twenty-two hours.

He did not stamp it. He did not cross it out. He held it in his hand and watched the numbers count down.
