Chapter 381: Sub-Contractor Unlicensed

# Chapter 381

Gerald Hutchins dangled in the void.

The air around him was not air. It was thick, sulfurous, and tasting of copper.

The fall had stopped abruptly, halted by a web of glowing red filaments that snapped taut beneath his boots.

He did not scream. Screaming wasted oxygen, and oxygen was a finite resource in a poorly ventilated space.

Above him, the hole in the floor of the rival dungeon remained open. Through it, he could see the silhouette of the flame-woman. She stood with her arms crossed, the edges of her cloak curling like smoke rings.

"Gravity," she said.

Her voice echoed, bouncing off the unseen walls of the chasm.

"A simple concept.

I enjoy watching things fall.

It simplifies the menu.

"

Gerald adjusted his grip on the red strands. They were hot.

Not burningly hot, but uncomfortably warm, like a radiator in winter.

He checked his wrist.

The System interface hovered in his peripheral vision, translucent and stubborn.

**STATUS: SUSPENDED.** **COMPLIANCE RATING: 47/100 (DEBUFF: MILD DISORIENTATION).**

"Ms. Core," Gerald said. His voice was steady.

It always was. Panic was inefficient.

Panic ruined the penmanship on Form 12-C.

"I am currently suspended in a confined space with no visible means of egress. This is a Class B confinement violation."

The woman tilted her head. The fire in her eyes flared brighter.

"You are dangling," she pointed out.

"I am contained," Gerald corrected.

"By unauthorized structural elements. Your floor lacks load-bearing support for the void space below it.

If a heavy adventurer were to fall through this hole, the tension on these webs would be insufficient.

They would snap. The adventurer would hit the bottom. That constitutes a fatality caused by structural negligence.

"

"Adventurers are not my responsibility," she said. She stepped closer to the edge of the hole.

Her face was inches from his.

"They are pests.

You are a pest.

"

"Under the Shared Infrastructure Act, subsection 4, you are responsible for the safety of any person who enters your dungeon via a breach in the boundary wall," Gerald said. "I entered via a breach. Therefore, I am a person. Therefore, I am your responsibility."

The woman laughed. It was a harsh, scraping sound.

"You are trying to bore me to death."

"I am trying to serve you," Gerald said. He reached into his vest pocket. The citation form was there.

It was a small, laminated card. He pulled it out.

The System recognized it.

A golden glow emanated from the paper.

**ACTION: SERVE CITATION.**

The woman hissed. The smoke around her body thickened, forming tendrils that whipped toward him. One tendril lashed out, striking the web he hung from.

The web shuddered. A thread snapped.

Gerald swung slightly. He kept his balance.

"If you strike the messenger," Gerald said, "the citation becomes immediate and non-appealable. You will suffer a 15% reduction in all thermal output for six hours."

"You think I care about heat?" she sneered. "I *am* heat."

"You care about efficiency," Gerald said. "And you care about your Compliance Rating. Look at your HUD. It's flashing red."

The woman glanced at her own interface. It was, indeed, flashing. A warning icon pulsed in her mind's eye.

*WARNING: UNRESOLVED CITATION. HEARING SCHEDULED: T-MINUS 00:04:30.*

"A hearing?" she spat. "In the middle of a void?"

"It is mandatory," Gerald said. "You cannot avoid it. You can only participate. If you do not, the System assumes a plea of 'No Contest.' The debuff is permanent until you fix the violation."

"Fix it," she said. "You are the inspector. You fix it."

"I am an inspector. I do not repair. I identify."

Gerald looked up at the hole. He looked down at the dark abyss below. Then he looked at the woman.

"I need you to pull me up," Gerald said.

"No."

"Then I will cite you for failure to assist an injured party."

"I have not injured you."

"I am suspended. That is an injury of status. My dignity is compromised."

The woman narrowed her eyes.

"You are absurd. A small, gray thing, hanging from a spiderweb, talking about dignity."

"I am a municipal employee," Gerald said. "We have tenure."

The woman hesitated.

The fire around her dimmed slightly. She was calculating.

He could see it, and she was weighing the cost of fighting him versus the cost of the debuff.

"Pull me up," she repeated. "And I will give you two minutes. If you cite me after two minutes, I will burn you."

"Agreed."

The woman raised a hand. A gust of hot wind erupted from the hole above.

It caught Gerald, lifting him.

The red webs loosened, and he floated upward, weightless, drifting toward the edge of the dungeon floor.

He landed on solid stone. He stood up. He brushed the dust from his trousers. He clicked his pen.

The woman stood ten feet away. Her form was shifting. One moment she was a woman in a dress of flame; the next, a swirling vortex of embers.

"Start talking," she said. "Why are you here? You could have cited me from across the border. Why risk the drop?"

Gerald looked at the hole in the floor. He pulled out his tape measure. He walked to the edge. He lowered the tape.

"Depth?" he asked.

"Fifty feet," the woman said. "A pit of magma. My favorite feature."

"Magma is not a valid egress route for humans," Gerald said. "It is a hazard. You have no ladder.

No railing. No warning signage.

"

"I have monsters," she said. "They warn the humans by eating them."

"That is not a code-compliant warning system."

Gerald walked back to the woman. He held up the citation.

"I am citing you for three violations," he said. "One: Lack of fall protection in hazardous areas. Two: Inadequate ventilation in the lower levels, evidenced by the heat buildup. Three: Failure to maintain the structural integrity of the shared wall."

The woman smiled. It was a cruel smile.

"Serve it," she said. "Serve it and die."

Gerald did not serve it.

Instead, he pointed to the ceiling.

"Look," he said.

The woman looked up. The ceiling above them was cracked. Orange light leaked through.

"That leak," Gerald said.

"It is not just heat. It is pressure. The dungeon below us—the one I own—has a water leak.

The steam is building up, which is pushing against your ceiling. If I do not relieve the pressure, your ceiling collapses.

And if your ceiling collapses, your magma pit loses its containment vessel.

"

The woman froze.

"What are you talking about?"

"I am talking about hydrostatic pressure," Gerald said. "Your dungeon is sealed. My dungeon is leaking.

The steam is trying to escape into your space. It is a shared pressure vessel.

"

"You are bluffing."

"Measure it," Gerald said. "Put your hand on the wall. Feel the vibration. That is the sound of glass breaking."

The woman approached the wall. She placed a hand of fire against the cold stone. She closed her eyes.

A low hum vibrated through the floor. A hairline fracture appeared in the stone near her feet. It grew. A puff of white steam hissed into the room.

The woman jumped back.

"You are sabotaging me!" she shouted.

"I am allowing the physics of the environment to take its course," Gerald said.

"If you fix your ventilation, the pressure equalizes.

If you kill me, the pressure remains. Your ceiling fails.

Your magma leaks. Your Compliance Rating drops to zero. The System deletes you.

"

The woman stared at the crack. The steam hissed louder.

"You are a villain," she whispered.

"I am an inspector," Gerald said. "I am neutral. The code is the code."

"Fix it," she said. Her voice was tight. "Fix the leak in your dungeon. Stop the steam."

Gerald looked at the citation in his hand. He looked at the woman.

"If I fix the leak, I save you," Gerald said. "But I have already drafted Citation 4099-A.

It is active, and the debuff is applied the moment the citation is served. I have not served it yet.

"

"Serve it now," she said. "And fix the leak."

"No," Gerald said.

"I will serve it when you admit that your dungeon is structurally unsound. I need a written statement.

On paper.

Signed by the Core.

"

"I cannot sign paper. I am made of fire."

"You can sign with a stylus. The System accepts electronic signatures."

The woman trembled. The steam in the room was getting thicker. The crack in the wall widened. A chunk of stone fell from the ceiling and shattered on the floor.

"Fine," she said. She spat the word out. "Give me the form."

Gerald handed her the laminated card. He handed her a stylus.

She took it. Her fingers scorched the edges of the card, but she did not let it burn. She pressed the stylus to the signature line.

**CITATION SERVED.**

A blue light flashed in the room. The woman gasped. Her flames dimmed. The orange glow in her eyes faded to a dull red.

**DEBUFF APPLIED: THERMAL OUTPUT -15%.** **DEBUFF APPLIED: STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY -10%.**

"Now," the woman said, her voice weaker. "Stop the steam."

Gerald pulled out his clipboard.

"I cannot stop the steam immediately," Gerald said. "I have to file a repair order. It takes time."

"You are killing me," she said. She slumped against the wall. The crack was growing.

"I am delaying the inevitable," Gerald said. "But I can offer a temporary mitigation. I can reverse the flow."

The woman looked up.

"How?"

"I can install a damper in my own ventilation system," Gerald said. "It will pull the steam out of the shared wall. It will relieve the pressure on your ceiling."

"Do it," she said. "Do it now."

"I will do it," Gerald said. "But I require a consideration."

The woman laughed. It was a dry, crackling sound.

"A consideration?

You have cited me.

You have debuffed me. What do you want?

"

"I want access," Gerald said.

"Access to what?"

"To your dungeon's electrical grid," Gerald said. "My dungeon has no power. My ventilation fans are manual.

They are inefficient. Your dungeon has a lightning-based power source, does it not?

I saw the sparks in the walls when I entered.

"

The woman stared at him.

"You want to steal my power."

"I want to tap it," Gerald said. "Legally. I will install a shared junction box.

It will be code-compliant.

It will provide you with a backup power source for your lava pumps. In exchange, I get steady current for my fans.

"

"That is... illegal," the woman said. "Sharing a power grid between rival cores is a Level 3 offense."

"It is a Level 3 offense only if unapproved," Gerald said. "If we file a Joint Utility Agreement, it is legal. The System encourages infrastructure sharing to reduce overall energy consumption."

The woman looked at the crack in the wall. The steam was hissing violently now. Her ceiling groaned.

"You are insane," she said.

"I am pragmatic," Gerald said. "Sign the agreement, or let your ceiling collapse."

She screamed. It was a sound of pure frustration. Flames erupted from her hands, scorching the floor.

She grabbed the stylus again. She slammed it onto a second piece of paper Gerald had produced. A form. **JOINT UTILITY AGREEMENT: FORM 88-C.**

She signed it.

Gerald took the paper. He placed it in his clipboard.

**AGREEMENT FILED.** **STATUS: PENDING APPROVAL.**

"The approval process takes twenty-four hours," Gerald said.

"Twenty-four hours?" the woman shrieked. "My ceiling will be gone in ten minutes!"

"Not if you turn off your lava pumps," Gerald said. "Turn them off. The heat source stops. The pressure drops."

"If I turn them off, my dungeon cools. My monsters weaken."

"Your monsters will survive. They will be cold. But they will be alive. And your dungeon will not collapse."

The woman looked at the ceiling. The crack was a chasm now. Dust fell like snow.

She raised her hands. The fire in the room died. The orange glow faded. The heat vanished.

The steam stopped hissing. The pressure equalized.

The woman slumped to the floor. She looked smaller. Dimmer.

"You win," she said. "For now."

Gerald looked at his clipboard. He made a checkmark.

"I have not won," Gerald said. "I have simply ensured compliance."

He turned to leave. He walked toward the hole in the floor.

"Where are you going?" the woman asked. Her voice was weak.

"Back to my dungeon," Gerald said. "I have a leak to fix. And I have a new utility box to install."

"You cannot leave," the woman said. "The hearing."

Gerald stopped. He turned back.

"What hearing?"

"The appeal," she said.

"You cited me. You applied the debuff. But the Joint Utility Agreement changes the context.

I am appealing the citation. I am arguing that the citation was invalid because it was served under duress.

"

Gerald felt a cold knot in his stomach. He hated hearings. He hated the improvisation required.

"Duress?" Gerald said. "I was hanging from a web. You threatened to drop me."

"I was protecting my dungeon," the woman said. "The System recognizes self-preservation as a valid defense against citation if the citation threatens structural collapse."

Gerald looked at the System interface.

**HEARING REQUESTED.** **OPPONENT: CORE 'EMBERHEART'.** **ARGUMENT: DURESS / SELF-PRESERVATION.**

**PROCEED?**

Gerald clicked **YES**. He had no choice. If he refused, the citation would be vacated by default.

The room dissolved. The stone floor vanished. The air vanished.

Gerald was standing in a white void. A long table sat before him. On one side, a chair. On the other, another chair.

Emberheart sat in the opposite chair, and she was no longer a swirling vortex of fire.

She was a human woman. She looked tired.

She wore a suit made of ash.

Across from her sat a figure in a black robe. The Faceless Judge. The Inspector General.

The Judge did not speak. He simply pointed to a gavel.

Gerald sat down. He placed his clipboard on the table.

"Inspector Hutchins," the Judge said. His voice was like grinding stones.

"You have cited Core Emberheart.

She has appealed. You have signed a Joint Utility Agreement.

"

"Yes," Gerald said.

"The appeal claims duress," the Judge said. "The agreement suggests collusion."

Gerald looked at Emberheart. She was smiling. A thin, sharp smile.

"Collusion is a violation of the Code of Ethics," the Judge said. "If you colluded to bypass the citation system, you are not a hero, Inspector. You are a fraud."

Gerald's hand shook. He placed it flat on the table to stop it.

"I did not collude," Gerald said. "I negotiated a temporary mitigation to prevent structural failure. The citation was served prior to the negotiation."

"The citation was served under threat of collapse," the Judge said. "Did you not know that serving a citation under imminent threat of destruction is prohibited?"

Gerald blinked, and he pulled out his manual. He flipped through the pages. He found **Section 12, Paragraph 7**.

*PROHIBITED: SERVING CITATIONS WHEN TARGET IS IN IMMINENT DANGER OF TOTAL CORE FAILURE.*

Gerald had missed it.

He looked at the Judge.

"I... was unaware," Gerald said.

"Ignorance of the code is not a defense," the Judge said.

Emberheart leaned forward.

"Vacate the citation," she said. "And fine him for procedural error."

The Judge nodded.

"Citation 4099-A vacated. Debuff removed."

The blue light around Emberheart flared back to life. Her flames returned. She stood up.

"However," the Judge said, looking at Gerald. "You have violated the Prohibition on Improvised Mitigation. You engaged in a Joint Utility Agreement without prior approval."

Gerald's blood ran cold.

"What is the penalty?" he asked.

"Demotion," the Judge said. "Your Compliance Core status is suspended. You are reclassified as a Sub-Contractor. You no longer have the authority to issue Citations."

Gerald sat there. The white void pressed in on him.

Sub-Contractor, and he could still inspect. He could still find violations. But he could not punish them. He could only report them.

He looked at Emberheart. She was laughing.

"Good luck, Inspector," she said. "See you in the next audit."

She vanished.

Gerald was alone.

The Judge looked at him.

"Report to your dungeon," the Judge said.

"A new entity has been detected in your sub-basement. It is not a rat.

It is not a mold spore.

It is a mimic disguised as a fire extinguisher.

"

Gerald stood up. He picked up his clipboard.

He walked out of the void, and he woke up on the floor of his own dungeon. The steam was gone. The ceiling was intact.

But the System interface was different.

**STATUS: SUB-CONTRACTOR.** **RANK: UNLICENSED.** **PERMISSION: OBSERVE ONLY.**

Gerald looked at the corner of the room. There was a red fire extinguisher on the wall.

It had eyes.

It opened its mouth.
