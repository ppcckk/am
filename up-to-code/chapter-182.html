Chapter 182: Pocketing the Fragment

# Chapter 182

**NO**

The word hung in the damp air, silent and absolute.

Gerald closed the notification window. The fragment remained in his hand, pulsing with a rhythm that matched his own heartbeat, though his heart was not beating. It was throbbing. A cold, mechanical throb.

The water level in the sub-basement had risen two inches. The sludge was creeping toward the exposed wiring of the main junction box.

If that box shorted, the entire grid would fail.

The library above would go dark. The fire suppression system would lock. And Gerald's dungeon, currently rated 47 out of 100, would lose its only remaining passive resistance: electrical stability.

He did not drop the fragment. He did not throw it back into the crack. He shoved it into the breast pocket of his vest.

The vest was made of spectral material, a gray canvas that smelled of old paper and mildew. It had no pockets. The fragment sank through the fabric and hovered against his chest, warm and heavy.

A new notification flashed in his peripheral vision. Red. Urgent.

**SYSTEM ALERT: UNREGISTERED HIGH-RADIUS ARTIFACT DETECTED.** **PROXIMITY TO STRUCTURAL LOAD: CRITICAL.** **AUDIT SCHEDULED: IMMEDIATE.**

Gerald ignored it. He turned to the junction box.

The wires were fraying.

Copper filaments hung loose like wet spaghetti. The insulation was peeling away, exposing the live current to the rising muck.

This was a code violation.

Specifically, Article 4.2: *Improper Grounding in High-Moisture Environments*, and he needed tools. He needed a non-conductive mat. He needed to isolate the circuit.

He did not have tools. He had three mold spores, a confused rat, and a growing sense of irritation.

The rat emerged from behind a pile of rotting drywall. It looked at the junction box. It looked at Gerald. It squeaked, a sound of profound confusion.

"Stay back," Gerald said. His voice was flat, the tone he used when informing a contractor that their permit was invalid. "This is a restricted zone. Unauthorized personnel will be cited."

The rat did not listen. It approached the water.

The water touched the exposed wire.

A spark jumped. Blue-white and violent.

The junction box exploded.

Not a large explosion. A small pop, followed by a hiss of burning plastic.

The lights in the sub-basement flickered, then died.

The emergency exit signs went dark, and the only light came from the glowing fragment in Gerald's chest and the faint bioluminescence of the mold spores.

**SYSTEM ALERT: POWER FAILURE.** **COMPLIANCE RATING: 45/100.** **PENALTY: VENTILATION REDUCED BY 15%.** **DEBUFF APPLIED: STAMINA REGENERATION HALTED.**

Gerald felt the change. The air in the room grew thick, stagnant. His spectral form felt heavier, weighed down by the lack of airflow.

He couldn't regenerate his mana. He was running on reserves.

He moved to the junction box, and the metal casing was warped. The wires were fused. He reached in, his fingers passing through the broken housing.

He didn't touch the wires.

He touched the *code*.

He visualized the schematic. He had memorized the city's electrical code forty years ago. He knew the amperage, the voltage drop, the grounding rod specifications.

He focused.

**ACTION: REWRITE LOCAL CIRCUIT LOGIC.** **COST: 500 MANA.** **WARNING: NON-STANDARD MODIFICATION. THIS MAY CONSTITUTE FRAUDULENT PERMITTING.**

Gerald paid the cost. He didn't have 500 mana. He had 480. He paid what he had.

The air in front of him shimmered.

The frayed wires rearranged themselves. The fraying stopped. The copper twisted into a neat, insulated coil.

The junction box sealed itself, the metal knitting together with a grinding sound.

The lights flickered back on. Dim, but functional.

**SYSTEM ALERT: CIRCUIT STABILIZED.** **COMPLIANCE RATING: 46/100.** **NOTE: UNAUTHORIZED MODIFICATION LOGGED. PENALTY WAIVED DUE TO IMMEDIATE THREAT TO LIFE.**

Gerald straightened.

His hands shook.

Not from fear. From the sheer effort of forcing the System to accept a non-standard repair.

It felt like arguing with a brick wall, and a rumble came from the ceiling. Dust fell from the rafters.

The library above was still shaking. The Inspector General's audit had left a structural scar on the foundation.

Gerald could feel it.

A hairline fracture running through the main support pillar. If that pillar failed, the sub-basement collapsed.

He had to reinforce it, and he had no concrete. He had no steel. He had a mold colony and a rat.

But he had code.

**ACTION: ISSUE CITATION.** **TARGET: STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY OF MAIN SUPPORT PILLAR.** **VIOLATION: LACK OF LATERAL BRACING.** **PENALTY: TEMPORARY REINFORCEMENT.**

This was a loophole.

Gerald had discovered it in Chapter 42. You couldn't fix a violation with your own mana directly, but you could issue a citation that forced the *System* to enforce compliance.

The System hated violations.

It would patch them to maintain its own integrity.

Gerald pointed at the pillar.

"By the authority vested in me by the Municipal Building Code, I am citing this structural element for inadequate lateral support," he said. His voice echoed in the small room. "Correction required within ten seconds."

The air around the pillar crackled.

**CITATION ISSUED.** **TARGET: PILLAR 4-B.** **STATUS: PENDING CORRECTION.**

Nothing happened.

Gerald frowned. He checked the range.

He was close, and the pillar was within his dungeon footprint.

It should have worked.

Then he saw it.

A faint, golden aura surrounded the pillar. It wasn't his. It wasn't the System's default gray.

It was gold. And it was pulsing in time with the fragment in his chest.

Someone else had cited it.

Or claimed it.

The gold aura solidified. Chains of light wrapped around the pillar, tightening, pulling it inward. The crack in the concrete widened.

Gerald's eyes narrowed. This wasn't a repair. This was a siege.

A voice echoed from the shadows near the sewer outlet.

"Nice try, Hutchins. But you're out of jurisdiction."

A figure stepped forward. Tall. Wrapped in robes that looked like they were made of shredded blueprints. The face was hidden behind a mask of polished obsidian.

Gerald recognized the silhouette. Core Vane. The neighboring dungeon core who ran the

"Labyrinth of Whispers" three blocks east. Vane had been citing Gerald for noise pollution for weeks.

Vane held a scroll in one hand. A golden scroll.

**ITEM: LETTER OF JURISDICTIONAL EXEMPTION.**

Gerald looked at the scroll. He looked at Vane.

"That's not valid," Gerald said. "The exemption form requires a notarized seal from the Inspector General's office. You're a mid-tier Core. You can't notarize your own paperwork."

Vane laughed. It was a dry, rasping sound.

"I don't need a seal. I have the Fragment."

Gerald froze.

Vane raised the scroll.

The golden light intensified. The chains around the pillar glowed brighter. The concrete crumbled.

Dust rained down on Gerald's head.

"I found it first," Vane said. "I just needed a reason to take it from you. A violation. A distraction."

The fragment in Gerald's chest flared. Hot.

Burning, which was reacting to Vane's scroll. They were resonating.

Gerald realized the truth. Vane hadn't sent the goblin scout to harass him.

Vane had sent it to test Gerald's defenses.

To see if he would pick up the fragment.

To see if he would register it.

Gerald hadn't registered it. But the System had logged the proximity.

And now Vane was using that log to claim ownership.

**SYSTEM QUERY: OWNERSHIP DISPUTE DETECTED.** **PARTY A: GERALD HUTCHINS (CORE 4021).** **PARTY B: CORE VANE (CORE 119).** **DISPUTE OVER: THE CORE FRAGMENT OF THE FIRST DUNGEON.** **RESOLUTION METHOD: STRUCTURAL LOGIC HEARING (IMMEDIATE).**

Gerald felt the pull. The world around him shifted. The sub-basement vanished.

The walls of the hearing room materialized. They were made of white marble, clean and sterile.

A long table sat in the center.

Two chairs.

Vane sat in one. Gerald sat in the other.

On the table between them sat the fragment. It was no longer in Gerald's chest. It was physical here. A shard of black crystal, jagged and sharp.

Vane leaned forward.

"The item was found in a sewer. Sewers are public infrastructure.

Therefore, the item is public domain.

My dungeon, *The Labyrinth of Whispers*, has jurisdiction over all public domain items within a three-block radius. My scroll proves this.

"

Gerald looked at the scroll. He looked at the fragment.

He thought about the building code. He thought about the definition of

"found."

He thought about the definition of

"public domain."

He opened his mouth.

"Objection," Gerald said.

Vane smiled behind the obsidian mask.

"On what grounds?"

"Article 1.4," Gerald said. His voice was steady. "Definition of 'Core Asset.' Any object that has been in direct contact with a registered Core for a period exceeding thirty seconds is considered part of that Core's inventory, regardless of location, unless explicitly released."

Vane's smile faltered.

"You didn't release it. But you didn't register it."

"Registration is optional," Gerald said. "Inventory status is automatic.

I held it.

I carried it.

It touched me. It is my asset.

"

"Prove it," Vane said. "The System doesn't know. It only saw the proximity log."

Gerald looked at the Judge. The Judge was a faceless entity made of floating ledgers. It waited.

"I have evidence," Gerald said.

He reached into his pocket. He pulled out nothing. He had no physical items in the hearing room.

But he had the log, and he remembered the moment he picked it up. The water lapping at his feet. The tremble in his hand. The decision to say *No*.

He had said *No*.

But he had also kept it.

Was keeping it without registration a violation? Yes. It was an unlicensed possession.

But was it *Vane's*?

No. It was Gerald's. Unlicensed, but Gerald's.

Vane's claim relied on the item being unclaimed. If it was claimed, even illegally, Vane's scroll of jurisdiction did not apply. You couldn't cite a private owner's asset as public domain.

Gerald had to prove the contact, and he looked at the Judge.

"Request access to my Core Log. Timestamp: 14:32:07."

The ledgers flipped. Pages rustled.

A voice boomed.

"Log found. Contact registered. Duration: 12 seconds."

Vane stood up.

"Twelve seconds! That's less than thirty.

It's not inventory. It's abandoned property.

"

Gerald didn't panic. He looked at the table. He looked at Vane.

"Article 3.9," Gerald said. "Grace Period for Item Acquisition. If an item is picked up in a hazardous environment, the grace period is extended to the time of exit from the hazard."

"What hazard?" Vane sneered.

"The rising water," Gerald said.

"The electrical shock. The structural collapse. I was in the hazard zone when I held it.

I exited the hazard zone when I repaired the junction box.

"

Vane blinked.

Gerald pointed to the fragment.

"I repaired the junction box.

That was the exit from the hazard.

I held the fragment from pickup until the repair was complete. That duration exceeds thirty seconds.

"

Silence.

The Judge's ledgers flipped again.

"Calculation verified. Duration: 45 seconds. Exceeds threshold."

Vane's mask cracked. A tiny fissure appeared in the obsidian.

"The item is claimed by Core 4021," the Judge announced. "Citation of Vane's scroll is invalid. Penalty for fraudulent citation: 10,000 Mana."

Vane screamed. A high-pitched, digital shriek. The hearing room shook. The marble walls cracked.

Gerald felt the power surge. He felt the fragment's energy flowing into him, not as an item, but as knowledge. The building code of the First Dungeon.

It wasn't just rules. It was *architecture*. It was the blueprint of the System itself.

He could see the code of the hearing room. He could see the code of Vane's dungeon.

He could rewrite it.

But the hearing wasn't over.

The crack in Vane's mask widened. Gold light poured out.

"You think you've won?" Vane's voice was distorted, layered. "I didn't come here for the fragment. I came here to bring you to the Inspector General."

The floor beneath Gerald's chair dissolved, and he wasn't in the hearing room anymore.

He was falling.

Down, through the marble, through the concrete, through the earth.

And below him, waiting in the dark, was not the Inspector General.

It was a door.

A massive, iron door, covered in rust and chains.

And on the door, scratched into the metal in frantic, desperate letters, was a message.

**DO NOT OPEN. THE CODE IS ALIVE.**

Gerald slammed into the ground. The impact jarred his spectral form. He scrambled up, dazed. The fragment was gone from his hand, embedded in the iron door.

Behind him, the hole in the ceiling closed. Vane was gone. The hearing was over.

But Gerald was no longer in Ashford, and he was in the Deep.

And the door was beginning to open.
