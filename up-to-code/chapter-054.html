Chapter 54: Wet Before Door

# Chapter 54

Gerald crouched at the base of the column and ran his fingers along the footing.

The concrete was sound. The rebar inside was not.

He could feel it through the surface, a faint vibration that had no business being there.

Concrete does not hum. Concrete does not resonate at a frequency that matches the water table three meters below.

He pulled his hand back and looked at the water.

The shared zone was exactly what he had expected from a boundary overlap: a strip of floor six meters wide where his dungeon's polished concrete met Core 6114's compacted earth. His side had handrails. His side had egress signage. His side had a fire extinguisher mounted at 1.2 meters, per code. 6114's side was mud and root systems and a faint chemical smell that Gerald identified, without consulting any manual, as hydrogen sulfide from anaerobic decomposition in poorly drained soil.

Core 6114 was a mass of dark geometry at the far end of the zone, which did not have a face. It did not have a body.

It was a cluster of angular shapes that shifted when Gerald looked at them directly, as if it were trying to be somewhere else.

Gerald had learned, over thirty-eight chapters of hostility, that this was not shyness. It was the System's rendering of a Core that had never bothered to define its own visual identity.

"You're touching the footing," 6114 said. Its voice came from everywhere in the zone. Flat. Male, if Gerald had to assign a gender, which he did, because the System insisted on pronouns and Gerald had stopped fighting that particular battle.

"I'm inspecting the footing," Gerald said.

"It's not your footing."

"It's a shared structural element. Section 4.2 of the Joint Liability framework. Shared structural elements are subject to inspection by either party."

"It's not a structural element. It's a load path."

Gerald looked up at the column. It was a steel-reinforced concrete pillar, 0.4 meters in diameter, running from the floor of the shared zone up through the ceiling and into the library above. The library's third-floor reading room was directly overhead. Gerald could see, through a crack in the column's surface, the shadow of a bookshelf.

"It's a structural element," Gerald said. "It's supporting a live load of approximately 2.3 tons. It's cracked along a 40-degree helical fracture line. It is, by any definition I've ever used, a structural element."

6114 was quiet for a moment. "Fix it."

"I'm trying." Gerald stood and walked back to his side of the zone. He opened his panel and pulled up the structural schematic the System had generated when the joint liability panel first appeared. The schematic showed the column, the footing, the water table, and a dotted line indicating the boundary between their two dungeons.

The water table was the problem. It had risen.

Gerald could see it in the schematic: a blue line that should have been at 3.2 meters below grade was now at 2.8.

That was a 0.4-meter rise, and the footing was at 2.5 meters; the water was undermining the soil beneath the footing.

The footing was settling. The column was cracking.

The water was coming from 6114's side. Gerald could see it: a slow seep at the boundary line, dark and sulfurous, creeping under the concrete footing like a tongue.

"Your drainage," Gerald said. "It's non-compliant.

Section 7.1, sub-clause C.

Minimum setback from shared structural elements is 0.6 meters. Your drainage channel is at 0.3 meters. You're bleeding into my footing."

"My drainage is fine."

"Your drainage is at 0.3 meters. The setback is 0.6. I can cite you."

"You can't cite me. Protected Inspection Status."

Gerald paused. He had invoked it. He had made sure of it. But he had not considered that 6114 might be counting on it.

"Right," Gerald said. "I can't cite you. But I can fix the problem."

"How?"

Gerald looked at the seep. He looked at his own drainage system, which was code-compliant, properly pitched, and terminated in a French drain that fed into the municipal storm system. He looked at the boundary line. He looked at the 0.3-meter gap between 6114's drainage channel and the footing.

He opened the System's Protected Inspection Status panel and scrolled to the sub-clauses. He had not read them all. He had read the invocation requirements. He had not read the operational parameters.

There they were.

Section 11.5.3: "During Protected Inspection Status, either party may propose a Provisional Code Amendment. A Provisional Code Amendment temporarily waives a specific code requirement for the duration of the remediation activity. Both parties must verbally agree. The amendment is void upon completion or abandonment of the remediation activity."

Gerald read it twice.

"Core 6114," he said. "I'm proposing a Provisional Code Amendment. I'm waiving your Section 7.1 drainage setback requirement for the duration of this remediation. In exchange, I'm rerouting your drainage through my compliant system."

6114's angular shapes stopped shifting. "You want to take my water."

"I want to take your water and put it where it's supposed to go. Your channel is at 0.3 meters. My French drain is at 1.8 meters from the footing. If I cut a tie-in at the boundary and route your flow into my drain, the water leaves the shared zone, the footing stops settling, and the column stabilizes."

"And after the remediation?"

"The amendment is void. Your drainage goes back to 0.3 meters. The problem comes back."

"So I'm not agreeing to a permanent fix."

"No. You're agreeing to a temporary fix that keeps the column from collapsing while we figure out a permanent one. Which is what joint liability is for."

Another silence. The angular shapes rotated slowly.

"Agreed," 6114 said.

Gerald felt the System register the amendment. A new line appeared on his panel: Provisional Code Amendment, Active. Waiver: Section 7.1, setback requirement. Duration: remediation activity.

He went to work.

He spawned a section of 100-millimeter PVC pipe from his inventory, which he had stocked in anticipation of exactly this kind of problem, because Gerald Hutchins did not enter a remediation without materials. He measured the boundary line. He cut a tie-in at the correct elevation, using a saw that the System provided for the duration of Protected Inspection Status, because the System was, in Gerald's experience, a generous entity when it came to tools and a miserly one when it came to time.

He laid the pipe from 6114's drainage channel across the boundary and into his own French drain.

He pitched it at 2 percent. He verified the slope with a level.

He connected it.

He watched the sulfurous water change direction and flow into his system, where it would be filtered, treated, and discharged into the municipal storm sewer under a permit he had filed forty-three chapters ago and never thought he would use.

The seep at the footing slowed. Stopped.

Gerald pulled out his moisture meter and pressed it against the concrete. The reading dropped. He pressed it again. Dropped further.

He looked at the column. The crack was still there, but it was no longer propagating.

He could see the hairline fracture line, 40 degrees, helical, running up the column's surface.

It would need epoxy injection, and he would need to install a steel collar. He would need to file a structural repair permit with the System, which was not the same as filing one with the City of Ashford, but which followed a similar enough process that Gerald had already pre-drafted it.

He was reaching for his panel when he noticed the tool marks.

They were on

They were on the column.

Gerald knelt, shining his inspection light on the footing where the helical fracture originated, and the tool marks were faint—barely a whisper in the concrete surface—but they were there. Parallel scoring, evenly spaced, running perpendicular to the fracture line; a diamond-tipped core drill, approximately 12-millimeter, operated at a slow RPM to minimize vibration.

He had seen this pattern before. Twice, in fact. Both times in the service corridors of Dungeon 4409, which belonged to a Core called Vexithar, a mid-tier dungeon Gerald had Cited in Chapter 31 for improper electrical rough-in and again in Chapter 47 for a blocked egress route that had, on the second occasion, cost Vexithar two of its four remaining elite spawns.

Vexithar had appealed both Citations. Vexithar had lost both Hearings.

Gerald pressed his thumb against the scoring and felt the slight ridge where the drill had bitten in before the operator withdrew. The marks were fresh. Within the last six hours. Someone had drilled into his load-bearing column from the 6114 side of the boundary, using a tool that matched the maintenance equipment Vexithar's spawn list included in its inventory manifest.

The seep had not been a drainage failure, and the seep had been a structural attack.

Gerald straightened. His panel was already open before he consciously decided to open it, because Gerald Hutchins did not need to decide to file a report. He reached for the Citation template, the one with the aggravated severity checkbox, the one he had never used, the one that required the target Core to be physically present and the violation to be willful.

He filled in the violation category: Unauthorized Structural Modification, Load-Bearing Element, Intentional.

He filled in the target: Dungeon 4409, Core ID Vexithar, and he filled in the service location.

He paused.

The service location required the target to be physically present. Vexithar's Core body was located in a chamber three hundred meters from the boundary, well outside the range of a standard Citation. Gerald had known this. He had known this when he filed the original Citations, which is why he had used the boundary-adjacent method, serving the Citation to Vexithar's spawn than to Vexithar itself.

This time the violation was structural. The System required the Core itself.

Gerald looked at the tool marks again. He looked at the crack that had nearly taken his column. He looked at the pipe he had laid, the pipe that had saved his footing from collapse.

He looked at the distance between here and Vexithar's chamber.

Three hundred meters, and he did not have Protected Inspection Status anymore; the waiver had expired the moment the remediation was complete.

Gerald rose, grabbed his moisture meter, and walked toward the boundary.

The meter read wet before he reached the door.
