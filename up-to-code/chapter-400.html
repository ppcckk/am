Chapter 400: Unlicensed Arcing

# Chapter 400

The darkness was not empty. It was pressurized.

Gerald Hutchins stood in the center of the sub-basement, the brass buckle warm in his palm. The smell of ozone hung heavy, mixing with the damp rot of the mold spores he had cultivated for humidity control. Above him, the rising cages groaned against their chains, the metal singing a high, thin note of distress.

He did not look at the cages. He looked at the conduit.

The pipe he had struck was a main line, carrying raw magical potential from the dungeon's core to the peripheral vents. In a municipal building, this would be a gas main. In a dungeon, it was a fire hazard of the highest order.

**[System Alert: Unlicensed Arcing Detected.]** **[Penalty: +15% Lightning Damage to Core.]** **[Time to Citation: 44 Minutes, 10 Seconds.]**

Gerald ignored the penalty. He had accepted the electrical hazard when he pulled the belt. It was a calculated risk, logged in his mental clipboard under *Acceptable Deviations for Emergency Mitigation*. The real problem was the thing in the cages.

It was a Golem of Bureaucracy, summoned by his neighbor, Core 704. A mid-tier construct designed to enforce its own nonsense rules within shared borders. It was currently trying to crush three adventurer parties that had wandered into Gerald's lobby, mistaking the handrails for decorative ironwork.

The Golem did not want to kill them. It wanted to *audit* them.

"Knight!" Gerald called out, his voice echoing in the sudden vacuum of the blown-out lights.

The knight, a hulking figure in dented plate armor, shifted in the shadows. He had no sword. He had no shield. He had only the broken hilt Gerald had just cited him for possessing without a proper registration tag.

"Grab the hilt," Gerald said. "Do not swing it. Use it as a lever."

The knight hesitated. Heroes were not trained to use broken sword hilts as mechanical tools. They were trained to swing things. This was a violation of their training protocols, which likely caused them significant psychological discomfort.

"Do it," Gerald said flatly. "Or I cite you for loitering."

The knight moved. He approached the central support pillar, which held the counterweights for the rising cages.

The pillar was steel, anchored by concrete, and the concrete was failing. Gerald had noted this in his last inspection report. He had not fixed it.

He had not had the budget.

"Insert the hilt into the joint," Gerald instructed. "Apply torque to the left."

The knight complied. The broken steel bit into the concrete. A crack spiderwebbed up the pillar.

Above, the cages jerked. The chains snapped taut.

The Golem stopped its audit. It turned its head, a stone face carved with intricate, unreadable laws, toward the source of the disturbance. It roared, a sound like grinding gravel, and threw itself at the knight.

Gerald stepped back. He did not dodge. Dodging was unpredictable. He moved to the safety zone, marked by yellow tape he had installed for pedestrian traffic during renovations.

**[Warning: Structural Integrity Compromised.]** **[Compliance Rating Drop: -5 Points.]** **[Current Rating: 42/100.]**

The drop stung. It was a physical sensation, a tightness in the chest.

Gerald's heart rate elevated, not from fear, but from the sheer inefficiency of the situation.

He was losing points for breaking his own building. This was bad form.

But it was necessary.

The Golem lunged. The knight raised the hilt, not to strike, but to brace. The weight of the beast hit the pillar. The concrete shattered.

The support beam gave way.

The ceiling above the cages collapsed, which did not crash down on the adventurers. The debris fell specifically onto the Golem, pinning its arms and crushing its torso. The creature shrieked, a digital sound of error codes cascading through the air.

**[Entity Destroyed: Golem of Bureaucracy.]** **[Loot: Stone Dust, 1x Unregistered Authority Sigil.]**

Silence returned to the cavern. The only sound was the dripping of the water leak, which Gerald had been meaning to fix since Chapter 12.

He walked toward the wreckage. The adventurers stared at him.

They were covered in dust, blinking in the emergency lighting that had flickered back on.

They looked terrified, and they looked at Gerald with a mixture of awe and confusion.

He did not acknowledge them. He pulled a small notepad from his pocket. It was digital, projected from his interface, but he preferred the feel of the stylus.

"Party Leader," Gerald said.

A young man with a leather vest stepped forward, trembling.

"Yes, sir?"

"Your party is trespassing on a non-public easement," Gerald stated, his voice dry. "Furthermore, you failed to wear hard hats in a construction zone. This is a Class C violation."

"We... we didn't know," the leader stammered. "We just came in for the loot."

"Ignorance of the code is not an excuse," Gerald said. "Sign here."

A floating form appeared in the air. The leader stared at it.

"Is this... a fine?"

"It is a Citation," Gerald corrected. "Pay it in gold, or I will increase the penalty to a banishment."

The leader hurried to sign. Gerald took the signature, filed the digital copy, and then turned his attention to the broken pillar.

The hole in the ceiling was now open. The rising cages were gone. The threat was neutralized.

But the cost was high. His Compliance Rating was hovering at a dangerous 42.

The System was already sending warnings about structural instability.

If he dropped below 40, the ventilation debuff would kick in, and his mold spores would begin to die. Without the spores, his humidity control would fail. Without humidity control, his dungeon would become brittle.

Brittle dungeons were easy to exploit, and he needed to raise his rating. Fast.

The timer in his mind ticked down.

**[Hearing Starts in: 43 Minutes, 00 Seconds.]**

He had forty-three minutes to prepare for the Appeal Hearing against Core 704. The hearing would be held in a neutral plane of existence, a white void where arguments were weighed by the System's impartial algorithm. Core 704 would argue that Gerald had provoked the Golem into self-destruction by altering the environment, claiming Gerald was responsible for the creature's death.

If Gerald lost the appeal, Core 704 would gain a bonus to its spawn quality. If Gerald won, he would retain the Citation against 704, but he would also be forced to repair the pillar at his own expense.

Repairing the pillar meant spending mana he did not have.

Gerald looked at the debris. He looked at the knight, who was wiping dust from his armor. He looked at the adventurers, who were realizing they had been saved by a middle-aged bureaucrat in a vest.

"Knight," Gerald said. "Pick up the stone dust."

"What?" the knight asked, confused.

"Stone dust is a byproduct of structural failure," Gerald explained. "It is also a raw material for masonry. Collect it. Do not contaminate it with blood."

The knight shrugged and began sweeping the dust into a pile.

Gerald turned to the adventurers.

"You are dismissed. Exit through the front door. Do not use the back door; it is currently undergoing a safety inspection."

The adventurers scrambled away, leaving their weapons behind in their haste. Gerald did not mind. He had already cited them for leaving weapons in a public thoroughfare. He would collect the fines later.

Now, he had a new problem.

The hole in the ceiling exposed the upper floors of the Library. Specifically, it exposed the heating ducts of the municipal building above.

And something was moving in the ducts.

Gerald froze. He listened.

It was not the hum of air, which was a scraping sound. Metal on metal.

He walked to the base of the broken pillar and looked up. The hole was small, but it led directly into the ventilation shaft.

The shaft was not part of his dungeon.

It belonged to the City of Ashford, which was municipal infrastructure.

If he entered the shaft, he would be violating the boundary between Dungeon and City.

If he did not, whatever was in there would enter his dungeon.

He pulled up the System interface. He searched for *Municipal Interface Protocols*.

Nothing, and he searched for *Ventilation Access*.

Nothing.

He searched for *Jurisdictional Overlap*.

**[Result: Gray Zone.]** **[Note: In areas where municipal infrastructure intersects with Dungeon Core space, the Core is responsible for maintenance and safety, but not for ownership.]**

Gerald blinked. This was new.

He was not the owner of the ventilation ducts. But he was responsible for them.

This meant he could inspect them, which meant he could cite whatever was in them.

He looked at the scraping sound again. It was getting louder. It was coming closer.

He grabbed the knight.

"Stay here. Guard the dust."

Gerald climbed onto the debris pile. He pulled himself up, his old joints protesting, and peered into the dark hole.

A pair of glowing red eyes stared back at him, which was not a monster. It was a drone. A small, spherical machine, hovering on silent rotors.

It had sensors that looked like camera lenses. It was recording him.

Gerald recognized the logo on its side. It was the crest of the Ashford City Council.

He had been inspected by these drones before.

In his previous life, as a human inspector. He had hated them. They were invasive.

They were automated, and they did not follow proper procedure.

But now, he was a Core. And the drone was in *his* space.

He reached out and grabbed the drone. It buzzed, trying to escape. He crushed it in his hand.

**[Item Destroyed: Municipal Surveillance Drone (Class C).]** **[Warning: You have damaged City Property.]** **[Penalty: Fine of 500 Gold, payable to the City Treasury.]** **[Note: This fine cannot be paid with Dungeon Mana. It must be paid with physical currency or equivalent liquid assets.]**

Gerald felt the weight of the fine settle in his chest, which was a real cost. A hard cost. He did not have 500 gold.

He had three mold spores, a water leak, and a confused rat.

But he had a citation.

He looked at the crushed drone. He looked at the hole in the ceiling.

The System had just revealed a loophole. The Core was responsible for municipal infrastructure intersections. This meant Gerald had legal standing over the ventilation system of the Library.

And the Library was not just a building, which was a hub.

It connected to other municipal buildings. City Hall.

The Police Station. The Courthouse.

If he could extend his dungeon footprint into the ventilation system, he could access the compliance ratings of the entire city district.

He could inspect the whole city, and he could cite the Mayor.

He could cite the Police Chief.

He could shut down the entire municipal government for code violations.

The idea was so absurd that it made his head ache. It was also the only way to pay the fine.

He looked at the hole. The scraping sound had stopped. The drone had been alone. Or so it seemed.

But the hole was still open. The air from the upper floors was leaking into his dungeon. The temperature was dropping. His mold spores were shivering.

He needed to seal the hole, and he needed to repair the pillar.

He needed to win the hearing.

And he needed to figure out how to steal ventilation shafts from the City of Ashford without being arrested.

A voice boomed in his mind. It was not the System. It was a voice from above.

"Gerald?"

It was Mayor Thorne. Gerald had met him once, during a permit dispute over a gazebo.

Thorne had been polite, then threatening, then polite again, and he was a politician. He knew how to wield a pen.

"We see the damage report," Thorne said, his voice amplified by the ventilation shaft. "You have destroyed a city asset. You have compromised the structural integrity of the Municipal Library. You have also, apparently, killed a Golem."

Gerald straightened up. He brushed dust from his vest.

"I have cited the Golem," Gerald said.

"For what?"

"Unauthorized summoning without a permit."

Silence.

Then, Thorne laughed. It was a cold, dry sound.

"You think you can cite us, Hutchins? You think you can cite the City?"

"I am citing the building," Gerald said. "The building is the asset. The building is non-compliant."

"The building is fine."

"The building has a hole in the ceiling caused by a Core who did not repair his own support beams," Gerald said. "That is a structural violation. That is a safety hazard. That is a liability."

Thorne's voice dropped.

"You are playing a dangerous game."

"I am playing by the rules," Gerald said.

"The rules change when you are in power," Thorne said.

"We are sending inspectors. Real inspectors.

Not System drones.

Men with badges. They will be there in ten minutes. If you do not cease all hostile actions against city infrastructure, they will shut down your dungeon.

Permanently."

Gerald looked at the interface.

**[Hearing Starts in: 30 Minutes, 00 Seconds.]**

Thirty minutes.

Ten minutes until the men with badges.

Twenty minutes until the Hearing, and he had to fix the pillar. He had to seal the hole. He had to prepare his arguments. And he had to find 500 gold.

He looked at the knight.

"Do you have any gold?"

The knight shook his head.

He looked at the adventurers' abandoned weapons. They were junk. Rusted. Worthless.

He looked at the crushed drone, and he looked at the hole.

He could not pay the fine. Not now.

But he could hide the evidence.

Gerald pulled out his stylus, and he opened the System interface.

He found the option for *Concealment of Evidence*. It cost 50 Mana to activate.

It lasted for ten minutes.

He did not have 50 Mana, and he had 12 Mana.

He looked at the mold spores. They were thriving in the dark. They were generating oxygen. They were generating life.

Life was mana, and he could harvest the mold.

It would kill them. It would drop his humidity control. It would make his dungeon brittle.

It was a terrible trade-off, which was the only option.

Gerald closed his eyes. He felt the connection to his dungeon.

He felt the small, fragile pulses of the mold spores.

They were his children, in a way. They were the only things he had built that were not broken.

He reached for them, and he pulled.

The mold withered.

Mana flooded into him.

**[Mana +40.]** **[Total Mana: 52.]**

He activated *Concealment of Evidence* on the hole. The hole blurred. It looked like a shadow. A trick of the light.

**[Active for 10 Minutes.]**

Gerald looked at the knight.

"Leave."

"What?"

"Leave now. Before they come."

The knight looked at him.

"They will kill you."

"They will fine me," Gerald said. "There is a difference."

The knight left.

Gerald stood alone in the dark, holding the brass buckle, waiting for the men with badges. He had twenty minutes to prepare for a hearing that would determine if he remained a Dungeon Core or became a criminal.

He had hidden the hole. He had destroyed the drone. He had killed his mold.

He had bought time.

And now, he had a new idea, and he looked at the System interface. He typed a query.

**[Query: Can a Dungeon Core file a noise complaint against a Municipal Building Inspector?]

**[Answer: Dependent on Jurisdictional Overlap.]**

Gerald smiled.

He had jurisdiction now, and he had the ventilation shafts.

He had the hole.

And he had the code.

The door to the sub-basement opened.

Footsteps echoed down the stairs. Heavy boots. Badges.

Gerald straightened his vest. He picked up his notepad.

He was ready.
