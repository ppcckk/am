Chapter 363: Not Wearing a Hard Hat

# Chapter 363

The Auditor did not walk. It arrived.

One moment, the air above the shaft was still, thick with the scent of damp stone and ozone. The next, the light bent.

Shadows stretched toward a point that did not exist, converging into a silhouette that defied geometry.

It wore no face. It wore a mask of shifting fractal patterns, grey against grey, like static on a dead channel.

Vex paused at the lip of the breach.

Below, the sub-basement of the Ashford Municipal Library churned with the chaos of a failed system. The Necromancer, a dwarf named Kaelen who smelled of grave dirt and bad coffee, was on his knees. He was scrambling backward, away from the central pillar, his hands raised in surrender.

But it was the Core that held Vex's attention.

Gerald Hutchins stood in the center of the room, and he looked small. He looked brittle.

His form flickered, a translucent overlay of a man in a high-visibility vest over a grid of exposed mana conduits.

One arm hung limp. The other gripped a clipboard so tightly his knuckles had turned the color of old parchment.

Vex tilted its head. The sound was like grinding glass.

"Anomaly detected," Vex said. The voice did not come from a mouth.

It vibrated in the teeth of everyone present.

"Pattern deviation: 94%.

Probability of fraudulent compliance: 99.9%.

"

Kaelen whimpered.

"I told you," the dwarf gasped. "I told you he was cheating.

Look at his left side. The mana flow is backwards.

He's routing power through the ventilation shafts.

That's a Class-4 Structural Bypass.

"

Vex drifted closer. The air temperature dropped ten degrees. Frost crept along the walls, tracing the outline of the handrails Gerald had installed three chapters ago.

"Explain the discrepancy," Vex commanded.

Gerald did not answer immediately. He was staring at the voided citation in his hand. The text was gone, replaced by a single, jagged scar in the air where the System had tried to punish him and failed.

Then, he looked up.

"I am Gerald Hutchins," he said.

His voice was flat, stripped of all inflection.

"Municipal Inspector, Grade 4.

I am currently undergoing a routine re-inspection of my own facility.

I have found multiple non-conformances.

"

Vex paused. "You admit to non-conformances?"

"I admit to *pending* corrections," Gerald corrected. He raised the clipboard.

"As you can see, I have already identified the primary failure point.

The load-bearing integrity of the north-west quadrant is compromised.

I have initiated a self-citation for immediate remediation.

"

Kaelen stopped breathing. The dwarf's eyes went wide. He knew Gerald.

He had seen Gerald measure the width of a doorframe to the millimeter while a dragon burned the library roof. He knew Gerald did not lie.

He knew Gerald did not improvise.

And he knew Gerald was currently lying about his dungeon's health.

Vex hovered lower. The fractal mask cycled through colors: blue, red, grey.

"Show me the audit log."

Gerald stepped forward. He walked into the cold spot. He did not flinch. He opened the clipboard.

The page was blank.

Vex recoiled. The shadows snapped back.

"Error," Vex hissed. "Data corruption detected. Re-scan."

"I cannot display the log," Gerald said. "The file is locked. Pending review by the Inspector General."

That was the loophole.

The System allowed a Core to freeze its own audit data if the Core was in the process of filing a self-correction report. It was a bureaucratic shield, designed to prevent double-penalties for the same violation during active repair cycles. It had never been used by a Core in active combat. It had certainly never been used by a Core in the middle of an Auditor's visit.

But Gerald had not been fighting. He had been *inspecting*.

He had spent the last ten minutes deliberately introducing minor, harmless defects. He had loosened a bolt on the handrail. He had adjusted the angle of a mirror to create a glare. He had routed a trickle of mana through the ventilation system to mimic a pressure leak.

None of these were violations. They were *observations*.

And by observing them, he had triggered the Self-Report Protocol.

Vex scanned the room again. The scanner beams swept over Gerald. They found the loose bolt.

They found the glare, and they found the pressure leak.

They did not find the anomaly.

Because Gerald had broken the pattern.

The perfect, unbroken chain of his successful appeal had been shattered by the introduction of intentional noise. To the System's logic engine, Gerald was no longer a Core that had beaten the rules. He was a Core that was failing to follow them.

And a failing Core was not an anomaly. It was a liability.

"Status: Inactive for Audit," Vex stated.

The voice was colder now. Disappointed.

"Re-evaluation scheduled in 48 hours.

Correct your deficiencies, Inspector. Or I will authorize demolition.

"

Vex dissolved.

The light snapped back to normal. The frost receded.

Kaelen slumped against the wall, sliding down until he hit the floor.

"You... you broke your own dungeon."

Gerald lowered the clipboard. His hand was shaking. He hid it behind his back.

"It was necessary," Gerald said.

"You loosened the main support beam."

"It is not a support beam. It is a non-load-bearing partition. I checked the blueprints. Twice."

Kaelen stared at him.

"You checked the blueprints while an Auditor was descending on your head."

"I checked them while I was walking to it."

The dwarf put his head in his hands.

"Gerald, if the Inspector General audits you in forty-eight hours, and they see what you did... they won't just fine you.

They'll revoke your license. They'll turn you into a dungeon without a body.

You'll be a hole in the ground.

"

Gerald looked at the voided citation. The scar in the air was fading.

"I am aware of the risk," Gerald said.

He looked around his dungeon. The water leak was still dripping. The rat was hiding behind the server rack. The handrail was loose.

To anyone else, it looked like a failure.

To Gerald, it looked like a job site, and he walked to the north-west quadrant. He picked up a wrench.

It was a standard 10-millimeter spanner, not a weapon.

It was not a sword. It was not a fireball.

He tightened the bolt.

But he did not tighten it all the way. He left it exactly half a turn loose.

Just enough to rattle when the wind blew.

Just enough to trigger the vibration sensor.

Just enough to keep the Self-Report Protocol active.

"Kaelen," Gerald said.

"Yes?"

"Get up. We have work to do."

The dwarf groaned.

"We just survived."

"We have not survived. We have deferred judgment." Gerald opened a new page on his clipboard. He wrote in neat, block capitals.

**PROJECT: DEFECT MANAGEMENT** **PHASE 1: VISIBLE NON-COMPLIANCE**

"I need you to help me break things," Gerald said.

Kaelen blinked.

"What?"

"The Inspector General will look for patterns of success.

They will look for efficiency. They will look for optimization.

I need to give them the opposite.

I need to make my dungeon look like it is falling apart.

"

"But that will hurt my spawns," Kaelen said.

"It will hurt my stats," Gerald corrected. "But it will save my existence. If we hide the success, they cannot erase it. If we display the failure, they will wait for us to fix it."

Kaelen stood up. He brushed grave dirt off his tunic. He looked at Gerald. looked at him.

"You are insane," Kaelen said.

"I am compliant," Gerald replied.

He turned and walked toward the ventilation shaft. He pulled a piece of duct tape from his pocket. It was silver, standard-issue HVAC tape.

He slapped a piece over the crack in the ductwork.

Then he peeled it back, leaving it half on.

The airflow wobbled. The hum changed pitch.

A new System message appeared in the air, green and angry.

**VENTILATION ERROR: PARTIAL OBSTRUCTION.** **DEBUFF APPLIED: -5% REGENERATION.** **SELF-CITATION ACTIVE.**

Gerald nodded.

"Good," he said. "Next."

***

Two hundred yards away, in a dungeon that smelled of sulfur and expensive cologne, Core 8841 known as 'The Baron' watched the energy spike from the library sub-basement.

He was a goblin king in a suit made of stitched-together adventurer skins. He had been Cited by Gerald three times in the last week. He was furious.

But he was also confused.

The energy signature from Gerald's dungeon had spiked, then dropped. It hadn't vanished. It had *degraded*.

"Boss," said the goblin lieutenant. "The signal is messy. It looks like a glitch."

The Baron narrowed his eyes. He tapped his claw on the armrest of his throne.

"Gerald Hutchins does not glitch," the Baron said. "He measures. He cites. He files."

"Then what is that?"

The Baron stood up. He adjusted his tie.

"He is hiding," the Baron said. "And he is doing it badly."

The Baron pulled up the map of the city. He saw the library. He saw the shaft. He saw the faint, pulsing red light of Gerald's dungeon.

It was flickering. Irregularly.

Like a dying bulb.

"Prepare the warparty," the Baron said.

"For an attack?"

"For an inspection," the Baron said.

He smiled. It was a nasty, jagged thing.

"Gerald thinks he can fool the System with noise. But noise leaves a trail. If he is hiding his power, he has to channel it somewhere. And if he channels it... he creates a bottleneck."

The Baron walked to his map table. He pointed a claw at the ventilation shaft in Gerald's dungeon.

"He is using the vents to mask his mana signature," the Baron said. "That is a structural violation of the Airflow Distribution Code, Section 4, Paragraph B."

The lieutenant blinked.

"Boss? How do you know that?"

"Because he is an inspector," the Baron said. "And he is too stupid to know that hiding is just another form of disclosure."

The Baron grabbed his sword. It was enchanted with a spell that ignored physical armor, but it was still a sharp piece of metal.

"We are going to the library," the Baron said. "And we are going to bring our own clipboards."

***

Back in the sub-basement, Gerald was three hours into his sabotage.

He had broken two handrails. He had disconnected three emergency lights. He had created a tripping hazard near the exit sign.

Every action triggered a System message. Every message added a debuff.

His regeneration rate had dropped to 10%.

His fire resistance was nullified.

His physical damage reduction was gone, and he felt weak. He felt fragile.

But the green text of the Self-Report Protocol remained locked over his core.

**AUDIT SUSPENDED.** **REASON: ACTIVE CORRECTION.**

Kaelen was sitting on a crate, eating a granola bar. He looked miserable.

"This is stupid," Kaelen said. "You're making yourself vulnerable. Any low-level mob can kill you right now."

"That is the point," Gerald said. He was using a screwdriver to unscrew the plate from a smoke detector.

"If the Inspector General comes back early," Kaelen said, "they will see the damage. They will fine you. They will strip your core."

"They will see the *progress*," Gerald said. "They will see that I am trying to fix it. The System values effort. It does not value perfection in the middle of a repair cycle."

Kaelen chewed his granola bar slowly.

"You know, in thirty years of adventuring, I have never met anyone who used bureaucracy as a shield."

"It is not a shield," Gerald said. "It is a permit."

He dropped the smoke detector plate. It clattered on the concrete floor.

A new message appeared.

**SAFETY VIOLATION: SMOKE DETECTOR DISCONNECTED.** **DEBUFF APPLIED: -20% AWARENESS.**

Gerald frowned. That was worse than he had planned.

"Kaelen," Gerald said. "Watch the ceiling."

"What?"

"The awareness debuff. I cannot see the small things anymore. The dust.

The shadows. The goblins.

"

Kaelen stood up. He drew his axe.

"Then why are we doing this?" Kaelen asked.

Gerald looked at the door. The heavy steel door that led to the library stairs.

"Because the Baron is coming," Gerald said.

Kaelen froze. "What?"

"I felt a shift in the ambient mana three minutes ago. A directional spike. Someone is moving toward the library entrance. And they are carrying a citation."

Gerald picked up his clipboard. He smoothed out a new form.

Form 11-B: Unlicensed Biological Entity.

"I have been expecting him," Gerald said.

"He has been Cited three times. He is angry. He is impatient.

He will not use stealth.

He will use force.

"

"So we fight?" Kaelen asked.

"No," Gerald said. "We inspect."

He walked to the door. He did not open it. He locked it.

Then he wedged a chair under the handle, which was a terrible lock. It would not stop a goblin. It would not stop a fireball.

But it would delay them.

And delay was the most valuable resource a bureaucrat possessed.

Gerald turned to the water leak. He grabbed a bucket. He placed it under the drip.

Then he tilted the bucket.

Water spilled onto the floor.

**SLIPPERY FLOOR HAZARD.** **DEBUFF APPLIED: -30% MOVEMENT SPEED (ALL ENTITIES).**

Kaelen looked at the puddle.

"You're making us slower?"

"I'm making the door harder to open," Gerald said.

The Baron would have to break the lock. Or break the door. Or break the chair.

Every second he spent doing that was a second the System recognized Gerald's

"active repair" status.

And while the Baron struggled with the physics of the door, Gerald would be struggling with the physics of the dungeon.

He had twenty minutes before the Inspector General's forty-eight-hour timer reset.

He had a Baron on the other side of the door.

He had a dwarf with an axe.

And he had a bucket of water.

Gerald looked at the voided citation. He looked at the locked audit status.

He picked up his pen, and he wrote one more line on his clipboard.

**ITEM: INTENTIONAL OBSTRUCTION.** **STATUS: PENDING.**

He waited.

The door handle rattled.

Then, it burst open.

The Baron stepped through, sword raised, suit torn, eyes burning with the light of a hundred stolen cores.

"Inspection!" the Baron screamed.

Gerald did not run. He did not fight.

He held up the clipboard.

"Please sign here," Gerald said. "And here. And initial the liability waiver."

The Baron stopped.

Confusion rippled through his face.

The System hovered in the air between them.

**CITATION ISSUED.** **TARGET: BARON 8841.** **VIOLATION: UNAUTHORIZED ENTRY DURING ACTIVE CORRECTION WINDOW.**

The Baron's sword dipped.

Gerald smiled. It was a dry, thin, terrifying smile.

"You are trespassing on a construction site," Gerald said. "And you are not wearing a hard hat."

The Baron roared. He lunged.

But his feet slipped on the wet floor.

And Gerald's clipboard hit him in the face.
