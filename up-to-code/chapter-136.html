Chapter 136: Expanded Inspection

# Chapter 136

The roots tasted like wet concrete and old secrets.

Gerald floated in the suspension.

Darkness pressed against his sensory input.

Not the black of a power outage.

The black of a place that had never known sunlight, and he tried to orient himself.

The water around him churned with data packets.

They hit his consciousness like hail.

*Structure: Unknown.* *Load: Critical.* *Occupancy: Zero.*

A voice rumbled through the sludge.

It vibrated in his chest cavity, or what served as one now.

"You are late for the docket."

Gerald adjusted his buoyancy, and he pushed against the current.

"Inspector General, I am currently in transit."

"You are in the substrate."

The voice was cold.

Administrative.

Like a parking meter collecting a fee.

"I am filing a Form 30-C."

"You are in violation of Section 4, Paragraph 12."

Gerald felt a tug, and he resisted it.

"Paragraph 12 applies to registered footprints. I am in the public easement."

The dark shifted.

A spotlight of raw System code illuminated him.

It felt like a cold shower.

"The easement is contested."

Something moved in the shadows ahead, which was not water.

It was a wall.

Massive.

Ancient.

Covered in barnacles and glowing runes.

The old core's voice returned.

It sounded different here.

Deeper.

More resonant.

"Let the inspector speak."

Gerald straightened his posture, and he didn't have shoulders.

He projected the posture anyway.

"I am Gerald Hutchins. Municipal Inspector. Badge 402."

"Badge 402 is revoked."

The Inspector General's avatar manifested.

It looked like a floating clipboard.

Made of obsidian.

"Revoked by whom?"

"By the Court."

Gerald pushed forward.

"I did not receive notice of revocation."

"Notice is implied by the audit."

"That is not in the manual."

"The manual is for citizens."

Gerald paused.

The water swirled around his form, and he focused on the obsidian clipboard.

"Then I am not a citizen. I am a public servant."

The clipboard hovered.

It did not move.

"A public servant is bound by statute."

"I am bound by the code."

"The code has changed."

Gerald extended a tendril of water, and he touched the clipboard.

It burned.

Cold fire.

"Show me the amendment."

Silence.

The dark held its breath.

The old core waited.

Gerald felt the connection to his sub-basement, which was thin.

Frayed.

But it was there.

He pulled on it.

Just enough to anchor himself.

"If you have no amendment, you have no jurisdiction."

The clipboard flickered.

"Jurisdiction is inherent."

"Jurisdiction is geographic. My footprint ends at the library foundation. This is the sewer main. It is city infrastructure."

"Your presence constitutes an expansion."

"My presence is incidental."

Gerald reached out with his mind, and he scanned the walls of the sewer.

He looked for the stamp.

Every pipe had one.

A date.

A grade.

A material spec.

Here, the stamp was gone.

Corroded away.

"Where is the inspection tag?"

The old core laughed.

A low, grinding sound.

"It has been two hundred years, Gerald."

"Then the pipe is obsolete."

Gerald turned to the clipboard.

"An obsolete asset cannot be audited. It is decommissioned property."

"Decommissioning requires a filing."

"Did you file it?"

The clipboard stayed still.

"We are not the filing authority."

"Then who is?"

"The Council."

Gerald felt a spark.

A loophole.

A tiny, beautiful crack in the armor.

"The Council of Ashford? They are human."

"The System recognizes their authority over municipal infrastructure."

"Does the System recognize their authority over dungeon cores?"

"No."

"Then we are not in the Council's jurisdiction. We are in limbo."

The water around Gerald heated up.

Not with danger.

With friction, and the Inspector General was thinking.

This was rare.

Most cores panicked.

Gerald waited.

He counted the seconds.

One.

Two.

Three.

"You are parsing."

"I am verifying."

"You are stalling."

"I am ensuring compliance."

Gerald focused on the old core.

It hung in the water like a dead fish.

Its eyes were gone.

Its mouth was a cavity of rot.

"Why did you come here, old friend?"

The old core's voice was weak.

"I wanted to see the roots."

"The roots are rotting."

"Yes."

"Did you file a structural deficiency report?"

"I was the structure."

Gerald nodded.

Or he imagined nodding.

"Then you are the complainant and the defendant. That is a conflict of interest."

The Inspector General's clipboard shifted.

The obsidian plates rearranged.

"Explain."

Gerald floated higher, and he gathered the data.

He organized it.

He built the argument.

"Under Section 19, Subsection B, a core cannot cite itself for self-inflicted damage. Therefore, the decay of the Old Core is not a violation. It is a natural process."

"That is incorrect."

"Read the text. 'Natural process includes entropy, decay, and dissolution.' The core is dissolving. It is not violating code. It is complying with nature."

The old core groaned.

A sound of pain.

Or relief.

"He is right."

Gerald turned to the clipboard.

"You are prosecuting a natural process. That is an invalid charge. Case dismissed."

The water grew still.

still.

No current.

No movement.

Just the cold weight of judgment, and the Inspector General did not speak.

It hovered.

The obsidian cracked.

A thin line appeared down the center.

Then another.

The clipboard broke.

Shards of black glass floated in the water.

Gerald held his breath, and he didn't have breath.

But he held it anyway.

**"Case status: Pending Review."**

The text flashed in red.

**"Adjudicator: Appointed."**

A new presence arrived, which was not water.

It was not data.

It was a man.

He wore a suit.

A grey suit.

Tie loosened, and he looked tired.

He looked like he hadn't slept in a week.

He stood on the water.

He looked at Gerald.

He looked at the old core, and he looked at the broken clipboard.

"Gerald Hutchins."

Gerald bobbed in the water.

"Sir."

"You are causing a lot of noise."

"I am filing paperwork."

"You are disrupting the substrate."

"I am identifying violations."

The man smiled, which was a sad smile.

"You know, I used to be an inspector too."

Gerald's form rippled.

"You?"

"Before the Awakening. Before the Cores. Before the System took over the plumbing."

The man stepped closer, and he pointed at the old core.

"That thing is a mess."

"It is non-compliant," Gerald agreed.

"It is also my brother."

Gerald froze.

The water around them turned icy.

"Your brother?"

"Thomas Hutchins. The original owner of this parcel."

Gerald looked at the rotting mass.

The face in the decay, which was familiar.

A heavy brow.

A broken nose.

A scar on the chin from a childhood fall.

Gerald had seen that face in the family album.

In the attic.

Behind the water heater.

"Thomas?"

The old core's eyes opened, and they were milky.

Blind.

But they found Gerald.

"Gerry?"

The voice was small.

Childlike.

"You found me."

Gerald felt a physical pull.

A yank in his gut.

Not pain.

Grief, which was heavier than any citation he had ever written.

"Thomas, you are in violation of—"

"Stop."

The man in the suit held up a hand.

"The hearing is over."

Gerald looked at the Inspector General.

Or what remained of it.

"It was not finished."

"It is over."

The man walked toward Gerald, and he walked on the water.

He stepped into Gerald's form.

Gerald didn't flinch.

He couldn't.

"You have won the point," the man said.

"But you have lost the context."

"Explain."

"The System is not about the code, Gerald. It is about the narrative."

Gerald's mind raced, and he searched for a rule.

He couldn't find one.

"This is new."

"It is always new."

The man looked at the old core.

Thomas was sinking.

Bubbles rose from his mouth.

Not air.

Data.

"Thomas filed the first violation," the man said.

"He cited the city for neglect."

Gerald looked at the old core, and he looked at his own hands.

They were made of water.

But they felt like Thomas's hands.

Rough.

Calloused.

"Then why did the System punish him?"

"Because he didn't have the authority."

"And I do?"

The man looked at Gerald.

His eyes were dark.

Infinite.

"Do you?"

Gerald opened his mouth, and he closed it.

He checked his internal log.

Badge 402.

Active.

Registered.

"Authority is not granted by badge," the man said.

"It is granted by consensus."

"Consensus?"

"The players. The adventurers. The Cores. They decide who is valid."

Gerald felt a vibration in the water.

Above him.

Far above.

Through the concrete.

Through the earth.

Adventurers, and they were in his dungeon.

They were kicking down doors.

They were breaking handrails.

They were calling him a trap.

"They think I am a villain," Gerald said.

"They think you are a puzzle."

"And Thomas?"

"Thomas is a ghost."

The old core groaned.

"I am not a ghost. I am a foundation."

Gerald looked at the broken clipboard, and he looked at the man in the suit.

"If Thomas is the foundation, and I am the inspector..."

"Then you are the landlord."

The word hit Gerald hard.

Landlord.

Not owner.

Manager.

Custodian.

But also: Responsible.

Liable.

Gerald looked at Thomas.

Thomas was fading, and the water was reclaiming him.

"He is going to dissolve," Gerald said.

"Yes."

"If he dissolves, the foundation fails."

"Yes."

"And my dungeon collapses."

"Yes."

Gerald looked at the man.

"How do I stop it?"

"You can't stop decay."

"Then what can I do?"

The man smiled, which was a cruel smile.

"You can renovate."

"Renovate?"

"Rebuild the foundation. Use your own core. Merge the footprints."

Gerald recoiled.

"Merge?"

"Assimilation."

"That is a hostile takeover."

"It is a merger."

Gerald felt sick.

Not the nausea of improvisation.

The nausea of a bad deal.

"He will lose his identity."

"He already has."

Gerald looked at Thomas.

Thomas looked back, and he didn't speak.

He just waited.

He trusted Gerald.

Because Gerald was the only one who had ever checked the load-bearing walls.

Gerald floated there.

The decision hung in the water.

If he merged, he gained strength, and he gained the old core's mass.

He gained the deep roots.

He became a monster.

If he didn't, Thomas died.

And the city above them might sink.

"The Inspector General said the case was pending."

"The Inspector General is gone."

"Then who is in charge?"

The man in the suit stepped back, and he pointed at Gerald.

"You are."

Gerald looked at his hands.

Water.

Just water.

But it was Thomas's water.

And Gerald's water.

And it was getting hot.

"Option A or B?" Gerald asked.

"There is no C."

Gerald took a breath, and he reached out.

He touched Thomas.

The connection snapped into place.

Power flooded Gerald.

It was not clean, which was dirty.

Old.

Heavy.

It felt like carrying a building on his back.

"Thomas," Gerald said.

"Yes, Gerry."

"Put on your hard hat."

Thomas smiled, which was a real smile.

He dissolved.

Into Gerald.

The water exploded.

The roots of the city shuddered.

Above, in the library, the floor cracked.

In the sewer, Gerald grew, and he was no longer a sub-basement.

He was the sewer.

He was the street.

He was the city.

And he had a lot of paperwork to do.

A new window opened.

Not for the Inspector General.

For the Players.

**"Dungeon Update: Zone 2 Unlocked."**

**"New Hazard: Structural Instability."**

**"New Mechanic: Load Bearing."**

Gerald looked up.

Through the layers of earth and concrete, and he could feel them.

The adventurers.

They were running.

They were screaming.

But they weren't running from monsters, and they were running from the floor giving way.

Gerald smiled.

He picked up a pen made of bone.

He opened a fresh log.

**"Subject: Gerald Hutchins. Status: Expanded."**

**"Next Inspection: Scheduled."**
