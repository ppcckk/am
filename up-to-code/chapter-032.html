Chapter 32: Breathing Below

# Chapter 32

The concrete was warm. Not warm like sun-heated pavement in July.

Warm like a living thing.

Gerald pressed his Core interface flat against the exposed slab and watched his readings scramble, and the frequency response was wrong. It was not the flatline of inert stone or the slow oscillation of water table.

It was a rhythm.

Something below was breathing at a rate that matched his own resting heart rate, as if it had been listening to him walk and had adjusted itself to match.

He pulled the interface back. The rhythm stopped.

Gerald set the interface on the ground and sat on the curb.

He took out his mental file cabinet. Thirty-one years of municipal work had taught him one thing above all others: when you encounter something you cannot read, you do not guess. You classify.

You assign it a category. You force the unknown into the known so that the known can then be regulated.

He opened his Core interface and searched the form registry.

FORM 7-C: PROVISIONAL ENTITY CLASSIFICATION.

Available to: Registered Cores, Grade 2 and above.

Gerald was Grade 2. He had earned it the hard way, by surviving the Hearing with UNDERFLOW and accumulating enough Citation points to trigger the grade increase. He had been Grade 2 for six days. He had not yet used Form 7-C because he had never encountered an entity the System could not already see.

Until now, and he filled out the form.

Entity Description: Substrate.

Non-biological. Responds to Core interface frequency at matched cadence.

Absorbs biological input via registered conduit network. Location: beneath Ashford Municipal Parking Structure, east wall. Estimated volume: unknown.

Classification Category: he scrolled through the dropdown. Substrate: Foundation.

Substrate: Aquifer.

Substrate: Mineral Deposit. Substrate: Unpermitted.

The last option had a tooltip. It read: Applied to structures or entities that exist within System boundaries but have no prior registration, no compliance history, and no identified Core of origin. Classification triggers immediate Demolition Order.

Gerald stared at the tooltip. He read it again.

Demolition Order.

Not Citation. Not fine.

Demolition.

The System's most severe enforcement action. It did not debuff. It did not restrict.

It erased, and he could classify the entity and the System would destroy it.

No Hearing.

No appeal window. The entity had no registered Core to file an appeal, because it was not a Core.

It was not a dungeon. It was not anything the System had a framework for, and that was precisely why it could be destroyed. The classification was the kill switch.

Gerald's hand hovered over the SUBMIT button, and he thought about the [NULL] designation; he considered how the entity had pushed back against his interface.

Not aggressively.

Not defensively. It pushed back like a dog nudging a held-out palm with its nose. Curious.

Testing, and he thought about what the Inspector General would do to an entity the System had no framework for. The notification had already been sent.

The audit was in forty-six hours.

If Gerald did nothing, the Inspector General would arrive and find an unclassified entity receiving biological input from a registered Core, and the Inspector General would do whatever the System did to things it could not categorize. Gerald had no idea what that was. He had never seen the System's enforcement against an unclassified entity because unclassified entities were supposed to be impossible.

The System did not have a box for things that did not fit in boxes.

Gerald pressed SUBMIT.

The interface flashed.

FORM 7-C FILED. CLASSIFICATION: SUBSTRATE: UNPERMITTED.

COMPLIANCE RATING ASSIGNED: 0/100.

DEMOLITION ORDER ISSUED.

EFFECTIVE IMMEDIATELY. SCOPE: ALL STRUCTURES IN DIRECT CONTACT WITH UNPERMITTED SUBSTRATE.

Gerald read the scope line three times.

All structures in direct contact.

His dungeon. The conduits UNDERFLOW had built passed through Gerald's sub-basement before reaching the entity below the parking garage. His dungeon was in direct contact with the unpermitted substrate. His dungeon was now, by association, also unpermitted.

His interface chimed.

CONDEMNATION NOTICE: Your dungeon is now classified as a structure in direct contact with an Unpermitted Substrate. Per System Code 11.4.2, condemned structures must be vacated within 24 hours of notice. Vacated: all spawned entities removed, all constructed structures dismantled, all registered conduits severed. Failure to comply will result in System-enforced demolition.

Gerald sat on the curb for a long time, and he did not feel physically ill. That surprised him. He had expected the paralysis, the nausea, the reflexive need to file a grievance with a department that did not exist.

Instead he felt something he had not felt since the UNDERFLOW Hearing. Something cold and procedural and sharp.

He was going to be condemned.

His dungeon was going to be demolished in twenty-four hours unless he vacated it. And the audit that was supposed to kill him was now irrelevant, because the System does not audit condemned structures. There is nothing to audit.

A condemned structure is already dead.

It just has not stopped moving yet, and he stood up. He walked back toward the library.

He had twenty-four hours. He needed to sever the conduits. The conduits were UNDERFLOW's construction, threaded through Gerald's dungeon walls like wires in a conduit chase. Severing them meant cutting into his own dungeon's infrastructure, which would drop his Compliance Rating further, which would weaken his spawns, which would make them easier to remove, which was fine because they needed to be removed anyway.

He had three mold spores, one water leak, and a rat. He had added two more rats last week because the first rat had seemed lonely and Gerald had felt a pang of something he refused to examine.

He could remove them. He could dismantle the handrails he had installed, the egress signage, the fire suppression piping. He could vacate. He could survive the audit by ceasing to exist as an auditable structure.

But if he vacated, the entity below the parking garage would still be there.

The Demolition Order would still be in effect. And the Demolition Order's scope was "all structures in direct contact." If Gerald vacated, his dungeon would no longer be a structure. It would be a void.

A void cannot be in contact with anything. The entity would be alone.

Uncontacted.

Alive.

Gerald walked faster, and he reached the library sub-basement at hour twenty-two. The conduits were embedded in the east wall, UNDERFLOW's dark threads running through his concrete like veins.

He could see them. He could cut them. He had a System tool for severance, a simple option in his construction menu labeled REMOVE CONDUIT.

One tap. The connection would end.

The Demolition Order would lose its scope.

His dungeon would be condemned but not demolished, because condemnation without contact was just a label. He could keep his dungeon. He could keep his handrails.

He reached for the tool, and he stopped.

If he severed the conduits, UNDERFLOW would notice.

UNDERFLOW's entire strategy was the conduits. They were its supply line to the entity.

If Gerald cut them, UNDERFLOW would know.

UNDERFLOW would come. UNDERFLOW was a Grade 4 Core with a Compliance Rating of 71 and a spawn list that included a wraith, two armored constructs, and a creature Gerald had only ever seen described in the System's bestiary as "the thing that walks in the walls." UNDERFLOW had already sent scouts. UNDERFLOW had already tried to kill him twice.

Severing the conduits was not a solution. It was a declaration of war against a Core four grades above him, with twenty-four hours to prepare.

Gerald set the tool down. He opened his interface and searched the form registry again.

FORM 7-D: APPEAL OF CONDEMNATION NOTICE.

Available to: Condemned structures. Filing deadline: 12 hours from notice. Appeal Hearing: automatic, within 6 hours of filing.

He checked the time. The notice had come at hour twenty-two.

He had twelve hours to file.

The Hearing would be within six hours of filing.

If he filed now, the Hearing would be at hour twenty-eight; he would have six hours to prepare his argument.

The argument he needed to make was simple. His dungeon should not be condemned because the contact with the Unpermitted Substrate was not of his making. He had not built the conduits.

UNDERFLOW had. He had not known the conduits existed until he discovered them.

The contact was incidental.

Involuntary. The kind of thing that happens when a neighbor runs an unpermitted pipe through your wall.

He could make this argument, and he had made similar arguments in municipal court a hundred times. Property owner did not install the violation.

Property owner discovered the violation.

Property owner should not be penalized for a violation they did not create and could not have reasonably discovered.

He opened the appeal form. The argument came out in one pass.

ARGUMENT: The condemned structure (Gerald Hutchins, Core ID 4471-B) did not construct the conduit network that established contact with the Unpermitted Substrate. Contact was established by Core ID 2209-A (UNDERFLOW) without the knowledge or consent of Core ID 4471-B.

He paused. He looked at his own words. He looked at the logs.

The logs said he had known. The logs said he had filed the report that flagged the anomaly.

The logs said he had walked to the parking garage at hour nineteen and pressed his interface against the slab.

The logs said he had felt the entity press back.

The logs said he had pulled his hand away, and he had known; he had known the conduits were there. He had known the entity was below. He had not known the entity was unpermitted because it had no classification, but he had known the conduits were not his.

The argument was true. The conduits were not his. But the argument required him to claim he had not known about the contact, and his own filing of Form 7-C proved he had known.

He had known enough to classify the entity. He had known enough to trigger the Demolition Order.

The Demolition Order had condemned his dungeon because of contact that he had, by his own action, confirmed.

He deleted the argument. He started again.

ARGUMENT: The condemned structure (Gerald Hutchins, Core ID 4471-B) filed Form 7-C in good faith to bring an Unpermitted Substrate into compliance. The resulting Demolition Order, while procedurally correct, applies to structures that were in contact prior to the classification. The condemned structure was in contact prior to the classification and prior to the discovery of the contact.

The contact was discovered through the condemned structure's own investigation, which led to the classification.

The condemned structure should not be penalized for conducting a compliance investigation that revealed a violation it did not create.

Better. True. Arguable.

He submitted the form.

APPEAL FILED. HEARING SCHEDULED: HOUR 28.

He had six hours. He closed the form and opened his dungeon map. He looked at the conduits. He looked at the entity's location, now marked with a red Demolition Order icon that pulsed like a wound.

He was still typing a list of evidence when his interface chimed.

NEW FORM FILED IN YOUR JURISDICTION.

FORM 7-C: PROVISIONAL ENTITY CLASSIFICATION.

Filed by: ENTITY ID [NULL]. Classification Category: Substrate: Unpermitted. Target: Core ID 4471-B (Gerald Hutchins).

Gerald read it twice. The entity had filed a Form 7-C. The entity had classified Gerald's dungeon as Unpermitted Substrate.

DEMO ORDER ISSUED. SCOPE: ALL STRUCTURES IN DIRECT CONTACT WITH UNPERMITTED SUBSTRATE.

The icon on his map turned red.

His dungeon was now classified as Unpermitted Substrate by the entity's own filing, and the Demolition Order's scope now included everything in contact with Gerald's dungeon. Which was the library above.

Which was the parking garage. Which was the municipal water main running beneath the east wall.

His interface displayed a new line.

DEMO ORDER CASCADING. TARGETS: 4. STRUCTURES FLAGGED: ASHFORD MUNICIPAL LIBRARY, ASHFORD MUNICIPAL PARKING STRUCTURE, CITY WATER MAIN LINE 7, CORE ID 4471-B.

Gerald looked at the list. He looked at the timer.

CONDEMNATION NOTICE: Your dungeon is now classified as Unpermitted Substrate. Vacate within 24 hours.

He had already been condemned. Now he was condemned twice, and the second condemnation was broader, because the entity had named him, and naming was the first act of demolition.

He opened the appeal form again. His hands were steady. They had always been steady. Thirty-one years of holding a clipboard over a load-bearing wall while a contractor screamed at him had made his hands steady.

He wrote the second argument slower than the first.

ARGUMENT: The appellant (Core ID 4471-B) did not construct the Unpermitted Substrate classified by ENTITY ID [NULL].

The appellant is a registered Core with a valid Compliance Rating of 68 and a complete inspection history.

The appellant should not be classified as Unpermitted Substrate because the appellant is not a substrate. The appellant is a structure.

The distinction is material. A substrate is a foundation. A structure is a building.

A building cannot be a foundation for itself, and he paused. He read what he had written.

The entity had classified him as a substrate.

A substrate is something you build on; the entity was claiming that Gerald's dungeon was the foundation for something else. That Gerald's dungeon was not a building but a base.

A thing that supported another thing.

Gerald looked at the map. He looked at the entity's location. He looked at the conduits that passed through his walls.

The entity was not in his dungeon. His dungeon was in the entity.

The entity had been there first, and the entity was the substrate. Gerald's library, his parking garage, his municipal infrastructure, all of it sat on top of something that had been there before any of it existed.

And now the entity was saying: you are my foundation. You are my substrate. And I am filing a Demolition Order against my own foundation.

Gerald deleted the argument. He started again.

He had four hours and twelve minutes. The Hearing was in six hours.

He had to prove that he was not a substrate, and he had to prove that his dungeon was a structure, not a foundation.

He had to prove that the thing below was not what everything above was built on.

He did not know how to prove that.

His interface chimed.

HEARING REMINDER: HOUR 28. PREPARATION TIME REMAINING: 4:11:47.

And below him, the entity pressed back against the concrete again. Gerald could feel it through the floor. A slow, patient pressure. The pressure of something that had been waiting for a long time and had just remembered how to file paperwork.

It had been waiting a long time.
