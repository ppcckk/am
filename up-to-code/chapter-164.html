Chapter 164: Cite Your Own Home

# Chapter 164

The reset protocol did not arrive like a thunderclap. It arrived like a form letter sliding under a door.

Gerald felt the edges of his consciousness peeling back, revealing the raw data streams beneath. The Admin Override was not a weapon.

It was a cleanup routine.

It viewed his existence in the sandbox as a temporary error state requiring deletion. The pull in his gut tightened, a visceral yank that threatened to unmake the concept of *Gerald Hutchins*.

He could not speak.

He could not move his limbs with intention. But he could think.

And Gerald Hutchins did not think in metaphors.

He thought in subsections.

*If the system views my presence as an unauthorized modification, then my presence must be reclassified as authorized.*

The logic was simple. It was the same logic he had used when a contractor tried to bypass the permit office by claiming the project was "urgent infrastructure." You did not fight the urgency. You attacked the classification.

Gerald focused his will on the only object still solid in the frozen server: the key. He had locked the door, trapping himself and the shadow.

But he had also trapped the *context*.

The System saw him as a user.

A user who had stayed too long. A user who had broken protocol.

But Gerald was not a user. He was an Inspector.

He reached for the interface. It was gone. The HUD, the status bars, the inventory slots—all dissolved into white noise.

What remained was the raw architectural layer. He saw the code of the sandbox not as glowing runes, but as structural beams, load-bearing walls, and ventilation ducts.

The shadow was a crack in the foundation.

The reset was the demolition crew.

Gerald did not try to escape the demolition. He tried to file an injunction.

He couldn't type. He couldn't select from a menu. But he remembered the shape of the complaint forms he had filled out for thirty-one years. He remembered the specific syntax required to halt a municipal project.

*Form 11-B. Unlicensed Biological Entity.* *Form 4-C. Immediate Structural Hazard.* *Form 9-D. Request for Stay of Execution Pending Administrative Review.*

He couldn't file them. The interface was locked.

But the lock-out had a flaw.

He had locked the *editor*. He had not locked the *reader*.

The System still needed to read his status to determine if he was a threat.

It needed to process his input to decide how to delete him.

Gerald pushed his thought into the processing queue. He did not send a command. He sent a query.

*Query: Is the current entity classified as 'User' or 'Inspector of Record'?*

The white noise screamed. The pull in his gut intensified, dragging his memories toward the drain. He felt his name slipping away. *Gerald.* *Hutchins.* *Inspector.* The words were becoming abstract data points.

He needed to anchor them. He needed to prove that the role was intrinsic to the structure, not just an overlay.

He looked at the shadow. It was frozen, a blur of static trapped in the lock.

The shadow was the bug.

The bug was the space between the lines.

Gerald had filled that space with himself. By doing so, he had become a load-bearing element of the current state.

You cannot demolish a building if a load-bearing wall is still standing. You must first support it.

Gerald focused on the ventilation shafts he had meticulously measured and cleared in Chapter 40. He focused on the fire exits he had cited for being non-compliant in Chapter 12. He focused on the handrails he had installed, one bolt at a time, in the dark.

He poured his memory of *compliance* into the query.

*Query: Does the current entity meet the criteria for 'Inspector of Record' under Subsection 7, Paragraph 3 of the System Administrative Code?*

The System paused.

The white noise dropped in volume. It didn't stop, but it slowed. The pull in his gut eased by a fraction.

*Processing...*

The text appeared in his mind, not as a notification, but as a raw string of code that translated into a question.

*ERROR: INSPECTOR STATUS NOT FOUND IN LOCAL USER DATABASE. DEFAULT TO USER CLASS.*

Gerald's blood ran cold. The System did not recognize him as an inspector. It recognized him as a bug. A user who had broken the rules.

He had to change the database. He couldn't edit it. But he could submit a ticket.

He couldn't click. But he could *assert*.

Gerald remembered the Hearing. The one he had won by proving the rival core's fire exits were blocked. He had argued structural logic.

He had won on a technicality. He had forced the System to acknowledge the *physics* of the dungeon, not just its narrative.

Here, the physics were broken.

The sandbox was unstable. The shadow was eating the code. If Gerald was deleted, the sandbox would collapse.

The collapse would breach the containment field.

The breach would spread.

That was the argument.

*Assertion: Deletion of current entity will result in catastrophic failure of Sandbox Integrity. Entity is required for containment of localized anomaly (See: Shadow/Static).*

The System froze again. This was not a pause. This was a calculation.

The Admin Override halted. The pull stopped.

Gerald hung in the void, suspended between existence and non-existence.

He was a man in a suit, holding a key, standing in a dark room. But the room was no longer dark. It was grey.

The color of wet concrete.

A new message appeared. Not on the wall. In his bones.

**ADMIN OVERRIDE: PENDING. WAITING FOR COMPLIANCE REVIEW.**

Gerald exhaled. The air was solid, but he could breathe.

He looked at the shadow. It was still frozen. But the static was fading. The space between the lines was closing.

He had bought time. But he had not fixed the problem. The shadow was still there. And the Admin was still waiting.

He needed to file the Citation. He needed to serve it. But he was locked out of the editor.

Unless...

He looked at his hands. They were still fading. The reset was waiting.

Gerald looked at the key. It was a physical object in a digital world. He had manifested it through sheer will. He had made the abstract concrete.

He realized he had been asking the wrong question. He was asking if he *could* edit. He should have been asking if he *was* allowed to edit.

The System granted permissions based on role. Gerald had no role. But he had an *action*.

He had locked the door.

Locking a door was an administrative action. It was a restriction. It was a code enforcement measure.

Gerald reached out with his mind. He didn't try to unlock the door. He tried to *cite* the door.

*Violation: Unauthorized Containment. Section 14, Subsection B: No entity shall be held without proper signage and egress route.*

The System hesitated. It was parsing the syntax. It was looking for the authority.

Gerald had no authority. He had only the act.

But the act of locking *was* the authority. In the real world, an inspector did not need a permit to close a condemned building. The inspection *was* the permit. The danger *was* the justification.

Gerald focused on the shadow.

He did not see a monster.

He saw a hazard. A blocking of the egress route.

A violation of the minimum clearance requirements for safe passage.

He filed the violation in his mind. He served it to the void.

*Serve Citation to: Current Environmental State. Violation: Obstruction of Egress. Penalty: Immediate Remediation.*

The text flashed red. Then white. Then green.

**CITATION ACCEPTED.** **PENALTY APPLIED: FORCED REMEDIATION.**

The shadow screamed. It was a silent, digital scream that vibrated through the server floor. The static exploded outward, shredding the frozen pause.

The shadow was not defeated. It was *evicted*.

It was ripped out of the server by the force of the System's own compliance enforcement. It was dragged into the garbage collection routine, where it would be deleted not by Gerald, but by the System's own hygiene protocols.

Gerald stood in the center of the room. The shadow was gone. The static was gone.

But the room was changing. The walls were shifting. The floor was cracking. The forced remediation was tearing apart the sandbox structure to clear the obstruction.

The Admin Override resumed.

**ADMIN OVERRIDE: USER 'Gerald_Hutchins' detected. Session terminated.**

The pull returned. Stronger this time. Violent.

Gerald did not panic. He looked at the fading walls. He looked at the key in his hand.

He had won. The shadow was gone. The bug was deleted.

But he was still a user. And users were deleted when the session ended.

He needed a new role.

The key was still in his hand. It was the only thing that was real. It was the only thing that had survived the reset.

Gerald looked at the key. He looked at the lock on his chest.

He hadn't opened it. He had locked it.

If he unlocked it, he would be free. He would be back in his body. Back in the library. Back in the municipal building.

But if he stayed here, he was a bug.

If he left, he was a man.

But what was a man against a System that could erase him?

Gerald looked at the text hovering in the air.

**SESSION TERMINATION IN 3... 2...**

He made his choice.

He did not try to stay. He did not try to fight. He tried to *transfer*.

He had transferred Cites between dungeons. He had transferred authority from one Core to another during the Hearings. It was a loophole. A way to pass the burden of responsibility to another party.

He couldn't transfer himself. But he could transfer his *status*.

Gerald focused on the key. He focused on the lock. He focused on the concept of *Inspector*.

He took the key out of his chest.

The pain was blinding. It felt like his soul was being pulled through a needle.

He held the key up. It was small. Iron. Old.

He threw it.

Not at the Admin. Not at the wall.

He threw it at the *System Core*. The central processor of the sandbox. The heart of the server.

The key flew through the air, trailing sparks of corrupted data. It struck the core.

It did not break the core.

It *inserted* itself.

The key slid into a slot that had not existed a second ago. A slot that Gerald had just created by defining it as a *Lock Mechanism*.

**ERROR: INVALID INPUT.** **RETRY?**

**NO.**

**STATUS: KEY ACCEPTED.** **ROLE CHANGE: USER -> ADMIN.**

The white noise vanished. The silence returned. But it was not the silence of emptiness. It was the silence of a office after hours.

Gerald stood in the server. His hands were solid. His suit was crisp. His tie was straight.

He looked at his status bar. It was no longer a health bar. It was a clipboard.

**ROLE: SYSTEM ADMINISTRATOR (PROVISIONAL).** **PERMISSIONS: READ/WRITE (LIMITED TO COMPLIANCE MODULES).** **RESTRICTIONS: NO CREATION. NO DELETION. NO COMBAT.**

Gerald blinked.

He was an Admin. But he was an Admin who could only issue fines.

He looked around the server. The sandbox was stable. The shadow was gone. The library sub-basement was restored.

But the Admin Override was still running. It was just waiting for him to finish his shift.

A new message appeared.

**ADMIN HUB: CONNECTION ESTABLISHED.** **WARNING: YOU ARE NOW RESPONSIBLE FOR ALL SANDBOX COMPLIANCE.** **CURRENT VIOLATIONS: 14,000,000.** **NEXT AUDIT: IN 5 MINUTES.**

Gerald stared at the number. Fourteen million.

He looked at the clock in the corner of his vision. It was ticking down.

He had five minutes to process fourteen million violations.

He had five minutes to save the System from itself.

Gerald straightened his tie. He picked up the imaginary pen that had materialized in his hand.

"Let's begin," he muttered.

He did not look at the clock. He looked at the first violation. It was a minor electrical fault in a tutorial dungeon in Sector 4.

He clicked it.

**CITATION ISSUED.**

The clock ticked.

**VIOLATIONS REMAINING: 13,999,999.**

Gerald's hands shook. Not from fear. From exhaustion.

He had never been asked to measure door widths this fast.

He had never been asked to check egress routes this often.

He had never been asked to determine whether a load-bearing wall was, in fact, load-bearing, at the speed of light.

But he had been asked to check egress routes. He had been asked to determine whether a load-bearing wall was, in fact, load-bearing.

He had been asked to do this for thirty-one years.

And now, he had five minutes to do it for the entire Universe.

The screen flickered. A new notification popped up, red and flashing.

**ALERT: CORE 'MOTHER_OF_ALL_BUGS' DETECTED IN SECTOR 1.** **STATUS: UNREGISTERED.** **ACTION REQUIRED: IMMEDIATE CITATION OR PURGE.**

Gerald froze.

He had just cited fourteen million minor errors. He had not had time to look up the Sector 1 Core.

But the name was familiar.

He had seen it on the old library maps. The ones he had burned in Chapter 1.

The Mother of All Bugs was not a bug.

It was a Dungeon Core.

And it was located in the Ashford Municipal Library.

Right under his feet.

Gerald looked at the clock.

**4:59:59.**

He had to cite his own home.

He had to cite the dungeon that had birthed him.

He had to serve the Citation to himself.

And if he failed, the Purge would erase him.

Gerald lifted his pen. His hand was steady.

He wrote.
