Chapter 408: System Blackout

# Chapter 408

The magma churned with a violence that defied physics.

A woman in charred leather armor clung to a stalactite. Her breath fogged inside her helmet, creating a small, desperate cloud. Below her, the river of fire roared, feeding into a cavernous maw that pulsed with an obscene, rhythmic light.

She was Kaelen, a scout for the Adventurer's Guild. She had survived three dragon lairs and a necromancer's tomb. She had not survived this.

The heat was not merely hot. It was heavy.

It pressed against her plate mail like a physical weight.

The air tasted of sulfur and copper. Her vision blurred at the edges, the edges of the world dissolving into orange static.

Something fell from the ceiling.

Not a rock. Not a spark.

A man, and he wore a high-visibility vest over a dress shirt. The tie was loosened. The clipboard was strapped to his chest. He landed on the edge of a cooling obsidian shelf with a grace that seemed entirely wrong for a human body.

Kaelen stared. The man did not burn. The magma lapped at his boots, and the leather soles smoked, but the man did not flinch. He looked up at the ceiling, then down at the churning lava lake below him.

He pulled a pen from his vest pocket.

Kaelen almost laughed. The absurdity broke through her panic for a second. Then the ground shook.

A face formed in the lava. It was vast, made of molten rock and hate. Vorax, the Ancient Core of the Caverns, looked down at the intruder. The eyes were pits of white fire.

"YOU DARE," Vorax boomed. The voice vibrated in Kaelen's teeth. "TO ENTER MY DOMAIN UNINVITED?"

The man in the vest did not draw a sword, and he did not cast a spell. He looked at his clipboard.

He tapped the pen against the clipboard. *Tap.

Tap. Tap.*

The sound echoed in the cavern. It was small, insignificant, and annoying.

Vorax's eyes narrowed. The magma shifted, trying to form a hand to crush the insect.

The man pointed a finger at the lava flow.

"Excessive turbulence," the man said. His voice was dry.

It cut through the roar of the fire.

"That indicates poor flow regulation.

Your thermal expansion rate is inconsistent with your structural load.

"

Vorax paused. The hand of fire hovered.

"WHAT ARE YOU?" Vorax asked.

"Gerald Hutchins. Municipal Inspector. Ashford City Code Compliance," Gerald said.

He did not look at Vorax. He looked at the lava.

"And you are currently in violation of Section 402, Subsection C: Uncontrolled Thermal Venting in a Shared Foundation Zone."

Kaelen gripped her dagger. This was suicide. Everyone knew Vorax.

Vorax ate heroes for breakfast.

Heroes brought swords. Heroes brought magic.

Heroes did not bring paperwork.

Gerald stepped off the shelf. He walked on the air.

Kaelen blinked. She rubbed her eyes. He was walking on a transparent barrier that hadn't been there a second ago.

A faint blue grid pulsed beneath his feet. The System's interface.

Gerald was not walking. He was walking through a permission layer.

Vorax roared. The lava surged. A wave of fire crashed toward Gerald.

Gerald did not dodge. He raised his clipboard.

**[System Notification: Citation Issued. Violation: Lack of Fireproofing on Structural Supports.]** **[Penalty: Thermal Conductivity Reduced by 40%.]**

The wave of fire hit the invisible barrier and broke. It splashed harmlessly to the sides. The heat that had been melting Kaelen's visor dropped instantly. The air cooled.

Gerald walked closer to the lava lake.

The face of Vorax screamed.

The magma rippled, trying to reform, but it moved sluggishly. The flow was thick, slow.

Like cold honey.

"You cannot cite me," Vorax hissed. The voice was distorted now, wet and confused. "I am the Master of the Deep. I am fire."

"Fire is a state of matter," Gerald said. "It is not a legal entity.

You are a Dungeon Core.

You are subject to the Inter-Dimensional Building Code. You registered with the System. Therefore, you have obligations.

"

Gerald stopped ten feet from the lava. He looked up at Kaelen.

"You," Gerald said. "Get down. The ceiling is unstable. I'm going to shut down your support beams."

Kaelen didn't argue. She dropped to the obsidian shelf, pressing herself flat against the rock.

Gerald turned back to Vorax. He spoke, not to the Core, but to the air. He was dictating.

"Observation One: The primary conduit feeding the magma chamber is undersized. Observation Two: The ventilation shafts are blocked by calcified debris. Observation Three: There is no emergency egress route for thermal staff."

"I have no staff!" Vorax shouted.

"Then you are understaffed and improperly zoned," Gerald said. "I am issuing a Stop-Work Order."

The System flashed gold in the air.

**[Hearing Initiated.]** **[Subject: Vorax vs. Gerald Hutchins.]** **[Charge: Critical Structural Negligence.]**

The cavern froze. The lava stopped flowing. The heat vanished.

Kaelen looked around. The world had turned gray. The vibrant orange of the magma was now a dull, dead brown. Vorax's face was frozen in a mask of shock.

Gerald stood in the center of the gray void. He clicked his pen.

"State your defense," Gerald said.

Vorax tried to speak. The System blocked him. The rules of the Hearing were absolute. Only the accuser could speak until the subject was granted the floor.

Gerald continued.

"You claim to be a dungeon. A dungeon is a building.

Buildings must have exits.

Your current configuration is a sealed pressure vessel. If I were an adventurer, I could blow your entire foundation with a single fireball. Not because I am strong, but because you have no relief valves.

"

Vorax's eyes flickered. He understood.

He knew the geometry of his own lair, and he had built it to keep things in.

He had never thought about letting things out.

"I... I have traps," Vorax thought-spoke. The words appeared in the air.

"Traps are not exits," Gerald said. "Traps are liabilities. A trap that cannot be disengaged from the outside is a murder device.

The System classifies it as a code violation under Safety Standard 12. You are currently operating an illegal slaughterhouse.

"

Kaelen watched, her mouth open. This was not a battle. This was an interrogation.

Gerald pulled a new form from his vest. It was bright yellow. He held it up.

"I am adding a new clause to the inspection," Gerald said. "The Loophole of Shared Liability."

Vorax's form trembled. The gray void rippled.

"What is that?" Vorax asked.

"Because you share a foundation with my sub-basement," Gerald said, "and because I have filed a Joint Inspection Request, we are legally co-owners of this cavern. If you fail to bring your dungeon up to code within thirty seconds, the liability transfers to the nearest compliant entity. Which is me."

Gerald smiled. It was a terrifying smile.

"And if I am the owner, I can demolish the structure at my own expense.

To correct the violation. You will die. But you will die as a condemned building, not a defeated monster.

Your Core will be stripped for scrap metal.

"

Vorax recoiled. The horror in the Core's eyes was genuine.

He had fought paladins, and he had fought archmages. He had never fought a man who could legally delete his existence with a form stamp.

"Wait," Vorax said. "The appeal window..."

"Gone," Gerald said. "Because you didn't file it in the first three seconds.

You were too busy yelling about being Master of the Deep.

You ignored the notification tone.

I heard it. You didn't.

That is a procedural default.

"

The System pulsed.

**[Verdict: Guilty.]** **[Penalty: Immediate Structural Dampening.]** **[Bonus: Co-Owner Rights Activated.]**

The brown lava turned black. It hardened. The heat died completely.

Vorax screamed. It was a silent scream, the sound of a soul being ripped from a body. The face in the rock cracked. Chunks of stone fell into the solidified obsidian.

Gerald walked to the edge of the lake. He looked down at the dead rock.

"Kaelen," he said.

Kaelen scrambled up.

"Did... did you just kill him?"

"I cited him to death," Gerald said. He looked tired.

His eyes were red.

His hands shook, but he kept the clipboard steady.

"He didn't have a sprinkler system. That's the real problem."

He looked at the blackened face of Vorax.

"However," Gerald said, "I now have a problem."

"What problem?" Kaelen asked.

Gerald pointed to the base of the obsidian lake, and a crack had formed in the stone. Not a natural crack.

A geometric one.

Perfectly straight.

"He didn't die," Gerald said. "He moved."

Kaelen leaned in.

"Moved?"

"Look at the compliance rating," Gerald said.

Kaelen looked at her interface. She had never looked at a Core's rating before. It was a hidden stat.

**[Vorax Core: Rating 0. Status: Dormant. Location: Relocated.]**

"He went deeper," Gerald said. "He used the chaos of the citation to shift his anchor point. He's not in the cavern anymore. He's in the bedrock."

"Where is he?"

Gerald looked down. Below the black stone, below the magma, below the earth.

"He's in the Municipal Library's water main," Gerald said.

Kaelen stared.

"What?"

"I checked the maps when I joined the System," Gerald said. "The library's basement is connected to the city's primary aquifer. Vorax found the pipe. He's living in the water."

Gerald pulled out a red stamp. He slammed it onto his clipboard.

**[Citation: Water Contamination.]** **[Target: Vorax.]**

But the citation fizzled.

**[Error: Target Out of Jurisdiction.]**

Gerald frowned. He tapped the clipboard. It didn't work.

"He's not a dungeon anymore," Gerald said. "He's a utility hazard. The System doesn't regulate utilities. It regulates buildings."

Kaelen looked at the crack in the floor. Water was beginning to seep through. It was black, oily water.

"He's poisoning the city," Kaelen said.

"No," Gerald said. "He's poisoning *my* dungeon.

And if he poisons my dungeon, I lose my Compliance Rating.

If I lose my rating, the System revokes my Core status.

I die."

Gerald looked at the black water. It was rising.

"I cannot cite him," Gerald said. "I have to fix the pipe."

"How?" Kaelen asked. "It's miles deep."

Gerald took off his high-visibility vest. He folded it neatly. He placed it on the obsidian shelf.

"I am going to go down the drain," Gerald said.

Kaelen stared at him.

"You're a Core. You can fly."

"I am an inspector," Gerald said. "Inspectors do not fly. They descend. They check the work."

He walked to the edge of the cracked obsidian. The black water bubbled up. It smelled of old copper and rot.

"Wait," Kaelen said. "If you go in, you'll drown."

"I am a dungeon core," Gerald said. "I do not breathe. I exist.

But the water is toxic. It is violating the sanitation code.

I can't cite it because it's not a dungeon.

I can only repair it.

"

He looked at Kaelen.

"Stay here. If I don't come back in ten minutes, file a report. Tell the Guild I was eaten by a leaky faucet."

Before she could answer, Gerald stepped into the black water.

The liquid swallowed him instantly. No splash. No ripple. Just a hole in the world.

Kaelen stood alone in the silent cavern. The gray void had returned to orange, but the lava was cold and dead.

She waited.

One minute.

Two minutes.

Five minutes.

The water level in the cavern dropped. It drained rapidly, flowing down the crack.

Then, the floor shook.

A voice echoed from the drain. It was not Gerald's voice. It was wet and gurgling.

**"HELP ME."**

It was Vorax.

Kaelen ran to the edge. She looked down.

The water was gone. But in the dark, far below, a light flickered. It was blue. Gerald's interface light.

And next to it, a massive, dark shape.

Vorax was not in the pipe. He was waiting. He had lured Gerald down.

Gerald's voice echoed, distorted by the distance.

**"He is not a Core anymore. He is a parasite. I have to cut him out."**

The light flickered.

**"He is eating the foundation."**

Kaelen pulled out her emergency beacon. She activated it.

**[Distress Signal Sent. Location: Sub-Basement 7.]**

Nothing happened, and the System had blacked out.

Kaelen looked at her hands. Her interface was gone. No health bar. No mana pool.

She was just a woman in a hole.

And in the dark below, the water rose again.
