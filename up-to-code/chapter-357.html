Chapter 357: Compliance Rating Zero

# Chapter 357

The stamp hit the air before the woman did.

A blue rectangle of solidified code slammed into Gerald's chest. It didn't hurt, not physically. It hurt administratively. The sensation was like being audited by the IRS while simultaneously having a load-bearing wall removed.

Gerald stumbled back, his boots scraping against the polished obsidian floor of the Core Chamber. The cage of lines tightened around him, humming with the low-frequency vibration of unresolved zoning disputes.

"Missing permit for a High-Velocity Sub-Structural Alteration," the woman said. Her voice was crisp, the kind of tone used by clerks who had denied applications for three decades and enjoyed every minute of it.

She held a clipboard that glowed with hostile amber light.

"Also, failure to display current occupancy limits. And let's not talk about the fire suppression system. It's decorative at best."

Gerald wiped a smear of black ichor from his cheek. He didn't look at her face. He looked at the stamp on his chest. The text burned into his skin: **VIOLATION 402-B: UNAUTHORIZED ZONING CHANGE.**

He reached for his inspector's satchel, though he knew the physical leather wouldn't do much against a metaphysical Core. He pulled out his pen. The nib snapped off.

"You can't cite me," Gerald said, his voice steady despite the tremor in his hands. "This is a private chamber. It's not public space. You need a warrant from the Inspector General to enter a private Core instance."

The woman tilted her head. She looked exactly as she had in the courtroom, only older. The hard hat was pristine white, not the dirty yellow of a construction worker. This was an official's hat.

"I don't need a warrant," she said.

"I have a subpoena for your compliance records. You've been operating at 400% capacity for the last six hours.

That's a fire hazard, Gerald.

A big one."

Behind her, the code-woven cage shifted. The bars turned into rebar. The air grew hot, smelling of ozone and burning plastic.

Gerald's HUD flickered. A new window popped up, red and flashing.

**SYSTEM ALERT: CITATION SERVED.** **TARGET: GERALD HUTCHINS (CORE ID 001)** **VIOLATION: UNAUTHORIZED STRUCTURAL EXPANSION (ZONE 4, SUB-BASEMENT)** **PENALTY: IMMEDIATE DEBILITATION OF SPAWN CAPACITY BY 80%.** **APPEAL WINDOW: 3 MINUTES.**

Eighty percent.

Gerald's three mold spores, his water leak, and the confused rat were his only defenses. If their stats dropped by eighty percent, a single aggressive slime mold from the neighboring dungeon could wipe them out. The Architect's blue wall was already crumbling at the edges, held together by Gerald's sheer will and a temporary patch job he'd done with duct tape and prayer.

He had three minutes.

Not to argue. Not to explain. To appeal.

Appeals weren't debates. They were logic puzzles. The System wanted to see if Gerald could prove that the violation was technically a compliance requirement of another code.

He looked at the woman. She wasn't attacking.

She was waiting.

Her clipboard was full, and she wanted him to fail.

"Who are you?" Gerald asked.

"Clerk 774. Formerly of the Ashford Municipal Library," she said. "Currently acting as the Auditor for the Architect's unfinished projects.

You're the loose end, Gerald. You're the guy who filed the report that woke him up.

You're the guy who broke the loop.

"

"I followed code," Gerald said.

"Code is a cage," she replied. "And you're standing in it."

Gerald ignored the insult. He closed his eyes. He thought about the Architect.

The blue wall.

The strange geometry of the library's sub-basement, and he remembered the measurements.

When he had first registered as a Core, he had measured the room.

Four thousand square feet. Standard municipal basement. But when he had expanded the dungeon, he hadn't just dug deeper.

He had widened the load-bearing pillars.

Why? Because the original blueprints had a deficiency. The columns were too narrow for the weight of the library above. Gerald had reinforced them.

The woman called it an unauthorized expansion.

But was it?

Gerald opened his eyes, and he tapped his satchel.

He didn't have his pen. He used his finger, tracing a line in the air.

The System recognized the gesture as a command input.

**ACTION: FILE MOTION FOR STAY OF EXECUTION.** **ARGUMENT: STRUCTURAL NECESSITY.**

The text floated in front of him. The woman raised an eyebrow.

"Structural necessity?" she scoffed. "Your dungeon is a sub-basement.

It's not carrying the weight of a library anymore, and the library is gone. The city is gone. This is a dungeon.

You can expand wherever you want.

"

"Wrong," Gerald said. He spoke faster now, the adrenaline making him sharp.

"The System treats the dungeon footprint as a fixed municipal lot.

You cited me for 'Unauthorized Zoning Change.' That implies I changed the boundaries of the lot. I didn't.

"

He pointed at the floor.

"I changed the *load*," Gerald said. "I added support columns to prevent collapse. According to Municipal Code Section 14, Article C, 'any modification that increases structural integrity is deemed a maintenance action, not a zoning change.' Maintenance actions do not require permits. They require only documentation."

The woman's smile vanished.

"You filed that documentation," she said. "Where is it?"

Gerald didn't answer. He looked at the HUD.

**SYSTEM PROCESSING...**

The air grew hotter. The rebar cage tightened.

Gerald felt his spine compress.

His vision blurred, and the debuff was taking effect. His health bar dropped from green to yellow.

He had to find the file.

He didn't have a physical file. He had a memory. He had filed Form 11-B when he first became a Core.

He had filed a structural amendment when he reinforced the walls against the goblin's assault. He had filed a ventilation upgrade after the water leak started.

All those forms. All those stamps.

The System had recorded them.

But the woman had erased the logs. She had come from the courtroom, the place where records went to die.

Gerald's mind raced. He couldn't produce the paper. He had to produce the logic.

He looked at the woman's clipboard. It was glowing amber.

"Your citation," Gerald said. "It's based on a lack of a permit. But you didn't check the maintenance logs. Did you?"

She hesitated. Just for a second.

Gerald seized it, and he shouted, not at her, but at the System.

**ACTION: REQUEST AUDIT OF CITATION SOURCE.** **ARGUMENT: PROCEDURAL ERROR. INSPECTOR FAILURE TO VERIFY MAINTENANCE RECORDS PRIOR TO ISSUANCE.**

It was a long shot. A procedural trap. A bureaucratic booby trap.

The System loved procedural traps. It lived for them.

The air froze. The heat vanished.

The rebar cage loosened.

The woman stepped back, her eyes widening.

"No," she whispered. "That's not... that's not how it works. I checked the main ledger."

"The main ledger was corrupted during the Erasure," Gerald said. He pointed at the blue wall behind her. "The Architect's data was wiped. If your citation relies on the main ledger, and the main ledger is incomplete, your citation is void."

The HUD flashed.

**SYSTEM ALERT: HEARING INITIATED.** **PARTICIPANTS: CORE 001 (Gerald Hutchins) vs. AUDITOR 774.** **TOPIC: VALIDITY OF CITATION 402-B.** **RULE: THE BURDEN OF PROOF LIES WITH THE CITING PARTY.**

Gerald felt a surge of power. Not physical power. Bureaucratic power.

The woman looked at her clipboard. She flipped pages. She flipped them faster.

"It's here," she muttered. "It has to be here."

"It's not," Gerald said.

He walked toward her. The rebar cage parted for him, recognizing his authority as the accused who had triggered the hearing.

"You relied on the Architect's blueprints.

But the blueprints were for a library, which is a dungeon. The codes are different.

You applied Library Code to a Dungeon Code.

That's a jurisdictional error.

"

The woman looked at Gerald. For the first time, she looked afraid.

"You're twisting the rules," she said.

"I'm following them," Gerald replied. "You're the one violating them. By citing me under the wrong code, you've committed a Code Violation of your own. Section 9, Paragraph 3: 'Improper Classification of Regulatory Authority.'"

A red light flashed on the woman's clipboard.

**AUDITOR 774: VIOLATION DETECTED.** **PENALTY: STAT DEBUFF. 'CONFUSION.'**

The woman blinked. She shook her head, trying to clear the fog.

Gerald didn't wait. He reached out and grabbed her wrist.

"You have to serve the citation in person," Gerald said. "I'm serving mine."

He slammed his hand onto her clipboard.

**ACTION: ISSUE CITATION.** **TARGET: AUDITOR 774.** **VIOLATION: IMPROPER CLASSIFICATION OF REGULATORY AUTHORITY.** **SEVERITY: CRITICAL.** **DURATION: 24 HOURS.**

The amber light on her clipboard turned red.

The woman screamed. It wasn't a sound of pain, but of static. Her form flickered. The hard hat tilted, then dissolved into pixels.

Gerald stepped back. He watched her disintegrate.

But she didn't vanish, and she fragmented.

Pieces of her code flew into the room, embedding themselves in the walls, the floor, the ceiling. The cage of rebar shattered, but the shards remained, floating in the air, sharp and dangerous.

The HUD flashed a new message.

**SYSTEM ALERT: APPEAL WON.** **PENALTY REVERSED.** **BONUS: +100 COMPLIANCE POINTS.** **WARNING: TARGET CORE HAS SPLIT.** **FRAGMENTS DETECTED: 12.** **FRAGMENTS ARE ACTIVE.**

Twelve fragments.

Twelve pieces of an Auditor Core, loose in his dungeon.

And they were angry.

From the shadows of the corners, shapes formed. Small, jagged things made of red code and broken glass. They skittered across the floor, leaving trails of corruption.

One of them latched onto Gerald's leg.

His health bar dipped. **DAMAGE: STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY 5%.**

Gerald shook it off, but two more jumped onto his arms.

He couldn't fight them with force. They were too fast, too small. They were bugs in the system. Literal bugs.

He needed to delete them.

But you couldn't delete a Core fragment without a specific tool.

Gerald looked around the room. The desk. The file folder. **CASE FILE 001: THE ARCHITECT.**

He ran to the desk. The fragments swarmed behind him, clicking like insects.

He grabbed the file folder.

Inside was the photograph of the Architect and the woman. And beneath it, a single sheet of paper.

It was a blueprint. But not for the library.

It was a blueprint for the System itself.

A map of the server farm.

At the bottom, in handwritten red ink, was a note.

*"THE SERVER ROOM IS UNDER THE FOUNDATION. TO DELETE, YOU MUST GO TO THE SOURCE."*

Gerald's heart stopped, and he looked at the floor.

He looked at the fragments crawling up his legs.

He looked at the blueprint.

The floor wasn't just obsidian. It was a hatch.

He grabbed the edge of the desk and heaved it aside. Underneath, there was a seam in the stone. A crack.

The fragments were gaining on him. Ten seconds. Maybe five.

Gerald kicked the seam.

It didn't break, and he kicked it again.

A crack spiderwebbed across the floor.

The fragments leaped.

Gerald dove.

He hit the floor, sliding through the crack, falling into darkness.

The last thing he saw before the darkness swallowed him was the HUD.

**LOCATION: UNKNOWN.** **COMPLIANCE RATING: 0.** **WARNING: OFF-SYSTEM ZONE DETECTED.**

He fell.

The floor below was moving.
