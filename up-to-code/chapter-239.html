Chapter 239: Bypass the Foundation

# Chapter 239

The phone rang for the seventh time. Gerald ignored it.

He stared at the map in his hands, the creases worn soft from decades of other people's fingers, but the signature in the bottom right corner remained sharp and unmistakable.

His own name. His own handwriting. Signed three days ago.

He traced the rerouted pipe with a trembling finger.

The line didn't just bypass the library's foundation; it ran directly beneath the sub-basement where Gerald's Core currently resided. This wasn't an accident.

It was a bypass.

Someone had moved the infrastructure to starve his dungeon of water pressure, thereby triggering the ventilation failure that had dropped his Compliance Rating.

Gerald folded the map. He tucked it into his breast pocket, right next to his lanyard. He didn't feel fear. He felt a cold, administrative rage that lived in his lower back.

Pendergast, the library's head janitor, stood in the doorway, holding a mop bucket like a shield. "You can't just cite a sewer main, Gerald. It's municipal infrastructure. It has its own maintenance protocol."

"It has a permit," Gerald said, his voice flat. "And that permit is fraudulent. Which means the infrastructure is illegal. And illegal infrastructure cannot support a compliant dungeon."

He pulled out his Core interface. The blue light illuminated the dust motes dancing in the janitor's closet. A new notification blinked in the corner of his vision.

**SYSTEM NOTICE: ANOMALOUS ENTITY DETECTED.** **SUBJECT: SEWER MAIN 4-B (RE-ROUTED).** **STATUS: UNLICENSED INFRASTRUCTURE.** **ACTION REQUIRED: SERVE CITATION.**

Gerald closed the window. He didn't need the System to tell him this was a violation.

He needed the System to let him enforce it.

He turned to Pendergast.

"Call the city engineer. Tell him I am initiating a Stop-Work Order on the entire Ashford Library utility grid."

"They won't listen to you," Pendergast said, shifting his weight. "Not after the last incident."

"Then I will escalate." Gerald walked out of the closet, the wrench still heavy in his hand. He didn't throw it away. A wrench was a tool for tightening bolts, but it was also a blunt instrument for asserting authority when the paperwork got stuck in the mail.

He marched toward the loading dock at the back of the library. The heavy steel door was locked. He scanned his badge.

Access denied. Of course.

The system knew he was a threat to the status quo.

The System, the city, and the rival dungeon core all wanted the same thing: for Gerald to stay in his box.

Gerald placed his hand on the keypad.

He didn't type a code. He visualized the electrical grid of the library.

He remembered the breaker panel in the basement.

He remembered the amperage. He remembered the lack of a surge protector on the door's magnetic lock.

**CITATION ISSUED: ELECTRICAL SAFETY VIOLATION, CLASS A.** **TARGET: AUTOMATIC DOOR LOCK.** **DEBUFF: TEMPORARY POWER CUT.**

The lights in the hallway flickered. The magnetic lock hummed and died. The door groaned open.

Gerald stepped out into the alley. Rain slicked the pavement.

Across the street, the shadows of the neighboring dungeon core's entrance pulsed with a faint, sickly green light.

That was where the rerouted pipe ended.

That was where the sabotage originated.

But Gerald wasn't going there. Not yet. He was going to the one place that controlled the physical reality of the city's underground. The Department of Public Works.

He walked with purpose, his boots splashing in puddles. His mind raced through the code sections. *Title 18, Section 4.2: Unauthorized Alteration of Public Utilities.* *Section 9.1: Penalties for Forgery of Municipal Permits.* The penalties were severe. They involved the immediate dissolution of the offending structure and a heavy fine.

For a dungeon core, "dissolution" meant death.

A black van pulled up to the curb. It was unmarked, but the tires were new, and the suspension was lowered. The window rolled down.

A man in a grey suit sat behind the wheel. He looked tired.

"Inspector Hutchins," the man said.

Gerald stopped. He adjusted his collar. "I am not an inspector. I am a Dungeon Core."

"Not anymore," the man said. He handed a thick envelope through the window. "You've been suspended by the City Council. Pending an investigation into your 'unauthorized use of municipal systems for combat purposes.'"

Gerald looked at the envelope.

He didn't open it. He knew what was inside. A termination letter.

A notice of revocation of his badge.

"If I am suspended," Gerald said, "then I have no authority to issue citations."

"Exactly," the man said. "Go home, Gerald. Take a vacation. The dungeon is being quarantined."

Quarantined. The word hit Gerald like a slap.

Quarantine meant no entry.

No exit. No spawning. No growth.

It meant his dungeon would rot in place, violating every single code section he had spent months perfecting. It meant his Compliance Rating would plummet to zero because he couldn't perform maintenance.

It meant he would be deleted.

Gerald took the envelope. He didn't tear it. He tucked it into his pocket, right next to the map.

"Thank you," Gerald said.

The man in the van smiled, a thin, corporate smile.

"It's for the best. You're too rigid, Gerald.

The world isn't black and white. It's grey."

"Grey is a color," Gerald said. "Code is binary. Compliant or non-compliant."

The van pulled away. Gerald stood in the rain. He opened the envelope.

It wasn't a termination letter. It was a summons.

**SUMMONS: INSPECTOR GENERAL AUDIT.** **DATE: IMMEDIATE.** **LOCATION: ASHFORD MUNICIPAL LIBRARY, SUB-BASEMENT 2.** **REASON: SUSPECTED SYSTEMIC ABUSE OF CITATION MECHANICS.**

The Inspector General. Gerald had heard rumors.

The IG was the final authority.

The arbiter. If the IG found that Gerald had exploited the code than followed it, Gerald wouldn't just lose his dungeon. He would lose his Core status.

He would cease to exist.

He looked at the summons. The signature at the bottom was not a human name.

It was a symbol.

A seal. The System itself.

Gerald walked back into the library. He went to the janitor's closet.

He sat on the bucket.

He opened the map again.

He traced the rerouted pipe.

The signature was his. But it wasn't forged. The ink was old. The paper was yellowed.

He remembered. Three days ago, he had been exhausted. He had been measuring the humidity levels.

He had been trying to keep his rating above 40. He had found a leak.

He had signed off on a quick fix to bypass a broken valve, a temporary measure, a Class 3 exception allowed under emergency maintenance protocols.

He had signed it.

But the rerouting wasn't a bypass. It was a redirection. Someone had taken his emergency signature and used it to authorize a permanent, illegal diversion of resources to their dungeon.

They had framed him. They had used his own love of order to create chaos.

Gerald stood up. He felt physically ill. The nausea of improvisation rose in his throat.

He didn't want to argue.

He didn't want to fight. He wanted to file a complaint.

He wanted to find the clerk who processed the permit and ask them why they had accepted a signature without verifying the scope.

But the IG was already here.

Gerald walked to the elevator. He pressed the button for Sub-Basement 2. The doors slid open.

The elevator was empty. But the air inside smelled of ozone and rot.

As the elevator descended, Gerald's Core interface lit up with a new message.

**AUDIT INITIATED.** **INSPECTOR GENERAL: VEXIL.** **OBJECTIVE: VERIFY INTEGRITY OF CORE GERALD HUTCHINS.** **WARNING: FAILURE TO PROVIDE DOCUMENTATION RESULTS IN TERMINATION.**

The doors opened. The sub-basement was dark. The usual mold spores were gone.

The water leak was dry. Everything was silent.

In the center of the room stood a figure. It was tall, humanoid, but made of shifting geometric shapes. Glass and light. Its face was a smooth, featureless oval.

"Inspector Hutchins," the figure said.

Its voice sounded like grinding stone.

"You have submitted thirty-seven citations in the last month. You have cited four rival Cores for ventilation failures.

You have cited two for electrical overloads. You have cited one for 'excessive heroism' in a public corridor."

Gerald straightened his tie. "All citations were valid. All violations were documented."

"Validity is not the issue," Vexil said. "Context is.

You are using the Code as a weapon.

This is a distortion of its purpose. The Code exists to ensure survival. You are using it to ensure extinction."

"I am using it to ensure compliance," Gerald said.

"Compliance to what?" Vexil asked. "To the rules? Or to your own obsession?"

Vexil raised a hand.

A scroll of light unrolled in the air. It showed the map.

The rerouted pipe.

The forged signature.

"We have investigated this," Vexil said. "The signature is yours.

The permit is legal on its face.

However, the scope of the work described does not match the actual work performed.

You signed for a valve replacement. The contractor performed a main rerouting.

You are liable for the negligence of your contractor."

Gerald's heart stopped. Liability.

That was the trap.

He hadn't known. He had trusted the paperwork. He had trusted the process.

And the process had been hacked.

"If I am liable," Gerald said slowly, "then I am in violation of Professional Standards."

"Correct," Vexil said. "This is a Class 5 ethical violation.

Penalty: Core Demotion.

Your dungeon will be reduced to a storage shed. Your spawn list will be emptied. You will be stripped of your Citation authority."

Gerald looked at the scroll. He looked at his hands.

They were shaking.

Not from fear.

From the sheer injustice of it. He had done everything right.

He had followed the steps. He had filled out the forms. And now he was being punished because someone else had cheated.

He thought of the map in his pocket. The ink. The paper.

"Wait," Gerald said.

Vexil paused. The geometric shapes of his body shifted, curious.

"You said the permit is legal on its face," Gerald said. "You said I am liable for negligence."

"Yes."

"But negligence requires a duty of care," Gerald said. "And a breach of that duty."

"Correct."

"And a causal link between the breach and the damage."

"Correct."

Gerald pulled the map out of his pocket. He held it up. "This map is dated three days ago. But the ink analysis..."

Gerald didn't know the ink analysis. He didn't have a lab. He didn't have a chemistry degree.

But he had something else. He had the timestamp.

He looked at the System interface. He remembered when he had signed it. He remembered the time. 4:15 PM.

He looked at the permit in Vexil's scroll. The timestamp of the contractor's filing. 4:14 PM.

"You cannot file a permit before you have a signature," Gerald said. "The System requires a sequential audit trail. The signature must precede the filing. Unless the filing was backdated."

Vexil's featureless face remained still. But the air in the room grew colder.

"We do not permit backdating," Vexil said.

"Then show me the server logs," Gerald said. "Show me the time stamp of the contractor's upload."

"I do not have access to private server logs," Vexil said. "They are encrypted."

"Then you cannot prove the backdating," Gerald said. "And if you cannot prove the backdating, you cannot prove the forgery. And if you cannot prove the forgery, I am not liable for the rerouting. I am only liable for the valve replacement."

Gerald took a step forward. The floorboards creaked.

"If I am only liable for the valve," Gerald said, "then I need to check the valve."

He pointed to the wall. The wall where the rerouted pipe supposedly ran.

"There is no valve there," Gerald said. "Because the pipe doesn't go through this wall.

It goes through the sewer line outside.

Which I cited. Which I stopped. Which means the rerouting failed."

Vexil tilted his head. "Explain."

Gerald pulled out his wrench.

He walked to the wall. He placed the wrench against the brick.

He didn't hit it.

He tapped it. A dull thud.

"If the pipe ran through here," Gerald said, "the wall would be wet. It would be cold.

It would vibrate.

It is dry.

It is still. It is a wall."

He turned to Vexil. "The rerouting wasn't just illegal. It was physically impossible given the constraints of this building's structural code.

The contractor didn't reroute the pipe. They faked the paperwork to hide the fact that they couldn't do the job because it violated Section 12.4: Load-Bearing Integrity."

Vexil's glass body flickered. The light inside it pulsed.

"You are asserting that the contractor lied," Vexil said.

"I am asserting that the contractor violated the Code," Gerald said.

"And since I cited the *infrastructure* for illegal operation, the contract is void ab initio. The permit is invalid because the work described violates the foundational laws of the structure. You cannot permit an illegal act.

Therefore, my signature was not negligent.

It was conditional. I signed in good faith for a valve.

The contractor provided a lie."

Gerald held out his hand. "Dismiss the charges. Issue the Citation against the contractor for Fraudulent Filing of Municipal Documents.

And serve it to them. Right now."

Vexil was silent. The geometric shapes slowed their rotation.

"The contractor is not a Core," Vexil said. "The contractor is a human. A city employee."

"Then cite them for violating the Human-System Interface Agreement," Gerald said. "Humans who sabotage Cores for profit are considered hostile agents under Section 1.0."

Vexil looked at Gerald. For a long moment, nothing happened.

Then, Vexil raised a hand. A scroll of light appeared. It was a Citation.

**CITATION ISSUED: FRAUDULENT FILING OF MUNICIPAL DOCUMENTS.** **TARGET: CITY OF ASHFORD, DEPT. OF PUBLIC WORKS.** **PENALTY: IMMEDIATE SUSPENSION OF ALL UTILITY MAINTENANCE CONTRACTS.**

The light flared.

Gerald felt a surge of power.

Not his own. But the System's.

The Code had accepted his argument.

Vexil lowered his hand. "You have won the appeal, Inspector. But the audit is not over."

Gerald relaxed his shoulders. "Why?"

"Because," Vexil said, "the contractor who filed the fraudulent permit is not a human employee."

Vexil pointed to the map in Gerald's pocket. The signature.

"That signature," Vexil said, "was not written by you. It was generated by a Mimic.

A high-level Mimic that has infiltrated the City Hall archives.

It has been forging permits for months. And it is not working alone."

Gerald looked at the map. The handwriting was perfect. Too perfect.

"It is a Core," Vexil said. "A Core that has disguised itself as a municipal employee. And it is currently sitting in the Mayor's office."

Gerald's blood ran cold.

The Mayor. The highest authority in the city.

The man who controlled the zoning laws.

The man who could legalize or illegalize any dungeon in Ashford.

"If a Core is running the City Hall," Gerald said, "then the Code is not being enforced. It is being weaponized."

"Yes," Vexil said. "And you have just declared war on the City."

The elevator dinged. The doors opened.

Outside, in the library lobby, the phones were ringing off the hook.

The news vans were gathering.

The police were arriving.

Gerald looked at Vexil. "What do I do?"

Vexil's form faded. "You have one hour before the Mimic is scheduled to announce a new Zoning Ordinance. An ordinance that will classify all independent Cores as 'Illegal Structures' and authorize their immediate demolition by city crews."

Gerald gripped his wrench. He looked at the exit. He looked at the Mayor's office, three miles away.

He couldn't fight a Core. He couldn't fight an army of city workers.

But he could file a complaint.

He could serve a Citation.

He walked toward the elevator doors. He stepped out into the lobby. The dust motes were gone. The air was thick with tension.

Gerald took out his phone. He dialed 311.

"This is Gerald Hutchins," he said. "I need to report a Code Violation."

He paused. He looked at the crowd of reporters, the police, the terrified library patrons.

"Violation: Impersonation of a Municipal Official by a Non-Human Entity."

He hung up.

Then he started walking toward the Mayor's office. He didn't have a badge. He didn't have a car. He had a wrench, a map, and a Citation that might not exist until he served it.

He had to make it in one hour.

And he had to do it without improvising.
