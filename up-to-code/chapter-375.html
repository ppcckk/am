Chapter 375: The Rat Brings a Shovel

# Chapter 375

The appeal form was wrong.

Gerald knew this before he finished writing the word

"denial." The rat watched him, its tiny black paws resting on the edge of the wooden table. It had not moved since Vane left. It had not blinked. It sat with the rigid, terrified posture of a creature that understood its own precarious legal standing.

The fine was one loot crystal. The violation was

"failure to wear proper safety gear." Gerald had not been wearing a helmet. He had been sitting in a concrete cell, writing an appeal against a citation for not wearing a helmet. The logic was circular and, therefore, inadmissible.

He crossed out the last line. He started again.

**APPEAL #: 001-A** **CLAIMANT: GERALD HUTCHINS** **BASIS: THE CONCEPT OF

"SAFETY GEAR" IS INAPPLICABLE TO NON-COMBAT ADMINISTRATIVE HOLDING FACILITIES.**

The rat squeaked. It was a short, sharp sound, like a nail hitting tile.

Gerald paused. He looked at the rat.

"Is that a counter-argument?" Gerald asked.

The rat did not answer. It pushed the green shard closer to Gerald's hand. The shard pulsed with a faint, sickly light.

Gerald picked it up. The moment his skin touched the crystal, a window slammed into his peripheral vision.

**SYSTEM ALERT: LOOT CRYSTAL DETECTED.** **SOURCE: UNLICENSED ENTITY.** **STATUS: CONTRABAND.**

Gerald set the crystal down. He wiped his hand on his trousers.

"Keep the crystal," Gerald said. "I am not handling evidence without gloves."

The rat hesitated. Then, with a speed that belied its size, it snatched the shard back and shoved it deep into its suit pocket.

A low hum vibrated through the walls. The concrete floor beneath the table trembled. This was not the vibration of the library's HVAC system. This was the vibration of a Core being audited.

Gerald stood up. He walked to the door of the cell. He knocked. The metal rang dull and hollow.

"I require clarification on the definition of 'safety gear' in a sub-basement detention facility," Gerald called out.

No answer, and he returned to the table. He looked at the rat. The rat looked at the door.

"We are not leaving until the appeal is filed," Gerald said. "And you are not citing me again until you have reviewed my previous filings."

The rat sat back on its haunches. It reached into another pocket of its tiny suit. It pulled out a second scroll. This one was red.

Gerald's stomach tightened. He hated red scrolls. Red meant immediate enforcement.

He unrolled it.

**CITATION #: 002** **ISSUED TO: GERALD HUTCHINS** **VIOLATION: IMPROPER STORAGE OF CLASS-A CONTRABAND.** **FINE: 500 STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY POINTS.** **DEBUFF APPLIED: MINOR FRACTURES IN ALL LOAD-BEARING WALLS.**

Gerald felt it. A crack, thin as a hairline, splintered across the concrete behind him. Dust drifted down from the ceiling.

"You cited yourself?" Gerald asked, his voice flat. "For *my* violation?"

The rat nodded. It pointed a claw at Gerald's chest.

*You are holding the crystal,* the rat's posture seemed to say. *It is in your zone. It is your liability.*

Gerald stared at the rat. For a moment, the dry, bureaucratic detachment that had kept him sane for thirty years flickered.

"That is not how liability works," Gerald said. "The crystal is physically located within the rat's inventory. The Core's inventory is separate from the Inspector's person. Unless you can prove I took possession, the citation is void."

The rat tilted its head. It produced a quill. It dipped it into an invisible inkwell that hovered in the air.

**COUNTER-CITATION #002-A** **BASIS: CONSTRUCTIVE POSSESSION.** **ARGUMENT: THE CLAIMANT TOUCHED THE CONTRABAND. THE CLAIMANT DID NOT DISCARD IT. THE CLAIMANT RETAINED CONTROL OF THE ZONE.**

Gerald read the argument. It was stupid.

It was also, terrifyingly, legally plausible within the System's arbitrary logic.

If he argued this, he would lose. The System favored physical proximity.

He needed a different angle.

Gerald looked at the crack in the wall. Then he looked at the rat.

"Where did you get the crystal?" Gerald asked.

The rat pointed to the floor. Specifically, to a small drainage grate near the door.

"Through the egress route?" Gerald asked.

The rat nodded.

Gerald walked to the grate, and he crouched. He pulled a measuring tape from his pocket.

He did not need the tape; he knew the dimensions of municipal drains better than he knew his own birthday.

But the act of measuring centered him.

He extended the tape. Six inches wide. Four inches deep.

"This is a non-compliant drainage opening," Gerald said.

The rat blinked.

Gerald turned back to the table. He grabbed the red citation. He wrote on the back of it.

**COUNTER-CITATION #002-B** **ISSUED TO: THE SYSTEM (VANE/INSPECTOR GENERAL)** **VIOLATION: FAILURE TO MAINTAIN COMPLIANT EGRESS IN DETENTION FACILITY.** **BASIS: DRAINAGE GRATE WIDTH LESS THAN 8 INCHES PER MUNICIPAL CODE 14.2-B. BLOCKAGE OF EGRESS VOIDS ALL LIABILITY FOR ITEMS ENTERING THROUGH THAT EGRESS.**

Gerald underlined the line twice.

"If the egress is illegal," Gerald said to the rat, "then anything that enters through it is legally inadmissible. The crystal did not enter your dungeon.

It entered a non-dungeon space.

Therefore, it cannot be cited as Core Contraband.

It is merely municipal waste.

"

The rat stared at him. Its whiskers twitched.

For a long second, nothing happened.

Then, the red scroll dissolved into ash.

The crack in the wall stopped spreading. The dust stopped falling.

Gerald exhaled. It was a short, sharp breath. He felt the tension in his shoulders drop an inch.

The rat reached into its pocket again. It pulled out a third scroll.

This one was blue.

Gerald's hand froze on the pen. Blue was not citation. Blue was invitation.

He unrolled it.

**NOTICE OF HEARING** **CASE: 375-IG-001** **PRESEIDING OFFICER: VANE** **SUBJECT: JOINT LIABILITY OF UNLICENSED ENTITY AND INSPECTOR** **TIME: 10 MINUTES** **LOCATION: THE VOID BETWEEN WALLS**

Gerald looked at the rat.

"It's not a citation," Gerald said. "It's a hearing. They're bringing us both into the same case."

The rat nodded. It dressed itself. It adjusted its tie.

It straightened its cuffs. It looked less like an idiot and more like a junior associate at a law firm that was about to be disbarred.

"Where is the 'Void Between Walls'?" Gerald asked.

The rat pointed to the corner of the room. The corner that contained a small, unsealed crack in the foundation.

Gerald walked to the corner. He placed his hand on the concrete. It felt cold. Not cold like winter cold, but cold like absence.

"We need a witness," Gerald said. "Or a mediator. Vane is the prosecutor and the judge. That is a conflict of interest."

The rat looked at him. It shook its head. *No witnesses here.*

Gerald closed his eyes. He thought of the library. He thought of the shelves. He thought of the strict, silent order of the stacks.

He opened his eyes. He looked at the rat.

"You have a license," Gerald said. "Vane said you have an expiring visa."

The rat nodded eagerly.

"If you are deported," Gerald said, "you cease to exist in this jurisdiction. You cannot argue. You cannot be held. You vanish."

The rat went still.

Gerald picked up the blue scroll. He held it over the drainage grate.

"If I drop this into the non-compliant egress," Gerald said, "the System will record it as entering a prohibited zone. The hearing notice becomes invalid. The case is dismissed for lack of standing."

The rat lunged. It was fast, faster than a rat should be. It grabbed the edge of the scroll.

Gerald held on, and they stared at each other. Man and rat. Inspector and Partner.

"If you lose the hearing, you are cited for aiding and abetting," Gerald said.

"If I dismiss the hearing, you lose your visa. But you also lose the crystal. You lose your partner.

You lose your code.

"

The rat's grip tightened. Its claws dug into Gerald's thumb.

Gerald did not pull away. He watched the rat's eyes.

They were not animal eyes, and they were calculating. They were desperate.

"Why help me?" Gerald asked.

The rat's mouth opened. A tiny, raspy voice emerged. It sounded like dry leaves skittering over pavement.

"Because," the rat said, "you are the only one who reads the footnotes."

The word *footnotes* hung in the air.

Gerald's heart hammered against his ribs. He had never heard a spawn speak with intent before.

Never.

The rats in the library were nuisances.

This rat was a lawyer.

"If you speak," Gerald said, "you are no longer a biological entity. You are a sentient core-adjacent construct. You require a Form 11-C.

You require a mental capacity evaluation. You are in violation of your own license.

"

The rat smiled. It was a horrible, toothy smile.

"I know," the rat said. "I'm running on borrowed time. So are you."

The wall behind Gerald exploded, which was not an explosion of fire or force. It was an explosion of light. White, blinding, sterile light.

The concrete dissolved. The table fell into the abyss. The rat fell with it.

Gerald scrambled to grab the edge of the floor. His fingers slipped on the smooth, newly formed glass-like surface of the Void.

He hung there. Below him, the rat hung by one claw, its suit torn, its green shard falling into the darkness.

Vane stood on the other side of the gap. He held the blue scroll.

"Too late for a dismissal, Gerald," Vane said. His voice echoed in the void. "The hearing has begun."

Vane raised his hand. A gavel made of pure geometry slammed down in the air between them.

**HEARING OPENED.** **CHARGES: FRAUDULENT CITATION BY GERALD HUTCHINS.** **CHARGES: TREASON BY UNLICENSED ENTITY (RAT).** **PENALTY: TOTAL STRUCTURAL COLLAPSE.**

Gerald looked down at his hands. They were shaking.

He looked at the rat.

"Jump," the rat said.

Gerald looked at the empty air below.

"Jump where?" Gerald asked.

"The Void has floors," the rat said. "But you have to know where they are. Do you know the load-bearing limits of the System's aesthetic?"

Gerald looked at the shimmering, unstable ground beneath his dangling feet. It looked solid. It felt like nothing.

"No," Gerald said.

"Then measure," the rat said. "Or we fall."

Gerald's mind raced. He did not have a tape measure. He did not have a clipboard. He had thirty-one years of instinct.

He closed his eyes, and he visualized the grid. The standard municipal spacing for library shelving. Three feet.

Four feet.

The center line.

He kicked off the ledge.

He fell.

For a second, there was only wind and the scream of the rat fading away.

Then, his foot hit solid ground.

Not concrete. Not dirt. But a thin, translucent plane of light, exactly three feet wide.

Gerald stumbled. He caught his balance. He looked around.

He was standing in a vast, dark space. Above him, the library ceiling was gone. Below him, an infinite grid of light-paths stretched into the darkness.

The rat landed on a path ten feet away. It straightened its tie.

Vane stood at the end of the path, fifty yards away. He held a ledger.

"Welcome to the Code," Vane said. "Now, prove you belong here."

Gerald looked at the path under his feet. He looked at the rat. He looked at the infinite, branching grid of light that represented the entire dungeon system.

He took a step forward. The light under his foot flickered.

It turned red.

**WARNING: LOAD BEARING EXCEEDED.**

Gerald stopped. He looked at his own body. He looked at his shoes.

"Take off your shoes," the rat said.

Gerald looked at the rat.

"My shoes?"

"Its a weight limit," the rat said. "The System calculates mass based on intent. You are carrying the weight of your own doubt. It's heavier than you."

Gerald stared at the rat.

"That is not physics," Gerald said.

"That is code," the rat said. "And you are currently non-compliant."

Gerald unbuckled his belt. He took off his shoes. He placed them on the light-path.

The light turned green, and he took another step. And another.

As he walked, the grid around him changed.

The red warnings faded. The dark void filled with schematics. Walls appeared.

Doors appeared. Windows appeared.

He was building his dungeon. Not with spawn. Not with combat. With structure.

Vane watched. He did not move. He did not strike.

When Gerald reached the center, he stopped. He looked at Vane.

"I have re-calculated the load," Gerald said. "The citation is invalid because the target zone was non-existent at the time of issuance."

Vane smiled. It was the same thin, cruel smile.

"Good," Vane said. "Then you are ready for the real assignment."

Vane snapped his fingers.

The grid shifted. The walls of Gerald's imaginary dungeon shattered. The floor dropped away.

In its place, a massive, roaring fire erupted in the void. Blue, magical fire. It consumed the air. It consumed the light-paths.

And running out of the fire, screaming, was a party of four adventurers. They were burning. They were dying.

They ran toward Gerald.

"Help us!" the knight screamed.

Gerald looked at Vane.

"Fire," Gerald said. "That is a Class 4 violation."

Vane pointed at the adventurers.

"They are your spawns now," Vane said. "If they die, you lose your core. If you stop the fire, you break the rules of the System. You have to choose."

Gerald looked at the burning adventurers. He looked at the fire. He looked at his bare feet.

He reached into his pocket. He did not have a fire extinguisher. He did not have a spell.

He had a pen, and he wrote in the air.

**CITATION: UNAUTHORIZED COMBUSTION.**

The fire froze.

The adventurers froze.

Vane's smile vanished.

"That is not a solution," Vane said. "That is a pause."

Gerald looked at the frozen flames. He looked at the rat, who was now hiding behind Gerald's leg.

"It is a delay," Gerald said. "And delays are how we win."

The ground beneath Gerald shook. Not from the fire. From below.

Something massive was rising.

Vane looked down. His face went pale.

"You didn't read the fine print on the last citation, did you?" Vane whispered.

Gerald looked down through the translucent floor.

Beneath the void, beneath the grid, beneath the fire, a single, confused rat was digging a hole.

And this time, it was bringing a shovel.
