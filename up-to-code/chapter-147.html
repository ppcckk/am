Chapter 147: The High School Walks

# Chapter 147

The library groaned.

It was not the settling of foundations or the creak of timber. It was a vocalization, deep and resonant, vibrating through the floorboards beneath Gerald's shoes. The violet light of the Inspector General pulsed in rhythm with the sound, casting long, jagged shadows against the circulation desk.

Gerald did not back away. He stepped forward.

His pen was broken. The plastic casing lay in two pieces on the linoleum.

He ignored it.

His mind, usually a fortress of procedural caution, had locked into a single, rigid trajectory. The opponent was not a monster. It was a structure.

And structures have rules.

"The library has awakened," the IG said. The voice did not come from the figure's mouth, for it had none.

It emanated from the clipboard, which hovered in the air with unnatural stability.

"Core Registration is complete. Designation: Ashford Municipal. Classification: Public Facility.

Threat Level: Catastrophic."

Brix stepped up beside Gerald. The knight's hand rested on the hilt of his sword, but he did not draw it. He looked at the swirling light, then at Gerald.

"Can we fight a building?" Brix asked.

"You can fight the things inside it," Gerald replied. His voice was flat, devoid of the tremor that had threatened to paralyze him when he first saw the anomaly in his own reflection. "But you cannot fight the code."

The library shifted.

Shelves rearranged themselves. A wall of encyclopedias slid sideways, revealing a corridor that had not existed moments before. The air pressure dropped. The scent of old paper and dust intensified, becoming thick and suffocating.

Gerald pulled a fresh pen from his pocket. He clicked it. The sound was tiny, insignificant against the rumble of the awakened structure, but it anchored him.

"Violation One," Gerald said.

He spoke to the air, projecting his voice as he would in a courtroom, though he was standing in a circulation area.

"Improper egress. The newly formed corridor lacks illuminated exit signage.

It also lacks a minimum clear width of thirty-two inches for accessibility compliance."

The violet light flickered.

"Core Ashford is contesting the citation," the IG said.

"Overruled," Gerald said. "The corridor leads to a dead end.

I can see the drywall through the gap.

That is a trap. Under System Rule 4.2, a trap without a corresponding loot drop or combat encounter is a violation of Fair Play and Basic Safety Standards."

The library roared.

A section of the floor buckled. Brix stumbled, catching himself on the desk. The adventurers watched, their faces pale, their weapons drawn but useless. You could not stab a zoning ordinance.

Gerald walked toward the buckled floor. He did not look at the abyss opening up. He looked at the edges.

"Violation Two," he continued, writing on his clipboard as he spoke.

"Structural compromise. The flooring joists are exposed.

There is no subflooring material.

This creates a tripping hazard and a risk of catastrophic failure under dynamic load. I am citing the Core for Negligence in Maintenance."

The library reacted violently.

A shelf of hardcovers detached itself from the wall. It swung like a pendulum, aimed directly at Gerald's head.

Brix shouted a warning. Gerald did not move.

The shelf hit an invisible barrier three feet in front of Gerald. It splintered against a force field generated by his own Compliance Rating.

His dungeon, the sub-basement beneath this library, was currently rated 47.

It was low, but it was active.

The citation he had just issued had triggered a System enforcement pulse.

The shelf shattered into confetti.

"Interesting," the IG said. The figure tilted its head. "You are using your own low compliance to anchor the enforcement radius. You are citing the library while standing within the range of your own deficient zone."

"I am a witness," Gerald said. "And the Core of Ashford Municipal Library is within the jurisdiction of the Ashford Municipal Building Inspector."

"This is not a municipal dispute," the IG said. "This is a Hearing. The opponent is a Core. You must prove the violation is intentional or negligent to apply a debuff."

Gerald paused. He looked at the broken shelf. He looked at the violet light.

"Prove it?" Gerald asked.

"Yes. Argue the logic. If the violation is accidental, the citation is voided. If it is negligent, the Core takes a 15% stat reduction to all physical attributes for the duration of the Hearing."

The library did not speak. It simply grew.

The ceiling lowered. The walls pressed inward. The air grew hot.

"Violation Three," Gerald said, his voice rising. "Inadequate ventilation. The humidity levels are exceeding 60% relative humidity.

This promotes mold growth. I have three Mold Spores in my own dungeon.

They are level one.

They are weak. But they are here. And the library is creating an environment that allows them to thrive."

Gerald pulled a small vial from his pocket. It contained a sample of water he had collected from his own sub-basement leak.

He uncorked it.

"I am introducing a biological agent into the hearing zone," Gerald said. "This is a controlled test. If the library's air filtration system is non-compliant, the spores will multiply. If they are compliant, they will be neutralized."

He dropped the vial.

The glass shattered. The water spread.

The library's response was instantaneous.

Wind rushed from the vents. Not the gentle, circulating air of a functioning HVAC system, but a gale. The paper on the desk flew into the air. The adventurers ducked, covering their faces.

The spores did not multiply. They were blown away, scattered into the dust motes.

"Neutralized," the IG said. "The library's ventilation is functioning. Citation Three is void."

Gerald narrowed his eyes.

He had expected this. The library was old, but it was maintained.

It had a system.

He needed to find the crack in that system. He needed to find the part of the building that was truly, fundamentally broken.

He looked at the load-bearing column in the center of the room. It was wrapped in decorative oak paneling.

"Violation Four," Gerald said. "Concealment of structural defects."

He walked to the column. He placed his hand on the wood.

"Core Ashford, you are hiding a crack. I can feel it.

The vibration is irregular.

The oak paneling is installed to mask a fissure in the concrete core.

This is a violation of Code Section 12.4: Disclosure of Material Defects."

The library shuddered.

"You have no proof," the IG said.

"I have a hammer," Gerald said.

He reached into his bag. He did not have a hammer. He had a tape measure.

He had a level. He had a flashlight.

He took out the flashlight. He clicked it on.

He shone the light through the keyhole of the decorative door that had appeared in the oak paneling. It was a small, ornate door, likely a maintenance access.

Gerald did not look through the keyhole. He looked at the shadow cast by the crack he knew was there.

"Look at the shadow," Gerald said to the IG.

"The light source is from the ceiling. The shadow is falling from left to right. But the column is cylindrical.

The shadow should be uniform.

It is not. It is jagged.

That indicates an internal fracture."

The library's walls pulsed. The violet light dimmed.

"Core Ashford contests this," the IG said. "The shadow is caused by the angle of the flashlight."

"Then the Core is lying," Gerald said. "Because I am holding the flashlight steady. My hand is not shaking.

My measurements are precise. I have inspected this building for thirty-one years.

I know the grain of this oak.

I know the age of this concrete.

The crack was there when I filed Form 8-C last year. The library has been hiding it."

The air went still.

The adventurers held their breath.

"Core Ashford," the IG said. "You have one opportunity to argue the defense."

The library did not speak. But the floor beneath Gerald's feet tilted.

Not a sudden drop. A slow, deliberate tilt.

The circulation desk slid toward the center of the room. The shelves groaned. The books spilled onto the floor, a cascade of paper and leather.

Gerald did not run. He planted his feet. He widened his stance. He looked at the angle of the tilt.

Three degrees.

"Violation Five," Gerald said. "Active destabilization of the hearing environment. This is obstruction of justice."

He pulled out his pen. He wrote the citation.

"The Core is attempting to physically disqualify the Inspector by creating an unsafe condition during the Hearing. This is a Critical Violation. The penalty is immediate suspension of all spawning and movement abilities."

The library tilted further. Five degrees.

Brix grabbed Gerald's arm. "Gerald, the floor is going to give out."

"Let it," Gerald said.

"What?"

"If the floor gives out, it proves the structural defect I cited. It is self-evident proof. The Core cannot argue against a collapse it caused."

The library froze.

The tilt stopped.

The violet light flared, brighter than before. It was angry. It was afraid.

"Core Ashford," the IG said. "The argument is noted. The System is evaluating the validity of the self-inflicted collapse claim."

Gerald waited. He watched the cracks in the oak paneling. They were widening.

"If the collapse is deemed accidental, you are fined for obstruction," the IG said. "If it is deemed intentional, the Core is stripped of its Core Status for one hour."

One hour.

An hour without a Core meant the library would be inert. It would be just a building. And an inert building could be inspected.

And an inspected building could be condemned.

Gerald looked at the IG. He looked at the library.

"Strip it," Gerald said.

The IG paused. "You are asking for maximum penalty. The Core has appealed three times in the last ten minutes. The system requires a unanimous decision from the adjudicating logic."

"I am the logic," Gerald said. "I am the inspector. I am the witness. I am the code."

He stepped onto the tilted floor. He balanced. He looked at the crack.

"Core Ashford," Gerald said. "You have thirty seconds to prove the crack was not intentional. If you do not, I will file a report that brings the entire municipal district to a halt."

The library did not respond.

The crack widened.

A piece of the oak paneling fell off.

Beneath it, the concrete was black. It was not just cracked. It was rotting. The rebar was exposed, rusted orange and flaking away.

The IG's clipboard glowed red.

"Violation confirmed," the IG said. "Intentional concealment. Material defect. Critical safety risk."

The violet light shattered.

The library screamed.

It was not a sound. It was a feeling. A wave of despair that hit Gerald in the chest, knocking the wind out of him. He staggered, but he did not fall.

The Core of Ashford Municipal Library vanished.

The swirling light was gone. The oppressive presence was gone. The building was just a building. Old, tired, and broken.

But the danger was not over.

From the shadows of the stacks, a new figure emerged.

It was not the library. It was not the IG.

It was a humanoid shape made of ink and paper. It wore a suit. It had a tie. And it was holding a briefcase.

The IG looked at the figure. The IG's face, if it had one, seemed to frown.

"Who is that?" Brix asked, raising his sword.

Gerald lowered his clipboard. He looked at the figure. He recognized the tie. He recognized the briefcase.

"That is the Auditor," Gerald said.

The Auditor stepped forward. The floor did not tilt for it. The air did not grow hot for it. It walked with the casual certainty of a man who owns the land he stands on.

"Gerald Hutchins," the Auditor said. His voice was dry, like paper rubbing against paper. "You have won the hearing."

"I did," Gerald said. "The library is condemned?"

"No," the Auditor said. "The library is suspended. Which means you are now the primary authority in this sector."

Gerald frowned. "That is not right. The library is a public facility. It belongs to the city."

"The city is a concept," the Auditor said. "The Core is the reality. And you have just defeated the Core. By System Law, the victor absorbs the jurisdiction."

The Auditor opened his briefcase.

Inside was not documents. Inside was a map.

It was the map of Ashford. And it was changing.

Lines of red light spread from the library, connecting to the other dungeons in the city. The bank. The hospital. The high school.

"You have claimed the library," the Auditor said. "Which means you have claimed the easement rights for the entire municipal grid."

Gerald looked at the map. He looked at the red lines.

"I didn't claim anything," Gerald said. "I cited a violation."

"You cited the library," the Auditor said. "The library is the hub. The hub controls the network. You are now the Hub."

The Auditor handed Gerald a new clipboard. It was black. It hummed with power.

"Your Compliance Rating is 47," the Auditor said. "But your jurisdiction is now Ashford City. If your rating falls below 50, the entire grid collapses.

Every dungeon in the city dies. Including yours."

Gerald looked at the black clipboard. He looked at his hands. They were shaking.

He looked at Brix.

Brix looked at Gerald. He did not speak. He only stepped back.

Gerald looked at the map.

He saw the red lines connecting to the other cores. He saw the names of the dungeons. He saw the Compliance Ratings of the other cores.

They were all high.

They were all safe.

Until now.

He was the weak link.

He had to raise his rating to 50. Immediately.

Or the city died.

Gerald picked up the black clipboard. He clicked his pen.

"Where do I start?" Gerald asked.

The Auditor smiled. It was a thin, papery smile.

"Start with the high school," the Auditor said. "They have a fire code violation. And they have an army."

The red line connecting the high school to Gerald's dungeon pulsed.

Gerald felt a tug in his chest. A physical pull. The high school was pulling on his Compliance Rating. He could feel his own stats dropping.

He looked at his spawn list.

**[Current Spawns: 3 Mold Spores (Level 1), 1 Confused Rat (Level 1), 1 Water Leak (Level 2 Hazard), 1 Egress Sign (Level 3 Witness).]**

It was not enough.

He needed more.

He needed an army.

But he was a building inspector.

He looked at the Auditor. He looked at the door to the library exit.

"I need a permit," Gerald said.

The Auditor's smile widened.

"Denied," the Auditor said.

"But you have an opening. The high school's auditorium is currently undergoing renovations. It is unpermitted.

That is a violation. You can cite it."

"I can cite it," Gerald said. "But I need to be there to serve it."

"Go," the Auditor said.

Gerald turned. He looked at Brix.

"We're going to the high school," Gerald said.

Brix looked at the black clipboard. He looked at the red map. He looked at Gerald's face.

"That's a dungeon, Gerald," Brix said. "Not a library."

"It's a building," Gerald said. "And it's out of code."

Gerald walked toward the door. His steps were heavy. The weight of the city was on his shoulders. The weight of the red lines was on his back.

He pushed the door open.

The world outside was burning.

Not with fire. With red light.

The sky above Ashford was turning crimson.

Gerald stepped out.

And the high school walked.
