Chapter 131: Permit for Police

# Chapter 131

The pen hovered over the blank space marked **USE CLASSIFICATION**.

Gerald's hand trembled. Not from fear, though the water was at his shins and the clock ticked down in the corner of his vision, but from the sheer indignity of improvising.

He had prepared for this moment with the same rigor he applied to filing Form 11-B.

He had memorized the zoning ordinances of the Ashford district. He had memorized the System's core registration protocols. He had not, however, memorized what to do when the only legal document left to him was a permit application for a structure that technically did not exist.

The air shimmered. A holographic interface popped into existence, hovering inches from his nose. It was not the usual blue. It was a sickly, bureaucratic green.

**PERMIT APPLICATION INITIATED.** **STATUS: PENDING REVIEW.** **APPLICANT: GERALD HUTCHINS (CORE).** **NOTE: YOU ARE NOT A PERSON. PLEASE PROCEED.**

Gerald gritted his teeth. He wrote.

*Use Type: Residential.*

The text vanished. A red *X* appeared over the line.

**REJECTED. CORES ARE NOT RESIDENTIAL ZONES.**

Gerald wiped his nose with a wet sleeve. The water was cold. It smelled of rot and old drywall. He looked at the rat, which was sitting on a floating piece of insulation, grooming its whiskers with an air of profound boredom.

"Helpful," Gerald muttered.

He tried again.

*Use Type: Commercial.*

Another red *X*.

**REJECTED. COMMERCIAL ZONES REQUIRE ACTIVE RETAIL LEASES.**

The clock read **AUDIT RESUMES IN: 07:32**.

The water lapped at his knees. The ceiling was lowering, a slow, grinding descent of concrete slabs that had not been properly anchored. The System was literally crushing the space to enforce the violation he had just identified: *Structural Integrity Failure: Unsupported Overhang.*

Gerald closed his eyes.

He breathed in. He breathed out.

He thought about the library upstairs.

He thought about the load-bearing columns he had inspected last Tuesday. He thought about the specific, nuanced difference between a *public easement* and a *private utility corridor*.

He opened his eyes. He wrote in block letters.

*Use Type: Utility Corridor (Non-Public).*

The green interface blinked. It processed. The red *X* did not appear. Instead, a small, yellow checkmark flickered into existence.

**ACCEPTED. PROVISIONAL STATUS GRANTED.** **CONDITION: CORE MUST DEMONSTRATE ACTIVE MAINTENANCE FUNCTIONS FOR THE DURATION OF THE AUDIT.**

Gerald stared at the checkmark. He had not fixed the ventilation. He had not fixed the fire suppression. He had merely reclassified his entire existence.

"Active maintenance," he whispered. "I can do that."

The air shifted. The green hologram expanded, filling the room. The water stopped rising. The ceiling halted its descent with a groan of stressed metal.

**MAINTENANCE WINDOW OPEN.** **YOU HAVE 07:28 TO DEMONSTRATE COMPLIANCE VIA ACTIVE INTERVENTION.** **FAILURE TO DEMONSTRATE WILL RESULT IN IMMEDIATE DEMOLITION.**

Gerald scrambled to his feet. The water was still knee-deep, but it was no longer rising.

He looked at the locked door.

The zoning restriction had not lifted, but the urgency had changed.

He did not need to leave. He needed to *maintain*.

But he was a Core. He had no hands. He had no tools. He had only his will and the passive abilities granted by his new classification.

"Who is it?"

The voice was rough, gravelly, and came from the shadows near the ventilation shaft.

Gerald froze. He turned slowly.

Stepping out of the darkness was a figure. It was humanoid, but wrong. Its skin was the color of wet clay, and it wore armor made of fused bones and rusted rebar. It held a spear that looked like it had been salvaged from a dumpster.

It was a Goblin. Not a scout. A brute. A **Tier 2 Brawler**, according to the small text that popped up when Gerald focused on it.

"You're the one who locked the door," the Goblin said. It spat on the floor. The spit sizzled against the concrete.

Gerald looked at the Goblin. He looked at the clock. **06:58**.

"I am the Core," Gerald said. His voice was flat. He hated that he had to speak.

He preferred memos. "And you are trespassing on a non-public utility corridor."

The Goblin laughed.

It was a wet, gurgling sound. "Utility corridor? You're a dungeon.

You're food.

You're loot."

"I am a regulated infrastructure asset," Gerald said.

He pointed a spectral finger at the Goblin. "Your armor is composed of unauthorized materials. Specifically, rusted rebar.

This violates Section 4.2: *Prohibition of Corrosive Load-Bearing Elements*."

The Goblin tilted its head. "Code?"

"Compliance," Gerald said. "You are non-compliant."

The Goblin shrugged. "What are you gonna do? Write me a ticket?"

"I have already issued a Citation," Gerald said. "It is pending."

The Goblin roared. It charged.

Gerald did not dodge. He did not have a dodge. He had a **Passive Resistance: Structural**, granted by his recent compliance with basic foundation codes. The Goblin's spear hit Gerald's chest.

It bounced off.

The impact knocked the wind out of the Goblin, but Gerald barely felt it. The Spear shattered against his spectral form.

The Goblin stared at the broken weapon. Then it looked at Gerald. Fear flickered in its beady eyes.

"How?" the Goblin whispered.

"You are not permitted to use sharp objects in a Utility Corridor without a safety guard," Gerald said. "That is a Class B Violation."

He reached into his inventory. He did not have a sword. He did not have a spell. He had a **Wrench**.

It was a standard, adjustable pipe wrench, 14 inches long. It had spawned as part of his *Maintenance* kit.

Gerald threw it.

The wrench struck the Goblin in the forehead with a sickening *crack*. The Goblin stumbled back, clutching its head.

"Another Citation," Gerald said. "For reckless handling of tools in a confined space."

The Goblin growled. It pulled a knife from its boot. "I'll gut you."

"If you cut me," Gerald said, "you will be cited for unauthorized biological damage to municipal property. The penalty is immediate expulsion from the System."

The Goblin hesitated. It looked at the water. It looked at the ceiling. It looked at Gerald.

"You're bluffing," the Goblin said.

"Am I?" Gerald asked.

He raised his hand. The green interface flared.

**CITATION ISSUED.** **VIOLATION: UNAUTHORIZED WEAPON POSSESSION.** **PENALTY: -10% SPEED FOR 60 SECONDS.**

The Goblin slowed down. It tried to lunge, but its legs felt like they were moving through syrup. It snarled, swinging the knife wildly, but it couldn't catch Gerald. Gerald stepped aside, calmly, like a man avoiding a puddle.

"Go away," Gerald said.

The Goblin spat. "The Boss won't like this. The Boss is coming."

"Which Boss?" Gerald asked.

"The one in the next sector. The one with the fire traps."

Gerald's mind raced. *Fire traps.* That meant **Fire Code**. That meant **Ventilation**. That meant **Exits**.

If the neighboring Core was aggressive, it might try to breach the wall.

If it did, Gerald would need to defend.

But he was low-level. He was weak.

He needed leverage.

"The Boss is coming," the Goblin repeated. "He'll burn this place down."

"He cannot," Gerald said. "My Compliance Rating is 12.

I am currently operating under a Provisional Maintenance Permit.

Any external fire damage will be classified as *Uninsured Catastrophic Failure*. The System will void the Core. He will lose his dungeon."

The Goblin stopped. It looked confused. "He'll lose his dungeon?"

"Yes," Gerald said. "He will cease to exist. Is that what he wants?"

The Goblin lowered its knife. It looked at the broken spear. It looked at the wrench on the floor. It looked at Gerald.

"He's stupid," the Goblin said. "He likes fire. He likes burning things."

"Then he is a liability," Gerald said. "You should leave. Now. Before the Audit resumes."

The Goblin nodded. It backed away into the shadows. "Good luck, little box."

It vanished.

Gerald exhaled. His knees buckled. He sat back down in the water.

**AUDIT RESUMES IN: 04:15**.

He had bought time.

He had repelled a threat. But the water was still there.

The ventilation was still broken.

And the neighboring Core was coming.

He looked at the permit application. The yellow checkmark still flickered.

**MAINTENANCE WINDOW OPEN.** **ACTIVE TASK: CLEAR BLOCKAGE IN VENTILATION SHAFT.**

Gerald looked at the shaft. It was in the corner of the room, covered in grime. A large piece of debris—a chunk of ceiling plaster—was jammed in the opening.

He could not reach it. He was a Core. He had no body. He could only manipulate the environment within his footprint.

He needed a tool. He needed a worker.

He looked at the rat.

The rat was watching him. It was sitting on the insulation, grooming its tail.

"Hey," Gerald said.

The rat squeaked.

"I need you to climb into the vent," Gerald said. "There is a blockage. If you clear it, I will grant you permanent residency status. You will be exempt from the *Unlicensed Biological Entity* fine."

The rat stopped grooming. It looked at Gerald. It looked at the vent.

It ran to the wall. It climbed up. It reached the vent. It looked at the blockage.

It squeaked again. A high, piercing sound.

"It's too heavy," the rat seemed to communicate, though Gerald knew rats did not speak. But the System translated intent. The rat was indicating the weight of the debris.

Gerald closed his eyes. He focused on the debris. He focused on the **Structural** passive. He tried to lift it with his will.

Nothing happened.

He was too low-level. His **Telekinesis** was tied to his **Compliance Rating**. With a rating of 12, he could barely move a pebble.

He needed a boost.

He looked at the inventory.

*Building Permit Application.* *Wrench.* *Water.* *Debris.*

He looked at the water.

The water was rising again, slowly. The Audit was resuming.

If the Audit resumed, the water would rise faster. The pressure would increase. The ceiling would continue to lower.

Unless he changed the classification.

He looked at the permit. It was still active.

*Use Type: Utility Corridor (Non-Public).*

Utility corridors often housed heavy machinery. They had hoists. They had pulleys.

Gerald opened the menu for **Utility Maintenance Tools**.

It was empty.

He had no tools.

But he had a **Wrench**.

The wrench was on the floor. The Goblin had dropped it.

Gerald focused on the wrench. He used his **Telekinesis**. It was weak. The wrench wobbled in the water.

He tried to lift it. It moved an inch.

Not enough.

He needed leverage.

He looked at the rat. The rat was still in the vent. It was pushing against the debris. It was not strong enough.

Gerald looked at the walls. The walls were wet. The drywall was saturated.

He had an idea. It was a bad idea. It was an idea that violated several safety codes. But it was the only idea he had.

He looked at the water.

He looked at the ceiling.

He looked at the clock.

**AUDIT RESUMES IN: 02:30**.

If the Audit resumed, the water would rise to chest height. The pressure would burst the pipes. The room would flood.

Unless he drained it.

He needed a drain.

He looked at the floor. There was a grate. It was covered in silt.

If he cleared the grate, the water could drain into the sub-basement's main sewer line. But the sewer line was blocked. He had cited it for **Improper Grade** last month.

He needed to unblock it.

He needed help.

A pounding came from the door.

**THUD. THUD. THUD.**

"Gerald!"

The voice was deep. It was a man's voice. It was familiar.

Gerald froze.

He knew that voice. It was the voice of the Head Librarian. Or rather, the voice of the person who had been standing in the library when Gerald had vanished.

"Gerald! Are you in there?"

Gerald stared at the door. The door was still locked. **ZONING RESTRICTION.**

But the voice was loud. It was demanding.

"Open the door!"

The System flashed.

**EXTERNAL CONTACT DETECTED.** **IDENTITY: UNKNOWN.** **ACTION: RESTRICT ACCESS.**

Gerald looked at the clock. **01:45**.

He looked at the door.

If he opened the door, he would be exposed. He would be found. He would be arrested. Or worse, he would be erased.

But if he did not open the door, he would die.

And the voice was getting louder.

"Open the door, Hutchins! I know you're in there! I saw you go down!"

Gerald looked at the rat. The rat squeaked. It looked at the blockage.

He looked at the wrench.

He looked at the permit.

He made a choice.

He focused on the door. He used his **Maintenance** ability. He tried to unlock it.

**ACCESS DENIED. ZONING RESTRICTION.**

He could not open it.

But he could send a message.

He opened the chat interface. It was usually used for party members. He had no party.

He typed: **HELLO.**

The door did not answer.

He typed: **I AM IN HERE. PLEASE DO NOT BREAK THE WALL.**

A pause.

Then, text appeared on his screen.

**WHO IS THIS?**

**IT'S GERALD. I AM THE CORE.**

A silence.

Then:

**PROVE IT.**

Gerald looked at the room. He looked at the water. He looked at the clock.

**AUDIT RESUMES IN: 00:58.**

He could not prove it without opening the door. And he could not open the door without breaking the rule.

Unless he used a loophole.

The System allowed **Emergency Access** for fire suppression.

There was no fire.

But there was water.

**Flooding** was a Class A Emergency.

Gerald focused. He tried to trigger the **Sprinkler System**.

He did not have one.

He had a **Water Leak**.

The leak was a spawn. It was part of his dungeon.

He could command the leak.

He directed the water. He made it gush from the ceiling. He made it pour into the room.

**EMERGENCY PROTOCOL: FLOODING DETECTED.** **SYSTEM ACTION: UNLOCK EXTERNAL DOORS TO FACILITATE EVACUATION AND RESCUE.**

The lock clicked.

The handle turned.

The door swung open.

A man stood there. He was holding a flashlight. He was wearing a librarian's vest. He looked terrified.

He looked at the water. He looked at the broken ceiling. He looked at the floating wrench.

He looked at Gerald.

Gerald was not there. There was no Gerald. There was only a dark, wet room and a single, glowing core embedded in the wall.

The man stepped forward.

"Gerald?"

The Core pulsed. A voice came from the walls. It was Gerald's voice, synthesized and dry.

"Yes."

The man stepped back. "What... what is this?"

"I am the building," Gerald said. "And you are in violation of Section 7: *Unauthorized Entry During Active Maintenance*."

The man blinked. "I'm trying to help you."

"You are trespassing," Gerald said.

The man looked at the water. It was rising fast. The Audit had resumed.

**AUDIT RESUMES IN: 00:00.**

The ceiling groaned. A chunk of concrete fell. It landed near the man's foot.

The man jumped.

"Gerald, we have to get you out of here!"

"I cannot be moved," Gerald said. "I am the foundation."

The man looked at the core. He looked at the falling debris. He looked at the water.

He pulled out a phone.

"I'm calling the police," the man said.

"Do not," Gerald said. "The police do not have jurisdiction over Core-Adjacent Zones."

"The police have jurisdiction over *me*," the man said. "And you're killing me!"

Water splashed over the man's shoes.

Gerald looked at the man. He looked at the phone.

He had a new problem.

He had a witness.

He had a witness who knew his name.

And the water was rising.

**RATING: 12/100.** **VIOLATION: ACTIVE HAZARD TO UNAUTHORIZED PERSONNEL.** **PENALTY: -50% MAINTENANCE SPEED.**

Gerald's control over the water weakened. The leak slowed. The room filled faster.

The man screamed. He ran back into the hallway.

"Help!" he yelled. "There's something in the basement!"

Gerald watched him go.

He looked at the door. It was open.

He looked at the water. It was at his waist.

He looked at the clock.

**AUDIT IN PROGRESS.**

He had not fixed the ventilation. He had not fixed the structure. He had only invited disaster.

The man was calling the police.

The police would come.

And the Inspector General was still watching.

Gerald felt a chill that was not the water.

He looked at the permit.

**PERMIT APPLICATION: PENDING.** **CONDITION: DEMONSTRATE COMPLIANCE.**

He had failed.

But he had not been deleted.

Because the man had left.

And the man had seen the door open.

**SYSTEM ALERT: EXTERNAL OBSERVATION LOGGED.** **STATUS: PUBLIC.**

Gerald stared at the word **PUBLIC**.

He was no longer a secret.

He was a news story.

And the System hated news stories.

The water rose to his chest.

Gerald closed his eyes.

He opened his inventory.

He had one item left.

*Building Permit Application.*

It was still blank.

He had to fill it out again.

But this time, he had to fill it out for the police.

**USE CLASSIFICATION: LAW ENFORCEMENT STAGING AREA.**

Gerald smiled. It was a tired, desperate smile.

He wrote.
