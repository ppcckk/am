Chapter 241: Audit Has Begun

# Chapter 241

The mold spores did not attack. They hovered.

Gerald backed into the drywall of the lobby, his boots scuffing against the polished marble. The air tasted of ozone and wet rot. Behind him, the Mayor's corpse lay in a heap of expensive suit fabric and shattered electrical components. Before him, the swarm of micro-Cores drifted, a shimmering cloud of biological and fungal entities that had coalesced from the city's neglected infrastructure.

"Go away," Gerald said. His voice sounded thin. Too human.

The cloud didn't disperse. It pulsed.

A larger shape coalesced from the mist. A rat, but wrong.

Its eyes were too bright, its posture too upright.

It spoke. Not with a mouth, but with a vibration that rattled Gerald's teeth.

"The Inspector is suspended," the Rat said.

Gerald tightened his grip on the wrench. The metal was cold. Unsanctioned. Useless.

"I am still standing," Gerald said.

"Standing is not compliance," a second voice added.

This one came from the ceiling vents. A patch of black mold spread across the plaster, forming a jagged, dripping face.

"Your rating is zero.

Your authority is null. You are, by definition, a hazard."

"Who are you?" Gerald asked. He looked at the status bar in his peripheral vision. 71:45. The red text burned into his retinas.

"We are the unregistered," the Mold said. "The things that grow in the cracks.

The things that leak.

You cited the big ones.

The flashy ones. But you missed us.

You missed the damp."

Gerald's stomach turned. The Rat's accusation wasn't just mockery.

It was a System notification he hadn't received yet.

If they were considered hazards, they were within the scope of his old jurisdiction. But he couldn't issue Citations. The suspension locked that ability.

"Leave the building," Gerald said. "This is a public space. You are obstructing a public thoroughfare."

"Obstruction is a Class C violation," the Rat said.

It took a step forward. Its claws clicked on the marble.

"But you cannot cite it.

You are mute."

Gerald looked at the exit. The glass doors were locked.

The police sirens wailed outside, muffled by the thick stone walls.

The news vans were a distant rumble.

He had forty minutes before the police breached the doors and found him alone with a corpse and a swarm of sentient mold.

He needed a way to enforce the code without using his Core powers. He needed to be an inspector, not a god.

"Form 11-B," Gerald muttered.

The Rat paused. "What?"

Gerald didn't answer. He walked past the cloud of spores, his heart hammering against his ribs. He moved toward the Mayor's body. The Rat hissed, a sound like tearing paper, and lunged.

Gerald sidestepped. It was a clumsy maneuver, fueled by panic than skill. The Rat crashed into a marble pillar. Stone chips rained down.

"You are attacking a citizen," Gerald said. He didn't raise the wrench. He picked up a piece of rebar that had fallen from the Mayor's body.

"I am a Core," the Rat snarled.

"You are a biological entity found in a non-biological zone," Gerald corrected. He pointed the rebar at the creature. "I am conducting a field assessment."

The Mold on the ceiling dropped a chunk of wet plaster onto Gerald's shoulder. He didn't flinch. He kept walking.

"Why are you doing this?" the Mold asked. Its voice was slower now, confused. "You have no power."

"Power is irrelevant," Gerald said. "Compliance is mandatory."

He reached the main lobby desk. It was a massive slab of mahogany, currently splintered. Gerald ran his hands along the edge.

He checked the joints. He checked the grain.

"Load-bearing structural failure," he said aloud.

The Rat froze. "What are you talking about?"

Gerald ignored it.

He pulled a small, battered notebook from his jacket pocket. It was his old inspection log. It wasn't linked to the System.

It was paper.

Ink. Durable.

He flipped to a fresh page.

"Violation: Unsecured heavy furniture obstructing egress path," Gerald recited. He wrote it down. The scratch of the pen was loud in the silent lobby.

The Mold laughed. A wet, gurgling sound. "Paper does not stop monsters, Inspector."

Gerald didn't look up. "Violation: Improper disposal of hazardous material." He pointed the pen at the Mayor's body. "Biological waste left in a high-traffic zone. No biohazard signage."

He wrote it down.

The Rat shifted. "You're wasting time."

Gerald turned to the cloud of spores. "Violation: Unauthorized atmospheric particulate matter exceeding OSHA permissible exposure limits."

He wrote it down.

The cloud swirled. The Rat's eyes narrowed.

"You cannot cite us," the Rat said. "You are suspended."

"I am not citing you," Gerald said.

He tore the page out of the notebook. He held it up. "I am documenting the scene.

For the appeal."

The silence stretched. The Mold stopped dripping. The Rat stopped breathing.

"Appeal?" the Mold whispered.

Gerald looked at the status bar. 71:20.

"The System allows for a Hearing," Gerald said.

His voice was steady now.

Deadpan. "A Hearing requires two parties.

One party presents a violation. The other party presents a rebuttal. If the violation is valid, the Citation is enforced."

"We are not a party," the Rat said. "We are the environment."

"The environment is not exempt," Gerald said. "Section 4, Paragraph 2: 'Natural phenomena that pose a structural threat are subject to mitigation protocols.' You are not natural. You are sentient. You are interfering with municipal operations."

Gerald walked back toward the lobby doors. He didn't attack. He measured.

He took a tape measure from his belt. It was a standard steel rule, five feet long. He snapped it open.

"Distance between egress exits: four meters," he said. He looked at the Rat. "Code requires two meters. You are blocking the secondary path."

The Rat growled. "Move it or lose it."

"I am not moving it," Gerald said. "I am measuring it."

He stepped forward. The Rat lunged.

Gerald didn't strike. He dropped to his knees and slid under the Rat's claw. The rebar clattered on the floor. He stayed low, moving to the side of the lobby door.

He pulled out his pen. He wrote on the doorframe.

"Violation: Obstruction of emergency egress by hostile biological entity."

He underlined it.

The Rat screamed. It spun, trying to bring its weight down on Gerald.

Gerald rolled away. He stood up. He didn't run. He stood between the Rat and the Mold.

"System," Gerald said.

Nothing happened. The suspension held.

Gerald closed his eyes. He focused on the paper in his hand. The physical act of writing. The tangible evidence.

"System," he said again. "Requesting emergency appeal. Cause: Imminent structural collapse due to non-compliant entity interference."

The air in the lobby shimmered.

The Rat paused. It looked at Gerald. Then it looked at the air.

A blue box appeared in the center of the room. It wasn't glowing with the usual gold light of a Citation.

It was grey.

Dull. Administrative.

**HEARING REQUESTED.**

**PROBABLE CAUSE: EXISTENTIAL THREAT TO CORE INTEGRITY.**

**ACCEPT?**

Gerald didn't hesitate. "Yes."

The box vanished.

The Rat's eyes widened. "What did you do?"

"I filed a motion," Gerald said. "And I have evidence."

He pointed to the notebook.

"I have measured the obstruction. I have documented the hazard.

I have identified the source.

I have not used force. I have not used a weapon. I have used inspection."

The Mold pulsed. "You cannot use the Code during a suspension."

"I am not using the Code to attack," Gerald said. "I am using the Code to define the situation. The suspension prevents me from *issuing* Citations. It does not prevent me from *requesting* a Hearing to determine if my dungeon is under attack by a non-compliant entity."

The grey box returned.

**HEARING INITIATED.**

**PARTY A: CORE 402 (Gerald Hutchins).**

**PARTY B: UNREGISTERED AGGREGATE (The Swarm).**

**TOPIC: DEFENSIVE ACTION vs. OBSTRUCTION OF JUSTICE.**

**TIME LIMIT: 10 MINUTES.**

Gerald looked at the clock. 71:10. He had ten minutes to prove that the Mold and the Rat were not part of his dungeon, and that their presence constituted a violation severe enough to grant him emergency defensive powers.

The Rat laughed. It was a cruel, sharp sound.

"You think you can out-argue us?

We are the city.

We are the rot. You are a man with a wrench."

"I am an inspector," Gerald said. "And you are in violation."

He opened his notebook. He turned to a new page.

"Question one," Gerald said. He looked at the Mold. "Define your spatial footprint. What is your total square footage within my dungeon boundaries?"

The Mold rippled. "We are everywhere."

"That is not a measurement," Gerald said. "Give me a number."

"We do not have numbers," the Mold said.

"Then you cannot claim residency," Gerald said. He wrote: *No declared residency. No registered address.*

He turned to the Rat. "Question two. Do you have a valid permit to be in the lobby?"

The Rat snarled. "I am a Core. I exist."

"Existence is not a permit," Gerald said. "Section 12, Paragraph 4: 'All entities within a Core's domain must declare their status or face removal.' You have not declared."

He wrote: *Undeclared entity. Trespassing.*

The grey box pulsed.

**ARGUMENT 1: LACK OF REGISTRATION.**

**PROBABILITY OF VIOLATION: HIGH.**

The Rat growled. It charged.

Gerald didn't move. He stood his ground.

"System," Gerald said. "Pause proceeding. Additional evidence."

The box froze.

The Rat stopped inches from Gerald's face. Its breath smelled of sewer gas.

"What evidence?" the Rat hissed.

Gerald pointed to the Mayor's body. "The Mayor was a registered entity. He was under contract with the City.

He was a client. His death was caused by a malfunction in the electrical grid."

Gerald pulled out his pen. He tapped the notebook.

"Who controls the grid?"

The Rat's eyes narrowed. "The Mayor owned it."

"The Mayor was dead when the surge hit," Gerald said. "The surge came from the walls. From the wiring. From the *infrastructure*."

Gerald looked at the Mold. "You are the infrastructure, aren't you? You are the wiring. You are the plumbing."

The Mold remained silent.

"If you are the infrastructure," Gerald said, "then you are responsible for the maintenance of the electrical systems. You are responsible for the fire codes. You are responsible for the safety of the occupants."

He wrote: *Negligence.*

"The surge was a failure of maintenance," Gerald said.

"A Code violation. Electrical Code 7-20. 'Grounding must be maintained to prevent arc-flash hazards.' You failed to ground the system.

You created the hazard that killed the Mayor."

The grey box brightened.

**ARGUMENT 2: FAILURE TO MAINTAIN SAFETY STANDARDS.**

**PROBABILITY OF VIOLATION: CRITICAL.**

The Rat looked at the Mold. For the first time, it looked uncertain.

"You're framing us," the Rat said. "We didn't cause the surge. The Core did."

"I am the Core," Gerald said. "And I did not cause the surge. I *mitigated* it. The surge was caused by the unregistered entities attempting to override my dungeon's power grid."

He pointed to the scorched marks on the wall. "Look at the burn patterns.

Radial.

Centered on the vent shafts. That's where you entered. That's where the power draw spiked."

Gerald ripped another page from the notebook. He held it up.

"I have documented the entry points. I have measured the damage. I have identified the cause."

He looked at the grey box.

"System. Request for immediate injunction."

The box hovered.

**REVIEWING.**

**WAIT.**

The clock ticked. 70:45.

The Mold coalesced into a larger shape. It looked like a storm cloud. A face formed in the mist. A giant, dripping face.

"You are playing games," the Mold said. Its voice boomed. "You are a small man in a big house. You think rules will save you?"

"Rules are the only thing that saves anyone," Gerald said. "Without them, it's just chaos. And chaos is not code compliant."

The face in the mist smiled. It was a terrible smile. Full of sharp, jagged pieces of drywall.

"Then let's see how compliant you are when I eat your bones."

The Mold lunged.

The grey box flashed.

**INJUNCTION GRANTED.**

**STATUS: EMERGENCY MAINTENANCE PROTOCOL ACTIVATED.**

**CITATION AUTHORITY RESTORED FOR DURATION OF THREAT.**

Gerald felt the power surge back into him. The wrench in his hand glowed blue.

But the Mold was already on him.

Gerald swung the wrench.

He didn't hit the Mold. He hit the wall.

Specifically, he hit the junction box where the electrical wires entered the drywall.

**VIOLATION: UNAUTHORIZED BYPASS OF SAFETY FUSE.**

The wall exploded.

Sparks showered the lobby. The Mold shrieked as the electrical current arced through its moist form. It recoiled, sizzling.

Gerald stood in the smoke, panting. The wrench was hot.

The Rat stared at him. "You... you blew the fuse?"

"I followed the code," Gerald said. "When you bypassed the main breaker, I was required to cut the power to the affected zone. It's standard procedure."

The Rat looked at the sparking wires. It looked at Gerald.

"You're crazy," the Rat said.

"I'm compliant," Gerald said.

The grey box updated.

**HEARING PAUSED.**

**THREAT NEUTRALIZED TEMPORARILY.**

**NEXT PHASE: ADJUDICATION OF DAMAGES.**

The Mold retreated into the vents. The Rat slunk back into the shadows.

Gerald slumped against the doorframe. His legs were shaking. He looked at the notebook. The pages were singed.

The clock reset. 10:00.

He had ten minutes to finish the argument. To prove that the Swarm was not just a threat, but a criminal organization operating within his dungeon without permits.

He looked at the entrance. The police were pounding on the door.

He looked at the Swarm. They were regrouping. They were angry.

Gerald opened a new page. He wrote the title.

**CITATION 1: CONSPIRACY TO COMMIT INFRASTRUCTURAL SABOTAGE.**

He looked up.

"Who's next?" he asked.

From the darkness of the library stacks, a new voice emerged. Not from the Mold. Not from the Rat.

It was a human voice.

"Mr. Hutchins," the voice said. "I believe you have a lot of explaining to do to the Inspector General."

Gerald froze.

The Inspector General.

The System's Auditor.

The one entity that could not be cited. The one entity that could override everything.

Gerald looked at the shadows between the bookshelves. A figure stepped forward.

It was tall.

It wore a suit that was too perfect.

It had no face, just a smooth, blank surface where features should be.

It held a clipboard.

"Your suspension is lifted," the Figure said. "But your audit has begun."

Gerald gripped his wrench.

He had won the battle.

But the war had just started.
