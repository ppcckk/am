Chapter 364: Written in Stone

# Chapter 364

The clipboard snapped.

Not the wood. The plastic. The thin, brittle spine of the municipal issue had given way under the impact of the Baron's gauntleted palm. Sheets of carbon copy scattered across the wet floor like dead leaves, drifting into the stagnant puddle Gerald had created for traction.

Gerald stepped back. His shoes made a wet sucking sound on the tile.

The Baron roared again, a sound that vibrated in Gerald's molars. The giant was a mess of stolen essence and torn velvet, his eyes leaking raw mana that sizzled where it hit the water. He raised the sword, the blade humming with a frequency that made the air taste like copper.

Gerald did not flinch. He reached into his vest pocket and pulled out a backup copy of Form 402: *Immediate Cease and Desist.*

"Please," Gerald said, holding the paper up with two trembling fingers. "Your signature is required before you can proceed with any further kinetic activity."

The Baron swung.

The sword missed Gerald's head by three inches. It struck the wall behind him, biting deep into the brickwork. Dust rained down. But the swing left the Baron's left side exposed, his weight shifted forward, his boots sliding on the slick surface.

Gerald saw the angle. He saw the load-bearing capacity of the wall failing.

He didn't move away. He moved forward.

He pressed his palm flat against the vibrating brick, right next to the sword's entry point. A new System notification flashed in his peripheral vision, bright and urgent.

**STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY COMPROMISED.** **IMMEDIATE ACTION REQUIRED.**

Gerald ignored the warning. He ignored the pain in his shoulder from the previous encounter. He focused on the one thing he had learned in thirty-one years of inspecting crumbling foundations: if you cannot stop the force, you must redirect the failure.

He tapped the wall. One tap. Two taps. A rhythmic, percussive code.

The System read the input.

**INPUT: MANUAL REINFORCEMENT (PROVISIONAL).** **COST: 15% CORE ENERGY.**

The bricks groaned. They didn't crack further.

They fused.

The mortar hardened, pulling the stone tight against the incoming blade. The Baron's sword stuck. Not because it was sharp, but because the wall had decided, for a split second, that it was no longer allowed to move.

The Baron yanked. The sword did not budge.

"Unorthodox," the Baron snarled, gritting his teeth. He put his weight behind the pull, his boots scrabbling for purchase on the wet tile. "You are cheating, core."

"I am reinforcing," Gerald said, his voice flat, devoid of the adrenaline that should have flooded his system. "Section 4, Paragraph B of the Municipal Code allows for emergency shoring during active stress events. You are applying lateral force without a permit."

The Baron sneered. He released the sword and punched the wall with his bare fist.

The brick shattered.

The sword clattered to the floor.

Gerald's heart skipped a beat.

Not out of fear. Out of calculation.

The wall was now unstable.

The ceiling above them was sagging, the weight of the library's main reading room pressing down on a compromised support structure.

If the ceiling fell, the library collapsed. If the library collapsed, the City of Ashford sued. If the City sued, the System might revoke Gerald's Core Registration for

"Gross Negligence Leading to Property Damage."

He needed a way out. A way to make the Baron leave. Or to make the Baron stop caring.

Gerald looked at the voided citation on the floor. Then he looked at the dwarf, Thrum, who was currently hiding behind a support pillar, axe raised but not swinging.

"Thrum," Gerald said. "Stop hitting the pillar."

"I'm waiting for him to fall over!" Thrum yelled, his voice echoing in the small basement. "That bucket trick was good, boss. Did you hear him?"

"He is not falling over," Gerald said. "He is stabilizing. Look at his stance."

The Baron had reset. He was standing wide, knees bent, center of gravity low. He looked less like a monster and more like a man who had finally realized he was in a fight he could lose.

Gerald picked up the broken clipboard. He tore off the top sheet, the one that had been ruined by the punch.

He kept the second sheet.

The carbon copy.

The faint, purple ghost of the first form remained, and he walked toward the Baron.

The giant tensed, raising a hand to cast a spell of disintegration.

Gerald held up the carbon copy.

"Read this," Gerald said.

The Baron blinked. The spell fizzled, mana dissipating harmlessly into the air.

"What?"

"It is the duplicate," Gerald said. "The original was destroyed. The System recognizes the physical act of destruction as a filing error. Therefore, the violation is pending until re-submitted."

The Baron stared at the paper. Confusion clouded his stolen eyes. This was not how dungeons worked.

Dungeons did not care about paperwork. They cared about power.

They cared about dominance.

"You are wasting my time," the Baron said, though his voice lacked its earlier thunder.

"I am ensuring the record is accurate," Gerald said. He stepped closer. "You have breached the perimeter.

You have damaged the structure.

You have entered a restricted zone. Under the new Inspector General audit protocols, any Core found to be operating with an unresolved structural deficiency citation is subject to immediate quarantine.

"

The Baron's eyes widened.

"Quarantine?"

"Sealing," Gerald said.

He tapped the paper.

"The System will seal this room. No one enters.

No one leaves. You will be trapped here with me.

And I have twenty minutes of oxygen before the ventilation fails completely.

I am fine.

I do not breathe. You, however, require air.

"

The Baron looked at the sealed door. He looked at the broken window. He looked at the water. He looked at Gerald.

"You are bluffing," the Baron said.

Gerald did not answer. He simply held the clipboard out.

The Baron hesitated. His hand twitched. He could snap Gerald's neck.

He could burn the paper. He could smash Gerald into paste.

But he remembered the last time he had tried to break the rules. The debuff. The weakness. The way his own spawns had turned on him when their stats dropped below the threshold.

If he was quarantined, he would be cut off from his dungeon network. He would be alone. And if Gerald had rigged the room to fail, the Baron would die in the dark.

The Baron reached out. He did not take the clipboard. He grabbed Gerald's wrist.

His grip was iron. Pain shot up Gerald's arm.

"Show me the seal," the Baron hissed.

Gerald felt the System pulse. A new notification appeared, invisible to the Baron, blazing red in Gerald's mind.

**ACTION DETECTED: PHYSICAL INTIMIDATION OF INSPECTOR.** **NEW VIOLATION: ASSAULT ON REGULATORY AUTHORITY.** **PENALTY: 500 POINTS. IMMEDIATE DEBUFF: 40% REDUCTION IN PHYSICAL STRENGTH.**

The grip loosened.

The Baron's face twisted. His muscles spasmed. The stolen cores within him flickered, dimming as the System enforced the penalty.

"Get off me," Gerald said.

He pulled his arm free. He stepped back. He picked up the broken clipboard.

The Baron stumbled back, clutching his chest.

"You... you used a loophole."

"There are no loopholes," Gerald said. "There is only code."

He looked at Thrum. The dwarf had lowered his axe. He was staring at the Baron with wide, wondering eyes.

"Boss," Thrum whispered. "Did you just nerf the Baron?"

"I cited him," Gerald corrected. "For assault. And for creating a tripping hazard."

The Baron was panting.

The red light in his eyes was fading to a dull, angry orange. He looked smaller. Not physically, but in presence.

The aura of stolen power was suppressed, dampened by the System's heavy hand.

"This isn't over," the Baron spat. He looked at the door.

He looked at the window, and he looked at Gerald.

"I will appeal."

"There is no appeal for criminal assault," Gerald said. "Only for administrative errors.

You know the difference.

I wrote it down.

Section 12, Subsection C.

"

The Baron lunged again. Not with a sword. With his body. He tried to tackle Gerald.

But with his strength reduced by forty percent, the lunge was clumsy. He slipped on the wet tile. He crashed into the support pillar. The pillar, already stressed by the earlier sword strike, groaned.

Cracks spiderwebbed up the stone.

Dust fell.

Thrum yelled, "Get out! It's coming down!"

Gerald looked at the ceiling. He looked at the Baron, who was struggling to stand up, his limbs heavy and uncoordinated.

Gerald had a choice, and he could leave. He could retreat to his core chamber, seal the entrance, and let the ceiling collapse. The Baron would be buried. The problem would be solved.

But if the ceiling collapsed, the library above would be damaged. The City would investigate. The Inspector General would audit. Gerald's Compliance Rating, barely stabilized, would plummet.

He needed to save the building. He needed to save himself. He needed to save the Baron, because a dead Baron was a messy audit, but a living, cited Baron was a leverage point.

Gerald ran to the center of the room. He slammed his hands onto the floor.

**COMMAND: EMERGENCY SHORING.** **COST: 30% CORE ENERGY.** **WARNING: INSUFFICIENT MATERIALS.**

It wasn't enough. He didn't have the materials. He had water.

He had rats. He had mold.

He looked at the Baron, and the giant was on his knees, looking up at Gerald with hatred and fear.

"Help me," Gerald said.

The Baron laughed, a wet, gurgling sound.

"Go to hell, inspector."

"Fix the pillar," Gerald said, his voice cutting through the dust.

"Help me fix the pillar, or we both get crushed. And if you get crushed, you lose your appeal rights. You become debris.

Unregistered debris.

"

The Baron's eyes narrowed. He understood the stakes.

A Core could not be resurrected easily if it was destroyed in a non-compliant manner.

If the ceiling fell on him, the System would rule it a

"natural disaster" caused by the Core's negligence. No appeal. No recovery.

Just deletion.

The Baron looked at the cracking pillar. He looked at Gerald.

He stood up. His movements were jerky, weakened by the debuff. He walked to the pillar. He placed his hands on the stone.

"Push," Gerald said.

The Baron pushed. His muscles strained, the stolen cores flickering under the strain of the System's penalty.

Gerald pushed too. He added his own weak, core-energy reinforced hands to the stone.

"Thrum," Gerald said. "Get the water bucket. Pour it on the base."

"What?" Thrum asked, grabbing the bucket. "Why?"

"Hydration," Gerald said. "The mortar needs to set. The water activates the binding agent."

It was nonsense, which was bureaucratic nonsense.

But the System liked procedures. If Gerald described the action as a procedural repair, the System would allow it.

If he described it as magic, the System would tax it.

Thrum poured the water.

The Baron pushed. Gerald pushed.

The cracks stopped spreading.

The dust settled.

The ceiling held.

For a moment, silence reigned. The three of them stood around the pillar, breathing hard, watching the stone.

Then, the System chimed.

**ACTION COMPLETE: EMERGENCY REPAIR.** **STATUS: PROVISIONAL.** **NOTE: INSPECTOR GERALD HUTCHINS HAS DEMONSTRATED CROSS-DOMAIN COOPERATION.** **NEW MECHANIC UNLOCKED: JOINT LIABILITY.**

Gerald stared at the text.

*Joint Liability.*

He hadn't heard of that before. He looked at the Baron.

The Baron was staring at his own hands. The red glow in his eyes was gone. He looked tired. He looked like a man who had just been beaten by a filing cabinet.

"You..." the Baron began, his voice raspy.

Gerald pulled out a new form. Not 402. Not 11-B.

Form 550: *Collaborative Remediation Agreement.*

"You are no longer a hostile entity," Gerald said, his voice steady, though his hands shook. "You are a contractor. Under emergency provisions, you may assist in the repair of non-compliant structures. In exchange, the citation for assault is reduced to a warning."

The Baron looked at the form. He looked at Gerald.

"If I sign this," the Baron said, "I become your employee?"

"No," Gerald said. "You become my partner in liability.

If this building fails, you are partially responsible.

If it holds, you gain credit for the repair. Your Compliance Rating will improve. Temporarily.

"

The Baron stared at him, and the hatred was still there, but beneath it was something else. Calculation.

He needed that credit.

His own dungeon was falling apart, his spawns weak, his reputation in tatters. He needed a win. Even a small one.

Even a humiliating one.

"Sign it," the Baron growled. He reached for the pen Gerald offered.

Gerald held out the pen.

But before the Baron's fingers touched the ink, the air in the basement changed.

The temperature dropped. The water in the puddle froze.

A voice boomed, not from the Baron, not from Thrum, but from the ceiling. It was cold, metallic, and absolute.

**AUDIT IN PROGRESS.** **TARGET: GERALD HUTCHINS.** **STATUS: SUSPICIOUS ACTIVITY DETECTED.**

The ceiling did not crack. It opened.

A beam of blue light descended, illuminating the center of the room. It did not touch Gerald. It did not touch the Baron. It touched the Form 550.

The paper dissolved into ash.

The Baron pulled his hand back, his eyes widening.

Gerald felt a cold weight settle in his chest. The Inspector General. Forty-eight hours was up.

The light spread, forming a holographic figure in the air. It was a silhouette made of grid lines and red text.

**INSPECTOR GENERAL: AUDITOR PRIME.** **GREAT HUTCHINS. YOU HAVE BROKEN THE CODE.**

Gerald looked up.

"I have followed the code."

**YOU HAVE FORMED AN ALLIANCE WITH A HOSTILE ENTITY WITHOUT FILING A CONFLICT OF INTEREST DISCLOSURE.** **VIOLATION: 10,000 POINTS.** **PENALTY: CORE SEIZURE.**

The blue light surged.

It wrapped around Gerald, and he couldn't move. He couldn't speak.

He felt his connection to the dungeon stretching, thinning, about to snap.

The Baron stepped forward. He looked at the Auditor. He looked at Gerald.

He picked up the broken sword, and he raised it.

And he threw it.

The sword did not hit Gerald. It did not hit the Auditor.

It hit the light beam, shattering the hologram.

The blue light flickered. The connection broke.

Gerald fell to his knees, gasping for air he didn't need.

The Auditor's figure reformed, darker, angrier.

**ACT OF DESTRUCTION ON REGULATORY INSTRUMENT.** **TARGET: BARON 8841.** **STATUS: EXCLUDED FROM APPEAL PROCESS.**

The Baron stood there, chest heaving, looking at the fading light. He had saved Gerald. He had damned himself.

Gerald looked at the Baron. The giant met his gaze.

"Why?" Gerald whispered.

The Baron smiled. It was a thin, dry, terrifying smile. It was Gerald's own smile, mirrored back at him.

"Because I want you to sign it," the Baron said, pointing at the ash where the form had been. "And I want you to know that I am still here."

The ceiling groaned. The true collapse was coming. Not because of the repairs. Because the Auditor had stopped the stabilization.

Gerald looked at the door. It was gone. Replaced by a wall of stone.

He looked at the window. Sealed.

He looked at the Baron.

"We are trapped," Gerald said.

The Baron picked up his sword.

"No," he said. "We are not trapped. We are under investigation."

He turned to the wall.

"Start writing," the Baron said. "I will hold the line."

Gerald raised his clipboard. The pen was gone. He had no pen. He had no paper.

He looked at his own skin. He looked at the Baron's blade.

He pressed his thumb against the wall, and he carved the first letter of the new citation into the stone, using his own body as the ink.

**CITATION: 001.**

The stone screamed. The Auditor returned.

And Gerald did not stop.
