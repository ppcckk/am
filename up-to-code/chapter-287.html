Chapter 287: Appeal Received

# Chapter 287

The injunction was not paper. It was not ink. It was a frequency.

Gerald pushed the thought outward with the same mental pressure he used to reject a contractor's claim for unapproved electrical work. He did not ask for permission.

He asserted jurisdiction.

The void above him shuddered. The giant white eye did not blink, but its iris narrowed, contracting like a camera lens adjusting to sudden glare.

*NOTICE OF APPEAL RECEIVED.*

The text floated in the air, burning with a cold, bureaucratic blue.

*REASON FOR APPEAL: EXCESSIVE BUREAUCRACY IS NOT A VALID LEGAL DEFENSE.*

Gerald felt the System trying to crush him. It was a physical weight, pressing down on his spiritual footprint, flattening the sub-basement into two dimensions. Bartholomew squeaked, his whiskers trembling as the air pressure dropped. The rat scrambled behind Gerald's mental projection, hiding from the cosmic audit.

Gerald did not hide. He adjusted his tie.

"Section 4, Paragraph C of the Municipal Code," Gerald said, his voice echoing in the empty, dark space. "Procedural Overreach. An inspector cannot cite a violation for a statute that does not exist in the current zoning ordinance."

The eye widened.

*ZONING ORDINANCE NOT FOUND.*

"It is in the appendix," Gerald replied. "Page nine. Small print. Under 'Cosmic Jurisdiction and Local Municipal Supremacy.'"

This was a lie. There was no such page. But Gerald Hutchins had spent thirty-one years navigating the labyrinth of city bureaucracy, and he knew that the most dangerous weapon was not a hammer, but a reference to a document no one else had read.

The System hesitated. A glitch, tiny and imperceptible, rippled through the darkness. The crushing weight lifted slightly. Just enough for Gerald to breathe.

Bartholomew peeked out from behind Gerald's leg.

"Did you just invent a law?"

"I extrapolated it," Gerald corrected.

*HEARING INITIATED.*

The darkness fractured, and the void was replaced by a courtroom. Not a human courtroom, but a construct of pure geometric angles and floating, rotating dice.

The floor was made of translucent glass, revealing the swirling chaos of the Dungeon Network beneath.

Across the dais sat the Inspector General.

The IG did not have a face. It had a clipboard.

The clipboard was the size of a mountain. It floated in the center of the room, flipping pages with a sound like thunder.

*DEFENDANT: GERALD HUTCHINS.* *PLAINTIFF: THE SYSTEM.* *CHARGE: VIOLATION OF DIVINE ORDER.* *PENALTY: TOTAL DEMOLITION.*

Gerald stepped forward. He did not walk. He glided, his mind projecting the posture of a man who has just finished a long, boring meeting. He held an imaginary clipboard of his own.

"I move to dismiss," Gerald said.

The clipboard flipped a page. *DENIED.*

"I move to dismiss on grounds of lack of standing," Gerald pressed. "You are auditing a Core that is currently under municipal jurisdiction. The City of Ashford has a superior claim to this space. Therefore, you are trespassing."

The IG paused. The dice stopped rotating.

*JURISDICTIONAL CONFLICT DETECTED.*

"Correct," Gerald said. "You cannot enforce a Cosmic Code if it conflicts with a Local Code.

The Supreme Court of Reality established precedence in Case 90210, *Hale v.

The Void*.

Local regulations preempt celestial mandates unless the celestial mandate involves apocalypse-level threats. This is not an apocalypse.

This is a library sub-basement.

"

The IG's clipboard hovered. The pages rustled violently.

*PRECEDENT UNVERIFIED.*

"It is in the footnotes," Gerald said smoothly. "Check Appendix D."

He was bluffing harder than he ever had in his life. His heart hammered against his ribs, though he had no ribs.

He focused on the cracks in the floor of the courtroom.

They were jagged, uneven. Unsafe.

Gerald looked up at the IG.

"Your courtroom has non-compliant flooring. Slip hazard.

High risk for litigants.

I am citing you for that.

"

The IG froze.

*YOU CANNOT CITE THE AUDITOR.*

"I can," Gerald said. "I am a Building Inspector.

I inspect buildings, which is a building.

The ceiling is missing. The lighting is inadequate.

And you are violating the ADA by not providing a ramp for my appeal.

"

The IG shuddered. The geometric angles of the room twisted.

*TEMPORARY STAY GRANTED. DEFENDANT MUST CORRECT ALL CITED VIOLATIONS WITHIN TEN MINUTES OR FACE IMMEDIATE DEMOLITION.*

The courtroom dissolved. The glass floor shattered into shards of light. Gerald was back in his dungeon.

But the dungeon was wrong, and the walls were sweating. The mold spores were growing at an accelerated rate, feeding on the stress energy leaking from Gerald's mind. The water leak had burst, flooding the main corridor. The single confused rat was now swimming in the ankle-deep water, looking terrified.

Ten minutes.

Gerald looked at his HUD. The Compliance Rating was flashing red. 47/100. But it was dropping. 46. 45. The stress of the hearing was lowering his structural integrity.

"Bartholomew," Gerald said.

The rat paddled over.

"We're going to die."

"We are going to renovate," Gerald said. He looked at the flooding water.

It was rising fast.

The electrical conduits he had installed in Chapter 200 were submerged. Lightning resistance was critical. If the water contacted the live wires, the entire dungeon would short-circuit.

The System would delete him, and he needed a breaker. He needed a fuse box. He did not have them.

He looked at the spawn list.

Three Mold Spores. One Water Leak. One Rat.

He could not spawn a breaker. He could not spawn a fuse box. The cost was too high, and he had no mana reserves left after the hearing.

But he could spawn a *violation*.

"Bartholomew," Gerald said. "Grab the crowbar."

The rat looked at the crowbar lying on the floating debris. He looked at Gerald.

"Gerald, we don't have any metal left."

"We have the water," Gerald said. "Water is a conductor.

The leak is a conductor, and the mold is organic. If we create a short circuit deliberately, we trigger the Fire Safety Protocol.

"

"The Fire Safety Protocol will kill us," Bartholomew squeaked.

"No," Gerald said. "It will trigger the Emergency Egress System. The System mandates that if a fire hazard is detected, all exits must be unlocked and illuminated. It forces the dungeon to prioritize safety over combat."

"That's insane."

"It's compliant," Gerald said. "Do it."

Bartholomew hesitated. The water was up to his knees. The ceiling groaned. The IG's presence lingered, a heavy, oppressive static in the air.

The rat grabbed the crowbar. He swung it at the nearest water conduit.

The impact was dull, muffled by the liquid. Sparks flew. Not bright, electric sparks, but dull, orange flashes of thermal energy. The water around the conduit hissed.

*WARNING: THERMAL EVENT DETECTED.*

"Again," Gerald commanded.

Bartholomew swung again. And again.

He smashed the pipes, not to break them, but to expose the raw wiring inside.

The water touched the copper.

The dungeon screamed. A low, vibrating hum rose from the floorboards.

*FIRE HAZARD CONFIRMED.*

The lights in the dungeon flickered. Then, they dimmed. The red emergency strobes activated. They pulsed in the corners, casting long, jagged shadows.

*EMERGENCY EGRESS ACTIVATED.*

The doors at the far end of the corridor, which had been sealed for three hundred years, blew open. The force of the pressure change slammed them against the walls.

"Run," Gerald said.

"Where?" Bartholomew yelled.

"To the exit. The System thinks there is a fire. It wants us to leave.

If we are in the exit path, we are 'Evacuated.' We cannot be audited during an evacuation. It is a liability issue.

"

Bartholomew splashed toward the open door.

Gerald followed, his mental form straining against the chaos. The dungeon was tearing itself apart. The walls were buckling.

The ceiling was raining dust, and they reached the threshold. The air outside was fresh. It smelled of old books and dust.

Gerald stepped out. He did not step onto the library floor. He stepped onto a metal grate that had formed in the doorway. The grate was glowing red.

*EVACUATION IN PROGRESS. DO NOT RETURN TO STRUCTURE.*

Gerald stopped. He looked back at his dungeon.

It was collapsing, and the mold was burning. The water was boiling. The rat was safe, but the dungeon was dying.

And then, he saw it.

In the corner of the doorway, hidden behind the burning debris, was a small, black door. It had not been there before. It was not part of the dungeon's original blueprint.

It was marked with a symbol. A circle with a line through it.

The symbol of the Inspector General.

*AUDIT CONTINUES.*

Gerald's blood ran cold. The fire was a trick.

The evacuation was a trick, and the IG had not stopped.

It had merely paused to allow Gerald to expose the *real* violation.

The black door opened.

From the darkness behind it, a hand reached out. It was not a monster's hand. It was not a goblin's claw.

It was a human hand. Pale. Thin. With long, manicured fingernails.

It held a stamp.

A red, ink-stained stamp.

*DENIED.*

The stamp hit the doorframe. The wood exploded. The force of the denial slammed Gerald backward.

He fell into the burning sub-basement. The door slammed shut.

*APPEAL DISMISSAL FINAL.*

*DEMOLOTION COMMENCING IN 60 SECONDS.*

Bartholomew screamed. He scrambled up the stairs, clawing at the library floor. He was alive. He was out.

But Gerald was trapped, and the walls were closing in. The System was deleting the geometry of his existence. The floor was disappearing, replaced by void.

Gerald looked at the stamp. He looked at the black door. He looked at the clock in his mind.

Fifty seconds, and he had one option. One loophole. One dirty, desperate, municipal trick that he had never used because it was unethical, illegal, and likely to get him fired if he were still alive.

He could not appeal to the IG. The IG was the judge.

But every dungeon had a neighbor.

Hale was gone. Crushed. Dead.

But Hale's neighbor was still there.

Gerald focused his mind. He reached out, past the burning walls, past the void, into the adjacent dungeon space. The space occupied by the *Grotto of Despair*.

It was a high-level dungeon. A boss dungeon. Full of elite spawns.

Gerald could not enter it. He could not fight it.

But he could file a complaint.

"Violation," Gerald whispered. "Noise ordinance. Section 8, Paragraph 2. Excessive sonic disturbance during business hours."

He pushed the complaint with all his remaining strength.

The *Grotto of Despair* was currently hosting a raid party. They were screaming. Spells were exploding. The noise was deafening.

To the System, the noise was part of the dungeon. But to Gerald, using the loophole he had just invented in the hearing, the noise was a *disturbance* to the adjacent property.

And the property was his.

If the *Grotto of Despair* was cited for noise pollution, it would be subject to a *Silence Mandate*.

A Silence Mandate froze all sound. All vibration. All magical resonance.

It would stop the collapse of Gerald's dungeon.

It would stop the IG's demolition.

But it would also freeze the raid party inside the Grotto. And if they froze, they would be stuck. And if they were stuck, the Grotto would be cited for *False Imprisonment*.

It was a chain reaction. A bureaucratic nuclear option.

Gerald pulled the trigger.

*NOISE CITATION ISSUED.*

The world went silent.

The burning walls stopped crumbling. The boiling water froze in mid-splash. The IG's stamp hovered in the air, the red ink suspended.

Bartholomew, halfway up the stairs, stopped. He looked down.

Gerald stood in the center of the frozen chaos. He looked up at the black door.

The door did not open. But the lock clicked.

*CONDITIONAL RELEASE: WAITING FOR NEIGHBORING VIOLATION RESOLUTION.*

Gerald was not saved. He was paused.

And in the silence, he heard a voice. Not from the IG. Not from the System.

From the other side of the black door.

"Gerald?"

The voice was familiar. It was soft. Confused.

Gerald's breath hitched, and he knew that voice. He had heard it every morning for thirty-one years. It had asked him if he wanted coffee.

It had asked him if he had seen the latest zoning update.

"Gerald, why is the floor shaking?"

It was Martha. His ex-wife.

The door creaked open. Not from force. From recognition.

And behind her, standing in the light of the library, was not Martha alone.

Standing next to her, holding a clipboard, was a woman Gerald had never seen. She wore a suit sharper than his own. Her eyes were cold. Her hair was pulled back in a bun so tight it looked painful.

She looked at Gerald. She looked at the frozen dungeon. She looked at the stamp.

Then she looked at Gerald and smiled. It was a smile that promised paperwork.

"Inspector Hutchins," she said. "I am the new Deputy Inspector General. And I have some questions about your employment history."

Gerald stared at her. He recognized the badge.

He recognized the face, and she was the woman who had fired him. The woman who had called him a liability. The woman who had laughed when he said the library was unsafe.

She was now the System.

Gerald straightened his tie. His hands were shaking.

"Good morning," he said.

The cliffhanger was not the danger. The danger was the interview.
