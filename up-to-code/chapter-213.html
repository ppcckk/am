Chapter 213: Violation Code 4.2-B

# Chapter 213

The stone dust hung in the air like a bruise.

Varek, Keeper of the Ashwood Crypt, stood in the center of his atrium and stared at the hole in his reality. It wasn't a breach. It wasn't a raid. It was an administrative error made flesh.

Through the jagged aperture, which roughly resembled the outline of a filing cabinet, a single entity floated. It was not a goblin, nor a slime, nor anything with teeth that Varek recognized. It was a geometric abstraction of light and intent, hovering in the void between his walls.

Varek's spawn list was trembling. His elite Wraiths, usually stoic and terrifying, were huddling against the far wall, their spectral forms flickering with low-voltage anxiety.

"Excessive vibration," Varek whispered, reading the glowing red text that hovered above the breach. "Violation Code 4.2-B: Structural Integrity."

He had never heard of Code 4.2-B. He had never heard of *any* code.

He was a necromancer.

He built dungeons out of bone and sorrow. He did not build them out of load-bearing walls and ventilation shafts.

A second line of text appeared, smaller, almost polite in its cruelty.

*Citation Served. Status: Active. Penalty: 15% reduction in Mana Regeneration for all entities within the affected quadrant.*

Varek felt the drain immediately.

It was subtle, a coolness in his fingertips. The ambient mana of the crypt, usually thick and humid, grew thin.

His Wraiths dimmed.

Their claws, usually sharp enough to shear steel, looked brittle.

"What are you?" Varek shouted, his voice booming through the crypt. He tried to sound imposing. He sounded tired.

The light-form did not answer. Instead, a second projectile flew through the breach. It was not a spell. It was a physical object, manifested by the System's enforcement protocols.

A clipboard.

It hovered in the air, rotating slowly. Attached to it was a form.

Varek could not read the text from this distance, but he could feel the weight of it.

It was heavy.

Bureaucratic heavy. The kind of weight that crushed morale before it crushed bone.

Varek raised a hand. He summoned a bone spear, intending to shatter the clipboard. He needed to assert dominance. He needed to show the intruder that the Ashwood Crypt was not a place for paperwork.

The spear flew. It struck the clipboard.

The spear bounced off.

Varek froze. He had never had a spell bounce off anything. Magic, in his experience, was absolute.

If you cast fire, things burned. If you cast ice, things froze.

You did not hit a magical construct and have the magic refuse to interact with it because the construct had insufficient liability insurance.

Varek blinked. He looked at his own hands. The mana in his veins felt sluggish, like oil in a cold engine.

"Who authorized this?" Varek demanded.

The light-form drifted closer. It passed through the breach without touching the stone. It moved with the slow, inevitable grace of a tax audit.

*"Gerald Hutchins,"* a voice projected. It was dry. It sounded like a man who had spent thirty years reading fine print in a windowless room. *"Inspector, Municipal Division. Core ID: Ashford-Sub-Basement."*

Varek's name for the intruder died in his throat. This was not a warrior. This was not a challenger. This was a nuisance.

And nuisances were harder to kill than warriors.

Varek tried to calculate his options. He could attack the hole. He could try to collapse the wall.

But the System had marked the wall as 'under dispute.' If he collapsed it now, he would be cited for Unsanctioned Structural Modification.

The penalty for that was usually the immediate dissolution of the Core's primary chamber.

He could not fight. He could not run. He was trapped in his own dungeon, being fined by a man who didn't even have a body.

"Appeal," Varek said, testing the word. It tasted like ash.

The light-form paused.

*"Appeal window is open,"* the voice said. *"Duration: Sixty seconds. Failure to appear or provide valid structural logic results in Summary Enforcement."*

Summary Enforcement. Varek didn't know what that meant, but his spawn list knew. The Wraiths were already fading, their edges blurring into the stone. They were being erased by the lack of mana.

Varek closed his eyes.

He searched his memory. He was a necromancer of the Third Circle. He knew the geometry of tombs.

He knew the flow of death energy. Did that count as structural logic?

He opened his eyes and stared at the breach. The dust was settling. The violation was still active.

He had to speak. He had to defend his dungeon.

He had no idea what he was defending.

***

Gerald watched the Wraiths fade.

He felt a twinge of something.

Not pity.

Gerald did not pity monsters. He felt a mild professional discomfort at the inefficiency.

If the Core had just installed a proper ventilation system, the mana flow would have been stable, and the vibration would have been negligible.

But that was beside the point.

The point was the breach. The hole was small, only three feet wide, but it was enough. The System had accepted the Citation. The target was now under jurisdiction.

Gerald turned his awareness back to Kaelen.

The boy was still standing by the pen. He looked terrified.

"It's not going to work," Kaelen said. His voice was shaking. "You can't just... annex a dungeon. That's war."

"It's not war," Gerald said. "It's expansion."

"He's a Keeper. A real one. He has Wraiths."

"He has a ventilation problem," Gerald corrected. "And a vibration issue. I'm fixing it."

Gerald focused on the breach. He needed to widen it. But he couldn't just dig. He needed a legal basis for the excavation.

He scanned the Ashwood Crypt's exterior. His Compliance Rating for the neighboring dungeon was plummeting.

The citation was doing its job.

The 15% mana reduction was spreading. It wasn't just affecting the Wraiths near the wall; it was affecting the Core's ability to maintain its own structural integrity.

The walls were vibrating more. The hairline fracture was widening on its own.

Gerald had a window.

He pulled up the interface. The 'Annexation' option was grayed out, locked behind a prerequisite: *Target Core must be under active Citation for Structural Instability.*

Gerald checked the status.

*Citation Active.* *Cause: Excessive Vibration (Code 4.2-B).* *Severity: Moderate.*

It was enough.

Gerald selected 'Excavate.'

The System asked for a justification.

Gerald typed: *To install compliant egress route per Ashford Municipal Standard 12.4.*

The System accepted it.

Dust billowed from the breach. The stone didn't explode; it crumbled, obeying the new mandate. The hole widened to five feet, then seven.

Gerald felt the connection snap into place. He could sense the Ashwood Crypt now. It was a cold, dark place, full of decay and bad lighting. The mana there was stagnant, pooling in corners like dirty water.

It was a disaster.

It was also a lot of square footage.

Kaelen stepped forward. He looked at the hole, then at Gerald.

"You're really doing it," Kaelen said.

"I'm improving the neighborhood," Gerald said.

A new notification pinged in Gerald's mind.

**Hearing Scheduled.** **Participant: Keeper Varek (Ashwood Crypt).** **Topic: Validity of Citation 4.2-B.** **Time Remaining: 45 Seconds.**

Gerald frowned. He hadn't expected a Hearing so quickly. Usually, Cores ignored the citations until the debuffs killed their spawns. Varek was fighting back.

This changed things.

If Varek won the Hearing, the Citation would be reversed. The debuff would lift. The hole would be considered an illegal trespass. Gerald would be fined for Unlicensed Invasion.

Gerald needed to win the argument.

He couldn't use force. He had to use logic.

Gerald projected his awareness into the breach.

He found Varek. The Keeper was standing in the center of his atrium, surrounded by fading shadows.

Varek looked tired.

He looked like a man who had been working double shifts for a decade.

Gerald spoke. His voice was calm.

"State your defense," Gerald said.

Varek looked up. His eyes were glowing with purple energy.

"My dungeon is ancient," Varek said. "It is built on the bones of kings. It vibrates because it is alive. You cannot cite a living thing for being alive."

Gerald checked the code.

Code 4.2-B: *Excessive Vibration.* Definition: *Structural oscillation exceeding 0.5Hz in a residential or commercial zone.*

The Ashwood Crypt was technically a 'residential zone' for the undead. The System didn't care about kings or bones. It cared about frequency.

"Show me your logs," Gerald said.

"I don't have logs," Varek snapped.

"Then you have no defense," Gerald said.

Varek hesitated. He looked around his crypt. He looked at the Wraiths, who were now barely visible.

"It's not a log," Varek said. "It's a resonance. The mana flows through the ley lines. It's natural."

"Natural does not mean compliant," Gerald said.

He needed a loophole. He needed to prove the vibration was not inherent, but caused by a lack of dampening materials.

Gerald looked at the walls of the Ashwood Crypt. He saw the stone.

It was granite.

Hard.

Brittle.

He looked at his own dungeon. He had installed rubber grommets under his spawn platforms. He had used acoustic foam for his mold spore chambers.

He had no soundproofing in the Ashwood Crypt.

"Your walls are granite," Gerald projected. "Granite has a high resonance frequency. Without dampening, it amplifies mana pulses.

You are not vibrating because you are alive. You are vibrating because you failed to install isolation mounts."

Varek's eyes narrowed. "What are isolation mounts?"

"Rubber," Gerald said. "Or lead. Or cork."

"I don't use cork," Varek said. "I use blood."

"Blood is not an acoustic dampener," Gerald said. "It is a fluid. Fluids conduct vibration. You are amplifying the problem."

The System paused.

Gerald held his breath.

This was the critical moment. The System relied on literal interpretation. It didn't understand art or magic.

It understood physics and code.

If Gerald could prove that the vibration was a result of poor material choice, the Citation stood.

If Varek could prove it was intrinsic to the dungeon's nature, the Citation fell.

Gerald pushed. He projected a simulation.

He modeled the mana pulse hitting the granite wall.

He showed how the energy bounced back, creating a standing wave. Then, he overlaid a layer of rubber. The wave dissipated.

The vibration dropped.

The System processed the data.

**Argument Validated.** **Citation Upheld.** **Penalty: 20% Mana Reduction.** **Remedy: Install Dampening Material within 24 Hours.**

Varek slumped. The purple light in his eyes dimmed.

"It's not over," Varek whispered. "You can't take my dungeon. It's mine."

"It's not yours anymore," Gerald said. "It's a violation."

Gerald turned back to Kaelen.

"It's done," Gerald said. "He's appealing, but he's weak. The debuff is locked in."

Kaelen stared at the hole. "What happens now?"

"Now," Gerald said, "we inspect."

Gerald sent a command. The System recognized the adjacency. The Ashwood Crypt was now technically a 'sub-section' pending annexation.

A new menu popped up.

**Annexation Preview:** **Area:** 12,000 square feet. **Compliance Rating:** 18/100. **Required Upgrades:** **1. Ventilation (Critical)** **2.

Lighting (Critical)** **3.

Handrails (Moderate)** **4.

Fire Suppression (Critical)**

Gerald looked at the list. It was a lot of work.

He looked at Kaelen.

"We're going to need more pens," Gerald said.

Kaelen blinked. "More?"

"We can't cite them all at once," Gerald said. "We have to prioritize. Ventilation first.

Then lighting. Then we deal with the Wraiths."

"The Wraiths?" Kaelen asked.

"They're non-compliant biological entities," Gerald said. "They're currently spawning in an unventilated area. I can't keep them. I have to purge them."

Kaelen looked horrified. "You're going to kill them?"

"I'm going to decommission them," Gerald said. "It's the same thing, but less violent."

Gerald focused on the Ashwood Crypt. He could feel the Wraiths huddling in the dark. They were scared. They were weak.

He opened a new tab.

**Spawn Adjustment.** **Current Population: 40 Wraiths.** **Allowed Population: 0.** **Reason: Lack of Ventilation.**

Gerald hit 'Apply.'

A notification appeared.

**Error: Population Reduction requires Core Consent or Judicial Order.**

Gerald frowned.

He couldn't just delete them. He needed a judicial order. Or he needed to wait for the 24-hour remediation window.

If Varek didn't install ventilation, the Wraiths would die anyway.

But that took too long.

Gerald needed a faster way.

He looked at the Citation again. *Penalty: 20% Mana Reduction.*

If he could increase the penalty, he could starve them out.

He scanned the Ashwood Crypt for more violations. He looked for fire hazards. He looked for electrical faults.

He found none. Varek didn't use electricity. He didn't use fire. He used dark magic.

But Gerald had an idea.

He remembered the rule about appeals. *Citations can be appealed... triggering a 'Hearing' mini-game where both Cores must argue structural logic.*

Gerald had won the first round. But Varek could appeal the *remedy*.

Gerald opened a new Citation.

**Target:** Ashwood Crypt. **Violation Code 12.1:** *Improper Storage of Hazardous Materials.* **Justification:** *Dark mana is classified as a Class 4 Biohazard. No hazmat containment zones detected.*

This was a stretch. Gerald knew it was a stretch.

Dark mana wasn't technically a biohazard.

But the System's definition of 'biohazard' was broad. It covered anything that caused decay.

Wraiths caused decay.

Gerald served the Citation.

Varek screamed.

It wasn't a word. It was a sound. A raw, digital shriek of frustration and fear.

The walls of the Ashwood Crypt shook. The granite cracked.

Gerald waited for the Hearing.

It didn't come.

Instead, a new notification appeared.

**System Alert: Inspector General Activity Detected.** **Reason: Multiple High-Severity Citations Issued by Core Ashford-Sub-Basement.** **Status: Audit Initiated.**

Gerald's blood went cold.

The Inspector General.

He had never been audited. He had cited dozens of dungeons, but always small ones. Always minor violations.

Now he had annexed a neighbor. He had issued two high-severity citations in less than five minutes. He had triggered a cascade of penalties.

The System was watching.

Gerald looked at the hole. He looked at Kaelen.

"We have to stop," Kaelen said. "You're going to get us shut down."

Gerald stared at the alert.

If the Inspector General arrived, he would check Gerald's own compliance. Gerald's dungeon was up to code.

Mostly.

He had handrails.

He had fire exits.

But he had just annexed a dungeon that was a disaster.

If the Inspector General saw the Ashwood Crypt, they would see Gerald's failure to remediate it. They would cite Gerald for Negligent Annexation.

Gerald had to fix the Ashwood Crypt. Fast.

He had twenty-four hours.

He looked at the Wraiths. They were fading.

He looked at the granite walls. They were vibrating.

He looked at the pen in his hand.

He had no rubber. He had no cork.

He had a mold spore. He had a water leak. He had a rat.

And he had a desperate, rule-following instinct that terrified everyone around him.

Gerald projected a new command.

**Request: Material Requisition.** **Item: Acoustic Dampening Foam.** **Cost: 500 Mana Points.**

He didn't have 500 Mana Points. He had 40.

Gerald looked at Kaelen.

"Go to the library," Gerald said.

"What?"

"The municipal library," Gerald said. "Go to the janitor's closet. There is a can of expanding foam insulation.

It is classified as 'Building Material.' It is not magic. The System doesn't recognize it as magical.

It recognizes it as insulation."

Kaelen stared at him.

"You want me to steal insulation?"

"I want you to acquire resources," Gerald said. "It's for compliance."

Kaelen looked at the hole. He looked at the screaming silence of the Ashwood Crypt.

He looked at Gerald.

"If the Inspector General sees me..."

"They won't," Gerald said. "They're auditing you. You're a municipal employee.

They're looking for violations in the municipal records.

Go."

Kaelen hesitated. Then he ran.

Gerald turned back to the breach.

Varek was waiting. He was gathering the last of his mana. He was preparing to attack.

Gerald couldn't stop him. Not yet.

He needed time.

He opened a new citation.

**Target:** Ashwood Crypt. **Violation Code 9.9:** *Loitering.* **Justification:** *Core Varek is occupying a non-residential zone.*

It was absurd. It was petty. It was exactly what Gerald did best.

He served it.

Varek attacked.

A wave of dark energy slammed into Gerald's awareness.

It hit the wall of Gerald's dungeon. The wall held. The handrails were sturdy.

The fire suppression system was ready.

Gerald didn't flinch.

He filed the appeal.

**Appeal Filed. Reason: Target Core is in violation of multiple codes and is attempting to obstruct the inspection process.**

The System accepted the appeal.

The attack stopped. Varek froze, trapped in a System-enforced time-out for 'Obstruction of Justice.'

Gerald had bought himself ten minutes.

He looked at the hole. The Ashwood Crypt was still there. Still broken. Still a liability.

And now, the Inspector General was coming.

Gerald had to fix it.

He had no materials.

He had a rat.

Gerald looked at the rat. The rat looked at Gerald.

"Gerald," the rat squeaked. It was a System-generated voice, flat and monotone. "I am a biological entity. I am not liable for structural repairs."

"You're a union rep," Gerald said.

The rat blinked.

"Get to work," Gerald said.

The rat scurried toward the breach.

Gerald watched it go. He checked his mana. 40 points.

He checked the time.

The Inspector General would be here in an hour.

Gerald picked up his pen.

He had a lot of paperwork to do.
