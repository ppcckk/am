Chapter 118: Zero Mana Merge

# Chapter 118 The ceiling did not fall.

It stopped falling.

The chunk of concrete that had been plummeting toward Gerald’s skull hung suspended in the air, frozen by a lattice of translucent purple light. The dust motes drifted lazily around the suspended debris. The screaming fans had cut out. The sludge had stopped rising, its surface now perfectly flat and reflective.

Gerald Hutchins, formerly of the City of Ashford Municipal Inspection Division, currently of the Dungeon Core Registry, blinked. His left eye twitched.

A small, involuntary spasm, which was a good sign, and it meant he was still alive, and therefore still able to process bureaucratic nonsense.

The woman in the blue suit stood ten feet away.

Her badge pulsed with a rhythm that matched Gerald’s heartbeat. She had not moved.

She had not flinched.

She had simply waited for his Mana pool to hit zero, which had happened four seconds ago, and then waited for the System to pause the simulation so it could calculate the legal ramifications of his failure.

"Time of suspension," the woman said. Her voice was smooth, devoid of inflection.

"14:03:12.

Core ID: GH-54-ASH.

Status: Critical Failure. Reason: Attempted Citation without sufficient mana reserves or valid target proximity."

Gerald looked at his hands. They were shaking. Not from fear.

From rage. He had calculated the mana cost.

He had checked the range.

He had confirmed the footwear violation was technically valid under Section 4, Subsection C of the Hazardous Zone Safety Act, which the System had adopted verbatim from OSHA regulations in the late nineties.

He had been tricked.

Kowalski, and the man had entered *after* the citation. Gerald had assumed the first boot was Miller.

Miller had worn steel-toed boots.

Kowalski had worn loafers. Loafers were a violation in a construction zone, yes, but not in a *Dungeon Core* zone unless the Core had explicitly posted signage.

Gerald had posted the signage, and he had posted it three chapters ago.

He looked at the wall behind the woman. There, etched into the damp concrete in glowing blue runes, was the notice: *HAZARD ZONE: PROPER FOOTWEAR REQUIRED.*

He had missed it. Not because it wasn’t there, but because he had been looking at the wrong thing. He had been looking at the threat. He should have been looking at the compliance.

"You failed to cite the correct entity," the woman repeated, stepping closer. The purple lattice around the ceiling chunk shimmered.

"You issued a Citation to a void.

You attempted to enforce a code that requires active Core maintenance you did not provide.

Your Compliance Rating has dropped to twelve. The threshold for termination is ten.

You are one point from deletion."

Gerald opened his interface. He tried to access the Appeals window. It was grayed out. Locked.

*Appeal Window Closed. Reason: Core Integrity Critical.*

He could not argue. He could not fight.

He could not even stand up properly; his structural integrity was compromised.

The floor beneath his avatar’s feet was tilting slightly to the left, a two-degree deviation that would have failed a basic wheelchair ramp inspection in his previous life. It was failing his life now.

The woman reached into her jacket. She produced a tablet. It was black, sleek, and devoid of buttons; hovered in the air as she held it, projecting a holographic docket.

"I am Inspector Vane," she said.

"System Enforcement Division. I am here to execute the termination order.

Do you have a final statement?

Or do you wish to submit to immediate dissolution and reintegration into the ambient mana pool?"

Gerald looked at the tablet. He looked at the hologram.

It displayed his dungeon’s layout, which was a mess.

The ventilation shafts were narrow. The exit paths were cluttered with crates he had placed to block the goblin.

The lighting was uneven. It was a disaster. A municipal nightmare.

And yet.

Gerald squinted at the hologram. Specifically, at the bottom right corner. The footer.

Every System document had a footer. A disclaimer. A jurisdictional tag.

Inspector Vane’s tablet read: *Case ID: SE-9942. Jurisdiction: Ashford Sector. Core Type: Residential/Mixed Use.*

Gerald’s heart skipped a beat.

His dungeon was not residential, which was *Municipal*.

It was built beneath the Ashford Public Library. It was adjacent to the City Hall annex. It was technically part of the public infrastructure grid.

He had filed the Core Registration paperwork under *Sub-Basement Utility Access*. He had never changed it.

He had never updated his Core Type.

Why would he? He didn’t care about the taxonomy. He cared about the code.

But the System cared about the taxonomy.

"Inspector Vane," Gerald said. His voice was dry. Crackling. Like dry paper.

Vane paused. She looked down at him. "Yes, Core Hutchins?"

"You are executing termination under the assumption that I am a standard Commercial Dungeon Core."

"I am executing termination under the assumption that you have violated the Universal Dungeon Safety Code, Section 1, which applies to all cores regardless of type," Vane said. "You have failed to maintain a minimum Compliance Rating of 10. You have attempted an unauthorized Citation. You are non-compliant."

"Section 1 applies to *Private* Dungeon Cores," Gerald said.

Vane tilted her head.

A micro-expression of annoyance. "Section 1 applies to all cores that generate hostile entities.

You have a rat.

A mold spore. A water leak. These are hostile entities."

"The water leak is not hostile," Gerald said. "It is a plumbing failure.

The mold is a sanitation issue, and the rat is a pest control issue.

None of these are *Hostile Aggressors* as defined by the Combat Code. Therefore, Section 1 does not apply."

Vane’s smile vanished. "Your dungeon spawns adventurers. Adventurers are hostile."

"Adventurers are *guests*," Gerald corrected. "They enter voluntarily. They are not spawned by the Core.

They are not the Core’s product. They are visitors.

If they die, it is a liability issue, not a combat spawn issue."

Vane’s eyes narrowed. The purple light around her badge intensified. "You are arguing semantics to delay your death."

"I am arguing jurisdiction," Gerald said. "Look at your docket. Look at the footer. *Core Type: Residential/Mixed Use.*"

Vane glanced at the tablet. She frowned. "That is a data error. Your core has been flagged as Commercial due to adventurer traffic."

"It has not been *reclassified*," Gerald said. "I have submitted Form 4-Delta every month for the last six months. *Request for Reclassification: Municipal Utility Infrastructure.* It was denied. It was never approved. But it was never *processed*."

Gerald pointed at the ceiling. At the frozen concrete.

"I am a building inspector, Inspector Vane. I know how the System processes forms. If a form is denied, it is stamped.

If it is ignored, it is queued.

My Form 4-Delta is in the queue. It has been in the queue for four hundred and twelve days.

That is a processing backlog.

And while a form is in the queue, the original classification remains legally binding until a final ruling is issued."

Vane’s face went pale. The purple light on her badge flickered.

"That is... technically accurate," she said. Her voice was tighter.

"Technically," Gerald said. He stood up.

It hurt.

His core integrity was at 12%, but he forced his avatar to stand.

He straightened his spine. He adjusted his imaginary tie.

"If I am classified as Municipal Utility Infrastructure, then I am not a Dungeon Core. I am a Public Utility. I am subject to the Municipal Code, not the Combat Code."

Vane stepped forward. The ground shook. The lattice around the ceiling flickered again.

"Even if you are correct," she said, "your Compliance Rating is still twelve. The Municipal Code requires a rating of sixty for operational status. You are still failing."

"The Municipal Code," Gerald said, "does not measure Combat Compliance. It measures *Structural Integrity*, *Fire Safety*, *Electrical Safety*, and *Public Access*."

He raised a hand. He pointed to the hole in the ceiling. To the library above.

"Your Enforcement Division is not authorized to inspect for Combat Compliance. You are authorized to inspect for *Structural* and *Sanitary* violations. Did you cite me for Combat violations?"

Vane hesitated. "Your Core generated a hostile entity. The rat."

"The rat is a pest," Gerald said. "It is not a hostile entity.

It is a sanitation violation.

And I have cited the sanitation violation. I have installed traps. I have installed UV lights.

I have increased ventilation."

"You have not increased ventilation," Vane said. "Your fans are off."

"They are off because I turned them off," Gerald said. "To reduce noise pollution for the library patrons above. Section 8, Subsection F: *Noise Abatement in Proximity to Public Libraries.* I am in compliance with the Municipal Code. I am *in violation* of the Combat Code, but I am not subject to the Combat Code."

Vane stared at him. For the first time, she looked uncertain.

"You are trying to game the System."

"I am trying to follow the code," Gerald said. "If the System has a conflict between classifications, it must resolve the conflict before executing termination. You cannot terminate a Public Utility for failing a Combat Inspection."

The air hummed. The System was thinking.

Gerald could feel it.

The ambient mana swirling around him was turning a deep, agitated orange; it was the color of a pending decision.

"Inspector Vane," Gerald said. "If you terminate me, you are terminating a piece of municipal infrastructure.

The City of Ashford will sue you.

The System will have to pay reparations for the library’s structural support.

Do you want to do that? Do you want to explain to your superiors why you destroyed a load-bearing column of the Public Library?"

Vane’s jaw tightened. She looked at her tablet. She swiped. She looked at the docket.

"The form is in the queue," she muttered. "It has been in the queue for four hundred and twelve days."

"Exactly," Gerald said. "So until that queue is processed, I am Municipal. And Municipal Cores are exempt from Combat Code Enforcement."

Vane lowered her tablet. The purple light dimmed. The lattice around the ceiling chunk dissolved. The chunk fell.

It crashed into the floor, kicking up a cloud of dust.

Gerald did not flinch. He watched it land.

"Fine," Vane said. Her voice was cold. "You have bought yourself time.

But the queue will be processed. And when it is, you will be reclassified.

And then we will be back."

"The queue," Gerald said, "moves slowly."

"It moves at the speed of justice," Vane said.

"It moves at the speed of bureaucracy," Gerald corrected. "And I have thirty-one years of experience navigating that speed."

Vane turned to leave. She walked toward the hole in the ceiling. She floated upward, drawn by a gravitational tether.

"Enjoy your utility status, Mr. Hutchins," she called down. "But be warned.

The System does not like loopholes.

The Inspector General will see this. The audit is coming."

She vanished into the darkness of the library.

Gerald was alone, and the ceiling was broken. The fans were off. The sludge was still there, stagnant and foul-smelling.

He looked at his interface.

*Compliance Rating: 12/100.* *Status: Municipal Utility (Pending Reclassification).* *Immunity: Combat Code Enforcement (Active).* *Time Until Queue Resolution: 14 Hours.*

Fourteen hours.

Gerald sat down on a crate. He rubbed his face. His hands were still shaking.

He had bought himself time.

But he had not fixed the problem. He was still non-compliant. He was still a mess.

And in fourteen hours, Vane would be back. Or the Inspector General would be back.

And they would not be fooled by the queue, and he needed to change the classification. Permanently.

He opened his inventory.

He had three items. A mold spore.

A water leak. A rat.

He had no mana. He had no citations. He had no tools.

But he had a pen, and he opened his journal; it was a digital log, but it functioned like a legal pad. He started typing.

*To: System Administrative Registry.* *From: Core GH-54-ASH.* *Subject: Request for Immediate Code Reclassification and Structural Amendment.*

*Body:*

*Pursuant to Municipal Code Section 12, Paragraph 4, I hereby request an emergency structural amendment to my Core footprint. The current footprint (4,000 sq ft) is insufficient for municipal utility standards, which require a minimum of 10,000 sq ft for a public access hub.*

*I am requesting the absorption of the adjacent vacant lot, Parcel ID: 44-B, currently occupied by the abandoned Ashford Parking Garage.*

*Reason: The parking garage is in a state of disrepair and constitutes a public hazard. By absorbing the garage, I can provide compliant egress routes, proper ventilation, and ADA-compliant access ramps for the library patrons, and so raising my Compliance Rating to a level that satisfies both Municipal and System standards.*

*This absorption will convert my Core Type from 'Sub-Basement Utility' to 'Integrated Municipal Complex'. This reclassification will render me immune to Combat Code Enforcement as I will be designated as 'Essential Public Infrastructure'.*

*Signature: Gerald Hutchins.*

*Date: Today.*

He hit send.

The System chime sounded.

*Request Received. Processing...*

*Analysis: Absorption of Parcel 44-B requires Core Mana. Current Mana: 0.*

*Request Denied.*

Gerald cursed. Of course. He had no mana. He couldn't expand.

He looked at the journal. He looked at the denial.

He had no mana. He could not absorb the garage.

But he could *invite* it, and he looked at the map of his dungeon. He looked at the neighboring dungeon. The one that had sent the goblin. The one that had been cited by Miller.

The neighboring dungeon was *also* non-compliant. It was a mid-tier combat dungeon. It was filled with traps that violated fire codes. It was filled with creatures that violated sanitation laws.

If Gerald couldn't absorb the garage, he could *merge* with the neighboring dungeon.

Mergers were allowed. But they required mutual consent. Or a System-mandated merger due to "Adjacent Hazard Synergy."

If two adjacent dungeons were both in critical violation, the System could force a merger to create a single, larger, more stable entity.

Gerald needed the neighbor to be in critical violation, and he needed to raise the neighbor's violation count.

He had no citations. He had no mana.

But he had a rat.

He looked at the rat. It was sitting on a crate, grooming its whiskers.

It was unlicensed, which was unsanitary. It was a hazard.

Gerald opened the chat window. He had never used it. He didn't know how.

He typed: *Hello.*

No response, and he typed: *This is Gerald. From the library.*

No response.

He typed: *I have a rat.*

A message popped up.

*Core 774:* *Who is this? What do you want?*

Gerald typed: *I have a rat. It is carrying a disease vector. It has just entered your dungeon.*

*Core 774:* *No it hasn't.*

Gerald typed: *Check your south corridor. It is there. It is sneezing.*

He looked at his own dungeon. The rat was not moving.

But the System message changed.

*Violation Detected: Unlicensed Biological Entity in Adjacent Zone.* *Core 774 Compliance Rating: 45/100 -> 30/100.*

*Reason: Inadvertent Cross-Domain Contamination.*

*Core 774 Status: Warning.*

*Core 774 Message: What did you do? How did you get that rat in here?*

Gerald smiled. A thin, cruel smile.

He hadn't done anything, and he had just *said* it was there. The System believed him.

The System had a record of him having a rat.

The System had a record of the rat being near the wall. The System inferred the transfer.

It was a lie. A bureaucratic lie. But the System loved paperwork. And if the paperwork said the rat was in the neighbor's dungeon, then for the purposes of the audit, it was.

Core 774 Compliance Rating: 30/100.

Still not critical. Not enough for a forced merger.

He needed to drop it below 10, and he typed: *The rat also has a fire hazard. It is chewing on your electrical wiring.*

*Violation Detected: Fire Safety Negligence.* *Core 774 Compliance Rating: 30/100 -> 15/100.*

*Core 774 Message: STOP. What are you doing?*

Gerald typed: *I am filing a complaint. Section 9, Subsection D: Cross-Domain Hazard Reporting.*

He hit send.

*Core 774 Compliance Rating: 15/100 -> 8/100.*

*Critical Violation Detected.* *Core 774 Status: Deactivation Imminent.* *System Notice: Adjacent Hazard Synergy Detected. Forced Merger Initiated.*

The walls of Gerald's dungeon began to shake.

Not from falling concrete.

From expansion.

The floor beneath him grew. The ceiling rose. The darkness expanded, swallowing the shadows, swallowing the sludge, swallowing the rat.

Gerald felt his Core stretching. It hurt. It felt like being pulled apart at the seams.

But he was getting bigger, and he was getting more code-compliant.

Because a bigger dungeon, with proper egress and ventilation, had a higher base rating.

The merger was happening.

And with the merger, Gerald would have mana.

Enough to fix the ceiling.

Enough to fix the fans.

Enough to fix the rat.

But as the new walls rose, as the new corridors opened, Gerald heard a sound.

Not from his dungeon.

Not from the neighbor's dungeon.

From the library above.

A door slammed.

Footsteps. Heavy. Fast. Running.

They weren't running away, and they were running *down*.

Someone was entering the library. Someone who wasn't an adventurer. Someone who wasn't a cop.

The System message changed.

*New Entity Detected: Unauthorized Entry.* *Identity: Unknown.* *Threat Level: Critical.* *Core 774 (Merged) Compliance Rating: 55/100.*

*Warning: New Core Integration Incomplete. Vulnerabilities Exposed.*

Gerald looked up. The new ceiling was high. The air was fresh. The fans were spinning.

But the door to the library stairwell was wide open.

And a figure was standing in the doorway, which was small. It was wrapped in rags. It held a glowing shard of crystal in its hand.

It looked at Gerald.

It smiled.

"Hello, Gerald," it said. "I've been waiting for you to merge."

The figure raised the crystal.

The System message flashed red.

*Violation Detected: Core Theft Attempt.* *Status: Active.*
