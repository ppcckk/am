Chapter 342: Pen Against Concrete

# Chapter 342

The hand punched through the concrete.

Fingers, thick and matted with gray dust, clawed at the edge of the newly formed chasm. The creature beneath was massive.

It didn't emerge fully.

It rose slowly, a hump of muscle and scarred tissue breaching the surface like a submarine surfacing. Its skin was the color of bruised iron. No eyes.

Only a mouth, a jagged rift in the face, filled with rows of serrated teeth that clicked against each other.

Gerald didn't flinch, and he stepped back, one precise footfall. His pen hovered over the clipboard.

His eyes scanned the entity's dimensions.

Height: approximately eight feet. Width: three feet at the shoulders. The creature let out a shriek that shattered a nearby support column.

Dust rained down from the ceiling of the sub-basement.

A hazard.

Not a monster. A hazard.

The Auditor watched from the edge of the expansion zone.

Her gavel was still raised, her face a mask of rigid protocol.

She hadn't stopped the citation. She couldn't.

The violation was real. The egress failure was real. The destruction of the library's foundation was real.

But this? This unauthorized breach of the structural integrity by an external biological agent was something else.

Gerald clicked his tongue.

"Form 11-B," he muttered. "Unlicensed Biological Entity. Trespassing on protected structural easement."

The creature lunged.

It moved with terrifying speed for something so large. It swiped a clawed hand at Gerald. The air displaced with a violent *whoosh*.

Gerald didn't dodge. He didn't have time. But he didn't need to.

He had installed handrails.

Standard municipal code 14.2: *All subterranean pathways exceeding ten feet in length must be equipped with continuous handrails of steel or equivalent material, positioned between 34 and 38 inches above the walking surface.*

Gerald had installed them, and he had installed them everywhere.

The creature's hand slammed into the cold steel railing that lined the newly created walkway.

Metal screeched against bone. Sparks flew. The creature roared, pulling its hand back, fingers broken and bent at unnatural angles.

It stumbled, off-balance, its momentum arrested by the barrier that Gerald had deemed non-negotiable.

**[VIOLATION DETECTED: PHYSICAL CONTACT WITH UNAUTHORIZED OBSTRUCTION.]** **[PENALTY: STRUCTURAL DISRUPTION TO AGGRESSOR.]**

The creature thrashed, trying to pull itself out of the hole, but its grip on the edge failed. It slipped back into the dark, cursing in a language that sounded like grinding rocks.

Gerald scribbled on his clipboard.

"Improper use of public infrastructure," he said. "Citation issued."

The Auditor lowered her gavel. She looked at Gerald. For the first time, her expression wasn't just annoyed. It was confused.

"You cited it?" she asked.

"I cited the barrier," Gerald corrected. "The barrier is compliant. The entity that interfered with the compliant barrier is non-compliant. Therefore, the entity is subject to removal under municipal safety protocols."

"You are twisting the System," the Auditor said.

"I am applying it," Gerald said. "Code is literal.

If you install a rail, you must not touch the rail.

If you touch the rail, you violate the code. The System enforces the code. Therefore, the System removes the violator.

"

The creature let out a final, gurgling scream. The hole sealed itself.

The concrete flowed back together like water, knitting shut with a hiss of steam.

The hand vanished, and the threat was gone.

But the problem remained.

The library above was gone.

Gerald looked up. The ceiling of his dungeon, the underside of the Ashford Municipal Library, was now a jagged ruin of rebar and broken marble. Above, in the real world, the building had collapsed into its own sub-basement.

And the expansion zone was still active.

Gerald's dungeon footprint had grown. The citation radius had forced the System to acknowledge the library's structural failure. By citing the library for inadequate egress, Gerald had effectively declared the entire library a hazard zone. And hazard zones were part of his dungeon.

He could feel it.

The library's foundations were now his floors. The library's basement was now his lobby. The library's reading rooms, the shelves, the tables, the thousands of books—all of it was now technically

"sub-structure."

Which meant it was under his jurisdiction.

Which meant it was up for inspection.

Gerald walked toward the new boundary line. It was a shimmering wall of blue light, separating his original 4,000-square-foot sub-basement from the newly annexed library ruins.

He stepped through.

The air changed. It smelled of old paper, dust, and ozone.

Gerald looked around. The library was a disaster. Shelves were toppled.

Books littered the floor. The ceiling had collapsed in three places, creating mounds of debris.

But it was also vast.

And it was full of things that weren't his.

A party of adventurers was standing in what used to be the periodicals section. There were five of them. A knight in dented plate armor.

A mage with a glowing staff.

A rogue picking at a lock on a door that no longer led anywhere. A cleric looking at the ceiling.

And a bard, trying to play a lute that had lost three strings.

They froze when they saw Gerald.

Gerald wore his work clothes. Gray slacks. A white shirt, tucked in.

A tie, knotted tightly. A clipboard in one hand.

A pen in the other, and he looked exactly like he had when he was inspecting the building, thirty-one years ago.

Except he was translucent now. And he was floating an inch off the ground.

The knight stepped forward. His sword was drawn.

"Who are you?" the knight demanded.

Gerald looked at the knight. Then he looked at the sword.

"Is that weapon properly registered?" Gerald asked.

The knight blinked.

"What?"

"Weapons are considered hazardous materials in municipal libraries," Gerald said. "Per code 8.4, 'Firearms and bladed weapons exceeding six inches in length must be sheathed and secured in a locked container when inside a public reading area.' You are holding your sword. You are not in a locked container."

The mage raised her staff.

"We're here to kill the core. We're here for the loot."

"Loot is defined as unclaimed property of value," Gerald said.

"In a municipal library, all items are state property. You cannot loot state property.

That is theft. Code 12.1.

"

The rogue sneered.

"It's a dungeon, old man. It's not a library anymore."

"It is," Gerald said. "I cited it. Therefore, it is part of my jurisdiction. And your behavior is non-compliant."

The Auditor's voice echoed in Gerald's mind.

**[WARNING: MULTIPLE UNLICENSED ENTITIES DETECTED IN EXPANSION ZONE.]** **[STATUS: HOSTILE.]** **[ACTION REQUIRED: REMOVAL OR CITATION.]**

Gerald looked at the party. They were strong.

He could feel it.

The knight's aura was thick with mana. The mage's staff crackled with lightning. They were mid-tier adventurers.

Dangerous.

But they were sloppy.

Gerald looked at the floor.

"Your footwear," he said.

The rogue looked down at his boots.

"What about them?"

"You are wearing soft-soled boots in a wet zone," Gerald said. "Code 3.3: 'All personnel entering subterranean environments with exposed water tables must wear non-slip footwear rated for wet conditions.' Your boots have zero traction rating."

The rogue laughed.

"So?"

"So," Gerald said, "you are violating safety protocols. If you slip and fall, and you injure yourself, the library is not liable. But if you slip and fall and damage the structural integrity of the adjacent shelving units, *you* are liable for the repair costs."

He pointed his pen at the mage.

"And you," he said. "Your staff emits light. Code 7.2: 'Illumination in subterranean corridors must not exceed 500 lumens to prevent disorientation of local fauna.' Your staff is at 1,200 lumens. You are blinding the mold spores."

The mold spores. Gerald's original spawn. They were floating near the ceiling, agitated by the bright light.

The mage frowned.

"Who cares about mold?"

"The System cares," Gerald said. "If you blind the mold, the mold's productivity drops.

If the mold's productivity drops, the dungeon's resource generation slows.

If resource generation slows, the dungeon enters a deficit state. If the dungeon enters a deficit state, the System applies a penalty to all entities within.

"

The cleric whispered something.

"He's right. The mana density in the air is fluctuating. That's a deficit sign."

The knight lowered his sword slightly.

"What do you want?"

"I want compliance," Gerald said. "Or I want removal."

The knight scowled.

"We don't comply. We fight."

He charged.

Gerald didn't move.

The knight swung his sword, which was a heavy, two-handed greatsword. It swung with enough force to cleave a boulder.

Gerald stepped aside.

Not quickly. Not gracefully. Just... precisely. He moved two inches to the left.

The sword missed.

The knight overextended. The momentum of the heavy blade carried him forward.

And he stepped on a book.

A hardcover. *Municipal Zoning Ordinances of Ashford, Vol 4.*

The knight's foot slipped, and he fell.

He crashed into a shelving unit. The shelves were metal. The System had flagged the shelving unit as

"Structural Element: High Value."

The knight hit it.

The shelves didn't break. But the *impact* registered.

**[VIOLATION DETECTED: IMPACT ON STRUCTURAL ELEMENTS WITHOUT PROTECTIVE GEAR.]** **[PENALTY: KINETIC REFLECTION.]**

The shelf didn't move. The knight bounced.

He flew backward, hitting the mage.

The mage stumbled, her staff striking the floor. The light flared.

**[VIOLATION DETECTED: EXCESSIVE ILLUMINATION.]** **[PENALTY: TEMPORARY BLINDNESS.]**

The mage screamed, clutching her eyes.

The rogue tried to run. He slipped on the wet floor. He fell hard, cracking a rib.

**[VIOLATION DETECTED: IMPROPER FOOTWEAR.]** **[PENALTY: LOSS OF BALANCE.]**

The cleric tried to heal the rogue. But his hand slipped. He missed the spell component.

**[VIOLATION DETECTED: IMPROPER SPELLFORM PREPARATION.]** **[PENALTY: SPELL FAILURE.]**

The bard stopped playing. He looked at Gerald.

"What are you doing?" the bard asked.

"I am enforcing the code," Gerald said.

The knight groaned, trying to stand up. But his armor was tangled in the shelves. The System had deemed the shelves

"Static Obstacles." The knight was now "Obstructed."

He couldn't move, and the party was disabled. Not dead. Disabled.

Gerald walked over to the knight.

He looked down.

"You have three violations," Gerald said. "Weapon unsecured.

Improper footwear.

Structural impact.

Total penalty: Temporary Stat Debuff, -50% Strength, -50% Agility, Duration: 24 hours.

"

The knight glared up at him.

"This isn't fair."

"It is code," Gerald said.

He turned to the Auditor.

"Cite them," he said.

The Auditor hesitated.

"They are not dungeon cores."

"They are entities within a regulated zone," Gerald said. "The code applies to entities. Cite them."

The Auditor raised her gavel, and she brought it down.

**[CITATIONS ISSUED.]** **[PARTY STATUS: NON-COMPLIANT.]** **[RESTRICTION: PARTY LOCKED IN PLACE UNTIL COMPLIANCE IS RESTORED.]**

The knight's armor fused to the shelves. The mage's staff dimmed to a dull gray. The rogue's legs stiffened.

They were stuck.

Gerald looked at them.

"Stay there," he said. "I will return when you are ready to file your appeal."

He turned and walked away.

But the expansion wasn't over, and the library had annexed more than just the sub-basement. The citation radius had expanded. The entire block was now under scrutiny.

And something else was coming.

Gerald felt a pull. A different kind of pull.

Not from the ground. From the sky.

He looked up.

Through the hole in the ceiling, through the ruins of the library, he could see the sky, which was dark.

And descending from the clouds, riding a chariot of black iron pulled by four skeletal horses, was a figure.

A giant.

Twelve feet tall. Wearing armor made of bones. Holding a banner that read: **DEPARTMENT OF PUBLIC WORKS.**

The Auditor's eyes widened.

"Oh," she said. "No."

Gerald looked at the giant.

"Who is that?" he asked.

"The Regional Supervisor," the Auditor said. "He audits the auditors."

The giant landed in the middle of the library. The impact shook the remaining shelves. The party of adventurers flinched, despite being locked in place.

The giant looked at Gerald.

Then he looked at the citations Gerald had written, and he looked at the citation for the library.

He looked at the citations for the adventurers.

The giant smiled.

It was a terrible smile. Too many teeth.

"Gerald Hutchins," the giant boomed. His voice sounded like gravel in a cement mixer.

"You have cited the city," the giant said. "You have cited the citizens. You have cited the infrastructure."

The giant stepped forward.

"You have also cited *me*."

Gerald blinked.

"I have not seen you before," Gerald said.

"You did not need to see me," the giant said. "You cited the *Department*. I *am* the Department."

The giant raised a hand. In his palm, a small, glowing red tag appeared.

**[VIOLATION: INSPECTOR OVERSTEPPING JURISDICTION.]** **[PENALTY: DEMOTION.]**

Gerald felt a cold wind blow through his core.

His Compliance Rating, currently hovering at 85 due to the successful citations, dropped.

80.

75.

70.

The giant leaned in.

"You are not an inspector," the giant said. "You are a nuisance. And nuisances are removed."

The giant pulled out a hammer, which was the size of a truck.

Gerald looked at the hammer.

He looked at his clipboard.

He looked at the giant's feet.

The giant was standing on a loose tile.

**[WARNING: UNSTABLE SURFACE.]**

Gerald didn't write a citation, and he wrote a *request*.

**[REQUEST: IMMEDIATE REINFORCEMENT OF LOAD-BEARING SURFACE UNDER TARGET.]** **[JUSTIFICATION: PREVENTION OF CATASTROPHIC FAILURE DURING MAINTENANCE.]**

The System paused.

The giant froze.

The hammer stopped mid-swing.

Gerald looked up at the giant.

"If you strike me," Gerald said, "you will strike the reinforced surface. The force will reflect. You will break your own arm. And you will be cited for assault on a municipal official."

The giant hesitated.

The hammer hovered.

The Auditor looked at Gerald.

"He is bluffing," she whispered.

Gerald didn't blink.

"Am I?" he said.

The giant looked at the tile, and he looked at Gerald.

He looked at the hammer.

Then, slowly, he lowered the hammer.

**[REQUEST ACCEPTED.]** **[SURFACE REINFORCED.]**

The tile glowed blue.

The giant stepped back.

"You are lucky," the giant said.

"I am compliant," Gerald said.

The giant turned. His skeletal horses snorted.

"I will return," the giant said. "When you are not in a position to cite me."

He vanished, and the library was quiet again.

Gerald stood in the center of the ruin.

The adventurers were still stuck.

The Auditor was still watching.

And his Compliance Rating was back to 85.

But the library was not safe, and the citation radius was still expanding.

And Gerald could feel it.

Something else was waking up.

Not in the library.

In the city.

Every building in Ashford was now a potential violation.

And Gerald had a pen.
