Chapter 202: Clipboard Against System

# Chapter 202

The rat did not chew with urgency. It chewed with the slow, deliberate rhythm of a creature testing the structural integrity of a load-bearing beam.

Gerald watched the fraying wood fibers separate. The sound was soft, a dry rasp that echoed in the silence of the sub-basement.

He wanted to scream.

He wanted to spawn a defensive spike or a puddle of acid. He could not. His mana pool sat at zero.

His Compliance Rating had dropped to 12 because the System had flagged his recent repairs as unauthorized cosmetic alterations than essential structural maintenance.

He reached for his clipboard. The object was solid, physical, heavy. It was the only weapon he had left that did not require magic.

The rat paused. One beady eye fixed on Gerald's core, a pulsing sphere of amber light hovering near the water leak. The other eye tracked the clipboard.

Gerald stepped forward. His movement was stiff, constrained by the dungeon's limited spatial allowance. He held the clipboard like a shield.

"Form 11-B," he said. His voice was flat, practiced. "Unlicensed Biological Entity. Failure to maintain sanitation standards within three feet of a potable water source."

The rat squeaked. It did not care about sanitation codes. It cared about the wet wood.

Gerald lowered the clipboard. He did not swing it. Swinging a clipboard at a rat was undignified and physically ineffective against a System-registered entity. Instead, he placed the board on the floor, directly in the path of the rat's next bite.

He tapped the paper. *Notice of Immediate Cease and Desist.*

The rat ignored the paper. It lunged for the beam.

Gerald kicked the clipboard. It slid across the wet concrete, hitting the rat's side. The creature yelped, scrambling back into the shadows near the ventilation duct.

The lockout timer ticked. 23 hours and 14 minutes.

Gerald's heart hammered against his ribs, a sensation that felt less like fear and more like the pre-audit jitters of his youth. He needed a lawyer.

The Consortium demanded a lawyer.

They knew his weakness. He had no mana, no spawns, and no allies. He had only paperwork.

But paperwork was a language. And languages could be misinterpreted.

He turned away from the rat. He walked toward the central corridor, his footsteps heavy. The dungeon was quiet, save for the drip of the leak and the distant hum of the Library's HVAC system above.

He needed to find a loophole in the meeting terms. *Bring your files. And bring a lawyer.*

The phrase was ambiguous. Did "bring" mean physically transport a human legal professional into his dungeon?

Or did it mean present a legal representative?

The System did not recognize human lawyers as dungeon entities.

A human lawyer in his dungeon would be an intruder. An intruder would be cited for trespassing.

If he cited the lawyer, the meeting would fail. If the meeting failed, the Consortium would appeal his probation. He would lose his license. He would cease to exist.

He had to bring a lawyer who was not a human.

He stopped at the junction where the main corridor met the storage annex. The storage annex was currently empty. It had been empty since he cited the previous owner for hoarding hazardous materials.

He needed to spawn something. Anything. A construct.

A golem. A mimic.

He looked at his mana pool. Zero.

He looked at the environment. Water. Wood. Concrete.

He looked at the System interface. It hovered in his peripheral vision, a translucent blue overlay.

*Compliance Check: Structural Integrity of Storage Annex.*

*Status: Pass.*

*Compliance Check: Fire Suppression Systems.*

*Status: Fail.*

*Penalty: Reduced HP regeneration for all Core-adjacent entities.*

Gerald stared at the text. The penalty applied to *all* Core-adjacent entities. That included the rat. That included the water leak.

It also included anything he could spawn.

He closed his eyes. He focused on the fire suppression code. Section 7.4.1. Portable extinguishers must be accessible within fifteen feet of any combustible material.

There was no combustible material in the storage annex. The rat was hiding near the ventilation duct, which was metal. The water leak was water.

He needed fuel.

He needed a violation.

He opened his eyes.

He walked to the water leak. He placed his hand on the dripping pipe. The cold metal bit into his skin.

He did not have a body, but he had a presence.

He pushed his will against the water, trying to redirect it.

The System resisted. Water flow was a natural phenomenon, not a dungeon construct. He could not control it without mana.

But he could control where it went.

He looked at the floor drain.

It was clogged with rat droppings and debris. The System had flagged this as a sanitation violation three chapters ago. The citation had been served.

The penalty had been applied.

The penalty was ongoing.

Gerald crouched. He picked up a piece of broken tile from the floor. It was sharp. He used it to scrape the clog out of the drain.

The water surged. It flowed freely into the drain. The sanitation violation cleared.

*Compliance Check: Sanitation.*

*Status: Pass.*

*Bonus: +1 Temporary Mana Regen.*

One point. Useless.

But the water was moving faster now. It was rushing toward the main corridor. Toward the storage annex.

Gerald ran. He didn't have legs, but his core moved with surprising speed, gliding over the wet concrete.

He reached the storage annex. He slammed his core against the heavy steel door. It was locked.

He didn't have the key.

He looked at the water. It was pooling outside the door. It was rising.

He needed to flood the annex.

He looked at the System message. *Bring a lawyer.*

A lawyer was a professional. A professional required a license.

Who had a license in this dungeon?

Gerald.

He was the inspector. He was the core. He was the one who knew the rules.

He could not be his own lawyer. The System forbade self-representation in high-stakes hearings. It was a conflict of interest.

But he could appoint an agent.

An agent needed to be present in the dungeon.

He looked at the rat. It had returned to the edge of the water. It was drinking.

Rats were not lawyers.

He looked at the water.

Water was not a lawyer.

He looked at the ceiling. The ventilation duct.

There was a maintenance hatch in the ceiling. It led to the Library's mechanical room.

The Library was owned by the City of Ashford.

The City of Ashford had a legal department.

If he opened the hatch, he would expose his dungeon to the Library's systems. The Library was not a dungeon. It was a public building.

The Consortium message said: *Location: The Ashford Municipal Library, Ground Floor, Reading Room 4.*

They wanted to meet in the Library.

They assumed Gerald would come to them.

Gerald intended to make them come to him.

But they said they would appeal his probation if he did not appear.

He had to appear. But he had to be there in a way that protected him.

He needed a proxy. A proxy that was part of his dungeon. A proxy that was part of the Library.

The overlap.

He remembered the first chapter. He had triggered Core Registration by filing a structural deficiency report on his own body. His body had dissolved into the dungeon footprint.

The footprint was 4,000 square feet. It was beneath the Library.

The Library's foundation was part of his dungeon.

If he claimed the Library's foundation was his dungeon, he could claim the Library's legal department was his legal department.

It was a stretch. A massive, bureaucratic stretch.

But Gerald Hutchins lived for stretches.

He moved to the center of the sub-basement. He raised his core. He focused on the connection between his dungeon and the Library above.

He did not push mana. He pushed intent.

He projected a mental image. Not of a monster. Not of a trap.

He projected a document.

*Form 99-Z: Jurisdictional Overlap Claim.*

*Claimant: Gerald Hutchins, Dungeon Core ID 7749.*

*Target: Ashford Municipal Library, Foundation Sub-Level.*

*Argument: The structural foundation of the Library is integral to the dungeon footprint of Core ID 7749. the legal entity that owns the Foundation (The City of Ashford) is a co-stakeholder in the Dungeon.*

*Request: Appoint Legal Representative from Co-Stakeholder.*

The System processed the claim. It did not care about truth. It cared about logic.

Was the foundation part of the dungeon?

Yes. The dungeon existed within the space of the foundation.

Was the City of Ashford a co-stakeholder?

Technically, the City owned the building. Gerald owned the space inside the building.

The overlap was ambiguous. Ambiguity was the enemy of the adventurer. But ambiguity was the fuel of the bureaucrat.

A message appeared.

*Claim Accepted. Processing.*

*Notification: A Legal Representative has been assigned to Core ID 7749.*

*Designated Agent: Office of the City Solicitor.*

*Status: Pending Activation.*

Gerald exhaled. A long, rattling breath.

He had a lawyer.

But the lawyer was not a person. It was an office. A conceptual entity.

The Consortium wanted a meeting in Reading Room 4.

Reading Room 4 was on the Ground Floor.

Gerald could not go to the Ground Floor. He was bound to the Sub-Basement.

He needed the meeting to happen in the Sub-Basement.

He looked at the timer. 23 hours and 10 minutes.

He looked at the ventilation duct.

If he cited the Library for a ventilation violation, the Library would have to fix it.

If the Library fixed it, they would send workers.

Workers were people.

People were vulnerable.

But the Consortium was not people. The Consortium was Cores. Cores did not send workers. Cores sent monsters.

Monsters could not enter the Library without triggering the Library's security alarms, which were linked to the City's police department.

If the Consortium sent a monster into the Library, the City would arrest them.

If the City arrested them, the meeting could not happen.

If the meeting could not happen, the Consortium's threat to appeal his probation was moot?

No. They could appeal remotely.

They would cite his self-citation. They would argue fraud.

He needed to prove the self-citation was valid.

The self-citation was for filing a report on his own body.

Was that fraud?

No. It was a unique loophole.

He needed to reinforce that loophole.

He moved to the center of the room. He looked at the water. He looked at the rat.

He looked at the System interface.

*Compliance Check: Egress Routes.*

*Status: Fail.*

*Reason: No marked exit path from Sub-Basement to Ground Floor.*

*Penalty: Cannot spawn entities that require egress (e.g., Golems, Constructs).*

Gerald stared at the penalty.

He couldn't spawn a golem. He couldn't spawn a construct.

He had tried to spawn a lawyer. The System said the lawyer was the City Solicitor.

The City Solicitor was not an entity. It was a role.

To "bring" the City Solicitor, Gerald had to make the City Solicitor present in his dungeon.

How do you make a government office present in a dungeon?

You file a claim.

He filed the claim. It was accepted.

Now he had to wait for the activation.

The rat squeaked. It had finished chewing the wood. The beam was weakening.

Gerald looked at the beam. If it broke, the ceiling would crack. The Library above would collapse.

The Library would be destroyed.

The City of Ashford would sue him.

He would be evicted.

His dungeon would cease to exist.

He had to stop the rat.

He had no mana.

He had no weapons.

He had a clipboard.

He picked up the clipboard. He walked to the beam. The rat was sleeping on a pile of sawdust.

Gerald raised the clipboard.

He did not swing.

He placed the clipboard on the floor, inches from the rat's nose.

He wrote on the back of the clipboard.

*Violation: Destruction of Municipal Property.*

*Fine: Immediate removal of entity.*

*Authority: Dungeon Core ID 7749.*

The System did not recognize the writing. It recognized the intent.

Gerald focused. He poured his frustration into the act of writing. He poured his fear into the act of signing.

The rat opened one eye.

It saw the writing.

It did not understand English. But it understood the vibe. It was being watched. It was being judged.

Rats hate being judged.

The rat scurried away. It ran toward the ventilation duct.

Gerald didn't stop it. He watched it go.

He turned back to the timer.

23 hours and 9 minutes.

A new message appeared.

*Activation Complete.*

*Legal Representative: Office of the City Solicitor.*

*Status: Online.*

*Note: The Representative is a conceptual construct. To interact, you must submit a Query.*

Gerald's core pulsed. He had a lawyer.

He needed to draft the defense.

He needed to prepare for Reading Room 4.

But he was stuck in the sub-basement.

He looked at the ventilation duct. The rat had gone into it.

The duct connected to the Ground Floor.

If he sent a message through the duct...

No. The duct was for air.

He looked at the water drain.

The drain led to the sewer.

The sewer connected to the Library's basement?

No. The Library's basement was his dungeon.

The sewer was outside.

He looked at the ceiling.

He looked at the floor.

He looked at the door to the storage annex.

He had flooded the area outside the door earlier. The water had stopped rising because he cleared the drain.

But the water was still there.

He needed to get the water into the Library.

He looked at the System.

*Compliance Check: Water Damage.*

*Status: Minor.*

*Recommendation: Dry floor within 24 hours to prevent mold.*

Mold.

Mold was a spawn.

Gerald hadn't spawned mold. The mold had appeared naturally.

Natural spawns were free.

Mold spores were small. They could float.

They could go up the ventilation duct.

They could go into the Library.

If mold entered the Library, the Library would be contaminated.

The Library would be closed.

The Consortium meeting in Reading Room 4 would be cancelled.

The Consortium would have to meet elsewhere.

Or, they would have to come to him to inspect the contamination.

If they came to him, they would be in his dungeon.

In his dungeon, he had jurisdiction.

In his dungeon, he could cite them.

He looked at the water. He looked at the mold spores that were beginning to bloom on the wet wood.

He smiled. It was a thin, grim smile.

He wrote a new citation.

*Violation: Unauthorized Biological Contamination.*

*Target: Ashford Municipal Library, Reading Room 4.*

*Authority: Gerald Hutchins, Inspector.*

He didn't have mana to spawn the mold. But the mold was already there.

He just had to encourage it.

He picked up a piece of wet sawdust. He placed it near the ventilation duct.

He waited.

The timer ticked.

The mold grew.

And then, a knock came at the heavy steel door of the sub-basement.

Not from the Consortium.

From the Library staff.

The door handle turned.

The lock held.

But the voice came through the metal.

"Inspector Hutchins? We have a leak."

Gerald looked at his core.

He looked at the mold.

He looked at the timer.

23 hours and 8 minutes.

He walked to the door. He placed his hand on the cold steel.

He didn't open it.

He opened his mouth.

"Go away," he said. "I am conducting a private audit."

The voice on the other side hesitated.

"We have orders to check the structural integrity of the floor."

"The floor is fine," Gerald said. "It is the air quality that is questionable."

A pause.

"We have a report from the HVAC system. There is a spike in particulate matter."

"Then fix your filters," Gerald said. "And do not disturb my workspace."

The voice sighed. The footsteps receded.

Gerald slumped. His core dimmed.

He had bought time.

But the Consortium was still coming.

And the mold was still growing.

He looked at the spores floating in the air, drifting toward the vent.

He wondered if the Consortium had lungs.

If they didn't, the mold wouldn't hurt them.

If they did, they would cough.

If they coughed, they would need to stop.

If they stopped, they would have to talk.

If they talked, he could cite them for noise pollution.

Gerald pulled out a fresh sheet of paper.

He drafted a Noise Ordinance.

*Violation: Uncontrolled Vocalization in a Quiet Zone.*

*Penalty: Temporary Deafness.*

*Applicability: All Dungeon Cores within 100 meters.*

He looked at the timer.

23 hours and 7 minutes.

He looked at the vent. The mold was gone. It had drifted up.

Into the Library.

Into Reading Room 4.

He waited.

Five minutes passed.

A new message popped up. Not from the Consortium.

From the Library's Maintenance Department.

*Alert: Respiratory Hazard Detected in Reading Room 4.*

*Action: Building Evacuation.*

Gerald sat down.

He picked up his clipboard.

He waited for the Consortium to arrive.
