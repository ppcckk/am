Chapter 228: Right of Transfer

# Chapter 228

The void did not smell like nothing. It smelled of ozone and burnt hair, a sharp, metallic tang that coated the back of the throat.

Mira stood on the edge of the white expanse, her clipboard pressed so hard against her chest that the plastic creaked. She was a mid-tier auditor, Class 4, and she had seen the Inspector General manifest only twice in her career. Both times, a Core had been erased.

The man in the grey suit loomed over the figure he had just struck.

Gerald Hutchins lay on his side. The sledgehammer had not crushed him. It had bounced off a surface that was no longer skin, but a shimmering, geometric lattice of blue light. The System's firewall.

Mira's pen hovered over her form. Her hand shook. Not from fear, but from the sheer bureaucratic impossibility of the situation.

"Existential Fraud," she whispered. The words felt heavy in the sterile air.

The Inspector General did not move. The faceless head tilted, a smooth, grey oval scanning the prone Core. The hammer remained raised, suspended in a physics-defying halt.

Gerald groaned. The sound was wet and small.

He pushed himself up on one elbow. His suit was torn, the municipal badge dangling from a frayed tie.

He didn't look at the hammer.

He looked at his hands. He flexed his fingers. Then he looked up at the faceless man.

"Section 8, Paragraph 12," Gerald said. His voice was ragged, stripped of its usual dry composure. "Provisional Authority."

The Inspector General's head tilted further.

"Speak," the entity said. The voice was not sound; it was pressure in the inner ear.

Gerald coughed. A speck of grey dust fell from his lip.

"I am not a Core," Gerald said. "I am an agent."

Mira blinked.

That was a lie. A big, fat, impossible lie.

The System registered every Core by its unique signature.

There was no such thing as a Core-agent hybrid in the standard taxonomy.

But the Inspector General paused. The hammer lowered an inch.

"Clarify," the entity commanded.

Gerald scrambled to his knees. He pulled a small, folded piece of paper from his pocket.

It was a union grievance form.

Form 99-C.

The one for 'Improper Termination of Contractual Obligations.'

"I filed for suspension of my Core status," Gerald said. "Ten minutes ago. Before the vine-core citation. I claimed the System had violated my labor rights by forcing non-consensual administrative labor."

Mira stared at him. He hadn't done that. He had run.

He had corrupted the cache. But she couldn't see the timestamp from here.

She could only see the outcome.

The Inspector General reached out with a free hand. The fingers were long, grey, and jointless. They plucked the paper from Gerald's hand.

The entity unfolded it.

Mira watched the paper. It was blank. White. Empty.

But the Inspector General did not toss it aside. It held it up to the light.

"Incomplete documentation," the entity said.

Gerald didn't flinch. He straightened his tie.

"It's a preliminary filing. The full package is in the local cache. The one you just audited for negligence."

A beat of silence stretched thin.

The Inspector General turned to Mira.

"Auditor Mira," the entity said. "Did you verify the cache integrity prior to the physical manifestation?"

Mira's breath hitched. Her pen stopped shaking.

"I..." She hesitated. "I relied on the automated scan."

"Automated scans do not account for manual overrides," the Inspector General said. "You failed to verify the physical presence of the filing. This is a procedural error."

Mira felt the blood drain from her face. A procedural error. In this void, that was a death sentence for her job, if not her existence.

"I will rectify it," she said quickly.

"You will note the discrepancy," the entity corrected. It looked back at Gerald. "The filing is invalid due to lack of certification.

However, the *intent* to appeal was logged.

Therefore, the Citation for Existential Fraud is suspended pending a secondary review."

The hammer vanished.

The white void cracked. Shards of light fell like snow.

Gerald slumped.

"Get up," the entity said. "The Hearing is not over. You have invoked the right to appeal. Now you must defend the structural integrity of your defense."

Gerald stood. His legs wobbled. He caught himself on the invisible floor.

"Understood," he said.

The void dissolved.

***

Gerald woke up on the cold concrete of the sub-basement.

The library's basement smelled of old paper and mildew, a comforting, mundane scent compared to the ozone of the void. His body felt hollow. The connection to the dungeon network was frayed, static hissing in his ears.

He sat up. The vine-core's tendrils were still wrapped around the support beams, pulsing with a slow, green rhythm. They had retreated during the Hearing, sensing the shift in authority.

Mira stood ten feet away. She looked pale. Her uniform was singed at the edges.

"You're alive," she said. It wasn't a question.

"Technically," Gerald said. He rubbed his temples. The headache was immediate and sharp. "I'd prefer 'administratively suspended.'"

Mira stepped forward. Her boots crunched on broken glass.

"You almost got yourself erased, Hutchins."

"I filed a form."

"You lied.

The Inspector General knows you lied. He just couldn't prove it within the timeframe of a standard audit." Mira's voice was tight. "He's not going to let you slide again.

The vine-core is still cited. The violation stands.

You have twenty-four hours to prove compliance, or the Citation becomes permanent.

And when a Citation is permanent, the System stops protecting you from external threats."

Gerald looked at the vines. They were thick as pythons, wrapped around the HVAC ducts.

"They're blocking the return air vents," Gerald said.

"Yes."

"And they're creating a localized humidity spike of eighty percent."

"Yes."

Mira tapped her clipboard. "The vine-core claims the humidity is necessary for its biological growth. It argues that the ventilation system is inadequate to handle the load, and therefore the blockage is a *systemic* failure, not a structural violation."

Gerald stood up. He dusted off his trousers. The action was deliberate, slow. He was buying time.

"The ventilation system is rated for forty percent humidity in a standard library environment," Gerald said. "Libraries are not jungles. The System's default code for a 'Civilian Infrastructure' dungeon does not account for aggressive botanical expansion."

"The vine-core is arguing that its evolution is natural," Mira said. "It's citing the 'Organic Growth Clause.'"

Gerald walked over to the nearest vine. He didn't touch it. He pointed at a small, yellowed leaf.

"Show me the growth chart," he said.

Mira frowned. "It's internal data. I can't just pull it up."

"You can," Gerald said. "You're an auditor. You have access to the Environmental Logs. Look at the CO2 output."

Mira hesitated, then waved her hand. A holographic chart flickered into existence above the vine.

Green lines spiked upward.

"See that?" Gerald pointed. "That spike correlates with the time the rat was in the room. It wasn't growing. It was *feeding*."

Mira's eyes narrowed. "Feeding on what?"

"Spores," Gerald said. "My spawn list.

The three mold spores. They didn't die of old age. They were consumed.

The vine-core isn't just growing; it's predating on my dungeon's native fauna to accelerate its own structural density."

Mira looked at the chart again. The correlation was perfect.

"Predation is a Class 3 violation," she murmured. "But only if the victim is registered."

"The spores were registered," Gerald said.

"I filed their birth certificates. Form 11-B. Every single one.

They are legal residents of this dungeon."

Mira looked up. Her expression shifted from skepticism to calculation.

"If the spores were eaten, the vine-core didn't just violate ventilation code. It violated Resource Management Code, Section 4."

"Which requires a hearing," Gerald said.

"No," Mira said. "Section 4 is summary judgment. If I log the predation, the Citation upgrades to Critical. The vine-core loses its spawn points for forty-eight hours."

Gerald felt a tightness in his chest. Losing spawn points wasn't just a penalty; it was a vulnerability. But for the vine-core, it meant starvation.

"Do it," Gerald said.

Mira raised her pen. "I need to verify the spore death. I need to see the remains."

Gerald pointed to the floor. A small pile of grey dust lay near the base of the vine.

"That's all that's left," he said.

Mira walked over. She knelt. She touched the dust with a gloved finger. She held it up to the light.

"It's not dust," she said. "It's silica."

Gerald froze.

Silica?

Mold spores were organic. They decayed into organic matter. They did not turn into sand.

"Silica is the byproduct of fire suppression foam," Mira said slowly. "Or... or glass."

Gerald's mind raced. He hadn't installed fire suppression. He had installed handrails.

"Unless," Mira said, looking at him, "the spores were exposed to extreme heat before they died."

"The vine-core doesn't generate heat," Gerald said.

"It does," Mira said. "Not fire.

Friction.

The vines are rubbing against the metal ducts. The friction generates heat. Enough to vitrify the spores."

Gerald stared at the dust.

"Vitrification is a structural change," he said.

"It's a waste product," Mira said. "But it's also evidence of illegal modification of the environment."

Mira stood up. She began writing. Fast.

"I'm logging the vitrification," she said. "That's a new violation. Non-standard waste disposal. The vine-core is creating hazardous waste without a permit."

Gerald watched her write. He felt a flicker of hope, but it was cold and brittle.

Mira finished. She tore the page off the clipboard.

"Holding this citation," she said. "You served it? Did you serve it physically?"

Gerald looked at the vine. The vines were retracting slightly, sensing the change in the air pressure.

"I'm serving it now," Gerald said.

He walked to the vine. He placed his hand on the thickest stalk.

He focused on the System interface in his mind. He visualized the Citation. A glowing red stamp.

*SERVED.*

The vine shuddered. A low groan echoed through the basement. The green glow of the vines dimmed, turning a sickly yellow.

Mira stepped back. "It's working. The debuff is applying."

Gerald pulled his hand away. His palm was blistered. The heat from the friction had burned him.

He looked at his hand. The skin was red, raw.

"It's not enough," Gerald said.

Mira looked at him. "What?"

"The Citation is applied. But the vine-core is still standing. It's not collapsing. It's adapting."

Mira checked her clipboard. "The debuff should reduce its regeneration by thirty percent."

"It's not reducing," Gerald said. "It's redistributing. Look at the ceiling."

Mira looked up.

The vines were not retracting. They were climbing higher. They were wrapping around the support beams, not to block the vents, but to reinforce the structure.

"It's using the penalty to strengthen its anchor points," Gerald said. "It's turning the Citation into a load-bearing element."

Mira's face went pale. "That's... that's against the rules."

"It's not," Gerald said. "It's a loophole. If you cite a Core for structural weakness, and it responds by reinforcing that structure, the System accepts the reinforcement as compliance. The violation is cured."

Gerald felt the familiar nausea rise in his throat. The inability to improvise. The paralysis of a rule that could be twisted.

"How do you stop that?" Mira asked.

Gerald looked at the blisters on his hand. He looked at the vine-core, which was now humming with a deeper, more dangerous resonance.

"You don't," Gerald said. "You change the citation."

"To what?"

Gerald looked at the dust on the floor. Silica. Glass.

He looked at the water leak from Chapter 1. It was still there, a steady drip into a bucket.

"Wet work," Gerald said.

Mira blinked. "Wet work? That's a construction category, not a violation type."

"No," Gerald said. "It's a hazard. If the vines are reinforcing the structure with glass-like vitrification, they are creating sharp edges. Sharp edges in a public access area are a Liability Violation, Class 5."

Mira looked at the vines. They were smooth. Smooth as glass.

"If I cite for Liability," Mira said, "the Core must pad all surfaces. It can't do that instantly."

"No," Gerald said. "It can't. It has to stop moving to pad them. If it stops moving, the structural reinforcement fails."

Gerald stepped forward. He raised his badge.

"Serve the new Citation," he said. "Liability. Sharp edges. Immediate remediation required."

Mira hesitated. "If you cite for liability, the System will demand padding. The vines will have to grow soft tissue over the hard surfaces. That takes energy."

"And energy they don't have," Gerald said.

Mira nodded. She raised her pen.

"Logging," she said.

She wrote. She tore the page.

Gerald walked to the vine. He placed his hand on it again.

*SERVED.*

The vine froze.

Then, slowly, painfully, the hard, glassy surface rippled. A soft, white mold spread over the green stalk. The vine was trying to grow padding.

The structural integrity groaned. The support beam creaked.

"It's working," Mira said. "It's weakening."

Gerald watched. His breath held.

The vine sagged. The yellow glow intensified, then faded.

Then, a sound.

Not a groan. A crack.

The main support beam, which had been reinforced by the vines, snapped.

A chunk of concrete fell from the ceiling, landing two feet from Gerald's feet.

The vine-core shrieked. A high, digital wail that shook the floor.

Mira stumbled back. "The structure! It's collapsing!"

Gerald looked up. The ceiling was cracking. Dust rained down.

The vine-core was dying. But it was taking the building with it.

"You have to stop it!" Mira shouted. "If the library collapses, the dungeon footprint is lost!"

Gerald looked at the falling debris. He looked at the vine.

He couldn't stop the collapse. He couldn't heal the beam.

But he could cite the collapse.

He pulled a form from his pocket. A blank one.

"Form 11-A," he said. "Emergency Evacuation Protocol."

He stamped it in his mind.

*SERVED.*

The System responded.

The doors at the far end of the basement blew open. Not from force. From the System's emergency override.

Adventurers. Three of them. They stood in the doorway, swords drawn, confused.

Gerald looked at them.

"Run," he said.

One of the adventurers, a young man with a bright green tunic, looked at the collapsing ceiling.

"What the heck?" he yelled.

"Code 9," Gerald said. "Evacuate."

The adventurer didn't move. He pointed at Gerald.

"Hey! Are you the Core?"

Gerald looked at the vine-core, which was now thrashing wildly, trying to hold the ceiling up with its dying body.

"I am the inspector," Gerald said.

The ceiling gave way.

A massive slab of concrete plummeted toward the adventurer.

Gerald didn't think. He didn't calculate. He didn't file a form.

He threw himself forward.

He didn't push the adventurer. He pushed the *citation*.

He slammed his hand against the falling concrete.

*VOID.*

The citation vanished. The slab stopped.

It hung in the air, ten feet above the adventurer's head.

The System froze.

Silence.

Gerald hung there, his hand pressed against the cold stone, his body suspended by the glitch in the physics engine.

Mira stared at him. Her mouth was open.

Gerald looked at the slab. He looked at the adventurer.

He looked at the vine-core, which was now still, confused by the sudden halt in gravity.

Gerald realized he hadn't just cited the collapse. He had cited the *fall*.

He had paused the event.

He had three seconds of control over local gravity.

And he had no idea what to do with them.

The adventurer stared up at him, eyes wide.

"What is this?" the adventurer whispered.

Gerald looked at the vine-core. It was waking up. It saw the opportunity. The vines curled toward the frozen slab, not to catch it, but to *use* it as a bridge.

Gerald had to choose. Let the slab fall and kill the adventurer, or let the vine-core use the pause to escape into the upper floors.

He looked at Mira. She was shaking her head.

"Don't," she mouthed.

Gerald looked at his badge. Badge #4402. Suspended.

He smiled.

He pulled his hand away.

"Next," he said.

The slab fell again.

But this time, Gerald wasn't there.

He had moved.

He was standing behind the vine-core.

And he was holding a new form.

*Form 12-C: Unauthorized Teleportation.*

He slammed it onto the vine's back.

The vine-core vanished.

The slab smashed into the floor.

Dust filled the room.

Gerald stood in the dust, alone.

Mira walked out of the cloud. She looked at the spot where the vine-core had been.

"Where did it go?" she asked.

Gerald looked at the form in his hand. It was warm.

"It went where the code says it must," Gerald said. "To the holding cell."

Mira looked at him. "There is no holding cell for Cores. There is only deletion."

Gerald looked at the ceiling. The hole led up. To the library. To the main floor.

"To the Inspector General's office," Gerald said.

Mira went still.

"He can't," she said.

"He just did," Gerald said. "He appealed his own deletion. He invoked the Right of Transfer."

Mira looked at the ceiling.

"He's coming for you, Hutchins."

Gerald adjusted his tie.

"Good," he said. "I have a lot of paperwork to file."
