Chapter 394: Balance Due

# Chapter 394

The whiteout did not fade. It calcified.

Gerald blinked. The pen was still in his hand.

The Architect was gone.

Not destroyed. Erased. In its place hovered a translucent blue rectangle, the size of a standard A4 sheet, floating in the center of the rubble-choked corridor.

It was a Receipt.

*Transaction Complete.* *Entity: The Architect (Class: Corrupted Core).* *Status: Terminated for Obstruction of Justice.* *Fee Assessed: 4,000 Mana.* *Balance Due: Immediate.*

Gerald stared at the number.

Four thousand. He didn't have four thousand.

He barely had forty.

The penalty for non-payment of a System fine was, according to Section 4.2 of the Penal Code he had memorized during his first week as a Core, immediate asset forfeiture. His dungeon. His library.

His life.

Martha kicked a shard of concrete. It skittered across the broken tiles.

"You killed it."

"I fined it," Gerald corrected.

He holstered the pen, and the click was absent now. The silence in the sub-basement was absolute, heavier than the stone above them.

"There is a procedural difference."

"The difference is that we're bankrupt," Martha said. She wiped soot from her forehead. Her hammer hung limp in her grip. "And that thing just paid for itself with a fee we can't cover."

A new window popped up. Not blue. Red. Bordering on the color of emergency stop buttons in municipal elevators.

**NOTICE OF LIEN.** *Debt Outstanding: 4,000 Mana.* *Grace Period: 0 seconds.* *Action: Seizing Assets.*

The floor beneath Gerald's consciousness dissolved. Not physically.

Conceptually.

He felt the library's foundation unspooling from his awareness, the connection to the electrical grid severing, the ventilation ducts becoming inaccessible. The dungeon was being repossessed.

Gerald's heart rate spiked, a physiological response he tried to suppress. Panic was inefficient. Panic was a code violation.

"Stop," he said.

The red window pulsed. *Request Denied.*

"Stop the seizure," Gerald repeated, louder.

He pulled the Form 11-B from his inventory. It was blank.

It had always been blank, and he had never successfully filed it because the System claimed he was the entity, not the regulator. But the System had just accepted a Citation for Obstruction of Justice. That implied jurisdiction.

If he had jurisdiction, he had standing.

He looked at the dissolving edges of his domain. The library's bookshelves were fading into static.

"Martha, grab the ledger."

"The what?"

"The Ledger. The one I keep for every citation I issue. The black notebook."

Martha reached into her pack, confused, and pulled out the small, spiral-bound notebook Gerald used to record inspection notes. It wasn't in her inventory. She had just grabbed it from where he'd left it on a crate.

"Hand it to the window," Gerald commanded.

"That's paper."

"It's a record. Submit it."

Martha thrust the notebook toward the red light. The paper sizzled. Ink lifted off the page, swirling into the red glyph. The System scanned it.

**ANALYZING EVIDENCE.** *Document Type: Primary Ledger.* *Entries Detected: 142.* *Relevant Entry: Citation No. 001-49-2 (Trespassing, Egress Blockage).* *Cross-Reference: Architect's Core ID.*

The red window flickered. The dissolving floor paused.

**ERROR: INSUFFICIENT PAYMENT.** **BUT: CREDIT FOR PREVIOUS VIOLATIONS DETECTED.** *Architect owed 12,000 Mana in unpaid fines for structural negligence.* *Offsetting against current debt.* *Net Balance: Credit of 8,000 Mana.*

Gerald exhaled. His shoulders dropped an inch.

"It worked. The debt is cleared."

"Since when does the System allow netting?" Martha asked, looking at the fading blue receipt.

"Since I proved that the Architect was a habitual offender.

The System hates inefficiency.

It prefers to consolidate debts rather than chase individual cores across the dungeon layer. I filed the paperwork.

The paperwork is the weapon.

"

A chime sounded. Not the harsh buzz of a violation. A soft, polite ping. The sound of a service counter opening.

A new window appeared. White. Clean. Professional.

**NOTICE OF AUDIT.** *Inspector General: Unit 77-B.* *Reason: Anomalous Asset Acquisition.* *Subject: Core 11-B (Gerald Hutchins).* *Appointment: Immediate.*

Gerald felt cold. Not the cold of the sub-basement. The cold of being watched by a superior who had read his entire file.

"An audit," Martha said. "We just got audited?"

"Every Core gets audited," Gerald said. He straightened his tie, a habit that persisted even though he no longer had a tie. "The question is whether we are up to code."

***

The Inspector General did not arrive as a person. It arrived as a presence. The air in the dungeon thickened. The dust motes stopped moving.

A figure materialized from the static. It was tall, slender, composed entirely of geometric shapes.

A prism for a head.

Cubes for joints. It wore a suit made of gray mist.

**"INSPECTOR GENERAL 77-B REPORTING,"** the entity said. Its voice sounded like the hum of a fluorescent light tube. **"PLEASE PRESENT YOUR COMPLIANCE FILE."**

Gerald stood tall, and he had been doing this for thirty-one years. He had inspected schools, sewage plants, and high-rise residential blocks.

He had found violations in places men died.

He knew how to stand when a superior was judging his work.

"The file is in the cloud," Gerald said. He pointed to the air. "It is organized by date, severity, and remedial action taken."

The Prism tilted its head. **

"THE CLOUD IS CORRUPTED. THE ARCHITECT'S DEATH CAUSED DATA BLEED. YOUR FILES ARE MIXED WITH THE DEAD."**

Gerald felt a spike of anxiety. Data bleed.

That was a new loophole.

A glitch in the matrix of their reality.

If his citations were mixed with the Architect's corrupted data, they could be considered invalid. Or worse, tainted.

"Then I will reconstruct them," Gerald said.

**"YOU HAVE NO RESOURCES TO RECONSTRUCT A 142-ENTRY LEDGER."**

"I have a pen," Gerald said.

The Prism laughed. It was a sound like glass breaking. **

"A PEN IS NOT A PROCESSOR."**

"A pen is a tool of verification," Gerald said. He pulled the pen from his pocket. He opened the notebook.

The pages were blank again, the ink having been consumed by the audit request. But Gerald had a memory.

A perfect, photographic memory for codes, sections, and clause numbers, and he wrote.

*Entry 1: Unauthorized spawning of biological entity (Goblin). Violation: Section 4.1.1. Citation Issued.* *Entry 2: Lack of handrails in Sector 4.

Violation: Section 9.2.

Citation Issued.* *Entry 3: Fire exit blocked by adventurer loot. Violation: Section 12.4.

Citation Issued.*

He wrote quickly. His hand cramped, but he did not stop. The System watched.

The Prism watched. Martha watched, holding her breath.

As he wrote, the System red-index the entries. Green checkmarks appeared next to each line.

**"VERIFICATION IN PROGRESS,"** the Prism droned. **"THIS IS UNCONVENTIONAL. MOST CORES USE AUTOMATED LOGGING."**

"Automated logging misses context," Gerald said, not looking up. "A blocked door is a violation. A blocked door during a fire is a felony. The System must distinguish between the two to assign the correct penalty weight."

**"CORRECT. BUT YOUR METHOD IS SLOW. YOU ARE WASTING PROCESSING POWER."**

"I am being thorough," Gerald said.

He reached Entry 45.

The Architect's name appeared. He did not write the death.

He wrote the violations that led to the death.

*Entry 45: Electrical hazard. Wire exposed.

Citation Issued.* *Entry 46: Water leak.

Structural damage. Citation Issued.* *Entry 47: Obstruction of Justice. Citation Issued.*

The Prism's prism-head rotated. **

"YOU ARE CITING THE DEAD FOR THEIR OWN VIOLATIONS. THIS IS... RETROACTIVE."**

"It is post-mortem compliance," Gerald said.

He underlined the last entry.

"If the entity is dead, it cannot appeal.

The debt is final.

But the debt must be proven. I am proving it.

"

**"THE SYSTEM ACCEPTS YOUR RECONSTRUCTION."**

The green checkmarks merged into a single, bright green line.

**"COMPLIANCE RATING RESTORED: 98/100."**

The dungeon stabilized. The library's bookshelves snapped back into existence. The lights flickered on, steady and warm. The feeling of repossession vanished.

Gerald lowered his pen. His hand was shaking. He hid it in his pocket.

**"AUDIT COMPLETE,"** the Prism said. **"YOUR RECORD IS CLEAN. BUT A NEW FLAG HAS BEEN RAISED."**

Gerald looked up.

"What flag?"

**"UNUSUAL PROFICIENCY IN COMBATIVE CITATION. THE SYSTEM IS CONFUSED. YOU USED VIOLATIONS AS WEAPONS. THIS IS NOT STANDARD OPERATING PROCEDURE."**

"It is standard procedure," Gerald said. "If a building is unsafe, you shut it down. If a dungeon is unsafe, you shut it down. The fact that it fights back is incidental."

**"INCIDENTAL,"** the Prism repeated. It seemed to be tasting the word. **"YOU HAVE ATTRACTED THE ATTENTION OF THE HIGHER UP. THE BUREAUCRACY OF DEATH DOES NOT LIKE AMATEURS."**

"I am not an amateur," Gerald said. "I am a Senior Inspector."

**"THEN YOU WILL BE PROMOTED."**

The Prism dissolved. The air returned to normal.

Martha slumped against a pillar.

"Promoted? What does that mean?"

Gerald looked at the new window that had appeared in the corner of his vision. It was not red. It was not blue. It was Gold.

**PROMOTION OFFER: INTER-DISTRICT INSPECTOR.** *Range: 500 meters.* *Authority: Level 4.* *Requirement: Pass Probationary Inspection by Dungeon Lord Kaelen the Flayer.* *Deadline: 24 Hours.*

"Kaelen," Gerald said. The name hit him like a physical blow.

Everyone in Ashford knew Kaelen, and he was the Core of the Sewer District.

He ran a dungeon that was a labyrinth of sewage, gas pockets, and mutant rats. His dungeon was a disaster zone.

It had a Compliance Rating of 0. It had been cited three times. It had never paid the fines.

"He is a slumlord," Gerald said.

"He is a nightmare," Martha said. "His monsters are immune to fire because he installed 'heat-resistant' walls that are actually just flammable garbage.

He kills adventurers by trapping them in rooms that fill with gas.

He's not a dungeon. He's a murder pit.

"

"He has a permit?" Gerald asked.

"He has a license to operate. It's expired."

Gerald pulled out a fresh sheet of paper. This was a standard Citation Form, Form 12-C: Notice of Hazardous Conditions.

"I need to inspect him," Gerald said.

"You can't inspect him," Martha said. "He's five miles away. You're a Core. You can't leave the sub-basement."

"I can issue a Citation from range if I have physical presence to serve it," Gerald said. "That's what I learned from the Architect. I need to be there. I need to stand on his property and stamp the paper."

"That's suicide," Martha said.

"It's an inspection," Gerald corrected. He signed the form. The signature was his own name, in his own handwriting, which the System recognized as legally binding.

**CITATION PREPARED: FORM 12-C.** *Target: Dungeon Lord Kaelen the Flayer.* *Violations: 14 Counts of Gross Negligence.* *Service Method: Physical Presence Required.*

Gerald looked at Martha.

"We need to move. Fast."

"To the sewers?"

"To the access point.

The library has a connection to the city's drainage system. Old code.

Pre-apocalypse.

It's not in the System's map because it's not supposed to exist.

"

Martha stared at him.

"You want to crawl through a sewer pipe to cite a murderer?"

"I want to follow the plumbing," Gerald said. "Plumbing is honest.

It goes where it's told.

Kaelen tries to hide in the chaos.

But chaos has flow rates. It has pressure limits.

It has building codes.

"

He pointed to the corner of the room, where a rusted grate led down into darkness. The air smelled of rot.

"There is a maintenance hatch there," Gerald said. "It was sealed in 1998.

I signed off on the sealing.

I remember the date. It was a Tuesday.

"

He walked to the grate. He pulled a crowbar from his inventory. He pried it open. The metal screamed.

Below, darkness waited. Wet, dripping, and filled with things that breathed.

Gerald looked at the form in his hand. He looked at the dark hole.

"Violation of egress standards," he muttered. "I'm going to fix it."

He climbed down the ladder. Martha followed, her hammer drawn.

The first thing they encountered was not a monster. It was a sign. A crude, hand-painted sign nailed to the wet stone wall.

**WARNING: NO INSPECTORS ALLOWED. KAELEN RULES HERE.**

Gerald picked up a piece of chalk. He drew a large red 'X' through the sign.

He turned to Martha.

"Check your watch," he said. "We have twenty-three hours and fifty-nine minutes to serve this paper. And if we miss the deadline, the System will revoke my license."

"What happens then?" Martha asked.

Gerald looked at the dark tunnel ahead. Something wet and heavy dragged itself out of the shadows. It had too many legs.

"Then I'm just a man," Gerald said. "And men don't win."

He stamped the air. The red stamp glowed.
