Chapter 227: One Word Appeal

# Chapter 227

The hallway narrowed.

Gerald's boots scraped against loose gravel. His lungs burned with the acidic taste of stagnant air.

Behind him, the sound of pursuit grew louder.

Heavy. Metallic. The Inspector General's enforcement drones did not walk; they slid over surfaces, ignoring gravity and friction alike.

Ahead, the darkness swallowed the beam of his headlamp.

He didn't stop. Stopping meant dying.

To his left, a wall of raw obsidian pulsed with faint, rhythmic light.

A vein-core. Active.

Gerald ignored it.

He focused on the geometry. The corridor here was a Class 4 egress route. Narrow.

Twisting. Designed for panic, not comfort.

Perfect.

His badge felt hot against his chest.

Badge #4402.

The physical anchor of his authority. The System recognized the badge.

The System recognized the intent. Gerald had triggered a Critical Structural Failure citation in the vine-core's lair. He had drained their mana.

He had broken their law.

Now the System wanted his file.

*Delete it,* the message had screamed. *Before they catch you.*

Gerald knew how to delete things.

Not with magic. With paper. Or rather, with the absence of it.

If a record did not exist in the primary ledger, it could not be audited.

The Inspector General was a bureaucratic entity. It operated on logic.

If Gerald could prove the record was malformed, redundant, or legally void, the audit would stall.

He reached a junction. Three paths.

Left: A dead end. Right: A drop-off. Center: A maintenance shaft.

Gerald chose the center.

The shaft was barely wide enough for his shoulders. He squeezed through, the stone pressing against his ribs. The air here was different.

Cooler. Smelling of ozone and old copper.

Electrical code territory.

A spark jumped from the ceiling. Then another.

*Good,* Gerald thought. *Lightning resistance.*

He had spent weeks upgrading his own dungeon's wiring standards.

Every outlet grounded. Every conduit shielded.

He had installed GFCIs in every wet zone. His Compliance Rating had climbed to 62. Not good enough to win a war, but good enough to survive a storm.

The drones found him.

A shadow detached from the ceiling. It was humanoid, made of shifting polygons and red error codes. It hovered at the mouth of the shaft.

**HALT,** the drone broadcast. The voice was a grinding synthesizer. **SUBMIT TO VERIFICATION.**

Gerald didn't answer. He crawled faster.

The drone followed. It didn't fit. It compressed itself, folding its limbs like origami, forcing its way into the tight space. Gerald heard the crunch of stone against polygonal skin.

He pulled himself up onto a metal grate. A ventilation duct.

The drone lunged. Its claw, sharp as a laser cutter, slashed at Gerald's ankle.

Gerald kicked back. His boot connected with the drone's chassis. Metal rang against metal. Pain shot up his shin.

He scrambled up the ladder built into the duct's side. The drone's claw caught his heel.

The fabric tore.

Gerald kicked again, harder this time. His heel struck the drone's optical sensor.

It shrieked. A burst of static.

Gerald pulled free. He climbed.

The drone fell. Or tried to. It hit the bottom of the shaft with a clang that echoed for ten seconds.

Gerald didn't look down. He pushed through a grate at the top of the duct.

He dropped into a larger room.

This was not a dungeon.

This was a server room.

Or what passed for one in the System's digital architecture. Rows of black pillars stretched into the darkness. Cables snaked across the floor like roots. In the center, a single console hummed.

Gerald landed on the cables. His knees buckled. He stayed down, breathing.

The room was cold. The air was dry. No humidity. No mold.

Compliance Rating: 100/100 in this specific zone.

Gerald stood. He brushed dust off his jacket. He walked to the console.

It was a simple interface. A keyboard. A screen.

He typed.

`SEARCH: GERALD HUTCHINS`

The screen flickered.

`ERROR: ENTITY NOT FOUND IN LOCAL INDEX.`

Gerald frowned. He typed again.

`SEARCH: CORE ID 742`

`ACCESS DENIED. SECURITY LEVEL: INSPECTOR GENERAL.`

A red light pulsed on the console.

Gerald felt a vibration in his pocket. He pulled out his inspection tablet. The screen was cracked, but it still worked. A notification blinked.

**NOTICE OF INTENT TO AUDIT** **SUBJECT: GERALD HUTCHINS** **VIOLATION: UNAUTHORIZED ENTRY TO SECURE ZONE** **TIME TO COMPLY: 00:04:59**

Four minutes.

Gerald looked around the room.

There were no other doors. Only the vent he had come through.

And the drone was still coming.

He could hear it climbing the ladder.

He needed to delete his file. But he couldn't access it.

Unless he changed the file's location.

Gerald walked to the nearest cable bundle. He inspected it.

Standard shielding.

Category 6 fiber optics.

Rated for high-frequency data transmission.

He looked at the console. The keyboard was locked. But the port was open.

He pulled a small, flathead screwdriver from his belt. It was his tool for prying off outlet covers. He had carried it for thirty-one years.

He wedged it into the port cover. Pried.

The plastic popped off.

Inside, the ports glowed blue.

Gerald didn't know how to hack. He wasn't a programmer. He was an inspector.

But he knew where things belonged. He knew that a file was just data stored in a location.

If the location was invalid, the file was orphaned.

He saw a label on a nearby junction box. *LOCAL CACHE: TEMPORARY STORAGE.*

He grabbed the screwdriver. He jammed it into the junction box. Shorted the connections.

Sparks flew.

The console beeped.

`WARNING: LOCAL CACHE CORRUPTED.`

Gerald moved to the next box. And the next.

He wasn't destroying the data. He was destroying the address.

If the Inspector General looked for him in the main server, they would find nothing. If they looked in the cache, they would find garbage.

He worked fast. His hands shook. Not from fear.

From adrenaline.

He hated improvising. He hated working without a plan.

But this was a plan.

A bad plan. But a plan.

He shorted the fifth box.

The room's lights flickered.

The drone entered the room.

It hovered in the air, red eyes scanning. It ignored Gerald. It went straight for the console.

**DETECTING ANOMALY,** it announced.

Gerald ducked behind a pillar. He watched.

The drone interfaced with the console. It uploaded code. Fast. Aggressive.

The screen flashed green.

`RESTORING BACKUP.`

Gerald's stomach dropped. Backups. He had forgotten about backups.

He needed to stop the restoration.

He looked at the ceiling. Above the console, a sprinkler head.

Fire suppression code required wet-pipe systems in high-risk zones. This zone was high-risk.

Gerald pulled a fire extinguisher from the wall. He checked the pressure gauge. Full.

He didn't throw it at the drone. That would be physical assault. The System would penalize him for unsanctioned combat.

Instead, he climbed the pillar.

He reached the sprinkler head.

He inspected the valve. It was sealed with wax. Standard tamper-proofing.

Gerald used his screwdriver. He pried the wax away.

The valve turned.

Water erupted.

Not just a spray. A torrent.

The pressure in the pipes was excessive.

Gerald knew it.

He had inspected the water mains for this district. They were over-pressurized.

The water hit the console.

Sparks. Smoke. Screaming electronics.

The drone recoiled. Water shorted its joints. It sparked and twitched.

But it didn't stop. It floated above the water, protected by a mana shield.

**CITATION ISSUED: DESTRUCTION OF SYSTEM PROPERTY.** **PENALTY: IMMEDIATE TERMINATION.**

The drone raised its claw.

Gerald jumped down. He ran for the vent.

The drone was faster. It caught up in two seconds. Its claw wrapped around Gerald's throat.

Gerald gagged. He couldn't breathe.

He felt the cold metal against his skin. He felt the energy draining from his body.

**TERMINATION IMMINENT.**

Gerald looked into the drone's red eyes.

He didn't beg. He didn't fight.

He spoke.

"Form 11-B," Gerald wheezed.

The drone paused.

"Unlicensed Biological Entity," Gerald continued. His voice was thin. Strained. "You are operating in a wet zone without a Class 4 Waterproof Certification."

The drone's head tilted.

"Section 8, Subsection C," Gerald said. "Electrical safety in humid environments. All equipment must be rated IP67 or higher."

The drone looked at its own body. Water dripped from its joints.

"Your chassis is unsealed," Gerald said. "You are a code violation."

The drone hesitated.

The System processed the statement.

**CHECKING SPECIFICATIONS...**

**USER: GERALD HUTCHINS. JOB: BUILDING INSPECTOR. COMPETENCE: VALIDATED.**

**TARGET: DRONE UNIT 7. SPECS: CLASS 3. NOT IP67 RATED.**

**VIOLATION DETECTED.**

**AUTOMATIC CITATION: SAFETY HAZARD.**

The drone's claw loosened.

It didn't crush Gerald. It dropped him.

The drone vibrated. A red light flashed on its chest.

**CITATION ACCEPTED. REMEDIAL ACTION REQUIRED.**

The drone floated away. It went back to the console. It repaired itself. Dry itself out.

Gerald lay on the wet floor. He coughed. Water filled his lungs.

He had bought himself time.

But not much.

The drone would return.

And the Inspector General would be there.

Gerald looked at the console. The screen was dark. Destroyed.

But the data was still there. In the cloud. In the main server.

He couldn't delete it.

He had to change it.

He crawled toward the console. He reached into the wet mess. He found a backup drive. A small, rectangular chip.

He pulled it out.

It was hot.

He put it in his pocket.

Then he heard a sound.

Not from the drone.

From the vent.

A soft *click*.

Gerald froze.

He turned his head.

Sitting on the ledge of the vent was a small, furry creature. A rat.

But not just any rat.

It wore a tiny vest. And a tiny badge.

Badge #4403.

Gerald stared.

He had no rats in his dungeon. His spawn list was mold and leaks.

The rat looked at him. It chattered.

Then, it spoke.

"Mr. Hutchins," the rat said. Its voice was small, squeaky, but clear. "The Inspector General is not coming for you."

Gerald blinked. Water ran down his face.

"Who is coming for you?"

The rat adjusted its tie.

"The union is here."

Before Gerald could ask what that meant, the wall behind the rat dissolved.

Not broke. Dissolved.

A woman stepped through. She wore a suit. Perfectly tailored.

Black. She held a clipboard.

She looked at Gerald. She looked at the drone. She looked at the water.

She sighed.

"Mr. Hutchins," she said. "You have filed three separate complaints in the last ten minutes."

Gerald tried to stand. His legs wouldn't work.

"Which complaints?" he asked.

"Improper zoning," she said. "Unauthorized entry. And failure to provide a witness for a verbal citation."

She pointed the clipboard at him.

"I am here to issue a Cease and Desist."

Gerald looked at the drone. The drone was still repairing itself.

He looked at the woman.

"I have a backup drive," Gerald said.

The woman raised an eyebrow.

"Did you?"

Gerald reached into his pocket.

His fingers closed around nothing.

The pocket was empty.

He looked at the rat.

The rat was chewing on the chip.

It swallowed.

"It's in the cloud now," the rat said. "But I'm not letting you log in."

Gerald felt a cold sweat break out on his neck.

"Why?"

"Because," the rat said, "the union doesn't like non-union inspectors. And you, Gerald, are technically freelance."

The woman clicked her pen.

"We have a motion to strip your badge, Mr. Hutchins. If we succeed, you lose access to all System functions. You become a mortal in a dungeon of gods."

Gerald looked at the hole in the wall. The dark beyond.

"If I lose," Gerald said. "I die."

"You cease to exist," the woman corrected. "It is a paperwork error. But it is fatal."

The drone turned around. It was dry now. Its claw gleamed.

**AUDIT RESUMED.**

The woman smiled. It was not a nice smile.

"Choose your representative, Mr. Hutchins. Or we will choose for you."

Gerald looked at the rat.

The rat looked back.

It pointed a claw at Gerald's chest.

"Pick the fight you can win," the rat squeaked. "Not the one you want."

Gerald looked at the woman. He looked at the drone. He looked at the exit.

He had no weapons. No magic. No support.

He had a badge. And a code.

And a secret that the System didn't know he knew.

He knew where the Inspector General's own file was stored.

It was in the same server room.

He just had to get to it.

Gerald stood up.

He straightened his tie.

"I appeal," Gerald said.

The woman paused.

"On what grounds?"

Gerald pointed at the rat.

"Him," Gerald said. "He's not a spawn. He's a stakeholder."

The rat stopped chewing.

"He has voting rights," Gerald said.

The woman frowned. She checked her clipboard. She flipped pages.

She stopped.

Her eyes widened.

"Subsection D," she whispered. "Union representation for sentient entities over 12 ounces."

The rat grinned.

"That's right," the rat said. "I'm 1.2 pounds. I'm a delegate."

The woman looked at Gerald.

"You're bluffing."

Gerald didn't bluff. He never bluffed. He measured.

"I have the weight certificate," Gerald said. "In my pocket. In the previous pocket. Before the water."

He tapped his left pocket.

It was dry.

The rat had given it to him.

The woman's face went pale.

"If he is a delegate," she said slowly, "then any action taken against you without his consent is a labor violation."

She looked at the drone.

"Stand down," she ordered.

The drone hovered, confused.

"Stand down!" she shouted.

The drone lowered its claw.

The woman turned back to Gerald.

"Then we have a Hearing," she said. "Now."

Gerald nodded.

"Good," he said. "I want to appeal the original Citation against the vine-core."

The woman sneered.

"You can't appeal a Critical Structural Failure. It's automatic."

Gerald smiled. It was a small, tight smile.

"Actually," Gerald said. "It's only automatic if the inspector is certified. And I'm not."

He held up his badge.

"Badge #4402 is suspended. I filed for suspension ten minutes ago. When I ran."

The woman's eyes darted to her clipboard.

She looked for the timestamp.

It wasn't there.

Gerald had filed it in the cache. The one he corrupted.

But he had also filed it in the union log. The rat had confirmed it.

The woman's face hardened.

"You're playing games, Hutchins."

"I'm following protocol," Gerald said.

He stepped forward.

"Start the Hearing. Or I file a complaint for negligence."

The woman looked at the rat.

The rat looked at Gerald.

The rat nodded.

The woman snapped her fingers.

The room changed.

The walls disappeared.

Gerald stood in a white void.

Opposite him stood the Inspector General.

It was not a drone.

It was a man.

He wore a grey suit. He had no face. Just a smooth, grey oval.

He held a hammer.

"Gerald Hutchins," the faceless man said. "You are cited for Existential Fraud."

Gerald looked at the hammer.

It was a sledgehammer.

"Show me the code," Gerald said.

The faceless man raised the hammer.

"There is no code for you," the man said. "You are a glitch."

The hammer came down.

Gerald didn't dodge.

He held up his badge.

And he said one word.

"Appeal."
