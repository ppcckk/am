Chapter 47

The form had a footnote.

Gerald had been reading the Core Operator License application for six hours when he found it. Small text, buried in the margin of page eleven, printed in a font so small he had to press his awareness flat against the page like a thumb against glass. The footnote read: Cross-reference Section 14.7(c) of the Municipal Operating Code for provisional status determinations.

He stared at it.

He read it again.

Section 14.7(c). He knew that section. He had cited three buildings for violating 14.7(c) in the last fiscal quarter alone. It governed provisional occupancy permits for structures operating on municipal land without full licensing. It was the mechanism by which a condemned building could be temporarily re-occupied if a licensed municipal authority filed a structural adequacy report.

Gerald was a licensed municipal authority.

His dungeon was on municipal land.

He sat with this for four minutes. Then he picked up his pen and started a new page.

He wrote: SECTION 14.7(c) APPLICATION FOR PROVISIONAL OPERATING STATUS.

He knew the form. He had filled it out forty times for condemned restaurants and half-finished parking garages. It required three things: a structural adequacy report, a statement of municipal ownership or jurisdiction, and a co-signature from a licensed municipal authority.

He could provide all three.

He was the municipal authority. The land was municipal. And the structural adequacy report was, in the most literal sense, a report on his own body. He was the structure. He could inspect himself.

He stood up. Or rather, he expanded his awareness across the forty-seven walls of his dungeon and began to look at them the way he looked at a building. Not as a core. As an inspector.

Wall fourteen. Load-bearing. He checked the material composition. Limestone, roughly two feet thick, adequate for the depth. Pass.

Wall twenty-two. Egress corridor. Width was thirty-one inches. The code minimum for commercial egress was thirty-six. Fail.

Wall thirty-one. Handrail bracket placement. The rat had gnawed the mounting screws. The bracket was hanging at a fifteen-degree angle. He fixed it by re-growing the screws from his own wall material. Pass.

He moved through the dungeon methodically. Thirty-one years of muscle memory. He checked wall thickness, joint integrity, moisture intrusion, ventilation aperture size, emergency signage visibility. He noted every failure and every pass. He wrote it all down on a page that appeared in his awareness when he needed it, the way the System always provided forms.

By hour eight, he had a complete structural adequacy report. By hour nine, he had filled out the provisional status application. By hour ten, he had co-signed it with his own name, under his own credentials, as the licensed municipal authority for the City of Ashford.

He submitted it.

The System accepted it. The processing time displayed: 72 hours.

Gerald looked at the clock. He had 37 hours left on the original 47-hour deadline. The provisional status would arrive in 72 hours. It would not arrive in time.

He had found a loophole. It was not fast enough.

He was still thinking about this when the message arrived.

NOTICE: CEASE AND DESIST REQUEST FILED AGAINST DUNGEON CORE GERALD HUTCHINS.

NOTICE: FILED BY: CORE DESIGNATION "THE HOLLOW," DUNGEON CORE, TIER 3, OPERATING IN ASHFORD MUNICIPAL ZONE 4.

NOTICE: BASIS OF REQUEST: OPERATION WITHOUT VALID CORE OPERATOR LICENSE.

NOTICE: REQUEST FOR IMMEDIATE CESSATION OF ALL CORE FUNCTIONS PENDING LICENSE DETERMINATION.

NOTICE: C&D RESPONSE WINDOW: 24 HOURS.

NOTICE: FAILURE TO RESPOND RESULTS IN AUTOMATIC CESSATION.

Gerald read it twice. Then he looked at the clock. Twenty-four hours to respond. The license would not arrive in time. The provisional status would not arrive in time. If he did not respond to the C&D, the System would shut him down regardless.

The Hollow. He knew it. A mid-tier dungeon that occupied an abandoned textile mill three blocks east. It had a Compliance Rating of 34. He had cited it twice for improper waste disposal and once for a collapsed corridor that killed two of its own spawns. It was not a sophisticated core. It was not a strong core. But it had found the one rule that Gerald could not beat.

He had no license. The C&D was technically valid.

Gerald sat in the middle of his dungeon. The rat was eating a wall crumb near the egress corridor. The handrails were in place. The ventilation was marginal. Everything was exactly as it had been an hour ago, and all of it was about to stop.

He opened the C&D response form.

The form had three sections. Section one: identity of respondent. Section two: grounds for contest. Section three: supporting documentation.

Section two required the respondent to demonstrate that the basis of the C&D was invalid or superseded by another regulatory authority. Gerald did not have a license. He could not argue the basis was invalid.

Section three required supporting documentation. The form listed acceptable documents: inspection reports, compliance certificates, variance approvals, structural adequacy assessments.

Structural adequacy assessments.

Gerald stopped.

He looked at the structural adequacy report he had already filed for the provisional status application. He looked at the acceptable documents list on the C&D response form. He looked at the System's definition of "supporting documentation."

The System recognized structural adequacy assessments as valid supporting documentation for C&D responses. A structural adequacy assessment, by definition, was a document that verified a structure met minimum operational standards. If Gerald could demonstrate that his structure met minimum operational standards, the C&D basis would be superseded.

But his Compliance Rating was 12 out of 100.

He could not demonstrate compliance.

Unless the definition of "minimum operational standards" was narrower than he assumed.

He went back to the form. He looked at the footnote on the C&D response form. There was a footnote. There was always a footnote.

The footnote read: For cores operating on municipal land, minimum operational standards shall be determined by the applicable municipal code as enforced by the licensed municipal authority of jurisdiction.

Gerald read it four times.

The System was deferring to municipal code for minimum operational standards. Not to its own Compliance Rating system. To municipal code. The code Gerald knew. The code he had enforced for thirty-one years. The code that governed buildings, not dungeons.

His dungeon was a building. It was a sub-basement beneath the Ashford Municipal Library. It had walls, a floor, a ceiling, ventilation apertures, and egress routes. Under municipal code, it was a sub-basement. Not a dungeon. A sub-basement.

And a sub-basement did not need a Compliance Rating of 100. It needed to meet the minimum standards of the International Building Code as adopted by the City of Ashford.

Gerald pulled the municipal code into his awareness. He had memorized most of it. He checked the requirements for sub-basement occupancy. Wall thickness: minimum eight inches. His walls were two feet. Pass. Egress width: minimum twenty-eight inches for residential accessory spaces. His egress corridor was thirty-one inches. Pass. Ventilation: minimum one air change per hour for enclosed spaces under 500 square feet. His dungeon was 4,000 square feet, but the ventilation apertures provided 0.8 air changes per hour. Marginal. He widened them. Two minutes of effort. Pass. Fire suppression: required for commercial occupancy only. A sub-basement beneath a public library was commercial. He did not have fire suppression. Fail.

But the code had a variance provision for structures under 5,000 square feet with no combustible storage. His dungeon had no combustible storage. The walls were stone. The floor was concrete. The handrails were metal.

He filed a variance request within the structural adequacy assessment. He cited the square footage exception. He cited the non-combustible materials provision. He co-signed it.

He attached the whole package to the C&D response.

Section one: Gerald Hutchins, Core Designation, Municipal Zone 4.

Section two: Basis of C&D superseded by municipal code compliance determination per footnote reference.

Section three: Structural adequacy assessment with variance approval, filed under municipal authority credentials.

He submitted it.

The System processed it. The response came back in eleven seconds.

NOTICE: C&D RESPONSE ACCEPTED.

NOTICE: C&D REQUEST PENDING REVIEW.

NOTICE: REVIEWER: INSPECTOR GENERAL'S OFFICE.

NOTICE: ESTIMATED REVIEW TIME: 6 HOURS.

Gerald exhaled. He did not know if the C&D would be denied. He had bought time. Six hours. The provisional status application still needed 62 more hours. The original license deadline was in 36 hours.

He was not saved. He was alive. That was different.

He started a new page. He began drafting a second structural adequacy assessment, this time targeting the specific code violations that had brought his Compliance Rating down to 12. If he could get the rating up before the Inspector General reviewed the C&D, the C&D would fail on its own merits. He had to raise the rating. He had to do it in six hours.

He started with ventilation. He had already widened the apertures. He checked the current rating. It ticked up. 12 became 14. Small. Meaningless.

He moved to egress signage. He grew glowing mineral deposits along the corridor walls at the required intervals. The System recognized them as illuminated exit markers. The rating ticked. 14 became 19.

He moved to fire suppression. The variance was approved, but the System's own fire code required suppression regardless of municipal variance. He could not satisfy both systems simultaneously. He filed a variance request with the System's fire code directly. The System accepted it but flagged it for review. The rating did not move.

He tried electrical code. His dungeon had no electrical systems. The System's electrical code did not apply. He filed a "not applicable" determination. The rating ticked up. 19 became 23.

He tried structural code. He reinforced three walls that had micro-fractures. The rating moved. 23 became 28.

It was not enough. He needed to reach at least 50 before the C&D review, or the Inspector General would side with The Hollow on the basis of inadequate compliance.

He was in the middle of re-routing a water channel to improve drainage when a new message arrived.

NOTICE: STRUCTURAL ADEQUACY ASSESSMENT REVIEWED.

NOTICE: REVIEWER: INSPECTOR GENERAL'S OFFICE.

NOTICE: DETERMINATION: ASSESSMENT VALID. CREDENTIALS VERIFIED.

NOTICE: GERALD HUTCHINS, LICENSED MUNICIPAL BUILDING INSPECTOR, CITY OF ASHFORD, CREDENTIAL NUMBER 4471-A.

NOTICE: CREDENTIAL VERIFICATION CONFIRMS DUAL-STATUS OPERATOR.

NOTICE: PER SYSTEM REGULATION 9.2.1, DUAL-STATUS OPERATORS ARE ELIGIBLE FOR APPOINTMENT TO THE INSPECTOR GENERAL'S FIELD STAFF.

NOTICE: GERALD HUTCHINS IS HEREBY APPOINTED PROVISIONAL INSPECTOR, DISTRICT 7.

NOTICE: FIRST ASSIGNMENT: INVESTIGATE COMPLAINT FILED BY CORE "THE HOLLOW" REGARDING UNLICENSED OPERATION.

NOTICE: INVESTIGATION SCOPE: ALL OPERATIONS WITHIN DUNGEON DESIGNATION "GERALD HUTCHINS."

NOTICE: INVESTIGATION MUST BE COMPLETED WITHIN 12 HOURS.

NOTICE: FAILURE TO COMPLETE INVESTIGATION RESULTS IN TERMINATION OF PROVISIONAL INSPECTOR STATUS AND AUTOMATIC C&D APPROVAL.

Gerald read the message. He read it again.

The Inspector General had appointed him to investigate himself.

He was now required to inspect his own dungeon, file a report on his own compliance, and determine whether his own operations were legal. If he found himself compliant, he would be investigating himself and clearing himself, which the System would flag as a conflict of interest. If he found himself non-compliant, he would be citing himself, which would approve the C&D against himself.

Either way, he lost.

Unless he did not investigate himself.

He looked at the appointment notice again. FIRST ASSIGNMENT: INVESTIGATE COMPLAINT FILED BY CORE "THE HOLLOW."

The complaint was filed by The Hollow. The investigation scope was his own dungeon. But the complaint itself was a document. A complaint had an author. An author could be investigated.

He opened a new page. He wrote: COMPLAINT FILED BY CORE "THE HOLLOW" CONTAINS THE FOLLOWING STATEMENT: "GERALD HUTCHINS IS OPERATING WITHOUT VALID CORE OPERATOR LICENSE."

He underlined it.

He wrote: THIS STATEMENT CONSTITUTES A CLAIM OF JURISDICTIONAL AUTHORITY OVER DUNGEON CORE OPERATIONS IN MUNICIPAL ZONE 4.

He wrote: UNDER MUNICIPAL CODE SECTION 3.1, JURISDICTIONAL AUTHORITY OVER STRUCTURAL COMPLIANCE WITHIN MUNICIPAL ZONES IS EXCLUSIVELY HELD BY THE LICENSED MUNICIPAL AUTHORITY OF JURISDICTION.

He wrote: THE HOLLOW DOES NOT HOLD MUNICIPAL CREDENTIALS.

He wrote: THEREFORE, THE HOLLOW'S CLAIM OF JURISDICTIONAL AUTHORITY CONSTITUTES UNLICENSED OPERATION OF A REGULATORY FUNCTION.

He looked at what he had written. He looked at the clock.

He opened a new form. He started filling it out.

NOTICE: NEW COMPLAINT FILED BY PROVISIONAL INSPECTOR GERALD HUTCHINS, DISTRICT 7.

NOTICE: TARGET: CORE "THE HOLLOW."

NOTICE: BASIS: UNLICENSED OPERATION OF REGULATORY FUNCTION WITHOUT MUNICIPAL CREDENTIALS.

NOTICE: REQUESTED ACTION: IMMEDIATE CESSATION OF ALL COMPLAINT-FILING FUNCTIONS PENDING CREDENTIAL REVIEW.

NOTICE: TARGET RESPONSE WINDOW: 12 HOURS.

The message went out. Gerald set down his pen.

He had not solved his problem. He had created a new one. The Hollow now had twelve hours to respond to a complaint that, if successful, would strip it of its ability to file C&Ds. But The Hollow could also appeal. And appeals triggered Hearings. And Hearings required both parties to be present.

And if Gerald had to appear at a Hearing, he would have to appear as himself. As the core. As the unlicensed operator. In front of the Inspector General.

He looked at the clock.

Thirty-five hours until his own license deadline.

Twelve hours until The Hollow's response.

Six hours until the C&D review.

He picked up his clipboard. He walked toward wall twenty-two. The egress corridor. Thirty-one inches wide. He needed it to be thirty-six. He pressed his awareness against the wall and began to widen it.

The wall resisted. It was his wall. It was part of him. Widening it felt like pulling a tooth.

He kept going.
