Chapter 51

The form had a field labeled "Target Core ID" and it auto-populated with his own number. Gerald highlighted it, deleted it, and typed it again. The System accepted it. He moved to the next field.

Violation type. He scrolled the dropdown. Electrical. Fire. Structural. Plumbing. Accessibility. Mechanical. Each one had subcategories nested like municipal code sections, and he knew them all the way down to the subsections because he had spent three decades reading them at his desk in the Ashford Municipal Building while drinking coffee that had gone cold an hour before he noticed.

He selected Structural. Subcategory: Load-bearing element failure. Specific violation: Unreinforced masonry wall, unrestrained lateral support, deficiency exceeding 15% of required shear capacity.

He had that violation. He had it right now. The east wall of his sub-basement was unreinforced. He had noted it in his own inspection notes six weeks ago and never fixed it because he had no construction queue slots available. The violation was real. He was not fabricating it.

He filled out the severity field. He had to be honest about this part. The System cross-referenced against its own compliance scan data. If he inflated the severity, the Fraud Flag would trigger automatically. If he underreported it, the Citation would not count toward his Citation Authority total.

He selected "Major." The System's own scan data agreed. The east wall was, by the System's own metrics, a Major structural deficiency.

He clicked Submit.

The Citation issued. A debuff notification appeared at the top of his interface.

**CITATION ISSUED: Self-Referential. Structural, Major. Debuff: Physical Damage Resistance reduced by 18% for 72 hours. Citation Authority progress: 43/70.**

One point. He had spent thirty days building his rating from twelve to forty-two. The self-citation gave him one point and cost him eighteen percent of his physical damage resistance for three days. He stared at the number.

One point per citation. He needed twenty-eight more.

The notification was still on his screen when a second notification appeared. This one had a red border.

**FRAUD FLAG INITIATED. Self-referential Citation detected. Hearing required. Time remaining: 4:59:58.**

Gerald read it twice. He had not known about the Fraud Flag. He had read the Citation Authority rules three times. They did not mention it.

He opened the Hearing rules.

A Fraud Hearing required the citing Core to demonstrate, before the System's adjudication protocol, that the cited violation was pre-existing and not manufactured for the purpose of Citation Authority accumulation. The citing Core had five hours to submit evidence. Failure to demonstrate pre-existence resulted in permanent revocation of Citation Authority and a Compliance Rating penalty of fifteen points.

Gerald sat with that for a moment. He had forty-two points. If he lost the Hearing, he would be at twenty-seven. He would have to rebuild from twenty-seven to seventy in twenty-nine days. That was not possible. He knew the math. He had done the math twice.

He had four hours and fifty-eight minutes to prove that his east wall violation had existed before he filed the Citation.

He opened his own inspection records. He had been logging everything since chapter one. Every measurement, every observation, every deficiency noted during his daily compliance walks. The System stored them in a Core Activity Log that was, he realized, timestamped and immutable.

He searched for the east wall entry. It was there. Six weeks old. Timestamped before the Citation by forty-two days.

That should be enough. He submitted it as evidence.

The Hearing opened.

The adjudication protocol was not a courtroom. It was a structural logic puzzle. The System presented the violation and asked the citing Core to trace the causal chain: when did the deficiency originate, what caused it, and could the Core have known about it before the Citation was filed?

Gerald traced it. The east wall was original construction. The library's sub-basement had been poured in 1987. The masonry was original. The deficiency had existed since the day the wall was built. He submitted the original construction records he had pulled from the library's maintenance office during his first inspection. The System accepted them.

Then the adjudication protocol asked a question he had not anticipated.

"Did the citing Core benefit from the deficiency prior to the Citation?"

Gerald stopped. He re-read it. Did he benefit from the unreinforced wall before he cited it?

He thought about what the unreinforced wall meant for his dungeon. It was a weakness. It meant that if an adventurer party attacked the east corridor, they could break through. It meant his dungeon was more vulnerable on that side. It was a liability. He had never repaired it because he lacked construction slots.

But was a liability a benefit?

He pulled up the System's definition of "benefit" in the context of Fraud Hearings. It was defined as "any measurable advantage in Combat, Economy, or Progression derived from the cited condition."

He checked his Combat log. The east wall had never been breached. No adventurer had ever attacked that side. No spawn had ever exploited it. There was no measurable advantage.

He submitted this as his argument. The System paused.

Then it asked a second question.

"Did the citing Core's knowledge of the deficiency alter its strategic behavior prior to the Citation?"

This was the trap. Gerald saw it immediately. He had known about the east wall for six weeks. Had that knowledge changed anything he did? Had he routed adventurers away from the east corridor? Had he placed his spawn list differently because of it?

He pulled up his spawn placement history. He had placed his mold spores, his water leak, and his confused rat. None of them were near the east wall. His adventurer flow had been directed through the central corridor. He had never used the east corridor as a combat zone.

Had he avoided the east corridor because he knew it was structurally weak?

He pulled up his own notes. He had written, six weeks ago: "East corridor unsuitable for adventurer flow. Structural concern. Recommend reinforcement before activation."

He had not activated the east corridor. He had avoided it. That was a strategic decision made on the basis of knowledge of the deficiency. The System could argue that his knowledge of the violation had altered his behavior, which meant the deficiency had provided a benefit: it had kept him from deploying resources into a weak area, which was a form of risk management, which was a form of advantage.

Gerald sat with this. He had thirty-one years of experience with municipal attorneys doing exactly this kind of interpretive work. He knew how they argued. He also knew how to counter-argue.

He pulled up the definition of "strategic behavior" in the Hearing rules. It was defined as "actions taken in response to a condition that would not have been taken in the absence of that condition."

He needed to prove that he would have avoided the east corridor regardless of the structural deficiency.

He pulled up his dungeon layout. The east corridor was the narrowest corridor in his dungeon. It was four feet wide. His central corridor was six feet wide. His adventurer flow was designed around the central corridor because it was wider, because it had better sightlines, because it was closer to his spawn points. He had chosen the central corridor for reasons that had nothing to do with the east wall's structural integrity.

He submitted this. He attached his original dungeon layout plans, timestamped before he had ever inspected the east wall. He showed that the east corridor had been excluded from adventurer flow on day one, before he knew it was structurally deficient. He had excluded it because it was too narrow for his intended combat configuration.

The System paused again. Longer this time.

Then: "Fraud Flag resolved. Citation upheld. Citation Authority progress: 43/70. No penalty applied."

Gerald exhaled. He had not been breathing. He noticed that now.

The debuff was still active. Eighteen percent reduced physical damage resistance. He had seventy-one hours left on it.

He opened the Citation form again. He needed twenty-seven more citations. At one point each. He needed to find twenty-seven more real violations in his own dungeon and cite himself for each one.

He started walking. He walked the perimeter of his dungeon, measuring. He checked the west wall. He checked the north wall. He checked the ceiling. He checked the floor. He checked the electrical conduit that ran along the south corridor. He checked the water leak, which was technically a plumbing violation if he classified it as a "continuous moisture intrusion affecting structural substrate."

He found four more violations in the first hour. Each one real. Each one already documented in his inspection logs. Each one Major or Minor severity.

He cited all four. The Fraud Flag triggered on the second one. He won that Hearing in three minutes because the evidence was identical in structure to the first one. The System seemed to accept the pattern after the third Hearing. The fourth citation went through without a Fraud Flag at all.

Citation Authority progress: 47/70.

He had twenty-three citations left to go. He had twenty-nine days. He had four hours of walking and measuring left before the debuff made him vulnerable to any external threat.

He was in the middle of measuring a ceiling joist spacing violation when his dungeon boundary pinged.

Something was outside.

He pulled up his perimeter awareness. The System showed him a single point of contact at the northeast corner of his dungeon footprint. It was pressing against the boundary. Not attacking. Pressing. Like something testing the wall.

He expanded his awareness outward. The contact point was adjacent to a dungeon he had not encountered before. Its Core ID was not on his Citation list. Its name was not registered in his local awareness range.

He had never seen it before.

He checked the Compliance Rating of the adjacent dungeon. The System displayed it.

Rating: 88/100.

Gerald felt something cold move through him. Eighty-eight. He had never seen a rating that high. His highest Citation had been against a dungeon rated sixty-one. Eighty-eight was something else.

The contact point pressed again. The wall shuddered. Gerald felt it through his Core awareness like a vibration in a floor joist.

A message appeared in his inbox. Not from the System. From the adjacent Core.

"Hello, neighbor. I see you have a structural deficiency on your east wall. I also see you have a reduced physical damage resistance debuff active. I would like to discuss a partnership."

Gerald stared at the message. The adjacent Core could see his debuff. That meant it could see his Compliance Rating. That meant it had access to information that should have been hidden.

He checked his own visibility settings. He had them set to Private. He had checked them twice. They were still set to Private.

He pulled up the System's visibility rules. There was an exception he had not known about. A Core with a Compliance Rating above 80 could view the Compliance Ratings of all adjacent Cores regardless of privacy settings. It was called "Regulatory Supremacy" and it was not in the beginner documentation.

The adjacent Core could see everything. His rating. His debuffs. His Citation Authority progress. All of it.

Another message appeared.

"I will make you a simple offer. I will help you reach seventy points. I have violations to report. Real ones. I will guide you to them. In exchange, you cite me for three of them. I want the Citation Authority points. I am building toward something and I need the points."

Gerald read it. He read it again. The adjacent Core was offering to help him find violations in his own dungeon. In exchange, Gerald would cite the adjacent Core for violations that the adjacent Core itself would identify.

It was a mutual citation arrangement. Both Cores would cite each other. Both would gain Citation Authority points. Both would take debuffs.

It was, Gerald realized, exactly what a pair of building inspectors would do if they were trying to game the system together. Cite each other for minor violations. Take the debuffs. Bank the points.

It was also, he realized, exactly the kind of arrangement that would trigger a Fraud Flag if the System detected coordination.

He typed a response.

"What is your name?"

The reply came immediately.

"You can call me Margaret."

Gerald set the message aside. He looked at his Citation Authority progress. 47/70. Twenty-three citations left. Twenty-nine days. An adjacent Core rated 88 that could see everything he was doing and was offering to help him find violations in his own dungeon.

He looked at the east wall. The unreinforced masonry. The violation he had cited himself for. The debuff that was still eating eighteen percent of his physical damage resistance.

He opened the Citation form. He looked at the "Target Core ID" field. He typed Margaret's Core ID. He left the violation field blank.

He had twenty-three citations to find. Margaret had offered to point them out to him. That was a gift. It was also a leash.

He needed to understand what Margaret wanted. Not what she said she wanted. What she actually wanted. He had seen this before. A developer offering to help an inspector find violations in a neighboring building. The offer was never about the building. The offer was about leverage.

He deleted Margaret's Core ID from the form. He typed his own ID again. He selected a violation. Minor. Plumbing. Continuous moisture intrusion. He submitted it.

Citation Authority progress: 48/70.

The debuff stack was growing. He now had two active debuffs. The structural one, reducing physical damage resistance by eighteen percent. The plumbing one, reducing regen rate by twelve percent. They overlapped. The System applied them multiplicatively. His effective physical damage resistance was down to seventy-six percent. His regen was down to eighty-eight percent.

He was getting weaker. Each self-citation was making him weaker. And he needed twenty-two more.

He walked to the water leak. He looked at it. He had cited it now. It was a violation. He was required to fix it. The System's compliance rules required the Core to remediate cited violations within the Citation window or face escalating penalties.

He opened his construction queue. The door assembly was still item three. Estimated completion: eleven minutes. If he added a plumbing repair, it would go to item four. Estimated completion: twenty-two minutes.

He had a choice. Fix the leak and lose construction time on the door. Or leave the leak and take the escalating penalty.

He selected the plumbing repair.

The construction queue updated. The door assembly moved to item four. Estimated completion: twenty-six minutes.

He had thirty days. He had a door that would take twenty-six minutes to build. He had twenty-two more self-citations to file. Each one would add to his debuff stack. Each one would weaken him. Each one would make him more vulnerable to the thing pressing against his northeast wall.

The thing pressed again. Harder this time. The wall flexed. Gerald felt it like a crack forming in plaster.

A new message from Margaret.

"Your northeast wall is not load-bearing. I can see it. You should know that before I press again."

Gerald looked at the northeast wall. He had never inspected it. He had never measured it. He had assumed it was load-bearing because it was exterior.

He pulled up the original construction plans. He checked the northeast wall.

It was not load-bearing. It was a curtain wall. Non-structural. Designed to bear only its own weight and wind loads. It was not designed to withstand impact.

Margaret was pressing against a curtain wall.

If she pressed hard enough, it would fail. And if it failed, Gerald would have a breach in his dungeon perimeter. And if he had a breach, the adjacent dungeon could enter his space.

He had a Citation form open. He had a Core ID in the field. He had no violation selected.

He looked at Margaret's message again. "You should know that before I press again."

It was not a threat. It was information. It was also a demonstration. She had just shown him that she could see his walls, that she could test them, and that she knew which ones would fail.

He typed a response.

"What do you actually want?"

The reply came in two seconds.

"I want you to cite me for a violation that will kill my spawns. I have three spawns that are too strong for my dungeon. They are out of control. I cannot remove them. The only way to weaken them is a Citation debuff. I need you to hit me with a Citation severe enough to reduce their stats below my dungeon's suppression threshold."

Gerald read it. He read it again.

Margaret wanted to be cited. She wanted a debuff. She wanted Gerald to hit her with a Citation so severe that her own spawns would be weakened enough for her to suppress them.

She had offered to help him find violations in his dungeon. She had offered to guide him to the east wall, the northeast wall, the ceiling joists. She was offering him a map of his own weaknesses.

And in return, she wanted him to use those weaknesses against her.

He looked at his Citation Authority progress. 48/70. He needed twenty-two more. He could find them himself. He could walk his dungeon and measure and cite. It would take days. It would weaken him further.

Or he could take Margaret's help. Find the violations faster. Cite himself faster. And then cite Margaret.

But citing Margaret meant hitting her with a debuff she had requested. That meant she trusted him to calibrate the severity correctly. If he hit too hard, he might kill her spawns permanently. If he hit too soft, she would not be able to suppress them, and they would continue to grow.

He needed to understand her dungeon. He needed to know what her spawns were, what their stats were, and what debuff level would bring them under her control.

She had not told him any of that.

He typed: "What are your spawns?"

The reply came instantly.

"Three. Tier 4. They were spawned by an error in my growth sequence. I cannot remove them. I can only weaken them."

Tier 4. Gerald had never fought a Tier 4. His highest encounter had been a Tier 2 goblin. A Tier 4 was something else entirely.

He looked at the northeast wall. It flexed again. A hairline crack appeared in the surface.

Margaret was pressing. She was waiting for his answer.

He looked at his debuff stack. Two active. Physical damage resistance at seventy-six percent. Regen at eighty-eight percent. He was weaker than he had been an hour ago. He would be weaker still after the next citation.

He looked at the Citation form. Margaret's Core ID was still in the field. The violation field was blank.

He had never cited a Tier 4 dungeon core. He did not know what severity to select. He did not know what debuff Margaret's spawns would need. He did not know if he could calibrate it correctly.

If he got it wrong, he would either fail to help her, or he would destroy something she needed.

And if he failed, she would press the northeast wall again.

He typed: "Tell me their exact stats."

The reply came in one second.

"I will send you the full spawn manifest. But first I need you to cite the northeast wall. It is a Major structural violation. You have not cited it yet. Cite it. Take the debuff. Show me you can calibrate."

Gerald looked at the northeast wall. The curtain wall. The one that was not load-bearing. The one Margaret was pressing against.

If he cited it, he would take a Major structural debuff. He would lose more physical damage resistance. He would be weaker. And then he would have to cite Margaret for a violation severe enough to kill her Tier 4 spawns.

He was being tested. She was testing whether he could calibrate. Whether he understood severity. Whether he could hit hard enough to solve her problem without destroying her.

He had thirty-one years of experience writing citations. He had never once been asked to calibrate one for a purpose other than correction. A citation was a correction. It was a penalty. It was not a tool.

Margaret was asking him to use a citation as a scalpel.

He opened the Citation form. He selected the northeast wall. He selected Structural. Major. Unreinforced curtain wall, lateral load failure risk exceeding 25%.

He hovered over the Submit button.

His physical damage resistance would drop to fifty-eight percent. His regen would drop to eighty-eight percent. He would be more vulnerable than he had ever been.

He clicked Submit.

The debuff landed. He felt it. Not physically. He had no body. But he felt it in his Core awareness. A thinning. A weakening. Like a wall that had lost its rebar.

Citation Authority progress: 49/70.

He typed to Margaret. "Done. Send me the manifest."

The manifest arrived. Three spawns. Tier 4. Names: Kethara, Voss, and the thing that had no name because Margaret had not given it one. Their stats were enormous. Kethara had 4,200 HP. Voss had 3,800. The unnamed one had 5,100.

Gerald stared at the numbers. He had never seen stats that high. His strongest spawn was a confused rat with 12 HP.

He pulled up the Citation severity table. A Major Citation applied a debuff proportional to severity and number of violations. One Major violation: 18% debuff. Two Major violations: 36%. Three: 54%.

If he cited Margaret for three Major structural violations, she would take a 54% debuff to all spawned entities. Kethara would drop from 4,200 to 1,932. Voss would drop from 3,800 to 1,748. The unnamed one would drop from 5,100 to 2,346.

Margaret's suppression threshold was listed in her manifest. She could suppress anything below 2,000 HP.

Kethara would be under. Voss would be under. The unnamed one would not.

He needed a fourth violation. He needed to hit Margaret with enough severity to bring the unnamed one below 2,000.

He needed four Major violations. That would be a 72% debuff. The unnamed one would drop to 1,428. Under the threshold.

He looked at Margaret's dungeon layout. She had attached it to the manifest. She had four Major structural violations. They were all real. They were all pre-existing. She had identified them herself.

He opened the Citation form. He selected all four. He hovered over Submit.

A new notification appeared. Not from Margaret. From the System.

**REGULATORY SUPREMACY AUDIT INITIATED. Core with Compliance Rating 88+ has been observed engaging in coordinated Citation activity with a Core below Rating 50. Inspector General notification dispatched. Time until audit: 71:59:58.**

Gerald's hands stopped.

The Inspector General. He had heard about it. Every Core had heard about it. It was the System's internal enforcement mechanism. It audited Cores that exhibited anomalous behavior. Coordinated Citation activity between a high-rated Core and a low-rated Core was anomalous.

He had seventy-two hours before the Inspector General arrived.

Margaret sent a message.

"I did not know about that."

Gerald believed her. Probably.

He looked at the Citation form. Four Major violations. The debuff that would save her spawns. The debuff that would also flag him for an Inspector General audit.

He looked at his Citation Authority progress. 49/70. He needed twenty-one more.

He looked at the northeast wall. The crack had widened. Margaret was pressing. She needed the Citation. She needed it now.

He clicked Submit.
