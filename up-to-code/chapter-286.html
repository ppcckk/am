Chapter 286: Objection Sustained

# Chapter 286

The crowbar swung.

It was not a large crowbar. It was no larger than a toothpick, but it moved with the weight of a structural failure. Gerald ducked. The rat's tiny hard hat bounced off the concrete floor, revealing a patch of matted gray fur.

"Objection," Gerald said.

The rat froze. Its whiskers twitched.

"Form 11-B is for biological entities lacking a valid permit to occupy residential zones," Gerald continued, his voice steady despite the hammering in his chest. "You are not a resident.

You are a previous tenant.

And you are currently holding a weapon in a designated safe zone. That is a Class A safety violation.

"

Hale's cube pulsed. The blue light fractured, casting long, jagged shadows across the stripped ventilation shafts.

"Victor," Hale said, his voice devoid of empathy. "The appeal was accepted because the entity was present during the demolition. The counter-citation is valid under Subsection 44, Paragraph C. Ownership transfer is automatic upon service of the citation."

Gerald looked at the rat. Bartholomew. The rat that had once gnawed through a load-bearing support beam because Gerald had forgotten to seal a crack in the drywall.

"Service," Gerald said. "You are serving me a citation? While I am in the middle of a hostile asset stripping?"

"Precisely," Hale said. "Compliance is universal."

Bartholomew squeaked.

A high-pitched, shrill sound that carried a strange resonance. The tiny crowbar clattered to the floor.

The rat did not attack, which stood on its hind legs, adjusting a minuscule tie that had appeared around its neck.

"Can you speak?" Gerald asked.

The rat nodded. Once. Sharp.

"Who is your legal representative?"

Bartholomew pointed a clawed finger at Hale.

Hale's light dimmed slightly. A flicker of irritation, perhaps. Or maybe it was just the failing power grid.

"The Core cannot be served while in active construction mode," Gerald said, tapping the air where his HUD usually hovered. It was blank.

"Subsection 12, Paragraph F.

Service must occur during operating hours.

I am currently in emergency remediation status.

"

"Emergency remediation is not a legal status," Hale said. "It is a descriptive state."

"Then I am filing a motion to dismiss," Gerald said. He reached into his pocket. He knew nothing was there.

His pockets were gone. His clothes were gone.

But he knew the location of his pen, and he knew the location of his clipboard. The System remembered. It always remembered the intent, if not the object.

He mimed writing.

*Motion to Dismiss: Citation 286-A.* *Grounds: Invalid jurisdiction. The demised entity (Bartholomew) has been evicted. Eviction voids tenancy rights, including the right to issue citations.*

The text floated in the air. Faint. Translucent. But visible.

Hale paused. The cube's rotation stopped.

"Eviction," Hale said. "Did you file an eviction notice?"

"I filed a Notice to Vacate," Gerald said. "Three weeks ago. Via Form 7-C. You ignored it."

"I did not ignore it. I deemed it moot due to the entity's utility."

"Utility is not a defense against zoning laws," Gerald said.

The rat tilted its head. It looked at Gerald. Then it looked at Hale. It seemed to be considering the legal merits of its own eviction.

"If the eviction stands," Gerald said, "Bartholomew is a trespasser. A trespasser cannot issue a citation. It has no standing."

Hale's light flared red.

"The System recognizes the bond between Core and familiar. Bartholomew was registered as a dependent entity."

"Dependent entities do not have voting rights," Gerald said. "They are part of the infrastructure. You cannot cite the infrastructure for demolishing itself."

"Semantics," Hale said.

"Code," Gerald corrected. "Semantics is for poets. Code is for inspectors."

A new message appeared. Not in Gerald's HUD. In the air. Written in smoke.

*APPEAL PENDING. HEARING SCHEDULED: IMMEDIATE.*

Hale's cube expanded. The shadows receded. The darkness lifted, replaced by a harsh, sterile white light.

"Gerald," Hale said. "You want a hearing?"

"I want a ruling," Gerald said. "On the validity of the citation."

"You know how this works."

"I know how *your* version works," Gerald said.

"I have read the Inspector General's addendums. Page 400.

Footnote 12.

In a Hearing, the burden of proof is on the issuer to demonstrate that the cited violation was *substantive* and not *technical*.

"

Hale was silent.

"Technical violations result in fines," Gerald said. "Substantive violations result in removal. If you claim the citation was for 'Unlicensed Demolition,' you must prove that the demolition was not authorized."

"I authorized it," Hale said.

"You authorized the demolition of *my* assets," Gerald said. "Bartholomew is citing you for *unlicensed* demolition. Meaning, you did not file the proper permits with the Inspector General before initiating the asset strip."

Gerald stepped forward. He felt the cold of the concrete through his feet.

He was barefoot, and he was a disembodied consciousness anchored to a rat and a cube.

But he was standing.

"Did you file Form 9-D?" Gerald asked. "Permit for Structural Alteration? Did you receive approval from the IG?"

Hale's light pulsed slowly.

"The IG is busy."

"Busy is not a waiver," Gerald said. "If you did not file, the demolition was illegal. Therefore, the citation for *unlicensed* demolition is a trap. It admits guilt."

The rat squeaked. It picked up the crowbar. It held it out to Gerald.

A gesture. An invitation.

Gerald took the crowbar. It was impossibly light. It felt like holding a thought.

"Hale," Gerald said. "You are arguing that you have the right to strip my dungeon. I am arguing that you stripped it illegally. Which means you are now subject to a penalty for unauthorized modification of a registered Core."

Hale's cube shrank.

"That is a slippery slope," Hale said.

"It is a ladder," Gerald said. "And I am climbing it."

A chime sounded. Not a warning chime. A gavel.

*HEARING STARTS.*

The white light intensified. It blinded Gerald. He closed his eyes.

When he opened them, he was no longer in the dark sub-basement. He was in a conference room.

Oak table. Twelve chairs. A window overlooking a void.

Hale sat at the head, and he was not a cube anymore. He was a man in a suit. Sharp.

Tailored.

His eyes were black voids.

Bartholomew sat in a high chair.

He was wearing a tie. A real tie. Silk.

Navy blue.

And Gerald was sitting opposite Hale.

On the table, between them, was a stack of papers.

"Proceed," Hale said.

Gerald picked up a paper. It was a blueprint of his original dungeon. The one with the handrails. The one with the ventilation.

"Exhibit A," Gerald said.

"The original layout.

Compliant. Ventilation rate: 400 cubic feet per minute.

Handrail height: 34 inches. Egress width: 36 inches.

"

"Correct," Hale said. "Until it wasn't."

"Exhibit B," Gerald said, holding up another paper. "The citation you served. Claim: Structural Instability. Evidence: None provided."

"The assets failed," Hale said.

"The assets were removed," Gerald said. "That is not a failure. That is a theft."

"Compliance requires maintenance," Hale said. "You failed to maintain the structural integrity of the non-compliant sections."

"There were no non-compliant sections," Gerald said. "I checked. Every day."

"You lacked the *spirit* of the law," Hale said.

"The spirit is not in the code," Gerald said. "I read the whole thing.

Three hundred thousand pages.

I have the footnotes memorized. There is no 'spirit.' There is only text.

"

Hale leaned forward. His suit rippled.

"You are arrogant, Inspector."

"I am thorough," Gerald said. "And I have a counter-exhibit."

Gerald pulled a third paper from the stack. It was blank.

"Exhibit C," Gerald said. "The Inspector General's Audit Log for the last fiscal quarter."

Hale's black eyes narrowed.

"That is classified."

"It is public record," Gerald said. "Subsection 2, Paragraph A. All audits are published in the Public Domain of the Core Registry. I downloaded it."

"What does it say?" Hale asked.

"It says your dungeon, 'The Spire of Echoes,' has a Compliance Rating of 89."

"And?"

"And you have a pending violation for 'Unregistered Noise Pollution.' You have not filed Form 15-E for the acoustic dampening."

Hale's suit tightened.

"Meanwhile," Gerald said, "you are trying to steal my Compliance Rating to fix yours. You are not stripping my assets for maintenance. You are stripping them for *transfer*."

Silence.

The rat squeaked. It pointed at Hale.

"Admission," Gerald said. "By failing to refute the claim, you admit the transfer attempt. That is a felony under the System's Penal Code. Core Fraud."

Hale stood up. The chair scraped against the floor. The sound was loud.

"You are lying," Hale said.

"Check the log," Gerald said.

"Page 412. Line 8.

'Hale, Victor.

Status: Under Review. Reason: Attempted Asset Misappropriation.'

"

Hale did not check. He could not.

The papers were not real, and they were constructs of Gerald's memory.

But the System *believed* them. Because Gerald believed them.

*HEARING RESULT: APPEAL UPHELD.* *CITATION VOID.* *PENALTY FOR FALSE CITATION: 500 COMPLIANCE POINTS DEDUCTED FROM ISSUER.*

The white light faded.

Gerald was back in the dark.

Bartholomew was still holding the crowbar. Hale's cube was dark.

"Victor?" Gerald said.

No answer.

Then, a crack.

Hale's cube split. A fissure ran through the blue glass.

"Who did this?" Hale whispered. The voice was weak. Fragmented.

"I cited you," Gerald said. "For fraud."

"You... you fabricated the log."

"I recited it," Gerald said. "From memory. The System doesn't care if you read it.

It cares if you *know* it. And I knew it.

"

Bartholomew dropped the crowbar. It squeaked again. This time, it was a sad sound.

Then, the floor shook.

Not from Hale. From below.

Deeper.

A new message appeared in Gerald's mind. It was not in his HUD. It was in his bones.

*INSPECTOR GENERAL ARRIVING.* *AUDIT IN PROGRESS.* *STATUS: GERALD HUTCHINS.* *COMPLIANCE RATING: RECALCULATING.*

Gerald looked up. The ceiling of the sub-basement was gone. Above him, instead of the library floor, there was a eye.

A giant, white eye.

It blinked.

"Inspector," a voice boomed.

It was not Hale's voice. It was not the System's voice. It was older.

Drier.

"Your dungeon has been cited for three hundred and twelve violations in the last hour."

Gerald straightened his back. He smoothed imaginary wrinkles from his suit.

"Which ones?" Gerald asked.

"All of them," the voice said. "Including the ones you didn't commit."

The eye closed.

The darkness returned. But it was different now. It was not empty.

Bartholomew stood next to Gerald. The rat looked up at the void.

"What now?" the rat squeaked.

Gerald looked at the cracks in Hale's cube. He looked at the empty space where his ventilation shafts had been.

"Now," Gerald said, "we file an emergency injunction."

The rat's eyes widened.

"Against whom?"

Gerald pointed at the ceiling. At the void. At the Inspector General.

"Against God," Gerald said. "For excessive bureaucracy."

Bartholomew picked up the crowbar. He looked at Gerald. He nodded.

They were not ready. They were not compliant. They were not even fully formed.

But Gerald Hutchins had never lost an argument with a building code.

He raised his hand. Not to strike. To gesture.

"Objection," he said.

The void listened.
