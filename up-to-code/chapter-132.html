Chapter 132: Ten Minutes to File

# Chapter 132

The blue ink smeared across the carbon copy. Gerald's fingers cramped around the ballpoint pen, the plastic casing slick with condensation from the rising humidity.

Outside the basement door, boots thudded against the concrete stairs.

Heavy. Deliberate. Law enforcement.

He didn't look up. He kept writing.

*Section 4, Paragraph B: Egress Obstruction.*

The water lapped at his knees now.

His legs were gone, dissolved into the floorboards, but the sensation of wet cold remained, a phantom limb of anxiety. The man who had screamed was long gone, fleeing up the stairwell, but the police were already here.

They would break the door down, and they would see the water. They would see the mold.

And they would not see a monster. They would see a crime scene.

Gerald's mind raced through the municipal code. Section 101.3. *Premises Liability.* If he treated the intruders as potential witnesses to a structural failure, they became protected personnel.

If they were protected personnel, they could not be attacked, and they could not be harmed.

But they could be detained.

He finished the form.

**PERMIT APPLICATION: SUBMITTED.** **TYPE: EMERGENCY LAW ENFORCEMENT STAGING AREA.** **STATUS: PROVISIONAL.**

The door to the basement splintered inward. Wood shards flew into the dark. A flashlight beam cut through the mist, blindingly bright, sweeping across the flooded floor.

Two officers stepped in. Uniformed.

Nervous.

Hands hovering near their belts.

"Police!" one shouted. His voice cracked. "Is anyone down here?"

Gerald did not speak. He could not. He was the room.

The water rippled.

Not from the officers' footsteps, but from Gerald's will. He focused on the ventilation shafts above the ceiling.

The grilles were rusted shut, which was a violation. A clear, undeniable violation of Fire Code 7.2.1.

He pushed against the rust. It resisted. The penalty for the active hazard—*Maintenance Speed -50%*—made the effort feel like moving through tar. Sweat, or whatever fluid passed for it in his current state, beaded on the invisible brow of his core.

"Jesus, it smells like a swamp in here," the second officer said. He wrinkled his nose. "Hey, Mike. Look at the walls."

Mike swept his light left. The plaster was peeling. Brown stains mapped the decay.

"Structural instability," Mike muttered. He pulled out his radio.

"Dispatch, we've got a potential collapse hazard at the Library.

Basement flooding.

I need Fire and Code Enforcement. Immediately.

"

Gerald froze.

Fire and Code Enforcement.

The Inspector General's audits were internal. They were abstract. But human Code Enforcement was physical.

They brought measuring tapes. They brought clipboards.

They brought the power to condemn the building.

If the city condemned the library, the dungeon was destroyed. Not deleted by the System, but erased by zoning laws. Gerald had not considered this.

He had been too focused on the goblins and the rats and the hidden stats.

He had forgotten that the world above was governed by other, older rules.

The water was at his waist. The officers were moving deeper. Their boots squelched.

Gerald needed a distraction, and he needed to make them leave, but he could not attack. Attack violated the provisional permit. It turned them from protected personnel into unauthorized trespassers, which allowed for self-defense, but self-defense against police was a felony that would bring federal agents.

He needed them to walk away voluntarily.

He looked at the ceiling. The main ventilation duct ran directly above the officers. It was clogged with fifty years of dust and dead birds.

He pushed harder. The penalty slowed him, but the desperation fueled a different kind of strength. He wasn't trying to fix the ventilation. He was trying to break it.

*Section 101.4: Imminent Danger.*

If the danger was imminent, the officers had a duty to evacuate.

Gerald focused on the latch of the ventilation grate, which was rusted to the frame.

He poured his remaining mana into the vibration of the metal. A high-pitched whine echoed in the small room.

The officers stopped.

"Did you hear that?" Mike asked.

"Wind," the other officer said, though his hand tightened on his holster.

Gerald increased the frequency. The metal groaned. Then, with a sound like a gunshot, the grate sheared off its hinges.

It fell. Four feet of corrugated steel crashed onto the flooded floor between the two officers. The impact sent a wave of brown water splashing up their legs. They jumped back, slipping, cursing.

"Stay back!" Mike yelled. He aimed his flashlight at the ceiling, then back at the officers. "That was the vent! The whole thing is loose!"

The other officer scrambled up, pulling Mike by the arm.

"We need to get out. Now. If the ceiling comes down..."

They didn't wait for permission. They turned and ran. The boots splashed up the stairs, faster than they had come down.

The door slammed shut. Silence returned, broken only by the dripping of water.

Gerald exhaled. The air in the room circulated slightly, the fallen grate creating a gap. The Compliance Rating ticked up.

**RATING: 12/100 -> 14/100.** **NEW VIOLATION: STRUCTURAL COMPROMISE (VENTILATION).** **PENALTY: -20% STAMINA RECOVERY.**

The gain was negligible. The penalty was immediate.

Gerald felt a wave of exhaustion wash over him, a heaviness that settled into his foundations.

He couldn't afford this. Every small victory came with a larger cost.

He needed to stabilize the room. The water was still rising. The library above was leaking into his domain. He had to stop the source.

He extended his senses upward.

Through the concrete, through the joists, he could feel the weight of the building. And he could feel the source of the leak.

A pipe in the second-floor restroom.

Old copper. Corroded at the joint.

He couldn't reach it. He was anchored to the sub-basement. But he could send something.

He opened his spawn menu. The list was short.

*1. Rat (Confused).

Level 1.* *2.

Mold Spore Cluster.

Level 0.* *3. Water Leak.

Level 0.*

He had no combat spawns. No goblins. No traps. Just biological hazards.

But the Water Leak was his. It was part of him. And the pipe above was part of the building. If he could merge them...

It was illegal. Merging a hazard with a utility was a Class C violation. *Unauthorized Modification of Public Infrastructure.*

Gerald stared at the option.

If he didn't do it, the water would keep rising. Eventually, it would reach the electrical main.

That would cause a fire.

A fire in a library without sprinklers was a death sentence. Not for him—he was a core, he would survive—but for the dungeon. The System would delete him for *Gross Negligence of Fire Safety.*

If he did it, he risked an audit, and he chose the risk.

**ACTION: MERGE ENTITY [WATER LEAK] WITH OBJECT [PUB-UTIL-04].** **WARNING: THIS ACTION MAY TRIGGER IMMEDIATE COMPLIANCE SCAN.**

**CONFIRM?**

Gerald clicked *Yes*.

The sensation was violent.

It felt like a hook pulling through his soul. He felt the pipe in the restroom above.

He felt the rust, the weakness, the thinning metal, and he poured his own mass into it. Not to fix it, but to plug it.

The water flow in the basement slowed. Stopped. The rising tide froze at his chest.

But the pain was blinding. It wasn't physical pain.

It was bureaucratic pain, and the System was rewriting his essence.

He was no longer just a dungeon; he was now partially a public utility.

**STATUS UPDATE:** **ENTITY TYPE: HYBRID (CORE/INFRASTRUCTURE).** **COMPLIANCE RATING: 14/100 -> 22/100.** **BONUS: +10% WATER RESISTANCE.** **DEBUFF: VULNERABLE TO CONDEMNATION ORDERS.**

The rating jump was significant. The vulnerability was terrifying.

Before Gerald could process the change, the air in the room grew cold. The water surface stilled. The dust motes stopped dancing.

**SYSTEM ALERT: AUDIT INITIATED.** **AUDITOR: INSPECTOR GENERAL, REGION 4.** **REASON: UNAUTHORIZED INFRASTRUCTURE MERGER.**

Gerald's heart, if he had one, would have stopped.

A figure materialized in the center of the room. It was not a police officer. It was not a monster.

It was a construct of pure geometry. Sharp angles.

White light.

No face. Just a clipboard that floated in the air, pages rifling themselves with the speed of a high-speed printer.

"Mr. Hutchins," the Auditor said. Its voice did not come from a mouth; it vibrated from the walls. "You have violated Code 101.9. *Prohibition of Symbiotic Infrastructure Integration.*"

Gerald tried to speak. He formed a mouth in the water.

"It was necessary," Gerald said.

His voice sounded like gravel grinding together.

"Imminent fire hazard.

Imminent structural failure.

I acted to preserve life and property.

"

The Auditor paused. The pages stopped rifling.

"Prove it," the Auditor said.

Gerald blinked. Water dripped from his phantom lips.

"Prove what?"

"The hazard. You claimed imminent failure.

You performed an unauthorized merge.

To avoid deletion, you must prove that the action was the only compliant solution available at the time of the incident.

If you fail to prove this, your core will be purged.

"

Gerald looked at the Auditor. Then he looked at the inventory.

He had one item left. *Building Permit Application.* It was still pending, but he had filled it out. He had cited the police. He had cited the hazard.

But he didn't have the police report. He didn't have the fire code inspection. He had only his own word, and his own actions.

The Auditor raised a hand. A pen appeared in its grip.

"You have thirty seconds to provide evidence of the hazard's severity. If the hazard did not exist, or if it was not *imminent* by the definition of Code 101.2, you are guilty of sabotage against municipal infrastructure. Penalty: Deletion."

Twenty seconds.

Gerald's mind spun. He needed proof. He needed a witness. But the only witnesses were the police, and they had left.

Wait, and the police had radioed for Code Enforcement. They had reported a *potential collapse hazard*. They had reported *flooding*.

Could he use their report?

He reached into the System's memory. Not his own memory, but the public record. The radio logs.

They were part of the city's infrastructure. And he was now partially infrastructure.

He pushed. It was like trying to lift a car with his teeth. The *Vulnerable to Condemnation* debuff weighed him down. The *Stamina Recovery* penalty made his thoughts sluggish.

Fifteen seconds.

The Auditor's pen hovered over the deletion button.

Gerald focused on the radio frequency. He grabbed the data stream. It was messy, corrupted, but he found it.

*Dispatch Log, 14:02. Officer Mike R.: "Basement flooding. Structural instability. Venting collapse risk."*

He pulled the log into the room. A holographic projection of the text appeared in the air, blue and glowing.

"Exhibit A," Gerald said.

His voice was stronger now. He stood up, the water receding around his waist.

"Officer R. identified 'structural instability' and 'venting collapse risk.' This constitutes an immediate hazard under Code 101.1.

My action was to mitigate a reported hazard, not create one.

"

The Auditor looked at the hologram. It tilted its head.

"The officer used the word 'potential.' Not 'confirmed.' Code 101.2 requires *confirmed* imminent danger for emergency waivers."

Ten seconds.

Gerald felt a chill. The Auditor was right. He had interpreted the officer's fear as fact.

He needed something else. Something undeniable.

He looked at the water. The pipe.

He could show the pipe, and he reached out with his senses. The pipe was still plugged by his mass.

It was holding.

But the metal around it was stressed. If he released it...

No. That would cause a flood.

But he didn't need to release it. He needed to show the *stress*.

He focused on the copper. He made it sing. A high-pitched frequency. The same vibration he used to break the vent.

The pipe groaned. The water in the basement shuddered.

"Listen," Gerald said.

The Auditor turned toward the ceiling. The groaning grew louder.

A crack appeared in the plaster of the floor above.

A drop of water fell from the ceiling, landing on the Auditor's shoulder.

The Auditor flinched.

"Water ingress detected," the Auditor said. "From a concealed pipe. Pressure anomaly. Structural fatigue."

"It is imminent," Gerald said. "The pipe is failing. If I had not plugged it, the burst would have occurred within the hour.

The water would have reached the electrical main. Fire code 7.2 would have been violated.

Loss of life probability: High.

"

The Auditor looked at the crack in the floor. It looked at the glowing radio log. It looked at Gerald.

Five seconds.

The pen tapped the clipboard. *Click. Click. Click.*

Gerald held his breath. The water held its breath. The room held its breath.

The Auditor spoke.

"Conditionally accepted. The hazard is deemed real. However, the method of mitigation—symbiotic integration—was non-standard. You are not deleted."

Gerald slumped. Relief washed over him, but it was cold, sharp relief.

"However," the Auditor continued. "You are now subject to *Probationary Status*. You have thirty days to rectify the unauthorized merge.

You must remove your essence from the pipe and install a proper, code-compliant repair.

If you fail, the penalty is not deletion. It is *Transfer*.

"

"Transfer?" Gerald asked.

"Your dungeon will be relocated to a site of the Inspector General's choosing. The site will be... less favorable."

The Auditor vanished. The white light faded. The radio log disappeared.

Gerald was alone again.

But he was not safe, and he felt the pipe above. It was still plugged by him. He couldn't remove himself without bursting the pipe. He couldn't fix the pipe without leaving his body.

He was trapped.

And then, a new notification pinged.

**NEW CITATION RECEIVED.** **FROM: DUNGEON CORE 'THE CRIMSON VAULT' (NEIGHBORING SECTOR).** **REASON: DISTURBANCE OF PEACE. VIBRATION HAZARD.** **PENALTY: -30% STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY FOR 24 HOURS.**

Gerald looked up. The floor above him cracked further. A chunk of concrete fell from the ceiling, landing in the water with a splash.

The Crimson Vault. His neighbor. It had felt the vibration. It had reported it.

It was using the System to hurt him.

And it was working.

Gerald looked at his inventory. The *Building Permit Application* was still there. But now, it had a new field.

**APPEAL WINDOW: 10 MINUTES.** **ARGUMENT REQUIRED: STRUCTURAL LOGIC.**

Gerald had to appeal. But he couldn't argue against a neighbor's complaint. He could only argue if he served a citation on them first.

He needed to find the Crimson Vault. But he was stuck in a basement, plugged into a pipe, with a crumbling ceiling.

He looked at the water. The water was his blood. The water was his body.

He could send the water.

Not all of it. Just a trickle. A single, compliant stream.

He focused on the crack in the floor. The water seeped upward, through the crack, into the floor above. Into the library.

He could feel the library. The shelves. The books. The people.

And he could feel the Crimson Vault, hidden in the sub-basement next door, behind a false wall.

The water reached the ceiling of the Crimson Vault.

Gerald smiled, and he had a witness.

He had a way in.

He had ten minutes to file the citation before his structural integrity gave out.

He typed.
