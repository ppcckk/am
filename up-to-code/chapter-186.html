Chapter 186: Jurisdictional Collapse

# Chapter 186

The ink was still wet on the blueprint when the air in the sub-basement changed pressure.

It wasn't a sound. It was a sensation, like the sudden drop in altitude inside an elevator, accompanied by the smell of ozone and stale coffee.

Gerald Hutchins did not look up from the 1974 foundation plan. He kept his pen steady. The nib hovered over the word *Emergency*. He finished the underline.

The System interface shimmered into existence above the drafting table. Not the usual translucent blue. This one was red. Deep, arterial red.

**SYSTEM ALERT: UNAUTHORIZED STRUCTURAL MODIFICATION DETECTED.** **SUBJECT: GERALD HUTCHINS, CORE ID #8940-ASH.** **CHARGE: FRAUDULENT CLASSIFICATION OF VOID ANCHOR.** **PENALTY PENDING: TOTAL SYSTEM ERASURE.** **TIME TO RESPONSE: 00:03:12.**

Kaelen, the adventurer who had been helping him move heavy stones earlier that morning, took a step back. His hand went to the hilt of his sword.

"Gerald," Kaelen said. His voice was tight. "That's not a glitch."

"I know what it is," Gerald said. He didn't look at the red text.

He looked at the rat sitting on the edge of the table.

The rat was grooming its tail. It seemed unbothered by the prospect of imminent non-existence.

Gerald turned to Kaelen.

"Get out," Gerald said.

Kaelen blinked. "I'm not leaving you alone with an Audit."

"You are a Level 40 Warrior. You are not a licensed municipal inspector." Gerald pointed a finger at the door. "This is a private office. You are loitering."

"The System is going to delete you!"

"Then I suggest you leave before you get deleted as an accessory to fraud."

Kaelen hesitated. The red light pulsed. Two minutes left.

"Fine," Kaelen snapped. "But if you die, I'm taking your loot."

"Understood."

Kaelen ran. The heavy iron door slammed shut. The latch clicked.

Gerald was alone.

He picked up the blueprint. He rolled it up. He placed it in a manila folder. He labeled it *Case File 1982-Rev-A*.

He walked to the center of the room. He stood on the concrete floor. He waited.

The air tore open.

It didn't open like a portal. It unzipped, like a suit being pulled apart by invisible fingers. From the tear stepped a figure.

It was seven feet tall. It wore a suit made of grey stone, tailored to fit a body that had no joints. Its face was a smooth slab of marble, featureless except for two glowing yellow eyes. It held a clipboard.

The Inspector General.

Gerald straightened his tie. He had put on a tie for this. It was a clip-on, because he didn't have the dexterity to tie a Windsor knot while his body was dissolving into dungeon geometry, but it looked professional.

"Good morning," Gerald said.

The Inspector General did not speak. It simply raised the clipboard. A voice echoed in Gerald's skull. It sounded like gravel grinding in a mixer.

**CITATION 001: UNLICENSED VOID TRANSPORT.** **CITATION 002: FALSE EMERGENCY EGRESS CLASSIFICATION.** **CITATION 003: FAILURE TO FILE FORM 11-B FOR PRESENT BIOLOGICAL ENTITY.**

The rat looked up. It squeaked.

Gerald adjusted his glasses.

"Objection," Gerald said.

The yellow eyes narrowed. The air temperature dropped ten degrees. Frost formed on the blueprint folder in Gerald's hand.

**STATE BASIS FOR OBJECTION.**

"The Void Anchor is not a transport mechanism," Gerald said.

His voice was steady, though his knees felt like water. "It is a load-bearing anomaly.

My dungeon's structural integrity relies on the tensile strength of the void connection.

Removing it would cause a catastrophic collapse of the library above, resulting in significant property damage to municipal assets."

The Inspector General paused. The stone suit shifted.

**THIS IS A CLASSIFICATION ERROR. VOIDS ARE NOT LOAD-BEARING. THEY ARE CONSUMERS.**

"According to the 1974 Ashford City Building Code, Section 4.1.2," Gerald recited, "any anomaly that stabilizes a foundation against lateral earth pressure is classified as a Retaining Feature, not a Hazard. If the System consumes the Void, the library collapses. The liability for that collapse falls on the Inspector General, not the Core."

The silence stretched.

The Inspector General tilted its head. The yellow eyes flickered.

**LIABILITY CLAIM NOTED. RE-EVALUATING.**

The red text on the screen paused. The countdown stopped at 00:01:45.

Gerald exhaled. It was a small, shaky breath.

He hated this.

He hated improvising.

He hated lying. But the lie was technically a reinterpretation of code, and Gerald had survived thirty-one years by finding the one interpretation that kept him out of trouble.

The Inspector General lowered the clipboard.

**HOLDING CITATIONS 1 AND 2 PENDING REVIEW. CITATION 3 REMAINS.**

"The rat is a tenant," Gerald said quickly. "He pays rent in formative waste."

**TENANCY AGREEMENT NOT FILED. CITATION STANDS.**

"I can file it now."

**WINDOW CLOSED. PENALTY APPLIED.**

A beam of red light hit Gerald's chest.

He didn't scream. He didn't fall. He just felt a sudden, hollow emptiness in his stomach.

The dungeon's mana regeneration rate plummeted. The handrails along the corridor flickered and dimmed.

The ventilation fans slowed to a wheeze.

His Compliance Rating dropped from 62 to 41.

The Inspector General stepped back toward the tear in reality.

**HEARING SCHEDULED FOR NEXT QUARTER. PREPARE YOUR DOCUMENTATION.**

The tear zipped shut. The red light faded. The room was just a room again.

Gerald slumped against the drafting table. His legs gave out. He slid down until he was sitting on the concrete floor.

The rat hopped down from the table. It scurried over and sat next to him. It nudged his hand.

"Thanks," Gerald whispered. "I think."

The door burst open.

Kaelen was back. He wasn't running this time. He was walking, fast. Behind him came three other people.

A dwarf with a hammer strapped to his back. A mage holding a staff that crackled with static. A rogue who was checking his pockets, likely for stolen goods.

"You're alive," Kaelen said.

"I am compliant," Gerald corrected. He tried to stand up. He failed.

The dwarf stepped forward. "Is it true? Did you just out-lawyer an Inspector General?"

"I reclassified a hazard," Gerald said. "It's a standard municipal tactic. You learn it in year three."

The mage looked at the red spot on the floor where the beam had hit. "You look terrible. Your dungeon is dying."

"It's not dying. It's in deficit." Gerald pulled himself up using the edge of the table. "The Citation drained my regen. I need to increase my Compliance Rating immediately, or the next audit will erase me."

The rogue laughed. "Increase your rating? In ten minutes?

You've got mold spores, Gerald.

You've got a rat. You've got a water leak.

How are you going to boost your rating?"

Gerald looked at the blueprint. He looked at the Void exit, now labeled *Emergency Exit*.

"I need a new category," Gerald said.

"What?" Kaelen asked.

"I need Electrical Code compliance," Gerald said. "Currently, my dungeon has zero lightning resistance. If I cite a rival dungeon for electrical hazards, I can appeal their citation during the Hearing and force the System to recognize my own electrical infrastructure as mitigating factor."

The dwarf scratched his head. "You want to build electrical wiring? In a dungeon?

With what? Copper?

You don't have gold."

"I don't need gold," Gerald said. "I need conductors. I need a grounding path."

He looked at the mage.

"Can you cast a continuous static discharge spell?"

The mage blinked.

"Yeah. Low level.

It's annoying. Gives people a shock."

"Do you know how many volts it produces?"

"Maybe fifty. Enough to tingle."

"That's enough for a Class 1 warning," Gerald said. He grabbed a piece of chalk.

He walked to the wall.

He drew a line from the ceiling to the floor. "I need you to channel that spell into this conduit I'm going to create. The System registers magical energy as electricity for code purposes.

If I install a 'Grounding Rod' that connects to the earth, I gain a passive resistance to lightning."

Kaelen stared at him. "You want to turn the mage into a battery?"

"I want to install a compliant lightning rod," Gerald said. "It's safer than what we have now. Which is nothing. And since I'm going to be fighting a thunder-based dungeon core next week, I need the resistance."

The rogue stepped closer. "Which dungeon core? Who's coming?"

"The Storm-Caller," Gerald said. "Core ID #5591. He's been citing my handrails for 'unsafe spacing' three times this week. He thinks I'm an amateur."

"He's a Level 30 Core," the dwarf said. "He controls lightning. He'll fry you before you get a Citation served."

"He will," Gerald agreed. "Unless he steps into a compliant conductive zone."

Gerald turned to the mage. "Start the spell. Do not stop."

The mage shrugged. He raised his staff. A small arc of blue lightning jumped from the tip. It zapped Gerald's hand.

"Ow," Gerald said.

"Keep going," Gerald instructed.

The dwarf grabbed his hammer. "I'll forge the rod. What shape?"

"Vertical. Six feet.

Copper-wrapped, if you have scrap.

If not, iron. Iron is fine for grounding, just less efficient."

The dwarf nodded. He went to work. Sparks flew.

The rogue watched Gerald. "Why are you doing this? You could just run. Leave the dungeon."

Gerald looked at the rat. The rat was gnawing on a piece of chalk.

"Where would I go?" Gerald asked. "I have a Compliance Rating of 41.

The System tracks me.

If I leave this footprint, I lose my anchor.

I become a stray. Strays get deleted by the Inspector General."

"So we're trapped here," Kaelen said.

"We're employed here," Gerald corrected. "By me. And you're helping. That makes you subcontractors."

The dwarf snorted. "Subcontractors don't get hazard pay."

"You get loot," Gerald said. "And the satisfaction of a job done well."

The mage finished the channeling. The air in the room smelled sharp. The chalk line on the wall glowed faintly blue.

**SYSTEM NOTIFICATION: LIGHTNING ROD INSTALLED (PROVISIONAL).** **ELECTRICAL CODE COMPLIANCE: +5 POINTS.** **PASSIVE RESISTANCE: LIGHTNING DAMAGE REDUCED BY 10%.**

Gerald felt a tingle in his toes. It was a good tingle. It was the tingle of progress.

"It's not enough," Gerald said. "I need structural code too. The Storm-Caller uses wind attacks. If he blows my walls down, I lose the dungeon."

"So build thicker walls," Kaelen said.

"I don't have time for masonry," Gerald said. "I need a loophole. Structural code allows for 'Tensile Strength via Tension Cables.' We don't have steel cables."

"We have rope," the rogue said. "I have a hundred feet of climbing rope."

Gerald looked at the rogue.

"Is it certified?"

The rogue paused. "Does it matter?"

"The System checks certification labels for high-grade materials. If the rope isn't rated for load-bearing, it counts as a hazard, not a support."

"I don't care about rating!" the rogue yelled. "I care about surviving!"

Gerald pulled out a form from his pocket. Form 7-A. *Waiver of Non-Standard Material*.

"Sign this," Gerald said. "It waives your right to a safe working environment in exchange for expedited compliance approval."

The rogue stared at the form. He looked at Kaelen.

"Do it," Kaelen said. "He's insane, but he's right. We need the points."

The rogue signed.

Gerald attached the waiver to the blueprint. He held it up to the System interface.

**APPLICATION FOR EXEMPTED MATERIAL: ACCEPTED.** **STRUCTURAL COMPLIANCE: +15 POINTS.** **CURRENT RATING: 56/100.**

"Better," Gerald said. "But the Storm-Caller is at the border. He's probing for weaknesses."

As if on cue, the dungeon walls shook. Dust fell from the ceiling. The lights flickered.

**SYSTEM ALERT: INTRUSION DETECTED.** **SOURCE: CORE #5591 (STORM-Caller).** **METHOD: LIGHTNING STRIKE ON EXTERNAL WALL.**

Gerald looked at the wall. A crack appeared. Spiderwebbing across the stone.

"He's testing the new rod," Gerald said. "He's trying to find a ground fault."

"Can we fight back?" Kaelen asked. He drew his sword.

"We don't fight," Gerald said. "We inspect."

He walked toward the crack. The air was charged with static. His hair stood on end.

"The Storm-Caller is violating the Fire Code," Gerald said. "Lightning is a heat source. If he strikes a non-combustible wall, it's fine. But if he strikes a combustible material, it's a fire hazard."

Gerald pointed to the crack.

"I didn't install any combustible material here," Gerald said. "But the rogue did."

He pointed to the climbing rope, which was now tied to the wall as a 'support cable'.

"Rope is combustible," Gerald said. "Highly."

The rogue's face went white. "You set me up."

"I utilized available resources," Gerald said. He pulled out his citation pad. He wrote.

**CITATION 004: ILLEGAL STORAGE OF COMBUSTIBLE MATERIAL IN STRUCTURAL SUPPORT SYSTEM.**

"Serve it," Gerald said.

The System interface flashed. The citation flew out of Gerald's hand and shot through the crack in the wall, heading toward the Storm-Caller's dungeon.

Then, a new message appeared.

**APPEAL FILED BY CORE #5591.** **HEARING COMMENCING IMMEDIATELY.**

The crack in the wall widened. A bolt of lightning struck Gerald's chest.

He didn't fall.

He absorbed it. The lightning rod glowed bright white. The damage was reduced.

Gerald smiled. A thin, dangerous smile.

"Welcome to the Hearing," Gerald said. "You have thirty seconds to explain why your lightning bolt didn't trip the breaker in your own dungeon."

The voice of the Storm-Caller boomed from outside. It sounded confused.

*"What breaker? I am a storm! I am power!"*

Gerald looked at Kaelen.

"Get ready," Gerald said. "He's going to lose. And when he loses, he's going to try to burn the library down."

"Why?" Kaelen asked.

"Because he's citing me for 'Emotional Distress to a Core' next," Gerald said. "And I'm going to countersue for Property Damage."

He turned to the rogue.

"Unclip the rope. Now."

The rogue fumbled with the knots.

**SYSTEM NOTIFICATION: STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY COMPROMISED.** **COMPLIANCE RATING: 56 -> 40.** **WARNING: DUNGEON COLLAPSE IMMINENT.**

Gerald looked at the ceiling. It was sagging.

"Kaelen," Gerald said. "Hold up the north wall."

"With what?"

"Your shoulders."

Gerald walked toward the front door. He had a new form in his hand.

**FORM 19-C: EMERGENCY VENTILATION OVERRIDE.**

He didn't have time to wait for the Hearing. The dungeon was collapsing. The Storm-Caller was attacking. And he was still being audited.

He had one move left.

He had to let the Storm-Caller win the physical fight.

He had to let the library collapse.

Because if the library collapsed, the building inspector of Ashford had a jurisdictional crisis. And Gerald was the only person who could fix it.

He opened the door.

The sun was shining.

The Storm-Caller was waiting.

"Come and get it," Gerald whispered.
