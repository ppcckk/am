Chapter 125: Copper Tasting Void

# Chapter 125

Darkness was not empty. It was heavy.

It pressed against the outer shell of the core, a crushing weight that tasted of copper and old dust.

Gerald floated in the void, his consciousness stretched thin across four thousand square feet of sub-basement concrete. The creature's grip tightened. His structural integrity dropped by twelve points.

*Damage Report: Core Casing Compromised.* *Structural Integrity: 88%.*

The rat squeaked.

It sounded wet and small. Gerald focused on the sound.

The rat was the only variable he could control.

The intruder was the constant problem. The intruder had steel-toed boots. The intruder had high-visibility compliance issues.

The intruder had the strength of a draft horse and the patience of a termite.

Gerald analyzed the grip.

The claws dug into the crystalline lattice of his core.

The pressure point was localized. The damage was concentrated at the apex.

If he let the core shatter, the dungeon would collapse. If he fought back, he would lose more structural points. There was no winning fight.

There was only a delaying tactic.

He reached out with his mind, not to the creature, but to the floor beneath it.

*Action: Subsurface Shift.* *Target: Floor Tile 4B, Sector North.* *Displacement: 0.5 inches, vertical.*

The floor jerked. The creature stumbled. The grip loosened for a fraction of a second.

Gerald seized the opening. He did not attack. He did not spawn a monster. He issued a second citation.

**Citation Issued: Improper Footwear on Uneven Surface.** **Reason: Slippery conditions present without anti-slip treads.** **Penalty: -5 Dexterity.**

The creature's balance faltered.

It grunted, a sound like gravel in a blender. It adjusted its stance, planting its steel toes into the concrete. The steel toes bit into the floor.

The creature regained stability.

It looked down at Gerald's core with renewed interest.

"Persistent," the creature growled. Its voice vibrated through the stone. "You are a bug. A bug with a clipboard."

Gerald felt the vibration. He cataloged it. *High decibel output. Close proximity. Potential hearing damage.*

*Compliance Rating: 33.* *Active Violations: 4.* *Penalty Accumulation: Imminent.*

The penalty was coming.

The System was patient, but it was also relentless. If his rating dropped below thirty, the ventilation fans would stop. If they stopped, the mold spores would grow unchecked.

If the mold grew, his air quality would plummet. If his air quality plummeted, his spawn regeneration would cease.

The rat would die.

The lattice would starve.

The dungeon would end.

He needed a loophole. He needed a rule the creature did not know.

The creature reached for the core again. Its hand was thick, calloused, and scarred. It was a miner's hand.

Gerald recognized the calluses. They were the same as his own, before he had traded a pickaxe for a clipboard.

"You have no business here," Gerald said. His voice was not loud. It was dry. It echoed in the small, dark chamber.

The creature paused. "This is my territory now. The contract was signed."

"Contracts require zoning variances," Gerald replied.

"Zoning?" The creature laughed. It was a ugly, wet sound. "There is no zoning here. There is only loot."

Gerald felt a twitch in his neural network. *Loot.* The word triggered a memory. It triggered a file. *Chapter 4, Section 9, Subsection C: Unlicensed Resource Extraction.*

The creature was not just an invader.

It was a scavenger. It had been mining the lattice. It had been pulling crystals from the walls to feed its own core.

That was not combat.

That was theft.

But theft was not a building code violation. Theft was a crime. The System did not enforce criminal law. It enforced structural law.

Unless the theft caused a structural violation.

Gerald focused on the hole in the wall.

The creature had torn a chunk out of the support column. The column was load-bearing. The ceiling above was sagging.

The ceiling was now classified as a *Temporary Overhang Without Temporary Support.*

**Citation Issued: Unsecured Ceiling Overhang.** **Reason: Load-bearing column compromised by external excavation.** **Penalty: -20 Structural Integrity to Local Area.**

The ceiling groaned.

Dust rained down from the cracks.

The creature looked up. It saw the fissures spreading like spiderwebs.

It did not care. It tightened its grip on Gerald's core.

"Stop it," the creature said. "Or I crush you."

Gerald felt the pressure increase. His vision blurred. The edges of his consciousness frayed.

*Structural Integrity: 74%.* *Core Integrity: Critical.*

He had one move left. It was a desperate move. It required him to break a rule he had always followed. It required him to violate his own code.

*Action: Self-Sacrifice of Non-Load-Bearing Wall.* *Target: South Wall, Sector 3.* *Reason: Egress Obstruction.*

The wall collapsed. Not onto the creature.

Onto the path behind it.

The creature was standing in the doorway. Behind it, the tunnel stretched back toward the library foundation. The collapse blocked the retreat.

The creature roared. It looked at the rubble. It looked at Gerald.

"You trapped me?"

"I cited the egress route," Gerald said. "It is now blocked. You are in a confined space without a secondary exit."

The creature's eyes narrowed.

It calculated. It realized the trap.

But it did not panic.

It raised its free hand. It gathered energy. It prepared a spell.

A blast of heat. It would melt the walls.

It would melt Gerald.

Gerald could not stop it. He had no mana. He had no defenses. He had only the paperwork.

He looked at the rat. The rat was huddled in the corner. The rat was the witness. The rat was the only living thing in the room that was not a threat.

*Action: Witness Deposition.* *Cost: 5 Compliance Points.* *Effect: Legal Standing.*

The System paused. The air in the room grew still. A translucent panel appeared before Gerald's core.

**Hearing Initiated: Involuntary.** **Petitioner: Core 402 (Gerald Hutchins).** **Respondent: Unidentified Entity.** **Charge: Trespassing and Illegal Construction Modification.** **Evidence: Witness Testimony.**

The creature stopped casting. It looked at the panel. It looked at Gerald.

"You're calling a hearing?" The creature's voice dropped. "You're calling a *hearing* while I'm holding your core?"

"I am calling a hearing," Gerald said. "And you are required to attend."

The System enforced attendance.

The creature's legs locked. It could not move.

It could not cast. It was frozen in place by the bureaucratic machinery of the afterlife.

Gerald felt the time stop. He had seconds. He used them.

He projected the image of the rat. The rat stood on its hind legs. It looked at the creature. It squeaked.

*Testimony: The entity entered without permission. The entity damaged the foundation. The entity created a trip hazard.*

The System processed the testimony. It scanned the damage. It scanned the violations.

**Verdict: Guilty.** **Penalty: Immediate Expulsion.**

The creature screamed. It tried to break the hold.

It tried to crush the core.

But the System pulled. It pulled the creature's essence, not its body. It yanked the connection between the entity and the dungeon's ley lines.

The creature dissolved.

Its form turned to smoke. Its boots clattered to the floor.

The steel toes remained.

The rest vanished.

Gerald floated in the silence. The dust settled.

The crack in the ceiling widened.

A chunk of concrete fell onto the core.

It bounced off.

*Structural Integrity: 70%.* *Compliance Rating: 28.*

He had survived. He had won. But the cost was too high.

His rating was in the red zone. The ventilation fans sputtered.

They stopped.

The air grew stale. The mold spores, dormant for hours, drifted. The water leak dripped faster. The puddle spread.

*Warning: Mold Growth Detected.* *Action: Remediation Required Within 4 Hours.* *Penalty: Total Dungeon Collapse upon Expiration.*

Gerald looked at the steel-toed boot lying in the rubble. It was heavy. It was expensive. It was a clue.

The creature had not been a random monster. It had been wearing safety equipment. It had been following a schedule. It had been *working.*

Who sent it?

A new notification pinged. It was not from the System. It was a message, encoded in a frequency Gerald recognized. It was the frequency used by the Ashford Municipal Union.

**From: User 'InspectorGeneral_01'** **Subject: Audit Notice.** **Body: Your dungeon has attracted attention. Compliance is mandatory. Report to Sector 7 for immediate review.**

Gerald felt a cold dread settle in his neural core. The Inspector General was not the System's automated auditor. The Inspector General was a player.

A high-level player.

And they were coming to check his paperwork.

If they found the mold, they would shut him down. If they found the water leak, they would shut him down. If they found the missing egress signs, they would shut him down.

He had four hours.

He looked at the rat. The rat was eating a piece of drywall.

*Action: Prioritize.*

He could not fix everything.

He had to choose. He could fix the mold. He could fix the water.

He could fix the signs. He had resources for one.

He looked at the boot. He looked at the ceiling. He looked at the dark, spreading mold.

He made his choice.

*Action: Seal Ventilation Ducts.* *Reason: Containment of Biological Hazard.*

He shut the vents. He trapped the air. He trapped the mold. He trapped himself.

The air quality dropped.

*Air Quality: Poor.* *Penalty: -10 Regeneration to All Entities.*

The rat slowed down. It curled into a ball. It stopped eating.

Gerald closed his eyes. He drafted a memo. It was a memo to himself. It was a request for assistance.

*To: The Union.* *From: Gerald Hutchins, Inspector.* *Subject: Emergency Zoning Request.* *Body: I require a crew. I require a crew immediately. I have a violation that cannot be fixed with saliva.*

He sent the message. He waited.

A response came. It was not from the Union. It was from the Inspector General.

**From: InspectorGeneral_01** **Body: You cited my agent.

That is a bold move.

I like bold moves. I am coming to see you.

Bring your finest forms.**

Gerald opened his eyes. The dark was not empty. It was waiting.
