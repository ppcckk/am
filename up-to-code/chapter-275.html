Chapter 275: Citing the City

# Chapter 275

The sludge lunged.

It did not roar. It did not scream. It hissed, a low, wet sound like steam escaping a cracked boiler. The humanoid shape of sewage and anger stretched across the sub-basement floor, a viscous tide rising against Gerald's boots.

Gerald did not step back. He stepped sideways.

His left foot found the dry patch near the electrical panel. His right foot remained on the wet concrete. Balance required a fulcrum, and the concrete was the only reliable variable in this equation.

"Stop moving," Gerald said.

The sludge-shape paused. Not out of respect, but out of confusion. Monsters did not usually command things to stop. They usually demanded they die.

"I am conducting an inspection," Gerald continued, his voice level, devoid of the adrenaline that should have been flooding his veins. "You are currently blocking a primary egress route. This is a Class 4 obstruction."

The Auditor's avatar rippled. Black bubbles popped on its surface, releasing the scent of rot and old rain. It raised a hand, formed of dripping filth, and pointed a jagged finger at Gerald's chest.

**"VIOLATION: UNLICENSED CITATION. PENALTY: IMMEDIATE TERMINATION."**

The words hung in the air, glowing with a sickly green light. The System was reacting to the sludge-shape's intent, not its actions. Gerald saw the notification flash in his peripheral vision. A red border pulsed around his field of view.

*Terminate.*

It was a blunt instrument. A hammer when a scalpel was needed. Gerald had spent thirty-one years dealing with hammers. He knew exactly where to place the nail.

Kael scrambled behind a stack of damp drywall sheets. The young hero was shaking, his sword drawn but useless against liquid matter. He looked like a man who had forgotten how to breathe.

"Gerald, hit it!" Kael yelled.

"With what?" Gerald asked. He pointed to the sludge. "My pen? It is in the sewer."

Kael's eyes widened.

"You threw the pen in the sewer?"

"I served the citation. The method of delivery is secondary to the act of service."

The sludge-shape surged forward again. This time, it did not hesitate.

It splashed against Gerald's legs, cold and heavy.

Gerald felt the weight of it, the sheer mass of displaced water and waste. It tried to envelop him, to pull him down into the drain.

Gerald leaned back, and he used the wall behind him for leverage. His spine pressed against the cold brick.

He did not fight the pull.

He analyzed the vector.

The sludge was moving from the drain to the center of the room. It was flowing with the gradient of the floor. The sub-basement had a one-inch slope per ten feet, designed for drainage. Gerald had checked this measurement three times during his initial registration.

He looked at the floor. He looked at the sludge.

"Kael," Gerald said. "Hand me the level."

Kael blinked. "The what?"

"The spirit level. It is in my belt. The third pocket."

Kael fumbled for the belt. Gerald's core body was still in the central pit, but this physical avatar he had manifested possessed pockets.

It was a glitch, perhaps, or a feature.

Gerald did not care, and he had tools.

Kael tossed the small yellow tool. It arced through the air and landed on the wet concrete. Gerald caught it without looking.

He slid it under the edge of the sludge-shape's leading edge.

The bubble moved slowly. It was not fast. It was heavy.

But it was moving. And it was moving *downhill*.

"You are violating the structural integrity of the floor slab," Gerald said to the Auditor. "Your presence is adding an estimated four hundred pounds of dynamic load to a section rated for static residential use only. Do you have a load-bearing permit?"

The sludge-shape stopped. The green text flickered.

**"DENIAL OF LIABILITY. ENTITY IS NOT STRUCTURAL. ENTITY IS BIOLOGICAL."**

"Biological matter retains mass," Gerald said.

"Mass exerts force. Force exerts pressure. You are exceeding the live load capacity of the sub-floor joists.

I am citing you for Structural Overload.

"

He pulled out a blank form from his other pocket. He didn't have a pen. He didn't have a stamp.

He bit his thumb. Hard.

The pain was sharp, immediate. He watched the blood well up, dark red against his pale skin. He smeared the blood across the paper.

**CITATION ISSUED: STRUCTURAL OVERLOAD.** **TARGET: AUDITOR AVATAR.** **SEVERITY: CRITICAL.** **ACTION: IMMEDIATE CESSATION OF MOVEMENT.**

The System processed the input. It looked at the blood. It looked at the sludge. It looked at the spirit level.

The spirit level showed the bubble off-center. The floor was tilting. Just a fraction. But enough.

The System, bound by the letter of the law, accepted the citation. Gerald had provided evidence.

The level.

The blood. The proximity.

The sludge-shape froze, which did not stop because it wanted to. It stopped because the System locked its kinetic energy. The molecules of the sewage were forced to hold their position relative to the floor. The momentum was arrested.

**"VIOLATION CONFIRMED. DEBUFF APPLIED: KINETIC FREEZE."**

The green glow intensified. The sludge-shape hardened. It turned into a statue of black ice and waste. It was stuck.

Gerald exhaled. His hands were trembling.

Not from fear, but from the sheer effort of holding his position against the inertia of the trapped mass.

If he let go, the floor might crack.

If the floor cracked, the library above would collapse.

"Nice work," Kael whispered. He sounded nauseous. "Did you just freeze it with a spirit level?"

"I cited it," Gerald corrected. "The System enforced the citation. I merely provided the metric."

He looked at the statue. It was ugly. It was dangerous.

But it was compliant. For now.

A notification pinged.

**CITATION APPEAL WINDOW OPEN.** **TARGET HAS FILED AN APPEAL.**

The sludge-shape did not move, but the green text swirled into a new configuration.

**"APPEAL ACCEPTED. HEARING SCHEDULED: INSTANT."**

The room changed.

The damp darkness of the sub-basement dissolved. The smell of sewage was replaced by the scent of old paper and dust. The walls shifted, becoming tall bookshelves stretching into a void that had no ceiling. The floor became polished mahogany.

Gerald stood in a library. A vast, impossible library.

Across from him stood the Auditor. But it was no longer a sludge-shape. It was a figure in a grey suit, faceless, holding a gavel made of bone.

"Gerald Hutchins," the Auditor said.

Its voice was the sound of pages turning.

"You have cited a System Agent for Structural Overload. The Agent is non-physical.

Therefore, it has no mass.

Therefore, it cannot overload a structure. Your citation is void.

"

Gerald looked around the hearing chamber. He recognized the layout. It was the Ashford Municipal Library's main reading room. The System was using his memories to construct the venue.

It was using his profession to trap him.

"You are correct," Gerald said. His voice echoed in the vast space. "The Agent, in its abstract form, has no mass."

The Auditor raised its gavel.

"Then the citation is invalid. Penalty: Core Suspension."

"However," Gerald continued.

He walked forward. His footsteps were loud on the mahogany.

"The Agent was manifesting through a physical medium.

The sludge. The sewage.

That medium has mass.

That medium displaced air.

That medium exerted hydrostatic pressure on the floor.

"

The Auditor paused. The gavel hovered.

"Did you not cite the *Agent*?" Gerald asked. "Or did you cite the *presence*?"

"I cited the Agent," Gerald said. "But the Agent utilized a physical conduit. The conduit violated the structural code. The citation applies to the violation, not the entity."

The Auditor lowered the gavel. It tilted its head.

"Semantics," it said.

"Compliance," Gerald corrected. "You are arguing logic. I am arguing code.

The code states that any object exceeding the load rating of a floor must be removed or reinforced. The sludge was not removed.

The floor was not reinforced.

The violation persists.

"

The Auditor looked at the bookshelves. It seemed to be searching for a precedent.

**"QUERY: IS THE SEWAGE A BUILDING MATERIAL?"**

"In this context," Gerald said, "it is a fluid transfer system component.

It is part of the plumbing infrastructure. Plumbing adds load.

You added load.

You did not obtain a permit for the additional load.

"

The Auditor's faceless head nodded slowly.

**"PRELIMINARY FINDING: CITATION VALID. BUT... THE LOAD WAS TRANSIENT. IT WAS NOT PERMANENT."**

"Transient loads are accounted for in the Live Load calculation," Gerald said. "Residential live load is forty pounds per square foot.

The sludge was three feet deep across a four-foot diameter area, which is approximately seven hundred pounds.

Spread over twelve square feet, that is fifty-eight pounds per square foot.

"

He paused. He let the number hang in the air.

"Fifty-eight. The limit is forty. You exceeded the live load by forty-five percent.

That is not transient. That is a structural failure hazard.

"

The Auditor's gavel clicked against its palm.

**"CALCULATION ACCEPTED.

BUT... THE AGENT IS IMMUNE TO PHYSICAL DEBUFFS. THE STRUCTURAL DAMAGE WAS NOT APPLIED TO THE AGENT.

IT WAS APPLIED TO THE DUNGEON.

"**

Gerald smiled. It was a thin, dry smile.

"Exactly."

He looked at the Auditor.

"The citation was not meant to hurt you. It was meant to prove that your manifestation method was unsafe.

I have now logged a violation against the Punsing Garden for *Unsafe Conduit Usage*.

The Punsing Garden is responsible for the Agent. The Agent is responsible for the conduit. The conduit is responsible for the load.

"

**"LOGGING: PUNSING GARDEN. CATEGORY: INFRASTRUCTURE SAFETY. PENALTY: -15% REGEN RATE FOR ALL SPAWNS FOR 24 HOURS."**

The Auditor stiffened.

"That is a minor penalty," the Auditor said.

"It is a precedent," Gerald said. "Next time I see your Agent, I will cite it for *Electrical Grounding*. Because that sludge was conductive. And your lightning attacks are not grounded."

The Auditor's face dissolved into shadow.

**"HEARING CLOSED. APPEAL DENIED. CITATION UPHELD."**

The library vanished.

Gerald was back in the sub-basement. The sludge-shape was gone. The drain was quiet.

Kael was on his knees, retching.

Gerald wiped the blood from his thumb. He checked his pockets. The blank forms were gone. The pen was still in the sewer.

He walked to the drain. He looked down into the darkness.

He could hear something moving down there. Not sludge. Something with steps. Heavy, wet steps.

**"NEW THREAT DETECTED. CORE 994 (PUNSING GARDEN) IS SENDING A SECONDARY TEAM. ESTIMATED ARRIVAL: 3 MINUTES."**

Gerald looked at Kael.

"Kael. Can you stand?"

Kael nodded, wiping his mouth.

"What did you do?"

"I created a liability," Gerald said. "The Punsing Garden's dungeon is now structurally compromised in the eyes of the System. If they send more agents through the sewers, they will be citing themselves. They will be triggering their own safety protocols."

Kael stared at him.

"You're turning their dungeon against them?"

"I am turning their *infrastructure* against them," Gerald said. "They relied on the sewers. I made the sewers illegal."

A sound came from the drain. A hiss.

**"WARNING: ELECTRICAL SURGE DETECTED IN SEWER LINES. CORE 994 IS ATTEMPTING TO ELECTROCUTE THE PATH."**

Gerald's eyes widened. He looked at the puddles on the floor.

Water, and the floor was wet from the battle. The sludge had left a residue. The water was conductive.

And the Punsing Garden was sending electricity through the water.

Gerald looked at the electrical panel behind him. He looked at the water on the floor. He looked at Kael, who was standing in a puddle.

"Move," Gerald said.

"What?"

"Move! Now!"

Gerald dove. Not away from the panel, but *into* it. He slammed his hand against the main breaker.

The lights flickered.

**"CIRCUIT OVERLOAD. BREAKER TRIPPED."**

The hum died. The water stopped glowing.

Silence.

Kael was safe. But Gerald was in the dark.

A new notification appeared.

**"ALERT: INSPECTOR GENERAL AUDIT INITIATED."** **"TRIGGER: UNAUTHORIZED MODIFICATION OF UTILITY INFRASTRUCTURE."** **"STATUS: PENDING. TIME REMAINING: 10 MINUTES."**

Gerald stood in the darkness. The breaker was off. The lights were off.

But the drain was still open.

And something was climbing out of it, which was not sludge. It was not a suit.

It was a hand. A human hand, made of roots and mud, gripping the edge of the grate.

Then another.

A face emerged. It was a woman. Her eyes were hollow, filled with dirt. She wore the uniform of the Punsing Garden's dungeon staff.

She looked at Gerald. She did not look angry. She looked tired.

"Mr. Hutchins," she said.

"The Inspector General is here.

He is in the lobby of the library.

He is asking for you.

"

Gerald felt a cold knot in his stomach.

"He is asking for me?"

"He is citing *you*," the woman said. "For *Obstruction of Justice*."

She pulled herself up. The grate clattered.

"And he brought the police," she added. "Real police. With warrants."

Gerald looked at the breaker. He looked at the woman.

"I have not committed a crime," Gerald said.

"You broke the law," the woman said. "You broke the code."

Gerald realized then that the System and the City were starting to merge.

The Inspector General was not just a System entity anymore. He had a badge.

Gerald walked to the stairs. He climbed them, one by one.

At the top, he pushed open the door to the library basement exit.

The hallway was bright. Too bright.

Standing there were three men in suits. They held clipboards. They held badges.

The tallest one held up a warrant.

"Gerald Hutchins," the man said. "You are under arrest for endangering public infrastructure."

Gerald looked at the warrant. He looked at the man.

"I have a right to an attorney," Gerald said.

"You have a right to a hearing," the man said. "And you have a Compliance Rating of 47. That makes you a flight risk."

Gerald looked past the man. Through the glass doors of the library, he could see the street.

And on the street, the Punsing Garden's dungeon entrance was glowing, which was expanding.

The sewers were bursting.

Black water was flooding the streets.

And in the water, thousands of tiny, glowing citations were floating.

Gerald had not just cited the dungeon. He had cited the city.

And the city was responding.
