Chapter 251: Form 11-B

# Chapter 251

The water rose.

It did not surge like a tide or crash like a wave. It simply climbed, an inch per second, defying the laws of hydraulics that Gerald had spent three decades enforcing. The level marked itself in the mud, a wet line creeping toward the paralyzed goblin's chest.

Gerald did not back away. He stepped forward.

**FORM 11-B: APPLICATION FOR LEGALIZATION OF UNLICENSED ENTITY.**

The text hovered in his vision, translucent and glowing. It required three signatures.

The first was his own.

The second required the entity's consent. The third required a witness.

Gerald looked at the Inspector General. The figure of light and parchment stood motionless, its blank face angled toward the rising flood. It waited. The System waited.

"You cannot sign for it," Gerald said. His voice was steady now, the panic drained out by the sheer weight of bureaucratic necessity. "I need its signature."

The Inspector General did not speak. It merely tilted its head. The sound of paper rubbing against paper echoed in the damp air.

Gerald walked to the edge of the water.

The cold bit through his trousers. He knelt again, this time not in fear, but in inspection.

He reached into his pocket and pulled out a grease pencil.

It was the only tool he had.

"Hey," Gerald said, speaking to the darkness beneath the surface. "I know you're there."

Silence. The water lapped against his knees.

"I have a Form 11-B," Gerald continued. "It requires a wet signature.

Literally.

And I need you to admit you are not a natural formation.

I need you to admit you are a Core."

The water rippled. A pale hand emerged from the murk, fingers splayed, hovering inches from the goblin's face. It did not touch. It hovered.

"If you remain silent," Gerald said, checking his watch, "I am forced to classify you as a Structural Hazard. That means I have to demolish the entire sub-basement. Do you want that? I have the permits."

The hand retracted. The water stopped rising.

For a heartbeat, the room was still. Then, a voice echoed. It was not loud. It was the sound of grinding stones, of roots snapping underground.

*You are small.*

Gerald did not flinch. He held up the grease pencil.

"Size is irrelevant to jurisdiction," Gerald said. "Code is code. You are occupying space without a Certificate of Occupancy.

I am offering you a path to compliance. Adverse Possession is a legal doctrine.

It states that if you occupy land openly, notoriously, and continuously for a period of time, you gain title.

I am the current occupant. You are the challenger. But the clock is running.

The Audit is live.

If you do not sign, you are evicted. And eviction for an entity with no mass means dissolution."

The water swirled. A face coalesced. It was not a face of flesh, but of sediment and rot. Eyes like black coins stared at Gerald.

*Why should I trust your paper?*

Gerald tapped the grease pencil against the concrete wall. *Click. Click. Click.*

"Because," Gerald said, "I am the only one here who knows how to fill out the back page.

You don't. You're a dungeon. You spawn monsters.

You don't spawn lawyers. You need me."

The Inspector General's parchment face smoothed itself. It wrote in the air:

**SUBJECT: CORE DESIGNATION.** **PENDING: WITNESS SIGNATURE.**

Gerald looked at the paralyzed goblin. The creature's eyes were wide, terrified. He could not sign. He was a spawn, not a citizen.

"I need a witness," Gerald said.

The monster's face dissolved back into the slurry.

Then, a cough.

It came from the ventilation duct above.

A grate rattled.

A small, scaly hand pulled itself up through the mesh. Then another.

A kobold dropped onto the concrete block. It was small, green, and shaking.

It looked at Gerald. It looked at the water. It looked at the Inspector General.

"Gerald?" the kobold squeaked.

Gerald recognized him. This was Snip. Snip had been in the dungeon for three weeks. Snip was a Level 1 mob, useless in combat, but excellent at cleaning the grime off the handrails Gerald had installed last week.

"Snip," Gerald said. "Are you a citizen of this dungeon?"

Snip looked at the floating Form 11-B. He looked at the glowing text. He looked at Gerald's tie.

"I clean the floors," Snip said.

"Then you are part of the infrastructure," Gerald said. "Infrastructure is public domain. I am invoking the Right of Adverse Possession on your behalf, as a representative of the dungeon's maintenance staff. Do you consent to witness the legalization of the entity known as 'The Damp'?"

Snip blinked. His tongue darted out to lick a piece of dust off his nose.

"The Damp?" Snip asked.

"It's a placeholder name," Gerald said. "We can change it later on Form 11-C. For now, do you consent?"

The water rose again. Faster this time. The black face returned, closer. The rot smell intensified, thick and cloying.

*He is not enough,* the voice from the water said. *He is dirt.*

Snip looked at Gerald. Gerald did not look at the kobold. He looked at the Inspector General.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** **WITNESS INSUFFICIENT STATUS.** **RECOMMENDATION: ESCALATE TO CORE-LEVEL TESTIMONY.**

Gerald closed his eyes. He breathed in. He breathed out.

He opened his eyes and looked directly at the black face in the water.

"I object," Gerald said.

The Inspector General paused. The air around the figure shimmered.

**QUERY: BASIS FOR OBJECTION?**

"The witness is a spawn," Gerald said. "Spawns do not have legal standing.

This is a known loophole in the Code, Section 4, Paragraph B.

Spawns are property. Property cannot witness contracts. However, I am the Core.

I am the landlord.

I am testifying. My testimony is sufficient."

Gerald raised his right hand.

"I, Gerald Hutchins, Core Designation GH-001, testify under oath that the entity known as 'The Damp' has been present in Sector 4 for a continuous period exceeding the statutory minimum for Adverse Possession. I testify that I have failed to evict due to administrative oversight, not legal incapacity. I testify that I am willing to issue a temporary occupancy permit, contingent on immediate compliance with sanitation standards."

The water froze.

The Inspector General's face rippled. The word **INSPECTOR** vanished for a second, replaced by **JUDGE**, then snapped back.

**ACCEPTING TESTIMONY.** **VERIFICATION REQUIRED: PROOF OF OCCUPANCY.**

"Proof," Gerald said.

He pointed to the goblin on the block. "That creature has been here for two hours. The water has been here for three days.

I have measured the moisture levels. They are constant.

The occupation is open and notorious.

Everyone in this room, including the monster, knows the water is here."

The black face in the water tilted.

*You want to own me?*

"I want to regulate you," Gerald said. "There is a difference.

Ownership implies control. Regulation implies standards. If you sign this form, you become a registered feature of the dungeon.

You gain the benefits of compliance: reduced damage from holy spells, increased regeneration in wet environments, and immunity to the System's 'Unlicensed Entity' purge."

The water receded. An inch. Two inches.

The goblin on the block groaned. His eyes fluttered. He was waking up.

Gerald looked at Snip.

"Sign it, Snip."

Snip looked at the grease pencil. He looked at the floating line where the witness signature should go. He looked at Gerald.

"If I sign," Snip whispered, "do I get a raise?"

"No," Gerald said.

"Then I sign."

Snip scrambled up the concrete block. He grabbed the grease pencil from Gerald's hand. He held it awkwardly, like a dagger. He slashed a jagged mark across the empty line.

The mark glowed red.

**WITNESS SIGNATURE: VALID.**

The Inspector General nodded. The parchment body unfolded, revealing a long, scroll-like tail that wrapped around the room.

**SUBJECT: THE DAMP.** **STATUS: LEGALIZED AS FEATURE.** **CLASSIFICATION: HAZARDOUS (CATEGORY 3).** **REQUIRED COMPLIANCE: DE-HUMIDIFICATION SYSTEMS WITHIN 48 HOURS. WARNING ISSUED. VIOLATION CITATION PENDING.**

A new window popped up in Gerald's mind.

**CITATION ISSUED TO: THE DAMP.** **FINE: 500 MANA.** **DEBUFF: MOISTURE CONTENT -10%.**

The water in the room dropped another three inches. The black face looked... confused. It looked smaller.

*What did you do?*

"I cited you," Gerald said.

He wiped the mud off his hands onto his trousers. "You're a hazard. Hazards pay fines.

You didn't have the mana to pay, so you're taking the damage from the environment itself.

The air is drier. You are weaker."

The Inspector General turned to leave. It dissolved into light, scattering like dandelion seeds.

Gerald stood up. His legs were numb. He looked at Snip.

"Go clean the water," Gerald said. "Use the mop. Do not use the bucket. The bucket is not rated for Category 3 Hazards."

Snip nodded and scurried away.

Gerald looked at the goblin. The creature was trying to sit up. He was alive. He was free of the paralysis, though he was in pain.

The goblin looked at Gerald. He did not attack. He did not flee. He looked at the glowing text that still hovered in the air.

"You are a monster," the goblin said. His voice was raspy.

Gerald looked at him.

"No," Gerald said. "I am an inspector."

The goblin shivered.

Gerald walked to the wall.

He ran his hand along the concrete. It was damp. Slick.

He pulled a tape measure from his belt.

He measured the distance from the water line to the ceiling.

"Four feet, six inches," he muttered. "Too low for a Category 3 Hazard. The ceiling needs to be raised. Or the floor needs to be sealed."

He wrote it down on a notepad.

**TASK 1: SEAL FLOORING.** **TASK 2: INSTALL DE-HUMIDIFIER.** **TASK 3: CHECK VENTILATION GRATES FOR SNAKE ENTRY.**

He felt a pull. A hook in his gut.

The System was reacting. His Compliance Rating was rising. But so was his visibility.

A new notification flashed in his vision.

**INCOMING MESSAGE:** **FROM: CORE 'IRONFIST' (SECTOR 2).** **MESSAGE: You cited my fire trap.

My lava golem burned.

I know who you are. I am coming.

Bring your clipboard. I will break it.**

Gerald stared at the message. Ironfist was a Tier 2 Dungeon.

They had a boss-level golem.

They had stone skin. They had fire immunity.

Gerald looked at his tape measure. He looked at his grease pencil.

He did not feel fear. He felt a tightening in his chest, a familiar, nauseating sense of obligation.

He had to prepare. He had to check the load-bearing walls. He had to ensure the egress routes were clear.

If Ironfist came, he wouldn't bring an army. He would bring a demolition crew.

Gerald opened his inventory. He selected **Form 11-B: Notice of Intent to Demolish.**

He had never issued one. He didn't know if he had the authority.

He looked at the paralyzed goblin, who was now limping toward the exit, avoiding the puddles.

"You," Gerald said.

The goblin stopped.

"Leave a review," Gerald said. "If you enjoy your stay, note the cleanliness. If you do not, file a complaint. I will be reading them."

The goblin ran.

Gerald turned to the dark corridor leading deeper into the dungeon. The air was cold. The lights flickered.

From the shadows, a voice spoke. It was not the water this time. It was not the Inspector General.

It was a new Core. A whisper in the walls.

*We see you, Inspector.*

Gerald raised his tape measure.

"I see you too," Gerald said.

He measured the corridor.
