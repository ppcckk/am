Chapter 38: The Dungeon Audits Him

# Chapter 38

Gerald spawned a second cubic foot of gravel and immediately regretted the geometry.

The pile sat in what had been the east corridor. It was not a foundation. It was not a wall. It was a mound of angular aggregate that the System had deposited with the same indifferent precision it applied to everything, as though the act of creation were a clerical error it was merely transcribing.

He checked his interface. Compliance Rating: 4/100. The gravel had added one point. One point for having a floor where there had been none.

He did the math again. Twenty-three hours and twelve minutes until Administrative Closure.

Five hours and fourteen minutes until the Inspector General arrived, and he needed to reach 10/100 to halt the closure. He had gone from 3 to 4 with a cubic foot of gravel. At that rate, he would need seven more cubic feet.

That was not the problem.

The problem was that a floor without walls, without egress, without ventilation, without fire suppression, without lighting, without any of the fourteen categories the System used to calculate Compliance, would never reach 10. A floor was one category.

He needed at least three categories occupied and compliant to reach the threshold.

He had six hours to build a room.

Not a dungeon. A room. One room. Properly constructed.

Gerald pulled up his spawn list and started typing.

Spawn: Concrete. Volume: 2 cubic feet.

The gravel shifted. It did not become concrete. The System deposited a slab of grey material at the base of the pile.

It was not cured. It was not reinforced.

It was wet and soft and Gerald could see the aggregate showing through the surface like a bad plaster job.

He adjusted. Spawn: Concrete, Type II, 4000 PSI. Volume: 2 cubic feet.

The slab hardened, which was not perfect. It was not poured by a crew with a pump truck and a screed and a vibrator.

It was spawned, which meant it existed in a state of theoretical perfection that the System did not bother to enforce at the microscopic level. Gerald could see hairline cracks at the edges. He could see the air voids.

He ignored them. He was not building a parking garage. He was building a room that met minimum occupancy standards.

Spawn: Wall assembly. Dimension: 10 feet by 8 feet by 8 feet. Material: CMU, 8-inch block, reinforced.

The wall appeared. It rose from the concrete slab with the speed of a time-lapse video, blocks stacking themselves, mortar filling the joints, rebar sliding into the cavities.

It was not beautiful, which was not a finished wall, and was a wall that existed in a state of structural sufficiency.

Gerald checked the interface. Compliance Rating: 6/100.

Two points. The wall had added two points. One for the wall itself, one for the fact that it was load-bearing and met structural code.

He needed three more points.

Egress. Ventilation.

Fire suppression, and he could not do all three. He could not do all three in six hours with a spawn list that included three mold spores and a rat.

He stopped typing. He looked at the wall. He looked at the interface.

Then he opened the Registration tab, which was a tab he had never used, and the Registration tab contained the official footprint of his dungeon as recorded by the System.

Four thousand square feet.

The sub-basement beneath the Ashford Municipal Library.

Every square foot of it was registered, which meant every square foot of it was subject to Compliance Rating calculation.

Gerald selected the footprint. He dragged the boundary inward.

The System flashed a warning.

Footprint modification requires minimum viable structure (one habitable room, minimum 80 square feet, with compliant egress). Confirm?

Gerald confirmed.

The footprint shrank. Four thousand square feet became one hundred and twenty square feet. The registered area was now the concrete slab and the CMU wall and the space between them.

Everything else, the rubble, the collapsed corridors, the torn-out sprinkler pipes, the knob-and-tube wiring still smoldering in the east wing, was now unregistered. Outside the boundary.

Not his dungeon anymore.

Compliance Rating recalculated.

11/100.

The Administrative Closure timer stopped.

Gerald exhaled. His chest did not move, because he did not have a chest, but the System registered the equivalent of an exhale as a minor fluctuation in ambient pressure and logged it.

He had done it. He had shrunk the building to meet code. A compliant room. That was all he needed.

He opened his spawn list again. He had work to do. The room needed a second wall, a roof, a door, a window for natural light, a ventilation opening.

He could do this, and he had done this. He had written three hundred and forty-seven inspection reports in his career that had ended with a contractor being told to tear out a wall and start over because it was not load-bearing.

He knew what load-bearing looked like.

He knew what egress looked like. He knew that a door needed to swing in the direction of egress and that the clear width needed to be thirty-two inches and that the hardware needed to be operable with one hand without tight grasping, pinching, or twisting of the wrist.

He spawned a second wall.

Spawn: Door assembly. Dimension: 36 inches by 80 inches. Swing: egress direction. Hardware: single-lever handle.

The door appeared. It was not installed.

It was simply there, standing in the gap between the two walls, unattached to a frame.

Gerald fixed that.

Spawn: Door frame. Material: Steel, 16-gauge.

The frame slid around the door. The door swung. It swung correctly. It swung in the direction of egress.

Compliance Rating: 14/100, and he was above the threshold. He had margin. He kept working.

Spawn: Ventilation opening. Dimension: 12 inches by 12 inches. Location: Upper wall, 7 feet above finished floor.

The opening appeared. Air moved through it. The System's ventilation metric ticked up.

Compliance Rating: 17/100.

Gerald allowed himself a thought that was not procedural. It was not a feeling, exactly. It was the recognition that he had done this before.

Not the spawning. The shrinking.

He had done this before, in 1997, with a condemned apartment building on Elm Street.

The owner had refused to repair the roof. Gerald had not condemned the building. He had de-registered the upper two floors, reclassified the ground floor as a commercial unit, and issued a conditional certificate of occupancy that kept the owner's tax status intact while forcing the roof repair within ninety days.

The owner had called him a fascist.

Gerald had cited him for unauthorized signage, and the System did not have a tax status. But it had a footprint. And a footprint could be modified.

He was halfway through installing the roof when his interface flashed a notification he had not seen before.

New registered structure detected within proximity of Core footprint. Distance: 47 feet. Core designation: Thirty-Nine.

Gerald looked up.

Through the ventilation opening he had just spawned, he could see the rubble of what had been his east wing. And in that rubble, something was moving. Not a rat.

Not mold. Something larger.

Something that was building.

Core Thirty-Nine was moving into his space. The space he had de-registered. The space that was no longer his.

He watched it for thirty seconds, which was fast. Aggressive.

The System deposited structures at a rate Gerald had never seen. Walls rose and fell and rose again, as though the core were experimenting. Spawn entities appeared and disappeared.

The air smelled of sulfur and wet stone.

Gerald checked his interface. The new core's footprint was overlapping the boundary of his registered area by three feet.

He opened his citation form.

Then he stopped, and he looked at the timer. Four hours and thirty-one minutes until the Inspector General. He did not have time to fight Core Thirty-Nine.

He did not have the spawn capacity.

He had three mold spores, a rat, and a hydrological feature that was currently flooding the street above.

He closed the citation form. He kept building.

The roof went up, which was flat, which was wrong for this climate, but it was structurally sound and it existed. Compliance Rating: 22/100. He had a room.

A real room. Four walls, a roof, a door that swung correctly, a ventilation opening, a concrete floor, a lighting fixture he had spawned as a simple incandescent bulb that provided the required foot-candles.

He sat in the middle of it. There was no chair. He sat on the concrete.

After four hours and nine minutes, he accessed his spawn list to add a second room.

The notification came at three hours and forty-seven minutes.

Inspector General arrival: Early. Current time to arrival: 1 hour, 12 minutes.

Gerald read it twice, and the System did not adjust arrival times.

The Inspector General did not arrive early. He checked the notification again.

It was not an adjustment. It was a change of status. The Inspector General had been reassigned.

Its current task was no longer "finalize closure." Its current task was "audit citation history."

Gerald felt something he could not name, and he had cited eleven dungeons in the past month. He had won two Hearings. He had issued one Ejectment that had destroyed his own dungeon.

The System had a record of all of it.

One hour and twelve minutes.

He spawned a second wall. He spawned a second door. He spawned a window, because the System required natural light for habitable rooms and he had forgotten. Compliance Rating: 26/100.

Forty-eight minutes, and he was building fast. He had never built fast.

Thirty-one years of inspection had taught him that speed was the enemy of compliance.

Speed produced shortcuts. Shortcuts produced violations. Violations produced his job.

But the System did not care about his career philosophy. The System cared about the number.

Thirty minutes, and he had two rooms.

A corridor between them.

The corridor was not wide enough. He widened it.

Thirty-six inches. Compliant.

Twenty minutes, and he spawned a fire extinguisher. It was a real fire extinguisher, a 2A10B-C unit, mounted at the correct height, with the correct signage above it. Compliance Rating: 31/100.

Ten minutes.

He checked the boundary. Core Thirty-Nine was still building.

It was close.

Too close. Its structures were within fifteen feet of his registered footprint. It was not encroaching.

It was not citing him.

It was simply building, and its building was loud, and the sound of it was the sound of a thing that did not care whether Gerald was alive or dead.

Five minutes.

Gerald stood in his corridor, and he looked at his rooms. They were small. They were ugly.

They were the ugliest rooms he had ever built, and he had once inspected a basement apartment in which the landlord had used particleboard for a load-bearing wall and called it "rustic."

But they were compliant.

Three minutes.

The air changed, which was not a temperature change.

It was a pressure change, a shift in the ambient atmosphere that the System used to announce the presence of an authority figure. Gerald felt it in his interface.

A new entity was registering.

Inspector General, which did not appear as a person. It appeared as a notification.

Inspector General registered at Core location. Inspection type: Citation History Audit. Scope: All citations issued by Core Gerald Hutchins in current cycle.

Gerald opened his citation log. Eleven entries.

Two Hearings won.

One Ejectment. One dungeon destroyed. His own.

The Inspector General did not comment. It did not ask questions. It simply read.

Then a second notification.

Inspector General action: Footprint expansion authorized. Reason: Citation history indicates structural instability at boundary. Expansion required to assess full impact radius.

Gerald's registered footprint expanded.

One hundred and twenty square feet became four hundred. Then eight hundred.

Then two thousand.

Then four thousand, and the rubble came back. The collapsed corridors.

The torn handrails.

The ruptured sprinkler pipes.

The knob-and-tube wiring, still smoldering. All of it, re-registered.

All of it, his again.

Compliance Rating: 3/100.

Administrative Closure initiated. Permanent deletion of registered structures: 24 hours.

Gerald stared at the timer.

Then a third notification.

Inspector General action: Citation history audit complete. Finding: Core Gerald Hutchins has issued eleven Citations with an average severity of 7.2; this exceeds the threshold for Inspector General intervention. New action: Summary Judgment.

Gerald had never heard of Summary Judgment. He searched his interface. He searched the System's help files. He searched the compliance manual, which he had read twice, cover to cover, in the first week after his registration.

It was not in the manual.

Summary Judgment: Authority to bypass Hearing process and apply permanent debuff to target Core without appeal. Applied when Citation history indicates pattern of regulatory abuse.

Gerald looked at the notification. He looked at his rooms, and looked at the four thousand square feet of rubble that was now his again.

The Inspector General had not come to audit his dungeon, which had come to audit him.

His dungeon had come to audit him.
