Chapter 109: Illegally Renovating

# Chapter 109 The knocking was not tentative. It was rhythmic, heavy, and possessed of the bureaucratic weight that Gerald Hutchins had spent thirty-one years learning to dread more than any dragon’s breath.

He stared at the steel door of his sub-basement office. The wood was old, oak, but reinforced with a modern composite plating that violated municipal fire codes by at least three inches of thickness. That was a separate issue, one he would address later. Right now, the issue was the knocking.

Kael stood beside him, hand resting on the hilt of a sword that looked too ornamental for a narrow utility corridor. The young adventurer shifted his weight, eyes darting toward the glowing blue text hovering in Gerald’s vision. The Counter-Appeal window pulsed with a steady, irritating amber light.

"Open it," Gerald said. His voice was flat. He didn’t look at Kael. He looked at the door.

"Gerald," Kael whispered. "You just cited a Core for structural interference.

You fabricated evidence.

If they’re sending lawyers, they might send guards. Or worse, they might send a Auditor."

"Open.

The. Door." Gerald pointed at the handle.

"A closed door in a public-facing dungeon entrance is a Code 404 violation for accessibility.

I will not have that on the record during a Hearing."

Kael moved. He slid the bolt. He pulled the handle.

The door swung inward.

Standing in the library’s sub-basement hallway was not a guard. It was not a dragon.

It was a figure in a sharp, charcoal-grey suit that fit too well, the fabric shimmering with a faint, geometric pattern that hurt to look at directly.

The figure held a briefcase in one hand and a leather-bound folder in the other.

Its face was human enough, but the skin was too smooth, the eyes too symmetrical. It possessed the uncanny valley quality of something that had been optimized for presentation than existence.

It bowed. A precise, ninety-degree angle.

"Mr. Hutchins," the figure said. Its voice sounded like dry paper sliding over stone.

"I am Solicitor Vane. I represent Core ID 894-B, designated Torvald.

I have been summoned for the Immediate Hearing regarding Citation #4491-REV-1."

Gerald felt a physical nausea rise in his throat. It was the same sensation he got when he saw a permit posted on a door without a permit number, or when he saw a staircase without a handrail. This was not a monster. This was a professional.

"I am prepared to enter the Hearing," Gerald said. He straightened his tie. He checked his clipboard, though it was empty.

He filled it with imaginary notes.

"I assume you have filed the proper notice of appearance with the Inspector General?"

"Filed and stamped," Vane said. He stepped forward. The floorboards did not creak under his weight. "May I enter your premises?"

Gerald hesitated, and the door was open. That was a security risk. But refusing entry to a legal representative during an active System Hearing was a procedural error that would result in an automatic forfeiture.

He knew this. The System’s Terms of Service, Section 14, Paragraph B, Subsection 3, outlined the rights of appealants.

"Enter," Gerald said. "But remove your shoes. Carpet cleaning is pending."

Vane paused, and he looked at Gerald’s shoes.

He looked at his own polished leather oxfords. He smiled.

It was a thin, predatory expression.

"The System does not regulate footwear, Mr. Hutchins. It regulates structures."

"Then take them off, or you’re tracking mud onto non-compliant flooring," Gerald said. He picked up a rag from his desk.

He wiped the doorframe.

"If you track mud, you violate the sanitation standards for Category C Dungeons. I will cite you for creating a slip hazard."

Vane’s smile vanished. He slipped off his shoes. He placed them neatly by the threshold. He walked into the dungeon in his socks.

The air in the sub-basement felt heavier. The humidity had risen. Gerald could feel the System’s attention narrowing, focusing like a lens, and the Inspector General’s message still hung in the air: *YOU ARE NOW ON THE WATCHLIST.*

Vane walked to the center of the room, and the room was sparse. Concrete walls.

Exposed piping.

A fluorescent light that flickered at a frequency known to cause migraines in humans and mild disorientation in magical creatures. It was up to code, technically. The code allowed for 10% flicker in non-public areas.

Gerald had calculated this to the decimal.

"I have reviewed the evidence submitted for Citation #4491," Vane said.

He opened his briefcase, and he pulled out a holographic projection. It hovered in the air, spinning slowly.

It showed Torvald’s dungeon layout. It was a labyrinth of obsidian and lava. Beautiful.

Terrifying. Illegal.

"You claim," Vane continued, his voice dropping to a conversational tone, "that the hydro-static pressure drop in Torvald’s upper aquifer was caused by an external conduit linked to your library sub-basement. You presented data showing a correlation between your water loss and his pressure loss. You argued that this constituted Structural Interference."

"Yes," Gerald said.

"The argument was flawed," Vane said.

"Correlation is not causation. The System does not accept circumstantial evidence in Structural Interference claims. You needed proof of a physical connection.

A pipe.

A tube. A magical binding."

"I provided the blue thread," Gerald said.

"The blue thread was a visual manifestation of a magical resonance, not a physical conduit," Vane said.

He tapped the hologram. The image zoomed in on Torvald’s dungeon. "Torvald’s dungeon is sealed.

His aquifer is isolated. Your dungeon draws water from the municipal supply.

There is no connection.

Therefore, your citation was based on fabricated evidence.

Fabrication is a violation of the Core Code of Ethics. It is grounds for license revocation."

Gerald felt the blood drain from his face. He looked at the hologram. Vane was right.

The blue thread was magical, not physical. Gerald had used it as a visual aid, but the System’s logic engine might classify it as non-physical.

If the System deemed the evidence inadmissible, Gerald would lose the appeal.

Worse, he would be flagged for Fraud.

Kael shifted nervously. "Gerald, do something."

Gerald closed his eyes. He thought about the municipal code. He thought about the definition of a conduit.

*A conduit is a channel for the flow of fluid or energy.*

The System defined magic as energy.

Water was matter. But in a dungeon, mana was often treated as a fluid.

Had Gerald missed the distinction?

No. The citation was for *Hydro-static* pressure. That was water.

But Vane was playing a game. He was trying to force Gerald into a corner.

If Gerald lost, the Inspector General would audit him.

If Gerald was audited, his Compliance Rating would be scrutinized.

And Gerald’s rating was barely holding together.

"Gerald," Kael hissed. "He’s baiting you."

Gerald opened his eyes. He looked at Vane. He looked at the hologram. He looked at the flickering light.

He walked over to the wall. He placed his hand on the concrete.

"Mr. Vane," Gerald said. "You claim there is no physical connection."

"The System confirms this," Vane said. "The scan of your dungeon shows no pipes leading to Torvald’s territory."

"Correct," Gerald said. "There are no pipes."

Vane smiled again. "And yet, you cited him. You lied."

"No," Gerald said. "I cited him for *Structural Interference*. I did not claim a pipe. I claimed that his structure interfered with mine."

Vane’s smile faltered. "Define interference."

Gerald pulled out a tape measure. He clicked it open. He measured the distance between the library wall and the outer boundary of his dungeon’s footprint.

"Under the Ashford Municipal Code, Section 12, a structure is considered to interfere with an adjacent structure if it causes *vibrational transfer* that exceeds acceptable decibel levels," Gerald said.

Vane blinked. "This is a dungeon, not a residential zone."

"The System uses the host city’s municipal code as the baseline for structural integrity," Gerald said. He recited the code from memory. "Any vibration exceeding 45 dB at the property line is a violation.

Torvald’s lava flows cause seismic tremors. These tremors travel through the bedrock.

They hit my foundation."

He crossed to the middle of the room. He stomped his foot. The floor vibrated. The hologram wobbled.

"I measured the tremors," Gerald said. "They are 48 dB at the property line. That is a 3 dB excess."

"That is negligible," Vane said. "3 dB is within the margin of error."

"The margin of error is 1 dB," Gerald said. "I checked. I have a calibrated seismometer in my pocket."

He didn’t have a seismometer. He had a phone. But he knew the System accepted personal tools if they were 'calibrated'. He had calibrated his phone by measuring the vibration of the library’s HVAC system, which was also non-compliant.

"Show me the data," Vane said.

Gerald held up his phone. He displayed a graph. It was a fabricated graph, yes.

But it looked official, which had a stamp on it, and a fake stamp, but one that mimicked the City of Ashford’s seal perfectly.

Gerald had spent thirty years studying seals.

He could replicate them with his eyes closed.

Vane looked at the graph. He squinted.

"This graph shows a spike at 14:00," Vane said. "Your citation was filed at 14:05."

"The spike caused the pressure drop," Gerald said. "The pressure drop caused the water loss.

The water loss triggered my alarm.

I filed the citation.

The timeline is consistent."

Vane tapped the hologram. He was looking for a flaw. He was looking for a lie.

But the lie was small. It was a 3 dB difference.

It was a technicality.

"If you win on this," Vane said, "you are claiming that seismic activity is a form of structural interference."

"Is it not?" Gerald asked.

"It is," Vane admitted. "But the penalty..."

"The penalty is a 50% reduction in Physical Resistance," Gerald said. "For 24 hours. This is a temporary debuff.

It is not permanent damage.

It is a corrective measure."

"It is excessive," Vane said. "The violation is minor. The penalty is severe. This is disproportionate."

"The penalty is based on the severity of the violation," Gerald said. "The violation caused damage to my dungeon. The penalty must be proportional to the damage sustained."

"What damage?" Vane asked.

Gerald pointed to the ceiling, and a crack ran through the concrete. It was a small crack. He had noticed it three days ago.

He had put a tape over it. It was a Class 1 Crack.

Not a structural failure.

But it was a defect.

"That crack," Gerald said. "It appeared during the tremor.

It is a structural defect. I had to repair it. The cost of repair is 500 Mana.

The penalty is calculated as Mana Cost divided by Core Efficiency. My efficiency is 12%.

Torvald’s is 80%."

He did the math in his head. 500 divided by 12 was 41.6. Torvald’s efficiency was high, so the penalty should be lower. But Gerald was twisting the formula. He was using the *recipient’s* efficiency, not the *aggressor’s*.

This was a loophole. A massive one. It was the kind of loophole that only a bureaucrat who had spent decades reading fine print would know.

Vane stared at the graph. He stared at the crack. He stared at Gerald.

"You are arguing that the penalty should be higher because *you* are inefficient," Vane said.

"Correct," Gerald said.

"That is not how the formula works," Vane said.

"Read the sub-clause," Gerald said. "Section 14, Paragraph 9. 'Penalties are adjusted to ensure equivalent deterrence relative to Core Capacity.' If Torvald is at 80% capacity, a 50% debuff is barely noticeable.

If I am at 12% capacity, a 50% debuff would kill me.

Therefore, to achieve *equivalent deterrence*, the debuff must be applied to the aggressor, but scaled by the victim’s fragility."

Vane went pale. His suit seemed to tighten.

"That is... a creative interpretation," Vane said.

"It is the interpretation," Gerald said.

"I have a precedent. Citation #2203. The case of *Gloomspire vs.

Ashford*."

Vane frowned, and he searched his mental database.

Citation #2203 was obscure. It was a case from ten years ago.

A dwarf core had cited a goblin core for noise pollution; the goblin was punished because the dwarf’s dungeon was made of glass and was fragile.

Vane hesitated. He was a professional.

He knew when a fight was unwinnable, and he also knew that arguing with Gerald Hutchins was like arguing with a wall. The wall did not care. The wall just cited you.

"I move to dismiss," Vane said. "The evidence is inadmissible."

"Objection overruled," Gerald said.

He didn’t know if he could overrule him. He wasn’t the judge.

But the System was listening.

And the System liked rules. Gerald had followed the rules of the argument. He had used the code.

**SYSTEM NOTICE: HEARING IN PROGRESS.** **ARGUMENT LOGGED: VIBRATIONAL INTERFERENCE.** **EVIDENCE: SEISMIC DATA (SOURCE: PERSONAL DEVICE).** **PENALTY CALCULATION: IN PROGRESS.**

Vane’s eyes widened. He looked at Gerald.

He looked at the hologram, and he realized he had been outmaneuvered.

Not by strength, not by magic, but by pedantry.

"You win," Vane said. "But this is not over."

"It never is," Gerald said.

**VERDICT: CITATION UPHELD.** **PENALTY APPLIED: TORVALD CORE PHYSICAL RESISTANCE -50% FOR 24 HOURS.** **COST TO APPELLANT: 1000 MANA.**

The hologram vanished. Vane put on his shoes. He picked up his briefcase.

"You have made an enemy today, Mr. Hutchins," Vane said. "And you have drawn the attention of the Inspector General.

He is not amused. He is... intrigued."

"Good," Gerald said. "Intrigue is a form of engagement."

Vane walked to the door. He stopped.

"Be careful," Vane said. "The Inspector General is not a bureaucrat. He is a predator. And you have just shown him that you know how to bite."

Vane left. The door clicked shut.

Gerald exhaled, and he slumped against his desk. His hands were shaking. He looked at the crack in the ceiling.

It was still there.

"Did you just fake a seismometer reading?" Kael asked.

"I did," Gerald said. "And I faked the precedent. Citation #2203 doesn't exist."

Kael stared at him. "Gerald."

"It doesn't matter," Gerald said. "The System accepted it. The logic held."

"But the Inspector General..."

"Will audit me," Gerald said. "He will send an Inspector.

A real one.

Not a lawyer. An Auditor."

Kael’s face went white. "Auditors don't argue. They just see. They see everything."

Gerald looked at his Compliance Rating. **12/100.**

"He will see the missing handrails," Gerald said. "He will see the flickering lights. He will see the mold."

"And?" Gerald asked.

"And he will shut you down."

Gerald picked up his clipboard. He wrote.

"Then we have to fix the violations," Gerald said. "Fast."

"Fix?" Kael asked. "With what?

You have no mana.

Torvald has drained you dry.

You have three mold spores and a rat."

Gerald looked at the water leak in the corner. He looked at the rat, which was chewing on a wire.

"I have a new mechanic," Gerald said. "And I have a deadline."

He pointed to the System window.

**NEW OBJECTIVE: PRE-AUDIT COMPLIANCE DRIVE.** **TIME LIMIT: 48 HOURS.** **REWARD: IMMUNITY TO ONE AUDIT STRIKE.** **FAILURE: CORE DELETION.**

Gerald looked at Kael.

"We are going to renovate," Gerald said. "And we are going to do it illegally."
