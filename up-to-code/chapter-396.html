Chapter 396: Motion to Stay

# Chapter 396

The red light in Gerald's chest pulsed in time with the cracking masonry. It was a rhythmic, ugly throb, syncing perfectly with the widening fissure running up the central support pillar.

Kaelen's grin was sharp enough to cut glass.

"Objection," Kaelen said, his voice echoing in the unstable chamber. "Overruled by your own negligence."

Gerald did not move. His eyes tracked the dust falling from the ceiling.

Martha stepped forward, her hammer raised, but Gerald held up a hand. Not a request for help. A stop sign.

"Stop," Gerald said.

"You can't fight him," Martha hissed, glancing at the dark energy coiling around Kaelen's fingers. "Your rating is dropping. I can feel the drain. If you stay here, he'll finish the citation."

"I am not staying to fight," Gerald said. "I am staying to file."

Kaelen tilted his head, amused.

"File what? Your resignation?"

"A Motion to Stay Enforcement," Gerald said.

Silence fell over the dungeon. Even the rats in the shadows seemed to pause.

Kaelen's smile faltered for a fraction of a second.

"That isn't a thing."

Gerald turned away from the Core, walking toward the cracked pillar. He pulled a small, battered clipboard from his spatial inventory. The object clattered against his armor.

"System," Gerald said. "Query: Can a Citation be stayed if the alleged violation is concurrent with an Active Emergency Repair Order?"

A blue window shimmered into existence.

**[SYSTEM NOTICE]** **[Active Emergency Repair Orders take precedence over Routine Citations.]** **[Please provide valid Order ID.]**

Gerald looked at Kaelen.

"I don't have an ID yet," Gerald said.

"Then you're done," Kaelen said. He raised his hand. The dark energy flared. "Prepare to be cited for Failure to Appear."

"Wait," Martha said. She looked at the ceiling, then at Gerald. "Gerald, what are you doing?"

Gerald didn't answer. He ran his fingers along the crack in the pillar. The stone was warm. It vibrated with a low hum.

"System," Gerald said. "Initiate Form 9-Alpha: Emergency Structural Stabilization. Reason: Imminent Collapse due to Atmospheric Pressure Differential."

A prompt appeared.

**[WARNING: Form 9-Alpha requires a certified Professional Engineer or Master Mason signature.]** **[You are not a certified Engineer.]**

Gerald's jaw tightened. He knew he wasn't.

He was an Inspector.

Inspectors find faults. Engineers fix them. The distinction was vital.

It was the line that kept buildings from falling down and inspectors from going to jail.

But he knew the code.

"System," Gerald said. "Apply Exception Clause 44-C: Inspector Override for Life-Safety Hazards."

The window flickered.

**[Exception 44-C is reserved for Imminent Threat to Occupant Life.]** **[Current Occupants: One (Gerald Hutchins).]** **[Threat Level: Moderate.]** **[Override Denied.]**

Kaelen laughed. It was a wet, cruel sound.

"The System knows you, Gerald. It knows you're just a man with a clipboard. You can't bluff the Code."

Gerald looked at the rat scurrying near his boot. He looked at the water leak dripping from the ventilation shaft he had fixed three minutes ago.

"Martha," Gerald said. "How many people are in the library above us?"

Martha blinked.

"What? Why?"

"How many?"

"Maybe twenty. A story time is ending. Parents. Children."

Gerald nodded. He turned back to the System window.

"Update Occupancy Count," Gerald said.

**[Updating...]**

"Twenty-two," Gerald said. "Plus three support staff. Total twenty-five."

The window changed.

**[Occupants: 25.]** **[Threat Level: High.]** **[Threat to Life: Imminent.]**

"System," Gerald said. "Re-evaluate Exception 44-C."

The blue text shifted. Green.

**[Exception 44-C Approved.]** **[You may now issue Emergency Repair Order.]** **[Warning: This action will mark you as 'Non-Compliant' for the duration of the repair.]**

"Do it," Gerald said.

A golden seal stamped onto his clipboard. The red light in his chest flared brighter, then stabilized.

Kaelen's eyes narrowed.

"You're buying time. You can't fix a load-bearing pillar in thirty seconds."

"I don't need to fix it," Gerald said. "I need to cite you for Obstructing an Emergency Repair."

Kaelen froze.

"System," Gerald said. "Serve Citation to Core Entity 'Kaelen.' Violation: Interference with Public Safety Infrastructure. Severity: Class A."

Kaelen snarled.

"I am the owner of this dungeon! I have right of way!"

"Wrong," Gerald said. He tapped the clipboard.

"The library above is a Public Access Site.

The sub-basement is a protected utility corridor. You do not have right of way. You are trespassing on a site under Emergency Repair Protocol.

"

The System chimed. A loud, discordant bell that shook the dust from the ceiling.

**[Citation Served.]** **[Target: Kaelen.]** **[Effect: Temporary Stun (10 seconds).]** **[Debuff: Core Output Reduced by 15%.]**

Kaelen staggered. The dark energy around his fingers dissipated into harmless sparks. He stumbled back, clutching his head.

"What..." Kaelen gasped. "What did you do?"

"I cited you," Gerald said. He didn't look at Kaelen. He looked at the pillar.

Martha lowered her hammer. She looked at the stunned Core, then at Gerald.

"You... you can cite him for that?"

"It's in the book," Gerald said. He pulled a pen from his pocket.

"Section 12, Paragraph 4.

'No entity shall impede the flow of emergency resources to a life-safety hazard.' I'm the emergency resource, Martha.

By standing in front of me, he was impeding me.

"

Kaelen shook his head, trying to clear the stun. His eyes burned with rage.

"You're cheating," Kaelen spat. "You're twisting the rules."

"I'm applying them," Gerald said. "And since you're stunned, you cannot appeal."

Gerald walked past the paralyzed Core. He approached the cracked pillar. He placed his hand on the stone.

"System," Gerald said. "Apply temporary shoring. Material: Condensed Mana (Grade 3). Duration: 60 seconds."

**[Cost: 50 Mana.]** **[Insufficient Mana.]**

Martha stepped forward. She placed her hand on Gerald's shoulder. A warm, golden light flowed from her into his chest.

"Use mine," she said.

Gerald didn't argue. He didn't have the luxury of pride.

"Approved," Gerald said.

A lattice of light sprouted from the pillar, wrapping around the crack like a glowing ivy. The groaning stone ceased. The red light in Gerald's chest dimmed, but did not disappear. It remained, a warning beacon.

Kaelen shook off the stun. He looked at the shored pillar, then at Gerald. The amusement was gone. Only cold calculation remained.

"You bought sixty seconds," Kaelen said. "And you spent your mana. You're vulnerable."

"I'm not vulnerable," Gerald said. "I'm compliant with the repair order."

"You're still in violation of my Citation," Kaelen said. "Your rating is still dropping."

Gerald looked at his status window.

**[Compliance Rating: 38/100]** **[Penalty: Physical Damage Taken Increased by 20%.]** **[Status: Under Investigation.]**

"Thirty-eight," Martha whispered. "Gerald, if you drop below thirty, the System shuts down your spawn list. No more rats.

No more mold. You'll be empty.

"

"I know," Gerald said. He turned to face Kaelen.

The Core had recovered. His hands crackled with black lightning.

"Run," Martha said. "Now. The shoring will fail in fifty seconds. We need to get to the library exit."

Gerald shook his head.

"No," Gerald said.

Martha stared at him.

"You're insane. Look at your chest. You're bleeding code."

"I'm not running," Gerald said. "Because if I run, I admit the violation was valid. I admit that I caused the damage by fixing the ventilation."

"But you did," Martha said. "The pressure..."

"I caused it," Gerald admitted. "But I didn't do it alone."

Gerald looked at the ceiling. He looked at the ventilation shafts.

"Kaelen," Gerald said. "When did you install those vents?"

Kaelen paused.

"What?"

"The intake vents in the north wall. The ones I fixed."

"I installed them last week," Kaelen said, wary. "Why?"

"They're non-compliant," Gerald said.

Kaelen scoffed.

"They're perfectly functional. They move air."

"They move air," Gerald said. "But they don't meet the Code for Air Exchange in a High-Density Entity Zone."

Gerald pulled a tape measure from his belt. He snapped it open with a sharp *click*.

"The diameter," Gerald said.

"Is too small for the volume of this room. I calculated it when I opened the shaft. I assumed you were just lazy.

But you weren't lazy.

You were trying to create a pressure vacuum.

"

Kaelen's eyes widened.

"You were trying to destabilize the foundation," Gerald said.

"You cited me for causing structural damage. But the root cause of the instability wasn't my fix. It was your original design flaw.

You built a system that would crack the pillar under normal operation. I just accelerated the timeline.

"

Martha looked at the pillar. The light lattice was humming.

"So?" Martha asked. "So he's negligent. Does that help us?"

"It changes the Citation," Gerald said. He turned back to the System window.

"System," Gerald said. "Amend Citation against Gerald Hutchins."

Kaelen laughed nervously.

"You can't amend a served Citation. That's final."

"I'm not amending," Gerald said. "I'm filing a Cross-Complaint. Violation: Gross Negligence in Structural Design. Severity: Class S."

The System window paused.

**[Cross-Complaints must be filed during the initial Citation window.]** **[The window has passed.]**

Gerald looked at Martha. He looked at Kaelen.

"The window passed," Gerald said. "Because you appealed. You invoked a Hearing."

"Yes," Kaelen said. "And the Hearing is active. You're losing."

"In a Hearing," Gerald said, "evidence can be submitted at any point prior to the final ruling. Is that correct, System?"

A blue window appeared.

**[Correct. Evidence submission remains open until the Judge's Gavel is struck.]**

Kaelen stopped laughing.

"Who is the Judge?" Martha asked.

Gerald looked at the ceiling. High above, in the darkness of the sub-basement's upper reaches, a pair of glowing eyes opened.

They weren't yellow. They were white. Pure, blinding white.

A figure descended, which was tall, slender, and made of translucent geometric shapes.

It wore no armor. It held no weapon.

It held a scale.

"The Inspector General," Gerald said.

Martha's face went pale.

"The IG? They don't come for minor disputes."

"They come for structural integrity," Gerald said. "And Kaelen just admitted to designing a life-safety hazard."

The Inspector General landed. The floor did not shake. The air simply stopped moving.

Kaelen stepped back. The dark energy around him flickered and died.

"Core Entity 'Kaelen,'" the Inspector General spoke. Its voice sounded like paper tearing. "You are under audit."

Kaelen tried to speak.

"This is a misunderstanding. The Inspector—"

"Silence," the IG said. It turned its white gaze to Gerald.

"Gerald Hutchins. You have filed a Cross-Complaint for Gross Negligence. Do you have evidence?"

Gerald held up his tape measure.

"The vent diameter is twelve inches," Gerald said. "The code requires eighteen inches for a room of this volume with this number of high-mana entities. The resulting pressure differential creates a cyclic stress load on the central pillar. Estimated lifespan of pillar under current conditions: four days."

The IG looked at Kaelen.

"Core Entity 'Kaelen.' Do you deny this calculation?"

Kaelen's mouth opened. Closed.

"It's... it's a custom design," Kaelen stammered. "It works. The air moves."

"The air moves," Gerald said. "The building shakes. That is a violation of Structural Code 1-4: Stability Under Load."

The IG raised its hand.

"Verdict pending," the IG said. "However, during the audit, all entities must submit to a Full Structural Scan."

The IG pointed a finger at Kaelen.

Kaelen screamed.

It wasn't a scream of pain. It was a scream of data being ripped from his core. Lines of blue code poured out of his chest, swirling into the IG's scale.

Gerald watched. His red chest light pulsed.

**[Warning: Your Compliance Rating is still dropping.]** **[Rating: 36/100]**

"Gerald," Martha whispered. "He's draining you too. The audit affects everyone in the zone."

"I know," Gerald said. He kept his eyes on the IG.

The IG's scale tipped.

"Core Entity 'Kaelen,'" the IG said. "You are found Non-Compliant in Category: Structural Integrity."

Kaelen fell to his knees. His form flickered.

"Penalty," the IG said. "Immediate Demolition of Non-Compliant Structure."

The walls of the dungeon dissolved.

Not into rubble. Into pixels.

Martha grabbed Gerald's arm.

"It's deleting him! It's deleting the dungeon!"

"It's deleting the violation," Gerald said.

The floor beneath Kaelen vanished. He fell into a void of white light. His screams were cut off as his form unraveled.

The ceiling above them remained intact. The library above was safe.

But Gerald's dungeon...

Gerald looked down at his hands. They were fading.

"System," Gerald said. "Query: Status."

**[Dungeon Core 'Gerald Hutchins' is under Audit.]** **[Your actions contributed to the violation.]** **[Penalty: Partial Core Reset.]**

Martha screamed his name.

Gerald felt a tearing sensation in his chest. Not pain. Absence.

He looked at the rat. The rat was gone.

He looked at the water leak. It was gone.

He looked at Martha. She was holding onto empty air where his arm had been.

"Gerald?" she cried.

Gerald looked at his clipboard. The Form 9-Alpha was blank.

"I didn't fix it," Gerald said. His voice was faint. "I just... cited it."

The white light consumed the room.

When the light faded, Gerald stood alone in an empty, white void. No walls. No ceiling. No library.

No Martha.

A single text box floated before him.

**[Core Reset Complete.]** **[New Objective: Rebuild to Code.]** **[Starting Compliance Rating: 0/100.]** **[Warning: You are now in a 'Void State'. No spawn list available.]**

Gerald looked around the infinite white emptiness, and he reached for his pen.

It was gone.
