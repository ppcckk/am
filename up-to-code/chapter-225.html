Chapter 225: Menu Locked

# Chapter 225

The elevator shaft was screaming.

It wasn't a mechanical whine. It wasn't the groan of stressed steel.

It was a harmonic resonance, a frequency that vibrated in Gerald's molars and turned his vision into a blur of gray static.

He pressed his palm against the cold concrete of the sub-basement ceiling, feeling the pulse of the structure above. The skyscraper had punched through three stories of the municipal library. It had shattered the bank's facade.

And now it was trying to eat the foundation he stood on.

Mana flooded his veins. It felt like ice water and adrenaline mixed together, a potent cocktail that made his hands shake.

He had accepted the assignment.

He had become the Structural Hazard Inspector. The title felt heavy, physical, pressing down on his shoulders.

A notification blinked in his peripheral vision, sharp and red.

**ALERT: ZONING CONFLICT DETECTED.** **STRUCTURE HEIGHT: 400M.** **ALLOWED HEIGHT: 15M.** **PENALTY: STRUCTURAL COLLAPSE IMMINENT.**

Gerald ignored the warning. He looked at his hands.

They were not the hands of a man who had spent three decades holding a tape measure.

They were armored in translucent, geometric constructs of mana, glowing with a faint blue light.

He flexed his fingers. The hammer he held—a simple, iron-headed tool he had manifested from his own mana pool—felt weightless.

He climbed the stairs.

The library sub-basement was gone. The shelves of local history, the dusty archives, the moldy corners he had lovingly cataloged—erased. In their place was a jagged wound in the earth.

The floor was tilted at a fifteen-degree angle. Debris floated in the air, suspended by the sheer density of the new Core's mana field.

At the top of the stairs, the door was gone.

The frame had been twisted into a spiral. Through the gap, Gerald saw the lobby of the bank. Or what used to be the lobby.

Now it was an open atrium, the floor replaced by a swirling vortex of black glass.

A figure hovered in the center of the atrium.

It was Vane. The Core that had been his rival in the last Hearing. Vane looked smaller now, shrunk down by the massive energy output required to sustain the skyscraper. His skin was pale, stretched tight over bones that glowed with unstable energy.

"You're late, Hutchins," Vane said. His voice boomed, echoing off the glass walls. It sounded like grinding stones. "I expected you to be crushed by the initial expansion."

Gerald stepped through the broken frame. His boots crunched on shattered tile.

He didn't look at Vane.

He looked at the load-bearing columns. There were none. The black glass walls supported themselves through magic, a trick that violated Section 402.1 of the Structural Integrity Code: *All vertical loads must be transferred to a foundation via physical materials.*

"Section 402.1," Gerald muttered.

Vane tilted his head. "What?"

"Your columns," Gerald said, pointing a gauntleted finger at the glass. "They aren't columns.

They're aesthetic features.

You're using mana to counteract gravity instead of distributing the weight.

That's not engineering. That's cheating."

Vane smiled. It was a thin, cruel expression. "This is a dungeon, old man.

Physics is a suggestion. Power is law."

"Power is not code," Gerald replied. He pulled a clipboard from his mana storage. The paper was blank, but the pen was real. "And I am here to apply the code."

Vane laughed.

The sound caused the glass walls to ripple. "You think a citation will stop this? Look at it.

It's beautiful.

It's a monument. It's a tower that touches the sky.

You can't cite beauty."

Gerald stopped writing. He looked up. The top of the skyscraper was gone, lost in the clouds.

The base was wider than the city block. The neighbors' houses were being pulled into the foundation, their roofs peeling off like skin.

"It's not beautiful," Gerald said, his voice flat. "It's a hazard."

He stepped forward.

The mana around him surged, forming a barrier.

A wave of force erupted from Vane, slamming into the barrier. Gerald skidded back, his boots carving deep grooves into the black glass floor.

The impact rattled his teeth.

Vane raised a hand. A spear of obsidian glass formed in the air, sharp and lethal. "Leave, Inspector. Or I will add your body to the foundation."

Gerald didn't retreat. He stepped into the shadow of the nearest glass pillar. He tapped his pen against the clipboard.

**SYSTEM NOTIFICATION:** **ACTION: INITIATE ZONING VIOLATION CITATION.** **TARGET: UNLICENSED HIGH-RISE.** **PROCESSING...**

A blue light enveloped Gerald. He felt the System reach into his mind, searching for the legal framework.

It was there.

Buried in the thousands of pages of municipal code he had memorized in his sleep, there was a clause. Not for dungeons. For buildings.

*Clause 19.4: Emergency Structural Intervention.*

If a structure posed an imminent threat to public life, an authorized inspector could initiate a *Stop Work Order*. This was not a combat action. It was a regulatory action. And because Gerald was now a Core, the System recognized his authority as absolute.

He slammed the pen down on the paper.

**CITATION ISSUED.** **VIOLATION: FAILURE TO PROVIDE EGRESS.** **PENALTY: MANDATORY STRUCTURAL WEAKENING.**

The effect was instant.

The glass walls groaned. Cracks spiderwebbed across the surface. The harmonic screaming stopped, replaced by a low, ominous rumble.

Vane's eyes widened. He looked at his own hands. The energy crackling around him flickered.

"What did you do?" Vane hissed.

"I cited you for lack of emergency exits," Gerald said.

He walked toward the center of the atrium. "Your design has no doors.

No stairs.

No way to escape if the structure fails. That is a violation. The System agrees."

"That's insane!" Vane shouted. He slammed his hands together, trying to reinforce the walls. "I am a Core! I create the rules!"

"No," Gerald said. "You create the space. I create the boundaries."

He raised his hand. The air around him distorted.

He wasn't attacking Vane.

He was attacking the *concept* of the building.

He was applying the penalty. The System's debuff was taking hold.

The structural integrity of the skyscraper was dropping. Not from damage, but from legal non-compliance.

The ceiling above them sagged.

Vane screamed. He threw a bolt of dark energy at Gerald.

Gerald didn't dodge.

He caught it on his mana barrier. The impact sent him flying backward, crashing into a pillar. The pillar shattered.

Pain exploded in Gerald's back.

He tasted blood. He rolled, coming up to his knees.

His mana was draining fast.

The *Stop Work Order* was expensive. It required constant concentration. It required him to believe in the validity of the code more than he believed in his own survival.

He had to be right. If he was wrong, if the System rejected the citation, the backlash would kill him.

Gerald stood up. He wiped blood from his lip.

He looked at Vane.

The rival Core was shrinking, his form becoming translucent.

The skyscraper was buckling. The top floors were beginning to tilt.

"Yield," Gerald said. "Accept the demolition order. Or I will find a violation in your electrical system. And that one burns."

Vane snarled. He looked at the crumbling walls. He looked at the sky, where debris was starting to rain down on the streets below.

He was losing control. But he was a predator.

He didn't yield.

He adapted.

Vane snapped his fingers.

The ground beneath Gerald shifted. The black glass floor rose up, trapping Gerald's legs. He struggled, but the glass hardened around him, locking him in place.

"You can't cite the floor," Vane said, breathing heavily. "The floor is part of the Core. It is me."

Gerald looked down.

The glass had fused with his boots. He couldn't move.

The *Stop Work Order* flickered, destabilizing.

His mana pool was emptying. The headache behind his eyes was becoming a migraine.

"You're right," Gerald said. His voice was quiet. "I can't cite the floor."

Vane smiled again. He raised both hands, gathering energy for a final blast. "Then die, Inspector. With your code."

But Gerald wasn't looking at Vane. He was looking at the clipboard.

He had prepared for this.

He had known Vane would adapt. He had known the rival Core would try to use the structure itself as a weapon. So he hadn't just cited the building. He had cited the *surrounding area*.

He flipped the page.

**ACTION: ISSUE ADJACENT ZONING NOTICE.** **TARGET: ADJACENT PROPERTY: CITY OF ASHFORD LIBRARY FOUNDATION.** **VIOLATION: UNAUTHORIZED SUBTERRANEAN EXPANSION.** **PENALTY: IMMEDIATE REVERSION.**

Gerald tapped the pen.

The effect was not on Vane. It was on Gerald's own dungeon.

The library's foundation, the ancient stone that had stood for a hundred years, reacted to the citation. The System recognized that Vane's skyscraper was encroaching on protected municipal land. The penalty for unauthorized encroachment was *reversion*. The land would snap back to its previous state.

The ground beneath Gerald exploded.

The glass cage shattered. The skyscraper's foundation, which was built on stolen land, lost its anchor. The entire structure, four hundred meters of black glass and ego, slid.

It didn't collapse. It *slid*.

The whole tower tilted, pulling Vane with it. The rival Core shrieked, his form dissolving into the chaos.

Gerald was thrown backward, out of the atrium, back down the stairs, back into the sub-basement. He hit the concrete hard. The air rushed out of his lungs.

Above him, the sound of tearing earth and shattering glass was deafening. The skyscraper was falling, not into itself, but sideways, into the street.

Gerald lay on his back, staring at the ceiling. The cracks in the concrete were gone.

The library was safe.

The bank was ruined.

The city was in panic.

A notification flashed.

**SYSTEM NOTICE:** **ACTION SUCCESSFUL.** **COMPLIANCE RATING INCREASED: 60/100.** **WARNING: STRUCTURAL DAMAGE TO MUNICIPAL PROPERTY DETECTED.** **LIABILITY ASSIGNED TO: CORE ID: GH-001.**

Gerald sat up. He looked at the message. *Liability assigned.*

He had saved the city. And now he owned the debt.

A new window opened.

**COURT SUMMONS: CIVIL LIABILITY HEARING.** **PLAINTIFF: CITY OF ASHFORD.** **DEFENDANT: CORE ID: GH-001.** **DEMAND: COMPENSATION FOR DAMAGED PUBLIC INFRASTRUCTURE.** **AMOUNT: 500,000 MANA UNITS.**

Five hundred thousand mana.

Gerald had a mana pool of four thousand.

He stared at the number. He had won the battle. He had stopped the skyscraper. He had proven that code was stronger than magic.

But the System didn't care about the moral victory. It cared about the balance sheet.

Footsteps echoed in the hallway above. Not adventurer footsteps. Not monster footsteps.

Heavy, orderly footsteps.

The door to the sub-basement creaked open.

A man in a gray suit stood there. He held a briefcase. His face was blank, his eyes like polished coins.

"Mr. Hutchins," the man said. "I am from the Inspector General's Office. You have been served with a subpoena."

Gerald looked at the man. He looked at the briefcase. He looked at the shattered remains of his sub-basement.

"I need a lawyer," Gerald said.

The man smiled. It was the first time Gerald had seen an IG agent smile. It was worse than Vane's.

"You don't need a lawyer," the agent said. "You need a new identity. The System has flagged you as a rogue variable.

You are no longer a Core. You are a suspect."

He held out a pair of handcuffs. They were made of dark, pulsating metal.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** **CORE REGISTRATION SUSPENDED.** **ACCESS TO SYSTEM MENU LOCKED.**

Gerald's blue interface vanished.

The mana in his veins cooled, turning back into blood. He felt weak. He felt human.

He felt terrified.

He reached for his hammer. It was gone.

The agent stepped forward.

"Please," the agent said. "Walk slowly. We have a lot of paperwork to do."
