Chapter 33

Gerald was on his hands and knees in Corridor 4 with a tape measure when the mold spores started dying.

He noticed because the humidity dropped. He had installed a dehumidifier three chapters ago, a proper one with a condensate line that drained into a floor drain he'd also installed, and the spores had been thriving in the damp corners because the dehumidifier was set to 55 percent relative humidity, which was code for habitable spaces. But the spores had adapted. They'd colonized the condensate line itself, turning it into a fungal highway.

Now they were desiccating. Gerald hadn't changed the setting. Something else had.

He straightened up. His knees popped. The tape measure was still extended, forty-two inches, measuring the distance between two conduit penetrations in the east wall. The conduits were thick. Too thick for electrical. Too thick for plumbing. They were eight inches in diameter, cast iron, and they ran through his walls at a slight downward angle, disappearing into the concrete below his floor slab.

He had been measuring them for two hours. The Hearing was in four.

He wrote in his notebook: *Conduit penetration. 8" cast iron. Non-standard. No evidence of structural connection to surrounding concrete. No rebar ties. No weld points. The conduits pass through the wall but do not bear on it.*

He underlined *do not bear on it* twice.

This was the key. The entity had classified Gerald's dungeon as a substrate. A foundation. A thing that supports another thing. For that to be true, Gerald's structure had to be transferring load to the entity below. The conduits were the connection. If the conduits were structural, if they were load-bearing, then Gerald's walls were supporting them, and his dungeon was a foundation for the entity's network.

But the conduits weren't load-bearing. They were penetrations. A pipe through a wall doesn't make the wall a foundation for the pipe. A wall with a hole in it is still a wall. The hole doesn't bear load. The wall does.

Gerald checked the next penetration. Same result. The conduit sat in a sleeve, a gap filled with what looked like expanding foam, but old. Centuries old. The foam had degraded. The conduit had settled. It was pressing against the bottom of the sleeve now, transferring a small amount of weight to the concrete.

A small amount. Not enough to be structural. Not enough to make Gerald's dungeon a foundation.

He moved to the next corridor. And the next. He checked eleven penetrations in total. Each one the same: a non-structural penetration that had degraded over time and was now transferring a negligible amount of load through friction and settling.

He wrote his argument. *The penetrations are non-structural. They are utility runs that pass through the structure without bearing on it. The structure is independent. The entity is not supported by the structure. The structure is not a substrate.*

It was a good argument. He'd written hundreds like it.

Then he checked the entity's filing history.

This was the part that took time. The System's filing interface was searchable, but only by permit number, and Gerald didn't have the entity's permit number. He searched by location. He searched by date range. He searched by conduit diameter, because the System had a field for that, and he was a building inspector who had once spent three weeks searching for a permit number on a 1987 addition to a gas station because the contractor had used a permit from a different jurisdiction.

The search returned nothing.

Gerald stared at the interface. He ran the search again with different parameters. Nothing. He tried searching by entity type. The System returned: *No Permit of Record found for entity designated as [UNCLASSIFIED SUBSTRATE].*

The entity had no permit. Its conduits had no permit. The entire network running through Gerald's walls, through the library's foundation, through the parking garage, through the municipal infrastructure above, none of it had ever been permitted.

Gerald sat down on the floor. He felt something he hadn't felt in thirty-one years of inspections. Not excitement. Not triumph. Something closer to the feeling he got when he found a contractor who had built an entire addition without a permit and then tried to get a certificate of occupancy by claiming the work was "pre-existing."

The entity was a pre-existing condition with no paperwork.

And that meant Gerald's argument was stronger than he'd thought.

---

The Hearing opened with the System's standard procedural language. Gerald had memorized it. He'd read the Hearing protocol four times in the last hour. He knew the order of operations. He knew the rebuttal window. He knew that if he could establish the entity's conduits were unpermitted, the Demolition Order would be void, because you cannot demolish what you cannot legally claim to own.

The entity spoke first. Its voice came through the floor. Not words exactly. Pressure patterns. The System translated them into text on Gerald's interface.

*The substrate supports the network. The network has existed for eleven hundred years. The substrate was built upon it. The substrate is a derivative structure. Demolition is the remedy for unauthorized derivative construction.*

Gerald's interface translated the entity's argument. He read it twice. Then he stood up. He hadn't prepared a speech. He prepared a document. He had printed it. The System allowed physical documents in Hearings if they were filed before the session opened, and Gerald had filed his seventeen minutes before the Hearing started, which was within the window.

He held up the document. The System scanned it.

"Permit of Record search," Gerald said. His voice sounded strange in the empty corridor. He'd been talking to himself for hours. "I request the System produce the Permit of Record for the conduit network currently passing through my dungeon's structural walls."

The interface responded: *No Permit of Record found.*

Gerald held up a second document. "I request the System confirm that the entity has filed no Structural Modification Permit, no Utility Easement, no Right-of-Way grant, and no Encroachment Authorization for the eleven conduit penetrations passing through my structure."

The interface responded: *No filings found.*

The entity's pressure patterns shifted. Faster now. Agitated.

*The network predates the System. The network predates the filing requirements. The network is primordial.*

Gerald waited. He let the entity finish. Then he said: "The System's Permit of Record requirement applies to all structures within the System's jurisdiction regardless of construction date. I cite Section 4.2.1 of the Core Filing Protocol: 'All structures, regardless of origin, must maintain a current Permit of Record to claim legal standing in System proceedings.' The entity has no Permit of Record. The entity has no legal standing. The Demolition Order is void."

Silence. The floor stopped pressing.

Then the System spoke. Not the interface. The System itself. The voice that came from everywhere and nowhere.

*Hearing resolved in favor of Gerald Hutchins. Demolition Order voided. Entity cited for operating without a Permit of Record. Penalty: structural debuff, forty percent, duration: indefinite, pending compliance.*

The floor groaned. Gerald felt it. A deep vibration, like a building settling. The entity's conduits were weakening. The debuff was applying.

Gerald exhaled. He picked up his tape measure. He was going to finish measuring the penetrations. He was going to document everything properly. He was going to file a Structural Impact Assessment showing that the entity's debuff was causing progressive deterioration in his walls, and he was going to demand remediation.

Then his interface chimed.

*NOTIFICATION: Conditional Use Classification Applied.*

Gerald stopped.

*Per System Code 9.7.3: Any structure built upon an unpermitted infrastructure network is classified as Conditional Use. Conditional Use structures may be demolished at any time without Hearing or appeal. Owner must obtain a Permit of Record within forty-eight (48) hours or face automatic demolition.*

Gerald read it three times.

He looked at his dungeon. Four thousand square feet. Handrails installed to code. Egress signage compliant. Fire suppression system, barely operational. Ventilation, adequate. His Compliance Rating was 74 and climbing.

He looked at the interface. Forty-eight hours.

He had to get a Permit of Record for his own dungeon. A dungeon that he had built himself, spawn by spawn, handrail by handrail, without ever filing a single permit. Because he hadn't known he needed to. Because the System hadn't required it until this exact moment.

He had built his entire dungeon on top of unpermitted infrastructure. And now that the infrastructure was officially unpermitted, his dungeon was officially conditional.

His interface chimed again.

*Permit Application Window Open. Estimated Processing Time: 72 hours. Note: Processing Time exceeds Conditional Use deadline. Applicant is advised to seek expedited review.*

Seventy-two hours to process. Forty-eight hours to demolish.

Gerald sat down on the floor. The tape measure rolled away from him. The mold spores, still desiccating in the dry air, did not move.

He opened the Permit Application form. It had forty-seven pages.
