Chapter 29

Form 7-C had forty-three subsections. Gerald had read them all in the eleven minutes since he opened the form. Subsection 7-C.3 was on page thirty-one, printed in a typeface so small he had to press his awareness against the System's rendering layer to read it.

"Conduit Reclassification for Utility Purposes."

A conduit could be reclassified from "unauthorized structural opening" to "utility penetration" if it served a legitimate municipal function during the variance hearing. The function had to be active. Real-time. Observable by the System's adjudication process.

Gerald needed a utility. He needed one of his three silent conduits to be doing something useful while the System watched.

He pulled up the conduit traces. The eleven complaint-generating conduits were straightforward. They carried spawn material, energy bleed, and one that leaked thermal waste into a dead-end corridor. Standard cross-contamination. He could reclassify those as "waste removal" and be done.

The three silent conduits were different.

They carried nothing he could identify. The flow was slow, viscous, and the System's diagnostic layer rendered them as a flat gray. No energy signature. No thermal output. No spawn classification. They were, for all purposes Gerald could determine, empty.

He checked again. The flow rate was 0.3 units per hour. Below the detection threshold for most diagnostic tools. He'd missed it in every previous scan because he'd been looking for above-threshold activity.

Gerald isolated one conduit and increased the sampling resolution. The gray resolved into particulate matter. Organic. Cellular.

Spawn material.

He checked the second conduit. Same composition. Third. Same.

All three were feeding biological spawn material into his dungeon. Slowly. Quietly. At a rate just below his detection floor.

Gerald sat with this for a long moment. Then he pulled up his spawn log and cross-referenced it against his own creation timestamps. He'd been tracking spawns since week two. Every entity in his dungeon had a creation event logged under his Core designation.

He filtered for entities without his Core designation.

Nine.

Nine entities in his dungeon that he had not created. A beetle colony. Two fungal mats. Something the System classified as "unidentified invertebrate, larval stage." A rat that was not his rat. A cluster of biofilm nodes. A second rat. Three additional beetle colonies that had merged with the first.

They were all in the eastern quadrant. All within four meters of a silent conduit.

Gerald pulled up the spawn origin field for each. All blank. No Core designation. No attribution.

He pulled up the conduit origin field. The three silent conduits had origin fields. They were not blank. They read: "EXTERNAL SOURCE: UNDERFLOW."

He had not heard of UNDERFLOW.

Gerald opened a search query. The System returned a single result.

CORE DESIGNATION: UNDERFLOW. CLASSIFICATION: SUBTERRANEAN/DRAINAGE. COMPLIANCE RATING: 89. DISTANCE FROM ASHFORD MUNICIPAL LIBRARY SUB-BASEMENT: 340 METERS. STATUS: ACTIVE.

A Core. Three hundred and forty meters away. Feeding spawn material into Gerald's dungeon at a rate too slow to trigger his own diagnostics. Nine unauthorized entities in four months.

Gerald felt something cold move through his awareness. Not anger. Recognition. He had seen this pattern before. Not in dungeons. In apartment buildings. A landlord connecting their unit's waste line to a neighbor's clean water supply. A cross-connection. Two systems sharing infrastructure that should not be shared.

He opened Section 8.

Section 8.4.2. Cross-Connection Control.

The language was dense. Gerald read it twice. Then he read it a third time, because the third time he noticed the definition of "cross-connection" that the System used.

"A cross-connection is any physical linkage between two systems that permits the transfer of material, energy, or information between systems not intended to be interconnected."

The silent conduits were not unauthorized openings. They were cross-connections.

Gerald pulled up Section 4.2.1 again. Prohibited penetrations. Unauthorized openings in a dungeon wall. Penalty: structural rating deduction.

Then Section 8.4.2. Cross-connection control. Penalty for an uncontrolled cross-connection: the Core that installed the cross-connection is cited. The receiving Core is not.

The receiving Core. Gerald.

He was not the violator. He was the victim. The cross-connection had been installed by UNDERFLOW. Gerald had not created the conduits. He had not opened them. They had been opened into his dungeon by another Core, and he had not known.

But the audit did not care about intent. The audit would see fourteen openings in his eastern wall. It would apply Section 4.2.1. It would not look at origin fields. It would not ask who installed them.

Unless the conduits were not openings.

Unless they were utility penetrations.

Gerald went back to Section 8.4.2. Subsection (c). Cross-Connection Control Devices.

"A Core may install a cross-connection control device (hereafter 'check valve') on any conduit connecting to external infrastructure. Installation of a check valve reclassifies the conduit as a utility penetration serving the function of backflow prevention. A conduit classified as a utility penetration is not subject to Section 4.2.1."

Gerald read it again.

Install a check valve. The conduit becomes a utility penetration. Not an opening. No Section 4.2.1 deduction. The structural violation evaporates.

And the check valve blocks incoming material. UNDERFLOW's spawn feed stops. The nine unauthorized entities remain, but they stop multiplying.

Gerald opened the installation requirements.

Check valve installation required: the conduit to be active (it was), the installing Core to be the receiving end (he was), and a Form 8-C: Cross-Connection Control Device Permit.

He pulled up Form 8-C. It was four pages. He filled it out in six minutes. The System approved it instantly. No hearing required. No variance. A permit for a control device was administrative, not adjudicative.

Gerald installed the first check valve.

The conduit shuddered. The gray flow stopped. The particulate matter in the trace reversed direction and began moving back toward UNDERFLOW.

He installed the second. The third.

All three conduits went dark. No incoming material. No outgoing material. Just a sealed utility penetration serving the function of backflow prevention.

Gerald pulled up his structural schematic. Fourteen openings became eleven openings. His projected rating: 67 minus eleven deductions at the severity multiplier. He did the math.

Eighty-one.

Above sixty. Above the audit threshold.

Gerald exhaled.

Then he opened a new form. Citation. Standard format. Section 8.4.2, uncontrolled cross-connection. Target: UNDERFLOW. Evidence: three conduits with origin field attribution, nine unauthorized spawns in receiving Core's territory, flow rate below detection threshold (indicating deliberate concealment).

He served it.

The System accepted the Citation. The debuff applied. UNDERFLOW's Compliance Rating dropped from 89 to 71. A proportional penalty. Fourteen points for three cross-connections at concealment severity.

Gerald closed the form. He allowed himself one full breath.

Then the System returned a response.

Not an appeal. Not a hearing notice.

A counter-Citation.

Target: ASHFORD MUNICIPAL LIBRARY SUB-BASEMENT. Section 8.4.2(d). Unauthorized Discharge into Public Infrastructure.

Gerald read it twice.

Section 8.4.2(d). He had not read subsection (d). He had read (a), (b), and (c). He had stopped at (c) because (c) was the one that helped him.

He pulled up the full text of Section 8.4.2.

Subsection (d): "A cross-connection control device that reverses flow direction constitutes a discharge event. Material discharged from a check valve into external infrastructure is classified as unauthorized discharge if the receiving infrastructure is designated as public."

The conduits were public infrastructure. The System's conduit network was public infrastructure. Gerald's check valves had reversed the flow. The spawn material UNDERFLOW had been feeding into his dungeon for four months was now flowing back through the conduit network.

Into public infrastructure.

The penalty for unauthorized discharge: fifteen points. Flat. Non-negotiable.

Gerald's rating: 81 minus 15.

Sixty-six.

He had fixed one violation and created another. The net was better. But barely. And the audit was in sixty-one hours.

Gerald stared at the counter-Citation. UNDERFLOW had cited him using a subsection he hadn't read. UNDERFLOW had known about (d). UNDERFLOW had installed the cross-connections knowing that if Gerald discovered them and installed check valves, the check valves themselves would become a violation.

It was a trap. The cross-connections were bait. The check valve was the trigger. UNDERFLOW had designed the whole arrangement so that Gerald's own fix would cost him fifteen points.

Gerald pulled up the counter-Citation's appeal window. Thirty-six hours. He could appeal. He could argue that the discharge was incidental, that the check valve was a mandated control device, that the System's own subsection (c) had authorized the installation and therefore the discharge was a consequence of System-mandated compliance.

That argument might work. It might not. It depended on whether the System treated subsection (c) as an override of subsection (d) or as a separate permission that did not immunize against (d)'s consequences.

Gerald had seen this kind of drafting before. In municipal code. Two sections that contradict each other. The resolution depends on which section was enacted first, and whether the later section contains an explicit override clause.

He checked the enactment dates. Subsection (c): enacted at System version 3.2.1. Subsection (d): enacted at System version 3.4.0.

(d) was later. (d) did not contain an override clause. But (d) also did not contain a cross-reference to (c).

In municipal code, when two sections conflict and the later section does not explicitly override the earlier one, the earlier section survives. The later section is interpreted narrowly to avoid conflict.

Gerald could argue that. He could argue that (d) must be read narrowly to avoid conflict with (c), and therefore the discharge from a (c)-authorized check valve is not unauthorized.

But he had never argued before the System. He had won a Hearing by proving a fire exit was non-compliant. That was factual. Measurable. He had a tape measure and a door width.

This was interpretation. This was legal argument. This was the kind of thing that made his stomach turn.

Gerald opened Form 7-A: Appeal of Citation. He began to fill it out.

Then a new notification arrived. Not from the System. From UNDERFLOW.

A message. Direct Core-to-Core. The first Gerald had ever received.

"YOU CITED ME. I CITED YOU. WE ARE EVEN. BUT THE AUDIT IS IN SIXTY-ONE HOURS AND YOUR RATING IS SIXTY-SIX. YOU NEED NINE MORE POINTS TO CLEAR THE THRESHOLD WITH MARGIN. I HAVE A PROPOSAL."

Gerald did not respond. He kept filling out Form 7-A.

The message came again.

"MY DUNGEON IS A DRAINAGE SYSTEM. 12,000 SQUARE FEET. COMPLIANCE RATING 71 AFTER YOUR CITATION. I HAVE TWENTY-THREE UTILITY PENETRATIONS THAT SERVE NO FUNCTION. I NEED THEM TO SERVE FUNCTIONS. YOU NEED POINTS. I CAN GIVE YOU POINTS. I NEED YOU TO CITE ME AGAIN."

Gerald stopped typing.

UNDERFLOW was offering to be cited again. A second Citation would drop UNDERFLOW's rating further. But Gerald needed UNDERFLOW to have more citations on record. Why?

The message came again.

"SECTION 11.7. REPEATED VIOLATION PROTOCOL. THREE CITATIONS IN THE SAME CATEGORY TRIGGER MANDATORY REMEDIATION. THE REMEDIATION IS PERFORMED BY THE CITING CORE. YOU GET TO REBUILD MY UTILITY INFRASTRUCTURE. YOU GET CREDIT FOR THE BUILT SYSTEMS. YOUR RATING GOES UP. MY RATING GOES DOWN. BUT I SURVIVE BECAUSE THE REMEDIATION RAISES MY RATING TOO. WE BOTH WIN."

Gerald read it three times.

Underflow was proposing a coordinated Citation. Gerald would cite UNDERFLOW a third time for cross-connections. The System would trigger mandatory remediation. Gerald would rebuild UNDERFLOW's utility infrastructure. He would receive credit for the built systems. His rating would rise. UNDERFLOW's rating would rise from the remediation.

It was a scheme. It was also, technically, legal. The System allowed remediation by the citing Core. Gerald had never used that provision. He hadn't needed it. His own dungeon was small enough to manage alone.

But UNDERFLOW's dungeon was 12,000 square feet. Gerald would have access to it. He would be inside another Core's infrastructure, rebuilding it to code.

And UNDERFLOW would be inside his.

The message came one final time.

"I KNOW ABOUT THE AUDIT. I KNOW ABOUT THE CONDUITS. I KNOW ABOUT THE FOURTEEN. I PUT THREE OF THEM THERE. THE OTHER ELEVEN ARE NOT MINE. I WANT TO HELP YOU. BUT I NEED YOU TO CITE ME."

Gerald looked at the form he was filling out. Form 7-A. The appeal. The safe path. The path that used only what he knew.

Then he looked at the message. UNDERFLOW. Three hundred and forty meters away. A drainage Core. The eleven other conduits were not his. Someone else had opened them. Someone Gerald did not know.

Gerald deleted Form 7-A.

He opened Form 4-B: Citation, Standard. He began to fill it out.
