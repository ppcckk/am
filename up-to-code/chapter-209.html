Chapter 209: Cite with Chalk

# Chapter 209

The pen snapped.

Gerald held the broken halves together, staring at the fractured plastic. A hairline fracture ran through the barrel, splitting the word *Compliance* in two.

He set the pieces on the concrete floor with deliberate care, aligning them edge-to-edge.

The click of the mechanism was gone. The tactile satisfaction of the plunger was nonexistent. He had no backup pens.

In the sub-basement, supplies were rationed by the System's inventory limits, and he had exhausted his allotment for writing implements three days ago.

Kaelen watched him from the corner, shifting his weight from one foot to the other. The young adventurer's boots scraped against the damp stone, a sound that grated on Gerald's nerves like nails on a chalkboard.

"Can I help?" Kaelen asked.

"No," Gerald said. He picked up a fragment of chalk from a nearby ledge.

It was white, dusty, and insufficient.

"This is a matter of record. Chalk smudges. Ink is permanent.

The Inspector General does not accept smudges.

"

The notification hovered in his peripheral vision, pulsing with a slow, rhythmic red light.

**Violation Log:** *Sector 4 Lighting Deficiency.* **Severity:** *Moderate.* **Status:** *Unaddressed.* **Time Remaining:** *00:58:12.*

Fifty-eight minutes. The tape figure had dissolved into the shadows, leaving the smell of adhesive and ozone hanging in the stagnant air. That was the smell of bureaucracy. It smelled like old paper and burnt copper.

Gerald looked up at the ceiling.

Sector 4 was the eastern corridor, the one leading to the mold farm, and the bulbs there were standard dungeon-issue incandescent spheres, rated for 40 watts. They had failed.

Not all at once, but gradually, flickering until one by one they died. The System had detected the drop in lumens below the minimum safety threshold of 5 foot-candles.

He needed light. He needed proof of light. And he needed to submit a Plan of Correction before the hour expired.

The problem was not just the bulb. The problem was the conduit.

Gerald moved toward the wall. His hand brushed against the rough brick.

Beneath the mortar, he could feel the faint hum of the magical current powering the dungeon's ambient mana grid.

It was irregular. Stuttering. That meant the problem was not the bulb itself, but the circuit feeding it.

A voltage drop.

If he simply replaced the bulb, it would burn out within the hour. The System would detect the instability and issue a second citation for *Unstable Infrastructure*. Two citations in one hour would trigger a Mandatory Shutdown.

He closed his eyes. He visualized the wiring diagram he had mentally memorized when he first installed the grid. Section 4, Line B. Shared circuit with the ventilation fans.

If the fans were running, the amperage was too high, and he walked back to the main console, a slab of obsidian that served as his control interface. He tapped the screen. The icons were jagged, pixelated from lack of maintenance resources. He navigated to the HVAC controls.

*Fan Speed: 100%.*

Of course. The mold spores preferred high humidity and low air turnover to grow faster, but the code required a minimum air exchange rate for human-occupied spaces. Since the dungeon had registered as

"semi-occupied" due to Kaelen's presence, the ventilation had auto-maximized.

He turned the dial down to 20%.

The hum of the fans dropped to a whisper. The air grew still. The dust motes in the beam of Kaelen's actual flashlight (which Gerald had forbidden him from using as a light source, only as a tool for visibility) danced lazily.

**System Alert:** *Air Quality Index declining.* **Penalty:** *Respiratory Debuff for All Spawns.*

Gerald sighed. He turned the fans back to 50%. A compromise. Not ideal, but survivable.

Now for the lighting, and he looked at Kaelen.

"Go to the supply closet in Sector 2."

"I don't know where that is," Kaelen said.

"Second corridor on the left, past the rat trap. It's behind a false panel marked with a red triangle."

"That's just a wall."

"It is a storage unit. Go."

Kaelen hesitated, then jogged off into the darkness. Gerald listened to his footsteps fading away.

He didn't trust the kid, but he trusted the code, and the supply closet was mandated by Fire Safety Regulation 14-C. It existed. It had to exist.

Gerald returned to the broken pen. He couldn't write a formal Plan of Correction without ink. But he could file an Emergency Variance Request. It required no pen, only vocal authorization and a physical demonstration of the hazard.

He walked to the center of the corridor. He raised his voice.

"System. I am invoking Emergency Variance Clause 9. Subsection D."

The air shimmered. A transparent panel materialized in front of him.

**Emergency Variance Request: Accepted.** **Requirement:** *Demonstrate immediate mitigation of hazard.* **Time Limit:** *00:55:00.*

He had fifty-five minutes to prove the lights were fixed, or that the lack of light was a controlled risk.

He couldn't fix the conduit. He didn't have copper wire.

He didn't have a soldering iron.

But he had something else, and he had a water leak.

The leak was in the ceiling above Sector 3, dripping onto the floor of Sector 4. It was a Class 3 Bio-Hazard violation, currently under probationary review. If he could redirect the water, he could use it.

Water conducted electricity, but pure water did not. The water in Ashford's municipal library sub-basement was hard, mineral-rich, and acidic. It was a poor conductor. But if he could mix it with something conductive, he could create a temporary bridge.

What was conductive in a dungeon?

Mana crystals, and he had three low-grade mana crystals in his inventory. They were worth nothing to him as currency. They were worth everything to him as components.

He pulled one from his pocket. It was cloudy, pulsing with a faint blue light. He held it up to the flickering bulb above him.

He climbed onto the crate. The bulb was dead. He unscrewed it.

The filament was gone. A blackened void where light had been.

He took the second mana crystal. He jammed it into the socket where the bulb should have been. It didn't fit. The threads were stripped.

He tried the third. Same problem.

The System was mocking him. The fixture was broken. This was a Structural Failure, not just an electrical one.

**New Violation Detected:** *Faulty Electrical Fixture.* **Penalty:** *Shock Damage to All Spawns.*

Gerald froze. He had made it worse.

He looked at the socket, and the metal housing was cracked. He could see the live wire inside, frayed and sparking. If he touched it, he would take damage.

But he was a Core.

He didn't have a body. He had a footprint.

He could manipulate the structure directly, and he reached out with his mind, trying to fuse the broken metal. The System resisted. *Unauthorized Modification.*

He needed permission. He needed a variance.

He spoke to the air.

"System. I am attempting a temporary repair to prevent fire hazard. This falls under Emergency Maintenance Exemption."

**Request Denied.** **Reason:** *Exemption requires a licensed electrician class. You are Class 0.*

Gerald grit his teeth. He didn't have a license. He had a thirty-one-year career of denying licenses to others.

He looked at the frayed wire. If he let it spark, it would ignite the dust. Dust was fuel. Fire was the ultimate violation.

He had to use the water, and he jumped down from the crate. He walked to the leak. The water dripped steadily, pooling on the concrete.

He cupped his hands. The water was cold.

It smelled of iron and rot.

He walked back to the broken socket. He didn't have a bucket. He had to use his hands.

He scooped up a handful of water. He held it over the socket.

"Kaelen!" he shouted. "Bring the bucket!"

Kaelen appeared from the shadows, holding a rusted metal pail. He looked confused.

"I found the closet," Kaelen said. "It was empty."

"Put the water in the pail. Bring it here."

Kaelen dumped the water. Gerald poured it over the socket.

The water flowed into the fixture. It hit the frayed wire.

For a second, nothing happened.

Then, a hiss. Steam rose from the socket. The water was boiling.

Gerald stepped back. He watched as the water mixed with the dust in the socket, forming a muddy paste. He pushed the mana crystal into the wet socket.

The crystal absorbed the water. The minerals in the water bridged the gap between the crystal's energy field and the live wire.

A dim, blue glow emerged from the socket.

It wasn't bright. It wasn't even ten foot-candles. But it was light.

The System's scanner swept the room.

**Lumens Detected:** *2.* **Violation Status:** *Active.* **Penalty:** *Vision Reduction.*

It wasn't enough. The threshold was five.

Gerald looked at the other bulbs in Sector 4. There were four of them. Only one was working, barely. The others were dark.

He had three crystals left in his inventory. He had one in the socket. That wasn't enough to cover all four fixtures.

He needed a multiplier, and he looked at Kaelen.

"Give me your sword," Gerald said.

"My sword?"

"It's enchanted. Low-level. It has a light cantrip. I need to bypass the fixture."

Kaelen drew his sword. The blade glowed with a soft, golden luminescence.

"Is this legal?" Kaelen asked.

"It is a temporary fixture," Gerald said. "Code does not specify the source of illumination, only the quantity."

He took the sword. He held it horizontally across the broken socket, using the blade as a conductor. The metal of the sword touched the wet crystal. The energy transferred.

The sword's light amplified. It cast a beam across the room.

It was bright. Too bright.

The shadows retreated. The mold spores in the corner shrunk back, curling into tight balls. Their vision debuff was gone.

But the System reacted differently.

**Alert:** *Unregulated Energy Source Detected.* **Penalty:** *Mana Drain for All Spawns.*

The sword flickered. Kaelen gasped, clutching his head.

"It's hurting them," Kaelen said.

Gerald looked at the sword. It was drawing mana from the dungeon's core to sustain the light. It was a parasite.

He couldn't use it. It was a different violation.

He dropped the sword. The light died. The room plunged back into darkness.

**Time Remaining:** *00:12:00.*

Twelve minutes.

Gerald's heart rate—simulated by the mana flow in his core—spiked. He was running out of options. He had violated three different codes in the last hour. He was approaching the threshold for a Total Shutdown.

He looked at the water on the floor, and he looked at the broken socket.

He looked at his inventory.

He had no more crystals. No more bulbs. No more wire.

But he had the leak, and the leak was a violation. But it was also a resource.

If he could turn the leak into a feature, he could justify the lighting.

Water reflects light.

If he could get light hitting the water, he could create a reflection. A mirror.

He didn't have a mirror. But he had the wet floor.

He needed a light source high up, low intensity, aimed at the pool.

He looked at the ceiling.

Above the leak, there was a small ventilation shaft. Inside the shaft, there were bioluminescent moss spores.

They were passive. They emitted a faint green glow. They were not electrical.

They were biological.

If he opened the vent, the moss would be exposed to the light of the broken socket.

But the socket was dead.

Wait.

The mana crystal in the socket was still glowing. Dimly.

He could use the crystal's glow to illuminate the moss. The moss would reflect the light downward. The wet floor would reflect it again.

It was a chain reaction. Inefficient. But it might just meet the threshold.

He climbed back onto the crate. He used his fingernails to pry open the ventilation grate. The metal screamed. It was rusted shut.

He pried it open.

Inside, the moss glowed green. It was pulsing. Alive.

He adjusted the angle of the mana crystal in the socket. He tilted it upward, aiming at the moss.

The green light intensified.

The light hit the moss. The moss reflected it. The light bounced off the ceiling. It hit the wet floor.

The floor shimmered.

The System scanned.

**Lumens Detected:** *4.*

Still not enough.

Gerald looked at Kaelen.

"Stand in the light."

Kaelen moved into the center of the room. His armor reflected the green glow.

**Lumens Detected:** *5.*

The number flashed green in Gerald's vision.

**Violation Status:** *Resolved.* **Penalty:** *None.*

Gerald exhaled. His mana flow stabilized.

He climbed down. He wiped his hands on his pants. They were stained with rust and mud.

The hour passed. The timer hit zero.

The System did not send an audit. It did not send a fine. It simply marked the violation as *Resolved via Improvised Means.*

Gerald slumped against the wall. He was exhausted. Not physically, but mentally. The mental strain of holding the dungeon together, of calculating angles and amperages, was heavier than any battle.

He had saved the dungeon. For now.

But he had changed the rules, and he had used a biological organism as a lighting fixture. He had used a magic item as a conductor. He had cited himself and then fixed it in real-time.

The System had allowed it.

Why?

Gerald frowned. He opened his interface. He looked at the logs.

There was a new entry. Not from the Inspector General. From the System itself.

**Anomaly Detected:** *User Gerald Hutchins utilized Non-Standard Infrastructure to meet Code Compliance.* **Note:** *This method is not illegal. It is inefficient.* **Reward:** *Skill Unlocked: *Improvisational Maintenance.*

A skill?

Gerald had never unlocked a skill before. He was a bureaucrat. He filed forms. He did not improvise.

He looked at the skill description.

**Improvisational Maintenance:** *Allows the Core to repurpose environmental hazards as temporary infrastructure solutions. Effectiveness decreases by 10% for each consecutive use.*

It was a loophole. A dangerous one.

It meant he could keep doing this. Citing himself. Fixing it with junk.

It was a slippery slope, and he heard a noise. A soft, clicking sound from the ventilation shaft.

Gerald froze.

The moss was moving. It wasn't just glowing. It was growing. It was spreading out of the shaft, creeping down the wall, toward the mana crystal.

It was consuming the energy source.

And something else was coming up the shaft.

A pair of yellow eyes appeared in the dark.

Gerald stepped back.

The thing emerged, which was small. It was made of moss and wire. It had no face.

It had only teeth.

It looked at Gerald.

It looked at the mana crystal.

It scuttled toward the socket.

**New Entity Spawned:** *Code-Infested Vermin.* **Classification:** *Hazard.*

Gerald didn't have a weapon. He didn't have a guard. He had a broken pen and a wet floor.

He looked at the notification.

**Action Required:** *Remove Hazard.*

He had to kill it. But if he killed it, he would have to file a report for *Unlicensed Biological Entity.* He had done that once, and it had taken him three days.

He couldn't file a report. He had work to do.

He looked at the creature. It hissed. The sound was like tearing paper.

Gerald picked up the broken halves of his pen, and he walked toward the creature.

He was going to cite it.
