Chapter 217: The Appeal Begins

# Chapter 217

The darkness smelled of ozone and wet concrete.

Gerald Hutchins hung suspended in a void that had no floor, no ceiling, and no code. His hands were bound by chains of compressed light, each link humming with a frequency that made his teeth ache.

He blinked.

The blackness shifted.

A desk appeared. Not a dungeon table.

A municipal desk.

Grey laminate. Chipped at the corner. A lamp with a frayed cord.

Gerald's stomach dropped. The frayed cord was a violation. Section 304.12, paragraph B. Electrical hazards in public access areas.

He tried to reach for his clipboard. It was gone.

In its place sat a single manila folder, and the retainer stood behind the desk. His suit was immaculate, but his face was blurred, like a photo left in the rain. He did not speak. He simply slid the folder across the laminate.

Gerald reached out. The chains tightened.

He gritted his teeth. He pulled. The light burned his wrists, but he pulled. His fingers brushed the tab.

He opened the folder.

Inside was a blueprint.

Not of the library. Not of Ashford.

It was a blueprint of the System itself, and the lines were drawn in red ink. They pulsed.

Gerald stared at the central node. It was labeled *Core Hutchins*.

The surrounding nodes were labeled *Coalition*, *Inspectorate*, and *Void*.

The connections between them were broken. Dashed lines.

"Contempt," the retainer said.

His voice sounded like dry leaves skittering on pavement.

"You failed to appear for the Audit.

You filed a motion.

You argued."

Gerald looked up.

"I filed a defense."

"Invalid," the retainer said. "The Audit Staff does not accept defenses from non-employees."

The chains yanked Gerald backward. He slammed into the chair behind him. The laminate cracked.

Pain shot through his spine. Real pain. Not the dull ache of a bruised ego, but the sharp, metallic sting of a neural override.

Gerald gasped. He looked at his hands. They were turning translucent. Blue light leaked from his fingertips.

He was being unmade, and the System was deleting him.

No. Not deleting.

Integrating.

The blueprint on the desk flared. The node labeled *Core Hutchins* merged with the node labeled *Inspectorate*.

Gerald's breath hitched. If he became part of the Inspectorate, he lost his Core status. He lost his dungeon. He lost his rights.

He became a tool. A pen. A stamp.

He could not let that happen.

Gerald closed his eyes. He focused on the blueprint. He looked for the flaw.

Every plan had a flaw. Every building had a crack.

He scanned the red lines. He traced the connection from *Core Hutchins* to *Void*.

There.

A single wire. Thin. Uninsulated.

It ran directly into the ground.

Grounding fault, and the System had forgotten to ground its own authority.

Gerald opened his eyes. He looked at the retainer.

"This audit is incomplete," Gerald said. His voice was steady, though his hands shook.

The retainer paused. The blurred face tilted.

"Explain," the retainer said.

"You cannot integrate a Core without establishing a physical anchor," Gerald said. "The blueprint shows a grounding wire.

But there is no earth.

No soil.

No foundation."

Gerald pointed to the dashed line.

"Your Authority is floating," Gerald said. "It has no code. It has no location."

The retainer stiffened. The air around him shimmered.

"Careful, Mr. Hutchins," the retainer warned.

"I am citing you," Gerald said. "Violation 7-9-Alpha. Structural instability of an appellate body."

The retainer's eyes narrowed. He picked up a stamp.

He brought it down on the folder.

*DENIED.*

The chains tightened.

Gerald screamed as the light seared his skin.

But he did not stop, and he reached into his pocket.

He had no pockets. He was a Core. He had no body.

But he had a memory, and he remembered the smell of the library basement. The damp. The mold.

He remembered the water leak he had installed himself. The one that violated code but also created the necessary humidity for his spawn to survive.

The leak was not a bug.

It was a feature.

Gerald pushed his will into the void. He pushed the concept of *moisture* into the blueprint.

The red lines bleeded.

Ink spread across the paper.

The grounding wire, exposed by the water damage, sparked.

The desk shook.

The retainer stumbled back. The blurred face rippled.

"Stop," the retainer hissed. "This is not permitted."

"Water damage," Gerald said. "Uncontrolled condensation. Violation of HVAC standards."

The blueprint tore.

The red lines snapped.

The chains around Gerald's wrists dissolved into mist, and he fell forward.

He hit the laminate floor.

The void collapsed.

Concrete rushed up to meet him.

Gerald gasped. Air filled his lungs. Cold, damp air.

He opened his eyes, and he was back in his dungeon.

The sub-basement.

The water leak was dripping from the ceiling. Plop. Plop. Plop.

The light in the room was flickering. The fluorescent tubes buzzed with a dying hum.

Gerald sat up. His wrists were red. Raw.

Around him, the dungeon walls groaned. The stone shifted.

The retainer was gone.

But the threat remained.

A new notification flashed in his vision.

**ALERT: STRUCTURAL FAILURE IMMINENT.**

**CAUSE: EXTERNAL INTERFERENCE.**

**STATUS: CRITICAL.**

Gerald stood up. His legs felt like lead.

He looked toward the main corridor, and the stone floor was cracking.

Not from age.

From pressure.

Something was pushing through the foundation.

Gerald walked to the crack. He knelt. He placed his hand on the fissure.

It vibrated.

A low, rhythmic thumping.

Like a heartbeat.

Or a drum.

Gerald's eyes narrowed. He recognized the frequency.

It was the same frequency as the Auditor's clipboard, and she was not gone.

She was waiting.

And she was not the only one.

From the darkness of the unlit corridor, a figure stepped forward.

It was not a goblin. Not a rat. Not a mold spore.

It was a human.

A young man in adventurer's gear. Leather armor. A sword strapped to his back.

He looked terrified.

But he held a scroll in his hand.

Gerald stood up. He wiped the dust from his pants.

"Excuse me," Gerald said. "You are trespassing on private property."

The adventurer flinched. He held up the scroll.

"Mr. Hutchins?" the man asked. His voice cracked.

"Please. You have to help me."

Gerald frowned.

"I am currently under audit. I do not accept visitors."

"They're coming," the adventurer whispered. "The Coalition. They're not gone. They're here."

Gerald looked at the scroll. The wax seal was black.

He took it, and he broke the seal.

He read the first line.

**SUBPOENA FOR IMMEDIATE INSPECTION.**

**ISSUED BY: THE GRAND MAGISTRATE OF THE SYSTEM.**

**SUBJECT: GERALD HUTCHINS.**

**ALLEGATION: FRAUDULENT COMPLIANCE.**

Gerald looked up. The adventurer was sweating.

"What did you do?" the adventurer asked.

Gerald looked at the blueprint in his mind. The bleeding red lines. The grounding fault.

He had not fixed the flaw, and he had used it to escape.

But the System did not forgive.

The System punished.

And the Grand Magistrate did not accept apologies.

The dungeon walls shook again. Debris fell from the ceiling.

A chunk of plaster hit the adventurer's shoulder. He cried out.

Gerald did not move, and he stared at the subpoena.

Under the allegation, there was a clause.

**PENALTY: TOTAL DEMOLITION.**

**TIMELINE: SIXTY MINUTES.**

Gerald looked at the clock on his internal HUD.

58 minutes left, and he looked at the adventurer.

"Who sent you?" Gerald asked.

"I don't know," the adventurer said. "They said you were the only one who could stop it. They said you speak the language of the walls."

Gerald looked at the crack in the floor.

The thumping grew louder.

The ground beneath the library was shifting.

Someone was digging.

Not through the dungeon.

Through the earth.

Underneath it.

Gerald realized the truth, and the Auditor had not just integrated him.

She had exposed him.

And now, the Coalition was using the System's own demolition protocol against him.

They were not attacking his dungeon.

They were attacking its foundation, and they were going to drop the library on his head.

Gerald straightened his tie. It was crooked.

He pulled it straight.

He looked at the adventurer.

"Get out," Gerald said.

"What?"

"Get out," Gerald said. "This building is condemned."

The adventurer hesitated.

"But if you're condemned, you die."

"If I stay, I die," Gerald said. "If I leave, I abandon my post. That is a Class A violation."

The adventurer looked at him with confusion.

Gerald turned away. He walked to the water leak.

He placed his hand on the dripping pipe, and he closed his eyes.

He did not try to fix the pipe.

He did not try to reinforce the wall.

He did something else.

He filed a report.

**REPORT: EMERGENCY SHUTOFF.**

**CAUSE: UNAUTHORIZED EXCAVATION.**

**ACTION: IMMEDIATE CESSATION OF ALL UTILITY SERVICES TO THIS ZONE.**

The water stopped, and the lights went out.

Total darkness.

The adventurer screamed.

"What are you doing?"

Gerald did not answer, and he listened.

The thumping stopped.

The vibration ceased.

The pressure on the foundation vanished.

For a moment, there was silence.

Then, a new sound.

A grinding noise.

Like stone against stone.

The walls slid.

Not down.

Sideways, and the dungeon was expanding.

The 4,000 square feet was becoming 8,000.

Then 12,000.

Gerald opened his eyes.

He was standing in a cavern.

The library above him was still there.

But the space beneath it had changed, and he had not just shut off the utilities.

He had triggered a safety override.

When a zone is condemned, the System isolates it.

It separates it from the rest of the world.

Gerald was no longer in Ashford, and he was in a pocket dimension.

A quarantine zone.

And the Coalition was outside the walls.

Trapped.

Gerald looked at the adventurer, and the man was staring at the expanding walls in awe.

"You... you cut us off," the adventurer said.

"I cited the excavation," Gerald said. "It violated the boundary laws. The System enforced it."

"But you're trapped," the adventurer said.

Gerald looked at the dark expanse of his new dungeon, and he looked at the spawn points.

They were glowing.

New entities were forming.

Not mold.

Not rats.

Constructs.

Made of light.

Made of code.

Gerald smiled. It was a thin, paper-cut smile.

"I am not trapped," Gerald said.

"I am in control."

A notification flashed.

**NEW MECHANIC UNLOCKED: QUARANTINE PROTOCOL.**

**BONUS: ALL SPAWNED ENTITIES GAIN +50% DEFENSE AGAINST EXTERNAL INTRUSIONS.**

**WARNING: EXIT REQUIRED TO RECONNECT TO MAIN SYSTEM.**

**TIME LIMIT: 10 MINUTES.**

Gerald looked at the timer.

9:59, and he looked at the adventurer.

"Leave," Gerald said. "Now."

The adventurer ran.

Gerald turned to the center of his new, vast dungeon, and he saw a figure standing there.

Not an adventurer.

Not a goblin.

The Auditor Prime.

She stood by a new desk, and she held a clipboard.

She looked at Gerald.

"You bought yourself time," she said.

Gerald looked at the timer.

9:58, and he looked at the Auditor.

He looked at the exit portal behind her.

It was shrinking.

Closing.

Gerald took a step forward.

Then he stopped, and he looked at the blueprint in his mind.

The red lines were gone.

But there was a new line.

A black line.

It ran from his Core to the Auditor's desk.

And it was vibrating.

Gerald realized something, and the Auditor had not come to stop him.

She had come to watch.

She was waiting for him to make a mistake.

If he ran, he escaped.

If he stayed, he died.

But if he did neither...

Gerald reached into his inventory, and he pulled out the broken seal of the subpoena.

He walked toward the Auditor.

He did not run.

He did not hide.

He walked, and he stopped five feet from her desk.

He placed the broken seal on the laminate.

"Motion to reopen," Gerald said.

The Auditor blinked.

The timer hit 5:00.

The portal behind her vanished.

Gerald was alone with her.

In the dark.

In the quiet.

The Auditor smiled, which was not a paper-cut smile this time.

It was hungry.

"Welcome to the appeal, Mr. Hutchins," she said.

She picked up her pen.

And she wrote.
