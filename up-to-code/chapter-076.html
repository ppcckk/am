Chapter 76: Moisture Intrusion

# Chapter 76

The Counter-Citation notice arrived at 0347, printed on a sheet of vellum that materialized on Gerald's clipboard with a soft pop, like a cork leaving a bottle. He did not look up from his draft. He had been writing for six hours and his pen was still moving, because stopping meant thinking about what he had written, and thinking about what he had written meant admitting that the Handrail Qualification Clause he had spent six hours constructing would, if applied to his own dungeon, disqualify his own handrail.

He finished the sentence. He set the pen down. He read the vellum.

"COUNTER-CITATION FILED. CORE 5523 v.

CORE 1.

CATEGORY: Constructive Egress Failure. ALLEGED VIOLATION: Handrail non-conformance to Code Precedent 9.4.1 as established by respondent in Hearing Case 4471-C. SERVED: 0347.

RESPONSE WINDOW: 48 HOURS.

HEARING SCHEDULED: 0800."

Gerald read it twice. Then he read it a third time, because the second reading had produced a feeling in his chest that he recognized from thirty-one years of municipal work: the feeling of a man who had cited the wrong building.

He stood.

His dungeon responded to his movement the way it always did, the walls shifting slightly to accommodate his attention, the handrail on the eastern corridor glowing faintly as if it knew it was about to be discussed in a legal proceeding. Gerald looked at it. Thirty-four inches.

Right side only. Level floor.

He looked at the vellum again.

Core 5523, and he knew the number.

He had not cited this one. It was not on his list of fourteen.

It was a new core, registered approximately three weeks ago in the industrial district, and he had not served it because he had not been close enough. The Citation range was two hundred meters. His dungeon was beneath the library.

The industrial district was four hundred meters east.

Which meant Core 5523 had come to him.

Gerald checked the service notation at the bottom of the vellum. "Served via public right-of-way: Ashford Municipal Water Main, Segment 14-C."

He sat back down. He picked up his pen. He did not write anything. He sat still for a long time.

At 0415, he opened his municipal code reference.

Not the System code. The actual Ashford Municipal Code, which he had memorized over three decades and which existed in his memory with the completeness that only a man who has read the same document forty times can achieve. Section 12.4.

Municipal Right-of-Way Definition, and he recited it silently. A public right-of-way is any strip of land or infrastructure owned by the municipality and dedicated to public use, including but not limited to streets, sidewalks, utility easements, and subterranean infrastructure corridors.

He then opened the System's Citation Service rules. He had read them once, in the first week, and had not needed to reread them because they were simple. A Citation requires physical presence to serve. The citing core must project a portion of itself into the target's dungeon footprint and deliver the notice directly.

But a Counter-Citation was not a Citation. It was a different procedural instrument. And Gerald noticed, in the fine print at the bottom of the vellum, a clause he had not seen before because he had never received a Counter-Citation.

"Counter-Citations may be served via any connected infrastructure pathway, including shared municipal utilities, where both cores' footprints intersect or abut a common public right-of-way."

Gerald stared at this clause. He stared at it with the same cold, methodical attention he had given the handrail precedent clause three chapters ago, aware that the details were wrong and always in the wrong font size.

He pulled up his dungeon footprint map.

He pulled up the municipal water main map, which he had digitized in his second week as a core because he had found it unsatisfying not to know what was beneath his dungeon. The water main ran directly beneath the library. It passed within three meters of Gerald's eastern wall.

It did not pass within three meters of Core 5523's footprint, because Core 5523 was four hundred meters away.

But the water main was a continuous pipe, which ran from one end of the city to the other.

And Gerald's dungeon was connected to it. And Core 5523's dungeon was connected to it.

Not directly. Not physically. But through the pipe.

Through the same pipe. The same municipal water main that passed through both cores' subterranean neighborhoods.

Gerald's pen moved before his brain finished the thought. He wrote: "Objection to Counter-Citation Service. Jurisdictional basis: public right-of-way exclusion."

He underlined "jurisdictional." He underlined it again.

The hearing was at 0800. He had three hours and forty-five minutes to prepare a defense that was not about handrails. It was about whether a water main constituted a valid service pathway.

At 0600, Gerald walked to the eastern corridor, and he placed his hand on the wall. He could feel the water main behind it, the faint vibration of municipal water pressure, the hum of infrastructure that had been running since 1987. He pressed his ear to the wall.

He heard the pipe.

He heard the flow. He heard the specific, slightly irregular rhythm of a pipe that had a minor blockage at the junction box near Elm Street, which he had reported to Public Works in 2019 and which had never been fixed.

He pulled out his clipboard. He wrote a note. He did not send it to Public Works. He kept it for himself.

At 0715, the System opened the hearing chamber.

It was the same space as always, a neutral gray room with two podiums and a elevated chair where the adjudicator sat. The adjudicator today was not the same one who had presided over the 4471 hearing. This one was taller, and its face had more angles, and it did not look at Gerald with the mild boredom of a functionary.

It looked at him with attention.

"Core 1," the adjudicator said. "You have filed an objection to service. State your basis."

Gerald stood at his podium. He did not look at Core 5523's podium. He did not need to. He had not seen Core 5523 in person and he did not intend to start now.

"The Counter-Citation was served via public right-of-way," Gerald said.

"Section 12.4 of the Ashford Municipal Code defines a public right-of-way as infrastructure dedicated to public use.

A municipal water main is public infrastructure. It is not part of either core's dungeon footprint.

It is neutral ground."

The adjudicator tilted its head. "The System's service rules permit Counter-Citation via shared municipal utilities."

"The System's service rules are not the only rules," Gerald said. "The System's rules govern dungeon-to-dungeon interaction.

They do not override municipal jurisdiction, and a water main is not a dungeon feature; it is a public utility. It belongs to the City of Ashford.

Neither Core 1 nor Core 5523 has standing to claim it as a service pathway, because neither of us owns it."

Silence. The adjudicator looked at Core 5523's podium.

"Core 5523, respond."

A voice came from the other podium. It was not a voice Gerald recognized. It was higher than he expected, almost musical, with a quality of amusement that made Gerald's stomach tighten.

"The System's service rules explicitly permit Counter-Citation via shared municipal utilities," Core 5523 said. "The respondent is citing a municipal code that does not apply to System proceedings. The System operates above municipal jurisdiction."

Gerald had expected this. He had prepared for this. He opened his clipboard.

"The System operates above municipal jurisdiction for purposes of dungeon regulation," he said. "It does not operate above municipal jurisdiction for purposes of property ownership.

The water main is not a System object, which was constructed by the City of Ashford in 1987. It is maintained by the City of Ashford. It is subject to municipal code.

The System did not create it. The System did not register it.

The System has no authority to designate it as a service pathway because the System has no authority over it in the first place."

He paused. He checked his notes.

"Furthermore," he continued, "the water main in question has an active maintenance deficiency.

A blockage at the Elm Street junction box was reported to Public Works on March 14, 2019, under Work Order 2019-0447. It has not been resolved.

The pipe is therefore not in a state of full operational compliance. A Counter-Citation served through a non-compliant utility pathway is void under the System's own service integrity rules, which require that service pathways be functional and unobstructed."

This was not in the service rules. Gerald had made this up. He had made it up with the confidence of a man who had spent thirty-one years writing citations that were technically correct in ways that other people could not immediately verify, and who had learned that the System's rules were written by a body that had not anticipated a man who would cross-reference them against actual municipal maintenance records.

The adjudicator was quiet for eleven seconds. Gerald counted them. He always counted.

"The objection is sustained," the adjudicator said. "Counter-Citation void for lack of valid service pathway. Core 5523 may refile via direct physical presence if it wishes to proceed."

The hearing chamber dissolved. Gerald was back in his dungeon.

The eastern corridor hummed.

The water main hummed. The handrail glowed faintly and did not know it had been saved by a lie.

Gerald sat down, and he picked up his pen. He looked at his draft amendment.

He looked at the handrail.

Thirty-four inches. Right side only. Level floor.

He crossed out the first paragraph of the amendment. He started over.

At 0900, a new notice appeared on his clipboard. It was not a Citation. It was not a Counter-Citation. It was a line of text in a font Gerald had never seen before, smaller than the System's usual typeface, and it read:

"AUDIT NOTICE: FULL COMPLIANCE INSPECTION. CORE 1.

SCHEDULED: 12 HOURS.

INSPECTOR: THE INSPECTOR GENERAL.

SCOPE: ALL CATEGORIES. NO THRESHOLD EXEMPTIONS."

Gerald read it. He read it again. He understood what "no threshold exemptions" meant.

He had built his dungeon to meet the minimum thresholds. Every category.

Ventilation, fire suppression, structural integrity, electrical, egress.

All at minimum. He had not exceeded them because exceeding them cost resources he did not have, and because his professional instinct was compliance, not excellence.

A full audit with no threshold exemptions did not ask whether his dungeon met the minimum. It asked whether his dungeon was perfect.

And Gerald knew, with the specific knowledge of a man who has spent his life looking at things that are almost right but not quite, that his dungeon was not perfect. It was adequate. Adequate was not perfect.

He thought about the water leak.

The one from the first week. The one he had measured, assessed, and determined was below the violation threshold for Category 7, Moisture Intrusion.

He had filed it internally, and he had noted it; had not fixed it because it did not require fixing.

A full audit would not note it. A full audit would cite it. And a full audit citation would not be a debuff. It would be a shutdown order.

Gerald looked at his clipboard. He looked at the draft amendment. He looked at the water main vibrating behind the eastern wall.

He had twelve hours. He had a water leak that was technically compliant; had an Inspector General who was going to look at everything.

He stood up. He walked to the eastern corridor.

He pressed his hand against the wall, and he felt the moisture.

He had always felt it. He had always known it was there.

He pulled out his pen. He opened a new page. He wrote: "Form 7-C: Moisture Intrusion Remediation Plan."

He had twelve hours to fix something he had decided was not broken.

He had decided wrong.
