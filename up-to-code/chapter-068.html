Chapter 68: Penalty Queued

# Chapter 68

Gerald was on his knees checking the foundation seal when the air pressure changed.

He knew the pressure change was coming, and tracked it as he would weather fronts in the library's HVAC system.

The System had announced the Inspector General's arrival, and the announcement had carried a pressure signature. Gerald had felt it in his walls.

Two minutes and eleven seconds remained. He had not finished checking the electrical panel. He had checked it. He was checking it again because the panel was the last thing and the last thing always had something.

He pulled the cover.

Bus bars clean. Ground continuity solid.

He touched the neutral bar and felt the hum of proper bonding through his fingertips.

He replaced the cover. He replaced the screws. He tightened them in sequence.

Star pattern. Three passes.

One minute and forty-three seconds, and he stood. He looked at his inventory. He looked at it again because the first look might have been wrong.

Zero blank forms.

He had used them all.

Form 14 had been the last, and Form 14 had been the right call.

It had been the only call. It had dissolved his Compliance Rating and made him municipal.

It had made him a parking garage.

But the Inspector General did not audit parking garages. The Inspector General audited Cores.

And Gerald was still, in some fundamental way, a Core, and the System had unclassified him, not unmade him. He was a Core with no rating. A Core with no classification.

A Core that the Inspector General was coming to classify.

Classification required a Form 0. Gerald had used his Form 0 to register.

He had used it on himself, and he remembered the exact moment; the pen had felt wrong in his hand. The paper had felt like skin.

One minute and twelve seconds.

Gerald walked to his egress route. He walked it.

He walked it again, and the handrails were secure.

The signage was compliant. The width was thirty-two inches, which was the minimum for residential egress and sufficient for a space with fewer than fifty occupants.

His space had no occupants. He was the occupant. He was also the building.

He had never written a citation on himself; had written Form 14 on himself, which was technically a self-classification, not a citation.

A difference existed; a citation was punitive. A self-classification was administrative. Gerald clung to this distinction as he did to the concept of load-bearing walls.

It was structural.

Without it, everything else collapsed.

Forty-one seconds.

The air pressure dropped again.

Not gradually this time. Instantly. Like a door opening in a pressurized cabin.

Gerald felt it in his floor, in his handrails, in the water pipes he had insulated last week.

The Inspector General did not appear.

It arrived.

A difference existed. Appearing implied a transition from nothing to something.

Arriving implied a transition from somewhere else to here. The Inspector General had always been somewhere else. It was coming from somewhere else.

It was a clipboard.

Gerald stared at it. The clipboard was four feet tall and three feet wide, standing upright on a base that looked like a surveyor's tripod.

The surface was not wood, which was not plastic. It was something the System had generated that looked like the memory of a clipboard, the way a dungeon corridor looked like the memory of a hallway. The paper on it was not paper.

It was a notification rendered in physical form.

Gerald looked at the notification.

[INSPECTOR GENERAL: AUDIT COMMENCED] [SUBJECT: CORE 001, GERALD HUTCHINS] [CLASSIFICATION: UNCLASSIFIED] [PROCEDURE: CLASSIFICATION-CONDITIONAL FULL COMPLIANCE AUDIT] [NOTE: Subject has no Compliance Rating. Audit will establish baseline. Subject will be classified per results.]

Gerald read it. He read it again.

Classification-conditional, and the audit was not checking whether he was compliant; the audit was determining what he was, and then checking whether that thing was compliant. If he was classified as a parking garage, the standards were one thing.

If he was classified as a residential dwelling, the standards were another. If he was classified as a Core, the standards were a third thing entirely.

He had been a Core. He had been a parking garage. He was now something the System did not yet have a word for.

The Inspector General's clipboard rotated.

A pen appeared on its surface.

Not a pen Gerald could use, and a pen that wrote in a language Gerald could not read but could somehow understand.

The pen started writing; the first check was ventilation. Gerald watched the pen move. It wrote a line.

[VENTILATION: ADEQUATE]

The second check was egress.

[EGRESS: COMPLIANT]

The third was fire suppression.

[FIRE SUPPRESSION: COMPLIANT]

The fourth was electrical.

[ELECTRICAL: COMPLIANT]

The fifth was structural.

The pen stopped.

Gerald waited. The pen did not move.

The clipboard rotated slightly, like a head tilting.

Then the pen moved again, but slower. It wrote a different kind of line. Gerald could tell it was different because the notification that appeared on his status screen had a different border color.

Not amber.

Not red. A color he had not seen before.

A deep, institutional blue.

[STRUCTURAL: LATENT NON-COMPLIANCE DETECTED] [DEFINITION: Current structure will fail to meet applicable code standards under foreseeable future conditions.] [FORESEEABLE CONDITION: Ashford Municipal Library plans vertical expansion (second floor addition) per City Council Ordinance 2024-117. Projected construction start: Year 11. Projected load increase on sub-basement foundation: 340%.] [ASSESSMENT: Foundation currently rated for 100% of present load.

Will be non-compliant at Year 11.] [CLASSIFICATION: VIOLATION. Severity: Moderate.

Category: Structural.]

Gerald read the notification three times, and he looked at the Inspector General's clipboard.

The pen was already moving to the next category. It had not paused for Gerald to respond.

The audit did not pause. The audit was a process. Processes did not pause for the subject's opinion.

Latent non-compliance. Gerald had never heard of it.

He had never seen it in thirty-one years of municipal inspection.

He had never seen it in the code. He had never seen it in any code, municipal or otherwise. You could not cite a building for a violation that had not yet occurred.

That was not how code worked.

Code was present tense. A violation existed or it did not.

But the System was not municipal code, and the System was not a building department. The System was a regulatory body that operated on a timescale Gerald had not previously been required to consider. The System did not care about the next inspection cycle.

The System cared about the next eleven years.

Gerald looked at his foundation.

It was concrete.

It was sound. It was rated for the load above it.

The load above it was a library that had stood for forty years without adding a second floor. City Council Ordinance 2024-117. He knew the ordinance.

He had read it. He had read it because he was a building inspector and building inspectors read ordinances.

He had read it and filed it in the back of his mind under "future projects, not my jurisdiction."

It was his jurisdiction now.

The audit continued. Plumbing: compliant. Accessibility: compliant.

Signage: compliant.

The pen moved through categories with the mechanical patience of a man who had done this a thousand times and would do it a thousand more.

Then the pen stopped again. The clipboard rotated. A new notification appeared. Blue border.

[AUDIT COMPLETE] [TOTAL VIOLATIONS: 1] [CATEGORY: STRUCTURAL (LATENT)] [SEVERITY: MODERATE] [CLASSIFICATION RECOMMENDATION: CORE (CONDITIONAL)] [NOTE: Subject will be reclassified as CORE upon audit completion. Compliance Rating will be re-established. Current provisional rating: 87/100.]

Gerald felt the number land in his chest like a physical weight.

Eighty-seven. He had been municipal. He had been a parking garage.

A parking garage with no violations. A parking garage that existed outside the System's tracking.

Now he was a Core again. A Core with a rating. A Core with a known violation that any rival could cite.

The clipboard rotated once more. A final notification appeared.

[APPEAL WINDOW: OPEN] [APPEAL REQUIRES: FORM 7 (CONDITIONAL VARIANCE REQUEST)] [APPEAL SUBJECTS MAY FILE VARIANCE FOR LATENT VIOLATIONS BY DEMONSTRATING THAT FORESEEABLE CONDITIONS ARE NOT CERTAIN] [NOTE: City Council Ordinance 2024-117 is not a certainty.

It is a plan.

Plans can be amended. Plans can be withdrawn.

Plans can be forgotten.]

Gerald stared at the notification. Form 7.

Conditional Variance Request, and he knew the form. He had filled it out dozens of times. A variance request was a request to be exempt from a specific code requirement based on unique circumstances.

You filed it when the code said one thing and reality said another.

You filed it when compliance was impractical, unnecessary, or based on a condition that did not actually exist.

The latent violation was based on a plan.

The plan was not certain. If Gerald could demonstrate that the plan was not certain, he could file a variance; the variance would nullify the latent violation.

His rating would go to 100. He would be compliant.

He needed Form 7. He had no Form 7. He had no forms at all.

The clipboard was right there. The Inspector General's clipboard. The clipboard that generated notifications in physical form. The clipboard that had a pen that wrote in a language Gerald could not read but could understand.

Gerald looked at the clipboard. The clipboard looked back, as clipboards do, which is to say, not at all.

He thought about municipal procedure.

An inspector carries forms.

An inspector carries forms because the inspector must be able to issue citations on-site, and a citation without a form is not a citation.

It is a verbal warning, and verbal warnings have no enforcement power. The System's citations had enforcement power. So the System's citations required forms.

So the Inspector General had forms.

Gerald reached for the clipboard.

His fingers closed around the edge, and the material was warm. It hummed. The pen stopped moving.

The entire clipboard tilted toward him like a body turning in its sleep.

A new notification appeared.

[SUBJECT HAS INTERACTED WITH AUDIT INSTRUMENT] [INTERACTION TYPE: UNAUTHORIZED] [PROCEDURE: UNAUTHORIZED INTERACTION WITH AUDIT INSTRUMENT IS A PROCEDURAL VIOLATION] [PROCEDURAL VIOLATION SEVERITY: HIGH] [PROCEDURAL VIOLATION PENALTY: IMMEDIATE RECLASSIFICATION TO NON-COMPLIANT CORE] [PROCEDURAL VIOLATION PENALTY: COMPLIANCE RATING SET TO 0] [PROCEDURAL VIOLATION PENALTY: ALL ACTIVE CITATIONS VOIDED] [PROCEDURAL VIOLATION PENALTY: ALL PASSIVE RESISTANCES VOIDED]

Gerald's hand was still on the clipboard, and the notification was already written. The violation was already logged. The penalty was already queued.

But the pen was not moving. The pen had stopped. The clipboard was waiting.

Gerald understood what waiting meant. The Inspector General was waiting for him to let go.

If he let go now, the violation would be logged but not enforced.

A grace period existed. He knew a grace period existed because he had granted them. Every citation he had ever written had a grace period.

You had to give people time to respond, and he had approximately ten seconds before the violation locked.

He did not let go.

He pulled the clipboard toward him.

The tripod base scraped against his concrete floor. The sound was loud in the silence of his dungeon.

The pen was still. The notifications were still. The penalty was still queued, still pending, still waiting for the ten seconds to run out.

Gerald found the back of the clipboard. He found a sheet of paper behind the notification stack.

It was not a System notification, which was a form. It had lines. It had checkboxes.

It had a header that read FORM 7: CONDITIONAL VARIANCE REQUEST.

He grabbed the pen.

The pen was warm. The pen was not his.

The pen belonged to the Inspector General, and the pen was part of the audit instrument. Using it was part of the unauthorized interaction. The violation was already logged.

The penalty was already queued.

He wrote the appeal before the penalty finished processing.
