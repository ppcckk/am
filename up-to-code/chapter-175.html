Chapter 175: Jurisdictional Loophole

# Chapter 175

The silence was heavier than the darkness. It pressed against Gerald's eardrums, thick and suffocating, smelling of ozone and old paper.

He lay on the cold concrete, his fingers still curled around the jagged shard of the pen.

The word **APPEAL** glowed faintly in the dark, etched into the foundation of the municipal library. A jurisdictional loophole. A technicality so absurd it had frozen the System's logic.

But the System was not the only thing listening.

A sound echoed from the far end of the sub-basement. Not footsteps. The wet, dragging rasp of something soft sliding over stone.

Gerald did not move.

His body felt hollowed out, stripped of the metaphysical weight that usually anchored his Core. He was just Gerald again.

Fifty-four years old.

Bad knees. Bad back. Bad eyesight.

The only thing he had left was the memory of the code.

"Jurisdiction," the older voice said.

It drifted from the shadows near the HVAC ducts.

"You invoke the city's ordinance to escape the System's decree. Clever.

Pedantic."

The figure emerged. It was not the Auditor.

The Auditor had been a suit of geometric light and sharp angles.

This thing was vast. It poured itself out of the darkness like oil, forming a shape that barely resembled a humanoid form. Its head was a cluster of floating eyes, each reflecting a different part of the library's layout.

"I am the Inspector General," the voice rumbled. It vibrated through the floor, rattling Gerald's teeth. "And you have violated Subsection 9, Paragraph C: Inter-Jurisdictional Contamination."

Gerald tried to sit up. His arms shook. He managed to prop himself on one elbow.

"The library is city property," Gerald said. His voice sounded thin, scraped raw. "The System cannot enforce dungeon regulations on municipal infrastructure without a consent decree. Which I did not sign."

"You did not sign it," the Inspector General agreed.

It drifted closer. The air grew colder.

Frost bloomed on the water stains on the floor.

"But you used it as a shield. You weaponized a building code to halt an audit. That is not a defense.

That is an obstruction."

"Obstruction requires intent to hinder," Gerald said.

He focused on the floor, looking for anything he could use.

A loose tile. A wire.

Anything. "My intent was compliance. I was filing an appeal.

Appeals are a protected right."

"The appeal is void," the IG said.

"Because the court you approached does not exist within the System's framework. You filed a lawsuit with a wall. A wall cannot hear.

A wall cannot rule.

Therefore, the filing is null. And the citation stands."

The air shimmered. A new text box appeared in Gerald's vision, hovering in the dark.

**CITATION REINSTATED.** **VIOLATION: OBSTRUCTION OF AUDIT.** **PENALTY: CORE INTEGRITY REDUCTION - 50%.** **SECONDARY PENALTY: SPAWN LIST PURGE.**

Gerald's stomach dropped.

A fifty percent reduction in Core Integrity meant he could no longer maintain the structural integrity of the dungeon. The walls would weaken. The floor would crack.

The roof might collapse. And the spawn purge?

That meant he lost everything.

The rat.

The mold spores. The water leak.

They would cease to exist.

He looked at the IG. The eyes blinked, slowly, independently.

"You think you are a lawyer now, Gerald," the IG said. "You are a dungeon core. You are a monster. And monsters do not get due process."

The pressure on Gerald's chest increased. He felt his essence thinning, stretching like taffy pulled too far. He gasped, but no air came.

He needed a rule. A hard, fast, unbreakable rule.

He looked at the IG. It was standing on a section of floor that had been patched in 1998.

The concrete was different there.

Rougher. He had inspected that patch. He had noted the aggregate mix.

"Subsection 4," Gerald wheezed.

The IG paused. The pressure eased slightly. Curiosity, perhaps. Or amusement.

"What?"

"Section 4, Article 2," Gerald said. He swallowed dryly. "Fire Safety and Egress. Specifically, the requirement for illuminated exit signage in any area where occupancy exceeds three entities."

The IG tilted its head. "You are citing egress requirements?"

"I am citing the current status of this room," Gerald said.

He forced himself to breathe. One breath.

Two.

"This room currently contains four entities. Me. The rat.

The mold colony. And you."

The IG's eyes narrowed. "I am not an occupant. I am an auditor."

"The System defines 'auditor' as a visiting entity with physical presence," Gerald said.

He remembered the manual.

He had read it forty times. He had highlighted the definitions.

"Physical presence triggers occupancy counts. If occupancy exceeds three, egress paths must be clearly marked and illuminated to a minimum of five foot-candles."

Gerald looked up at the ceiling. The lights were out. The emergency lighting had failed during the System freeze.

"The path to the exit is dark," Gerald said. "The signage is dead. Therefore, by keeping the lights off, you are creating a violation of the current state of the dungeon."

The IG remained silent. The oil-like form shifted, rippling.

"Are you threatening me with a code violation?" the IG asked. The voice was colder now. Dangerous.

"I am informing you that you are committing a violation," Gerald corrected. "If you proceed with the purge, you will be the cause of a non-compliant environment. You will be cited for Negligent Endangerment."

The IG laughed. It was a horrible sound, like gravel grinding in a mixer.

"You are bluffing," the IG said. "You have no jurisdiction to cite me. I am above the code."

"Are you?" Gerald asked. He looked at the shard of pen.

He looked at the word **APPEAL** glowing on the floor.

"The System's rules apply to all Cores. All entities. Even auditors.

If you kill the inhabitants, the population drops to one.

Me. The occupancy requirement drops.

The egress requirement drops.

But you caused the violation. You broke the environment to save the audit."

Gerald reached out with his mind. He couldn't spawn anything.

He couldn't build.

But he could interact with existing structures.

He could toggle switches he had installed.

He had installed a manual override for the emergency lights. It was a stupid, unnecessary addition. He had done it because the library's backup system had a three-second delay, and he hated delays.

He focused on that switch. It was behind a panel near the entrance.

"If you purge me," Gerald said, "the lights will stay off. The rat will be gone. The mold will be gone.

The room will be dark. Empty.

And you will be standing in a non-compliant void."

The IG's eyes widened. The floating orbs spun faster.

"You are gambling your existence on a technicality," the IG said.

"I am gambling my existence on the fact that you care about the audit," Gerald said. "You want to close this file. You want to mark me compliant.

If you cite me, you admit that the audit failed to account for environmental hazards caused by the audit itself.

You look incompetent."

The silence stretched. It lasted ten seconds. Twenty.

The IG stared at Gerald. Gerald stared back. His heart hammered against his ribs. His hands were trembling so badly he had to press them into the concrete to stop them.

"You are insufferable," the IG said.

"I am thorough," Gerald said.

The IG moved. Not away. Toward him.

It reached out a tendril of shadow. It touched Gerald's forehead.

Cold. Absolute zero cold.

**SYSTEM NOTIFICATION:** **HEARING SUSPENDED DUE TO CONFLICTING JURISDICTION.** **PENDING REVIEW: MUNICIPAL vs. SYSTEM LEGAL FRAMEWORK.** **CORE INTEGRITY LOSS: PAUSED.**

The pressure vanished. Gerald slumped forward, gasping for air. His lungs burned.

The IG pulled its tendril back. The shadowy form dissipated, reforming into the geometric suit of the Auditor. The older, colder voice was gone. The sharp, bureaucratic voice returned.

"Case suspended," the Auditor said. "Review pending. You will remain on notice. Do not attempt further jurisdictional maneuvers until the Legal Division issues a ruling."

The Auditor vanished into the dark.

Gerald lay on the floor.

He didn't move for a full minute. He just breathed. In.

Out. In.

Out.

He had survived. The purge was paused. The citation was on hold.

But he was still down.

Fifty percent integrity. No spawns.

The dungeon was dying. The walls were groaning under their own weight. The ceiling had cracked in three places.

If the structural integrity dropped any lower, the library above would collapse.

Gerald forced himself to sit up. His legs felt like water. He used the pen shard to pull himself up against the wall.

He looked around the dark, empty sub-basement.

He needed to rebuild.

He needed to restore the dungeon. But he had no mana. He had no resources.

He had no spawns to generate energy.

And he had no time. The Legal Division would review the jurisdiction conflict. They would find a way around it. They would find a way to cite him.

He needed help.

He needed an adventurer.

The thought made him sick. Adventurers destroyed things.

They broke props.

They looted containers. They were the antithesis of code compliance.

But they brought mana. They brought energy. They brought the raw material of a dungeon.

Gerald looked at the entrance. The heavy steel door was ajar. The Auditor had left it open.

Someone was approaching. He could hear it.

Footsteps.

Heavy boots.

Creaking wood.

Adventurers.

They must have heard the noise. Or they had been waiting.

Gerald pulled himself toward the entrance. He dragged his body across the concrete, leaving a smear of blood from his scraped palms. He needed to greet them.

He needed to guide them. He needed them to break something.

If they broke a wall, he could rebuild it stronger. If they killed a monster, he could harvest the energy. If they brought supplies, he could install them.

He reached the door. He pushed it open.

The hallway of the library was dim. The overhead lights were off. But there was light from a torch in the distance.

Three figures walked toward him.

They were not typical adventurers.

They wore heavy plate armor. They carried hammers, not swords. They looked like construction workers.

Or maybe dwarves.

The leader stopped. He looked at Gerald. Gerald looked at him.

The leader was a massive dwarf, his beard braided with copper wire. He wore a hard hat.

"You," the dwarf grunted. "You're the Core?"

"I am," Gerald said. He tried to straighten his spine. He failed.

"You look terrible," the dwarf said.

"I am under citation," Gerald said.

The dwarf looked at the other two. They were checking the walls. Tapping the concrete. Listening to the hollow sounds.

"This place is a mess," the dwarf said. "Load-bearing supports are weak. Ventilation is non-existent. You got cited?"

"I was cited for obstruction," Gerald said. "But the audit is paused."

The dwarf nodded. He pulled a scroll from his belt.

"We heard about you," the dwarf said. "Gerald Hutchins.

The Inspector.

We're from the Guild of Forensic Demolition. We specialize in... retroactive compliance."

Gerald blinked. "Forensic Demolition?"

"We break things," the dwarf said. "But we break them according to regulations. If we break a wall that was already non-compliant, the System doesn't penalize the Core. It credits the Core for 'voluntary remediation.'"

Gerald felt a spark of hope. A tiny, fragile spark.

"You can help me fix the structural damage?" Gerald asked.

"We can help you hide the damage," the dwarf said. "But you have to pay us.

And you have to let us work.

No interfering.

No measuring. No asking questions."

Gerald looked at the dwarf. He looked at the cracked ceiling. He looked at the dark, empty dungeon.

He knew he should say no. He should not trust outsiders. He should handle this himself.

It was his job. His responsibility.

But he was fifty percent dead. And he could not build a wall with bare hands.

"Show me your license," Gerald said.

The dwarf grinned. It was a toothy, dangerous grin.

"License?" the dwarf asked.

"Business License. Hazardous Materials Handling Certification. And a permit for interior demolition."

The dwarf pulled out a crumpled piece of paper. It was stained with grease and beer.

"Here," the dwarf said. "It's real. Mostly."

Gerald took the paper.

He studied it. The font was wrong. The seal was smudged.

The date was expired.

It was illegal.

Gerald looked at the dwarf. The dwarf looked at him.

"If I accept this," Gerald said, "I am complicit in an unlicensed operation."

"If you don't accept it," the dwarf said, "your dungeon collapses in an hour. And the library above falls on a school bus."

Gerald looked at the paper. He looked at the ceiling. He thought about the school bus.

He handed the paper back.

"Get to work," Gerald said.

The dwarf handed him a hammer.

"Start breaking things," the dwarf said.

Gerald held the hammer. It was heavy. It was crude. It was not a pen.

He raised it.

He swung.

The hammer hit the wall.

Cracks spiderwebbed across the concrete.

And then, a new text box appeared.

**VIOLATION DETECTED:** **UNAUTHORIZED STRUCTURAL ALTERATION.** **PENALTY: CORE INTEGRITY REDUCTION - 10%.**

Gerald dropped the hammer.

The dwarf laughed.

"Welcome to the gray area, Inspector."

From the shadows behind the dwarf, a fourth figure stepped forward. A human.

Tall.

Thin. Wearing a suit.

It was not an adventurer.

It was a lawyer.

And he was smiling.
