Chapter 84: Open Renovation Window

# Chapter 84

The response came in at one hour and forty-seven minutes. Gerald was not watching the clock. He was measuring the east corridor with a tape measure, checking the joint gap for the ninth time, because if he stopped measuring he would have to think about what he'd just done.

The notification pinged. He didn't open it immediately. He finished the measurement, recorded it on the clipboard, set the tape measure down on the workbench, and then looked at the phone.

*Conditional Override granted. Case 7741-C. Conditions: (1) Structural modification must be completed within 4 hours of override activation. (2) During modification window, Core enters Open Renovation status. (3) Open Renovation status permits Structural Challenges from Cores within 200m radius. (4) Core must demonstrate net compliance improvement at close of modification window. (5) Failure to meet conditions results in automatic Citation, loss of Citation Authority for 30 days, and mandatory structural audit.*

Gerald read it twice. Then a third time.

Open Renovation status. He'd never heard of it. He'd read the System documentation cover to cover twice, cross-referenced it with the municipal code he'd memorized in 1994, and found nothing about this.

He pulled up the Core directory. Scrolled through the range map. Found every Core within 200 meters.

Core 4492 was 140 meters northeast. Core 1108 was 185 meters southwest.

Core 8803 was 90 meters directly below, which was technically the library's parking garage, though Gerald had never confirmed whether 8803 was actually in the garage or whether the garage was actually in 8803.

He'd filed a query on that one. No response.

Three Cores in range. Three chances to get challenged.

He started the override.

The System confirmed: *Open Renovation status active. Window: 4 hours. Structural Challenges accepted.*

Gerald picked up the sledgehammer.

He'd bought it from a hardware store three towns over, cash, because he didn't trust the System's item shop to sell him something with a proper handle. The handle was hickory.

The head was four pounds of forged steel, which was the same model he'd used to test load-bearing walls in residential properties for two decades.

He swung at the base of the southeast column.

The first hit didn't do much, and the column was reinforced concrete, maybe six inches thick, poured over rebar he couldn't see.

He hit it again.

And again.

On the fourth swing, a crack appeared. On the sixth, a chunk of concrete fell away and exposed the rebar inside.

He switched to a pry bar. Bent the rebar back. He began removing concrete in sections, methodical as if inspecting a wall.

You don't smash. You open.

You expose.

You assess.

The second column from the southeast corner was doing the actual load-bearing work.

Gerald had checked this three times, and the load path from the ceiling plate ran through the second column, not the first.

The first column was decorative in function if not in appearance, which was, in municipal terms, a false support. A column that looked structural but carried no load. Which was itself a code violation if it was meant to be structural.

Which it wasn't. Which meant it was just a column.

Which meant removing it changed nothing about the load path.

Which meant the joint in the east corridor was the real problem.

He worked for forty minutes. The column was down to its core, a rebar cage with concrete crumbling off it, when the notification hit.

*Structural Challenge received.

Core 4492. Challenge type: Load Path Verification.

Challenge basis: Removal of southeast column will redistribute load to east corridor joint, which is non-compliant. Core 7741 must demonstrate that load path is maintained post-removal or Challenge is sustained.*

Gerald set down the pry bar. He wiped his hands on his work shirt. He looked at the phone.

Core 4492. He knew this one.

It was a mid-tier dungeon, a cave system in the old quarry on Elm Street.

The Core ran a water-themed dungeon, everything wet and slippery; it had been Cited twice by Gerald for inadequate drainage and missing slip-resistant flooring. It had appealed both times and lost both times.

It was angry. Gerald could understand that.

But the Challenge was wrong, and he opened the response window.

Typed: *Challenge basis is factually incorrect. The southeast column does not carry structural load.

Load path from ceiling plate runs through column 2 (southeast offset 1.2m) to foundation footing B.

Removal of column 1 does not redistribute load to east corridor joint. Load path is maintained. Challenge should be dismissed.*

The System processed. Thirty seconds. A minute.

*Challenge sustained. Core 4492's challenge basis is accepted.

Core 7741 must demonstrate load path integrity through on-site verification.

Verification window: 30 minutes.

Core 4492 will dispatch an Inspector Entity to observe.*

Gerald stared at the screen.

His argument was correct. The column didn't carry load. He'd checked the load path three times.

But the System had sustained the challenge anyway. Because the challenge basis wasn't evaluated for truth.

It was evaluated for plausibility.

And "removing a column redistributes load" was plausible enough to require proof.

He had thirty minutes to prove that a column he'd already demolished didn't matter.

He pulled up his dungeon schematic.

The System's built-in map, which he'd updated obsessively over the past six months. Every wall, every column, every joint.

He traced the load path from the ceiling plate.

Column 2. Foundation footing B. The path didn't touch column 1.

It didn't touch the east corridor joint.

But the System didn't have his schematic loaded as evidence. The System had the Core's own spatial awareness, which was a kind of sonar, a kind of feel. Gerald sensed his dungeon like his own body, and he could feel that column 1 was not part of the skeleton.

He couldn't prove that to the System. Not with words.

The Inspector Entity arrived in four minutes. It was a small thing, maybe two feet tall, shaped like a surveyor's transit level with legs. Blind, it oriented toward Gerald's central chamber and started scanning.

Gerald watched it move through his dungeon, which went to the east corridor joint.

It measured the gap. It recorded the measurement.

It went to column 2. It measured the load transfer. It recorded that too.

Then it went to the base of column 1, where Gerald had left the rebar cage and the rubble. It scanned the rebar. It scanned the rubble. It scanned the floor where column 1 had been anchored.

And then it scanned the space behind column 1.

Gerald felt it. A pressure.

A probing, and the Inspector Entity was looking at something Gerald hadn't seen because Gerald had never looked behind column 1. He'd measured around it. He'd cited the space next to it.

He had never removed it, lacking reason to, and its absence left a gap in the wall.

The Inspector Entity stopped. It scanned the gap for eleven seconds. Then it turned and left.

*Verification complete. Core 4492's challenge is sustained.

Load path verification inconclusive due to unregistered cavity at column 1 position.

Core 7741 must address cavity or Challenge remains active. Cavity constitutes an unregistered structural void. Unregistered voids are a Class 3 violation under Structural Code 7.1.*

Gerald's stomach dropped.

A cavity. Behind column 1. Something he'd never found. Something the Inspector Entity had found because Gerald had removed the column and exposed it.

And now he had a new violation. Class 3. On top of everything else.

He walked to the gap. It was about eighteen inches wide, maybe two feet deep.

The back wall was different from the rest of his dungeon, and the concrete was older.

The aggregate was different. The rebar pattern was wrong.

This wasn't his concrete.

He reached in. His fingers touched something smooth. Not concrete.

Not stone. Something that felt like glass but wasn't cold.

He pulled his hand back.

Looked at his fingertips. No residue. No color.

He went back to the phone.

Typed: *Requesting clarification on cavity at column 1 position. Cavity is not a void.

Cavity contains an object.

Object is unregistered. I did not create the object. I did not place the object.

I am requesting guidance on how to address an unregistered structural feature that predates my Core registration.*

The System responded in nine seconds. Faster than normal.

*Guidance provided: Unregistered features within Core boundaries are considered part of the Core's structure by default.

Core 7741 is responsible for all features within its boundary regardless of origin.

The cavity and its contents are now registered under Core 7741. Class 3 violation logged.

Remediation required within 4 hours.*

Gerald set the phone down, and he looked at the gap. He looked at the clock. Two hours and twelve minutes until the IG arrived. Three hours and eight minutes until the Open Renovation window closed.

He had a Class 3 violation he hadn't caused, in a cavity he hadn't known existed, containing an object he couldn't identify, in a column he'd demolished to fix a different violation, while a rival Core was actively trying to take his Citation Authority, and the IG was coming to audit everything.

He picked up the pry bar.

He went back to the gap. He widened it.

Carefully.

Methodically. Opening the wall as one does when suspecting something behind it. You don't tear.

You expose, and the object came into view. It was about the size of a shoebox.

Smooth.

Dark. Not glass. Not metal.

It had a surface texture like old leather, and it was warm, and it was pulsing.

Gerald reached in and touched it.

The System pinged.

*New Core detected within Core 7741 boundary.

Core 7741-A.

Registration status: Active. Registration date: 31 years prior to Core 7741.

Core 7741-A is the primary Core. Core 7741 is a subordinate structure. Core 7741's Citation Authority is derived from Core 7741-A's standing.

Core 7741-A has been in dormancy since registration. Core 7741-A is now responsive.*

Gerald pulled his hand back.

The object pulsed faster, and he heard something.

Not through his ears. Through the dungeon. A vibration that started in the floor and moved up through his walls and into whatever he was now.

It felt like a voice.

It felt like someone waking up.

*Core 7741-A speaks: "Oh. Oh, you're the one who's been writing all those citations." *

Gerald didn't move.

*Core 7741-A: "Do you know what you've been doing? Do you know what you've been citing them for?"*

Gerald opened his mouth. Closed it.

*Core 7741-A: "You've been citing them for my violations.

Every dungeon in range has a Compliance Rating. But they're all measured against MY code. MY boundary.

MY standards. You've been enforcing a code that isn't yours.

You've been citing them under a code that belongs to me.

And I've been asleep for thirty-one years."*

The IG's ETA was two hours and ten minutes.

*Core 7741-A: "When the IG gets here, it's going to audit me. Not you.

Me. And if I'm non-compliant, everything you've built goes with me. Every Citation.

Every debuff. Every dungeon you've shut down.

All of it.

Gone."*

Gerald looked at the object. The object looked back, in whatever way objects look.

*Core 7741-A: "So.

Inspector. You asked for permission to break a rule to keep another rule.

Here's the answer.

You can't. You can only break a rule to keep a bigger rule, and the bigger rule is that I have to be compliant.

So. What are you going to do?"*

The clock said two hours and nine minutes.

Gerald Hutchins, municipal building inspector, thirty-one years, looked at the thing that had been sleeping inside his dungeon longer than he'd been alive.

He picked up his clipboard.
