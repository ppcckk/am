Chapter 306: Zero Percent Compliance

# Chapter 306

Claw scraped against cured concrete. Dust plumed into the stagnant air of the sub-basement.

Gerald didn't stop. The rat's tiny lungs burned, but the rhythmic *shk-shk-shk* of digging was the only sound in the dark.

He needed a channel, and he needed a filter. He needed a form.

"Mr. Hutchins."

The voice came from everywhere and nowhere.

The air shimmered near the load-bearing pillar. A figure coalesced.

Not the leaf-suit woman this time, which was a man in a gray suit that seemed woven from static electricity. His face was a blur of features, like a smudged photograph.

"Name?" the static man asked.

"Gerald," the rat squeaked. His voice was a wet rasp. "Hutchins."

"Core ID?"

"402-B. Municipal Library Sub-Basement."

"Violation: Uncontrolled Mana Discharge. Status: Critical." The static man held out a glowing orange tablet.

"You are currently operating at 0% Compliance.

The Department of Ecological Balance has filed a preliminary injunction.

You have four hours until the Condemnation Order is finalized.

"

Gerald's paws froze. Four hours? The woman had said twenty-four.

"Time dilation," the static man explained, tapping the tablet. "Ecological hazards accelerate the System clock. You are not on the municipal schedule anymore.

You are on the Biological Schedule. Do you understand the distinction?

"

"No," Gerald said flatly. "I require a permit."

"You don't have a permit.

You have a citation.

" The static man leaned in. The smell of ozone was overwhelming. "Look at your floor.

You're digging a hole.

Why?"

"Mana filtration."

"With what materials?"

"Concrete."

"Concrete does not filter mana.

It blocks it. You are creating a dam. The pressure will build until the ley lines rupture.

That is a secondary violation. Structural Integrity Failure.

"

Gerald stopped digging, and he sat on his haunches.

He looked at the rough trench he'd carved. It was shallow.

Useless.

"I need a medium," Gerald said. "Porosity. Permeability. A substrate that allows flow without turbulence."

The static man checked his tablet.

"Recommendation: Use organic matter.

Roots.

Fungus. Living things.

"

"I have no living things," Gerald said. "I have a rat. Me. And I am not porous."

"Then you fail," the static man said. His voice dropped an octave, vibrating Gerald's teeth. "The injunction stands. Prepare for demolition."

"Wait." Gerald raised a claw. "I am invoking Section 12, Paragraph 4 of the Dungeon Core Operations Manual. Right to Cure."

The static man paused. The static around his edges flickered.

"Section 12, Paragraph 4 applies to structural defects, not ecological hazards."

"Mana flow is structural," Gerald said.

His heart hammered against his ribs, a frantic drumbeat.

"The ley lines are load-bearing.

They support the local reality grid.

If they fail, the library collapses. The library is a municipal building. I am the inspector for municipal buildings.

Therefore, the failure is within my jurisdiction.

"

Silence stretched. The static man tilted his head. It was a jerky, unnatural movement.

"Jurisdictional overlap," the man muttered. "Tricky."

"I can fix it," Gerald said. "But I need materials. I need a citation for the person who broke the ley line. The void entity."

"The void entity is dead," the static man said. "You killed it."

"I cited it. That is not the same thing."

"It is legally equivalent. A Citation results in neutralization. You have no target to sue for damages."

Gerald's mind raced. He couldn't fight. He couldn't run. He could only argue.

"I need to appeal the penalty," Gerald said. "To the Inspector General."

"You don't have standing. You are a Core. The IG audits Cores, not rats."

"I am the Core.

The rat is the avatar.

It's in the fine print. Page 402, line 12.

Avatar representation clause.

"

The static man hesitated. Then, with a sigh that sounded like a dial-up modem connecting, he pulled a second form from the air. It was yellow.

"Appeal hearing," he said. "You have ten minutes.

If you lose, the Condemnation Order executes immediately.

If you win, the penalty is suspended pending remediation. But you still need to build the filter. You just have more time to do it.

"

"Who is the opposing counsel?" Gerald asked.

"Me," the static man said. "I represent the System's interest in public safety."

Gerald nodded. He didn't know what to say. He looked at the trench. He looked at the yellow paper.

"I need to see the original Citation 306-A," Gerald said.

The static man waved his hand. The paper appeared. It was thick, heavy, smelling of wet earth.

Gerald squinted. The text was tiny. He couldn't read it well with rat eyes.

"Enlarge," he squeaked.

The static man frowned.

"No. That is a procedural request, not an argument."

"I cannot read the violation to rebut it," Gerald said.

"That violates due process. The Core must be informed of the nature of the accusation to prepare a defense.

This is standard municipal law, which is also System law. Check the precedent in *Hutchins v. The Void*.

"

The static man froze.

"There is no such case."

"There is now," Gerald said. "I am citing the precedent of *Self-Defense in Structural Collapse*.

The void entity attacked my structural integrity.

I cited it.

The citation caused the mana surge. Therefore, the surge is a consequence of lawful enforcement action.

The hazard is not my fault. It is the fault of the entity that necessitated the citation.

"

The static man's eyes narrowed.

"That is... a creative interpretation."

"It is a correct interpretation," Gerald said. "The void entity was non-compliant with zoning laws regarding spatial expansion.

It existed in a place it should not have existed.

My citation removed it. The mana discharge was the result of the removal. Like pulling a plug from a socket.

The spark is expected.

The spark is not a violation if the removal was legal.

"

"Prove the removal was legal," the static man challenged. "Show me the Citation."

Gerald pointed a claw at the corner where the green woman had vanished.

"The Citation is embedded in the dungeon's memory. It is part of the Core log. Access it."

The static man waved his hand again. A holographic screen appeared in the air. It displayed the timeline.

*09:00 AM: Core Registration.* *10:00 AM: Goblin Scout Repelled.* *02:00 PM: Void Entity Detected.* *02:05 PM: Citation 306-B Issued.* *02:06 PM: Mana Discharge.*

Gerald looked at the timestamps.

"Look at the date," Gerald said.

"What about it?"

"It was a Tuesday. The municipal inspection schedule is closed on Mondays and Tuesdays for administrative review. No inspections are performed. Therefore, no enforcement actions can be taken on Tuesdays under City Ordinance 44-B."

The static man's face went blank.

"The System does not observe municipal holidays."

"The System is integrated with the City of Ashford," Gerald said.

"The Core is located in a municipal building. The building is subject to city ordinances. If I violated the ordinance by acting on a Tuesday, then the Citation itself is void.

And if the Citation is void, the mana discharge was an unprovoked attack by the System on a compliant citizen. I am entitled to damages.

And a stay of execution.

"

The static man opened his mouth. Closed it. He looked at the timeline. He looked at Gerald.

"You're arguing that your own illegal action protects you from the consequences of your illegal action?"

"I am arguing that my action was procedural," Gerald said.

"I did not inspect. I filed.

Filing is not inspecting. Filing is administrative. Administrative actions are permitted on Tuesdays.

Only physical inspections are prohibited. I did not touch the void.

I sent a piece of paper.

"

The static man stared. The static around him crackled louder. Sparks flew off his suit.

"That is..." He struggled for a word. "Pedantic."

"It is Code," Gerald said.

The air grew heavy. The lights in the sub-basement flickered. The green glow of the ley line pulsed faster, agitated by the debate.

"If I accept this," the static man said, "I admit that the System's enforcement protocols are subject to local municipal law. That sets a dangerous precedent."

"It sets a legal precedent," Gerald said. "That is my job. I make things legal."

The static man raised his hand. A small, glowing gavel appeared.

"I will grant a temporary stay," he said. "Pending a formal review of the ordinance conflict. You have until sunrise to build the filter. If you fail, the stay is revoked, and the Condemnation Order executes with extreme prejudice."

"Thank you," Gerald said.

"Don't thank me," the static man said. "Fix it. Or I will be the one holding the wrecking ball."

He vanished.

Gerald sat in the silence. He was shaking. His paws were cramped.

He had saved himself, and he had used the rules.

But the rules hadn't built the filter.

He looked at the trench. He needed a medium. Porous. Organic.

He looked around the sub-basement. The library's sub-basement was concrete, rebar, and forgotten things. Old boxes. Mold.

Mold.

Fungi were porous. Fungi filtered.

Gerald crawled toward the corner where the water leak had created a patch of damp. In the dark, a patch of black mold clung to the wall. It was ugly. It was non-compliant.

It was perfect, and he approached the mold. He didn't have tools. He didn't have a shovel.

He had teeth.

He bit into the mold. It tasted like rot and iron. He tore a chunk free. It was slimy, wet, and clumped together.

He dragged it to the trench.

It wasn't enough. He needed more. He needed a network.

A shadow moved. Not the static man. Something else.

Small. Quick.

A spider. It dropped from the ceiling on a thread of silk. It landed on the wet concrete near Gerald's nose.

Gerald recoiled.

The spider didn't attack. It spun. It began weaving a web directly over the trench.

Gerald watched. The spider was building a structure. It was using the moisture in the air. It was creating a mesh.

"Hey," Gerald squeaked.

The spider paused. It looked at him. Eight eyes reflected the faint green glow of the ley line.

"You're building," Gerald said.

"Good. Keep building. Make it dense.

Make it filter the air.

"

The spider ignored him. It continued to spin. The web caught the dust from Gerald's digging. It caught the spores from the mold he had dragged over.

Gerald watched the web grow. It was a natural filter. It was chaotic. It was irregular.

It was non-compliant.

But it worked. The mana flowing from the trench dissipated through the silk. The green light softened. The pressure in the room eased.

Gerald looked at the web. He looked at the mold.

He could claim it. He could integrate the spider's work into the Core's structure. He could register the web as a

"Natural Ventilation System."

He needed to file the paperwork. He had no hands. He had no pen.

He had to think.

The spider finished a section. It waited.

Gerald crawled back to the center of the dungeon. He found a sharp piece of rebar poking from the concrete. He used his tail to drag it to the wall.

He needed to inscribe the compliance, and he couldn't write. Rats don't write.

But rats scratch.

Gerald dragged the rebar against the concrete wall.

He scratched a pattern.

A circle. A line through it.

The symbol for

"Approved" in his mind.

It looked like garbage.

The System didn't care what it looked like. It cared about intent.

*Intent: Ventilation.*

*Method: Organic Webbing.*

*Status: Active.*

Gerald scratched the symbol again. And again. And again.

The air hummed. A new notification pinged in his mind.

**[Ventilation System Detected.]** **[Type: Organic/Biological.]** **[Compliance Rating: Pending.]** **[Warning: Unregistered Entity (Arachnid) Detected in Infrastructure.]**

Gerald froze.

An unregistered entity.

The static man would cite him for having an illegal tenant.

He had to register the spider, and he crawled toward the spider. The spider raised its legs.

"Gerald," a voice echoed.

It wasn't the static man. It was the leaf-suit woman.

She appeared in the center of the room, and she looked tired. Her glass suit was cracked.

"You bought time," she said.

"But you created a new violation. The spider is not a Core entity.

It is a wild variable.

The System hates variables.

"

"It's a filter," Gerald said.

"It's a pest," she corrected. "Pests are removed. Not registered."

"If I remove it, the mana flow stops. The dam breaks. The library collapses."

The woman looked at the web. She looked at the green light pulsing through the silk.

"Then you must integrate it," she said. "You must make the spider part of the dungeon. You must spawn it."

"I can't," Gerald said. "I have no mana. I have no spawn points."

"Then you must offer it a deal," the woman said.

Gerald looked at the spider.

"What kind of deal?"

"Shelter," the woman said. "Safety. A place where the System won't delete it. In exchange, it works for you."

Gerald looked at the small, hairy creature. It was trembling. It knew it was illegal.

"Stay here," Gerald squeaked to the spider. "You're safe.

I'm the inspector.

I keep things up to code.

And you're part of the code now.

"

The spider hesitated. Then, it bowed. A small, jerky bow.

Gerald felt a pull in his chest. A connection forming.

**[New Asset Acquired: Weaver.]** **[Role: Filtration Agent.]** **[Cost: 1 Mana Point per Hour.]**

Gerald didn't have a mana point, and he was at zero.

"Pay it," the woman said. "From the citation. The penalty was mana discharge.

You have excess mana. It's dangerous.

Use it."

Gerald focused. He felt the green light in the ley line. He reached out with his mind. He pulled a thin thread of energy from the leak.

It burned. It hurt. But he fed it to the connection.

**[Transaction Complete.]** **[Weaver Registered.]**

The green light stabilized. The pressure dropped.

The woman smiled. It was a sad smile.

"Good," she said. "But you still have a problem."

"What?"

"The spider is registered. But the source of the leak is still open. The void entity's corpse is still in the walls.

The mana is still leaking.

The spider can only filter so much. You need to cap the source.

"

"I need to patch the hole," Gerald said.

"Yes," the woman said. "But you don't have hands. And you don't have mortar."

She pointed to the ceiling. Above the trench, a crack ran across the concrete. The green light was seeping from it.

"You have to go up," she said.

"To the library?"

"To the surface. To the source. You have to climb out."

"I can't," Gerald said. "I'm a rat in a sealed basement."

"The spider has a web," the woman said. "It has threads. You can use them. You can climb."

"If I go up, I'm exposed," Gerald said. "The System will see me. The City will see me. I'll be evicted."

"Or," the woman said, "you'll be caught. And they'll kill you. And the dungeon will die."

Gerald looked at the spider. The spider looked at the ceiling.

It spun a new line. A strong, thick line, leading up into the darkness above.

Gerald looked at the line. It was fragile. It was his only way out.

"Go," the woman said.

Gerald looked at the dark, twisting hole above, and he looked at his claws.

He took a breath.

He climbed.
