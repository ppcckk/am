Chapter 271: Shatter the Floor

# Chapter 271

Gerald hit the bottom.

It wasn't a floor. It was silt. Wet, black, and cold.

Pain flared in his left knee. He tried to stand, but the ground gave way under his weight, pulling him another inch deeper into the muck.

The air tasted of copper and ozone.

Above him, the hole in the reality of the library sub-basement had shrunk to a pinprick of grey light. The fog had dissipated. The hand was gone.

The woman was gone.

Only the silence remained, and the blinking red light of his Compliance Rating.

47/100, and he coughed. Mud sprayed onto his tie.

He wiped it with a sleeve that was no longer there, realizing with a dull shock that his body was no longer flesh.

It was stone. Dense, compressed, grey stone. But he could still feel the phantom pressure of a collar against his neck.

"Hello?"

The voice was small. Trembling.

Gerald turned his head. It moved with a grinding sound, like two millstones rubbing together.

A child sat ten feet away. Not a monster. A human boy, perhaps eight years old, wearing the oversized uniform of a Level 3 Adventurer. He held a broken wooden sword and was weeping into his shoulder.

"Don't cry," Gerald said. His voice came out as a low rumble, vibrating through the bedrock. "Crying increases oxygen consumption. It is inefficient."

The boy looked up, eyes wide with terror.

"Are you... are you the monster?"

Gerald paused. He looked at his hands.

They were jagged shards of granite.

"I am a municipal infrastructure entity," he corrected.

"And you are trespassing."

"I was running away!" the boy shouted. "The minotaur! It broke the ceiling!"

"It also broke the load-bearing supports," Gerald said. "Which is why you are currently sitting in a sinkhole. If you had followed the egress signage, you would have been safe."

"There was no signage!"

Gerald felt a twinge of irritation. He raised a stone hand. The air around him shimmered.

A window appeared. Not a magic window.

A standard, grey, bureaucratic interface.

**NOTICE OF VIOLATION: FAILURE TO INSTALL EGRESS SIGNAGE.** **ZONE: SUB-LEVEL 4, CHAMBER B.** **FINE: -100 COMPLIANCE POINTS.**

Gerald stared at the number. 47 minus 100 would put him in the negative. The System didn't allow negative ratings. It triggered Core Dissolution.

He looked at the boy. The boy looked at the window.

"What is that?" the boy whispered.

"A citation," Gerald said. He couldn't issue it. Not yet.

He didn't have the authority, and he was currently under lockdown.

"Are you going to kill me?" the boy asked.

"No," Gerald said.

"I am going to inspect you. Stay there. Do not move your feet.

The ground is unstable.

"

The boy froze. He didn't move.

Gerald pulled himself out of the mud. It took effort. The System was resisting him. *LOCKDOWN ACTIVE. CORE INTEGRITY AT 40%.*

He crawled toward the boy. Not with grace. With the heavy, inevitable momentum of a falling brick.

"Name?" Gerald asked.

"T-Toby," the boy said.

"Toby," Gerald said. "You are a witness. I need a witness."

"Why?"

"Because I am going to file a complaint," Gerald said.

He pointed at the ceiling.

A drop of water fell from the darkness and landed on Toby's head.

"This chamber is flooded.

The water table has breached the foundation. That is a Section 8.4 violation. Improper drainage.

"

Toby wiped the water from his face.

"Is that bad?"

"It is fatal," Gerald said. "To the dungeon.

To me.

And if the water hits the mana conduits in three minutes, it will explode. Taking you with me.

"

Toby's face went pale.

"Then help me!"

"I am trying," Gerald said.

"But I have no hands. I have no legs.

I am a pile of rocks in a hole.

I need leverage.

"

He looked at the broken bench Toby had been sitting on. It was made of high-grade oak. Reinforced with iron brackets.

"Move," Gerald said.

"No!"

Gerald sighed. The sound was like wind through a cracked window.

"I am not asking you to leave.

I am asking you to move the bench, which is a heavy object. It can be used as a fulcrum.

"

"To do what?"

"To pry open a fissure in the wall. There is a drainage channel behind the stone. If I can open it, the water will drain.

If it drains, I can rebuild the floor. If I rebuild the floor, I can issue a Citation to the Minotaur for causing the breach.

"

Toby blinked. "You can hurt the Minotaur by fixing the floor?"

"I can cite the Minotaur for property damage," Gerald said. "The fine will be deducted from the Minotaur's Core health. It is a lethal penalty."

Toby looked at the ceiling. A crack was spreading. Water was pouring through now, not dripping. It was a stream.

"Okay," Toby said. He grabbed the bench. He dragged it across the mud. It squealed against the stone.

Gerald watched.

"Not there. The left. Near the fissure."

Toby dragged it. He was struggling. The mud sucked at his boots.

"Use your legs," Gerald instructed.

"Your back is a lever arm. Bend the knees. Keep the spine straight.

Injury prevents future labor.

"

Toby grunted. He pushed. The bench slid.

Closer.

"Stop," Gerald said.

He extended a stone finger. He touched the wall.

The rock was wet.

Soft. He could feel the weakness in it. He pushed.

The rock crumbled.

Behind it, a dark void opened. Cold air rushed out.

"Drainage," Gerald muttered. "Finally."

The water from the ceiling swirled, sucked toward the new hole. The stream weakened.

Toby gasped. "Did you do it?"

"I utilized existing infrastructure," Gerald said. "I did not build it. I just revealed it."

He looked at his Compliance Rating.

47.

Still 47. The fine hadn't applied yet.

Because the violation was ongoing, and he had stopped the flood.

The fine was paused.

"Wait," Toby said. He pointed upward.

A shadow fell over them.

Not the woman. Not the hand.

A man. A large man in shining armor. He landed on the edge of the sinkhole with a heavy thud. He looked down at Toby, then at Gerald.

"Toby!" the man shouted. "Get away from the rock!"

"I'm working!" Toby shouted back.

The man jumped down. He landed in the mud with a splash. He drew a sword. It glowed with blue light.

"Step away from the Core," the man said. He pointed the sword at Gerald's stone face. "Identify yourself."

Gerald didn't move. He looked at the man's armor.

"Your plate is cracked," Gerald said.

The man frowned.

"What?"

"Left shoulder guard," Gerald said. "Structural compromise. If you swing that sword with full force, the pauldron will shatter.

You will lose mobility. You will be vulnerable.

"

The man looked at his shoulder. He touched the plate. A hairline fracture ran through the metal.

"How did you..."

"I am an inspector," Gerald said. "I see things."

The man lowered the sword slightly.

"You're not a monster."

"I am a dungeon core," Gerald said.

"Under construction. Currently facing a Class 4 flood event and a lockdown order. If you attack me, you will be destroying municipal property.

You will be fined.

Heavily."

The man hesitated. He looked at Toby.

"Kid, get out of here. This is a combat zone."

"It's not a combat zone," Gerald said. "It is a construction site. And you are violating safety protocols by standing in a flooded area without non-slip footwear."

The man looked down. He was wearing steel-toed boots. They were slippery.

"Who are you?" the man asked.

"Gerald Hutchins," Gerald said. "Formerly of the Ashford Municipal Planning Department. Currently, I am the only thing keeping this ceiling from collapsing on your head."

The man laughed. It was a nervous sound.

"You think you can stop a collapse?"

"I am fixing it," Gerald said.

"The water is draining. But the structural integrity is at 40%. I need reinforcement.

I need steel. I need concrete.

Do you have any spare rebar in your inventory?

"

The man blinked.

"Rebar?"

"Steel rods," Gerald said.

"Used for tensile strength. You are an adventurer.

You carry junk. I have seen adventurers carry forty pounds of cheese wheels. Surely you have some steel.

"

The man sheathed his sword. He looked at Toby.

"Did he say that?"

"He said it very clearly," Toby said. "He said my back was a lever arm."

The man sighed. He opened his inventory menu. A translucent screen appeared. He rummaged through it.

"I have... a broken shield," the man said.

"Break it apart," Gerald said. "The metal is high-quality. Use it to bridge the fissure."

"That's my family's shield," the man said.

"It is already broken," Gerald said. "You are admitting liability for the damage. If you do not use it to support the structure, I will cite you for negligent maintenance of public infrastructure. The penalty is death."

The man swallowed hard. He looked at the shield. He looked at Gerald.

"You're bluffing," the man said.

"Am I?" Gerald asked. He pointed to the water.

"Look at the water level.

It is still rising. Because the drain is too small. I need to widen it.

If I widen it without support, the wall collapses.

If the wall collapses, the library above falls into the void. Do you know how many books are in that library?

"

"Millions," Toby whispered.

"Exactly," Gerald said.

"Millions of books. And you. And me.

And the Minotaur. All crushed.

Unless you give me the shield.

"

The man gritted his teeth. He pulled the shield from his inventory. He smashed it against the stone floor. It shattered into jagged pieces of steel.

He picked up a piece. He walked toward the fissure.

"Where do I put it?" he asked.

"Gently," Gerald said. "Angle it forty-five degrees. Press it against the wet rock. It will act as a brace."

The man did it. He pushed the steel shard into the gap. He held it there. His arms shook.

"It's slipping," the man grunted.

"Brace with your foot," Gerald said. "Keep your center of gravity low."

The man adjusted. He leaned into the steel. The rock held.

"Good," Gerald said. "Now, pull the next piece."

Toby handed him another shard. The man wedged it in.

One by one, they braced the wall.

The water slowed. The ceiling stopped dripping.

Gerald felt the pressure in his stone mind ease.

**NOTICE OF COMPLIANCE IMPROVEMENT: STRUCTURAL BRACING INSTALLED.** **BONUS: +10 COMPLIANCE POINTS.**

57/100, and the man sat back in the mud, breathing hard.

"Did it work?"

"For now," Gerald said.

"But the lockdown is still active. I cannot spawn new entities.

I cannot repair the floor. I am trapped.

"

"What do you need to unlock it?" the man asked.

Gerald looked at the System interface. It was frozen. But the numbers were changing.

He saw a new option.

**APPEAL PENDING.** **REASON: EXTERNAL INTERVENTION BY UNLICENSED PARTY.** **HEARING SCHEDULE: IMMEDIATE.**

A new window popped up.

**SYSTEM NOTICE: A CORE HAS FILED AN APPEAL AGAINST YOUR LOCKDOWN.** **APPELLANT: CORE #9942, "THE IRON MAIDEN."** **CHALLENGE: DID YOU CAUSE THE FLOOD?**

Gerald stared at the name. The Iron Maiden. A torture-themed dungeon two blocks away.

"I did not cause the flood," Gerald said.

"But you benefited from it," the voice of the System echoed in the chamber. It was deeper now. Louder.

The man looked up.

"What is that?"

"A hearing," Gerald said. He closed his eyes. Or tried to.

He felt a pull. A tug in his core.

He was being dragged into the argument.

"I need you to stay," Gerald said to the man. "You are my witness. You helped fix the wall. You are a stakeholder."

The man nodded.

"I'm not going anywhere. Toby, stay with him."

Toby nodded.

Gerald's consciousness expanded. He was no longer in the mud.

He was in a white room.

A courtroom. But the judge was a giant, floating eye.

Across from him stood a shadow. A tall, slender figure made of black iron spikes.

"Gerald Hutchins," the shadow said. Its voice was the sound of grinding gears. "You cite me for negligence? You, who allowed a breach in his own foundation?"

Gerald straightened his imaginary tie.

"I cite you for unauthorized excavation," he said. "You dug through my sub-basement to plant a mine. That is a permit violation."

"The mine was for self-defense," the shadow said.

"Self-defense is not a valid exemption for zoning laws," Gerald said. "Your mine destabilized the strata. The flood was a direct result of your illegal action."

The Eye blinked.

"Evidence?" the Eye asked.

Gerald looked to the side. The man and Toby appeared in the white room. They were translucent, but visible.

"Witnesses," Gerald said.

"They saw the breach. They saw the water.

They assisted in the mitigation.

The mitigation proves the breach was accidental, but the cause remains illegal.

"

The shadow laughed.

"You have no authority. You are Level 1. I am Level 50."

"Code is code," Gerald said. "Level is irrelevant."

The Eye turned to the shadow.

"Does the appellant have a counter-argument?"

The shadow paused. It looked at Gerald. Then it looked at the witnesses.

"He... he helped us," the shadow said. "He helped fix the wall."

"Because he is a professional," Gerald said. "But that does not absolve you of the original sin."

The Eye hovered.

"Verdict," it droned.

The room held its breath.

Then, the ground shook.

Not in the white room. In the dungeon.

Gerald snapped back to his stone body, and the ceiling was cracking. A new sound. Heavy footsteps.

The Minotaur.

It burst through the far wall, ignoring the braced fissure. It roared, a sound that shook dust from the rafters.

"HE IS STILL THERE!" the man shouted. He grabbed his sword. "Gerald, what do we do?"

Gerald looked at the System interface. The verdict hadn't come through.

But a new message appeared.

**CITATION ISSUED: DUNGEON CORE #9942, "THE IRON MAIDEN."** **VIOLATION: UNAUTHORIZED EXCAVATION.** **PENALTY: -1000 HP.**

Somewhere, far away, a dungeon collapsed.

But the Minotaur was here. And it was angry.

Gerald looked at the man.

"You have a shield."

"It's broken," the man said.

"Pick it up," Gerald said. "Use it as a battering ram. Aim for its left knee. The patellar tendon is exposed if you hit it at a forty-five-degree angle."

The man didn't ask why. He didn't question the physics. He just ran.

Toby screamed.

Gerald watched them fight. He couldn't move. He was locked down.

But he could think, and he looked at the Minotaur's hooves. They were heavy. They were crushing the floor.

If the floor broke...

Gerald smiled. A thin, jagged thing.

He raised his stone hand. He didn't attack the Minotaur.

He attacked the floor, and he shattered the braced wall.

The water rushed back in.

And the man and the boy were standing right next to it.

The water rose.
