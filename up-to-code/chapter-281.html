Chapter 281: System Reset

# Chapter 281

The water was colder than the dungeon's ambient temperature suggested. It bit into Gerald's core, a sharp, metallic sting that threatened to dissolve his structural integrity before he even breached the surface.

He didn't fight the current.

Fighting the current required energy expenditure he could not afford. Instead, he aligned his mass with the flow of the drainage pipes, letting the municipal infrastructure carry him like refuse.

The pipes were tight.

Too tight for a human, but Gerald was no longer human. He was a spread of wet stone and calcified mold, pressing himself flat against the curved interior of the cast-iron pipe.

The friction burned.

Every inch of movement scraped away a fraction of his hit points.

*STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY: 14%.* *WATER DAMAGE: CRITICAL.*

The text pulsed in his peripheral vision, red and angry.

A grate appeared ahead. Narrow. Vertical bars spaced six inches apart.

Gerald stopped, and he couldn't fit. Not physically. His core was too dense.

But the code allowed for exceptions.

*SYSTEM PROMPT: SUBMIT EXCEPTION REQUEST FOR ACCESS VIA OBSTACLE.*

Gerald focused his intent. He didn't think about squeezing through. He thought about the building code. Specifically, Ashford Municipal Ordinance 14.2.B, subsection on emergency egress in historic districts.

"I am filing a variance," Gerald projected his thought into the System. "The obstruction impedes mandatory evacuation routes. Under Section 9, paragraph 4, temporary removal of non-structural barriers is permitted during active hazardous conditions."

The grate didn't move.

*REQUEST DENIED. OBSTRUCTION CLASSIFIED AS SECURITY FEATURE.*

Gerald felt a spike of genuine anger. Security features did not block mandatory egress. That was a violation. A clear, unambiguous violation.

He tried again, louder this time, pushing his will against the iron.

"Security features cannot override life-safety codes. I am citing the grate for blocking egress. Citation issued."

*ERROR: TARGET IS INANIMATE. CANNOT SERVE CITATION.*

Frustration bubbled up, hot and useless. The water rose around him, filling his lungs with phantom fluid. He was stuck.

Then, a vibration.

Not from the water. From the pipe itself. A rhythmic thrumming, heavy and deep. The grate rattled in its frame.

Gerald turned his awareness toward the source. Above him, through the grating, came the sound of footsteps. Heavy boots. The clatter of keys.

A voice, muffled by iron and water, spoke from the floor above.

"Hey, you hear that? Sounds like something's rattling in the sub-level."

"Yeah, probably rats. Or the leak from the east wing finally gave out."

"It's a rhythmic sound. Like knocking."

Gerald froze. He couldn't knock. He was a core. He didn't have hands.

But he had code, and he focused on the pipe's material. Cast iron. Brittle. Vulnerable to harmonic resonance if struck at the right frequency.

He couldn't strike it. But he could vibrate it.

Using the water damage debuff to his advantage, Gerald shifted his mass, compressing it against the pipe wall, then releasing it. A pulse. Then another.

*THUD.*

*THUD.*

*THUD.*

It wasn't a knock. It was a structural stress test.

The voice above paused.

"That's not a rat. That sounds like a pipe hammer."

"Call maintenance?"

"Call the Guild liaison. If it's a dungeon leak, we need to know if it's pressurized."

A pause. Then, the clatter of a phone being pulled from a pocket.

Gerald waited. His integrity dropped to 12%.

The phone call was short.

"Yeah, it's Hutchins again.

Yeah, the guy from the library.

No, he's not... look, he's in the pipe.

I'm serious. He's sending structural vibration patterns.

It's not random.

"

A long silence.

"Okay. I'll come down with the key. But if you're wrong, I'm writing you up for disturbing the peace."

The footsteps moved away.

Gerald's countdown timer ticked. *01:55:00.*

He had less than two hours to reach the Council chambers, file a grievance, and prove that the Auditor's presence constituted a hostile takeover. If he missed the filing deadline, the System would classify his absence as a default judgment. He would be dissolved.

The pipe widened. Not physically, but perceptually. The System was recognizing the vibration pattern as a request for inspection.

*SYSTEM NOTICE: INTERACTION DETECTED. GRATE STATUS: UNSECURED FOR MAINTENANCE ACCESS.*

The bars slid upward.

Gerald pushed upward, dragging his heavy, waterlogged mass through the opening. He emerged into a maintenance corridor. The air was dry, smelling of ozone and old dust.

He was on Sub-Level 1. The path to the Council chambers was open.

But he wasn't alone, and a figure stood at the end of the hall. Tall.

Clad in polished silver armor that gleamed under the flickering fluorescent lights.

A Paladin. Not a generic one. The crest on his chestplate was the Ashford Municipal Seal.

Gerald's heart—or whatever passed for it—stumbled.

The Paladin stepped forward. His hand rested on the hilt of a sword that hummed with holy energy.

"You're the library core," the Paladin said. His voice was calm, professional. "I'm Captain Vane."

Gerald tried to stand. His legs were sludge. He managed to prop himself up against the wall.

"I am filing a grievance," Gerald said. His voice was wet, gurgling. "Against the Auditor."

Vane didn't draw his sword. He looked at Gerald with an expression of mild curiosity.

"The Auditor is a neutral party. He monitors compliance."

"The Auditor is facilitating a hostile takeover of public infrastructure," Gerald said. "I have documentation."

Vane raised an eyebrow.

"Documentation? You're a dungeon core.

You don't have a filing system.

You have loot tables.

"

"I have a compliance record," Gerald said. "And I have a witness."

Vane's gaze shifted to the empty space beside Gerald.

"The Auditor isn't here."

"He is," Gerald said. "He's in the walls.

He's in the wiring.

He's auditing the electrical system of the Council chambers.

I can prove it.

"

Vane took a step closer.

"Prove it, and you might live. Fail, and I'll put you down. We've had enough of these rogue cores messing with the city's stability."

Gerald pulled the rusted keycard from his pocket. It was useless, but it was physical evidence.

"I need access to the main junction box," Gerald said. "Sub-Level 1, Sector 4. The one that feeds power to the Chamber's lighting."

Vane hesitated.

"That's restricted."

"It's public infrastructure," Gerald said. "And it's currently in violation of Ashford Ordinance 8.4, which prohibits unauthorized signal interference in municipal buildings."

Vane looked at the keycard. Then at Gerald's decaying form.

"You're barely holding together," Vane said. "If I take you to the junction box, you'll dissolve before we get there."

"Then carry me," Gerald said.

Vane blinked. "Excuse me?"

"I am requesting assistance under the Good Samaritan Act, Section 22. A citizen in distress is entitled to aid. I am distressed. My structural integrity is critical."

Vane stared at him. For a moment, the Paladin's stoic mask cracked. A flicker of confusion, then reluctant understanding.

"You're a monster," Vane said. "But you're also a taxpayer."

"I am a compliant taxpayer," Gerald corrected.

Vane sighed. It was a long, weary sound. He sheathed his sword.

"Fine. But if you try to hack the junction box, I will destroy you."

"Understood," Gerald said. "No hacking. Only auditing."

Vane walked over. He didn't touch Gerald directly. Instead, he placed a hand on the floor near Gerald's core. A soft light emanated from his palm, stabilizing Gerald's mass.

"Move," Vane said. "Quickly."

They moved through the corridors. The Council chambers were close. Gerald could feel the Auditor's presence growing stronger, a static charge in the air that made his skin prickle.

As they turned a corner, they ran into another group.

Three adventurers. A Mage, a Rogue, and a Fighter. They were blocking the path.

The Mage, a young woman with sparks flying from her fingertips, pointed at Gerald.

"There he is! The one causing the tremors!"

The Rogue stepped forward, dagger drawn.

"Boss said to kill him. He's disrupting the raid zone."

Vane stepped between them.

"Stand down."

The Mage frowned.

"Captain? That's a core. It's hostile."

"He is under municipal jurisdiction," Vane said. "And he is currently filing a grievance. Back off."

The Rogue scoffed.

"Grievance? What's he going to do, write a strongly worded letter?"

Gerald looked at the Rogue.

"I am citing you for obstruction of justice," Gerald said. "And for carrying unregistered edged weapons in a government building."

The Rogue laughed.

"Nice try, slime."

*SYSTEM NOTICE: CITATION ISSUED TO TARGET 'JAX THE SHADOW'.*

The Rogue froze. His dagger slipped from his hand, clattering to the floor. His speed stat, usually high, plummeted. He staggered, his legs buckling as the debuff hit.

*STATUS: ENCUMBERED (TEMPORARY). MOVEMENT SPEED -50%.*

The Rogue tried to move, but he dragged his feet, moving in slow motion.

The Mage's eyes widened.

"What did you do to him?"

"I enforced the code," Gerald said. "Now, move."

Vane looked at Gerald with new respect. Or perhaps fear.

"You really can do that," Vane said. "Anywhere. Anytime."

"Only if you violate the code," Gerald said. "Which you are doing by blocking public access."

The Mage lowered her staff.

"Okay. We're leaving. But the Guild won't like this."

"Tell the Guild," Gerald said. "That their agents are now on probation."

The adventurers retreated, dragging the slowed Rogue with them.

Vane led Gerald to the junction box. It was a heavy steel door, set into the concrete wall. A red light blinked above it.

"Open it," Gerald said.

Vane used his master key. The door hissed open.

Inside, the electrical grid was a mess of wires and breakers. But in the center, wrapped around the main fuse box, was a shimmering, translucent figure. The Auditor.

The Auditor looked up. His eyes were voids of black code.

*Gerald Hutchins,* the Auditor said. *You are late.*

"I am on time," Gerald said. "The hearing hasn't started."

*The hearing begins when the grievance is filed. You have not filed.*

"I am filing now," Gerald said. He pointed a tendril of stone at the Auditor. "I am citing the Auditor for Unauthorized Modification of Municipal Infrastructure."

The Auditor tilted his head. *Define.*

"Section 14, paragraph 7," Gerald said.

"The System may monitor. It may audit. It may fine.

It may not alter the physical state of a citizen's body without consent.

You altered my body. You forced my dissolution.

You forced my migration.

That is assault.

"

*My presence caused structural instability in the surrounding area,* the Auditor said. *The dissolution was a natural consequence of proximity to a high-density Core.*

"That is not a natural consequence," Gerald said. "That is a violation of the separation clause.

You are a regulatory body.

You are not a combatant.

By engaging in combat with my previous form, you violated the neutrality protocol.

"

The Auditor's shimmer intensified. *The neutrality protocol does not apply to hostile entities.*

"I was not hostile," Gerald said. "I was compliant. I was filing paperwork. You attacked me because I threatened your efficiency."

*Your threat is irrelevant. The City is unstable. I am stabilizing it.*

"By destroying it," Gerald said. "You are creating a vacuum. A power vacuum.

You are using the City's infrastructure to spawn your own compliance agents. Look at the wiring.

"

Gerald pointed to the wires wrapping the fuse box. They weren't just power lines. They were veins. Pulse points of dark energy.

"You're using the municipal grid as a spawn network," Gerald said. "That is a Class A violation. Unauthorized biological generation within a public utility."

The Auditor went still.

The System text flashed, white and bold.

*SYSTEM NOTICE: GRIEVANCE FILED.* *TARGET: THE AUDITOR.* *CHARGE: UNAUTHORIZED SPAWNING AND ASSAULT.* *HEARING COMMENCED.*

The walls of the room trembled. The air grew heavy.

Vane stepped back, his hand on his sword.

"What happens now?"

"Now," Gerald said, "we wait for the Judge."

But the Judge didn't come.

Instead, the shadows in the room lengthened. From the dark corners of the junction box, figures pulled themselves out of the walls.

They were not goblins. They were not adventurers.

They were Auditors. Dozens of them. Small, shimmering, faceless. They poured into the room like smoke.

*THE APPEAL IS DENIED,* the central Auditor said. *THE GRIEVANCE IS DISMISSED FOR LACK OF JURISDICTION.*

Gerald felt his core crack. *INTEGRITY: 3%.*

"You can't dismiss it," Gerald said. "I have standing."

*Standing is a privilege,* the Auditor said. *Not a right. And you have forfeited your right by entering the chambers without a permit.*

Vane drew his sword.

"He has the key! I saw him use it!"

*The key was stolen,* the Auditor said. *Evidence inadmissible.*

The Auditors closed in.

Gerald looked at Vane.

"The keycard," he said. "It's not stolen. It was abandoned.

Section 3, paragraph 1: 'Abandoned property becomes public domain after forty-eight hours.'

"

Vane paused. "You're citing the keycard?"

"I'm citing the theft allegation," Gerald said. "If it wasn't stolen, then I had the right to enter. And if I had the right to enter, my grievance is valid."

The Auditors hesitated.

The System text flickered.

*EVIDENCE REVIEW: KEYCARD STATUS.*

*ORIGINAL OWNER: MAINTENANCE DEPARTMENT.* *DISPOSAL DATE: [UNKNOWN].* *STATUS: UNVERIFIED.*

The Auditors surged forward.

Gerald's vision went dark. He could feel himself unraveling, piece by piece.

Then, a hand grabbed his shoulder.

Vane.

The Paladin wasn't attacking. He was holding Gerald together.

"I'm filing a witness statement," Vane said loudly. "I saw the maintenance log. I saw the disposal record. It was three days ago."

The Auditors stopped.

*WITNESS TESTIMONY: CAPTAIN VANE.* *VALIDITY CHECK: IN PROGRESS.*

The central Auditor turned to Vane. *You risk your rank for a monster.*

"I risk my rank for the truth," Vane said. "And the truth is, he didn't steal it. He found it."

The System hung in the balance.

*VALIDITY: CONFIRMED.*

*GRIEVANCE: VALID.* *HEARING: REOPENED.*

The Auditors scattered, dissolving into the wires.

Gerald slumped against Vane's leg. His core was barely holding.

*INTEGRITY: 1%.*

"You have one hour," Vane said. "Before the City Council arrives. If you don't fix your dungeon, they'll pave over you."

Gerald looked up at the Auditor, who was fading.

"One hour," Gerald wheezed. "Is enough."

"For what?"

"For the counter-citation."

Gerald smiled. A terrible, broken smile.

"You cited me for inadequate ventilation," Gerald said. "But you forgot to check the pressure."

The Auditor's form flickered. *Pressure?*

"I'm over-pressurized," Gerald said. "And I'm about to blow."

The System text turned red.

*WARNING: STRUCTURAL FAILURE IMMINENT.* *EXPLOSIVE DECOMPRESSION DETECTED.*

Vane's eyes widened.

"Get back!"

Gerald didn't move. He focused every remaining ounce of will on the single violation he had found in the Auditor's own code.

*VIOLATION: OVERPRESSURE IN REGULATORY ENTITY.* *PENALTY: SYSTEM RESET.*

Gerald pushed, and the world went white.

When the light cleared, Gerald was gone.
