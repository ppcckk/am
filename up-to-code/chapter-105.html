Chapter 105: Broken Catalyst

# Chapter 105 The glass was not clear. It was obsidian, black and slick, and it hummed with a frequency that made Gerald’s teeth ache.

He had been standing on solid concrete for thirty-one years. Concrete was reliable.

Concrete had rules.

Concrete did not become a frictionless mirror that reflected the void above it. But the floor beneath the library sub-basement had hardened into a crystalline lattice, sealing the breach where the Void had tried to escape.

Gerald slid.

It wasn’t a graceful slide. It was the undignified, flailing collapse of a fifty-four-year-old man who had spent three decades wearing steel-toed boots designed for traction, not for skating on volcanic glass. He hit the ground hard, the impact jarring his already depleted mana reserves. The rat, which had been riding his shoulder, squeaked and scrambled away, its claws finding purchase on the slick surface with surprising ease.

Gerald lay on his back, staring up at the ceiling, and the ventilation shaft, the one that had sucked the Void into oblivion, was gone. In its place was a perfect circle of smooth, black stone, seamlessly integrated into the architecture.

"Beautiful work," the dwarf said, his voice echoing strangely in the new acoustic environment. "A bit cold, but structurally sound."

Gerald tried to sit up. His arms shook. "I didn't do that."

"The System did that," the dwarf replied, tapping his pen against the clipboard he had taken from Gerald.

"You cited the Void for unauthorized structural modification of a load-bearing surface. The System interpreted the citation as a demand for immediate remediation.

So it remediated.

It turned the leak-proof barrier into glass. Very compliant. Very annoying."

Gerald groaned, rolling onto his side. He looked at his hands.

They were translucent, flickering in and out of existence, and he was running on fumes.

The dark. The blind.

The silence.

"Inspector General is coming," Gerald muttered. "I need to be presentable. I need to be in uniform."

"You’re a core," the dwarf said. "You don’t have a uniform. You have a dungeon. And right now, your dungeon is a slippery floor with a missing tooth."

"It’s not a missing tooth," Gerald corrected, his voice regaining some of its bureaucratic edge despite the pain. "It’s a non-compliant aperture. We need to seal it."

The dwarf looked at the hole in the ceiling, then at the glass floor. "The Void is gone. The threat is neutralized. Why fix it?"

"Because it’s a hole," Gerald said. "And I don’t leave holes. It’s a liability."

He tried to stand, but his legs buckled. The glass floor offered no resistance.

He slipped again, sliding backward until his back hit the wall.

The wall, too, was changing. The damp, moldy concrete was receding, replaced by the same black crystal. The transformation was spreading.

The dungeon was evolving.

Not into something stronger, but into something more legally defensible.

Gerald felt a pull in his chest. A warmth. It was the feeling of a new rule being written.

**SYSTEM NOTIFICATION: DUNGEON ARCHITECTURE UPDATE** **Substrate Material: Obsidian Glass** **Property: High Reflectivity, High Durability, Low Friction** **Compliance Bonus: +15 Structural Integrity** **Compliance Penalty: -20 Egress Safety (Slip Hazard)**

Gerald stared at the notification. Of course. The System rewarded durability, but it punished accessibility. He had traded one problem for another.

"I need handrails," Gerald said.

"You don’t have any materials," the dwarf pointed out. "You spent your mana flushing the Void. You have three mold spores and a rat. You’re out of stock."

Gerald closed his eyes, and he couldn’t improvise. Improvisation made him nauseous. But he could audit.

He could find a loophole.

"Check the incident report," Gerald said. "The one you wrote."

The dwarf raised an eyebrow. "For what?"

"Look at the timestamp. When did the Void enter?"

The dwarf glanced at the clipboard. "02:14 AM. During the library’s closed hours."

"And what is the library’s status during closed hours?"

"Unoccupied. Non-operational. Public access restricted."

"Exactly," Gerald said.

He felt a faint pulse of mana return.

"If the dungeon is in an 'Unoccupied/Non-Operational' state, then the primary function of the space is storage. Not transit.

Storage doesn’t require egress. It requires containment."

The dwarf stopped writing. He looked at Gerald. "You’re arguing that we don’t need to fix the floor because it’s no longer a hallway?"

"I’m arguing that we can reclassify the zone," Gerald said. "Form 44-C.

Change of Use.

If we declare this entire section a 'Secure Archive,' the building code changes. We don’t need handrails. We don’t need non-slip flooring.

We need security."

The dwarf grinned. It was a wolfish grin. "You want to turn the dungeon into a vault."

"I want to make it compliant," Gerald said. "And I want to charge for the privilege."

The dwarf tapped the pen. "Let’s see.

Change of Use requires a permit.

Permits require an inspection. Inspections require a Core present in the zone."

Gerald was present. He was the Core.

But he was weak.

"Do it," he said.

"File it now. Before the Inspector General arrives."

The dwarf’s fingers flew across the paper. The air around them shimmered.

**SYSTEM NOTIFICATION: PERMIT REQUESTED** **Type: Change of Use (Residential/Commercial to Secure Archive)** **Status: Pending** **Requirement: Proof of Security Measures**

"It’s pending," the dwarf said. "You need to prove security. You need a lock."

Gerald looked at the rat. The rat was sitting on the glass floor, grooming its fur. It looked unimpressed by the existential crisis.

"Use the rat," Gerald said.

The dwarf laughed. "You’re joking."

"I’m not. The rat is a biological entity. It’s currently occupying a restricted zone.

If I designate the rat as a 'Security Asset,' it becomes part of the dungeon’s defensive infrastructure. That counts as a security measure."

"That’s... technically true," the dwarf said, skeptical. "But it’s absurd."

"It’s within the code," Gerald said. "Paragraph 12, Section 4: 'Biological deterrents, if registered and fed, satisfy minimum security thresholds for Archive status.'"

The dwarf scribbled. "Okay. Let’s try it."

He wrote: *Security Asset: Rattus Norvegicus. Designation: Sentry.*

A chime sounded.

**SYSTEM NOTIFICATION: PERMIT APPROVED** **Zone Reclassified: Secure Archive** **New Passive Ability: Archive Lock** **Effect: Entry to the Archive Zone requires a key or permission from the Core. Unauthorized entry triggers automatic containment fields.**

The glass floor solidified.

It wasn’t slippery anymore. It was rigid. The walls stopped changing.

The mold spores in the corner stopped writhing, and the dungeon had settled.

Gerald took a breath. It was a shaky breath, but it was real. He stood up. His legs held.

"Now what?" the dwarf asked.

"Now we wait for the Inspector General," Gerald said. "And we prepare for the audit."

The air in the room shifted. The temperature dropped. The light from the emergency exit signs, which had been flickering, stabilized into a harsh, fluorescent white.

A door appeared in the wall. It wasn’t a door that had always been there, and was a door that had been inserted into reality, framed in polished brass. It opened without a handle.

Stepping through the door was a figure. It was tall, slender, and made of light. Its suit appeared to be made of red tape. Its face was a blank mask, featureless except for a pair of spectacles that magnified its eyes into giant, unblinking orbs.

This was the Inspector General, which did not speak; it simply looked at Gerald, then at the dwarf, then at the rat.

Gerald straightened his tie. He didn’t have a tie, but he straightened his nonexistent collar anyway. "Inspector General. Welcome to the Ashford Municipal Library Sub-Basement, now reclassified as Archive Zone A."

The Inspector General tilted its head. It pulled a scroll from thin air. The scroll was long. It unrolled, cascading down to the floor, covering the glass.

It started reading.

"Gerald Hutchins.

Core ID: 8940-A. Compliance Rating: 42/100. Up from 12.

This is... acceptable. Barely."

The IG looked up. "You used a biological asset as a security measure. This is a loophole. A significant one."

"It’s not a loophole," Gerald said, his voice steady. "It’s a precedent. I checked the Code. It’s allowed."

"It is allowed," the IG admitted. "But it is inefficient. And it attracts attention."

The IG gestured to the ceiling. To the spot where the Void had been.

"You flushed a Class-A entity," the IG said.

"You did not kill it.

You did not trap it. You used a ventilation system to move it to a higher-pressure zone, where it dissipated due to environmental incompatibility.

This is creative. It is also dangerous."

"It was effective," Gerald said.

"It was effective because the Void was stupid," the IG said. "Other voids will not be. Other voids will have cores. Other voids will have lawyers."

Gerald felt a chill. "You mean other dungeon cores?"

"I mean rival Cores," the IG said. "You have flagged yourself.

By resolving a Class-A breach with such... unconventional methods, you have registered as a 'High-Risk/High-Reward' Core.

Adventurers are already requesting access. But so are others."

The IG tapped the scroll. "There are three Cores within a five-mile radius who have filed complaints against you. They claim you are engaging in 'Unfair Competitive Practices.' Specifically, they claim that your use of Citations against their spawns constitutes harassment."

Gerald frowned. "I cited them for code violations. That’s my job."

"In the System, it’s a weapon," the IG said. "And weapons are regulated."

The IG pulled a pen from its sleeve. It was a fountain pen made of bone.

"I am placing you under Probationary Oversight," the IG said. "You may no longer issue Citations to other Cores within a two-mile radius without prior approval. This is a restriction on your primary tool. Do you understand?"

Gerald’s heart sank. Without Citations, he was just a basement. A damp, moldy basement with a rat.

"Understood," Gerald said.

"Good," the IG said. "However, I am also granting you a Temporary Variance."

Gerald blinked. "A variance?"

"For the next forty-eight hours," the IG said, "you are exempt from the Ventilation Compliance Requirement.

The System recognizes that you have converted the ventilation shaft into a structural element. Therefore, you do not need to fix the airflow.

You can seal the hole.

Permanently."

Gerald looked at the dwarf. The dwarf looked back.

"Seal the hole?" Gerald asked.

"Yes," the IG said. "But here is the condition.

You must seal it yourself.

You must craft the seal.

You cannot use the System’s auto-repair. You must build it.

With your own hands. With your own mana. And you must do it in the next hour."

The IG checked its wrist. It didn’t have a wrist. It checked the air.

"The Inspector General is busy," the IG said. "I will return in one hour.

If the seal is not complete, I will revoke the Variance.

And I will re-cite you for the ventilation violation. With interest."

The IG turned and walked back toward the brass door.

"Also," the IG added, pausing at the threshold. "The rat. It’s growing."

Before Gerald could ask what that meant, the IG vanished. The brass door dissolved into the wall.

The room was silent again.

Gerald looked at the ceiling. The hole was still there. A perfect circle of black sky.

"He said I have to build it myself," Gerald said. "He said I have to craft the seal."

The dwarf looked at him. "You have zero mana, Gerald."

"I have a rat," Gerald said.

The dwarf groaned. "Oh, for the love of—"

"Look at the Code," Gerald said, pointing to the air. "Section 9, Subsection D: 'Material Synthesis via Biological Catalyst.' If you have a biological entity that has consumed high-purity mana, you can use its waste as a binding agent for structural repairs."

The dwarf stared at him. "You want to use the rat’s... what?"

"Excrement," Gerald said.

"The rat has been eating mold spores. The mold spores were high in mana density because they were growing in a Void leak.

The rat is now a mana-dense biological unit.

Its waste is a binding agent."

The dwarf looked at the rat. The rat looked at the dwarf. The rat burped.

"This is disgusting," the dwarf said.

"It’s compliant," Gerald said.

He walked toward the hole. He didn’t look down.

He looked up, and he focused his mind.

He remembered the feeling of the glass. The structure.

The lattice.

He reached out with his mind, not his hands. He felt the edges of the hole. He felt the obsidian. And then, he felt the rat.

He didn’t command it. He requested it. He asked it to be useful.

The rat hopped onto the glass ledge. It looked at Gerald. Then it looked at the hole.

Gerald concentrated. He pulled on the last dregs of his mana.

It was painful.

It felt like trying to lift a boulder with a toothpick. But he had a plan. He had a method.

He visualized the seal.

Not a plug. A patch.

A patch made of resin.

Made of binding agent, and the rat sat. It waited.

Gerald held the structure in his mind. He waited for the rat to do its part.

The rat stood up. It turned around. It lifted its tail.

And then, a beam of pure, golden light shot from the rat’s rear end.

It wasn’t excrement. It was concentrated mana. The rat had metabolized the mold into pure energy. The System had misinterpreted the "biological waste" as "mana output" because the entity had been enhanced by the Void leak.

The beam hit the edge of the hole. It solidified instantly, forming a bridge of gold.

Gerald gasped. "Oh."

The dwarf dropped his clipboard. "That’s not excrement. That’s a laser."

"It’s a binding agent," Gerald corrected, though he was trembling. "It’s a high-efficiency binding agent."

More light poured out. The rat was draining itself. It was getting smaller. It was shaking.

Gerald panicked. "Stop!"

The rat ignored him. It was working. It was building.

The gold bridge extended. It wove through the air, forming a net.

The net tightened.

It sealed the hole.

The black sky vanished. The obsidian floor remained.

The rat collapsed. It was tiny. It was gray. It was barely breathing.

The System chimed.

**SYSTEM NOTIFICATION: SEAL COMPLETE** **Material: Refined Mana Resin** **Quality: Legendary** **Cost: 1 Biological Asset (Deceased)** **New Passive: Mana Regeneration +10%**

Gerald stared at the tiny, lifeless shape on the glass, and the rat was dead.

He had won. He had saved the dungeon. He had satisfied the Inspector General.

And he had killed his only friend.

The dwarf picked up the clipboard. His face was grim. "Gerald."

Gerald didn’t answer. He was looking at the gold seal. It was beautiful.

It was perfect. It was compliant.

Then, the message appeared.

*SENDER: INSPECTOR GENERAL* *SUBJECT: AUDIT COMPLETE* *TEXT: The seal is magnificent, and the efficiency is commendable. The loss of the biological asset is noted. But the method used to achieve this result has attracted the attention of the Void King.

He has noted your use of 'Mana Excretion' as a structural tool.

He is coming to test it.*

Gerald looked up. The air in the room grew heavy.

The floor under the gold seal vibrated. Not from the rat. From something else.

Something was coming up through the hole. And this time, it wasn’t a leak.

It was an invitation.

Gerald looked at the dwarf. He looked at the dead rat. He looked at the gold seal.

He had used a loophole. He had used a tool. And now, the tool had broken.

He had to find a new one. Before the thing that was coming up could get through the door.

"Get me a new rat," Gerald said.

The dwarf looked at him. "Gerald, we don’t have time."

"Then get me a cat," Gerald said. "I need a catalyst."
