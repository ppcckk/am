Chapter 335: Citation Null

# Chapter 335

The dust was still settling when the ground shook.

Not a tremor. Not an earthquake.

It was a rhythmic, percussive impact that traveled through the concrete floor and vibrated up through Gerald's spectral form.

A heavy boot. Then another. Then the scraping of metal against stone, loud and deliberate, like a man kicking a locked door that he intended to break down with his bare hands.

Gerald paused mid-measurement. His tape measure hovered three inches above a crack in the foundation that he had already flagged for epoxy injection. He did not look at the intruders. He looked at his clipboard.

*Form 11-B: Unauthorized Structural Alteration.*

He needed to document the source of the vibration before he could assess the damage.

The adventurers were still cleaning.

The leader, a man whose sword was nearly as large as Gerald's entire torso, was on his knees, scooping up gray particulate matter into a cloth bag. His party members hovered nearby, eyes darting between the mold residue and Gerald's floating, translucent form.

They looked terrified.

Not of the monsters. Of the paperwork.

Gerald ignored them. His attention was on the ceiling.

A hairline fracture zigzagged across the plaster. It ran from the load-bearing column to the northern wall.

It was not a structural failure, which was a stress riser caused by sudden, excessive weight transfer.

Someone was pushing against the ceiling from the other side.

"Stay down," Gerald said. His voice was flat, the same tone he used when telling a contractor that the plumbing rough-in did not match the approved blueprints. "You are currently in violation of occupancy limits. If you stand, you will trip over the safety barriers I intend to install in approximately four minutes."

The leader froze. He did not stand. He did not sit. He remained on his knees, holding the bag of dust like a man holding a grenade.

The knocking came again. Louder. The plaster dust sifted down onto the leader's shoulders.

"That's not our doing," the adventurer whispered. "That's... that's something else."

Gerald knew what it was. He had felt the presence an hour ago, a cold pressure pressing against the dungeon's perimeter like water against a dam. The Auditor had warned him. *Next time, I will bring a warrant.*

A warrant required a judge. But a warrant also required a crime to be present at the time of the service.

Gerald's fingers twitched. He pulled a fresh form from his spectral inventory. *Form 44-C: Emergency Egress Obstruction.*

He didn't need to see who was knocking. He needed to know if they had a permit to be there. And if they did not, and if their presence was blocking a required exit route, the citation could be served immediately.

Without a hearing. Without an appeal.

Because *Form 44-C* was a summary judgment violation. It could be issued on sight.

The door to the sub-basement didn't open. It exploded.

A section of the reinforced concrete wall, six feet wide and eight feet high, disintegrated.

Rubble cascaded into the room, kicking up a cloud of silica dust that coated the adventurers' armor. Through the hole strode a figure that was not human. It was seven feet of armored obsidian, shaped like a bear but moving with the jerky, unnatural gait of a construct.

Its eyes glowed with a dull, red light.

Behind it, a human figure in a sharp black suit and a white helmet stepped through the debris.

The human held a tablet. He did not look at the adventurers. He did not look at Gerald. He looked at the floor.

"Inspector Hutchins," the human said. His voice was amplified, echoing off the damp walls. "The Office of the Inspector General has received a complaint regarding noise pollution and unauthorized structural modification within this zone."

Gerald did not move. He kept his tape measure extended.

The construct growled. Steam hissed from its joints. It raised a massive, clawed hand, preparing to smash the load-bearing column.

"Stop," Gerald said.

It was not a shout. It was a command issued with the absolute authority of a man who had spent thirty-one years telling builders that their drywall was wet.

The construct hesitated.

The System's compliance algorithms were running in the background. The construct was a spawn. It had a Compliance Rating.

If it caused damage to a structure rated 'Compliant,' it would incur a penalty.

"You are blocking the egress route," Gerald said, pointing a spectral finger at the construct. "That is a Class A violation. Immediate citation is authorized."

The human in the suit looked up. He smiled. It was a thin, bureaucratic smile.

"Ah, but you are citing *us*," the man said.

"We are not spawns.

We are auditors. And you... you have a rating of forty.

You are failing.

"

Gerald looked at his dashboard.

**[COMPLIANCE RATING: 40.]**

It hadn't changed. The dust cleanup had been credited.

The mold removal had been credited.

But the hole in the wall? That was a new variable.

"And you have damaged the perimeter," Gerald said. "That is a Class B violation. But you are not here to fix it. You are here to audit me."

"Precisely," the auditor said.

"And the audit begins now. You have seventy hours to bring your rating to sixty-five, or your dungeon will be condemned.

Condemned means dissolved.

Core fragmentation. Total erasure.

"

The construct stepped forward. The ground cracked beneath its foot.

"This is a test," the auditor said. "The System tests for resilience. If you cannot defend your compliance, you do not deserve to exist."

Gerald looked at the hole in the wall. He looked at the construct. He looked at the dust.

He had a problem.

If he cited the auditor, he would be attacking a representative of the System. That would trigger a Hostile Response protocol.

The System would turn his dungeon into a hostile zone for all non-spawn entities.

The adventurers would leave, and the mold was dead. The rat was hiding.

He would be alone with the construct.

But if he didn't cite them, they would continue to damage the structure. Every crack reduced his rating. Every piece of rubble reduced his rating.

He needed a loophole.

Gerald looked at the construct again. It was a heavy hitter.

High strength.

High defense. Low intelligence. It followed orders.

He looked at the auditor. The auditor was watching the tablet. He was checking the data streams.

Gerald looked at the hole, and the hole was not a breach.

A breach was an unauthorized entry.

But what if the hole was not an entry? What if it was an *exit*?

Dungeon codes required minimum egress width. The original door was thirty-two inches wide. Non-compliant. It should have been thirty-six.

The hole was six feet wide, which was over-compliant.

But there was a rule about *obstructions*.

Gerald pulled a new form. *Form 77-D: Hazardous Obstruction of Compliant Egress.*

He didn't cite the auditor. He cited the *rubble*.

"The debris field," Gerald said, his voice gaining a dangerous edge. "It is obstructing the compliant egress route created by your unauthorized modification of the structure. You have created a compliant exit, but you have failed to clear the hazard. That is a negligence violation."

The auditor frowned.

"We are not responsible for debris management."

"You are responsible for the site condition," Gerald said.

"You broke the wall. You left the hazard.

By failing to clear it, you are obstructing the escape path for any potential victims within the dungeon.

Including me."

The System paused. The air grew cold.

A notification popped up in Gerald's peripheral vision.

**[NEW MECHANIC DETECTED: NEGLECT OF DUTY.]** **[CITATION ISSUED: AUDITOR UNIT 'VEX-9' AND ASSOCIATED SPAWN.]** **[PENALTY: TEMPORARY STUN AND FORCED CLEANUP PROTOCOL.]**

The construct froze. Its red eyes flickered.

The System's enforcement algorithms locked onto the debris, and the construct was programmed to attack threats.

But the debris was now classified as a *hazard that prevented the construct from performing its primary function: causing damage.*

To cause damage, it had to move. To move, it had to clear the path.

The construct groaned. It dropped its hand. It looked at the rubble at its feet.

It kicked the rocks out of the way.

The auditor's smile vanished.

"You're... you're citing the environment?"

"I'm citing the negligence," Gerald said. "Clear the path, or the citation escalates to a Shutdown Order. Your spawn will be deactivated for non-compliance with safety standards."

The auditor tapped his tablet furiously.

"That's... that's a technicality. The code doesn't say—"

"Section 4, Paragraph 12," Gerald interrupted. "'Any agent causing a structural breach assumes liability for all secondary hazards resulting from that breach until the hazard is removed or mitigated.' I wrote that paragraph into the bylaws of the Ashford Municipal Code. The System mirrored it. It's in the source."

The auditor stared at him. For the first time, he looked unsure.

"You cannot cite an auditor," the man said.

"I am not citing you," Gerald said. "I am citing the spawn for failing to clear the hazard it created. And you, as the commanding officer, are liable for the spawn's actions under the Chain of Command statute."

The construct had cleared a path. It looked at Gerald. It wanted to attack.

But the rubble was still blocking its left side. It couldn't swing its arm.

It was stuck in a compliance loop.

The adventurer leader looked at Gerald. His eyes were wide.

"Can you... can you do that to the mold?" the adventurer asked.

"I cited the mold," Gerald said. "The mold is dead."

The auditor gritted his teeth.

"Fine. We will clean up.

But this is not over, Inspector.

The audit continues. And we are bringing in reinforcements.

"

"Reinforcements must have permits," Gerald said.

"Send in the permits before the entity crosses the threshold. If it crosses without a permit, it is trespassing. If it is trespassing, it can be ejected.

No hearing required.

"

The auditor looked at the construct. The construct was now sweeping the floor with a broom made of its own arm, a grotesque parody of housekeeping.

"You are insane," the auditor said.

"I am compliant," Gerald corrected.

The auditor turned. He walked back out the hole. The construct followed, dragging its feet, clearing the debris as it went.

The dust settled.

Gerald looked at his dashboard.

**[COMPLIANCE RATING: 41.]**

One point up, which was not enough.

He had seventy hours. He had gained one.

But then a new notification appeared. It was red. It pulsed with a urgency that made Gerald's spectral form shudder.

**[ALERT: HIGH-VALUE TARGET DETECTED.]** **[ENTITY: 'THE ARCHITECT' HAS ENTERED THE DISTRICT.]** **[STATUS: UNLICENSED.]** **[ACTION: IMMEDIATE CITATION REQUIRED.]**

Gerald froze.

The Architect, which was a rumor in the deep networks.

A Core that didn't build dungeons.

It *edited* them. It changed the laws of physics within its borders.

It created spaces that shouldn't exist. It was not a builder. It was a vandal.

And it was here.

Gerald looked at the hole in the wall. The construct had swept the last of the rubble out. The path was clear.

But the air outside the hole was wrong. It shimmered. The geometry of the hallway beyond the library sub-basement was bending. The angles were non-Euclidean.

Gerald felt a wave of nausea. Not from the magic. From the lack of right angles.

He pulled out his protractor, and he had to measure this.

He had to cite this.

But as he floated toward the hole, a shadow fell over him. Not from the construct. From the ceiling.

The roof of the library above peeled away. Not physically.

Conceptually.

The bricks turned into glass. The glass turned into mirrors. The mirrors reflected infinite versions of Gerald, each one holding a different form, each one pointing a different citation.

The System was not just auditing him, which was testing him against a god.

Gerald looked at the form in his hand. *Form 11-B: Unlicensed Biological Entity.*

The Architect was not biological.

He needed a new form.

He needed to invent one.

Gerald floated into the hole. The air outside smelled of ozone and old ink.

He opened his mouth to speak, to issue a citation for the distortion of local space-time.

But the voice that came out of him was not his own.

"Violation," the voice said. "Code: Spatial Inconsistency. Citation: Null."

Gerald's form flickered. He couldn't speak.

The Architect was already inside.

And Gerald realized, with a cold dread that had nothing to do with the System, that he had no defense for this.

He had no code for this.
