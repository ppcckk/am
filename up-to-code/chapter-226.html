Chapter 226: Run into the Dark

# Chapter 226

The cold metal bit into Gerald's wrists.

It wasn't just cold. It was hungry.

The handcuffs pulsed with a faint, rhythmic throb, sucking the residual mana from his skin and replacing it with a static hum that made his teeth ache.

Gerald looked down at his own hands. They were pale, shaking, and entirely human. No blue glow.

No interface.

Just liver spots and the scar from a nail gun in 1994.

The agent, a man whose name tag simply read **AGENT ZERO**, tightened the chain between Gerald's wrists.

"Walk," Zero said.

Gerald didn't move. He stood in the center of his destroyed sub-basement. The concrete slab where he had once anchored his foundation was cracked open, revealing the raw earth beneath. Dust motes danced in the single beam of light cutting through the shattered skylight.

"I'm not walking," Gerald said.

Zero tilted his head.

The motion was mechanical, precise. "You are a suspect. You are being detained.

Refusal to comply constitutes obstruction of a System audit."

"I am not a suspect," Gerald replied.

His voice was dry, devoid of the panic that hammered against his ribs.

"I am a municipal building inspector. I have filed a complaint.

And you are trespassing on a non-public easement."

Zero blinked. "This is a Core zone. It is no longer municipal property."

"Wrong," Gerald said. "Read the fine print of the Core Registration Protocol.

Section 4, Paragraph B.

A Core does not *own* its space. It *leases* it from the System. The underlying substrate—the physical land, the concrete, the plumbing—remains under the jurisdiction of the host municipality.

Ashford Municipal Code, Chapter 12, Subsection 9."

Zero paused. His blank eyes flickered. For a microsecond, something like confusion crossed his face.

"You cannot cite me for trespassing," Gerald continued. "I am standing on my own property. You are standing on it without a permit. I am filing an immediate stop-work order."

The agent's jaw tightened. "Your Core registration is suspended. You have no authority to issue citations."

"I have authority because I am a citizen," Gerald said. He took a step forward.

The handcuffs pulled taut, but he didn't resist the pull.

Instead, he used the tension to brace himself. "And because you are violating the separation of powers between the System and local governance. I want to speak to your supervisor."

"Supervisors do not engage with suspects."

"Then I will speak to the Inspector General directly. I know the procedure for escalation. It requires a written request, stamped with the seal of the last competent authority before suspension. Which was me."

Zero stared at him. The air in the room grew heavier. The dust settled.

"You are stalling," Zero said.

"I am documenting," Gerald corrected. "Look at this floor. Look at that wall. Do you see the load-bearing pillar in the northeast corner?"

Gerald pointed with his chin. His hands were still cuffed, but his eyes were sharp, focused on the structural integrity of the space around them.

Zero looked. He looked at the cracked pillar. He looked at the debris scattered across the floor.

"I see damage," Zero said.

"I see a safety hazard," Gerald said. "If I walk out of here, leaving this structure unstable, and a pedestrian on the street above trips over the rubble I left, who is liable? Me? Or the System agent who removed the Core responsible for maintaining the structural integrity of the site?"

Zero's expression didn't change, but his posture shifted. He looked at the pillar. Then at Gerald.

"The System assumes liability for all Core-related incidents," Zero said.

"Does it?" Gerald asked. "Check the indemnity clause.

Specifically, the one regarding *negligent removal of a compliant entity*.

I was compliant.

My rating was rising. You suspended me based on a false positive.

If I leave, and this building collapses, the lawsuit will be against the IG Office. Not me."

Zero remained silent. The silence stretched, thick and suffocating.

Gerald knew he was gambling. He knew the System didn't care about lawsuits.

But he also knew that Agents were bureaucratic entities.

They feared paperwork more than monsters. They feared liability more than death.

"Let me go," Gerald said. "Or let me file the stop-work order. One of those two things has to happen right now."

Zero looked at his briefcase. He didn't open it. He just stared at the latch.

"You are exploiting a loophole," Zero said.

"I am following code," Gerald said. "That is what you are supposed to want."

Zero took a step back. The handcuffs went slack.

Gerald didn't move. He waited.

"Your suspension is temporary," Zero said.

"Pending review. You remain under observation.

You are not to leave the city limits.

You are not to interact with other Cores. And you are not to issue any more citations."

"Understood," Gerald said.

Zero turned. He walked toward the stairs. His footsteps were heavy, orderly.

Gerald watched him go. He waited until the door creaked shut.

Only then did he look down at his wrists.

The handcuffs were gone.

They had dissolved, leaving behind a faint, bruised ring of black ink on his skin.

He rubbed his wrists. They were sore. He felt weak. He felt human.

He walked to the center of the room.

There was no interface. No menu. No mana pool.

Just him. And the ruin of his home.

A rustle came from the shadows near the broken pillar.

Gerald froze. His hand instinctively reached for a hammer that wasn't there. He clenched his fist.

A small, black rat emerged from the rubble. It twitched its nose. It looked at Gerald.

Gerald looked back.

"Hello," he said.

The rat squeaked. It scurried toward the corner where Gerald had once built a small, perfectly insulated nest for it.

Gerald walked over. His knees popped. His back ached. He sat down on the broken concrete, his legs splayed out.

"Is the nest intact?" he asked.

The rat chattered.

Gerald sighed. He pulled a small notebook from his pocket. It was damp, the pages warped.

He pulled a pen from his other pocket. It was dry.

He wrote.

**Incident Report: Unlawful Detention of a Public Official.** **Date: Today.** **Location: Sub-basement, Ashford Municipal Library.** **Subject: Agent Zero, Inspector General's Office.**

He wrote with steady, precise handwriting.

He noted the time. He noted the location. He noted the exact words used by Agent Zero.

He noted the violation of municipal code.

He noted the potential liability for structural collapse.

He wrote for twenty minutes.

When he was done, he tore out the page. He folded it. He placed it in his pocket.

He looked at the rat.

"We have work to do," Gerald said.

The rat did not understand. It only understood hunger. And cold.

Gerald stood up. He walked to the corner where his workbench had been. It was gone, buried under three tons of concrete.

He dug.

With his bare hands.

He dug for an hour.

His fingernails broke. Blood smeared the concrete dust. He didn't stop.

He couldn't stop. Stopping meant thinking about the fear.

Stopping meant thinking about the cold.

Stopping meant admitting that he was alone.

So he dug.

He found a piece of rebar. Bent, jagged, heavy.

He wrapped it in a strip of cloth torn from his shirt.

He had a tool. It wasn't a hammer. It wasn't a weapon. It was a pry bar.

He looked at the hole he had made. It was deep enough to reach the sub-foundation.

He dropped into it.

The earth was damp. Smelled of mold and old pipes.

He crawled. The space was tight. His shoulders scraped against the dirt. He had to exhale to fit through the narrow gaps.

He crawled for ten minutes.

Then he heard a voice.

It wasn't loud. It was muffled, filtered through layers of soil and stone.

"...can't do this. The rating is dropping. We're losing spawns."

Gerald stopped. He pressed his ear to the wall.

Another voice, deeper, grinding like gravel. "The System says we are compliant. The citation was overturned."

"It was overturned on a technicality!" the first voice shrieked. "The fire exits were blocked by a decorative archway. The archway was *not* load-bearing, but it was *structural*! The hearing officer didn't check the blueprints!"

Gerald's heart hammered against his ribs.

He knew that voice.

It was Core 742. The one he had cited in Chapter 40. The one he had defeated in the Hearing.

Core 742. A mid-tier dungeon. A rival.

Gerald pushed through the remaining dirt. His head broke the surface.

He was in a crawlspace. Above him, a floorboard. Below him, dirt.

Through a crack in the floorboard, he saw light. Green, pulsating light.

And two figures.

One was a humanoid shape, tall and slender, made of woven vines. Core 742's avatar.

The other was a massive, stone-like golem. The enforcer.

They were arguing.

"The Inspector General is watching," the golem rumbled. "If we lose another hearing, we are deleted."

"Then we fix the exit," the vine-core said. "We build a new one. Up to code."

"We don't have the mana," the golem said. "We spent it all on the defense against Hutchins."

"Hutchins is gone," the vine-core said. "His Core is suspended. He is a suspect. He cannot issue citations."

"He is a variable," the golem said. "Variables are dangerous."

Gerald held his breath.

He couldn't issue citations. That was true. The System had locked his menu.

But he wasn't in his Core. He was in *their* Core.

He was physically present in their dungeon.

And he had a pry bar.

And he knew the code.

Gerald looked at the floorboard. It was thin. Rotted.

He looked at the golem. The golem was standing on a patch of dirt that was visibly unstable. The ground was sloping.

Gerald smiled. It was a small, tight smile.

He didn't have a hammer. He didn't have mana.

But he had gravity. And he had leverage.

He pushed the pry bar against the floorboard. He leaned his weight into it.

The wood creaked.

The vine-core looked up. "Did you hear that?"

The golem turned. "Hear what?"

Gerald shoved.

The floorboard snapped.

Gerald fell through the hole, landing on top of the golem.

He didn't attack. He didn't fight.

He slammed the pry bar into the dirt beneath the golem's feet.

Not to hurt the golem.

To destabilize the ground.

The golem roared. It stumbled.

The dirt, already wet, already soft, gave way.

The golem sank.

The vine-core screamed. "What are you doing?!"

Gerald scrambled up from the mud. He was covered in filth. His clothes were torn. His hands were bleeding.

He stood there, panting.

He looked at the vine-core.

"You have a violation," Gerald said.

The vine-core stared at him. "You... you are suspended."

"I am," Gerald said. "I cannot cite you."

He pointed at the sinking golem. The golem was waist-deep in the mud, struggling to pull itself out.

"But you," Gerald said. "You are violating Section 8, Paragraph 3: *Inadequate Foundation Support for Heavy Machinery*.

That golem is a spawning entity. It weighs four hundred pounds. The soil beneath it has a load-bearing capacity of fifty.

You didn't reinforce it. You didn't check the soil reports."

The vine-core's eyes widened.

"I cannot issue a citation," Gerald said. "But I can *report* the violation. To the System. Via manual submission."

He pulled a small, gray tablet from his pocket.

It wasn't a System interface. It was a municipal inspection tablet. Old.

Battery low.

But it worked.

He tapped the screen.

**VIOLATION REPORT.** **Location: [Unknown Coordinates, Detected as Core Zone 742].** **Violation: Structural Instability.** **Severity: Critical.** **Status: Submitted.**

The tablet beeped. A single, loud *ping*.

The vine-core screamed.

A red light flashed in the air.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** **CRITICAL STRUCTURAL FAILURE DETECTED.** **AUTOMATIC CITATION ISSUED.** **SOURCE: MANUAL REPORT VIA OFFICIAL INSPECTOR BADGE #4402.** **PENALTY: 50% MANA DRAIN.**

The golem froze. Its stone skin turned gray. It cracked.

The vine-core fell to its knees. Green light flickered around it, dimming rapidly.

Gerald stood there, covered in mud. He was breathing hard. He was tired. He was scared.

But he was standing.

The vine-core looked at him.

"How," it whispered. "How did you do that?"

Gerald straightened his spine. He wiped his hands on his pants.

"I told you," Gerald said. "I am a building inspector."

The golem crumbled. It fell apart into a pile of rocks.

The vine-core vanished.

Silence returned.

Gerald looked around the empty dungeon.

He had broken into a rival's home. He had destroyed a spawn. He had used a municipal loophole to trigger a System penalty.

He had won.

But the red light was still flashing.

And then, a new message appeared. Not on a tablet. In the air.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** **INSPECTOR GENERAL AUDIT INITIATED.** **TARGET: GERALD HUTCHINS.** **REASON: UNAUTHORIZED ACCESS TO CORE ZONE 742.** **STATUS: PURSUIT.**

Gerald looked at the message.

He looked at the hole he had fallen through.

He looked at the exit.

He runned.

But he didn't run away.

He ran deeper.

Into the dark.

Because he knew the code. And he knew that the only way to survive an audit was to find the one thing they hadn't checked yet.

His own file.

He had to delete it.

And he had to do it before they caught him.
