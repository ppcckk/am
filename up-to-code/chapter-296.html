Chapter 296: Group Violation

# Chapter 296

The stairwell was a deathtrap, and Gerald Hutchins was writing it up.

He stood on the third step from the top, his clipboard held at chest height. The paper was pulpy, the ink of the previous form bleeding into the wood grain of the railing. He did not care about the paper. He cared about the coefficient of friction on the wet stone treads.

"Move," Gerald said.

The adventurer in the lead, a man wearing armor that clanked like a dropped toolbox, shoved past him.

"Out of the way, old man. This is a dungeon. We're here to kill the core."

Gerald did not move. He extended his foot, blocking the man's path with a deliberate, flat pressure.

"Step one. Non-slip coating absent.

Step two.

No handrail continuity. Step three. Lighting deficiency in the egress path.

Do you have a permit to traverse this hazard?

"

The man blinked. He looked at Gerald. He looked at the water rising around their ankles.

"Get out of my way."

"I am citing you for reckless endangerment of a municipal employee," Gerald said. He tapped his pen against the clipboard. "And for failing to wear appropriate footwear for a Class B Hydrostatic Zone."

The adventurer laughed. It was a harsh, barking sound. He raised a mace.

"I'm going to hit you with this."

"If you strike a Code Enforcement Officer, the System will classify it as an Assault on a Regulatory Agent," Gerald said. He did not raise his voice.

He did not raise his clipboard.

"The penalty is immediate dungeon lockdown. Your party will be trapped in a flooded sub-basement with no oxygen circulation. Check your survival stats.

Do you have water breathing?

"

The adventurer froze. His eyes darted to his interface. A red icon blinked above his head. *Oxygen: 14%.*

He swallowed.

"Lower your weapon," Gerald said.

The man lowered the mace, and he stepped back.

The water lapped at Gerald's shins. It was cold.

It smelled of copper and rot.

Behind them, the rest of the party crowded into the narrow stairwell. A mage held a glowing orb, casting long, jagged shadows against the damp walls. A thief picked at the lock on the library door at the top of the stairs. A cleric hovered near the center, her robe soaked to the hem.

Sandra stood at the top of the stairs. She was dry. Her skin remained untouched by the rising flood. She watched the adventurers with the detached interest of a librarian watching a patron try to return a book late.

"Let them in," Gerald said to Sandra.

"They're going to kill us," Sandra said.

"They are going to try," Gerald corrected. "And then they are going to violate Section 4, Paragraph 2 of the Subterranean Safety Ordinance. I have the citation form ready."

The thief at the door grunted. The lock clicked. The door swung open, revealing the dry, carpeted hallway of the Ashford Municipal Library. The adventurers surged forward, eager to escape the rising water.

They did not escape.

The moment the first man crossed the threshold, a red barrier snapped into existence across the doorway. It hummed with a low, electric buzz.

"What the hell?" the mage shouted.

Gerald stepped out of the water. He climbed the last two steps and stood on the dry library carpet.

He wiped his wet shoes on the edge of the rug.

He did not look at the trapped adventurers. He looked at the doorway.

"Improper egress," Gerald said.

The barrier pulsed. The adventurers banged against it. It was solid.

"You can't leave," Sandra said, walking to stand beside Gerald. "Not without filing your exit report."

"Exit report?" the mage screamed. "We're trying to get out of a flood!"

"You are trying to leave a Class 4 Hazard Zone without completing the mandatory decontamination procedure," Gerald said.

He held up his clipboard.

"The System flagged the transition point as a cross-contamination risk.

You are carrying sewage pathogens into a public reading room.

That is a biohazard violation.

"

The thief kicked the barrier.

"Open it! I have keys! I have high-level lockpicking skills!"

Gerald looked at the thief. He looked at the lockpick in the thief's hand. It was made of steel. It was covered in slime from the stairwell.

"Contaminated tools," Gerald said. He wrote on his clipboard.

The ink, fresh and black, appeared instantly on the soaked paper.

The System's magic, or perhaps just the sheer weight of bureaucratic inevitability, dried the page as he wrote.

"You are cited for introducing hazardous materials into a public space. The fine is five thousand mana points."

"I don't have five thousand mana!" the mage yelled.

"Then you are insolvent," Gerald said. "And insolvent parties are subject to immediate asset seizure."

The barrier glowed brighter. The adventurers' armor glowed red. Their inventory slots flashed.

"Stop," the leader said. He raised his hands. "We surrender.

We're not looting. We're just... passing through.

"

Gerald paused. He looked at Sandra.

Sandra shrugged.

"He's lying. Look at their eyes. They're looking for the Core Room.

It's behind the reference desk.

"

"I am not looking for the Core Room," the leader said. His voice was tight.

Gerald looked at the leader. He looked at the leader's boots.

They were standard-issue adventurer boots.

Leather. No tread pattern.

"Your footwear is non-compliant," Gerald said. "And your intent to traverse non-public areas constitutes trespassing."

The leader's face twisted.

"We'll pay the fine. We'll clean the floor. Just let us out."

Gerald considered this. He looked at the flooded dungeon below.

The water was rising.

If he let them go, the water would spread into the library.

The books would be ruined. The library was a municipal asset.

He could not allow the library to be ruined.

But he could not let them leave without cleaning the water.

"You will stay," Gerald said. "You will dry this area. You will use your magic, your cloth, your hands.

You will remove all standing water from the egress path. Once the moisture level is below fifteen percent, the barrier will dissolve.

"

The mage looked horrified.

"My fire spells will evaporate the water, but the steam will trigger the smoke detectors. And the smoke detectors will trigger the ventilation shutdown."

Gerald smiled. It was a small, tight smile.

"Precisely," Gerald said. "Which is why you will use the towels from the bathroom. The bathroom is to your left. The sign is visible."

The thief groaned. He turned to look at the bathroom. The door was closed.

"I'm opening it," the thief said.

He pushed the door open. The bathroom was pristine. The tile was white. The sink was polished.

But the mirror was gone.

The thief froze. He stepped into the bathroom. He looked at the sink. He looked at the empty space on the wall where the mirror should have been.

"Where's the mirror?" the thief asked.

Sandra's expression changed. The dry smile vanished. Her eyes narrowed.

"Gerald," she said. Her voice was low. "Why is the mirror gone?"

Gerald turned. He looked at the bathroom. He looked at the empty wall.

"I did not remove the mirror," Gerald said. "The mirror was installed during the initial construction phase. It is a fixed fixture."

"Then where is it?" Sandra asked.

The leader of the adventurers stepped into the bathroom. He looked at the wall. He ran his hand over the plaster.

"It's not a mirror," the leader said. "It's a portal frame. The glass was removed to allow transit."

"Transit?" Gerald asked.

The leader pointed to the floor of the bathroom.

There were scorch marks. They were not from fire. They were from teleportation magic.

High-level, unstable teleportation.

"We didn't come through the door," the leader said. He looked at Gerald with genuine fear.

"We came through here.

Someone opened a portal inside your bathroom. That's why the water is leaking. That's why the pressure is building.

The portal is dumping water from somewhere else into your dungeon.

"

Gerald looked at the scorch marks. He looked at the empty wall.

He looked at Sandra.

Sandra was staring at the portal frame. Her face was pale.

"That's not a portal," she whispered. "That's an extraction point. They're not looting you, Gerald. They're harvesting you."

"Harvesting?" Gerald asked.

"Sewage extraction," Sandra said. "The System allows for resource harvesting from dungeon fluids if the dungeon is deemed 'non-compliant with water retention statutes.' If the water goes down there, it goes into a private reservoir. It's not in your dungeon anymore. It's in someone else's."

The adventurer leader nodded.

"We were hired to guard the extraction.

But the flow changed. It's coming up.

It's backing up into your stairs.

"

Gerald looked at his clipboard. He looked at the citation for improper footwear. He looked at the citation for reckless endangerment.

He looked at the empty bathroom wall, and he realized he had been citing the wrong violation.

He had been citing them for trespassing. He should have been citing them for unauthorized utility connections.

He pulled a new form from his pocket. It was blank. The ink flowed onto it as he thought.

"Form 114-C," Gerald said. "Unauthorized Utility Tap."

The mage frowned.

"What does that do?"

"It allows me to shut off the flow," Gerald said.

"You can't shut off a portal," the leader said. "It's a System anchor. It's tied to the Inspector General's audit trail."

Gerald stopped writing. He looked at the leader.

"The Inspector General," Gerald said.

"Yes," the leader said. "They're auditing the water usage.

They're going to see the drop in your compliance rating because your water is being stolen, not because you're failing to manage it.

You're being set up to fail. Your Compliance Rating is dropping because the water isn't there. And when it drops below 30, the System revokes your Core License.

You die.

The dungeon collapses. And the water flows into their reservoir.

"

Sandra grabbed Gerald's arm. Her grip was tight.

"He's right," Sandra said. "If you try to close the portal, you're interfering with an active audit. The Inspector General will view it as obstruction of justice. He'll cite *you* for tampering with evidence."

Gerald looked at the bathroom. He looked at the scorch marks.

He looked at the water rising in the stairwell, and he looked at the adventurers.

"If I close it," Gerald said, "I lose my license."

"Yes," Sandra said.

"If I let it open," Gerald said, "I lose my water. And my license eventually."

"Yes," Sandra said.

Gerald looked at the leader.

"Who hired you?" Gerald asked.

The leader looked away. He looked at the other adventurers. They were all looking at their feet.

"I don't know," the leader said. "Anonymous contract. Through the Guild."

"Anonymous contracts are illegal," Gerald said. "Under the Municipal Labor Code, Section 9."

"It's not municipal labor," the leader said. "It's dungeon commerce."

"Dungeon commerce is regulated by the System," Gerald said. "And the System requires transparency in all utility transactions."

Gerald looked at the portal frame. He looked at the empty space.

He had a loophole, and the portal was dumping water. The water was outside his dungeon's perimeter. It was technically not his water. It was external runoff.

If the water was not his, he could not be held responsible for managing it. But he could be held responsible for *preventing* it from entering his public egress path.

He could block the entrance.

But the entrance was in the bathroom.

And the bathroom was a public facility.

"Gerald," Sandra said. "Don't you dare."

Gerald looked at her.

"I am installing a temporary barrier," Gerald said.

"That's tampering with an audit site," Sandra said.

"I am installing a temporary barrier to prevent cross-contamination of a public reading room," Gerald said. "It is a safety measure, not a utility interference. I am not closing the portal. I am blocking the *path*."

Sandra's eyes widened.

"That's... technically legal," she said. "But it's going to cause a pressure backup. The water has nowhere to go."

"Then it will go back up the portal," Gerald said. "Into whoever opened it."

The leader took a step back.

"You'll flood their base."

"I will flood their lobby," Gerald said. "And they will have to file a cleanup report. And they will have to pay for the cleanup. And they will have to explain why they have a portal in the library bathroom."

The leader's face went white.

"They'll kill you," the leader said.

"They will have to wait in line," Gerald said. "Behind the other Cores I've cited."

Gerald stepped into the bathroom. He picked up a towel from the rack. It was white. It was clean.

He folded it. He placed it on the floor, directly in front of the scorch marks.

The System pinged.

**WARNING: Obstruction of Utility Flow detected.**

**Penalty: Compliance Rating -5.**

Gerald accepted the penalty. He did not flinch.

Then he picked up a second towel. He placed it over the first.

**WARNING: Obstruction intensified.**

**Penalty: Compliance Rating -10.**

His Compliance Rating dropped from 47 to 32.

The water in the stairwell churned. The pressure built.

"Gerald, stop!" Sandra shouted. "You're at the threshold!"

Gerald looked at his clipboard. He looked at the rating.

32, and he needed one more violation. He needed to drop below 30 to trigger the Emergency Shutdown Protocol, which would allow him to legally seal the area for

"imminent structural failure."

He looked at the leader.

"You are wearing wool," Gerald said.

The leader blinked.

"What?"

"Wool socks," Gerald said. "In a wet zone. That is a slip hazard. And it is unsanitary for a public facility."

Gerald wrote.

**Citation Issued: Improper Attire in Wet Zone.**

**Penalty: Compliance Rating -3.**

The rating dropped to 29.

The System flashed.

**EMERGENCY PROTOCOL ACTIVATED.**

**Structural Integrity Compromised due to Hydrostatic Pressure.**

**Egress Path Sealed.**

**Authority Granted: Core License Suspension for Remediation.**

The bathroom door slammed shut. The barrier around the library door vanished.

The adventurers were trapped in the library hallway.

The water in the stairwell stopped rising. The pressure reversed. The water sucked back down the drain, back through the floor, back toward the portal.

Gerald stood in the bathroom. The towels were wet. The scorch marks were gone, covered by the water receding.

He looked at Sandra.

Sandra was staring at the closed bathroom door.

"You just declared your dungeon a condemned structure," she said.

"I did," Gerald said.

"Which means no one can enter," Sandra said.

"Correct."

"Which means no one can attack you," Sandra said.

"Correct."

"Which means the Inspector General cannot audit you until the repairs are made," Sandra said.

"Correct."

Sandra looked at him. For the first time, she looked impressed.

"You just bought us time," she said.

"I just filed a variance request," Gerald said.

The water drained away. The stairwell was dry.

The bathroom door creaked, which did not open.

It rattled.

Something was on the other side.

Gerald looked at the door.

He looked at Sandra.

"Who is knocking?" Gerald asked.

Sandra listened, and the knocking was rhythmic. Three short. Three long. Three short.

It was a signal.

"It's not an adventurer," Sandra said.

Gerald looked at the door. He looked at his clipboard. He had no form for a visitor who was not a citizen, not a hero, and not a monster.

He had no form for this.

The door handle turned, which was locked.

The handle turned again. Harder.

The wood splintered.

Gerald stepped back. He raised his clipboard.

Sandra raised her hands.

The door burst open, and a man stood there. He was not an adventurer. He was not a monster.

He was wearing a suit. A grey suit. A white shirt. A red tie.

He held a briefcase, and he looked at Gerald. He looked at Sandra. He looked at the wet towels.

He smiled. It was a perfect, symmetrical smile.

"Mr. Hutchins," the man said. "I am from the Internal Revenue Service of the Dungeon System."

Gerald's stomach dropped.

"I didn't file my taxes," Gerald said.

"You did not," the man said. "And you have been claiming deductions for 'structural improvements' that were actually 'defensive fortifications.' That is tax evasion, Mr. Hutchins. And it is a Class A felony under the Core Code."

The man opened his briefcase.

Inside, there was no paper. There was a single, black, iron key.

"You are being audited," the man said. "Right now. Immediately. And you are under arrest."

Gerald looked at the key, and he looked at the man.

He looked at Sandra.

Sandra was shaking her head.

"He's an Auditor," she whispered. "He can rewrite your Core Code. He can delete you."

The Auditor stepped into the bathroom. He stepped over the wet towels.

"Please step out of the designated remediation zone," the Auditor said. "Your license is suspended. You are now a civilian entity. And civilians do not have the right to obstruct federal officials."

Gerald stepped forward. He held out his clipboard.

"I have a form," Gerald said.

The Auditor paused. He looked at the clipboard.

"For what?"

For the violation of entry procedures, Gerald thought.

For the violation of privacy.

For the violation of the Fourth Amendment.

The System didn't have the Fourth Amendment.

But the System had rules.

And Gerald Hutchins knew them all, and he wrote.

The Auditor watched him. He did not stop him.

Gerald handed the clipboard to the Auditor.

The Auditor looked down, and he read the citation.

His smile faded.

The Auditor looked up. His eyes were cold.

"You cited me," the Auditor said.

"I cited you for entering a remediation zone without a warrant," Gerald said.

"The audit *is* the warrant," the Auditor said.

"Not if the zone is sealed," Gerald said. "The seal was active. The entry was unauthorized. The evidence obtained is inadmissible."

The Auditor looked at the wet towels. He looked at the closed door. He looked at Gerald.

He looked at Sandra.

Sandra stepped forward. She stood between Gerald and the Auditor.

"He's right," Sandra said. "The seal was active. The entry was illegal. If you proceed, you are violating the Chain of Custody protocols."

The Auditor stiffened, and he looked at Gerald.

He looked at the clipboard.

He took the clipboard.

He looked at the citation.

He looked at Gerald.

"This is not over," the Auditor said.

"It never is," Gerald said.

The Auditor turned. He walked out of the bathroom. He closed the door.

Gerald heard his footsteps walking away. Down the library hallway.

Sandra let out a breath, and she leaned against the sink.

"You just cited an Auditor," she said.

"I cited an intrusion," Gerald said.

"An Auditor can revoke your license with a thought," Sandra said.

"Not if the revocation is based on inadmissible evidence," Gerald said.

Sandra looked at him. She looked at the empty mirror wall.

She looked at the wet towels.

"Gerald," she said. "You didn't just buy us time."

Gerald looked at the door.

"No," Gerald said. "I bought us an enemy who owes me a correction."

Sandra frowned.

"A correction?"

"If he appeals the citation," Gerald said. "He has to prove the seal was inactive. To prove that, he has to show the pressure levels."

"And?"

"And if he shows the pressure levels, he shows the portal output," Gerald said. "He shows the external source."

Sandra's eyes widened.

"He'll show who opened the portal," Sandra said.

"He'll show who stole my water," Gerald said.

"And that person..." Sandra trailed off.

"That person will be identified," Gerald said. "And I will cite them for theft of municipal resources."

Sandra smiled. It was a real smile this time.

"You're going to shut down the Auditor," she said.

"I'm going to shut down his client," Gerald said.

The bathroom door creaked again.

Gerald looked at the door, and he looked at Sandra.

"Who else is out there?" Gerald asked.

Sandra listened.

She heard footsteps. Many footsteps.

They were not walking away, and they were coming back.

"More Auditors," Sandra said.

Gerald picked up his clipboard.

He picked up a pen.

He looked at the door, and he looked at Sandra.

"Form 115-A," Gerald said. "Group Violation."

He wrote.
