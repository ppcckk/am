Chapter 59: Audited by the Auditor

# Chapter 59

The corridor was wrong. Gerald could feel it in the way the walls met the ceiling at an angle that should not have been possible under any load-bearing configuration he had ever inspected, but he did not have time to pull out his measuring tape because the Hearing was already in progress and Core 2847 was arguing that its east wall was a retaining structure than a partition, which would reclassify the entire violation category from "improper egress" to "structural support" and make his Citation void.

Gerald stared at the corridor. The walls were smooth, untextured, the kind of surface that formed when a Core generated space without specifying material composition.

The ceiling sloped at four degrees, and the floor was level. The junction between wall and ceiling was a clean ninety-degree corner that should have required a header beam, and there was no header beam, which meant either the wall was not load-bearing, which meant Gerald's Citation was valid, or the wall was load-bearing and the ceiling was transferring lateral load through a mechanism Gerald could not see because it was not rendered in the Hearing's spatial visualization.

"You are arguing," Gerald said, "that the ceiling is a load-transfer surface."

"I am arguing," Core 2847 replied, "that the corridor is a structural element."

"That is not the same thing."

"The distinction is irrelevant to the Citation."

Gerald looked at the corridor again, and he looked at the notification bar at the top of his vision, which read: HEARING IN PROGRESS. CASE 59-C.

JURISDICTIONAL CHALLENGE.

GERALD HUTCHINS v. CORE 2847. TIME REMAINING: 00:04:32.

Four minutes. He had four minutes to prove that a corridor he had never physically walked was not load-bearing, or his Citation would be reversed, his Oversight Liability would trigger, and his Compliance Rating would drop by whatever margin the System deemed appropriate for a missed violation.

He had cited Core 2847 for three violations. Improper egress width.

Missing fire-rated door assembly.

Inadequate ventilation in a subterranean corridor.

The Jurisdictional Challenge argued that Core 2847 had been registered under Grandfathered Compliance status, which exempted it from code categories introduced after its registration date. Gerald had accepted the challenge because the System required it.

Now he had to prove the exemption did not apply.

"The corridor was added after your registration date," Gerald said. "I can see the seam at the junction with the original structure.

The material density changes at the four-meter mark.

You expanded eastward. Grandfathered status does not cover post-registration modifications."

The visualization shifted.

A seam appeared in the wall, subtle but visible. Core 2847's response came as a text block: "Expansion does not constitute modification of existing structural elements.

The original corridor remains intact.

The new section is additive."

Gerald almost conceded. In municipal practice, he had seen this argument before.

A homeowner adds a room to the back of a house, and the original structure is grandfathered.

The addition must meet current code, but the original does not. If Gerald applied that logic here, the new corridor would need to meet current code, but the Citation would only apply to the new section.

And the Citation had been written against the entire dungeon.

He had written the Citation too broadly.

Time remaining: 00:03:11.

Gerald pulled up the original Citation document. He had written it in the standard format.

Violation type.

Location. Severity. He had listed the egress width violation as applying to "the primary corridor system." The primary corridor system included the original corridor and the expansion.

If only the expansion was subject to current code, then the Citation was valid for the expansion but invalid for the original.

Partial validity. The Hearing would award a reduced debuff than a full reversal, but it would also count as a partial win, which meant Gerald's Oversight Liability would still trigger because the System would flag the Citation as "overbroad."

He needed to narrow the Citation. He needed to do it in three minutes.

Gerald typed a revision into the Hearing interface. He changed the location field from "primary corridor system" "eastward expansion corridor, meters 4 through 12." He changed the violation type from "improper egress width, primary circulation" to "improper egress width, secondary circulation." The severity dropped from Class B to Class C. The debuff would be smaller. But the Citation would be valid.

Time remaining: 00:01:47, and he submitted the revision. The Hearing interface processed it. A new line appeared: CITATION REVISED. AWAITING OPPONENT RESPONSE.

Core 2847 did not respond. The time ran out.

The result appeared: CITATION UPHELD.

PARTIAL. DEBUFF APPLIED. CORE 2847: -12% REGENERATION, -8% STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY.

GERALD HUTCHINS: NO OVERSIGHT LIABILITY.

Gerald exhaled.

His hands were shaking, and he had not expected his hands to shake; he had written citations for thirty-one years and his hands had never shaken.

But he had never had to revise a citation in real time while a Core argued back at him.

He was about to close the Hearing window when a new notification appeared.

CORE 2847: COUNTER-CITATION FILED. HEARING WINDOW OPEN. CASE 59-D. TIME REMAINING: 00:06:00.

Gerald stared at it. Counter-Citations existed.

He had read about them in the System's reference documentation during his first cycle, when he had been trying to understand what a dungeon core was.

He had read the section on Appeals. He had read the section on Hearings. He had not read the section on Counter-Citations because it had been buried under a subheading called "Adversarial Compliance Procedures" and he had assumed it was theoretical.

It was not theoretical, and he opened the Counter-Citation. Core 2847 was citing him for a violation type he had never encountered.

REGULATORY PROXIMITY CONFLICT.

The violation description read: "Core 4401's dungeon footprint is located within 180 meters of a Core operating under a different compliance framework. This proximity creates a Regulatory Conflict Zone in which neither Core's code applies with full force, rendering both structures non-compliant by default."

Gerald read it twice. He had never heard of this violation type. He had never heard of a Regulatory Conflict Zone. He had been auditing for a cycle and a half and he had never seen this term in any System document.

He looked at the distance. 180 meters.

His dungeon was 4,000 square feet, roughly 60 by 70 feet. Core 2847's dungeon, according to the Hearing visualization, was roughly 12,000 square feet.

If they were in the same general area, 180 meters was plausible.

He had never mapped his surroundings beyond his own footprint. He had assumed the neighboring dungeons were far away because they had never physically interacted with his space.

They were not far away.

Gerald opened the Hearing interface. The visualization rendered both dungeons in overlapping space.

His was a rectangle.

Core 2847's was an irregular shape that wrapped around his on three sides.

The distance between their nearest edges was 174 meters. Inside that 174-meter gap, the visualization showed a hatched region.

The Regulatory Conflict Zone.

Time remaining: 00:04:52.

Gerald's mind went to municipal code. Not the System's code.

The actual code, and the one he had enforced for thirty-one years in the City of Ashford. There was a provision in the Ashford Municipal Code, Section 14.22.040, that dealt with adjacent properties under different zoning classifications. It was called a "Nonconforming Use Conflict." If two properties were adjacent and one was zoned residential and the other was zoned commercial, the boundary created a buffer requirement.

If the buffer was not maintained, both properties were cited.

The System's Regulatory Conflict Zone was the same mechanism. Different compliance frameworks.

Adjacent space.

Buffer requirement. No buffer. Both cited.

Gerald typed: "I acknowledge the proximity. I dispute the violation on the grounds that Core 2847's compliance framework is invalid."

The Hearing interface processed this. A new line appeared: DISPUTE NOTED. CORE 2847 RESPONSE REQUIRED.

Core 2847 responded immediately. "My compliance framework was established at registration. It predates your framework. It is valid by default."

Gerald typed: "Your framework was grandfathered. Grandfathered status does not confer validity in a Regulatory Conflict Zone.

It confers exemption from new requirements, which does not create a new framework.

A grandfathered structure is not operating under a different framework, and it is operating under the original framework, which the System has since superseded.

The Conflict Zone requires two active frameworks. You have one. I have one.

The Conflict is void."

The Hearing paused. The System was processing the argument. Gerald watched the progress bar fill.

Time remaining: 00:02:18.

The result appeared: DISPUTE ACCEPTED. COUNTER-CITATION VOID. NO DEBUFF APPLIED TO CORE 4401.

Gerald sat back.

His hands were still shaking. He looked at his Compliance Rating. Seventy-eight.

Unchanged, and he had survived.

Then a new notification appeared.

CORE 2847: APPEAL FILED. BASIS: REGISTRATION DATE PRECEDES SYSTEM DEPLOYMENT. REQUEST: FULL DOCUMENTARY REVIEW. HEARING WINDOW: IMMEDIATE.

Gerald read the appeal basis three times.

Registration date precedes System deployment, which meant Core 2847 had been registered before the System existed. Before the Compliance framework. Before the audit queue. Before any of it.

If that was true, then Core 2847 was not grandfathered; it was not operating under any framework the System had created. It was operating under a framework that predated the System.

And if the System's code did not apply to it, then Gerald had no authority to audit it.

And if Gerald had no authority to audit it, then every Citation he had filed against it was void.

And if every Citation was void, then the debuff he had just applied was illegal.

And if the debuff was illegal, then Gerald was in violation of the System's own rules.

The Hearing window opened.

The visualization reappeared. Core 2847's dungeon expanded in the rendering, pushing back the edges of the map, revealing a footprint that was not 12,000 square feet but 120,000. It extended far beyond the boundary Gerald had been operating within.

It had been there the whole time. Beneath the library.

Beneath the city.

Beneath everything.

A text block appeared. Core 2847's voice, for the first time, was not clipped. It was long.

"You are auditing me," it said, "because the System told you to. You are auditing me because your Compliance Rating requires it.

You are auditing me because you have spent your entire life filing forms and you have found a form that lets you file more forms. But I was here before the System. I was here before the library.

I was here before the city. I do not know what a Citation is.

I do not know what a Hearing is.

I do not know what a Compliance Rating is. I have been building for eleven thousand years and I have never once been asked whether my corridors meet a code that did not exist when I built them."

Gerald stared at the text.

"I am not arguing that your Citation is invalid," Core 2847 continued.

"I am arguing that your Authority is invalid. You cannot cite me because you cannot cite anything that predates the framework you cite under.

This is not a legal argument, which is a logical one. A building inspector cannot inspect a building that was constructed before the office of building inspector existed. You know this.

You have always known this. It is the reason you have never cited the cathedral on Fifth Street.

You have never cited the water tower.

You have never cited the bridge over the Ashford River, and they predate your authority. I predate the System's authority.

You cannot audit me."

Gerald looked at the visualization. Core 2847's footprint was vast.

It was not a dungeon, which was a geology. It was a foundation. The System had been built on top of it.

The entire audit framework, every Core in the queue, every Citation, every Hearing, every Compliance Rating, all of it existed inside a space that had been there first.

Time remaining: 00:00:31.

Gerald had thirty-one seconds to respond to an argument he could not defeat.

He looked at the clipboard. He looked at the forty-three target list. He looked at Core 2847's registration date, which the System had just rendered in the corner of the visualization.

It was older than the System.

Gerald typed one line.

"I withdraw all Citations against Core 2847."

The Hearing closed. The notification cleared. Gerald sat in his dungeon, in his 4,000 square feet, in his 174 meters of empty space, and he understood that he had been citing buildings that were standing on a foundation he could not inspect, and that the foundation had just told him so, and that the next notification on his clipboard was a summons.

CORE 4401: COMPLIANCE RATING REVISED. NEW RATING: 61/100.

CORE 4401: YOU HAVE BEEN FLAGGED FOR AUDIT.

CORE 4401: AUDITOR ASSIGNED: CORE 2847.
