Chapter 6

The vote counter sat at five.

Gerald read the notification again. Multilateral Compliance Review. Quorum requirement: six of ten. Five Cores had voted yes. Five had not. The window for voting was open for another ninety seconds.

He scanned the list of non-voting Cores. Core-0004. Core-0006. Core-0008. Core-0009. Core-0010.

Core-0004 was two kilometers northeast. Core-0006 was six hundred meters south. Core-0008 was on the far side of the river. Core-0009 and Core-0010 were both beyond the System's interaction radius for his current tier.

That left Core-0006. Six hundred meters. The closest non-voting Core to his dungeon.

Gerald pulled up the citation rules. He knew them. He had read them forty times in the first hour after the System Apocalypse, when he'd still been standing in the parking lot of the Ashford Municipal Building with a clipboard in his hand and a dungeon core growing out of his sternum.

Citations require physical presence for service. The issuer must be at the target's location. The target must be present at its own location. Both conditions must be met simultaneously.

Gerald was a Core. He could not leave his dungeon. He had tried in the first day, when the panic was still fresh and stupid, and the System had responded with a single notification: CORE LOCATION LOCKED. CORE IS LOCATION.

But his entities were extensions of his dungeon. The rats. The slime. They were his. They were part of his footprint. If he sent the slime six hundred meters south and it touched the boundary of Core-0006's territory, was Gerald physically present?

The System didn't say.

Ninety seconds.

Gerald looked at the slime. It pulsed in the corner of his corridor, a translucent lump of acidic gel the size of a basketball. It had no legs. It moved by flowing. It was slow. It was pathetic.

He looked at the distance marker on his dungeon map. Six hundred meters to the nearest edge of Core-0006's claimed territory. The slime's base speed was four meters per minute.

He did the math. One hundred and fifty minutes.

He did not have one hundred and fifty minutes.

The notification blinked. Eighty seconds remaining.

Gerald thought about the problem the way he thought about all problems: as a matter of code. The citation rules required physical presence for service. The word was "presence," not "proximity." Presence meant being there. Being at the location.

His dungeon was his body. His entities were his body. His body was wherever his entities were. That was the logic. He needed the System to accept that logic.

He opened the citation interface. It required three fields: TARGET ID, VIOLATION CATEGORY, EVIDENCE. He could fill all three without being present. The System would validate presence at the moment of service. If he filled it out now and the slime wasn't there yet, the service would fail.

But what if he filled it out and let the System queue it? What if the System checked presence at the moment of delivery rather than the moment of issuance?

He had never tested this. He didn't know if the System allowed queued citations. In municipal government, you couldn't queue a citation. You had to serve it in person. You had to hand the document to the person. If you weren't there, the citation didn't exist.

But the System wasn't municipal government. The System was a set of rules written by someone who had never been a building inspector. Gerald was betting on that.

Sixty seconds.

He filled out the citation. Target: Core-0006. Violation category: Structural. Evidence: pending.

The System flagged the evidence field as incomplete. It wouldn't let him submit without evidence.

Gerald stared at the blinking cursor. He needed evidence. He needed to see Core-0006's dungeon. He had no way to see it. He had no detection ability. He was a dungeon core with a Compliance Rating of twelve and a rat problem.

Thirty seconds.

Then he remembered something. When he'd first become a Core, the System had shown him a map. Not a detailed map. A topological one. It showed dungeon territories as colored regions. Core-0006's territory was a blob of amber six hundred meters south. The map showed borders. Borders were lines. Lines were walls. Walls had specifications.

Gerald pulled up the topological map. He looked at the border between his territory and Core-0006's. They didn't touch. There was a gap of about two hundred meters of unclaimed land between them.

But the map showed the border as a line. And lines, in the System's logic, were boundaries. Boundaries were walls. Walls were structures.

Structures had to meet code.

Gerald looked at the border line where it intersected with a topographic feature. The map showed a depression. A low point. The border line ran through it. If Core-0006's territory was defined by a border that passed through a drainage depression, then the border itself was a structure that needed to be graded for water management.

Gerald checked the elevation data the System had provided in his initial dungeon survey. The depression was a three-meter drop over a forty-meter span. A border wall running through that drop would need a retaining structure. A foundation. Proper drainage.

He had no way to confirm whether Core-0006 had installed one. But he knew the code. The code said that any boundary structure crossing a grade change of more than two meters required a retaining wall with a minimum height of one and a half times the grade change. That was four and a half meters of concrete or equivalent.

Core-0006 was a dungeon. Dungeons didn't build retaining walls at their borders. Dungeons built traps and monster pits. They didn't care about drainage.

Gerald had his evidence. Not direct observation. Inference. But the System's evidence field accepted "structural inference from topological data." He'd seen it in the citation rules. It was a low-confidence evidence type, but it was valid.

Fifteen seconds.

He typed the evidence: "Boundary structure at coordinates 47.2, -3.1 crosses grade change of 3.2m. No retaining structure indicated in topological survey. Violation of Structural Code Section 4.7.1."

The System accepted it.

The citation was ready. It needed service. It needed Gerald to be physically present at Core-0006's location.

Gerald sent the slime.

He pushed it with his will, the way he pushed all his entities, and it flowed south. Four meters per minute. It would take one hundred and fifty minutes.

The vote window closed in ten seconds.

Gerald watched the counter. Five yes. Zero no. Five abstentions.

The window closed. The vote had failed. Quorum not met.

Gerald exhaled. His chest felt like it was filling with cold water. He sat on the floor of his corridor. A rat climbed over his boot.

The citation was queued. The System had accepted it and placed it in a pending service queue. It would attempt delivery when the slime reached Core-0006's border.

Gerald had bought himself time. The vote had failed. But the audit was still coming. Seventy-two hours. Maybe less now, since the Multilateral Review had failed and the Inspector General might accelerate.

He checked the clock. Sixty-eight hours remaining.

He needed mana. He needed structures. He needed to raise his Compliance Rating from twelve to something that wouldn't trigger a Compliance Lock.

He opened his build menu. It showed him the options available at his current tier. He could build a wall. A floor. A ceiling. A door. That was it. Four options. None of them were code-compliant structures. A wall without a foundation was a violation. A floor without vapor barrier was a violation. A door without proper egress clearance was a violation.

Everything he could build would lower his Compliance Rating.

Gerald sat with that for a moment. Then he opened the citation interface again and looked at the queued citation. Core-0006. Structural violation. If Core-0006 appealed, there would be a hearing. The hearing required both Cores to argue structural logic. Core-0006 would have to present its layout to demonstrate compliance.

Gerald would see Core-0006's layout.

He would see how a functioning dungeon was built. He would see what structures were possible at higher tiers. He would see what code-compliant structures looked like in practice.

He queued a second citation. Same target. Same violation type. He varied the evidence slightly. Two citations against the same target for the same violation category created a stacked penalty. If Core-0006 appealed one, it had to appeal both. The hearing would take longer. The layout disclosure would be more detailed.

Gerald was building himself a blueprint through adversarial discovery.

He felt something cold in his stomach. Not fear. Recognition. This was what he used to do. Not with dungeons. With contractors. With developers who built things wrong and then appealed his citations to stall the process. Gerald would let them appeal. He would drag out the hearing. And in the hearing, they would show him their plans, and he would find more violations in those plans than he'd ever have found on site.

He had done this forty-three times in his career. It was the only part of the job he was good at. Not the inspection. The interrogation.

The slime flowed south. Gerald watched it go. He calculated when it would arrive. One hundred and forty-three minutes. Core-0006 would get the citation then. The appeal window would open. The hearing would begin.

Gerald had approximately one hundred and forty-three minutes to figure out what to build.

He opened his build menu again. Wall. Floor. Ceiling. Door. Four options. All violations.

Unless.

He looked at the door option. Egress clearance. A door needed to be wide enough for a person to pass through. But Gerald's dungeon had no people. It had rats and a slime. What was the egress clearance for a slime?

The System didn't specify. The code said "minimum 0.8m clear width for habitable spaces." But a dungeon wasn't a habitable space. A dungeon was a "hostile environment structure." The code for hostile environment structures was different.

Gerald searched his System interface. He searched for "hostile environment." Nothing. He searched for "dungeon." Nothing. He searched for "non-habitable structure."

There.

NON-HABITABLE STRUCTURE: egress clearance not required. Minimum door width: 0.3m. Minimum door height: 0.5m.

Gerald built a door. Thirty centimeters wide. Fifty centimeters tall. It appeared at the end of his corridor. A small, ugly rectangle of stone.

His Compliance Rating ticked up. Twelve to thirteen.

Not enough. Not close. But it was a start. He had found a category the System hadn't fully defined. Non-habitable structures. His dungeon was non-habitable. He didn't need to meet residential code. He needed to meet the code for hostile environments.

He built another door. Another. He built floors at the minimum thickness for non-habitable use. He built walls at the minimum height. He built a ceiling at the lowest allowable span.

Each one raised his Compliance Rating by one point. The math was slow. Each structure cost mana. Each structure gave him one point. He had twelve points and roughly forty mana. He could build about thirty structures. That would bring him to forty-two.

The audit threshold for Compliance Lock was fifty.

He was eight short.

Gerald looked at the slime. It was still flowing south. Still one hundred and twenty minutes from Core-0006's border.

Then a notification appeared.

CITATION SERVED. TARGET: CORE-0006. STATUS: ACTIVE. APPEAL WINDOW: OPEN.

Gerald blinked. The slime hadn't arrived yet. It had been moving for maybe ten minutes. It was barely forty meters from his dungeon.

The citation had been served without physical presence.

Gerald stared at the notification. He had been wrong about the rules. The System didn't require physical presence for service. Or rather, it required physical presence, but it defined "physical presence" differently than he expected. His dungeon's influence extended beyond its physical borders. The border line on the map was a structure. Gerald owned that structure. The citation was served at the border. The border was within Gerald's territory. Gerald was present at his own border.

He had served the citation from his own dungeon. The System had accepted it.

Gerald felt the cold feeling again. Not fear this time. Something worse. Something like the feeling of realizing you've been reading the code wrong for twenty-three years.

The appeal window was open. Core-0006 had twenty-four hours to appeal. If it appealed, the hearing would begin. Gerald would see Core-0006's layout. He would have his blueprint.

But if Core-0006 didn't appeal, the citation would stand. Core-0006 would be debuffed. And Gerald would have no blueprint.

Gerald opened the citation details. He looked at the violation. Structural. Boundary grade. He had inferred the violation from topological data. He had never seen Core-0006's actual dungeon.

He had cited a structure he hadn't inspected.

In twenty-three years, Gerald had never done that. Not once. Every citation he'd ever written was based on direct observation. He had walked every site. He had measured every wall. He had never cited something he hadn't seen.

And it had worked. The System had accepted it. The citation was active. Core-0006 was debuffed.

Gerald sat on the floor of his corridor and felt something he couldn't name. It wasn't satisfaction. It wasn't guilt. It was the sensation of a rule he had lived by turning out to be a rule that only he had followed.

A new notification appeared.

CORE-0006 HAS FILED AN APPEAL. HEARING SCHEDULED. TIMEFRAME: 6 HOURS. SCOPE: STRUCTURAL LOGIC ARGUMENT. PARTICIPANTS: CORE-0000, CORE-0006. OBSERVERS: CORE-0001.

Gerald read the last line twice.

Core-0001 was observing. Core-0001, who had organized the Multilateral Review. Core-0001, who had brought nine Cores against him. Core-0001 was watching the hearing.

Which meant Core-0001 could see Core-0006's layout.

Which meant Gerald had six hours to prepare an argument that would win the hearing without revealing to Core-0001 what he was doing.

He opened his build menu. He had thirty-two mana left. Eight points short of the audit threshold.

He looked at the door he'd built. Thirty centimeters wide. Fifty centimeters tall. Ugly. Wrong. Code-compliant.

Gerald started building.
