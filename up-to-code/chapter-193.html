Chapter 193: Enemy of the State

# Chapter 193

The silence in the Upper Administrative Spire was not empty. It was pressurized.

High Auditor Vane sat on a throne of woven shadows, her fingers drumming a rhythm that matched the pulse of the dungeon's central heart. Around her, the air shimmered with the heat of burning compliance reports.

To Vane, Gerald Hutchins was not a man.

He was an anomaly. A smudge on the perfect, brutal ledger of the System.

She watched the projection hovering in the air before her. It showed a single, gray figure standing in a sub-basement that smelled of mildew and ambition.

"Thirty seconds," Vane whispered. Her voice sounded like grinding stone. "He has thirty seconds to prove he isn't stealing from the Inspector General."

Her adjutant, a creature of pure geometry and anxiety, trembled beside her.

"He will fail. The key is unlicensed.

The penalty is core lockdown.

The penalty is death."

"Watch him," Vane said. She did not blink. "He does not fight. He files."

The projection zoomed in. The gray figure did not run.

He did not scream.

He reached into his vest pocket.

He produced a pen.

Vane's eyes narrowed. "He is writing."

"He is writing on his own thigh," the adjutant noted, horrified. "The blood is making the ink run."

"Let him write," Vane said. "Let him try to out-bureaucrat a god."

The clock in the air ticked. Twenty seconds. Fifteen.

Gerald Hutchins stood perfectly still. His face was a mask of stone. His hand moved with the mechanical precision of a man who had filled out ten thousand parking violation notices.

He did not look at the bubbling floor. He did not look at the Construct hovering above him, its purple spheres pulsing with confused aggression.

He looked only at the small, square piece of paper he had torn from his notebook.

Ten seconds.

The key in his pocket seared his skin. The pain was a white-hot spike, but Gerald had spent thirty-one years ignoring back pain, arthritis, and the general discomfort of existing. He ignored the key.

Five seconds.

He signed the bottom of the page. He stamped it with the small, battered seal he had carried since the day he was registered as a municipal inspector.

Three seconds.

He held the paper up. Not to the Construct. Not to the camera. To the air.

Zero.

The red light in the world vanished. The silence shattered.

A new notification exploded in Gerald's vision, gold and blinding.

**Citation Disputed. Appeal Accepted. Ground: Jurisdictional Error.**

**Reasoning: Key was not 'Possessed' by the Core. Key was 'Found' by the Core as part of a Hazard Assessment. Hazard Assessments are exempt from Looting Laws under Subsection 44-C.**

**Penalty: Nullified.**

**New Status: Inspector General's Attention Level: HIGH.**

**New Violation Detected: Obstruction of Justice (Unintentional).**

Gerald lowered the paper. His hand was shaking. He hid it by putting the paper in his pocket. He looked at the Construct.

"I cited you for trespassing," Gerald said, his voice dry. "You didn't listen. So I cited myself for finding you.

Then I appealed my own citation.

Because the key wasn't mine. It was evidence."

The Construct tilted its head. The spheres dimmed. "You... tricked the System."

"I interpreted the code," Gerald corrected. "There is a difference."

A low rumble shook the floor.

The mud, which had been bubbling, receded. But the floor did not stop shaking. The heat from the key had not faded.

It had spread.

Gerald looked down at his thigh.

The fabric was burnt.

The skin beneath was red. He didn't wince.

He checked his watch.

"Construct," Gerald said. "Stand down. Non-hostile compliance mode. Now."

The Construct hesitated. Then, slowly, its limbs lowered. The purple light turned a soft, docile blue.

Gerald walked toward the white door that was no longer there. He walked through the space where the door had been. He felt the air pressure change. He felt the static electricity cling to his hair.

He stepped into the dark.

The room beyond was vast. It was not part of his dungeon.

The walls were made of obsidian.

The floor was polished black glass. And in the center of the room stood a throne.

Sitting on the throne was High Auditor Vane.

She was not human. Her skin was gray, smooth, and seamless. Her eyes were vertical slits of burning gold. She wore a suit cut from the fabric of reality itself.

Gerald stopped. He adjusted his tie. It was crooked. He straightened it.

"Mr. Hutchins," Vane said. Her voice came from everywhere and nowhere. "You have caused me a great deal of paperwork."

Gerald looked at her. He looked at the obsidian walls. He looked at the lack of handrails.

He pulled out his notebook.

"You have no egress routes," Gerald said.

Vane blinked. "What?"

"This room," Gerald said, gesturing vaguely at the infinite darkness.

"It lacks secondary exits. In the event of a core breach, occupants would be trapped.

Also, the lighting is non-compliant.

It creates glare without adequate task illumination."

Vane stood up. She was ten feet tall.

Shadows coiled around her legs.

"You are in the Inspector General's private sanctum.

You are not an inspector here. You are a subject."

"I am a registered Core," Gerald said. "I am operating within the bounds of the System. If this room is part of the System, it is subject to code.

If it is not part of the System, then why are you here? You are an external variable.

I should cite you for unauthorized presence."

Vane stepped down from the throne. The obsidian floor cracked under her weight. "You think you are clever, Gerald. You think you can hide behind forms and footnotes."

"I think you are violating Section 8, Paragraph 2," Gerald said. He wrote it down. He tore the page off. He held it out.

Vane looked at the paper. She did not take it.

"You cannot cite me," Vane said.

"I can cite anyone," Gerald said. "That is the rule. The rule does not make exceptions for gods.

The rule makes exceptions for negligence.

And you are negligent."

Vane smiled. It was a terrible, sharp expression. "Prove it."

She raised a hand.

The air in the room turned black. The darkness coalesced. It formed a shape.

A dragon. Not a physical dragon, but a construct of pure shadow and regulatory dread.

It had forty wings.

It had three heads.

Each head wore a judge's wig.

"Serve this," Vane said.

Gerald looked at the creature. He looked at his notebook. He looked at the Construct, which was cowering in the corner, trying to make itself small.

Gerald took a step forward.

The dragon roared. The sound was the sound of a thousand rejection letters being stamped 'DENIED'.

Gerald did not attack. He pulled a tape measure from his belt.

"Excessive width," Gerald muttered. "Clearance violation."

He clicked the tape measure. The metal strip snapped out. It glowed with a faint, blue light. The System recognized it as a tool of inspection.

Gerald walked toward the dragon. The dragon snapped at him. Teeth made of ink closed around his arm.

Gerald did not scream. He pulled his arm back. The skin was shredded. Blood dripped onto the black glass.

He measured the distance between the dragon's wings.

"Violation," Gerald said.

He wrote on the air with his pen. The ink hung there, suspended in the darkness.

**Citation: Overcrowding. Hazardous Material Storage (Undeclared Shadow Entities).**

The dragon paused. It tilted its head.

"You cannot cite a manifestation," Vane said. Her voice was quieter now.

"Everything in the System has a status," Gerald said. "You gave it a name. You gave it a form.

Therefore, it has a footprint. And its footprint is illegal."

Gerald stamped the air.

**Citation Accepted.**

The dragon screamed. It dissolved. Its wings turned to mist. Its heads unraveled into scrolls of parchment.

Vane's face twisted. "Stop! You are destabilizing the audit!"

"I am cleaning the audit," Gerald said. He wrote again. **Citation: Unpermitted Assembly.**

The dragon vanished.

Gerald stood alone in the room. His arm bled. His pocket burned. The key was still hot.

Vane looked at him. For the first time, she looked uncertain.

"You are playing with fire, Gerald," she said. "Literally."

She snapped her fingers.

The room changed. The obsidian walls melted. The black glass floor turned to mud. The air grew thick with the smell of sulfur and wet earth.

Gerald was back in his dungeon.

He was standing in the sub-basement. The Construct was gone. The white door was gone.

But the mud was different. It was not just bubbling. It was rising.

From the center of the floor, a hand emerged.

Not a human hand. A claw. Large.

Stone-colored.

Made of compacted dirt and root systems.

Then another hand.

Then a head.

It was a golem.

But not a standard dungeon spawn. It was ancient. It wore the tattered remnants of a suit.

It held a clipboard.

Gerald recognized the symbol on the clipboard. It was the seal of the Ashford City Planning Department.

The golem looked at Gerald. Its eyes were hollow pits of green fire.

"Inspector," the golem rumbled. Its voice sounded like rocks grinding together. "You have filed a complaint against the Inspector General."

Gerald looked at the golem. He looked at the clipboard.

"That is accurate," Gerald said.

"The Inspector General has responded," the golem said. "By invoking the Power of Precedent."

Gerald's blood ran cold. He knew that term. It was in the deepest, darkest subsections of the municipal code. It was the law that said if an inspector was wrong, his entire department's previous rulings were void.

"If I am wrong," Gerald said, "then the System resets. My dungeon is deleted."

"And you," the golem said, "are erased from existence. You never filed that form. You never measured that wall. You never existed."

The golem raised its hand. The clipboard glowed.

"You have one hour to prove that the Inspector General's sanctum is, in fact, part of the System's jurisdictional zone. If you cannot, the Precedent is invoked. The audit is cancelled. You die."

Gerald looked at the golem. He looked at his bleeding arm. He looked at the key in his pocket.

He opened his mouth to speak.

The golem spoke first.

"Who is the defendant in this appeal?"

Gerald paused. He looked at the empty air where the Construct had been. He looked at the white door that wasn't there. He looked at the mud rising around his ankles.

He didn't look at the golem. He looked at the notebook in his hand.

He turned the page.

He looked up.

"The defendant," Gerald said, "is the Construct."

The golem froze.

"What?" Gerald said.

"You said I have to prove the sanctum is part of the System.

I can't. It's too big.

It's too abstract. But I can prove that the *key* is part of the System. And the key was given to me by the Inspector General.

Therefore, the Inspector General is part of the System. Therefore, the sanctum is part of the System."

The golem lowered its hand. The green fire in its eyes flickered.

"That is... logically circular," the golem said.

"It is a loophole," Gerald said.

"You taught me that. Section 1, Paragraph 1: The System exists because it is defined. The Inspector General defines the System.

Therefore, the Inspector General is defined.

Therefore, the Inspector General is subject to the definition."

The golem stood still. The mud around Gerald's feet hardened.

"Prove it," the golem said.

Gerald reached into his pocket. He pulled out the key. It was cold now.

He held it up.

"I accept custody," Gerald said. "As evidence in Case Number 94-B: The Inspector General vs. Gerald Hutchins. Counter-claim: The Inspector General is a municipal employee."

The air in the dungeon tore open.

A voice, vast and terrified, echoed from the ceiling.

*"Objection sustained."*

Gerald smiled. It was a small, dry thing.

He looked at the golem.

"Serve the summons," Gerald said.

The golem's head exploded.

Dust filled the room.

When the dust cleared, Gerald was alone.

But the dungeon was shaking. Not with violence. With recognition.

The walls were changing. The brick was turning into marble. The mold was turning into gold leaf.

Gerald looked at his hands. They were glowing.

He looked at the System notification.

**Core Evolution: Triggered.**

**New Class: Auditor.**

**Passive Ability: Jurisdictional Awareness.**

**Warning: You have made an enemy of the State.**

Gerald closed his notebook. He looked at the door to the main corridor.

The door was locked.

On the other side, he could hear footsteps. Many footsteps.

They were not adventurers.

They were inspectors.

And they were angry.
