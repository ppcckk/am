Chapter 7

Gerald had built three walls before he realized the problem.

The hearing would grant both participants inspection access. He hadn't known this. He'd known it the way you know a thing you've read in a manual you'll never use again, filed under "procedural context" in the back of your mind where useful information goes to die. The System's hearing protocol required mutual transparency. Both Cores would see each other's structures in real time. The observer would see both.

Core-0001 would see his dungeon.

Gerald stopped building. He looked at the corridor he'd made. Thirty-two mana spent on structural reinforcement. The walls were dense, properly graded, load-bearing at ratios that would make a structural engineer weep with joy. They were also four meters long. His entire dungeon was four meters long.

He did the math. Core-0001's dungeon, based on the topological data from the Multilateral Review, was approximately three hundred meters across. Gerald's was four. The ratio was seventy-five to one. Core-0001 would look at his dungeon and see a single corridor. A corridor with a door that was the wrong size for anything other than a very small rodent.

Gerald felt the familiar nausea. The one that came when he couldn't reconcile the rule with the situation. The rule said: you must inspect before you cite. The situation said: you cited without inspecting and it worked. The new rule said: the hearing will expose that you have no dungeon worth defending.

He sat down. He put his head in his hands. His hands were not shaking. They were steady. They had always been steady. Twenty-three years of steady hands on measuring tapes and clipboards.

The clock said five hours and forty minutes.

Gerald opened the hearing protocol again. He read it slowly. Line by line. The way he read code amendments at the office, cross-referencing against the master index.

The hearing protocol stated: "Both parties shall present structural logic arguments demonstrating compliance or non-compliance with the cited category. Evidence may include any structural data accessible to either party at the time of the hearing."

There. That was the loophole. Not the inspection access itself, but the evidence window. The System judged the citation's validity based on evidence available at the time of the hearing, not at the time of issuance. If Gerald could present new evidence during the hearing, the citation would be retroactively validated.

He didn't need to defend his method. He needed to find new violations in Core-0006's dungeon during the hearing itself. The inspection access would give him the data. He would find something. Something worse than the boundary grade violation he'd already cited.

He needed to know what to look for.

Gerald opened his build menu. He had thirty-two mana left. Eight points short of the audit threshold. He couldn't build anything meaningful. But he could build a reference. He could build a violation checklist.

He didn't know what Core-0006's dungeon looked like. He knew the topological outline. He knew the room count. He knew the corridor widths and the junction angles. He didn't know the ventilation.

Ventilation. That was a category. He hadn't cited it. He hadn't even thought about it because he hadn't seen the dungeon. But if the hearing gave him inspection access, he could see it. He could see the air channels. He could measure the cubic feet per minute against the occupancy load.

Occupancy load. That was the variable he couldn't predict. How many entities did Core-0006 spawn? He'd seen the combat data. The Multilateral Review had logged Core-0006's defense entities. He'd read the logs. He'd read them because reading logs was what he did.

Core-0006's dungeon had forty-three active defense entities at peak. The System's ventilation code, which Gerald had memorized in the same way he'd memorized everything else, required a minimum of fifteen cubic feet per minute per occupant. Forty-three occupants meant six hundred and forty-five cubic feet per minute minimum.

He didn't know Core-0006's air channel dimensions. Not yet.

But he could guess. He could guess from the topological data. The corridor widths were narrow. The junction angles were tight. A dungeon built for combat flow, not for airflow. Core-0006 had optimized for tactical advantage. Gerald had optimized for code compliance. Those were not the same optimization.

Gerald started building again. Not walls. Not doors. He built a calculation framework into his dungeon's structure. He used the mana to create a reference grid, a set of embedded measurement markers that would calibrate his inspection access during the hearing. It was the equivalent of bringing his laser level to a job site.

He spent eleven mana on it. Twenty-one left.

Four hours and twelve minutes.

Gerald slept for two hours. He slept on the floor of his corridor because he hadn't built a bed and he didn't have the mana to build one. When he woke, the clock said two hours and twelve minutes.

He opened the hearing interface. He reviewed his argument. He had one point. Core-0006's boundary grade was below code. He had cited it. The citation was active. Core-0006 was debuffed. If Gerald could add ventilation during the hearing, the citation would expand to cover two categories. The debuff would double. Core-0006 would lose access to two of its four primary defense formations.

Gerald could win. He could win if the ventilation was bad.

He bet it was.

The hearing began at the appointed hour. Gerald felt the System pull at his awareness like a hand on his shoulder. The inspection access opened. He saw Core-0006's dungeon.

It was beautiful. Gerald hated it immediately.

The layout was a spiral. A logarithmic spiral that funneled intruders inward through progressively narrower corridors, each one angled to expose the intruder's flank to the next wave of defenders. It was elegant. It was efficient. It was a combat engineer's dream.

It was a ventilation nightmare.

Gerald saw the air channels. They ran along the outer wall of the spiral, feeding fresh air inward through a series of junction boxes. The channels were narrow. Too narrow. He measured them with his embedded reference grid. The System translated his calibration into real-time data.

Channel width: forty centimeters. Channel height: thirty centimeters. Cross-sectional area: twelve hundred square centimeters. That was approximately one point three square feet. At a reasonable air velocity of six hundred feet per minute, that gave seven hundred and eighty cubic feet per minute at the main trunk.

But the trunk fed forty-three occupants. Six hundred and forty-five cubic feet per minute required. The trunk could deliver it. Barely. With zero margin.

Gerald looked closer. He looked at the junction boxes. The junction boxes were where the trunk branched into individual corridors. Each junction box had a damper. The dampers were set to equal flow. That was wrong. The inner corridors were narrower than the outer corridors. The inner corridors had fewer entities but the dampers were giving them the same airflow as the outer corridors, which had more entities and needed more air.

The System's ventilation code required proportional distribution based on occupancy load. Core-0006 had equal distribution. That was a violation. Not of the total volume. Of the distribution method.

Gerald found it. He found it because he had spent twenty-three years finding exactly this kind of thing. Equal-flow dampers in a variable-occupancy system. It was the most common ventilation violation in residential construction. It was the thing that made people get headaches in apartments. It was the thing Gerald cited four times a year without thinking.

He presented it.

The hearing interface translated his argument into System language. The System processed it. The System checked Core-0006's actual air distribution against the code. The numbers matched.

CITATION EXPANDED. CATEGORY: VENTILATION. SUBSECTION: DISTRIBUTION PROPORTIONALITY. SEVERITY: MODERATE. DEBUFF APPLIED: REGENERATION REDUCTION, TWENTY PERCENT.

Core-0006's argument came through. Gerald couldn't see Core-0006's face because Core-0006 didn't have a face. But he could feel the tone. Core-0006 was arguing that the equal-flow dampers were a deliberate tactical choice. That they prevented intruders from using ventilation channels as bypass routes. That the code violation was a combat feature.

The System did not care about combat features. The System cared about code.

APPEAL DENIED. ORIGINAL CITATION VALIDATED. EXPANDED CITATION VALIDATED. TOTAL DEBUFF: STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY REDUCTION, FIFTEEN PERCENT. REGENERATION REDUCTION, TWENTY PERCENT.

Gerald felt something. It was close to satisfaction but it wasn't. It was the feeling of closing a file. The feeling of a job done correctly.

Then Core-0001 spoke.

Core-0001 had been observing. Core-0001 had seen everything. Core-0001 had seen Gerald's dungeon.

"CORE-0000," Core-0001 said, through the hearing interface. The System translated Core-0001's communication into flat text. "Your structural integrity is below the audit threshold. You are eight mana points from a forced inspection by the Inspector General. I am filing a counter-citation."

Gerald read the notification.

CORE-0001 HAS FILED A CITATION AGAINST CORE-0000. CATEGORY: STRUCTURAL. SUBSECTION: MINIMUM VOLUME REQUIREMENTS. SEVERITY: CRITICAL. DEBUFF: ALL SPAWNED ENTITIES REDUCED TO FIFTY PERCENT STATS. HEARING SCHEDULED. TIMEFRAME: 24 HOURS.

Gerald looked at his dungeon. Four meters long. A single corridor. A door sized for a mouse. Twenty-one mana in his account. Eight short of the threshold.

He looked at the citation details. Minimum volume requirements. Gerald knew this code. He knew it better than he knew his own name. The minimum volume for a Core-class dungeon was fifty cubic meters. Gerald's dungeon was four cubic meters.

He was eleven times below the minimum.

Gerald sat on the floor of his corridor. He did the math. He had twenty-four hours. He had twenty-one mana. He needed to build forty-six cubic meters of code-compliant structure. That would cost approximately four hundred mana.

He had twenty-one.

He looked at the ceiling of his corridor. He looked at the walls. They were dense. They were properly graded. They were load-bearing at ratios that would make a structural engineer weep.

He looked at the citation again. CORE-0001 had filed it. Core-0001, who had seen his dungeon during the hearing. Core-0001, who had brought nine Cores against him. Core-0001, who was now using Gerald's own code against him.

Gerald opened his build menu. He looked at the numbers. Four hundred mana. Twenty-one available. He needed to find three hundred and seventy-nine mana in twenty-four hours.

He looked at the citation details one more time. There was a line he hadn't noticed. A small line at the bottom, in the same flat System text as everything else.

APPEAL AVAILABLE. SCOPE: STRUCTURAL LOGIC ARGUMENT. NOTE: APPELLANT MAY REQUEST EXTENSION IF CITATION IS FOUND TO BE PROCEDURALLY IMPROPER.

Procedurally improper.

Gerald read it again. He thought about Core-0001's citation. Core-0001 had cited him during a hearing. Gerald was currently in a hearing. The hearing was still technically open. The System had processed Gerald's appeal against Core-0006, but had it closed the hearing? Had it processed Core-0001's counter-citation as part of the same hearing or as a separate filing?

If it was the same hearing, Core-0001 had filed a citation during a proceeding where Gerald was the respondent. That was a conflict. That was like a judge citing the defendant during their own trial.

Gerald didn't know if the System recognized that analogy. He didn't know if the System had a rule about it. But he had twenty-four hours and he had a question and the question had a yes or no answer that he could find in the hearing protocol if he read it carefully enough.

He opened the protocol. He read it line by line. He read it the way he read code amendments. He read it the way he had read every document for twenty-three years.

On line forty-seven, in a subsection about observer privileges, he found it.

OBSERVERS MAY NOT FILE CITATIONS DURING ACTIVE PROCEEDINGS.

Gerald looked at the timestamp. Core-0001's citation was filed at 14:07. The hearing had closed at 14:06. One minute. Core-0001 had filed the citation one minute after the hearing closed.

One minute.

Gerald checked the System's processing time. The hearing closure notification had been sent at 14:06:03. Core-0001's citation had been received at 14:07:12. That was sixty-nine seconds after closure. Not during the proceeding.

Gerald closed the protocol. He opened it again. He read line forty-seven again.

OBSERVERS MAY NOT FILE CITATIONS DURING ACTIVE PROCEEDINGS.

He looked at the timestamp again. Sixty-nine seconds. Not during. After.

Gerald sat on the floor. He had twenty-four hours. He had twenty-one mana. He needed four hundred. He needed to build forty-six cubic meters of code-compliant structure or find three hundred and seventy-nine mana or find another way.

He looked at his corridor. Four meters long. One meter wide. One meter tall. Four cubic meters.

He looked at the door. Thirty centimeters wide. Fifty centimeters tall.

He looked at the citation. Critical severity. Fifty percent debuff to all spawned entities. He didn't have any spawned entities. He had never spawned anything. He was a dungeon with no monsters. A building with no tenants. An inspector who had never built a single thing worth inspecting.

Gerald opened his build menu one more time. He looked at the mana costs. He looked at the structural categories. He looked at the compliance bonuses.

He looked at the citation one more time. He looked at the word "procedurally." He looked at the word "improper."

He looked at the sixty-nine seconds.

Gerald started typing an appeal argument.
