Chapter 336: Narrative Compliance

# Chapter 336

The ink was already drying on the blank page.

Gerald stared at it. The paper was supposed to be white.

It was currently a shifting, oily black, rippling like crude oil on a stagnant pond.

His pen hovered above the surface, trembling. Not from fear, but from the sheer impossibility of the request.

*Form 11-B: Unlicensed Biological Entity.*

The Architect was not biological. The Architect was a concept wearing a suit made of geometry.

"You're wasting time," a voice said from the shadows.

Gerald didn't look up. He kept his eyes on the form.

"I am assessing the violation."

The voice stepped closer.

The air smelled of ozone and burnt sugar.

"You have no jurisdiction.

I am not within your municipal bounds.

I am within the *narrative* bounds.

"

"Then you are subject to Narrative Compliance regulations," Gerald said. He tapped the pen against his lip.

"Section 4, Subsection D.

Any entity that alters the structural integrity of a localized reality zone must file a Variance Request.

You did not file one.

"

The Architect laughed. It sounded like a stack of books collapsing.

"There is no such section."

"There is now."

Gerald wrote. *Violation: Unauthorized Alteration of Local Space-Time Geometry. Severity: Critical.*

The pen tip snapped. Ink sprayed across the desk. The black sludge from the paper rose up, forming a hand. It grabbed Gerald's wrist.

The grip was cold. Not temperature cold. Conceptual cold. The kind that made your thoughts stutter.

"Let go," Gerald said. He tried to pull, but the arm didn't move. The hand tightened, crushing the bones.

"You don't have a form for this," the Architect whispered. The voice was coming from everywhere now. From the walls.

From the floor. From the inside of Gerald's own skull.

"You don't have a code for godhood."

Gerald stopped pulling. He stared at the hand. He looked at the shattered pen. He looked at the ink spreading across the desk, forming jagged, angry runes.

"I don't need a form for godhood," Gerald said. "I need a form for zoning."

The hand paused.

"Explain."

"You are occupying a space that is not zoned for divine intervention," Gerald said. His voice was flat, the same tone he used when telling a contractor their load-bearing beam was three inches too short. "This is a residential sub-basement.

Class R-1.

Maximum occupancy: four mold spores, one rat, and myself. You are exceeding the occupancy load by one conceptual entity.

"

The hand loosened. Just a fraction.

"Occupancy load?" the Architect asked. The voice lost its echoing reverb, becoming sharper. More focused.

"Under the Ashford Municipal Code, Section 101.2, any structure used for assembly or gathering must be rated for its intended use," Gerald said.

He pulled his wrist back, wincing as the skin peeled away. He didn't look at the wound. He looked at the ink.

"You are an assembly of infinite selves. That is a high-density gathering.

You are blocking the egress routes.

"

The ceiling of the dungeon rippled. The mirrors that reflected infinite Geralds cracked. Not all of them. Just the ones on the left.

"I am not blocking anything," the Architect said.

"You are blocking the ventilation shaft," Gerald said.

He pointed a shaking finger at a grate in the corner.

"Your presence is displacing air mass.

The System calculates airflow based on volume. You are taking up volume. Therefore, the airflow is insufficient for the number of entities present.

"

"That is... pedantic," the Architect said.

"It is code." Gerald picked up a fresh pen. "If I cite you for poor ventilation, the System applies a debuff. Reduced stamina regeneration for all entities in the zone. Including you."

The Architect was silent. The black hand dissolved into smoke.

Gerald sat back. His heart hammered against his ribs.

He had made it up, and he had looked at the System's base code, found the ventilation clause, and applied it to a god. It was absurd. It was stupid.

It worked.

A notification pinged in his mind. *Citation Pending. Serving...*

"You cannot serve me," the Architect said.

The voice was no longer a whisper. It was a shout.

"I am not a physical entity in your world.

I am the author of the dungeon.

"

"I am the inspector of the dungeon," Gerald said. He stood up.

The floor tilted.

The angle of the room shifted from 90 degrees to 105 degrees.

Gerald grabbed the edge of the desk.

"And I am serving you.

Right here. Right now.

"

He pulled a blank sheet of paper from his desk. He held it out.

"Sign it."

The Architect materialized. Not as a shadow.

As a man.

Or something like a man. Tall. Too tall.

Skin like parchment.

Eyes like inkwells.

"You are bluffing," the Architect said.

"Am I?" Gerald pointed to the air. "Look at your stats."

The Architect looked down. His skin flickered. A red bar appeared over his head. *Stamina Regen: -15%.*

"Ventilation violation," Gerald said. "Proportional debuff. Section 304."

The Architect's face twisted.

"This is not fair. I am writing the story."

"I am enforcing the code," Gerald said.

The Architect raised a hand. A bolt of black lightning shot toward Gerald's chest.

Gerald didn't move. He didn't dodge. He just held out the form.

The lightning hit the paper.

And stopped.

The paper absorbed the energy. The ink on the page glowed blue.

"Electrical hazard," Gerald said. "Unshielded conductor. Citation: Immediate Shut-Down Order."

The Architect screamed, which was a sound like tearing metal. The black lightning reversed, flowing back into the Architect's hand. The hand withered.

The skin turned gray and brittle.

"Stop it," the Architect hissed. "You are killing me."

"No," Gerald said. "I am citing you."

"You are insane. This is a dungeon core. You are a rat and a mold spore with a clipboard."

"And you are a violation," Gerald said. He walked forward. The floor stabilized. The angles corrected themselves, forced back into right angles by the sheer weight of Gerald's authority.

"Who are you?" the Architect asked. The voice was weak now. The form was shrinking. The tall, parchment man was becoming a small, hunched figure.

"Gerald Hutchins," Gerald said. "Building Inspector, City of Ashford."

"Hutchins," the Architect spat. "You are small. You are weak. You have no power."

"I have power," Gerald said. "I have the power to say *no*."

The Architect lunged.

Not with lightning.

With words. "I will rewrite you.

I will make you forget your name. I will make you forget the code.

"

"You can try," Gerald said.

He held up the form. The paper was vibrating. It was warm.

"Serve," Gerald said.

He slapped the paper onto the Architect's chest.

It stuck.

The Architect froze. His eyes widened. The inkwell eyes flooded with tears of black bile.

*Violation Served. Compliance Rating: 47.*

The Architect fell to his knees. The room stopped spinning. The mirrors shattered completely.

Gerald looked down at the kneeling figure. He felt no triumph. He felt only the familiar, dull ache of a job well done and a day that had gone on too long.

"You have ten minutes to appeal," Gerald said. He checked his watch. "If you do not file a Hearing Request by then, the debuff becomes permanent. Your structural integrity will fail."

The Architect coughed. Blood, black and viscous, spilled from his nose.

"You... cannot... win," the Architect wheezed. "The System... is not... law. It is... chaos."

"It is code," Gerald said. "And you broke it."

A new voice cut through the silence.

"Mr. Hutchins?"

Gerald turned.

Standing in the doorway was a woman. She wore a suit that was too tight. Her badge was pinned crookedly to her lapel. She held a tablet that glowed with a harsh, white light.

Gerald recognized the uniform. He had seen it in the deepest levels of the System's administrative interface.

Inspector General Unit 7.

"Who are you?" the woman asked. She looked at the kneeling Architect. Then she looked at Gerald. Her expression was unreadable.

"Gerald Hutchins," Gerald said. He straightened his tie. "I issued a citation for spatial distortion and electrical hazards."

The woman walked into the room. She stepped over the rubble of a mirror. She did not look at the Architect. She looked at the form on his chest.

"This is an invalid citation," she said.

Gerald blinked.

"Excuse me?"

"Form 11-B is for biological entities," the woman said. She tapped her tablet.

"The Architect is a Class A Conceptual Entity.

You cannot cite a Concept under Biological codes. You need Form 99-Z. *Theological Zoning Variance.*

"

Gerald felt a chill run down his spine.

"I didn't have a Form 99-Z."

"You should have," the woman said. "It was issued to all Cores at Level 10."

"I am Level 4," Gerald said.

"Which means you are operating out of your depth," the woman said. She walked closer. "You applied a ventilation debuff to a god. Do you know what that does to the local narrative stability?"

"It makes him tired," Gerald said.

"It unravels the fabric of the dungeon," the woman said.

She pointed to the walls. The bricks were turning into dust.

The floor was dissolving into static.

"Look at your dungeon, Mr. Hutchins. You are killing it."

Gerald looked. The woman was right.

The corners of the room were fading, and the handrails he had installed were rusting in seconds.

The mold spores were dying.

"I stopped the threat," Gerald said.

"You created a bigger one," the woman said. She held out her hand. "Give me the form."

"No," Gerald said.

The woman stopped. Her eyes narrowed.

"This is not a request, Inspector. This is an audit."

"I have jurisdiction," Gerald said. His voice shook, but his hand did not. He placed himself between the woman and the Architect.

"This is my dungeon. I cited the violation.

I served the citation.

The process is underway.

"

"The process is void," the woman said.

"You used a nonexistent code. You created a loophole.

The System is correcting it.

Look."

She pointed to the Architect. The black ink on his chest was spreading. It was eating the form. It was eating the Architect.

"He is being erased," the woman said. "And when he is erased, the energy he contained will be released.

Into your dungeon.

Into the library.

Into the city."

Gerald looked at the Architect. The figure was translucent now. His mouth moved, but no sound came out.

"Can I stop it?" Gerald asked.

"You can appeal," the woman said. "But you cannot appeal for yourself. You have to appeal on his behalf."

Gerald stared at her.

"You want me to defend him?"

"I want you to fix the error you created," the woman said. "The Inspector General does not care about justice, Mr. Hutchins.

We care about stability. You broke the stability.

You fix it.

Or we demolish this entire sector.

"

"Demolish?" Gerald asked.

"Raze," the woman said.

"Total reconstruction. Your Core will be deleted.

Your dungeon will be replaced with a void.

A clean slate."

Gerald looked at the dying Architect. He looked at the dissolving walls. He looked at his own hands.

He had spent thirty-one years ensuring that buildings stood up. That walls held. That people could walk safely through a room without being crushed by a falling beam.

Now a beam was falling. And it was his fault.

"I need a Form 99-Z," Gerald said.

"It is not available to you," the woman said.

Gerald closed his eyes. He thought about the rules. He thought about the loopholes. He thought about the one thing the System could not ignore.

"I can write a new form," Gerald said.

The woman laughed. It was a dry, rasping sound.

"You are a Core.

You have access to the template library.

You cannot invent forms. That is a violation of the Creator's Exclusivity Clause.

"

"I am not inventing," Gerald said. He opened his eyes. "I am adapting."

He reached into his desk drawer. He pulled out a blank sheet of paper. He pulled out a pen. He pulled out a ruler.

"What are you doing?" the woman asked. She stepped back. Her hand moved to her side.

A weapon? No.

A tablet, and she was recording.

"I am citing myself," Gerald said.

He wrote.

*Violation: Failure to Maintain Structural Integrity. Severity: Critical.*

He wrote fast.

His handwriting was jagged, but legible. He outlined his own dungeon.

He outlined the damage, and he outlined the threat.

Then he wrote the next line.

*Remedy: Immediate Transfer of Jurisdiction.*

He signed it.

He slammed the paper onto the desk.

"I am citing myself for incompetence," Gerald said. "And I am requesting that the Inspector General assume direct control of this dungeon until the citation is processed."

The woman stared at him. Her face was pale.

"You... you just handed over your Core," she said.

"I handed over the liability," Gerald said. "The System cannot delete a Core that is under active appeal by the Inspector General. It has to process the citation first. That buys time."

The woman looked at the paper. She looked at the dissolving walls. She looked at the fading Architect.

"If you do this," she said slowly, "you lose your autonomy. The System will run this dungeon. Not you."

"My dungeon is falling apart," Gerald said. "And I am not strong enough to hold it up alone. But I am strong enough to know when I need backup."

The woman picked up the paper. She scanned it. The System interface on her tablet flashed green.

*Hearing Requested. Jurisdiction Transfer Initiated.*

The Architect screamed. The black ink vanished. The Architect solidified. He gasped, sucking in air like a drowning man.

The walls stopped dissolving. The bricks reformed. The handrails stopped rusting.

Gerald felt a pull in his chest. A hook, dragging him backward.

"I did it," Gerald said.

The woman looked at him with something that might have been respect. Or horror.

"You cited yourself into a coma," she said. "You won't be able to speak. You won't be able to spawn. You will be a passive observer while the System rebuilds your dungeon."

"I will be an inspector," Gerald said. "I will watch. And if they mess it up, I will cite them too."

The hook pulled harder. Gerald's vision blurred. The room spun.

The last thing he saw was the Architect, looking at him with wide, terrified eyes.

The last thing he heard was the woman's voice.

"Welcome to the real job, Mr. Hutchins."

Then, silence.

And the sound of a thousand new hammers starting to build.

The hammers struck a rhythm that Gerald had never heard before.
