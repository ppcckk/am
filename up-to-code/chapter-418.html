Chapter 418: Defendant the System

# Chapter 418

The grey suit did not wrinkle. It did not crease at the elbows or bunch at the shoulders when she moved. It hung on the woman like a second skin, a perfect, seamless layer of authority that absorbed the light than reflecting it.

Mira watched from the threshold. Her hand hovered over the hilt of her sword, trembling not from fear, but from the sheer, oppressive weight of the air in the room.

She had seen dragons, and she had fought liches. But she had never seen something that made her feel so small, so administratively insignificant.

The woman in the suit—Agent Kael, though she had not introduced herself by name—walked to the center of the void. The floor beneath her boots clicked against a surface that looked like polished obsidian.

Behind her, the courtroom stretched into infinity. Rows of empty seats filled the darkness, tiered like a stadium for spectators who did not exist. At the head of the room sat the Judge.

It was not a person, which was a wall.

A massive, load-bearing wall of reinforced concrete, rising into a ceiling lost in shadow. Embedded in the concrete was a single, glowing red eye. It blinked once, slow and deliberate. The sound was the hydraulic hiss of a heavy door closing.

Gerald stood in the center of the room. His posture was rigid. His clipboard was clutched to his chest like a shield. He looked less like a prisoner and more like a man who had arrived ten minutes early for a hearing he did not want to attend.

Agent Kael turned to him. Her face was a mask of neutral beige.

"Mr. Hutchins," she said. "You are accused of Unlicensed Jurisdictional Expansion and Malicious Code Compliance."

Gerald adjusted his glasses.

"The expansion was permitted under Section 4, Subsection B of the Core Autonomy Clause. My citation against the Void-Eater dungeon was filed prior to the expansion.

The System accepted the filing.

Therefore, the expansion was valid at the time of issuance.

"

"The System accepted the filing," Kael said. Her voice was flat, devoid of inflection.

"The System did not accept the precedent.

You are arguing that a procedural error grants you the right to destroy a sovereign Core, which is not law. That is vandalism.

"

"That is enforcement," Gerald corrected. "I served the citation. The entity failed to appeal within the window.

The debuff applied. The entity ceased to function.

I did not destroy it.

I cited it."

"You reduced a Level 40 Boss to a Level 1 Slime," Kael said.

"You killed it. Intent does not matter.

Outcome does.

The Union has filed a Class A Complaint.

"

Mira stepped forward.

"You can't hold him here. He's a registered Core. He has due process."

Kael did not look at Mira. Her eyes remained fixed on Gerald.

"Due process is a privilege of the compliant.

Mr.

Hutchins is currently in a state of Non-Compliant Autonomy. He has no rights until he submits to the jurisdiction of the Building.

"

The eye in the wall narrowed. The red light intensified, casting long, sharp shadows across Gerald's face.

Gerald did not flinch. He looked at the wall. He looked at the cracks in the concrete. He tilted his head slightly, analyzing the stress lines.

"The structural integrity of this chamber is compromised," Gerald said.

Kael paused. "What?"

"The vibration from the gavel," Gerald said, gesturing to the empty space where a judge's hammer would have struck. "It causes micro-fractures in the left support column. Look at the discoloration.

That's water intrusion. From a leak in the roof above.

You have a roof leak.

"

Silence.

The eye in the wall blinked.

"Mr. Hutchins," Kael said. "This is a courtroom. Not a job site."

"It is a building," Gerald said. "Buildings leak. Buildings fracture.

If you are going to judge me for breaking the code, you must first acknowledge that this room violates the Structural Safety Act of the System.

Section 12, Paragraph 3: All judgment chambers must be watertight and structurally sound to prevent contamination of evidence.

"

Kael frowned. It was a tiny movement, a slight tightening of the lips.

"Evidence is not physical. Evidence is data."

"Data is stored on hardware," Gerald said.

"Hardware is housed in a building. If the building is wet, the hardware corrodes. If the hardware corrodes, the data is lost.

If the data is lost, the trial is invalid. I am citing this courtroom for environmental hazards.

"

Mira stared at him.

Then she looked at the wall, and she saw the faint, damp patch near the base of the concrete pillar. She had seen it before, in the library.

In the sub-basement.

"You're bluffing," Kael said. But her voice lacked its previous certainty.

"I am inspecting," Gerald said. He pulled a tape measure from his pocket. It was a standard yellow steel tape. It looked absurdly small in the vast, dark void.

He walked toward the wall.

"Stop," Kael ordered.

Gerald kept walking.

"If you stop me, you are obstructing a registered inspector. If you let me measure, you are admitting the possibility of non-compliance. Which is it?"

Kael looked at the eye in the wall. The eye did not move.

Gerald reached the wall. He extended the tape measure. The metal blade hissed as it extended, a bright, reflective line against the dark concrete. He touched the tape to the crack.

"Two millimeters," he said. "That's within tolerance for thermal expansion.

But the moisture content in the concrete behind it is high.

I can smell it. Damp. Rot.

You have a plumbing failure in the upper levels.

It's seeping down.

"

He pulled a small moisture meter from his other pocket. He pressed it against the wall. The device beeped. A sharp, electronic chirp that echoed in the silence.

"85% saturation," Gerald said. "This wall is failing. If I were to apply a Citation for Negligent Maintenance, I would have to shut down this chamber. And if I shut down this chamber, the trial cannot proceed."

Kael's face went pale. Her grey suit seemed to fade into the background.

"You cannot cite a courtroom," she said. "It is part of the System infrastructure."

"Infrastructure is subject to code," Gerald said.

"Even the System. Especially the System. Who maintains the System?

Who inspects the inspectors?

"

He turned to Mira.

"Check the ceiling."

Mira looked up. High above, in the darkness, a dark stain was spreading. A drip fell. It hit the obsidian floor with a soft *plink*.

*Plink.*

*Plink.*

Kael looked at the drip. She looked at Gerald.

"You are trying to disrupt the proceedings," Kael said.

"I am trying to ensure the proceedings are valid," Gerald said.

"If this room is unsafe, your verdict is inadmissible.

You want a conviction? You need a safe room.

You need to fix the leak. Then we can talk about my jurisdiction.

"

Kael opened her briefcase. It was black leather, worn at the corners. She pulled out a thick file. The papers inside were white, stark against the grey.

"Mr. Hutchins," she said. "You are wasting time."

"Time is a resource," Gerald said. "And you are wasting it.

Because you didn't check the moisture content before setting up.

You assumed the infrastructure was perfect. You assumed the System doesn't rot. But it does.

Everything rots.

Even code."

He stepped closer to the wall. He placed his hand against the cold concrete. He closed his eyes.

**SYSTEM MESSAGE:** **INSPECTION IN PROGRESS.** **SUBJECT: JUDGMENT CHAMBER ALPHA.** **DETECTING: STRUCTURAL DEGRADATION.** **ISSUING: PROVISIONAL CITATION (UNSERVED).** **STATUS: PENDING VERIFICATION.**

A red stamp appeared in the air, glowing like a brand. It hovered over Gerald's head.

Kael stepped back.

"You cannot serve a citation on a System node."

"It's a node," Gerald said. "But it's also a building. And I'm an inspector."

He pulled a pen from his pocket. He wrote on the invisible stamp.

*Violation 1: Water Intrusion.* *Violation 2: Lack of Emergency Egress.* *Violation 3: Unauthorized Use of Load-Bearing Elements for Seating.*

He pointed to the empty seats in the shadows.

"Those seats are attached to the support beams. If the beams fail, the seats collapse. That's a liability.

You're putting the audience at risk. Do you have an audience?

Yes.

The Union.

They're watching. They're waiting for the verdict.

If the roof collapses on them, who gets sued?

"

Kael's hands shook. The file in her hands trembled.

"You are insane," she whispered.

"I am thorough," Gerald said.

The eye in the wall widened. The red light pulsed, brightening into a harsh white.

A new message appeared in the air. Not for Gerald. For Kael.

**SYSTEM MESSAGE:** **ALERT: STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY COMPROMISED.** **AUTOMATED MAINTENANCE PROTOCOL INITIATED.** **CHAMBER LOCKDOWN IMMINENT.**

The floor beneath them vibrated. A low rumble, deep in the bedrock. Dust fell from the ceiling.

Kael looked at the message. She looked at Gerald. For the first time, her blank mask cracked. Fear flickered in her concrete-grey eyes.

"You broke it," she said. "You broke the courtroom."

"I found the cracks," Gerald said. "You built them."

Mira grabbed Gerald's arm.

"We need to move. The floor is going to drop."

"No," Gerald said. He pulled away.

He looked at Kael.

"The Union has filed a complaint. But you have a maintenance emergency. Which takes priority?"

Kael looked at the briefcase. She looked at the file. She looked at the dripping ceiling.

She closed the briefcase.

"We will recess," she said. Her voice was tight. "The chamber is unsafe. The trial is suspended."

"Suspended or cancelled?" Gerald asked.

"Suspended," Kael said. She turned away. "Come. We are leaving."

She walked toward the door. The grey steel door that had not been there before. It appeared in the wall, sliding open with a screech of metal.

Mira looked at Gerald.

"What now?"

"Now," Gerald said, "we wait. The System will fix the leak. And when it does, it will check who caused the damage."

He looked at the red stamp still hovering in the air. It was fading.

"But the citation is already in the record," he said.

"It's not served. But it's logged.

And once it's logged, I have leverage.

The Union wants a conviction. I have a counter-suit. For negligence.

"

Mira stared at him.

"You're suing the System?"

"I'm citing the System," Gerald said. "It's different. One is a request. The other is a demand."

He picked up his clipboard. He checked his notes.

"Chapter 418 is over," he said. "Let's see what they do next."

He walked toward the door. But before he crossed the threshold, the floor beneath his feet gave way.

Not a hole. A shift.

The obsidian slab slid three inches to the left.

Gerald stumbled. He caught himself on the doorframe.

Kael was already gone. The door was closing.

Through the gap, Gerald saw the courtroom. The seats were gone. The wall was gone.

In its place was a void. A true void.

No light.

No floor.

No ceiling.

Just static.

And in the static, a figure emerged. Not Agent Kael. Not the Judge.

It was the Inspector General, and he was not in a suit. He was in rags. His eyes were missing.

He pointed at Gerald.

**SYSTEM MESSAGE:** **AUDIT TRIGGERED.** **REASON: EXCESSIVE CITATION FREQUENCY.** **PENALTY: CORE DECOMMISSIONING REVIEW.**

The door slammed shut.

Gerald stood in the dark. The silence was absolute.

Mira was beside him. She was breathing hard.

"He's coming for you," she said.

"No," Gerald said. He tapped his pen against the clipboard. "He's coming for the record. And I have a lot of records."

He looked at the blank wall where the door had been.

"Next up," he said, "is the appeal."

He didn't look at Mira. He looked at the wall. He started writing.

*Form 11-C: Request for Hearing.* *Reason: Procedural Irregularity.* *Defendant: The System.*

His hand didn't shake.

Not anymore.

The static returned. It swallowed the room.

And Gerald Hutchins, Municipal Building Inspector, Dungeon Core, and now Defendant, waited for the next sentence.

The next sentence wasn’t a form.
