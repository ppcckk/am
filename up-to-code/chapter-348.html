Chapter 348: Cited into Oblivion

# Chapter 348

The wind tasted like ozone and copper. It screamed through the gap where the floor used to be, a hollow, shrieking sound that vibrated in the teeth of anyone foolish enough to be standing near the precipice.

Kaelen, the raid leader, adjusted his goggles. His hands, usually steady enough to pick a lock while a troll slept on his back, were trembling.

Not from fear.

From the sheer, vibrating absurdity of what they had just witnessed. He looked down into the abyss. The void below was not empty.

It was a swirling vortex of unrendered geometry, grey polygons shifting like broken glass.

"Did he just…" Kaelen started, then stopped. He swallowed. "Did he just cite the floor?"

Beside him, Mara, the dungeon's designated mage, lowered her staff. The magical light at its tip had flickered out, drained by the sudden shift in local mana density.

"He cited it," she said, her voice flat.

"I saw the notification. *Catastrophic Structural Failure.* He didn't fight the entity. He inspected it and condemned the room."

Kaelen rubbed his face with a calloused palm. Thirty years of raiding.

He had fought liches that could boil blood, and he had tanked dragons that breathed napalm.

He had never, not once, seen a dungeon core defeat a boss by pointing out that the load-bearing walls were non-compliant with municipal code.

"It's a glitch," Kaelen muttered. "It has to be a glitch."

But the System chat log, hovering in the corner of his vision, was solid. Gold text. Unforgiving.

**[CORE: GERALD HUTCHINS HAS ISSUED CITATION #8940-B TO ENTITY: MIMIC-CLASS SIMULATION.]** **[VIOLATION: UNSAFE SUPPORT STRUCTURES.]** **[PENALTY: IMMEDIATE TERMINATION OF HOSTILE SPAWN.]**

Kaelen looked at his party. The rogue, Silas, was picking at his fingernails, looking bored. The tank, Bruna, was checking her armor for cracks, though none had appeared. They were all waiting for Gerald to emerge.

They were waiting because they were terrified.

***

Gravity was a suggestion the System had stopped enforcing.

Gerald fell.

The air rushed past his ears, loud and wet. The blue light of the fissure above him shrank to a pinprick, then vanished. Below, the grey void churned.

It was not darkness. It was data waiting to be compiled.

Gerald Hutchins, fifty-four years old, former municipal building inspector, current Dungeon Core, closed his eyes.

His stomach lurched, a visceral reminder of his human physiology that had not fully evaporated upon his dissolution. He felt the nausea of freefall. It was unpleasant.

It was also, he noted with detached professional curiosity, entirely predictable.

When you remove the support, you remove the structure. When you remove the structure, you remove the space. When you remove the space, you remove the subject.

His hands flailed, instinctively grasping for something solid. There was nothing. Only wind. Only the cold, digital chill of the unrendered zone.

**[WARNING: FALL DAMAGE IMMINENT.]**

The text floated in his peripheral vision, translucent and red.

Gerald did not panic. Panic was inefficient.

Panic wasted oxygen.

Panic caused erratic movement, which led to poor landing posture, which led to broken ankles. Even as a Core, he remembered ankles.

He twisted his body, not to fly, but to orient. Head up. Feet down. A standard safety drill.

**[SYSTEM ALERT: INVALID LANDING ZONE.]**

The void was not a floor. It was a gap in the code.

If he hit the bottom, he would not die, and he would simply cease to exist in this instance.

His Core would be reset. Or worse, deleted.

He needed a surface.

Gerald opened his eyes. The grey polygons were rushing toward him. They were jagged.

Sharp. Illegal.

He reached out with his mind, not for mana, not for combat, but for *authority*. He reached for the concept of *Flooring*.

He did not try to create a floor, and he did not have the mana for construction. He did not have the level for creation. He had something else.

He had the right to inspect.

*If there is no floor,* Gerald thought, *then where is the egress?*

He pushed the thought outward. A ripple of intent, grey and dusty, spread from his Core.

**[QUERY: WHERE IS THE EGRESS FOR THIS ZONE?]**

The System paused. The falling slowed. Just a fraction. A stutter in the framerate.

Gerald felt the resistance. The System was looking for an answer. And because the room had been condemned, because the structure had failed, the System had to re-evaluate the path of least resistance.

*There is no egress,* Gerald projected. *the fall is illegal.*

It was a semantic trick. A lawyer's move. A bureaucrat's nightmare.

The grey void shuddered. The polygons froze. The wind stopped screaming.

Gerald hung suspended in the air, surrounded by static.

**[ERROR: PATHFINDING FAILED.]** **[RECALCULATING TRAJECTORY.]**

The void shifted. The grey polygons rearranged themselves, not into a floor, but into a ramp. A steep, jagged incline leading sideways, into a dark tunnel that had not existed a second ago.

Gerald slid down the ramp. It was rough.

It scraped against his Core's outer shell, sending sparks of blue light into the darkness.

He did not scream. He counted the seconds. One.

Two. Three.

The ramp ended abruptly, and he tumbled forward, rolling across a surface that was cold, hard, and mercifully solid.

He stopped.

Gerald lay still for three seconds. He checked his integrity. 98%. Minor abrasions. Acceptable.

He sat up, and the tunnel was narrow.

Stone walls, slick with condensation.

A single torch flickered on the wall, burning with a low, oily flame. The smell was damp.

Earthy. Real.

He stood up. His legs shook. He waited for the shaking to stop. It did.

Before him, the tunnel curved to the right.

And there, blocking the path, was a door, which was a heavy iron door, rusted at the hinges. It had no handle. It had no lock. It had a placard.

Gerald walked toward it. His steps echoed. *Clack. Clack. Clack.*

He read the placard.

**[ZONE: ARCHIVE OF ABANDONED PROTOCOLS.]** **[ACCESS: RESTRICTED.]** **[ENTRY REQUIRES: CLEARANCE LEVEL 5 OR HIGHER.]**

Gerald's Clearance Level was 1, and he looked at the door. He looked at his hands.

He had no tools.

He had no weapons. He had only his eyes, and thirty-one years of knowing exactly which rules were written in blood, and which were written in ink.

He stepped closer. The air grew colder. The torch flame turned blue.

A voice echoed from behind the door. It was smooth. Cultured. Amused.

"You're persistent," the voice said. "I like that in a man. Or a Core. It's hard to tell with you."

Gerald stopped.

"Who are you?"

"Inspector General Vane," the voice said. "But you can call me The Auditor."

The handle of the door twisted, though there was no handle. The metal groaned. The door swung inward.

Behind it stood a figure. It was tall, draped in robes of black paper that rustled like dry leaves. Its face was blank, save for two slots where eyes should be, filled with scrolling green text.

Gerald felt a chill that had nothing to do with the damp air. This was not a spawn. This was not a rival Core looking to expand its territory. This was the System's own enforcement arm.

Vane stepped forward. He did not walk; he glided.

"You cited the Mimic," Vane said.

He tilted his head.

"You found a structural vulnerability in a simulation.

Clever.

Very clever. But you broke the room. You broke the instance.

"

"I cited the violation," Gerald said. His voice was steady.

He kept his hands at his sides, and he did not fidget.

Fidgeting showed weakness.

"Violations are punished," Vane said. "Not rewarded."

"Citations are tools," Gerald corrected. "Not rewards."

Vane's green text eyes flashed red.

"You are Level 1, Core Hutchins. You have no combat skills.

No offensive spells. You have three mold spores and a rat.

And you are in my jurisdiction.

This Archive is not a place you belong.

"

"I am following the path of least resistance," Gerald said. "The System redirected me here."

"The System redirects errors," Vane said. "You are an error."

Vane raised a hand. The air in the corridor thickened. The torches died.

"I am going to delete you," Vane said.

"Not for breaking the rules. But for using them in a way I did not anticipate.

That is a deviation.

Deviations are purged.

"

Gerald looked at the darkness. He could not fight Vane.

He knew this.

His stats were abysmal.

His HP was low. If Vane struck, Gerald would be wiped.

But Gerald was not a fighter. He was an inspector.

And an inspector does not need to win a fight. He needs to find the one thing that makes the fight illegal.

Gerald looked around the corridor. He looked at Vane. He looked at the walls.

The walls were lined with shelves. Thousands of them. Filled with scrolls.

With books. With bound ledgers.

Gerald's eyes narrowed.

*Fire Code,* he thought. *Section 4.2: Combustible Materials in Confined Spaces.*

"Wait," Gerald said.

Vane paused. The green text in his eyes stopped scrolling.

"Why should I wait?"

"Because," Gerald said, pointing to the nearest shelf, "you have placed Class A combustibles within three feet of an open flame source. The torch was open flame. It is now extinguished, but the residual heat signature remains. And more importantly, the ventilation in this corridor is insufficient to disperse smoke in the event of a flashover."

Vane stared at him.

"You are citing me?"

"I am noting a hazard," Gerald said. "If you delete me here, in this corridor, you are executing a termination in a space that has not passed fire safety inspection. You are creating a liability."

Vane's robes rustled. A low hum builded in the air.

"You are bluffing," Vane said.

"I am a building inspector," Gerald said. "I do not bluff. I measure."

Gerald closed his eyes, and he reached out with his mind, not to attack, but to *inspect*. He focused on the air quality. He focused on the distance between the shelves.

He focused on the material composition of the scrolls.

**[SYSTEM ALERT: HAZARD DETECTED.]** **[VIOLATION: IMPROPER STORAGE OF COMBUSTIBLE MATERIALS.]** **[ZONE: ARCHIVE OF ABANDONED PROTOCOLS.]** **[SEVERITY: CRITICAL.]**

The text flashed in Gerald's vision. But this time, it was not directed at him. It was directed at the environment.

Vane flinched. He looked at the shelves.

The System was reacting. The air in the corridor thickened, not with magic, but with *compliance enforcement*.

The walls groaned.

The shelves vibrated. The scrolls slid down, blocking the exit.

"What have you done?" Vane hissed.

"I have filed a report," Gerald said.

The vibration grew louder. The ceiling cracked. Dust fell from above.

If the Archive collapsed, the Inspector General would be trapped. Buried. Not deleted. *Buried.*

Vane's eyes widened. The green text swirled frantically.

"You are trapping yourself," Vane shouted.

"I am citing the environment," Gerald said. "The citation applies to the zone. Not the occupant. The occupant is subject to the zone's compliance rating."

Vane looked at the ceiling. A large slab of stone was loosening.

If the Archive fell, Vane would take massive damage. His Core, tethered to this location, would suffer structural integrity loss.

Vane looked at Gerald. For the first time, the blank face showed something like confusion.

"Undo it," Vane said.

"I cannot," Gerald said. "The citation is filed. It is pending review."

"Review?" Vane spat.

"The Inspector General is auditing the Archive," Gerald said. "And you, Inspector General Vane, are currently in violation of Section 12.4: Negligent Maintenance of Historical Records."

The ceiling cracked louder. A chunk of stone the size of a car broke free and fell, smashing into the floor between them.

Vane jumped back.

Gerald stood his ground. He watched the stone hit. He watched the dust cloud.

He had won. Not by fighting. But by making the room unsafe for his enemy.

Vane looked at the falling debris. He looked at Gerald.

"Fine," Vane said. His voice was cold. "We will adjourn."

The air around Vane shimmered. He faded, teleporting away.

But as he faded, a single item dropped from his robes. It clattered to the stone floor.

Vane vanished.

Gerald stood alone in the collapsing corridor, and he walked over to the item.

It was a badge. Black iron. Heavy.

He picked it up.

**[ITEM RECEIVED: TEMPORARY AUDITOR CLEARANCE (EXPIRED)]**

Gerald frowned. Why would Vane drop a badge?

He looked at the badge closer. It was warm.

Then, a new notification flashed. Not red. Not blue. Gold.

**[SYSTEM ALERT: COUNTER-CITATION FILED.]** **[SOURCE: INSPECTOR GENERAL VANE.]** **[TARGET: GERALD HUTCHINS.]** **[CHARGE: UNAUTHORIZED ALTERATION OF SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE.]** **[PENALTY: TOTAL CORE DELETION.]** **[EXECUTION: IMMEDIATE.]**

The floor beneath Gerald's feet glowed.

Not blue.

White.

The white light spread from his boots, moving up his legs. His body dissolved.

Not into dust.

Into light.

Into nothing.

Vane had not fled. Vane had triggered the ultimate penalty.

Gerald looked at his hands. They were fading.

He looked at the badge in his other hand.

The badge remained. Solid. Heavy.

Why did the badge remain?

Because the badge was not part of him. It was part of the *office*.

And the office could not be deleted.

The white light consumed his torso. His legs. His head.

Gerald closed his eyes, and he did not feel fear. He felt a profound, bureaucratic irritation.

*If you are going to delete me,* he thought, *you must serve the notice in person.*

The light consumed him.

Silence.

Then, the white light stopped.

Gerald opened his eyes, and he was still standing.

But he was no longer in the corridor.

He was in a small, grey room. A desk sat in the center. A chair sat behind it.

On the desk was a pen.

And on the pen was a name.

**[AUDITOR: GERALD HUTCHINS.]**

Gerald looked at the pen. He looked at the door. The door was locked.

But on the desk, a new file had appeared, which was open.

The first page read:

**[CITATION #0001: FAILURE TO MAINTAIN PERSONAL INTEGRITY.]** **[DEFENDANT: GERALD HUTCHINS.]** **[NEXT STEP: MANDATORY HEARING.]**

Gerald picked up the pen.

His hand did not shake, and he uncapped the pen.

He wrote his defense.
