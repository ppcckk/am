Chapter 362: Sabotage Compliance

# Chapter 362

The stone hand did not strike. It pointed.

The clipboard clattered against the cavern floor, cracking a stalactite on its way down. Gerald stared at the object.

It was slate.

Standard municipal grade. The paper attached to it was not parchment, but a thick, fibrous sheet that smelled of ozone and old blood.

Gerald's fingers twitched. He wanted to pick it up. He wanted to check the stamp. He wanted to verify the issuing authority.

The hand retracted into the earth. The ground sealed itself with the speed of healing flesh.

"Did you see that?" the Necromancer asked. His voice was a dry rasp. He did not look at Gerald. He looked at the hole where the hand had been.

"I saw it," Gerald said. He kept his voice level. Professional. "It was a summons."

"It was a threat," the dwarf corrected.

He gripped his pickaxe with white-knuckled desperation.

"Do you not understand what you have done, Inspector?

You have tethered your existence to mine.

If I fail, you cease to exist. If I succeed, you inherit my debt.

"

"The debt is manageable," Gerald said. He walked to the edge of the excavation site.

The mud here was viscous, black, and smelled of sulfur.

"The issue is the angle.

You are digging vertically. That creates a columnar collapse risk.

If the support beams are not angled at fifteen degrees, the ceiling will not hold the weight of the mana conduit.

"

The Necromancer laughed. It was a wet, hacking sound.

"You speak of beams.

I speak of souls. The ceiling is made of bone and will.

"

"Bone is organic material.

It has a tensile strength rating," Gerald said. He reached into the air.

The System interface flickered into existence before his eyes, which was a translucent blue rectangle, hovering over the muddy trench.

**System Notification: Structural Integrity Check Initiated.** **Target: Necro-Vault-7 Primary Shaft.** **Current Status: Non-Compliant.** **Violation: Lack of Lateral Support Bracing.** **Recommended Action: Install Cross-Beams (Material: Obsidian or reinforced Bone).**

"See?" Gerald said. "The System agrees.

Bone has a high compressive strength but poor shear resistance.

When the mana flows through this shaft, the vibration will shear the walls.

You will not just fail the excavation. You will trigger a Category 4 Structural Collapse.

"

The Necromancer stopped digging. He looked at the glowing text. His eyes narrowed. The purple glow of his magic dimmed for a second, then flared.

"You are insufferable," the Necromancer said.

"I am necessary," Gerald replied. "You have a deadline. The Inspector General is auditing this sector.

If this shaft collapses during the audit, your Compliance Rating drops to zero. Deletion is immediate.

My liability agreement prevents my deletion, but it does not prevent my paralysis.

I will be frozen in a state of bureaucratic limbo for the remainder of the System's uptime.

"

The dwarf spat on the ground.

"You think I care about your limbo?"

"I think you care about your mana pool," Gerald said.

"Shared liability means shared resources. If the shaft fails, the mana backfires.

It will drain your reserves dry.

You will be weak. You will be a target.

"

The Necromancer looked at the hole. He looked at the clipboard, still lying in the mud. Then he looked at Gerald. The hatred in the dwarf's eyes was replaced by a grudging, reluctant calculation.

"Where do you want the bracing?"

Gerald pointed to a protruding ledge of rock on the left wall.

"There.

And there." He pointed to a similar ledge on the right.

"Connect them with a beam.

Use the obsidian shards from the floor. They are hard.

They do not rot.

"

The Necromancer stood. He walked to the ledge.

He raised his hand.

Purple light coalesced around his fingers. He pulled a shard of black glass from the rock wall. It hummed with dark energy.

"This will take time," the dwarf said.

"Time is the only resource we have," Gerald said. "Start."

The Necromancer worked, and he shaped the obsidian with magic, fusing it into a beam. The sound was like grinding teeth. *Screech.

Crunch.

Hiss.*

Gerald watched. He did not help physically.

He had no strength, and he had no magic.

He had only his eye. He scanned the geometry of the shaft.

He checked the angles. He noted the depth of the cuts.

**System Notification: Partial Compliance Achieved.** **Structural Rating Increased by 2 points.** **Current Rating: 49/100.**

It was a small gain. It was not enough.

"Angle is wrong," Gerald said. "The left beam is dipping. Fix it."

The Necromancer paused. He adjusted the beam.

"Better?"

"Acceptable," Gerald said.

They worked for an hour. The light in the cavern did not change.

The mana conduit above them pulsed, a slow, rhythmic throb that matched Gerald's heartbeat.

The vibration in the ground grew stronger. It was not just an excavation anymore. It was a response.

Something down there was waking up.

Or something was coming to inspect them.

Gerald felt the shift in the air pressure. It was subtle.

A drop in humidity.

A cooling of the skin. The System's ambient temperature regulation was kicking in, trying to stabilize the environment.

"The ventilation is failing," Gerald said.

"I am a Necromancer," the dwarf said. "I do not need air."

"The System does," Gerald said. "Your minions do.

The rats in your upper levels.

The ghouls in the crypt.

If the air quality drops, their regeneration rates fall. They become brittle.

They break easily. And if they break, your dungeon's population count drops. Your rating drops further.

"

The Necromancer slammed his pickaxe into the ground.

"What do you want from me, Hutchins?

I am digging a hole.

You are counting beams. What is the connection?

"

"The connection is the audit," Gerald said. He looked at the stone hand's clipboard, still in the mud. He walked over to it. He picked it up.

It was heavy. The slate was cold.

He read the text.

**CITATION NO. 7742-B** **ISSUED TO: NECRO-VAULT-7** **VIOLATION: Unauthorized Excavation of Sub-Basement Zone 4 (Geothermal Ventilation Shaft).** **ACTION REQUIRED: Cease excavation immediately. Submit Plan B: Surface Ventilation Retrofit.** **PENALTY FOR NON-COMPLIANCE: Immediate Mana Throttle (50% reduction in output).**

Gerald's heart sank. A Mana Throttle.

"That is a death sentence," Gerald said.

He looked up at the Necromancer.

"If they throttle your mana, you cannot maintain the bone walls.

They will crumble.

The shaft will collapse. We die.

"

The Necromancer's face went pale. Even for a dwarf, the pallor was extreme.

"Then we must finish."

"We cannot," Gerald said. "The citation is already active.

The System is watching.

If we continue, the penalty triggers instantly.

We have to stop.

"

"Stop?" The dwarf roared. "We are three feet from the reservoir! If we stop now, the pressure builds. It will explode!"

"The System does not care about pressure," Gerald said. "It cares about code."

Gerald looked at the clipboard. Then he looked at the System interface. He looked at the Shared Liability Agreement.

**Shared Liability Agreement: Active.** **Joint Responsibility for Compliance.** **Appeal Window: 00:04:59 remaining.**

Four minutes, and he had four minutes to appeal a citation that was currently being enforced by an invisible, omnipotent bureaucracy.

Gerald closed his eyes. He breathed in the stale, sulfur-scented air. He thought of his old office.

The smell of coffee. The stack of forms.

The fear of the mayor.

The fear of the budget cuts, which was the same. Just different stakes.

He opened his eyes. He turned to the Necromancer.

"Stop digging," Gerald said.

The dwarf stared at him.

"Are you insane?"

"Stop. Digging." Gerald's voice was flat. Commanding. It was the voice he used when a contractor tried to cut corners on a sewer line.

The Necromancer flinched. He lowered his pickaxe.

Gerald stepped forward. He raised the clipboard. He held it high.

**System Notification: Appeal Initiated.** **Appellant: Gerald Hutchins (Core Designation: LIB-01).** **Defendant: Necro-Vault-7.** **Reason for Appeal: Jurisdictional Error.**

The air in the cavern froze. The dust motes stopped moving. The light dimmed.

A new window opened. It was larger than the others. It spanned the width of the cavern.

**HEARING SESSION 1: JURISDICTIONAL DISPUTE.** **ARGUMENT REQUIRED.**

The Necromancer looked at the window. He looked at Gerald.

"What are you doing?"

"Proving that Zone 4 is not yours," Gerald said. He spoke. His voice echoed in the dead silence.

"Zone 4 of the Ashford Sub-Basement Complex was originally surveyed by the City of Ashford Engineering Department in 1994.

The survey maps designate this area as 'Public Utility Infrastructure, Geothermal Exchange.' It is not private dungeon territory. It is municipal land.

Necro-Vault-7 has no legal claim to Zone 4.

Therefore, the citation is invalid. The excavation is not unauthorized. It is a maintenance necessity for public safety.

"

Gerald pointed at the stone hand's mark on the ground.

"The entity that issued this citation was a mimic disguised as a compliance officer.

Mimics are classified under Biological Nuisance, not Regulatory Authority.

A mimic cannot issue a legal citation.

The document is void.

"

He was lying. Partially.

The System did not care about 1994 city maps. The System cared about logic.

But Gerald knew the System's logic. It was bureaucratic. It was rigid. It hated ambiguity.

If he could prove that the *issuer* was invalid, the *citation* was invalid.

The System window flickered. Red text appeared.

**Analyzing Jurisdictional Claim...** **Checking Survey Records...** **Checking Mimic Classification...**

Gerald held his breath. He did not move. He did not blink. He kept his hand steady on the clipboard.

The Necromancer watched him. The dwarf's hand tightened on his pickaxe. He looked at the shaft.

The mana was beginning to boil. The rocks were shaking.

"If this fails," the Necromancer whispered, "I will eat your heart."

"It will work," Gerald said.

He did not know if it would work. He felt his stomach churning. The familiar nausea of uncertainty rose in his throat.

He suppressed it, and he focused on the text.

**Analysis Complete.**

The text turned green.

**Ruling: Jurisdictional Error Confirmed.** **Citation Voided.** **Penalty Reversed.** **Bonus for Successful Appeal: +10 Compliance Rating to both parties.**

The light returned. The dust motes danced. The vibration in the ground ceased.

The Necromancer let out a breath that sounded like a sigh. He slumped against the rock wall.

"You... you actually did it."

Gerald lowered the clipboard. His hands were shaking. He hid them in his sleeves.

"It was a procedural loophole," Gerald said.

"The System recognizes municipal survey data as a higher tier of truth than Core-generated land claims. It is a bug. Or a feature.

I do not know."

"We are safe?" the dwarf asked.

"For now," Gerald said.

"But the Inspector General will notice.

The mimic was a scout. If we void the citation, we expose ourselves as non-compliant in other ways.

We have to clean up this mess before they come back.

"

Gerald looked at the shaft. It was deep. Dangerous. But it was stable.

"We need to install the ventilation shaft," Gerald said. "Public utility code requires a minimum airflow rate of 15 cubic feet per minute for sub-basement levels. Your current airflow is zero. That is a Class A violation."

The Necromancer groaned.

"I cannot build a fan."

"You do not need a fan," Gerald said. He pointed to a crack in the ceiling, high above the mana conduit.

"You need a chimney effect.

Hot air rises. If you create a vertical channel from here to the surface, the pressure difference will pull air through. It is passive.

It is free.

And it is compliant.

"

The dwarf looked up, and he looked at the crack. He looked at Gerald. A strange expression crossed his face.

Respect? Confusion?

"You are a monster," the Necromancer said.

"I am an inspector," Gerald said. "Dig the chimney. I will measure the diameter."

The Necromancer nodded. He dug again. Faster now.

Gerald stood back, and he watched the dwarf work.

He felt a small surge of pride. He had saved them.

He had used the rules. He had beaten the System at its own game.

Then, a new notification pinged, which was not in the corner. It was in the center of his vision. It was large. It was red.

**System Notification: High-Priority Audit Incoming.** **Auditor: Inspector General 'Vex'** **Target: LIB-01 (Gerald Hutchins)** **Reason: Anomalous Appeal Success Rate (100%).** **Status: En Route.** **ETA: 10 Minutes.**

Gerald froze.

Vex.

He had heard of Vex. Every Core in the city knew of Vex. The Auditor who did not care about rules, but about *pattern recognition*. The Auditor who deleted Cores for being too smart.

Gerald looked at the Necromancer. The dwarf was digging, oblivious.

Gerald looked at his own dungeon map in his mind. His sub-basement.

The library above.

The confused rat. The water leak. The missing handrail.

He had ten minutes to prepare for an audit by the most dangerous entity in the System.

He did not have a plan. He did not have a weapon.

He had a clipboard, and he had a connection.

He had a liability.

And he had a deadline.

Gerald walked to the edge of the shaft. He looked down into the dark. He felt the cold mana rising. He felt the weight of the library above him.

He turned to the Necromancer.

"Stop," Gerald said.

The dwarf looked up.

"What now?"

"We need to hide," Gerald said. "Not the dungeon. The evidence."

"The evidence of what?"

Gerald looked at the voided citation. He looked at the green text fading in the air.

"The evidence that I can win," Gerald said. "If Vex sees that I can appeal, he will not just audit me. He will erase me."

The Necromancer's eyes widened.

"Then what do we do?"

Gerald took a deep breath. The air was still stale. But it was moving.

"We make it look like we didn't win," Gerald said. "We make it look like we failed."

He reached into his core, and he pulled out a thread. Not a link.

A tear.

A flaw.

He sabotaged his own compliance.
