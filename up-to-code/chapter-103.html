Chapter 103: Signed in Stone

# Chapter 103 The liability insurance clause was forty pages long, printed in a font so small it required a magnifying glass that Gerald did not possess.

He did not have the magnifying glass. He did not have the time.

The bone-armored contractor stood in the center of his main chamber, blocking the only viable egress route.

Behind him, the mist churned, thick and gray, pressing against the newly stabilized limestone walls Gerald had just forced into compliance. The rat in his backpack shivered, a tiny vibration against his spine.

"Liability," Gerald said.

His voice was steady, though his hands were not. He gripped the clipboard so hard the plastic clip cracked.

"You are claiming liability for what?

The void? The mist? Or the fact that I am currently standing in a non-compliant zone due to your unauthorized presence?"

The contractor smiled. It was a wet, unpleasant expression, visible through the gap in the bone helm.

"We claim liability for the outcome, Mr.

Hutchins.

If the structure collapses because of your negligence, the Guild is not responsible for the cleanup. Nor for the adventurer deaths that will result."

Gerald looked at the contractor’s chest. The purple runes pulsed. They were intricate, woven into the armor plates like circuitry.

Gerald recognized the pattern. It was a sub-routine.

A specific code block used by high-level Core entities to bypass standard inspection protocols.

"You’re not a partner," Gerald said. "You’re a loophole."

The contractor’s smile vanished. "Careful. Accusations of fraud require evidence.

And you have no evidence.

You have a rat."

"I have a clipboard," Gerald corrected.

He stepped forward.

The floor beneath him groaned. The mist tried to slip through the cracks in the stone he had cemented shut an hour ago. It failed.

The *Reclassify* command held. The mist was now technically a "load-bearing atmospheric phenomenon," a designation that required it to maintain structural integrity under tension.

Gerald knew this because he had just written the definition. He had abused the *Reclassify* tab, dragging the "Air" property from the inventory and merging it with "Solid Rock." It was a dirty trick. It violated three subsections of the Material Mixing Bylaws. But the System, in its infinite bureaucratic blindness, had accepted the merge as a "temporary stabilization measure."

Now, he needed to exploit that acceptance.

"What is your permit number?" Gerald asked.

The contractor blinked. The bone armor shifted, clicking like dry joints. "Permit number?"

"For this entity," Gerald said, pointing a pen at the contractor.

"You are a spawn.

You are a biological and magical construct existing within my dungeon boundaries. You have not registered with the local Core Authority.

You have not filed Form 11-B. You are, in technical terms, an unlicensed biological entity."

The contractor laughed. It was a harsh, grinding sound. "I am a Guild Representative. I am exempt from local spawn registration under the Mutual Defense Pact."

"Pact?" Gerald flipped through his mental index. Thirty-one years of municipal code.

He knew the Mutual Defense Pact, which was a treaty signed by the major city dungeons to share defense costs against high-level threats. It was also, as Gerald recalled from a footnote in Section 4.4.2, explicitly void if the host dungeon was undergoing active construction without a valid Building Permit.

And Gerald did not have a Building Permit.

He had a *Structural Deficiency Report*. Which was, legally, the precursor to a permit. But it was not the permit itself.

"Show me the contract," Gerald said.

The contractor stepped closer.

The mist recoiled from his bone armor, hissing. "You want to see the contract?

Fine.

But if you cite me, I will invoke the Arbitration Clause. Your dungeon will be locked. No spawns.

No mana flow. No growth.

You will starve."

"Then you better hope I don’t find a violation," Gerald said.

He didn’t look at the contractor. He looked at the floor.

The contractor stood on a tile.

A single, cracked tile near the entrance to the secondary chamber. Gerald had noticed it during his initial sweep.

It was uneven. A tripping hazard. Under standard municipal safety codes, any surface with a gradient exceeding five degrees in an exit pathway required a warning sign.

Gerald didn’t have a warning sign.

But he had a *Citation*, and he had saved one use. One single, high-severity Citation, reserved for the Inspector General audit.

He had been saving it because he thought it was for emergencies.

This was an emergency. Not for his life, but for his dungeon’s survival.

Gerald raised his pen. He did not aim at the contractor. He aimed at the floor beneath the contractor’s feet.

"Violation," Gerald said. His voice was flat, monotone, the voice of a man who had denied a thousand renovations for missing smoke detectors. "Failure to maintain slip-resistant surfaces in primary egress routes. Severity: High."

The System pinged. A red holographic overlay appeared in the air.

[CITATION ISSUED] [TARGET: DUNGEON CORE (UNNAMED)] [VIOLATION: SLIP HAZARD (EGRESS NON-COMPLIANCE)] [DEBUFF: +25% STUN DURATION ON MOVEMENT] [STATUS: APPEAL WINDOW OPEN]

The contractor froze.

The bone armor rattled as his muscles locked, and he took a step forward, and his foot slipped on the uneven tile.

He stumbled, catching himself on the wall.

The purple runes on his chest flickered.

"That’s a minor violation," the contractor spat, his voice strained. "A five-degree gradient? That’s within tolerance."

"According to your Guild’s internal safety guidelines, maybe," Gerald said. "But according to the *Ashford Municipal Code*, which the System adopts as the default legal framework for all registered Cores, the tolerance is three degrees. I measured it. It’s four."

The contractor’s face went pale behind the bone. "You measured it?"

"I have a laser measure in my inventory," Gerald said. It was a lie.

He had used his own eye, and he had calibrated his eye against the standard doorframe of the library above.

He knew exactly how wide a door was. He knew exactly how much a floor could slope before he felt it in his knees.

"You’re cheating," the contractor said. "You’re using local code to enforce a global System rule."

"The System *is* local code," Gerald said. "That’s the whole point. You’re a guest in my building. You follow my rules, or you get evicted."

The contractor roared. He raised a gauntleted hand, gathering purple energy. A spell formed, swirling around his fingers. It was a high-tier attack, designed to bypass physical damage and strike directly at the Core’s mana pool.

Gerald didn’t flinch. He didn’t dodge. He waited.

The contractor lunged. His foot planted on the sloped tile.

The debuff hit.

+25% Stun Duration.

The contractor’s momentum faltered. His foot slid backward. His arms windmilled. The spell fizzled, dissipating into harmless sparks.

Gerald stepped forward. He didn’t attack. He didn’t need to. He just walked to the edge of the void, where the mist still churned, and held out his hand.

"Get out," Gerald said. "And take your partner’s name with you. I’ll be filing a complaint with the Guild’s regulatory board."

The contractor scrambled to his feet, glaring at Gerald with pure hatred. The purple runes on his chest dimmed, and he looked at the mist, then at Gerald, then at the cracked tile.

"This isn’t over, Hutchins," the contractor growled. "The Inspector General will hear of this. Your Compliance Rating is garbage. You’re barely holding it together."

"My rating is my business," Gerald said.

The contractor stepped back into the mist. The fog swallowed him, swirling violently before settling. The pressure in the room eased. The rat in Gerald’s backpack stopped shaking.

Gerald exhaled. He looked at his mana bar. It was low.

The *Reclassify* merge was draining it by the second. The citation had cost him 500 mana.

He was running on fumes, and he looked at the wall he had stabilized. It was solid. For now.

But the contractor had said *partner*.

Gerald opened his System menu. He navigated to the *Partnerships* tab. It was empty.

He had never invited anyone, and he had never agreed to anything.

So where did the contract come from?

He scrolled down. A grayed-out line item sat below the empty slots.

[PENDING INVITATION: GUILD CONTRACTOR (AUTO-ACCEPTED VIA LIABILITY WAIVER)]

Gerald’s blood went cold. He had signed it.

Not on paper.

In the fine print of the *Reclassify* command. When he had forced the mist to become solid, he had technically created a new structural element. The System had offered him a "Professional Management Option" to help maintain it.

He had ignored the pop-up. He had clicked "Dismiss."

But he hadn’t read the terms.

And the terms said: *By dismissing the management assistant, the Core accepts liability for all structural maintenance, including the cost of external contractors hired to mitigate risks.*

The contractor wasn’t an enemy. He was a *vendor*. And Gerald had hired him.

Gerald looked at the inventory. He searched for the contractor’s name. It appeared under *Services Rendered*.

Cost: 50% of all future Mana Generation.

Gerald stared at the number.

Fifty percent.

Half of his income. Gone.

Before he even earned it, and he felt sick. Not the usual sickness of rule-breaking. This was different. This was the sickness of a man who had followed the rules, played by the book, and still lost everything because he hadn’t read the footnote.

He looked at the rat. The rat squeaked, demanding food.

Gerald sat down on the floor. He pulled out his clipboard. He began to write.

He needed to appeal the contract.

To do that, he needed to prove the contract was invalid.

To do that, he needed to prove that the "Structural Maintenance" was not, in fact, required by the System.

Which meant he had to prove that the mist was not a structure.

Which meant he had to un-stabilize it.

Which meant he had to let the void eat him.

Gerald looked at the void. The mist curled toward him, hungry.

He looked at his mana bar. 12%, and he looked at the Contractor’s entry in the log.

He looked at the *Hearing* protocol.

He had one chance. He could challenge the validity of the partnership.

He could force a Hearing.

But in a Hearing, he had to argue the structural logic. He had to convince a System Judge that the mist was not a load-bearing element, but merely an atmospheric hazard.

If he won, he could dissolve the mist, void the contract, and save his mana income.

If he lost, the contract would become permanent. And he would starve.

Gerald opened his inventory. He selected *Limestone Dust*. He selected *Water*. He selected *Unstable Gravel*.

He dragged them all into the *Reclassify* tab, and he did not try to make them solid.

He tried to make them *temporary*.

He typed a new command.

*DEFINE: MIST = NON-PERMANENT ATMOSPHERIC ANOMALY*

The System paused.

[WARNING: THIS ACTION MAY RESULT IN INSTANT COLLAPSE OF STABILIZED STRUCTURES] [WARNING: LIABILITY WAIVER MAY BE VOID] [CONFIRM?]

Gerald’s finger hovered over the button.

The rat squeaked.

The mist pressed against the walls, cracking the stone he had just cemented.

Gerald pressed confirm.

The walls shattered.
