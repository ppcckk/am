Chapter 174: Jurisdictional Error

# Chapter 174

Gerald wrote.

The pen scratched against the damp concrete, a sound like a knife dragging across bone.

Three minutes.

The System's notification pulsed in his peripheral vision, a crimson eye refusing to blink. **AUDIT INITIATED. SUB-CODE 7.2: EGRESS OBSTRUCTION. PENDING REVIEW.**

He didn't look at the eye. He looked at the ink.

The self-inflicted citation was a trap. A deliberate, calculated error in the ledger.

If the Inspector General arrived and found a locked door, the audit would halt.

The auditor would have to verify the violation before assessing the structural integrity of the room. That verification took time. Time was the only currency Gerald had left.

He needed to prove the lock was not a barrier to escape, but a temporary administrative hold.

He needed paperwork.

Gerald's dungeon core interface floated in the air, translucent and ghostly. He dragged a blank Form 11-C, *Temporary Access Modification*, from the virtual stack. It hovered above the wet floor, waiting for input.

His hand shook. The pen tip wavered.

He steadied it by pressing his wrist against the cold stone of the wall.

*Reason for lock,* he wrote. *Prevention of unauthorized entry during active fluid displacement event.*

He underlined *fluid displacement*.

Then he added: *Reference Incident Report 44-B. Water Table Surge. Structural Compromise.*

This was the lie.

The truth was that the water table hadn't surged. The adventurers' weight had compressed the sub-basement's foundation, narrowing the doorframe by two inches.

But the System didn't care about truth.

It cared about consistency. If the paperwork said *water*, the System would look for water.

And Gerald had plenty of water.

He walked back to the leak. The bucket was full.

The mop was dripping.

He dumped the gray sludge onto the floor near the door.

It pooled, spreading into the cracks of the concrete.

The System's eye flickered.

**DETECTING FLUID. ANALYZING COMPOSITION.**

Gerald held his breath.

If the water was clean, the lie failed. Clean water didn't cause structural displacement. It just made things slippery.

He needed it to look like a leak. Like a breach.

He kicked a pile of wet newspaper from the corner of the room into the puddle. The ink bled into the gray water, turning it a muddy, slurry brown.

**FLUID ANALYSIS: CONTAMINATED. SEDIMENT DETECTED.**

The crimson eye softened. It didn't disappear, but the pulsing slowed.

**CITATION PRELIMINARY REVIEW: PENDING.**

Gerald exhaled. His ribs ached.

He picked up his pen again.

Now came the hard part. The argument.

The Inspector General wasn't a person.

It was a protocol. A set of if/then statements wrapped in divine bureaucracy. To win the audit, Gerald had to satisfy every conditional check.

He had to prove that the *intent* of the code was met, even if the *letter* was violated.

He opened a new document. Form 99-A, *Waiver Request*.

*Waiver requested for Section 7, Subsection 2,* he wrote. *Justification: Hazardous conditions outweigh egress requirements. Immediate evacuation would result in slip-and-fall liability, increasing injury potential by 40%.*

He paused.

Liability.

That was the word. The System didn't care about heroism. It cared about risk management.

If the adventurers slipped on the water, the dungeon core was liable for their injuries. If the core blocked the exit to prevent them from slipping, the core was protecting its own asset value.

It was absurd. It was also legally sound.

Gerald signed the waiver with a flourish.

The System processed it.

**FORM 99-A RECEIVED.** **PROCESSING...**

A new notification appeared. Not crimson this time. Amber.

**NOTICE OF INQUIRY.** **INQUIRY: WHY WAS THE LEAK NOT REPAIRED PRIOR TO THE AUDIT?**

Gerald stared at the amber text.

He hadn't planned for this.

The System was asking why the hazard existed. It was asking why he hadn't fixed it.

If he said he couldn't, it meant he lacked the resources. That would lower his Compliance Rating. It would make him weak. Weak cores were easier to audit.

If he said he had fixed it, it contradicted the water on the floor.

Gerald looked at the water. Then he looked at the door.

He walked to the door. He unlocked it. The click was loud.

He opened it.

The library lights were dim. The silence of the books pressed against his eardrums.

He didn't step out. He stepped back.

He dragged a heavy oak chair from the corner of the room. It scraped against the concrete, a screech that echoed in the small space.

He placed the chair in front of the door.

Then he kicked a bucket of water onto the chair. The water splashed over the legs, soaking the wood.

He picked up his pen.

He wrote a new addendum to Form 11-C.

*Access Modification Revised: Egress route obstructed by temporary maintenance equipment (Chair) and active cleanup zone (Water). Barrier is not a lock. Barrier is a work zone.*

He signed it.

The System read the addendum.

**RE-EVALUATING CITATION.** **NEW STATUS: WORK ZONE VIOLATION (MINOR).** **PENALTY: 5 POINTS OFF COMPLIANCE RATING.**

Five points.

Gerald's Compliance Rating dropped from 47 to 42.

The debuffs hit him instantly. The air in the room grew thin. The regeneration rate of his dungeon entities slowed. He felt a tightness in his chest, a restriction of his core's mana flow.

But the Audit had stopped.

The crimson eye vanished.

The amber text faded.

**AUDIT SUSPENDED. REASON: CONFLICTING VIOLATIONS PENDING RESOLUTION.**

Gerald slumped against the wall. His legs gave out. He slid down until he hit the floor.

He wasn't saved. He was delayed.

The System had flagged the conflict. It would take time for the Inspector General to determine which violation took precedence: the blocked exit or the unsafe work zone.

He had bought himself perhaps an hour.

But the water was still on the floor.

He looked at the puddle. The muddy sludge was seeping into the concrete cracks.

Gerald realized something.

The water wasn't just a prop. It was a problem.

A real problem.

He looked at the notification log.

**WATER DAMAGE DETECTED. STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY COMPROMISED.**

His lie had become a truth. By dumping the water to fake a leak, he had actually caused a leak. The water was eating into the foundation.

If the foundation failed, the sub-basement collapsed.

If the sub-basement collapsed, Gerald died.

He stood up. His knees cracked.

He grabbed the mop.

He mopped.

He wrung it out.

He mopped again.

The bucket filled. The floor dried.

But the damage was done. The concrete was darker in that corner. A stain that wouldn't wash away.

Gerald looked at the stain.

Then he looked at the door.

A figure appeared in the library doorway.

It was a goblin. Small. Green. Wearing the tattered rags of a low-level spawn.

Gerald froze.

He hadn't summoned the goblin. He hadn't spawned it.

The goblin stepped into the dungeon.

It looked at the wet floor. It sniffed the air. Then it looked at Gerald.

The goblin didn't attack. It tilted its head.

**UNLICENSED ENTITY DETECTED.** **TYPE: GOBLIN SCOUT.** **ORIGIN: CORE ID 884 (THE GRIM HOLD).**

The Grim Hold. A rival dungeon three blocks away. Known for its labyrinthine confusion and aggressive ambush tactics.

Gerald raised his pen.

"Identify yourself," he said. His voice was flat. Professional.

The goblin chattered. It pointed at the puddle.

Gerald looked at the puddle.

**VIOLATION DETECTED: UNAUTHORIZED WASTE DISPOSAL.**

The System flagged the water as waste because it was outside a designated drainage area.

The goblin grinned. It pulled a piece of parchment from its rag.

It held the paper out.

Gerald took it.

It was a citation.

Not from the System. From the Grim Hold.

**CITATION FROM CORE 884.** **OFFENSE: ENVIRONMENTAL HAZARD.** **DESCRIPTION: FLUID OBSTRUCTION IN PUBLIC ACCESS ZONE.** **FINE: 10 MANA POINTS.**

Gerald stared at the paper.

A rival core could cite him?

No. That wasn't right. Only the System could issue citations.

He looked closer at the parchment.

The signature wasn't from the System. It was a forged stamp. A crude approximation of the Inspector General's seal.

The goblin laughed. A high, screeching sound.

It snapped its fingers.

The water on the floor boiled.

Gerald stumbled back. The steam rose, scalding and thick.

The goblin wasn't just a scout. It was a saboteur. It had used the citation as a distraction while it cast a heat spell on the water.

The steam obscured the room.

Gerald coughed. His eyes watered.

He couldn't see the goblin.

He couldn't see the door.

He reached for his pen.

He had to cite the goblin. He had to cite the heat.

**CITATION ISSUED: UNAUTHORIZED THERMAL DISCHARGE.** **TARGET: UNLICENSED ENTITY.** **PENALTY: STAT DEBUFF.**

The System processed the citation.

But it didn't apply the debuff.

**ERROR: TARGET OUT OF RANGE.**

The goblin had moved. Or it had cloaked itself in the steam.

Gerald spun around. He swung the mop like a staff. He hit nothing but air.

The steam was thickening. The temperature was rising.

His dungeon's ventilation was poor. The heat had nowhere to go.

The Compliance Rating dropped again.

**COMPLIANCE RATING: 40/100.** **DEBUFF: RESPIRATORY RATE REDUCED.**

Gerald gasped. His lungs burned.

He fell to his knees.

The goblin's voice came from everywhere.

"Code violation," it whispered.

Gerald crawled toward the door. His fingers scraped against the wet concrete.

He had to get to the threshold. He had to reopen the door.

If he opened the door, the steam would escape. The heat would vent.

But opening the door meant admitting the goblin had been inside. It meant admitting the security breach.

The System would fine him for the breach.

But if he didn't open the door, he would suffocate.

He reached the door. He grabbed the handle.

It was hot.

He pulled anyway.

The door swung open.

The steam rushed out into the library.

The goblin stepped through the steam, back into the library.

Gerald lay on the threshold, gasping.

The library lights flickered.

A new notification appeared. Not in his vision. On the library wall.

Projected in light.

**SYSTEM ALERT: CORE BREACH DETECTED.** **RESPONSIBLE CORE: GERALD HUTCHINS.** **ACTION: AUDIT RESUMED.** **SEVERITY: CRITICAL.**

Gerald looked up.

The Auditor was no longer waiting.

The Auditor was already here.

A figure stood in the library shadows. Tall. Robed. Faceless.

It held a clipboard.

Gerald tried to stand. He couldn't.

The figure walked toward the dungeon entrance.

Gerald looked at his pen. It was broken.

He looked at the water. It was drying.

He looked at the figure.

The figure stopped. It looked at Gerald.

It didn't speak. It just raised the clipboard.

**FORM 1: FINAL NOTICE.**

Gerald closed his eyes.

He thought about the water.

He thought about the lie.

He thought about the code.

And then, he did something he had never done in thirty-one years of service.

He smiled.

He picked up a shard of broken pen.

He wrote on the floor.

On the concrete.

In permanent marker.

He wrote a single word.

**APPEAL.**

The Auditor paused.

The System froze.

**APPEAL FILED.** **REASON: PROCEDURAL ERROR IN CITATION ISSUANCE.** **HEARING SCHEDULED: IMMEDIATE.**

Gerald's eyes snapped open.

He hadn't filed the appeal with the System.

He had filed it with the *wall*.

The library wall was part of the municipal building.

The municipal building was under the jurisdiction of the City of Ashford.

The City of Ashford was not under the jurisdiction of the Dungeon Core System.

Gerald had just sued the System in municipal court.

The Auditor stared at the word on the floor.

The System didn't know how to handle a jurisdictional conflict.

The lights in the library went out.

Total darkness.

A voice came from the dark. Not the Auditor.

A different voice. Older. Colder.

"You missed the comma," the voice said.

Gerald's heart stopped.

He hadn't missed a comma.

He had missed the *jurisdiction*.

And now, something else was awake in the library.
