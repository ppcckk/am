Chapter 53

The Hearing chamber was a flat grey plane. No walls, no ceiling, no floor that registered as solid. Gerald stood on it because the System told him he was standing on it. Core 6114 manifested as a column of black smoke roughly two meters tall, with a single red point where an eye would be if the Core had eyes. It did not have eyes. It had a Compliance Rating, which was 67, and it had a smugness that was not quantifiable.

The Hearing Arbiter was not a person. It was a floating number. The number was 0. It would change based on the quality of the arguments. Gerald had seen it happen before, in his own Hearing against Core 2201, the one where he'd proven the fire exits were non-compliant and the debuff had killed three elite spawns. The number had gone from 0 to 73 during that Hearing. Core 2201 had not appealed.

Gerald opened his brief.

"Core 1 acknowledges the column's cross-section is below current code minimum for a load-bearing element in a subterranean structure."

Core 6114's smoke column pulsed. The red point brightened. It had expected Gerald to argue the column was compliant. He was not arguing that.

"Core 1 does not contest the Citation's factual basis. The column is non-compliant under Section 4.2.1 of the Structural Code."

The number stayed at 0. Core 6114 shifted its mass to one side, a gesture that looked like a shrug.

Gerald continued. "Core 1 moves to dismiss the Citation on the grounds that enforcement would produce a greater violation than the one cited."

Core 6114 spoke. Its voice was not a voice. It was a pressure change in the grey plane, a vibration that Gerald's non-existent eardrums translated into words.

"That is not a valid dismissal ground. Section 11.3 permits appeal on factual error, procedural defect, or disproportionate penalty. You have not identified any of these."

Gerald pulled up the 1974 foundation plans. He had found them in the library's records office three days before the Citation, filed under a heading that read "Structural Additions, East Wing." He had not known why he'd pulled them until now. He'd pulled them because they were the only plans that showed the column's footings.

"Core 1 requests the Arbiter display the shared footing beneath the column in question."

The number flickered. It went from 0 to 3. Core 6114's smoke column went still.

The footing appeared. It was a rectangular mass of poured concrete, roughly 1.2 meters by 0.8 meters, buried beneath the column's base. Gerald zoomed the display. The footing extended beyond the column's footprint. It extended east. Into the grey plane's edge. Into a region that, when the System rendered it, showed up as a hatched pattern.

Hatched pattern meant: another Core's dungeon footprint.

Gerald looked at Core 6114. Core 6114 looked at the footing. Neither spoke for four seconds.

"Core 1 identifies a shared structural dependency," Gerald said. "The footing beneath the cited column extends 0.4 meters into Core 6114's registered dungeon footprint. The column's load is transferred through this footing into a shared foundation element. Removing the column would remove a load-bearing support that Core 6114's dungeon relies on for its own structural compliance."

The number jumped to 41.

Core 6114's smoke column contracted. The red point narrowed.

"That is not correct. The footing is within Core 1's registered footprint. It does not extend into mine."

Gerald pulled up Core 6114's registration document. He pulled up the 1974 foundation plans again. He overlaid them. The hatched pattern lined up with Core 6114's registered boundary to within 0.1 meters.

"Core 1 presents the overlay as evidence. The shared footing predates both Cores' registration. It was installed during the 1974 library renovation, before either Core existed. The System's registration process assigned footprints around existing structural elements. It did not sever them."

The number went to 58.

Core 6114 was moving now, its smoke swirling in a pattern that Gerald recognized as agitation. It had found the column. It had found the thin ceiling. It had built its Citation on the assumption that Gerald would have to defend the column's compliance, which he could not do. It had not expected Gerald to defend the column's necessity.

"Core 6114 moves to withdraw the Citation," the pressure-voice said.

Gerald felt something that might have been satisfaction if he had a face. He did not have a face. He had a Compliance Rating of 71 and a dungeon with a handrail that had caught a fifth spawn in the last hour. He had eleven minutes of debuff propagation remaining when the Hearing began. That time had elapsed. The debuff had reached the column. The column was now 18% weaker. It was still standing. But it was weaker, and the library above was still loading down through it, and the ceiling slab was still 0.9 meters thick where the plans said 2.1.

"Core 1 objects to withdrawal," Gerald said.

The number dropped to 52. Core 6114's red point flared.

"Core 1 objects on the grounds that the Citation has already propagated. The debuff is active. The column is weakened. Core 1 requires a formal ruling that establishes the shared dependency, because without that ruling, the next Citation against this column will not have the dependency on record."

The number went to 64.

Core 6114's smoke went flat against the grey plane. It was trying to think. Gerald could tell because it had stopped moving, which for a smoke column was the equivalent of a man putting his head in his hands.

"Core 1 further notes," Gerald said, "that if the Arbiter rules the dependency exists, the Citation is void ab initio. The debuff is reversed. The column returns to full strength. Core 6114 receives a Hearing penalty for citing a structural element that supports its own dungeon."

The number went to 79.

"Core 1 further notes that if the Arbiter rules the dependency does not exist, the Citation stands, the column is weakened, and Core 1 will file a Counter-Citation against Core 6114 for structural negligence. Core 6114's dungeon has been relying on a load-bearing element that it did not register, did not inspect, and did not maintain. That is a Section 4.7 violation. Unregistered structural dependency. Penalty: 25% debuff to all spawns, permanent until remediated."

The number went to 91.

Core 6114 spoke. Its voice was quieter now. "You would counter-cite me for my own Citation."

"I would counter-cite you for not knowing what was under your own floor."

The number went to 94.

The Arbiter's number stopped climbing. It held at 94 for six seconds. Then it flashed white and the grey plane dissolved.

Gerald was back in his dungeon. The column was back to full strength. The debuff was gone. The northeast wall stopped groaning. The handrail caught a sixth spawn and Gerald made a note to inspect the bracket mounting.

He had won. He had not argued the column was compliant. He had argued that removing it would be a worse violation than leaving it. He had used the System's own registration logic against a Core that had been trying to exploit a structural weakness.

He opened his citation log. He added Core 6114 to the list. Section 4.7, Unregistered Structural Dependency. He had the evidence. The 1974 plans, the overlay, the Hearing ruling. It was clean. It was airtight.

He was about to file it when a new notification appeared.

It was not from the Arbiter. It was not from a Core. It was from the Inspector General.

The message was short.

"CORE 1 AND CORE 6114: JOINT STRUCTURAL ELEMENT IDENTIFIED DURING HEARING PROCEEDINGS. JOINT LIABILITY ASSESSED. REMEDIATION WINDOW: 72 HOURS. FAILURE TO REMEDIATE RESULTS IN STRUCTURAL CONDEMNATION OF BOTH DUNGEON FOOTPRINTS. INSPECTOR GENERAL."

Gerald read it twice.

Joint liability. He had not known this mechanic existed. He had never heard of it. It was not in the System documentation he had read. It was not in the Core registration handbook. It was not in the Hearing procedures.

It was a new mechanic. The Inspector General had introduced it during the Hearing, or it had been triggered by the Hearing's outcome. The shared footing was now a joint responsibility. Both Cores had to fix it. Both Cores had to make it compliant. If they could not agree on how to fix it, if they could not coordinate, if either one refused, both dungeons would be condemned.

Condemnation meant dissolution. It meant the dungeon ceased to exist. It meant Gerald's 4,000 square feet, his handrails, his mold spores, his water leak, his confused rat, his Compliance Rating of 71, all of it gone.

He opened the joint liability panel. It required both Cores to submit a remediation plan. The plans had to be compatible. If they conflicted, the Inspector General would impose its own plan, and imposed plans were always worse than negotiated ones.

Gerald looked at the remediation requirements. The shared footing had to be brought up to current code. That meant enlarging it. That meant excavation. That meant breaking ground in a section of the dungeon that was shared with Core 6114.

He could not excavate into Core 6114's dungeon without Core 6114's permission. Core 6114 could not excavate into Gerald's dungeon without Gerald's permission.

They had to cooperate. Gerald Hutchins, who had never once been asked to fight a dragon, had to coordinate a construction project with a rival dungeon core who had spent thirty-eight chapters trying to destroy him.

He opened a communication channel to Core 6114.

The channel was blank. Core 6114 had not opened its side.

Gerald typed a message.

"Core 1 to Core 6114. Re: Joint Liability Assessment, Shared Footing. Core 1 proposes a joint remediation meeting within 24 hours. Core 1 will provide structural calculations, excavation plan, and timeline. Core 1 requests Core 6114 provide its own footprint survey and spawn density map for the affected zone. Core 1 will not proceed without Core 6114's input. Core 1 will not proceed past 72 hours without a joint plan."

He sent it.

The channel remained blank for ninety seconds.

Then a response appeared.

"Core 6114 to Core 1. Core 6114 does not agree to a meeting. Core 6114 proposes unilateral remediation. Core 6114 will excavate the shared footing from its own side. Core 6114 will enlarge the footing without entering Core 1's footprint. Core 6114 requires Core 1 to vacate the affected zone for 48 hours."

Gerald read it. He read it again.

Core 6114 was proposing to do the work alone. From its side. Without entering Gerald's dungeon. That was technically possible. The footing extended 0.4 meters into Core 6114's footprint. Core 6114 could excavate from its side and extend the footing eastward, enlarging it without touching Gerald's space.

But it would change the footing's geometry. It would shift the load distribution. It would alter the column's base. And Gerald's column, the one that supported the library above, sat on that footing. If Core 6114 changed the footing's shape without consulting Gerald, the column's base would be modified without Gerald's approval.

That was a Section 4.9 violation. Unauthorized modification of a shared structural element.

Gerald typed.

"Core 1 to Core 6114. Re: Unilateral Remediation Proposal. Core 1 does not consent to unilateral modification of a shared structural element. Section 4.9 prohibits modification without written consent from all parties holding joint liability. Core 1 requires a joint plan. Core 1 will file a Section 4.9 Citation against Core 6114 if unilateral work commences."

He sent it.

The response came back in four seconds.

"Core 6114 to Core 1. Core 6114 will proceed. Core 6114 will cite Core 1 for the original Section 4.2.1 violation if Core 1 blocks remediation. Core 6114 has a new Citation prepared. It is ready to serve. Core 6114 will serve it the moment Core 1 enters the shared zone."

Gerald stared at the message.

Core 6114 was threatening to serve a Citation on Gerald inside the shared zone. A Citation required the target to be physically present. Gerald would have to enter the shared zone to inspect the work. Core 6114 would be there. Core 6114 would serve the Citation on Gerald's face, in the one place where Gerald could not retreat without abandoning the joint liability requirement.

It was a trap. It was a very good trap. Gerald could not enter the zone without risking a Citation. He could not stay out of the zone without risking condemnation.

He opened his citation log. He opened the Section 4.9 citation he had drafted. He looked at the requirements. The target had to be physically present when unauthorized modification commenced. If Core 6114 started work before Gerald entered, Gerald could not serve the citation. If Gerald entered first, Core 6114 could serve its new citation.

They were both waiting for the other to move first.

The joint liability panel showed 71 hours remaining.

Gerald opened the shared zone map. He measured the entry points. He measured the sight lines. He measured the distance between the two dungeon boundaries.

Then he opened a new file.

He titled it: Remediation Plan, Joint.

He typed the first line.

"Core 1 proposes simultaneous entry. Both Cores enter the shared zone at the same moment. Neither serves a Citation. Both observe. Both consent. The work proceeds under joint supervision."

He sent it.

The response came back immediately.

"Core 6114 to Core 1. Core 6114 does not trust Core 1 to enter simultaneously. Core 6114 requires Core 1 to enter first."

Gerald closed the panel. He opened the shared zone map again. He looked at the entry point from his side. It was a narrow gap between two wall segments, a gap that the 1974 plans showed as a construction seam. The seam was 0.3 meters wide. Gerald's dungeon footprint ended at the seam. Core 6114's footprint began on the other side.

He could see the seam. He could feel the pressure differential. Core 6114 was on the other side of it, waiting.

He had seventy-one hours. He had a rival who would cite him the moment he stepped through. He had a library above that would collapse if the column failed. He had a joint liability that would dissolve both dungeons if they did not coordinate.

He opened his spawn list. Three mold spores. One water leak. One confused rat. A sixth spawn he had not yet identified, caught on the handrail.

He could not send a spawn through the seam. It would not be him. The Citation required him to be present.

He could not avoid the seam. The joint liability required his presence.

He could not trust Core 6114. Core 6114 had told him, in writing, that it would cite him.

He opened the Hearing rules one more time. He read Section 11.3. He read Section 11.4. He read Section 11.5.

Section 11.5 was new. He had not read it before. It had been added during the Hearing, or it had always been there and he had missed it. He was not sure which.

Section 11.5: "A Core under joint liability may invoke Protected Inspection Status. Protected Inspection Status grants immunity from Citations for the duration of a joint remediation activity. Protected Inspection Status must be invoked before entry into the shared zone. It must be invoked by both parties simultaneously. It must be verbally declared."

Gerald read it three times.

He opened the communication channel.

"Core 1 to Core 6114. Core 1 invokes Section 11.5. Protected Inspection Status. Core 1 declares verbally: Protected Inspection Status invoked. Core 1 awaits Core 6114's declaration. Core 1 will not enter the shared zone until Core 6114 has also invoked. Core 1 will not enter the shared zone until the status is active."

He sent it.

He waited.

The channel was blank.

Thirty seconds. A minute. Two minutes.

The joint liability panel ticked down. Seventy hours. Sixty-nine. Sixty-eight.

Then, at the three-minute mark, the channel flickered.

"Core 6114 to Core 1. Core 6114 invokes Section 11.5. Protected Inspection Status invoked."

Gerald felt the System lock into place. A new status appeared on his panel. Protected Inspection Status. Active. Duration: joint remediation activity.

He looked at the seam.

He looked at the clock.

He looked at the spawn list.

He looked at the library above, which was loading down through a column that was, technically, non-compliant, and which he was about to walk through a 0.3-meter gap to fix, in the company of a Core that had tried to kill him for thirty-eight chapters.

He walked toward the seam.
