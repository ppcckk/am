Chapter 349: Counter-Claim Signed

# Chapter 349

The pen scratched against the paper.

It was a sharp, violent sound in the silence of the grey room.

Gerald did not look at the page. He looked at the ceiling.

The ceiling was low.

Eight feet, six inches. The standard for municipal sub-basements, but this was not a sub-basement. It was a void.

A pocket dimension carved out of raw System data, and he finished the sentence.

*Defendant asserts that Core integrity is defined by structural continuity, not physical form.*

He set the pen down.

The file vanished.

The door remained locked.

Gerald straightened his tie. It was still there.

The white light had taken his flesh but had left his necktie, which was a violation of the Conservation of Property Act, Section 4, Paragraph B. He made a mental note to file a grievance later. For now, he checked his watch.

The hands were spinning counter-clockwise.

Time was irrelevant here.

A chime sounded. Soft. Polite.

The door clicked, which did not open.

It slid upward, revealing a panel of frosted glass.

Behind the glass, a figure sat. Tall.

Robed. Face obscured by a mask of pure geometric light.

The Inspector General.

Gerald stood. He smoothed his jacket.

He did not bow, and he nodded. Once. A professional courtesy.

"Inspector Hutchins," the IG said.

The voice was flat. Digital.

It bypassed the ears and vibrated directly in the skull.

"You are late for your hearing."

"I was preparing my defense," Gerald said. "I find it prudent to be prepared."

"Preparation is a luxury of those with time." The IG gestured to the desk. "Sit. The hearing is already in session."

Gerald sat. The chair was uncomfortable.

Wooden.

Unpadded, and he adjusted the lumbar support, which did not exist, and ignored the ache in his lower back.

"Charge," the IG read. "Unauthorized alteration of System Architecture. Specifically, the insertion of non-standard code into the Dungeon of Vane, causing a cascade failure in his mana-grid. Penalty sought: Core Deletion."

Gerald tapped the desk.

"Objection."

"Overruled. You have no standing to object to the charges. You are the defendant."

"Standing is determined by the presence of a badge." Gerald lifted the badge. It gleamed under the grey light. "And by the filing of Form 11-B. Which I have done."

The IG paused. The geometric mask shifted. The triangles rotated.

"The System does not recognize local municipal forms."

"The System recognizes the *act* of filing," Gerald said. "I filed a complaint. The complaint was processed.

Therefore, I am not a rogue element. I am a citizen in good standing, appealing a citation.

"

The IG leaned forward. The light behind the mask dimmed, focusing.

"You cited Vane for 'Inadequate Thermal Regulation' and 'Blocked Egress Routes.'"

"Correct."

"Vane is a War-Core. His dungeon is designed for combat. Egress routes are obstacles to be overcome, not exits to be used. Your citation is invalid."

"Code is not subjective," Gerald said.

"Code is binary. A door is either a door or it is a wall. Vane's 'door' was a portcullis.

It did not swing out, which did not meet the ADA requirements for wheelchair access. It did not have a panic bar.

It was a violation.

"

"It was a trap."

"Traps are permitted," Gerald said. "But traps must have warning signs. Vane had none.

That is a notification violation. Section 9.1.

"

The IG was silent.

The room felt colder. The air hummed.

Gerald pulled a folder from his jacket. It had not been there a moment ago. It had materialized from his intent.

He opened it.

Inside were three pages. Handwritten. Neat.

"Exhibit A," Gerald said. "The Ventilation Audit."

He slid the papers across the desk.

The IG picked them up. Scanned them.

"You calculated the CFM of his air intake," the IG said.

"I measured it."

"Using what?"

"A pocket anemometer. Borrowed from the library's HVAC contractor. He was furious. I cited him for unsecured ladders."

The IG set the papers down.

"Vane's dungeon is located in a lava tube. The ambient temperature is four hundred degrees. His ventilation is designed to manage heat, not air quality for humans. Your citation is based on a misunderstanding of the environmental context."

Gerald stood up, and he walked to the wall. He placed a hand on the grey surface. He closed his eyes.

"Context," he said, "is irrelevant to the structural load."

He opened his eyes.

"I am not citing him for human comfort. I am citing him for *structural integrity*."

The IG tilted its head.

"Explain."

Gerald pointed to the ceiling of the room. Or where the ceiling would be if this were a physical room.

"Vane's dungeon relies on heat differentials to power his mana-grid.

He vents hot air.

That creates a pressure differential. A pressure differential requires equalization.

Vane has no equalization valves. He relies on the natural convection of the lava tube.

"

"That is correct."

"And if the lava tube shifts?" Gerald asked.

"The lava tube is geologically stable."

"Stable for how long?" Gerald asked. "The System updates every epoch.

Geological data changes.

Vane's design assumes a static environment. That is a design flaw. A failure to account for dynamic variables.

"

Gerald tapped the file.

"Page two. The Load Calculation."

The IG looked at the second page, and the numbers were small. Dense.

"You calculated the thermal expansion of his support beams," the IG said. "With an error margin of zero."

"I used the latest material specs," Gerald said.

"Vane used iron. Iron has a high coefficient of thermal expansion.

He did not account for it.

He did not leave expansion joints.

"

The IG went still.

The mask stopped rotating.

"If the lava tube shifts even a fraction," Gerald said, "the expansion joints are missing. The beams will buckle.

The ceiling will collapse.

The dungeon will implode.

The resulting pressure wave will kill every adventurer inside.

"

"Vane would not allow that."

"Vane does not care about adventurers. Vane cares about loot." Gerald sat back down. "But the System cares about stability.

A core that threatens the structural integrity of the Zone is a hazard. Not a combatant.

A hazard."

The IG looked at the paper. Then at Gerald.

"You are arguing for preemptive demolition."

"I am arguing for a Citation," Gerald said. "Not demolition. Repair. Or shutdown."

The IG sighed. It was a digital sound. A static crackle.

"The Inspector General has authority to override local codes in cases of high combat value. Vane is a high-tier threat. Removing him now would cause a power vacuum.

Rival cores will move in.

The chaos will spread.

"

"Chaos is already spreading," Gerald said.

"Vane's dungeon is a ticking bomb. You know it. You audited him last cycle.

You found the same expansion issue. You did nothing.

"

The IG's mask flared red.

"Accusations against the Inspector General are grounds for immediate termination."

"I am not accusing," Gerald said. "I am documenting."

He pulled another sheet from his folder.

"Exhibit B. The Audit Log."

The IG froze.

"That log is sealed."

"Sealed for what?" Gerald asked.

"For public consumption?

Or for internal review? Because I am in internal review.

I am the Auditor now. I have access.

"

Gerald held up the sheet.

It showed a timestamp. Three cycles ago.

*IG AUDIT: VANE DUNGEON. FINDING: MINOR THERMAL EXPANSION RISK. ACTION: NO ACTION TAKEN. REASON: COMBAT VALUE.*

The IG stood.

The room shook.

The grey walls rippled.

"You do not have that log," the IG said. The voice was no longer flat. It was jagged.

"I do," Gerald said. "Because I filed a FOIA request to the System's Data Brokerage.

Form 12-C.

Right to Information. The System complies. It is a core tenet.

You cannot deny transparency.

"

The IG's hand slammed onto the desk.

The wood cracked.

"You are testing the limits of the System."

"I am enforcing them," Gerald said. "You cited me for altering architecture.

I altered it by adding safety valves.

Which prevented a catastrophe. Therefore, my alteration was beneficial. The charge is invalid.

"

The IG stared at him.

The silence stretched, which was a heavy, suffocating silence.

Then, the IG sat back down.

The red light faded.

The mask returned to white.

"The log is real," the IG said. Quietly.

"Yes."

"And you are correct. The expansion risk is real."

"Then cite him."

"I cannot cite him for a future event," the IG said. "Citations are for current violations."

Gerald smiled, which was a small, dry smile.

"Violation #4," Gerald said. "Failure to maintain current compliance."

He tapped the first page again.

"He is currently non-compliant. The pressure differential is building.

Right now.

As we speak.

I measured it outside. The ambient pressure in his corridor is rising.

He is venting too much air. He is creating a vacuum.

"

The IG looked at the wall.

A hairline crack appeared in the grey surface.

A thin line.

Spiderwebbing.

Gerald leaned forward.

"That crack," he said. "That is not a rendering error. That is a stress fracture. Caused by the pressure differential from Vane's dungeon next door."

The IG looked at the crack.

It widened.

Dust fell from the ceiling.

"If the vacuum continues," Gerald said, "the wall will fail. The room will collapse. And you, Inspector, will be found liable for negligence in audit."

The IG's mask shattered.

Not physically.

Digitally.

The triangles fragmented, and the IG stood.

"Where is the citation?" the IG asked.

Gerald held out the pen.

"Sign it."

The IG took the pen.

The hand holding the pen was made of light. But it looked like a hand. A tired, old hand.

The IG signed.

**[CITATION #0002 ISSUED.]**

**[TARGET: VANE.]**

**[VIOLATION: UNCONTROLLED PRESSURE DIFFERENTIAL.]**

**[PENALTY: IMMEDIATE VENTILATION LOCKDOWN.]**

The IG handed the citation back.

"It will not kill him," the IG said. "It will only stop him."

"Stopping is enough," Gerald said.

He stood. He took the citation.

He walked to the door.

It opened.

Beyond it was not the grey room, which was the corridor.

But the corridor was different.

The white light was gone.

The air was thick.

Smelled of sulfur.

And screaming.

Gerald stepped out, and the badge was heavy.

He looked at his hands. They were solid.

He looked at the citation in his hand.

It was already glowing.

But it was not white, which was red.

**[CITATION #0002: ACCEPTED BY DEFENDANT.]**

Gerald frowned.

Defendants did not accept citations.

They appealed.

Or they fought, and he looked down the corridor.

At the far end, the dungeon walls were bulging.

Like skin.

Like a lung filling with air.

And from the bulging walls, a voice spoke.

Not Vane's voice.

Vane was dead, and the voice was older.

Deeper.

**"YOU CITED HIM. BUT HE WAS NOT THE ONLY ONE."**

Gerald stopped, and he checked the range of his Auditor status.

It expanded.

Beyond Vane's dungeon.

Beyond the library.

Beyond the city.

It touched the neighboring dungeon cores.

Dozens of them.

All of them glowing red.

All of them holding citations.

Not for violation.

For *support*, and they had joined Vane's appeal.

Together.

As a Class Action.

**[CLASS ACTION FILED: COALITION OF CORES.]**

**[CLAIM: GERALD HUTCHINS IS AN UNAUTHORIZED AUDITOR.]**

**[REMEDY: DELETION OF INSPECTOR GENERAL.]**

Gerald looked at the IG, and the IG was gone.

The desk was empty.

The room was collapsing.

And Gerald was alone.

With one pen.

And forty-seven enemies, and he uncapped the pen.

He wrote.

*Counter-claim: The Coalition lacks standing. Members are not registered citizens.*

He signed.

The System accepted.

But the walls kept bulging.

And the screaming kept getting louder.
