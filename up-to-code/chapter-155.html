Chapter 155: An Empty Clipboard

# Chapter 155

The clipboard was empty.

Gerald Hutchins looked down at the clipboard in his hands. It was empty.

He knew it should be empty because he had not yet begun the audit, but he also knew that emptiness was a failure state for a municipal inspector.

An empty clipboard implied a lack of preparation. A lack of preparation implied negligence. Negligence implied liability.

His hands did not shake. They were steady. They were always steady. Even when the void above him was opening like a wound in the fabric of reality, Gerald's hands remained perfectly level, holding the clipboard at exactly waist height, parallel to the floor, with the angle of inclination set to fifteen degrees for optimal readability.

The eye in the void was vast.

It was white, featureless, and blind, yet it saw everything. It saw the forty thousand square feet of dungeon beneath the library.

It saw the mold spores.

It saw the confused rat, currently hiding behind a support beam. It saw Gerald.

**[AUDIT COMMENCING.]**

The text burned into his mind. It wasn't a notification. It was a summons.

Gerald adjusted his glasses. He did not look up at the eye.

Looking up was undignified.

Looking up invited condescension.

He looked down at his dungeon. Specifically, he looked at the ventilation ducts running along the eastern wall.

They were non-standard.

The air flowed through them, but it flowed too fast. The velocity exceeded the recommended cubic feet per minute for a residential-scale sub-basement. Fast air caused drafts.

Drafts caused discomfort. Discomfort was a violation of habitability standards.

Gerald reached into his pocket. He pulled out a small, red flag. He placed it on the duct.

The eye blinked.

**[VIOLATION DETECTED: EXCESSIVE AIR VELOCITY.]** **[SEVERITY: MINOR.]** **[PENALTY: 1% REDUCTION IN STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY.]**

Gerald nodded.

One percent was acceptable. One percent was within the margin of error for aging infrastructure. He had been reducing structural integrity by one percent for thirty years.

It was the municipal way.

The eye widened. It seemed confused.

Gerald moved to the next corridor. The floor here was concrete.

It was gray.

It was flat. It was perfect. Too perfect.

The lack of expansion joints was a critical error. Concrete expands and contracts with temperature changes.

Without joints, the concrete cracked.

Cracks were entry points for water.

Water was a hazard.

He pulled out a second red flag. He placed it on the floor.

The eye stared. The white pupil dilated.

**[VIOLATION DETECTED: MISSING EXPANSION JOINTS.]** **[SEVERITY: MODERATE.]** **[PENALTY: 5% REDUCTION IN MANA CAPACITY.]**

Gerald frowned. A five percent reduction was significant. It would hurt his spawn rates.

The rat would have less mana to sustain its confusion. The mold would grow slower.

But he had cited the violation.

The System had recorded it. The audit trail was established.

If the Auditor was here to judge him, Gerald would not be the defendant. Gerald would be the inspector.

He walked to the center of the room.

The air here was thick. The green haze of the ventilation deficit swirled around him.

It smelled of wet paper and old dust.

It was the smell of a library that had been left unattended for a decade.

Gerald pulled out a third flag. He placed it in the air.

The flag hovered. It defied gravity. It was a System construct, a physical manifestation of a citation.

The eye in the void pulsed. A low hum vibrated through the floor.

**[WARNING: INSPECTOR HUTCHINS IS ATTEMPTING TO SELF-CITE.]** **[ACTION: ILLEGAL.]** **[PENALTY: IMMEDIATE SUSPENSION OF CORE FUNCTIONS.]**

Gerald stopped. He looked at the third flag. He looked at the eye.

He did not remove the flag. He picked up his pen. He wrote on the clipboard.

*Subject: Self-Citation.* *Violation: Lack of External Oversight.* *Status: Pending Appeal.*

He held the clipboard up. He showed it to the eye.

The eye did not blink. It did not speak.

But the hum changed pitch.

It dropped.

It became a groan.

A form materialized in the air. It was not the standard red citation form. It was gray.

It was heavy. It looked like it was made of stone.

**[FORM 99-OMEGA: AUDIT CHALLENGE.]** **[DESCRIPTION: THE CORE MAY APPEAL ANY VIOLATION FOUND DURING THE AUDIT BY PROVING THE INSPECTOR HAS FAILED TO FOLLOW PROCEDURE.]** **[TIME LIMIT: 60 SECONDS.]**

Gerald read the form. He read it twice. Then he smiled. It was a small, tight smile, the kind he used when a contractor tried to pass off a patch job as a repair.

Sixty seconds.

He had thirty-one years of experience. He had more than sixty seconds. He had decades of procedural knowledge.

Gerald closed his eyes.

He reached into the System code. He did not look for mana. He did not look for spawn points.

He looked for the rulebook.

It was there. Buried under the fire safety codes and the electrical regulations. It was a tiny, obscure subsection. Section 4.2, Paragraph C.

*"An Auditor must verify the identity of the Core through biometric or structural resonance scan before applying any penalty."*

Gerald opened his eyes.

He pointed his pen at the eye.

"Prove my resonance," Gerald said. His voice was flat.

It was the voice he used when denying a permit application.

It carried no emotion, only the weight of bureaucratic certainty. "Scan my structural signature. Compare it to the registered footprint of the Ashford Municipal Library Sub-Basement."

The eye stared.

Time ticked. Fifty seconds.

Gerald stood still. He did not breathe. He did not blink. He waited.

Forty seconds.

The eye twitched. A beam of white light shot from the pupil.

It swept over Gerald.

It swept over the dungeon.

It swept over the red flags.

It paused.

It scanned the gray concrete patch where the breach had been sealed.

It scanned the mold spores.

It scanned the rat.

The rat squeaked.

The beam returned to the eye. The eye narrowed.

**[SCAN COMPLETE.]** **[RESULT: RESONANCE MISMATCH.]** **[EXPLANATION: THE STRUCTURAL FOOTPRINT HAS BEEN MODIFIED WITHOUT A PERMIT.]**

Gerald's heart skipped a beat. Just once. He did not let it show.

He had sealed the breach with perfect concrete. He had done it quickly. Efficiently.

Efficiency was a violation.

"Objection," Gerald said.

The eye ignored him.

**[NEW VIOLATION DETECTED: UNAUTHORIZED STRUCTURAL MODIFICATION.]** **[SEVERITY: CRITICAL.]** **[PENALTY: CORE DISSOLUTION PENDING APPEAL.]**

The floor shook. The support beams groaned. The ceiling, the real ceiling, cracked. Dust fell from the library above.

Gerald looked at the shaking floor. He looked at the eye.

He realized the trap.

The Auditor was not trying to find violations. The Auditor was trying to provoke them. Every time Gerald tried to fix a problem, he created a new one. Fixing the ventilation required installing fans, which required power, which required electrical wiring, which required a permit.

It was a infinite loop of bureaucracy.

Gerald had to break the loop. He had to stop fixing. He had to stop improving. He had to become the violation itself.

He looked at the red flag on the duct. He looked at the red flag on the floor.

He picked up the flag on the floor. He crumpled it.

The eye flared.

**[WARNING: DESTRUCTION OF AUDIT EVIDENCE.]** **[PENALTY INCREASED.]**

Gerald did not care. He walked to the wall. The wall with the glowing red crack. *Structural Weakness.*

He pressed his hand against the crack. He pushed.

He pushed hard.

The crack widened. Dust poured out. The wall bowed.

The eye watched.

Gerald pulled out his pen. He wrote on the clipboard.

*Subject: Intentional Structural Failure.* *Reason: To Test Egress Routes.* *Status: Compliance Check.*

He pushed again. A chunk of concrete fell. It hit the floor with a heavy thud.

The eye froze.

Gerald had created a hole in the wall. A direct path to the neighboring dungeon.

He had not sealed it. He had not patched it. He had left it open.

An open wall was a hazard. It was a violation.

But it was also an easement.

If the wall was open, the two dungeons were connected. If they were connected, they shared a compliance boundary.

And if they shared a boundary, Gerald could cite the *other* side.

He looked through the hole. He saw the dark, jagged tunnels of the neighbor. He saw the spikes.

He saw the lack of handrails. He saw the fire exits that led nowhere.

Gerald smiled.

He pulled out a fourth flag. He placed it on the edge of the hole.

He cited the *neighbor* for creating an unsafe opening in a shared structure.

The eye blinked.

The penalty stopped.

The shaking stopped.

**[VIOLATION TRANSFERRED.]** **[TARGET: NEIGHBORING CORE 'GROK-THE-UNBREAKABLE'.]** **[PENALTY: 10% REDUCTION IN DEFENSE FOR 10 DAYS.]**

Gerald stepped back. He adjusted his glasses.

He had used a loophole. He had cited a third party for a violation that Gerald himself had caused, but had framed as a test of egress compliance.

It was a dirty trick. It was a gray area. It was exactly the kind of thing Gerald had spent his career avoiding.

But it had worked.

The eye in the void narrowed. It did not look confused anymore. It looked angry.

**[AUDIT PAUSED.]** **[REASON: APPEAL HEARING REQUIRED.]** **[LOCATION: SYSTEM COURT.]** **[DATE: NOW.]**

The void faded. The eye closed. The white light vanished.

Gerald was alone in the basement.

The silence returned. The dust settled. The rat squeaked again, louder this time. It had found a crumb of bread near the hole.

Gerald looked at the hole in the wall. It was jagged. It was dangerous. It was a violation.

He should seal it.

He should fill it with concrete. He should install a door. He should file a Form 14-C (Opening in Partition Wall).

He looked at his clipboard.

It was full now. Four citations. One self-citation.

One transfer.

His hands were steady. But his stomach was turning.

He had appealed to a higher authority. He had invoked a hearing.

And he had not prepared for the hearing.

He had no witnesses. He had no precedent. He had only his own word.

Gerald looked at the hole. Through it, he saw a shadow moving. A large shadow. It was not the neighbor.

It was something else.

Something had come through the hole.

It was not a goblin. It was not a rat. It was a man.

He wore a suit. A gray suit. He held a briefcase. He had no face.

Where his face should have been, there was a stamp. A rubber stamp. It was red. It read **DENIED.**

The figure stepped into Gerald's dungeon.

It looked at Gerald.

It looked at the hole.

It looked at the red flags.

The figure opened its briefcase. Inside was a single sheet of paper.

It handed the paper to Gerald.

Gerald took it. His hands did not shake.

He read the paper.

It was a summons.

**[SUBPOENA: GERALD HUTCHINS.]** **[CASE: STATE OF THE SYSTEM VS. GERALD HUTCHINS.]** **[CHARGE: OBSTRUCTION OF AUDIT.]** **[EVIDENCE: THE HOLE IN THE WALL.]** **[JUDGE: THE UNLICENSED.]**

Gerald looked up. The faceless man was waiting.

Gerald looked at the hole. He could still see the neighbor's dungeon. He could see the spikes. He could see the lack of fire exits.

He could still cite the neighbor.

But if he cited the neighbor now, he would be citing the evidence. You cannot cite the evidence in a case where you are the defendant.

He was trapped.

He had to go to court. He had to go to the System Court. And he did not know the rules of the court.

The faceless man tapped the paper.

Gerald folded it. He put it in his pocket.

He looked at the hole.

He looked at the rat.

He picked up the rat. It struggled. It squeaked.

Gerald held the rat tightly. He walked to the hole. He looked through it.

The neighbor's dungeon was quiet.

Gerald reached into the hole. He threw the rat through.

It landed in the neighbor's dungeon.

The shadow in the void stirred.

**[ENTITY INTRODUCED: UNLICENSED BIOLOGICAL AGENT.]** **[LOCATION: NEIGHBORING DUNGEON.]** **[STATUS: TRESPASSING.]**

Gerald turned back to the faceless man.

"I am invoking my right to silence," Gerald said.

The faceless man did not speak. It simply pointed at the hole.

Gerald looked at the hole.

The rat was gone.

Something else was coming through.

It was large. It was heavy. It dragged itself across the floor.

It was the Auditor's true form. Not the eye. Not the stamp.

It was the code itself. The raw, unedited, contradictory code of the System.

It poured through the hole like liquid mercury. It flowed into Gerald's dungeon.

It covered the floor.

It covered the walls. It covered the red flags.

It erased them.

The green haze of the ventilation deficit returned. It was thicker. It was darker. It smelled of burning plastic.

Gerald backed away. He hit the wall. The wall was cold.

The mercury code rose up. It formed a shape. A shape that looked like a person. A person made of text.

**[I AM THE CODE.]** **[YOU ARE OUT OF COMPLIANCE.]** **[CORRECT YOURSELF OR BE DELETED.]**

Gerald looked at the text-person. He looked at his clipboard.

He had no form for this.

He had no rule for this.

He had no precedent for this.

Gerald Hutchins, Inspector of Buildings, took a deep breath. He exhaled.

He looked at the text-person.

"I need to see a permit," Gerald said.

The text-person paused.

**[WHAT PERMIT?]**

"A permit," Gerald said, "to exist without structure."

The text-person tilted its head.

**[DEFINE EXISTENCE.]**

Gerald pulled out his pen. He wrote on the clipboard.

*Subject: Ontological Compliance.* *Violation: Lack of Definition.* *Status: Pending Clarification.*

He held up the clipboard.

The text-person stared at the clipboard.

It did not attack. It did not delete.

It waited.

Gerald waited.

The clock ticked. The dungeon breathed. The mold grew.

And in the neighbor's dungeon, the rat spoke.

It spoke in Gerald's voice.

"Insufficient lighting," the rat said.

Gerald froze.

The rat looked at him. Its eyes were not confused. They were sharp. They were bureaucratic.

The rat had absorbed the citation. The rat had become a proxy.

And the proxy was now judging Gerald.

Gerald looked at the text-person. He looked at the rat. He looked at the hole.

He realized he had made a mistake. A fatal mistake.

He had cited the neighbor. The neighbor had countered. The counter was now in Gerald's dungeon.

And it was wearing Gerald's face.

The text-person dissolved. It poured back through the hole. The mercury retreated. The hole remained.

Gerald was alone again.

Except he was not alone.

The rat was sitting on the pile of broken concrete. It was holding a small, red flag. It had made it from a scrap of paper.

It raised the flag.

**[CITATION SERVED.]**

Gerald looked at the flag.

He looked at the rat.

He looked at the hole in the wall.

He knew what he had to do.

He had to go through the hole.

He had to go to the neighbor's dungeon.

He had to file a form in a foreign jurisdiction.

He had to break the one rule he had never broken.

He had to leave his dungeon.

Gerald lifted his clipboard. He stepped toward the hole. The jagged concrete scraped his shoe.

He did not look back.

He stepped through.

The darkness on the other side was not empty. It was waiting.

And it had a stamp.
