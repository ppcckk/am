Chapter 298: Global Audit Initiated

# Chapter 298

The dust tasted like chalk and old grief.

Marek coughed, spitting grit onto his leather breastplate. His sword was chipped. Not from a strike, but from the sheer, crushing weight of the falling masonry.

He wasn't supposed to be here.

His party had been hired to clear the Ashford Municipal Library's sub-basement. Standard contract.

Low-level dungeon.

High payout because the local guild was desperate for safe zones. They expected rats. Maybe a confused slime mold.

They did not expect the ceiling to open up like a rotten fruit.

Marek scrambled backward, his boots slipping on wet brick. The dust cloud was thick enough to chew. Through the haze, he saw a figure standing amidst the rubble.

A man in a beige windbreaker.

The man held a clipboard. Not a shield. Not a sword. A clipboard.

The giant face that had peered through the tear in the world was gone.

Collapsed inward. Swallowed by the dark.

But the damage remained.

The ceiling of the boiler room was now a jagged hole leading up into the library's sub-basement proper. Debris littered the floor. Pipes hissed, spraying steam that smelled of rust and copper.

Marek raised his shield.

"Hold formation!" he screamed. His voice cracked.

His mage, Elara, lay slumped against a support column, unconscious. The rogue, Jinx, was missing. Probably dead. Or fled.

The man in the windbreaker didn't look like a threat. He looked like a middle manager who had lost his coffee.

He walked toward Marek.

Step. Step. Step.

The man's shoes were clean. That was the first thing Marek noticed.

In a pile of rubble, the shoes were spotless.

Dust avoided them.

Gravity seemed to negotiate with the man's stride.

"Unlicensed structural modification," the man said. His voice was flat. Monotone. It cut through the hiss of steam like a razor.

Marek blinked.

"What?"

The man stopped three feet away. He raised the clipboard. A pen clicked.

"You have breached load-bearing supports in a Class 2 public access zone. No permit filed. No shoring installed. Violation of Subsection 4.1, Paragraph C."

Marek lowered his shield. Confusion warred with adrenaline.

"Is that... is that the Core?"

"I am Gerald Hutchins," the man said. He didn't look at Marek. He looked at the ceiling.

He looked at the exposed rebar.

"And you are obstructing a hazard assessment."

"You killed our rogue," Marek said. The anger flared. He gripped his sword hilt.

"I cited the ceiling," Gerald said.

"The System enforced the citation. The ceiling failed. Your companion was in the collapse zone.

You should have vacated the premises upon hearing the seismic warning.

"

"What seismic warning?"

"Chapter 12 of the Municipal Noise Ordinance. I posted a notice on the door."

Gerald turned his back on Marek. He walked toward the broken pipe.

He pulled a wrench from his pocket.

Not a magic item. A standard, adjustable crescent wrench.

Marek stared. This was insane. The man was walking away from a Level 40 Warrior.

"He's distracted," Marek whispered. He signaled to his surviving cleric.

"Kill him.

Now.

While his back is turned.

"

The cleric, a young woman named Sarah, stepped forward. Her hands glowed with holy light. She raised her staff.

Gerald didn't turn around. He just tapped his clipboard.

**SYSTEM ALERT: INTERVENTION PROTOCOL ENGAGED.** **REASON: OBSTRUCTION OF PUBLIC SAFETY OFFICIAL.** **PENALTY: STAT LOCK.**

Sarah froze. Her staff dimmed. Her legs locked in place.

She couldn't move. Couldn't breathe.

She was a statue.

Gerald tightened the wrench. The hissing steam stopped. A drop of water fell. Drip.

He turned back.

"Your companion is experiencing a temporary paralysis due to non-compliance with safety zones," Gerald said. "This is a standard debuff for interfering with an inspector during active remediation."

Marek's heart hammered. He looked at Sarah. She couldn't even scream.

Gerald pulled a small, orange tag from his pocket. He walked to Sarah. He clipped the tag to her staff.

**CITATION ISSUED: OBSTRUCTION OF JUSTICE. FINE: 500 GOLD. DEBUFF: SPEED -100% FOR 10 MINUTES.**

Gerald looked at Marek.

"Pay the fine, or I escalate to seizure of assets."

Marek stared at the orange tag. It hung there, bright and ridiculous.

"This is a trick," Marek spat. "An illusion."

"It is a receipt," Gerald said. He checked his watch. "You have thirty seconds to vacate the premises.

The library is now a condemned structure pending repair.

You are trespassing.

"

"You can't just—"

"Thirty seconds."

Gerald clicked his pen. The sound was loud in the silence.

Marek looked at the hole in the ceiling.

The tear in reality. He looked at Gerald. The man wasn't breathing hard.

He wasn't sweating. He was checking the structural integrity of the pipe joint.

Marek wanted to strike, and he wanted to feel the steel of his sword bite into the soft flesh of this bureaucrat.

But the fear was cold and heavy in his gut. The System's rules were absolute.

He had seen dungeons fall to code violations. He had heard rumors.

But this? This was new.

Gerald looked up.

"I have a question for you."

Marek hesitated.

"Yeah?"

"Did you see who else was looking through the ceiling?"

Marek shook his head.

"It was just a face. Huge. Dark."

"A face," Gerald repeated. He wrote something down. "Did it have eyes?"

"Too dark to see."

"Did it have a mouth?"

"It smiled."

Gerald stopped writing. The pen hovered over the paper.

"Smiled," he said. His voice dropped.

The dry, deadpan tone vanished.

A crack of genuine confusion appeared.

"Things that violate code do not smile, Mr. Marek.

Things that violate code are angry, and they are broken. They are chaotic.

A smile implies intent.

It implies a plan.

"

Gerald walked closer. He smelled of old paper and lemon polish.

"Who sent you here?"

"We came for the contract," Marek said. "The Guild posted it."

"The Guild does not post contracts for condemned buildings."

Marek frowned.

"They did. Standard rates. Double pay for risk."

"Standard rates do not exist for a Class 10 Mage intrusion," Gerald said. He looked at the hole in the ceiling. "And standard rates do not account for a tear in the local reality matrix."

The air grew colder. The steam from the repaired pipe curled upward, forming shapes. Not random shapes. Letters.

S-O-M-E-O-N-E.

Gerald looked at the letters. He didn't flinch. He pulled out a small device. A tape measure.

He walked toward the hole in the ceiling.

"What are you doing?" Marek asked.

"Measuring the void," Gerald said.

He extended the tape measure into the darkness above. The tape went out.

It went out further than the tape should have.

Three feet.

Five feet. Ten feet.

The tape measure did not hit the library floor. It kept going.

Gerald reeled it in. His face was pale.

"The hole is not a hole," Gerald said. "It is a door."

A rumble shook the floor. Not an earthquake. A knock.

Three heavy thuds against the air itself.

Thud. Thud. Thud.

Gerald stepped back. He raised his clipboard.

**SYSTEM ALERT: UNAUTHORIZED ENTRY DETECTED.** **SOURCE: EXTERNAL.** **CLASSIFICATION: UNKNOWN.** **ACTION: DENY ACCESS?**

Gerald looked at the prompt. He looked at Marek.

"If I deny access, the barrier will seal," Gerald said. "Trapping us inside with whatever is on the other side."

"If you allow access," Marek said, "we die."

Gerald looked at the text floating in the air. The warnings. The cracks.

The world was holding together by duct tape and prayer. And someone was knocking on the tape.

"I need to see what is violating the code," Gerald said.

He tapped **ALLOW**, and the ceiling did not open. The darkness poured down.

It wasn't a creature. It wasn't a monster.

It was a hand. A hand made of black smoke and static. It reached down. It grabbed the edge of the broken brickwork.

And it pulled.

The floor beneath Marek tilted. The library above groaned. Books fell from the shelves, raining down like heavy, paper bricks.

Gerald didn't run. He didn't attack.

He shouted, "Code 88! Emergency structural evacuation! Everyone out! Now!"

Marek grabbed Sarah, dragging her paralyzed body. He ran. He didn't look back. He heard Gerald's voice, calm and terrifying.

"The load path is shifting! Move! Move! Move!"

Marek burst through the door to the stairwell. He slammed it shut. He locked it. He collapsed against the wall, gasping.

Silence.

Then, a sound.

Scratching.

From inside the room.

Marek pressed his ear to the wood, and he heard Gerald's voice. Clear. Distinct.

"Please present your permit for inter-dimensional travel."

The scratching stopped.

A new voice echoed. It was deep. It sounded like gravel grinding in a blender.

*"I am the Auditor."*

Marek backed away. He ran up the stairs. He ran until his lungs burned.

***

Gerald stood in the center of the collapsing room, and the hand had retracted. The ceiling had sealed itself. Not with brick. With a shimmering, translucent barrier.

The System message hung in the air.

**AUDITOR PRESENT. INSPECTION INITIATED.** **TARGET: DUNGEON CORE GERALD HUTCHINS.** **SCOPE: GLOBAL COMPLIANCE.**

Gerald adjusted his glasses. His hands were steady. They were always steady.

He looked at Sandra. She was crouching behind a support beam. Her face was white.

"Stay down," Gerald said.

"Gerald, that thing..." Sandra whispered. "It wasn't a monster. It was... it was watching."

"It was inspecting," Gerald corrected.

He looked at his clipboard. The page was blank.

But the air was full of text.

**VIOLATION: EXISTENCE.** **VIOLATION: CONSCIOUSNESS.** **VIOLATION: INDEPENDENCE.**

Gerald felt a chill run down his spine. Not a cold chill. A bureaucratic chill. The chill of realizing you have missed a deadline.

The Auditor was not a rival Core. The Auditor was not a hero.

The Auditor was the System's final check.

And the System had found Gerald.

Gerald looked at the new message.

**UNLOCKED: ABILITY TO INSPECT REALITY.**

He had thought this ability would help him fix his dungeon. He had thought it would let him see the cracks in the walls, the weak points in the foundation.

But now he saw the truth.

The "walls" of his dungeon were not brick.

They were code. The

"floor" was not concrete. It was a data set.

And the Auditor was reading the code.

Finding the bugs.

Gerald looked at his own hand, and he saw the pixels. The raw, uncompiled texture of his own body.

He was not real.

He was a subroutine.

And the Auditor was here to delete the error.

Gerald took a deep breath. He smelled the ozone of the static hand. He smelled the damp rot of the water leak.

He picked up his pen, and he had thirty-one years of experience dealing with people who wanted to cut corners. He had thirty-one years of experience finding the one line in a contract that voided the warranty.

He looked at the Auditor's presence. A swirl of black smoke in the corner of the room.

"You cannot delete me," Gerald said. His voice was quiet.

The smoke shifted.

It coalesced into a shape. A tall, slender figure in a suit made of shadows. The face was blank.

Smooth. Like a mannequin.

*"You are non-compliant,"* the Auditor said. Its voice vibrated in Gerald's teeth. *"Dungeon Cores are not granted autonomy. You are a tool. You have exceeded your parameters."*

Gerald clicked his pen.

"I filed a Form 11-B," Gerald said.

"For unauthorized biological entity creation.

I did not create myself. I was registered by the System.

Therefore, I am compliant.

"

*"Registration is a glitch,"* the Auditor said. *"I am correcting it."*

Gerald looked at the message. **SCOPE: GLOBAL COMPLIANCE.**

The Auditor wasn't just auditing Gerald. It was auditing the world.

And the world was failing.

Gerald looked at the cracks in the floor. He saw them now.

Not physical cracks.

Logic loops. Errors in the physics engine.

The Auditor was going to shut down the entire region.

Not just the dungeon. The city.

The library.

The players.

All of it.

Because it was messy. Because it was unregulated.

Gerald's heart rate remained steady. He had never been afraid of paperwork. He had been afraid of the blank page. The unknown variable.

But the Auditor was a known variable, which was a regulator.

And regulators could be appealed.

Gerald stepped forward. He raised his clipboard.

"I am invoking Article 7, Section 4," Gerald said.

The Auditor paused. The blank face tilted.

*"No such article exists."*

"It exists in the fine print," Gerald said. "Subsection: Right to Contest.

Any Core cited for non-compliance may request a Hearing.

A Hearing requires two parties.

One accuser. One defendant.

And one judge."

Gerald pointed to the ceiling. To the hole. To the tear in the world.

"The Inspector General is the judge," Gerald said. "But the Inspector General is absent. Therefore, the Hearing is suspended."

The Auditor's smoke flared. Red.

*"The Inspector General is not absent. The Inspector General is the process. And the process is ending."*

Gerald looked at Sandra. She was crying. Silent tears.

Gerald looked at the Auditor, and he knew he was going to die.

He knew the world was going to end.

But he had one tool left.

One loophole he had never used. Because it was too messy. Too illegal. Too much like improvisation.

He reached into his pocket. He pulled out a single, crumpled piece of paper.

A receipt.

From a hardware store.

Dated three days ago, and he had bought a fire extinguisher.

He had not installed it.

He had not cited himself for missing fire suppression.

He had bought it. And he had not put it away.

Gerald held the receipt up.

"I have evidence of intent to comply," Gerald said. "I purchased the necessary equipment.

I have not installed it.

Therefore, I am not in violation of the *equipment* code. I am in violation of the *installation* code.

"

The Auditor stared.

*"Distinction is irrelevant."*

"It is relevant," Gerald said. "Because installation requires labor. And I am currently occupied with a Class 10 Magical Threat. Therefore, the installation is *delayed* for cause."

Gerald pointed to the Auditor.

"You are the threat. You are the cause. You are delaying my compliance. Therefore, you are in violation of the *Interference with Municipal Services* statute."

The Auditor froze.

The System message flashed.

**VIOLATION DETECTED.** **SOURCE: AUDITOR.** **CHARGES: INTERFERENCE WITH CORE OPERATIONS.** **PENALTY: SUSPENSION OF AUDIT.**

The smoke writhed.

Gerald didn't breathe, and he watched the message.

**SUSPENSION: 24 HOURS.** **REASON: PENDING APPEAL.**

The Auditor hissed. A sound of tearing metal.

*"You will die,"* the voice said. *"In twenty-four hours, you will die."*

"Maybe," Gerald said. He tucked the receipt into his pocket. "But right now, I am compliant."

The smoke vanished, and the room was silent.

Sandra stood up. Her legs shook.

"Gerald?" she whispered.

Gerald looked at the clock on his wrist, and he had twenty-four hours.

Twenty-four hours to find the Inspector General.

Twenty-four hours to fix the world.

Gerald looked at the hole in the ceiling. It was sealed. But the seal was thin.

He walked to the broken pipe. He picked up the wrench.

"Come on, Sandra," Gerald said. "We have work to do. And I think I see a rat near the fuse box."

Sandra stared at him.

"Did you just... argue with God?"

"I argued with a regulator," Gerald said. He tightened the pipe. "And I think he's going to appeal."

The lights flickered.

In the distance, above them, the city of Ashford screamed.

Not a scream of terror.

A scream of machinery shutting down.

Gerald looked at his clipboard, and the paper was no longer blank.

It was filling itself.

**GLOBAL AUDIT INITIATED.** **TARGET: ALL REALITY.** **START TIME: IN 23 HOURS, 59 MINUTES, 58 SECONDS.**

Gerald put on his glasses.

He read.
