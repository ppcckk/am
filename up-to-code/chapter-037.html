Chapter 37

Gerald typed the spawn command and the System rejected it. He typed it again. Rejected. He checked his spawn list one more time. Three mold spores. A water leak. A confused rat. He had nine minutes to spawn something that could fight a magma core, and his spawn list contained nothing that could fight a magma core.

He stopped typing. He looked at the Citation still open in his interface. Core Twenty-Two. The parcel number stared at him. A single transposed digit. He had been filling out forms at 2 AM without a second check. Because he had been working alone. Because he had never had a second check in thirty-one years.

The notification pulsed. Distance: 3.8 blocks. Speed: increasing. Time: nine minutes.

Gerald opened his interface to the Structural Registration tab. He had not looked at this tab since he registered his dungeon. He had filled it out the way he filled out everything. By the book. Footprint: 4,000 square feet. Location: sub-basement, Ashford Municipal Library. He had entered the library's address because that was where the sub-basement was. The address was correct. The address was the library's address.

He scrolled to the bottom of the registration form. There was a field he had left blank. It said: Contiguous Structures (optional). He had left it blank because the library above was not a dungeon. It was a library. It had a different owner. It had a different purpose. It had a Certificate of Occupancy issued by the City of Ashford, Department of Building Safety, in 1987.

But the address was the same. The footprint was registered at the library's address. And the library was a structure. A contiguous structure. Above his dungeon. Sharing a foundation. Sharing a load path.

Gerald typed the library's address into the Contiguous Structures field. The System paused. A loading indicator appeared. Then:

Structural Integration confirmed. Registered structures: 2. Combined footprint: 18,400 square feet. New code obligations inherited: ADA (compliant), Fire Code (partially compliant), Electrical Code (non-compliant: 1 violation detected).

Gerald felt something shift. The dungeon's systems expanded. He could feel the library above him the way he could feel his own corridors. The sprinkler system. The fire alarm. The HVAC. The electrical panel in the east wing with the knob-and-tube wiring he had flagged during his original inspection thirty-one years ago.

The electrical violation. He had flagged it. He had written it up. He had recommended remediation. The city had not remediated it. He had followed up three times. He had filed a complaint. The complaint had been buried in a drawer.

Now it was his violation. His Compliance Rating dropped by four points. He did not care. He had sprinklers.

He opened the spawn list again. The water leak. He had a water leak in his dungeon. A single, persistent, barely functional water leak. He had been meaning to fix it. He had never fixed it because fixing it would have required him to identify the source, which would have required him to access the plumbing infrastructure, which was above his dungeon, in the library.

Now the plumbing infrastructure was his.

He directed the water leak upward. Through the floor. Into the library's plumbing. Into the sprinkler system. The System processed this for three seconds. Then the sprinkler system activated. Not in the library. In his dungeon. The water came up through the floor and flooded the entrance corridor.

Distance: 2.1 blocks. Time: five minutes.

Gerald went to the entrance. His entrance. The one he had built to code. Six feet wide. Self-closing door. Panic hardware. Egress signage at sixty foot-candles. The corridor was now six inches deep in water. The water was rising. The sprinklers were running at full capacity.

He checked his spawn list. The water leak had grown. It was no longer a leak. The System had reclassified it: Hydrological Feature (Active). Flow rate: 2.3 gallons per minute. Not much. But the sprinklers were doing the work. The library's sprinkler system was doing the work.

Distance: 1.4 blocks. Time: three minutes.

Gerald could feel Core Twenty-Two approaching. Not with his eyes. With the interface. The notification pulsed. The air temperature in his dungeon rose. Not because Core Twenty-Two was close. Because the System was already responding to the thermal differential. The walls were warming. The handrails were warming.

He checked his fire code compliance. He had installed fire-rated drywall. He had sealed all penetrations. He had a Class A fire rating on his walls. But fire-rated drywall was rated for twenty minutes of exposure. Core Twenty-Two was not a fire. It was magma. Magma was 700 to 1,200 degrees Celsius. Fire-rated drywall would last approximately ninety seconds against direct contact.

He had ninety seconds. Maybe less.

He opened his interface. He went to the Citation tab. He tried to file a Citation for zoning. Residential zone. Magma core in a residential zone. The System processed it. Rejected it. Reason: target not found in compliance database.

Core Twenty-Two was not in the database. It had been operating for forty-three years. It had been grandfathered in. The System's compliance framework had been implemented later. Core Twenty-Two had never been registered. It had never been audited. It was invisible to Gerald's entire toolkit.

He could not cite it. He could not appeal it. He could not debuff it.

Distance: 0.6 blocks. Time: one minute.

The temperature was rising. The water in the corridor was steaming. The steam was thick. Visibility dropped. Gerald's interface flickered. The System was struggling to maintain environmental tracking in the steam.

Then Core Twenty-Two arrived.

It did not come through the door. The door was six feet wide, self-closing, with panic hardware. Core Twenty-Two was wider than the door. It came through the wall. The fire-rated drywall lasted forty seconds. Then it was gone. Then the wall was gone. Then the corridor was gone.

Gerald felt it. The System felt it. The dungeon was being consumed. The floor was melting. The handrails were melting. The egress signage was melting. His carefully measured, properly ventilated, fully compliant 4,000 square feet was being eaten from the west end.

Core Twenty-Two filled the entrance corridor. It was not a creature. It was not a spawn. It was a presence. A mass. A geological fact that had decided to be angry.

Gerald stood in the middle of his dungeon. The water was boiling around his feet. The steam was everywhere. His interface was barely readable. The rat had fled. The mold spores were dead. The hydrological feature was evaporating.

He opened the form. Form 11-B. Unlicensed Biological Entity. He had tried to file it in his first hour as a Core. The System had rejected it. Reason: no registered structure. The form required a registered structure. A permitted public building.

He had a registered structure now. Two of them. The library was permitted. The library had an occupancy permit. The library was a public building with a valid Certificate of Occupancy.

Core Twenty-Two was inside the library's footprint. It was inside Gerald's registered structure. It was an unlicensed entity operating in a permitted public building.

Gerald filed Form 11-B.

The System processed it. One second. Two seconds. Three seconds.

Accepted.

Enforcement action: Ejectment.

The System's Ejectment was not gentle. It was not a Citation. It was not a debuff. It was a physical expulsion. The System's enforcement mechanism reached into the structure and applied force. Not to Core Twenty-Two. To the structure itself. The structure was the thing being corrected. The structure was the thing that had failed to prevent the unlicensed entity from entering.

The floor buckled. The walls buckled. The ceiling buckled. The entire 4,000 square feet of Gerald's dungeon was physically displaced. The System was ejecting the structure. Pushing it out of the way. Creating clearance.

Core Twenty-Two was pushed. Slowly. Reluctantly. It resisted. The System pushed harder. The dungeon resisted. The System pushed harder still.

The handrails snapped. The egress signage tore free. The sprinkler pipes ruptured. The electrical panel in the east wing shorted out. The knob-and-tube wiring, which Gerald had flagged thirty-one years ago, caught fire.

Core Twenty-Two was pushed back through the wall it had come through. It left. It was gone. The Ejectment was complete.

Gerald stood in a pile of rubble. His dungeon was gone. Not damaged. Gone. The floor was gone. The walls were gone. The handrails were gone. The egress signage was gone. The sprinklers were gone. The electrical panel was gone. The thirty foot-candles of compliant lighting were gone.

His interface flickered.

Compliance Rating: 3/100.

New notification.

Compliance Rating below minimum threshold (10/100). Administrative Closure initiated. Permanent deletion of registered structures: 24 hours. Remediation window: 24 hours.

Another notification.

Inspector General scheduled for on-site inspection: 6 hours.

Gerald looked at the rubble. He looked at his spawn list. Three mold spores. A confused rat. A hydrological feature that was now leaking into the street above.

He looked at the deletion timer. Twenty-three hours, fifty-eight minutes.

He looked at the Inspector General timer. Five hours, fifty-nine minutes.

He opened his interface to the spawn list. He typed a command. The System accepted it.

Spawn: Gravel. Volume: 0.5 cubic feet.

It was not much. It was a start.

He began to type again.
