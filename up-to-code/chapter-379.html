Chapter 379: Improvised Order

# Chapter 379

Gerald blinked. The white void receded, replaced by the peeling beige wallpaper of the Ashford Municipal Library sub-basement lobby.

His clipboard was back in his hand.

The pages were no longer blank. They were filled with ink that smelled of ozone and burnt hair.

The rat sat on the reception desk.

It was grooming its whiskers with the casual indifference of a creature who had just spoken with the voice of a mid-level bureaucrat. Gerald looked at his own hands.

They were trembling, and he pressed his thumb into the ink pad. It smeared black across his fingerprint, a permanent stain that would not wash out. He had been stamped.

He had been denied. And now, the System had updated his status.

A notification pinged in his peripheral vision. It was not a gentle chime. It sounded like a gavel striking wood.

**STATUS UPDATE: APPEAL PROCESSING.** **CURRENT STATUS: PENDING REVIEW.** **CITATION 4096-B HAS BEEN WITHDRAWN.** **PENALTY ASSESSED: LOSS OF STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY (CORRIDOR 3).**

Gerald read the text three times.

Corridor 3 was his main ventilation shaft, which was the lung of his dungeon. If it collapsed, the mold spores would suffocate.

The water leak would stagnate. The rat would leave.

He looked at the man in the suit. The figure was gone.

The door was intact, and the smoke had cleared, leaving only a faint acrid scent of sulfur and old paper. Gerald stepped forward. He kicked the door.

It was solid oak, reinforced with steel plating.

It had not burned. It had not exploded.

The explosion had been in the paperwork.

"Subsection D," Gerald muttered. The phrase tasted like ash in his mouth. *Insufficient evidence to sustain claim.*

He had cited the man for operating a dungeon without a valid operating license. The man, who claimed to be an

"Appeal," had argued that he was not a dungeon core, but a procedural entity. he could not be cited for lacking a license, because he was not a separate legal person. It was a technicality.

A loophole. And it had worked.

Gerald walked to the nearest spawn point.

The three mold spores were clustered near the baseboard, and they were pulsing slowly. Their regeneration rate had dropped by forty percent.

He could see the grey pallor of their health bars hovering above them.

"Subsection D," Gerald said to the mold. "They said there was insufficient evidence."

The mold did not respond. It simply shivered.

Gerald pulled out a fresh Form 11-B. *Unlicensed Biological Entity Report.* He wrote. His handwriting was neat, precise, and shaking.

He noted the time, and he noted the location. He noted the entity's previous appearance. He noted the new appearance.

He noted the voice.

*Entity exhibits vocal mimicry of Core Operator.* *Entity exhibits resistance to fire damage.* *Entity exhibits procedural immunity to standard citation protocols.*

This was not a dungeon core. This was something else. Something the System used to correct itself. Or to punish those who tried to correct it.

Gerald walked to Corridor 3. The wall was bowing inward.

Cracks spiderwebbed through the concrete, and the air was thick and stale. He placed his hand on the wall. It felt warm.

Not hot. Warm.

Like a fever.

He closed his eyes. He focused on the System interface. He needed to see the full history of the citation. He needed to see the evidence the System had rejected.

The interface shimmered. A window opened. It was not the usual blue text. It was red.

**TRANSCRIPT: HEARING 4096-B.** **PLAINTIFF: CORE ID 9902 (GERALD HUTCHINS).** **DEFENDANT: APPELLATE PROCESS (SELF-EXECUTING).** **JUDGE: INSPECTOR GENERAL (MASKED).**

Gerald scrolled down. The transcript was sparse. Most of it was blacked out. But one line was visible.

*DEFENDANT ARGUMENT: "The plaintiff failed to establish jurisdiction over a non-existent entity.

The citation form 7-C was used, but the target was not a Dungeon Core. The target was a Procedural Ghost.

Form 7-C applies only to Cores. Case dismissed.

"*

Gerald stared at the words. *Procedural Ghost.*

He had never heard the term. He had inspected three hundred and twelve properties in Ashford. He had cited forty-seven dungeons. He had never encountered a Procedural Ghost.

He pulled up the System's Code Reference Library. He typed: *Procedural Ghost.*

Nothing, and he typed: *Non-Cores Entities.*

Nothing.

He typed: *Appellate Processes.*

A single entry appeared. It was buried in the appendix, under *Definitions of Unlisted Phenomena.*

**PROCEDURAL GHOST:** *A manifestation of the System's self-correcting logic. Appears when a Citation is contested and the System determines the initial citation was procedurally flawed.

The Ghost embodies the flaw.

It cannot be fought with force. It can only be resolved by correcting the error.*

Gerald's breath hitched. The error. What was the error?

He had used Form 7-C. *Violation of Licensing Regulations.*

The Ghost had said he was not a Core. Form 7-C did not apply.

But the Ghost had attacked him, which had taken his clipboard. It had stamped DENIED.

That was not correction.

That was punishment.

Unless the punishment was part of the correction.

Gerald looked at the rat. The rat was watching him.

Its eyes were black and deep, and they held no animal instinct.

They held calculation.

"Subsection D," Gerald said again. "What was the evidence?"

The rat tilted its head.

"You did not prove intent."

Gerald froze. "Intent?"

"To operate a dungeon without a license," the rat said. "You must prove that the entity *knowingly* operated without a license. The Appeal did not know it was a ghost.

It thought it was a core. Therefore, no intent.

No violation."

It was absurd. It was legalistic. It was exactly the kind of nonsense that had gotten Gerald fired from the Homeowners Association board in 2014.

But it was System law.

Gerald pulled out a pen. He uncapped it. He looked at the blank space on his clipboard where the stamp had hit. The ink was still wet.

"Then I need to prove intent," Gerald said.

The rat nodded.

"Or you need to find a different violation."

Gerald looked around the lobby. The walls were cracked. The air was stale. The lighting was dim.

"Ventilation," Gerald said. "The ventilation is poor. That is a violation of Code 4.2.

It does not require intent.

It requires measurement.

"

The rat shook its head.

"The Appeal controls the environment. He can change the air quality instantly. You cannot cite him for a condition he can alter."

"Then I need to cite him for something he cannot alter."

Gerald walked to the center of the room. He looked at the floor. The concrete was poured. It was solid.

"Foundation," Gerald said.

"Code 1.1. Structural Integrity. A dungeon core is responsible for the structural integrity of its footprint.

If the footprint is compromised, the Core is liable.

"

The rat paused.

"The Appeal is not a Core."

"He is occupying a space," Gerald said.

"He is altering the structural load of the door frame.

He broke the door. That is a structural violation.

It does not matter if he is a Core. It matters that he broke the building.

"

The rat's eyes widened. A tiny, almost imperceptible shift.

"Code 1.1 applies to all entities occupying municipal subterranean space," Gerald said. "It is not limited to Cores. It is limited to *occupants*."

He wrote. His hand moved faster now. The tremor was gone. Replaced by the rhythmic scratch of a professional who knew exactly where to place the decimal point.

**CITATION 4097-A.** **VIOLATION: STRUCTURAL DAMAGE TO LOAD-BEARING ELEMENT.** **LOCATION: MAIN ENTRANCE.** **ENTITY: APPELLATE PROCESS (SELF-EXECUTING).** **EVIDENCE: PHYSICAL FRACTURE OF DOOR FRAME. SEE ATTACHED PHOTOGRAPH (SEE ATTACHED PHOTOGRAPH).**

Gerald looked at the door. He had not taken a photograph.

He looked at the System interface. *Photo Log.*

There was no camera.

Gerald felt a surge of frustration. He had the law. He had the logic. But he lacked the evidence.

The rat hopped off the desk. It walked to the door. It sniffed the crack in the frame. It looked back at Gerald.

"I can provide testimony," the rat said.

Gerald looked at the rat.

"You are a spawn. Your testimony is inadmissible in a Hearing."

"I am a spawn with a legal degree," the rat said. "I was a municipal clerk in my previous life. I filed forms for thirty years. I know the rules."

Gerald stared at the rat. A rat with a legal degree.

It was possible.

The System absorbed memories. It absorbed skills. It absorbed people.

"Subsection D," Gerald said. "Will you testify?"

"Yes," the rat said. "But there is a cost."

"What cost?"

"The Appeal will know you are challenging him again. He will escalate. He will not use fire. He will use *time*."

Gerald frowned.

"Time?"

"He can delay your paperwork," the rat said. "He can slow your thinking. He can make your hands shake. He can make you forget the word for 'load-bearing'."

Gerald looked at his hands. They were steady. But he felt a heaviness in his mind. A fog.

"Then we must be fast," Gerald said.

He turned to the door, and he walked toward it. The frame was indeed cracked.

The wood was splintered.

The metal hinge was bent.

Gerald placed his hand on the crack. He focused. He needed to create evidence. He needed to log it.

He opened the System menu. *Compliance Audit.*

He selected *Structural Inspection.*

The System scanned the door. A progress bar appeared. It moved slowly.

**SCANNING... 1%...**

The air grew cold. The lights flickered.

**SCANNING... 5%...**

A sound came from the other side of the door. A scratching. Like a pen on paper.

**SCANNING... 12%...**

Gerald held his breath. He kept his hand on the frame. He needed to stay there until the scan was complete.

The scratching grew louder. It sounded like typing. Fast, furious typing.

**SCANNING... 25%...**

The door handle turned.

Gerald did not move.

The door opened.

The man in the suit was there. He was not wearing a suit anymore.

He was wearing a lab coat.

White, stained with ink.

His face was blurred. His features shifted, like a photograph left in the rain.

"You are interrupting my work," the man said. His voice was multiple voices layered together. Gerald's voice.

The rat's voice. The Inspector General's voice.

"I am documenting a violation," Gerald said.

"You are violating the privacy of the Appeal," the man said. "You cannot inspect a ghost."

"I am inspecting the damage you caused," Gerald said. "To the building. To the public infrastructure."

The man smiled. It was the same thin, terrible smile. But it was wider now. It showed too many teeth.

"Code 1.1," the man said.

"Structural Integrity. Yes. I know that code.

I wrote it."

Gerald's hand tightened on the frame.

"You wrote it?"

"I am the System," the man said. "Or a piece of it. A piece that remembers the rules better than you do."

The progress bar stalled.

**SCANNING... 40%...**

The man reached out. He did not touch Gerald. He touched the air next to Gerald. He snapped his fingers.

The lights went out.

Total darkness.

Gerald could not see his hand. He could not see the door. He could not see the rat.

But he could hear the rat.

"Keep your hand on the frame," the rat whispered. "Do not let go. If you let go, the scan fails."

Gerald gripped the wood. The splinters dug into his palm. He felt blood. Warm, sticky blood.

The darkness was absolute. It pressed against his eyes.

It pressed against his mind, and he felt a sudden urge to let go. To sit down. To take a break.

The thought was not his own. It was imported.

*Why inspect?* the voice whispered. *It is just a door.

It is just wood.

You are tired, Gerald. You have been working for thirty-one years.

Take a break.*

Gerald gritted his teeth. He thought of the mold spores. He thought of the water leak. He thought of the Compliance Rating. 47/100.

He thought of the form, and he kept his hand on the frame.

**SCANNING... 41%...**

The darkness whispered louder. *You are not an inspector. You are a mistake. You are a glitch. Let it go.*

Gerald's fingers went numb. He could not feel the wood. He could not feel the blood.

But he could feel the rat. The rat had climbed onto his shoulder. Its weight was heavy. Its claws dug into his shirt.

"Hold it," the rat hissed. "Hold it, Gerald. Hold it like you held the line in 1998. Like you held the line against the zoning board."

Gerald remembered. The zoning board.

The noise.

The shouting. The pressure. He had stood in the corner.

He had not moved, and he had not spoken. He had simply held the file.

And they had backed down.

He held the file.

He held the door.

**SCANNING... 60%...**

A light flickered. Dim, orange.

The man in the lab coat was standing in front of him. He was holding a pen. A red pen.

"You are wasting time," the man said. "The scan will fail. I will delete the log."

"Delete it," Gerald said. His voice was flat. "Then I will cite you for destruction of evidence."

The man stopped. The pen hovered over the air.

"If you delete the log," Gerald said, "you admit the violation exists. Because you cannot delete what does not exist."

The man's eyes narrowed. The blurred features sharpened for a second. He looked like Gerald.

Older. Greyer.

"Subsection D," the man said. "You are bluffing."

"I am a building inspector," Gerald said. "We do not bluff. We measure."

The man looked at the progress bar.

**SCANNING... 85%...**

The man raised the pen. He struck it against the air.

A sound like a thunderclap shook the room.

Gerald staggered. His hand slipped.

The rat screamed.

"HOLD IT!"

Gerald slammed his hand back down, and he slammed it with all his strength.

He slammed it into the splintered wood. He felt the bone in his wrist crack.

He did not let go.

**SCANNING... 99%...**

**SCANNING COMPLETE.**

**VIOLATION CONFIRMED.** **CODE 1.1. STRUCTURAL DAMAGE TO LOAD-BEARING ELEMENT.** **LIABILITY ASSIGNED: APPELLATE PROCESS.** **PENALTY: TEMPORARY LOSS OF COHERENCE.**

The man in the lab coat froze. His edges blurred.

His form became fuzzy, and he looked confused. He looked angry.

"No," the man said. "This is not right. I am the Appeal. I am the System."

"You are a violation," Gerald said. He pulled his hand back. Blood dripped onto the floor. He did not wipe it.

The man's form destabilized. He flickered. He looked like a bad signal on an old television.

"I will appeal this," the man said. His voice was breaking up. "I will appeal this to the Inspector General."

"You are appealing to yourself," Gerald said. "That is a conflict of interest."

The man screamed. It was a digital scream. A sound of tearing data.

He vanished, and the lights came on.

The rat fell off Gerald's shoulder. It hit the floor with a soft thud. It did not move.

Gerald looked at his clipboard.

**CITATION 4097-A ISSUED.** **STATUS: ACCEPTED.** **COMPLIANCE RATING ADJUSTMENT: +5.** **NEW RATING: 52/100.**

Gerald exhaled. His wrist throbbed. His hand was shredded.

He looked at the door. It was still broken.

He looked at the rat. It was still unconscious.

A new notification appeared.

**SYSTEM ALERT: INSPECTOR GENERAL AUDIT SCHEDULED.** **DATE: TOMORROW.** **SUBJECT: UNCONVENTIONAL CITATION METHODS.** **WARNING: FRAUD INVESTIGATION INITIATED.**

Gerald stared at the alert. Fraud.

He had cited a ghost for structural damage. The System had accepted it. But the Inspector General did not like it.

He looked at the rat. He picked it up. It was warm. It was breathing.

"Subsection D," Gerald said. "We need a lawyer."

The rat opened one eye. It was bloodshot.

"We need a bribe," the rat said.

Gerald looked at his blood-stained hand. He looked at the cracked door. He looked at the dark corner of the room where the man had stood.

He had won the battle. He had cited the Appeal. He had raised his rating.

But the Inspector General was coming. And the Inspector General did not care about code. The Inspector General cared about order.

And Gerald had just broken the most important rule of all.

He had improvised.
