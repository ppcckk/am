Chapter 376: Code 11 Structural Remediation

# Chapter 376

The shovel struck the bedrock with a sound like a hammer hitting an anvil in a cathedral.

Gerald Hutchins watched the rat ascend through the translucent floor of the void. The rodent was not merely climbing; it was engineering its way up, clawing into the metaphysical substrate of the dungeon's foundation with a tool that should not have existed. A rusted, six-inch trowel. Standard issue for municipal groundskeeping.

Vane stared at the rat, his face a mask of fractured light.

"That is not a spawn mechanic," the entity whispered. "That is an anomaly."

"It is a corrective action," Gerald said. He did not look at Vane.

He looked at the rat.

"Code 11.4. Structural integrity maintenance. If the foundation is compromised, the core is liable for remediation.

The rat is remediating.

"

"You are pausing the apocalypse with a rodent." Vane's voice cracked. "The fire is still frozen. The party is still dying. The System is watching."

"The System is reading," Gerald corrected. He turned his attention to the frozen adventurers.

They hung in the air, suspended in a tableau of agony, their flesh mid-blacken.

The knight's sword had melted into a puddle of slag at his feet. The mage's spell components were locked in amber-like stasis.

Gerald pulled a small, green notebook from his vest. He flipped it open. The pages were blank, save for a single line of text that had appeared moments ago: *Citation Validity: 4 Minutes. Appeal Window: Closed.*

"I need to serve the Citation," Gerald said. "I can't serve a frozen target. I have to thaw them to serve them."

Vane laughed, a harsh, barking sound.

"You'll kill them.

The fire is Class 4.

If you unfreeze time, they burn to ash in three seconds.

And if they burn, you lose the core. It's a trap, Gerald.

You're standing in a trap.

"

"It is not a trap," Gerald said. "It is a sequence."

He walked toward the frozen knight. The heat radiating from the magical flame was palpable, even in stasis.

It smelled of ozone and charred hair.

Gerald stopped three feet from the knight's suspended boots. He raised his pen.

"What are you writing?" Vane asked.

"A variance," Gerald said.

"You cannot issue a variance to a death sentence."

"I am issuing a variance to the definition of 'combustion,'" Gerald said. He wrote in the air. The ink hovered, glowing faintly. **VARIANCE 702-A: TEMPORARY RECLASSIFICATION OF COMBUSTION TO 'THERMAL ANOMALY' FOR PURPOSES OF EGRESS.**

Vane stepped closer.

"That is nonsense. Fire is fire."

"In the municipal code, fire is a hazard," Gerald said.

"In the Dungeon System, fire is a damage type. But a hazard is something you can mitigate without destroying the structure.

If I classify this fire as a thermal anomaly, it becomes a ventilation issue.

And ventilation issues do not kill you. They just make you uncomfortable.

"

"You are bending the physics engine," Vane hissed.

"I am interpreting the language of the license," Gerald said. "The System is a bureaucracy. Bureaucracies run on definitions. I am defining this fire as a nuisance, not a lethal force."

He tapped the floating text.

The world stuttered, and the blue flame did not reignite. Instead, it dimmed to a dull, smoky orange.

The adventurers gasped.

Air rushed into their lungs.

The knight fell forward, catching himself on his hands. He coughed, smoke pouring from his mouth.

The mage scrambled backward, eyes wide, staring at Gerald.

"Who..." the knight wheezed. "What did you do?"

Gerald did not answer. He turned to Vane.

"Serve the Citation," Gerald said.

Vane shook his head.

"You broke the rules. The fire should have killed them."

"The rules say I can cite violations," Gerald said. "They do not say I can't cite the violation before it causes harm. Preventative maintenance is the heart of my profession."

Vane looked at the adventurers, then at Gerald. His form flickered, unstable.

"You are a glitch, Gerald. You are a glitch in the code."

"I am a building inspector," Gerald said. "Glitches are just errors in the documentation."

The knight stood up. He was trembling. He looked at Gerald with a mixture of horror and awe.

"You... you stopped the fire."

"I cited it," Gerald said. "You are now under citation. You must vacate the premises."

The mage raised a hand. A spark danced on her fingertip.

"We are adventurers. We do not 'vacate.' We fight."

Gerald pulled a red clipboard from his pocket. It had not been there a second ago. The System had provided it. **Form C-1: Notice of Violation - Unauthorized Combat Within Residential Zone.**

"This is a residential zone," Gerald said. "My dungeon.

My sub-basement.

You are not authorized to engage in hostile actions. You are trespassing.

"

The mage fired. A bolt of lightning arced toward Gerald.

Gerald did not dodge, and he stepped to the left. The lightning missed him by an inch, striking the wall behind him.

The wall did not crumble.

It glowed, then absorbed the energy.

**PASSIVE RESISTANCE TRIGGERED: ELECTRICAL CODE COMPLIANCE.**

The lightning dissipated.

The mage stared at the wall.

"It... it ate it."

"Grounding," Gerald said. "I spent four hundred hours installing copper wiring in the north corridor. It is up to code. Your attack is non-compliant."

The knight drew his sword. The blade was chipped, but it was still a weapon.

"We are leaving," the knight said. "But we are not paying any fine."

"You must pay the fine," Gerald said. "It is in the bylaws."

"We don't have gold," the mage said.

"Then you provide labor," Gerald said.

The three adventurers froze.

"Labor?" the knight asked.

"Remediation," Gerald said. "Your party caused structural damage.

The fire charred the ceiling beams.

The lightning cracked the foundation.

You are responsible for the repair costs. Since you lack currency, you will serve your debt through manual labor.

You will help me bring my Compliance Rating up to 60.

"

The knight looked at Vane. Vane looked at Gerald. Vane's expression was unreadable, but his form was beginning to solidify, regaining its terrifying height.

"He is proposing a contract," Vane said. "A binding contract."

"It is a standard agreement," Gerald said. He held out the clipboard.

"Sign here.

You will clean the mold spores. You will install the missing egress signs. You will replace the water leak.

In exchange, I will waive the Citation and allow you to leave with your lives.

"

The mage looked at the knight. The knight looked at the melted sword. He looked at Gerald, who stood calm, waiting, pen poised.

"If we refuse?" the knight asked.

"Then I issue a second Citation," Gerald said. "For Failure to Abate Hazard.

That carries a debuff of -50% to all stats.

You will be too weak to fight. You will be too weak to run. You will be terminated by the System for non-compliance.

"

The mage swallowed.

"You are insane."

"I am thorough," Gerald said.

The knight stepped forward. He took the pen. His hand shook. He signed his name in the air. **Sir Kaelen of the Iron Vow.**

The mage followed. **Elara Nightbinder.**

The rogue, who had been silent, stepped forward last. He was missing an ear. He signed with a shaky scrawl. **Jinx.**

"Done," Gerald said. He pulled the clipboard back. **ACCEPTED.**

A golden light swept over the three adventurers. The burns on their skin faded, not healing completely, but stabilizing. The System acknowledged the contract.

Vane stepped forward. He towered over Gerald.

"You have three slaves now.

They will not help you.

They will betray you.

"

"They are contractors," Gerald said. "And I have already scheduled their first task."

He pointed to the ceiling.

"The ventilation system in Sector 4 is failing. The mold spores are thriving because the humidity is at 85%. You will install a dehumidifier."

"We don't know how to build one," the mage said.

"Gerald," Vane said, his voice dropping to a dangerous whisper. "Stop. You are playing a game you do not understand.

The Inspector General is coming. He saw the Citation.

He saw the Variance.

He is coming to audit you.

"

Gerald looked at Vane.

"Good."

Vane blinked. "Good?"

"I need an audit," Gerald said. "I have been operating in a gray area. I need an official ruling. I need to know if my interpretation of the code holds up under scrutiny."

"You will be destroyed," Vane said. "The Inspector General does not care about your interpretations. He cares about power. He will crush you for being weak."

"I am not weak," Gerald said. "I am compliant."

He turned to the adventurers.

"Move out. Sector 4.

Bring tools.

I will provide the blueprints.

"

The adventurers hesitated, then shuffled toward the edge of the void. The rat remained by Gerald's leg, holding its trowel.

Vane did not move.

"You think this is about building?" Vane asked.

"It is about existence.

The System wants to delete you.

It wants to delete all cores that play by the rules because they are boring. It wants chaos.

It wants blood.

"

"Then it will get blood," Gerald said. "But it will be blood drawn for medical examinations, not combat. It will be blood spilled only when safety protocols are breached."

Vane shook his head.

"You are missing the point.

The Inspector General is not a bureaucrat, and he is a hunter. He comes to cull. And he is already here.

"

The ground shook again. Not from the rat. From above.

The void above Gerald cracked.

A beam of white light pierced the darkness. It was not magical fire.

It was sterile.

Clinical.

A voice boomed from the light. It was not Vane's voice. It was louder, deeper, vibrating in Gerald's bones.

**"CITATION 376-ALPHA. SUSPECT: CORE DESIGNATION 'ASHFORD_SUB_B'. VIOLATION: UNAUTHORIZED INTERPRETATION OF STATUTORY CODE. SUBJECT IS REQUESTED TO PRESENT FOR IMMEDIATE AUDIT."**

Gerald looked up. The light coalesced into a figure. It was tall, wearing robes of pure white paper. Its face was a smooth, featureless oval.

The Inspector General.

Gerald straightened his vest. He adjusted his tie. He pulled out his pen.

"Come down," Gerald said. "I have the forms ready."

The figure descended. The air grew cold. The adventurers froze again, not from fire, but from fear. They huddled in the corner, watching.

The Inspector General stopped ten feet from Gerald. He did not speak. He reached out a hand. A scroll unrolled in the air, miles long, filled with red text.

**"YOU CITED A FIRE,"** the Inspector General said. The voice did not come from a mouth.

It came from the scroll itself. **

"YOU DEFROSTED IT.

YOU CREATED A VARIANCE.

THIS IS NOT IN THE MANUAL.

"**

"It is in the spirit of the law," Gerald said.

"The law is the letter," the Inspector General said. **"YOU ARE OUT OF CODE."**

Gerald looked at the rat. The rat was still digging. The rat had not stopped.

Gerald looked at the adventurers. They were terrified, but they were alive.

Gerald looked at the Inspector General.

"I have a counter-citation," Gerald said.

The Inspector General paused. The scroll stopped unrolling. **

"COUNTER-CITATION?"**

"Yes," Gerald said. "I am citing you."

The silence was absolute. Vane laughed, a shrill, terrified sound.

"You cannot cite the Inspector General. He is the System."

"He is a Core," Gerald said. "All Cores are subject to the Municipal Compliance Code. Even the Inspector General. Especially the Inspector General."

Gerald pointed to the white robes.

"Your robes," Gerald said. "They are made of paper. Flammable.

Highly flammable. And you are standing in a zone with active magical combustion residue.

You are a Class 5 Fire Hazard.

You have no fire suppression system on your person. You are blocking the primary egress route of this dungeon with your body.

"

The Inspector General's face remained blank. But the scroll trembled.

**"THIS IS ABSURD."**

"Fire code section 302.1," Gerald recited.

"No combustible materials shall be stored within twenty feet of an ignition source. You are the ignition source.

You are the combustible material.

You are the obstruction.

"

Gerald wrote in the air.

**CITATION: IMMEDIATE CLOSURE FOR FIRE SAFETY VIOLATIONS.**

The red text on the Inspector General's scroll turned black.

The white robes smoked.

**"YOU CANNOT,"** the Inspector General said. But for the first time, there was uncertainty in the voice. **"YOU CANNOT CITE ME."**

"The citation has been served," Gerald said. "You are now under arrest by the System for non-compliance."

The Inspector General raised a hand. The white light flared.

But the light did not hit Gerald.

It hit the rat.

The rat, still holding its trowel, looked up. It did not flinch. It dug deeper.

And the hole widened.

Beneath the hole, beneath the bedrock, beneath the concept of the dungeon itself, something else rose. It was not fire. It was not light.

It was paperwork.

Stacks and stacks of red tape, binding the Inspector General's arms.

Gerald smiled. A small, dry smile.

"The hearing," Gerald said, "will be interesting."

The Inspector General screamed. Not in pain. In frustration. As the red tape wrapped around him, tightening, he looked at Gerald with the only emotion his blank face could convey: rage.

**"YOU WILL REGRET THIS."**

"I am already in the file," Gerald said.

Gerald turned his back, walking into the dark.
