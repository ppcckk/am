Chapter 293: Unregistered Structural Modification

# Chapter 293

The clipboard was the first thing to go.

Gerald watched it dissolve into white static, his fingers scrabbling at the empty air where the form had rested. The surface of the audit plane was not black, as one might expect of the void, but a blinding, sterile white. It smelled of ozone and old paper.

His feet did not touch the ground. He hovered, suspended in a column of suspended dust motes that drifted in slow, deliberate circles.

Below him—if "below" had any meaning here—was a lattice of glowing green lines.

The structural blueprint of his dungeon. The 4,000 square feet of sub-basement beneath the Ashford Municipal Library.

Gerald tried to orient himself. He tried to find the north wall, the designated egress route, the load-bearing column he had personally reinforced with reinforced concrete and a polite note to the city planning department.

There was no north. There was only the grid.

And standing at the center of the grid was the Inspector General, which did not have a face. It had a stamp.

The entity was a towering construct of folded light, its limbs articulated by the sharp creases of legal documents. It held a ledger the size of a dining table. The pages flipped themselves with a sound like a whip crack.

Gerald tried to speak. His mouth moved, but the air in the audit plane was thick, resistant. He pushed against it. He tried to form a sentence, a defense, a reference to Section 12, Paragraph 4 regarding the definition of *Force Majeure* in the event of spontaneous combustion.

Nothing came out.

Instead, a red stamp materialized in the air before his eyes. It hovered there, rotating slowly, waiting for his signature. Or his lack thereof.

Gerald's vision blurred. The white light stung. He blinked, and for a fraction of a second, the sterile void vanished.

He was back in the sub-basement.

But he was not Gerald, and he was looking at Gerald.

The perspective was high, detached. The angle suggested a security camera, but the lens was wet. A rat's eyes, perhaps, or the multifaceted gaze of a mold spore that had gained sentience during the chaos.

Gerald was on his knees.

In the real world, the explosion had shattered the main junction box.

Sparks rained down like fireworks. Water sprayed from the broken pipe, pooling around Gerald's knees.

The man looked small, and he looked old. His tie was askew. His clipboard was gone, lost in the debris or dissolved in the transition.

He was vibrating. Not from fear, but from a frequency so low it rattled the teeth of every creature within ten feet.

Sandra watched from the shadows of a support pillar. She clutched her side, blood seeping through her fingers. She had used him. She had let the void-core detonate its own core, trusting Gerald's bureaucratic reflex to catch the fallout.

She saw the white light envelop him. She saw him fall through the floor, not into the earth, but into a space that did not exist on any municipal map.

Sandra's heart hammered against her ribs. She had never seen a Core enter the Audit Plane. The stories said it was a place of judgment. The stories said you did not come back with the same soul.

Gerald lifted his head. His eyes were blank, white orbs.

Then, he blinked. The whiteness faded. He looked at the puddle of water at his knees. He looked at the sparking wire.

He did not scream. He did not run.

He reached into his pocket.

Sandra flinched. She expected a weapon. A spell. A desperate, chaotic blast of mana.

Gerald pulled out a ballpoint pen, which was blue. Ink was leaking from the tip, staining his thumb.

He looked at the puddle. He looked at the wire. He looked at the debris blocking the emergency exit.

Gerald Hutchins, Municipal Building Inspector, Class IV, stood up.

His joints cracked. The sound was loud in the silent, flooded basement.

He walked to the nearest piece of rubble. A chunk of drywall. He kicked it aside, revealing the floor drain beneath.

The drain was clogged.

Gerald frowned. He tapped the side of his head, a gesture he used when recalling a specific code violation in the 1994 municipal handbook.

"Obstruction," he muttered. The word was dry, devoid of fear.

Sandra stayed hidden. She could not move.

Her mana reserves were depleted, and she could not intervene.

She could only watch.

Gerald reached down. He did not use magic. He did not use his Core's mana pool.

He used his hands. He dug into the sludge, pulling out wet drywall, a twist of copper wire, and a crushed energy bar wrapper.

He placed the debris in a neat pile.

Then he turned to the exposed wiring. It hissed, spitting blue sparks at his face.

Gerald did not flinch. He looked at the frayed insulation. He looked at the proximity to the water source.

"Distance violation," he said. "Less than six inches. Not per code."

He reached for the wire.

Sandra wanted to shout a warning. The current would kill him. Or worse, it would corrupt his Core.

Gerald grabbed the live wire.

The air smelled of burnt hair. His hand blackened at the fingertips. Smoke curled from his cuff.

He did not let go, and he twisted the wire. He pulled it away from the puddle. He isolated the circuit.

The sparks stopped.

Silence returned to the basement.

Gerald looked at his burnt hand. He flexed the fingers. They moved stiffly, but they moved.

He turned to the void-core, Core 404.

404 was a swirling mass of dark energy, trying to reassemble its shattered form.

It pulsed with anger. It had been cited. It had been suspended.

It had been erased from the local grid for thirty seconds.

When the suspension lifted, 404 roared. The sound was a physical blow, shaking the walls.

"YOU," 404 screamed. The voice was layered, a chorus of dying monsters. "YOU FILED A REPORT ON THE EXPLOSION."

Gerald adjusted his glasses. One lens was cracked.

"I filed Form 11-B," Gerald said. "Unlicensed Biological Entity. Subsection C: Unauthorized Combustion."

"You are a Core," 404 said. "You are part of the System."

"I am an Inspector," Gerald corrected. "And you are in violation of the Fire Safety Act, Article 7, Section 2."

Gerald raised his burnt hand. He pointed at 404.

"Your heat output exceeds the thermal tolerance of the surrounding infrastructure. You have failed to install a cooling system. You have failed to maintain a clear path to the exit."

404 laughed. It was a terrible, wet sound.

"I will consume you."

"You will be fined," Gerald said.

The System responded.

But not with a Citation.

A new notification flashed in Gerald's HUD. It was gold. It was bright. It was the first gold notification he had seen since Chapter One.

**AUDIT PROTOCOL: INSPECTOR'S DISCRETION**

**You have successfully identified a Critical Structural Failure during a State of Emergency.**

**Loophole Detected: The Inspector General's audit focuses on administrative errors. It does not account for on-site remediation performed under duress.**

**Action: You have physically corrected a code violation (electrical isolation) during the audit window.**

**Reward: Immediate suspension of pending closure.**

**Penalty: 404's suspension is extended due to 'Refusal to Remediate'.**

Sandra watched as the white light around Gerald faded. The audit plane had rejected the suspension. Gerald had done something no other Core had ever done. He had fixed the problem before the judge could rule on it.

He had inspected his way out of death.

Gerald looked at his hand. The skin was charred, black and peeling. He wiped the blood from his lip. He looked at Sandra.

"You can come out now," he said. "The area is secure."

Sandra stepped into the light. She limped toward him. Her eyes were wide.

"How?" she whispered. "The IG... it doesn't care about the damage. It cares about the paperwork."

"The paperwork," Gerald said, "is a description of the damage. If you change the damage, you change the paperwork."

He held up his burnt hand. The ink from the pen had smeared across his palm, mixing with the soot. It looked like a signature.

"I didn't just cite the explosion," Gerald said. "I cited the cause. And I removed the cause."

404 pulsed. The dark energy swirled violently.

It was not defeated, which was merely paused. The System's suspension prevented it from attacking, but it did not prevent it from growing.

And 404 was growing.

The dark tendrils were seeping into the floor drains. They were using the water Gerald had cleared as a conduit.

Gerald saw it. He saw the black sludge moving toward his foundation.

He looked at the library above. The weight of the books, the patrons, the entire municipal structure, rested on his foundation.

If 404 reached the support pillars, the library would collapse.

Gerald's flaw paralyzed him. He could not improvise. He could not create new spells. He could not fight.

He could only follow code.

And the code said that if a foundation is compromised, it must be shored up immediately.

Gerald looked at the rubble. He looked at the water. He looked at 404.

"I need more concrete," Gerald said.

Sandra stared at him.

"You don't have concrete."

"I have debris," Gerald said. "And I have the System."

He opened his Core Menu. It was a mess of red warnings and gold loopholes.

He scrolled past the combat tabs, and he scrolled past the spawn tabs.

He went to the Infrastructure tab.

*Build: Support Column.*

*Cost: 50 Mana.*

*Requirement: Raw material (Stone, Metal, Wood).*

Gerald looked at the debris pile. Wet drywall. Broken copper. Plastic wrappers.

It was not stone. It was not metal.

But it was structural waste, and he selected the build.

The System hesitated.

**Material Not Valid.**

Gerald's finger hovered over the cancel button. He could not improvise. If the material was invalid, he could not proceed.

His heart rate spiked. He felt the familiar nausea of uncertainty.

Then, a thought.

*Recycling.*

He looked at the water. He looked at the drywall.

He selected the build again. But this time, he targeted the water.

*Build: Slurry.*

*Cost: 10 Mana.*

*Effect: Temporary solidification of organic/industrial waste upon contact with catalyst.*

He didn't have a catalyst, and he looked at his burnt hand. The blackened skin. The ash.

Ash was a catalyst for cement.

Gerald looked at Sandra.

"Do you have a lighter?"

Sandra shook her head.

Gerald looked at the sparks that had stopped. He looked at the broken wire he had isolated.

He grabbed the wire. He rubbed it against the drywall.

A single spark jumped.

It hit the pile of wet drywall and plastic.

Nothing happened.

Gerald tried again. He rubbed harder. The friction burned his skin, but he ignored the pain.

Another spark.

The plastic melted.

Gerald grabbed a chunk of concrete from the floor. He smashed it into the melting plastic. He mixed it with the water from the leak.

He created a slurry, which was ugly. It was messy. It smelled of burning chemicals and damp basement mold.

But it was structural.

He poured it into the mold he had shaped with his hands. A small column.

It hardened. Not instantly, but enough to hold weight.

**System Alert: Non-Standard Construction Detected.**

**Compliance Rating: -5 (Aesthetic Violation).**

**Structural Integrity: +10 (Emergency Shoring).**

404 lunged. The dark tendril whipped toward the new column.

The column held.

404 screamed. It pulled back, confused. The dark energy could not dissolve the slurry; it was too dense, too heterogeneous.

Gerald looked at the column. It was crooked. It was ugly. It was a violation of every aesthetic standard in the municipal code.

But it was holding.

And it gave him time.

Gerald looked at the ceiling. Above the library. Above the city.

The Inspector General was still watching. The audit was still pending.

Gerald realized something, and the IG did not care about the dungeon. It cared about the *record*.

If the record showed that Gerald had acted within the bounds of emergency maintenance, the closure would be lifted.

But 404 knew this. 404 was trying to break the structure so that the collapse would be recorded as a failure of maintenance, not an act of war.

Gerald had to prove that the damage was external.

He had to prove that 404 was the aggressor, not him, and he looked at Sandra.

"I need you to do something," Gerald said.

"What?" Sandra asked. She was backing away. The dark energy was spreading.

"I need you to steal the citation," Gerald said.

Sandra blinked.

"What?"

"The IG is holding a citation for Core 404. It is in the audit plane. You have shadow step. You can enter the audit plane for a fraction of a second."

"I can't," Sandra said. "It will kill me."

"It will not," Gerald said.

"You will be citing it for 'Theft of Official Document'. The System will be confused. Confusion creates delay.

Delay gives me time to build the rest of the wall.

"

Sandra looked at him. He was insane. He was a bureaucrat who had turned his own soul into a legal loophole.

"Why me?" she asked.

"Because you are a thief," Gerald said. "And theft is a crime, not a violation.

The IG does not handle crimes.

It handles violations. If you steal the paper, the System has no category for the offense. It will default to 'Pending Investigation'.

"

Gerald held out his hand.

"Go."

Sandra looked at his burnt hand. She looked at the crooked column. She looked at the darkness creeping closer.

She vanished into the shadows.

Gerald turned back to his dungeon. He mixed another batch of slurry.

His hands shook. Not from fear. From the effort of holding the chaos at bay with nothing but mud and rules.

And then, a new notification.

**System Alert: External Audit Request Received.**

**Source: The Ashford Municipal Planning Committee.**

**Reason: Unregistered Structural Modification.**

Gerald froze, and he had not just cited a dungeon core.

He had attracted the attention of the people who signed his paycheck.

And they were coming to inspect the library.
