Chapter 305: Teeth and Paws

# Chapter 305

Consciousness returned not with light, but with the distinct, dusty taste of old paper.

Gerald blinked. His vision was low, ground-level, and framed by long, twitching whiskers.

A damp chill permeated his fur, and he tried to stand, but the mechanics were wrong. Too many joints in the legs. The center of gravity was low and precarious.

He tried to speak, to announce his presence as a registered Core, but only a wet, high-pitched squeak emerged.

Panic flared, hot and sharp, before the System interface slammed into his mind with the weight of a slammed binder.

**[System Alert: Temporary Body Swap Protocol Activated.]** **[Host Entity: *Rattus norvegicus* (Common Brown Rat).]** **[Duration: Until Structural Integrity Restored or Ejection Triggered.]**

Gerald sat on his haunches. The rat's nose twitched.

It was an involuntary reflex, driven by biology than intent, and it disgusted him.

He looked around. The reading room of the Ashford Municipal Library was a disaster zone of overturned chairs and scattered hardcovers. The hole in the floor, the one that had swallowed him moments ago, was sealed.

The wood grain was seamless. No gaps.

No splinters.

Perfection. It was the only thing that had ever made him feel safe.

But the voice still echoed in the dark corners of his new, small skull. *That's why you're an inspector.*

A shadow detached itself from the bookshelf to his left. It wasn't a person. It was a construct, a jagged tear in the air shaped like a man in a suit, but made of static and gray noise. The Inspector General.

The figure hovered above the rat. It had no face, just a swirling vortex of compliance data.

"You violated Subsection 4, Paragraph 12," the voice said.

It didn't use a mouth. The words vibrated against Gerald's eardrums, which were now sensitive and terrified.

"Unauthorized transition of consciousness. Failure to maintain primary anchor stability. This is a Class B infraction."

Gerald tried to stand on two legs. He wobbled. He settled back onto four, hating the dehumanizing posture, hating the way his tail curled involuntarily around his hind leg.

"I was falling," Gerald squeaked. The voice was thin, reedy, but the intent was clear.

He was using the System's translation protocol.

"Gravity is a constant. I did not choose the destination."

"Choice is irrelevant to compliance.

Outcome is mandatory," the Inspector General replied. The static figure drifted closer.

"Your Compliance Rating has dropped to 15.

The void is hungry, Mr. Hutchins. It eats code.

It eats structure. You are currently a violation of physical law.

"

Gerald's claws dug into the carpet, and the fibers were coarse.

He could feel the individual strands. This was not a dream.

The sensory input was too detailed, too nauseatingly real. The smell of old glue and decaying wood was overpowering.

"Appeal," Gerald said.

The static figure paused. The swirling noise slowed.

"You may request a Hearing," the voice said. "But you are not in a dungeon.

You are in a library.

And you are a rodent. The burden of proof is inverted. You must prove your existence is compliant.

The void will provide counter-evidence.

"

"Who is the void?" Gerald asked. He looked at the sealed floor. He looked at the rat's paws. They were pink and raw.

"The void is the space between the rules," the Inspector General said. "And you are about to be deleted."

The figure vanished, and the room went silent. The dust motes dancing in the shafts of light seemed to freeze.

Gerald felt a pressure build behind his eyes.

Not pain, exactly. A compression. Like a hydraulic press closing in on a metal sheet.

He stood up. The rat's legs shook.

*Move,* he told himself. *Measure.*

He scurried toward the nearest bookshelf. The wood was oak, solid, well-maintained.

He scratched at the base.

No rot.

No insect damage. He moved to the next shelf.

Same. He checked the ventilation grates near the ceiling. They were clogged with dust, a minor violation, but not enough to cause immediate structural collapse.

The pressure in his head increased. It felt like a migraine, but sharper. Metallic.

He needed a citation. He needed to assert his authority over the space. If he was part of the space, he could not be deleted. He had to be essential.

He ran to the center of the room, where the hole had been. He sniffed the floorboards.

The seal was perfect.

Too perfect. It had no expansion gaps. Wood expands.

Wood contracts, and a perfect seal is a lie. It is a structural lie.

Gerald bit into the floor.

It hurt.

His teeth were small, weak things. He bit again, harder. Splinters of oak pierced his gums.

He tasted blood, coppery and bright.

**[Action: Physical Inspection of Floor Seal.]** **[Result: Critical Failure. Material mismatch detected. Oak substrate bonded with synthetic void-resin. Expansion coefficient incompatible.]**

A notification pinged in his mind, bright and golden.

**[Violation Detected: Non-Compliant Bonding Agent.]** **[Citation Available.]**

Gerald froze, and he could cite the floor?

He was standing *on* it. But the citation wasn't for the floor.

It was for the entity that created the floor.

The void, and he focused on the System interface. The option to

"Issue Citation" pulsed red.

The target was not a person.

It was the anomaly. The hole that wasn't there.

*Serve it,* he thought. *You need to be present.*

He was present. He was the rat. He was the inspector.

He dragged his mind into the citation form.

His paws shook, but his mind was steady. This was work.

This was what he did, and he filled out the form.

**[Citation 305-A: Unauthorized Material Integration.]** **[Target: Void Entity (Local Manifestation).]** **[Violation: Use of non-structural adhesive causing stress fracture risk.]** **[Penalty: Immediate Structural Debilitation.]**

He hit submit.

The world tilted.

The air above the sealed floor rippled. The void tried to pull him back, to drag the rat's body into the sub-basement, into the true dungeon.

But the citation was a anchor.

It pinned him to the surface.

It declared him a legal part of the library's infrastructure.

A sound tore through the room. A low, grinding screech, like metal on stone.

The sealed floor cracked. Not because of damage, but because the void-resin shattered under the weight of the legal argument. A fissure opened, black and deep.

From the crack, a hand reached out, which was not a human hand. It was long, gray, and made of shadow. It grabbed the edge of the rug. It pulled itself up.

The Receiver.

It was smaller than Gerald remembered. Diminished. The void's influence was fading because of the citation.

The creature looked like a sketch of a monster, lacking detail, lacking definition. It had no eyes, just smooth, blank skin where they should be.

It looked at the rat.

"You served it," the Receiver rasped. Its voice was like dry leaves skittering on pavement. "You cited the void."

Gerald stood his ground. His tail lashed.

"The bond was defective. It was a hazard."

The Receiver tilted its head.

"You are a rat, Hutchins. Rats chew things. They do not cite them."

"I am a Municipal Inspector," Gerald squeaked. "And you are trespassing on private property without a permit."

The Receiver lunged, which was fast.

Too fast for a human. But Gerald had been thinking like a builder. He knew the room.

He knew the obstacles, and he dove under the overturned chair. The Receiver's claw slashed the air where he had been, knocking the chair over. Wood splintered.

**[Damage Taken: Minor. Furniture Breakage.]**

Gerald scrambled toward the circulation desk. It was heavy, metal, bolted to the floor.

He circled it.

The Receiver followed, its movements jerky, glitching. The citation was working. The debuffs were applying.

The Receiver's speed was dropping. Its form was becoming more unstable, flickering in and out of visibility.

Gerald jumped onto the desk. He looked down at the creature.

"Section 8," Gerald said. "Improper storage of hazardous materials. You are the hazard."

The Receiver hissed. It raised a hand, and a bolt of black energy shot toward the desk.

Gerald had anticipated it. He had moved to the far edge. The bolt hit the metal surface. It scorched the paint.

**[Fire Code Violation Detected: Electrical Discharge in Public Space.]**

Another citation option appeared.

Gerald didn't hesitate. He served it.

**[Citation 305-B: Prohibited Energy Emission.]** **[Penalty: Mana Discharge Lockout.]**

The Receiver's hand went limp.

The black energy dissipated into harmless smoke.

The creature screamed, a sound of tearing reality. It clawed at the desk, trying to pull Gerald off, but its strength was gone.

The debuffs were stacking. It was shrinking.

Gerald felt a surge of power. It wasn't magic. It was administrative force. It was the weight of the law, made physical.

He jumped down. He ran to the door.

It was locked.

A deadbolt. Simple. Secure.

He tried to turn the handle. It was too high. His paws were too small.

The Receiver was getting back up, which was larger now, feeding on the chaos. The citations were temporary.

He needed a permanent solution.

He needed to close the dungeon.

But he was the dungeon. And he was in a rat.

If he closed the dungeon, he closed himself. He would be trapped in this body forever. Or worse, he would be deleted along with it.

There was another way.

The fire alarm, and the library had a strict fire safety protocol. Manual pull stations. Red boxes on the wall.

Gerald ran to the nearest one. It was on the wall, waist-high for a human. For a rat, it was a mountain.

He climbed. He used the bookshelf as a ladder.

He slipped, caught himself, climbed again.

His claws scraped the wood.

His breath came in rapid, tiny gasps, and the Receiver was close. It was ten feet away. It could reach him.

Gerald reached the pull station. He grabbed the handle with his teeth. He pulled.

It didn't move. It was stiff. Rusted.

A violation.

**[Violation Detected: Malfunctioning Safety Equipment.]**

Gerald bit down harder. He put his back into it. He used his body weight. He leveraged his tail against the wall for traction.

*Snap.*

The metal broke. The handle gave way.

Alarms blared. Red lights flashed. The system in the library, the mundane, city-mandated system, woke up.

The noise was deafening. It was a frequency the Receiver could not handle. It was ordered, regulated, municipal sound. It was the sound of compliance.

The Receiver shrieked. It covered its blank face. Its form unravelled.

The static broke apart. It was being rejected by the environment.

The fire alarm was a code requirement, and the void was not. The environment chose the code.

The Receiver dissolved into mist, sucked into the ventilation shafts.

Silence returned, except for the ringing bells.

Gerald hung from the broken pull station, his teeth aching, his body trembling.

He looked at the floor, and the fissure was closing. The seal was reforming, but this time, it was honest.

Cracks showed.

Gaps showed. It was imperfect. It was real.

A door burst open.

A police officer rushed in, flashlight sweeping the room. He looked around, confused by the overturned furniture, by the smell of ozone, by the broken fire alarm.

He saw the rat hanging from the wall.

"Jesus," the officer muttered. He aimed his flashlight at Gerald. "Is that... is that a rat?"

Gerald held on. He didn't move. He waited.

The officer stepped closer. He reached out, trying to grab the rat.

Gerald dropped, and he hit the floor and ran. He didn't run toward the exit.

He ran toward the sub-basement stairs.

The door at the bottom of the stairs was open.

The void was gone, but the staircase remained.

He descended into the dark.

The air down there was cold. Damp. It smelled of mold and concrete. It smelled like his home.

He reached the bottom. The library sub-basement. His dungeon.

It was empty. No mold spores. No water leak. Just the bare concrete floor and the hum of the System.

Gerald sat in the center of the room. He waited for his body to return. For the rat's body to dissolve, for his consciousness to snap back to the Core.

But it didn't happen.

**[System Alert: Body Swap Protocol Extension.]** **[Reason: Anchor Instability.]** **[New Timer: 24 Hours.]**

Gerald looked at his paws. They were still pink. Still small.

He was still a rat.

And then, from the shadows of the far corner, a light appeared. Not the red of a citation. Not the black of the void.

It was green. A soft, pulsing green light.

A figure stepped out. It was a woman, tall, wearing a suit that looked like it was made of green leaves and glass. She held a clipboard.

"Mr. Hutchins," she said. Her voice was calm, professional.

"I represent the Department of Ecological Balance. We've been monitoring your recent citations."

Gerald squeaked, trying to form words.

"I... inspected."

"You created an ecological hazard," she said.

She pointed at the corner.

"By citing the void, you disrupted the local mana flow. You killed the receiver, yes.

But you also destabilized the ley lines beneath the library.

"

She held up a paper. It was a citation. For *him*.

**[Citation 306-A: Uncontrolled Mana Discharge.]** **[Penalty: Mandatory Ecological Remediation.]**

Gerald's heart stopped. He looked at the paper. He looked at the woman.

"Remediation," he squeaked.

"You must restore the flow," she said. "You must build a new structure. Something that filters the mana. Something compliant."

She turned to leave.

"Gerald," she said, pausing. "If you don't fix this by morning, the System will classify your dungeon as a Biohazard. And they will condemn it."

She vanished.

Gerald sat in the dark. He was a rat.

He had no hands, and he had no tools. He had twenty-four hours to build a mana filter in a concrete box, using only his teeth and his paws.

And he had never done carpentry in his life.

He looked at the concrete floor. He looked at his claws.

He picked up a stone, and he dug.
