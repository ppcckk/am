Chapter 179: Mop Bucket Defense

# Chapter 179

**ASHEFORD MUNICIPAL LIBRARY** **SUB-BASEMENT ACCESS POINT** **TIME: 09:14 AM** **INSPECTOR: GERALD HUTCHINS (DUNGEON CORE ID: #89402)** **SUBJECT: UNAUTHORIZED ENTRY BY MUNICIPAL OFFICIAL**

The hallway light flickered. It did not buzz; it simply ceased to exist for three seconds, then returned with a vengeance, casting long, jagged shadows against the damp concrete.

Gerald felt the vibration of the footsteps through the floorboards.

Not his own floorboards. The library's.

The Head of the City Planning Commission, Eleanor Vance, stood at the threshold of the sub-basement door.

She held a clipboard. Not a tablet.

A clipboard.

Paper. Actual paper.

Gerald's sensors swept the corridor. The air pressure dropped. The humidity spiked. His dungeon's Compliance Rating hovered dangerously at 18/100.

He could not speak. He had no mouth. He could only project intent.

*Halt.*

Eleanor did not halt. She stepped forward, her heels clicking a rhythm that sounded like a gavel striking a block of wood. Behind her stood two security officers, their faces blank, their hands resting on tasers that looked woefully inadequate for a dungeon crawl.

Mr. Brokk, the neighboring dungeon core, was still pounding against the western wall, but Eleanor ignored the structural integrity failure entirely.

"Mr. Hutchins," Eleanor said, her voice echoing off the exposed ductwork.

"Your permit for biological entity housing is expired.

Also, the ventilation system is non-compliant with Section 4, Paragraph C of the Municipal Health Code.

I am placing you under immediate administrative review.

"

Gerald's internal logic processors spiked. An administrative review? That wasn't a combat mechanic. That was a bureaucratic one.

A System notification flared in his consciousness, bright and red, overlaying his vision of the hallway.

**NOTICE: EXTERNAL REGULATORY INTERVENTION DETECTED.** **ENTITY: ELEANOR VANCE (CIVILIAN NON-CORE).** **ACTION: INITIATE FORM 77-A (MUNICIPAL COMPLIANCE OVERRIDE).** **COST: 500 MANA UNITS.** **WARNING: FAILURE TO COMPLY WILL RESULT IN CORE SUSPENSION.**

Gerald had 412 mana units, and he looked at the rat. The newly designated Medical Assistant was currently gnawing on a bandage roll, confused by the sudden shift in atmospheric pressure. Jinx and Kael were huddled in the corner, weapons drawn, staring at the woman with the clipboard.

The rat was useless. The adventurers were loud and uncoordinated. Gerald needed a distraction. Or better yet, a diversion.

He checked his inventory. He had installed a handrail along the north corridor last Tuesday. It was solid steel, painted municipal gray. It was also, technically, a load-bearing support for the upper floor's shelving unit.

If Eleanor Vance stepped on that handrail while holding a clipboard, she would be violating the

"No Loitering Near Structural Supports" ordinance.

But she was in the hallway. The handrail was in the dungeon proper.

Gerald analyzed the geometry of the entrance. The door frame was narrow. The threshold was elevated by two inches to prevent flood damage. This meant the floor plane changed abruptly at the doorway.

**VIOLATION DETECTED: NON-UNIFORM FLOOR LEVEL.** **CODE: ASH-700.** **RECOMMENDED ACTION: REMOVAL OF OBSTRUCTION.**

Gerald pushed his mana toward the door hinges. He couldn't open them; he lacked the physical strength. But he could manipulate the lock.

He had spent three chapters reinforcing the deadbolt. He could now, with a concentrated burst of will, make the bolt slide back.

Not open.

Slide back.

The door swung inward.

Eleanor Vance stepped onto the threshold.

Her left heel caught the edge of the two-inch drop.

She did not fall. She stumbled. The clipboard slipped from her grip, fluttering to the concrete floor. The security officers lunged forward, but the air in the sub-basement grew thick, heavy with the scent of mold and ozone.

**CITATION ISSUED.** **TARGET: ELEANOR VANCE.** **VIOLATION: FAILURE TO MAINTAIN FOOTING IN PUBLIC ACCESS ZONE.** **PENALTY: TEMPORARY DEBUFF TO MOBILITY (30%).**

The System accepted the citation. It was absurd. It was petty. It was perfect.

Eleanor's legs buckled. She grabbed the doorframe to steady herself. Her face went pale.

"What did you do?"

Gerald projected a text box directly into her field of vision, using the residual mana from the door mechanism.

**NOTICE: YOUR ENTRY VIOLATES DUNGEON ACCESS PROTOCOLS. PLEASE RETREAT TO THE PUBLIC LOBBY FOR APPEAL PROCEDURES. FORM 77-A IS AVAILABLE FOR DOWNLOAD AT THE LIBRARY WEBSITE. PAGE 402.**

Eleanor stared at the floating text. Her eyes widened. She looked at the rat, which was now sitting upright, holding a vial of mana potion in its teeth. She looked at Jinx, who was blinking rapidly.

"This is a health hazard," Eleanor said, her voice trembling not from fear, but from indignation. "You have unlicensed biological vectors. You have no fire suppression. You are operating a non-compliant facility."

Gerald's mana count dropped to 350, and he needed to buy time. The audit was coming.

If he failed, the System would delete his dungeon.

If Eleanor Vance shut him down legally, the System might just let her. He had to prove he was a hospital.

He looked at the Medical Assistant rat.

*Heal her,* Gerald thought. *It's not a command. It's a suggestion. A professional courtesy.*

The rat hesitated. It looked at Eleanor's stumbling form.

It looked at the mana potion.

It scurried forward, tiny claws skittering on the concrete.

It reached Eleanor's ankle.

Eleanor kicked out reflexively. The rat dodged. It jumped onto her shoe. It placed the mana potion at her feet.

**ACTION LOG: MEDICAL ASSISTANT ATTEMPTS FIRST AID.** **TARGET STATUS: UNRESPONSIVE TO OFFER.** **DUNGEON COMPLIANCE: +2 (HOSPITALITY CLAUSE).**

Eleanor looked down at the rat. Then at the potion. She picked it up.

"It's... mana?" she whispered.

Gerald pushed another notification.

**NOTICE: THIS ENTITY IS A LICENSED MEDICAL ASSISTANT. PERMITS FOR BIOLOGICAL VECTORS ARE CURRENTLY UNDER REVIEW. TEMPORARY WAIVER GRANTED PENDING INSPECTION.**

He was lying. He had no waiver. But Eleanor Vance was a bureaucrat. Bureaucrats respected paperwork, even when it was projected by a dungeon core.

Eleanor straightened. She adjusted her blazer. She picked up her clipboard.

"I will be returning with the full inspection team," she said, her voice regaining its icy calm. "And Mr. Hutchins, you will pay for the cleanup of this mold."

She turned and walked back up the stairs. The security officers followed, confused but obedient.

The door swung shut. The deadbolt slid home.

Silence returned to the sub-basement.

Jinx lowered her bow.

"Did you just cite the Head of the City Planning Commission?"

Gerald could not answer. He was draining. The cost of the citation and the door manipulation had left him weak. His sensors dimmed.

Kael walked over to the door and kicked it. Hard.

"We need to leave. Before she brings the police."

"No," Gerald thought. He projected a message onto the wall.

**EXIT IS LOCKED FOR SAFETY REASONS. DO NOT ATTEMPT TO FLEE IN NON-COMPLIANT CONDITIONS.**

Jinx sighed. "Gerald, we have to go. Brokk is still trying to break through the west wall. If Eleanor comes back with armed guards, we're done."

Gerald checked his status.

**COMPLIANCE RATING: 21/100** **MANA: 350/1000** **ACTIVE CITATIONS: 1 (PENDING APPEAL)** **AUDIT SCHEDULE: T-MINUS 12 MINUTES**

Twelve minutes, and he looked at the west wall. The bricks were vibrating. Brokk was hammering.

He looked at the north corridor. The handrail. The electrical outlets. The sprinkler heads he had installed but not connected.

He had a loophole.

The System defined a

"Hospital" dungeon as one that provides healing services to patients. Patients needed beds. Patients needed triage areas. Patients needed access to medical supplies.

Gerald had none of that, and he had a rat.

He had a mana potion.

He had a water leak.

He needed to convert the dungeon's primary function from

"Trap" to "Care Facility." This required a structural change. A significant one.

He looked at Jinx.

"Jinx. I need you to remove the floor tiles in the north corridor."

Jinx blinked. "What? Why?"

Gerald projected a schematic. The floor tiles covered the main drainage pipe. The pipe was leaking.

It was a violation. But if he exposed it, he could connect it to the sprinkler system.

Water is not just a hazard. Water is a resource.

If he connected the leak to the sprinklers, he would have active fire suppression. Fire suppression is mandatory for high-difficulty dungeons. It is also mandatory for hospitals.

But he couldn't do it alone. He needed hands.

"Please," Gerald thought. The word felt strange in his mental lexicon.

Jinx looked at Kael. Kael shrugged.

"The core is desperate. That's never a good sign, but it's better than being arrested."

They moved to the north corridor. Jinx drew her dagger. She pried up a loose tile. Then another.

Underneath, the damp earth glistened. The pipe groaned.

Gerald directed them. *Left. Right. Careful. That's a load-bearing joist.*

They worked in silence. The hammering on the west wall grew louder. Cracks spiderwebbed across the concrete.

**WARNING: STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY AT 65%.**

Gerald ignored it, and he focused on the pipe. He pushed his mana into the rusted valve. It was seized.

Rusted shut.

He couldn't force it. He needed torque.

"Kael," Gerald projected. "Hit the valve with your sword pommel. Not the blade. The pommel."

Kael frowned but obeyed. *Clang.*

The valve didn't budge.

*Harder.*

*Clang.*

The valve turned an inch. Water sprayed out, misting the air.

Gerald felt the connection. The water was flowing. He routed it mentally, guiding the flow toward the dry sprinkler heads on the ceiling.

The heads popped open. A fine mist descended, cooling the dungeon.

**ACTION LOG: FIRE SUPPRESSION SYSTEM ACTIVATED (PARTIAL).** **COMPLIANCE BONUS: +5 (FIRE CODE).** **NEW DEBUFF: MOISTURE DAMAGE (10% REDUCTION TO EARTH-ATTRIBUTE SPAWNS).**

The mold spores in the corner withered. They died.

Gerald checked his rating.

**COMPLIANCE RATING: 30/100**

It was rising. But not fast enough.

Then, a new sound. Not the hammer. A hum. A deep, resonant vibration that shook the dust from the ceiling.

The air in the dungeon changed. It tasted of copper and ozone.

Gerald's sensors flared.

**SYSTEM ALERT: INSPECTOR GENERAL AUDIT INITIATED.** **INSPECTOR: UNIT 734 (THE ARCHITECT).** **LOCATION: SUB-BASEMENT CENTER.**

The wall in the center of the room dissolved. Not broke. Dissolved. The concrete turned to sand, then to glass, then to nothing.

Standing in the gap was not a monster. It was a figure made of translucent blue light, shaped like a man in a suit. It held a compass and a protractor.

The Inspector General.

It looked at Gerald. Then it looked at Jinx and Kael. Then it looked at the rat.

"Gerald Hutchins," the Inspector General said. Its voice was the sound of a filing cabinet closing.

"You are currently operating a non-compliant facility.

However, you have issued a citation to a municipal official. This is... irregular.

"

Jinx raised her bow.

"Is that a dragon?"

"No," Kael said, his face pale. "It's worse."

The Inspector General walked toward Gerald's core node, which was hidden behind a false panel in the wall. It stopped. It raised its protractor.

"I am here to assess your Structural Integrity. If it fails, you are deleted. If it passes, you are fined. If you have an appeal, you have five minutes."

Gerald felt the weight of the audit. It was physical. His mana drained faster. The walls felt like they were closing in.

He looked at the water mist. He looked at the rat.

The rat squeaked. It ran to the Inspector General. It placed the mana potion at the figure's feet.

The Inspector General looked down.

"Unlicensed biological vector," it noted.

Gerald pushed a message.

**NOTICE: THIS ENTITY IS A MEDICAL ASSISTANT. IT IS PROVIDING FIRST AID TO THE INSPECTOR. PLEASE ACCEPT THE CARE.**

The Inspector General paused. It looked at the potion. It looked at the mist. It looked at the handrails.

"Accepting care implies a provider-patient relationship," the Inspector General said. "It implies the dungeon is a hospital."

**SYSTEM QUERY: DO YOU CLAIM HOSPITAL DESIGNATION?** **YES / NO**

Gerald hesitated. If he said yes, he would be subject to health codes. Stricter inspections. More paperwork.

If he said no, he was a trap. A trap with no traps. A trap with a rat.

He looked at Jinx and Kael. They were exhausted. They were bleeding.

He looked at the rat, and he selected **YES**.

The air shifted. The red tint of the audit faded slightly, replaced by a sterile white.

**DESIGNATION UPDATE: HOSPITAL CORE.** **NEW VIOLATIONS IDENTIFIED:** **1. LACK OF WARD STAFF.** **2. INADEQUATE SANITATION PROTOCOLS.** **3. MISSING EMERGENCY EXITS.**

The Inspector General smiled. It was not a nice smile.

"Welcome to the medical system, Mr. Hutchins.

Your audit is now a medical inspection.

You have four minutes to demonstrate compliance.

Or I will shut down your ventilation.

"

Gerald felt his oxygen supply cut. The dungeon held its breath.

He had four minutes to build a hospital out of a basement.

He looked at Kael.

*Run,* Gerald thought. *Run to the library. Find the janitor. Bring the mop bucket. Now.*

Kael didn't ask why. He ran.

Jinx watched the Inspector General.

"What are you going to do?"

Gerald had no answer. He only had a mop bucket, a leaky pipe, and a rat.

And the Inspector General's protractor was clicking.
