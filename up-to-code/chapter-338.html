Chapter 338: Tapes the Void

# Chapter 338

The Gilded Maw did not roar. It hummed.

A low, resonant vibration that rattled the teeth of every sentient being within a hundred yards. Kaelen, a Level 45 Paladin with a shield the size of a dining table, felt his helm vibrating against his skull. He gripped the haft of his warhammer until his knuckles turned white, watching the breach in the eastern wall of the municipal library's sub-basement.

Smoke poured out of the hole, thick and smelling of burnt sugar and ozone.

"Steady," Kaelen growled. His voice echoed in the confined space.

His party formed up behind him. Elara, the rogue, was crouched low, checking the tension on her crossbow strings.

Thorne, the mage, was already weaving a barrier spell, his fingers twitching in complex patterns.

They had heard the rumors. A new Core. Weak.

Fragile.

Easy pickings for loot and experience points.

But the smoke wasn't behaving like normal dungeon exhaust. It wasn't rising. It was pooling, staying low, hugging the floor like a fog bank.

"Ventilation issue?" Elara whispered, pulling her scarf up over her nose.

"Probably," Kaelen said. "Don't breathe too deep. The Maw specializes in corrosive auras."

He stepped forward, his boot crunching on broken brick. The breach was ragged, jagged edges of stone jutting out like teeth. But the stones weren't just broken. They were measured.

Kaelen frowned. He stopped.

He looked at the pile of rubble.

Someone had stacked the debris. Not in a chaotic heap, but in neat, uniform piles, sorted by material density. Concrete here.

Brick there. Rebar coiled into perfect circles.

"What is that?" Thorne asked, pointing his staff at the sorted trash.

"Debris management," Kaelen said, confused. "Who sorted this?"

Before anyone could answer, a figure emerged from the smoke.

It wasn't a monster.

It wasn't a skeleton or a slime, which was a man. Older, in his mid-fifties, wearing a faded blue windbreaker that had seen better decades.

He held a tape measure in one hand and a clipboard in the other. He looked at Kaelen's party with the detached curiosity of a man looking at a misplaced filing cabinet.

Gerald Hutchins adjusted his glasses. He did not look at the Paladin's glowing sword or the Mage's crackling energy. He looked at the floor.

"Excuse me," Gerald said. His voice was flat, devoid of threat or fear. "You are standing in a designated egress route."

Kaelen blinked. He lowered his hammer slightly.

"We are adventuring."

"I have not received a permit for an adventure group within this radius," Gerald said. He tapped his clipboard with a pen.

"Form 12-A.

Group Size Declaration. Did you file it?

"

Elara snorted, and she stood up, crossing her arms.

"Old man, get out of the way.

The Gilded Maw is coming through that hole.

We're going to kill it. You're just in the way.

"

Gerald looked at her. He looked at the hole. He looked back at her.

"That hole is four feet wide," Gerald said.

"So?" Kaelen asked.

"According to Municipal Code Section 7.4, subsection C, any breach in a load-bearing structure exceeding three feet requires immediate shoring and a safety perimeter of at least six feet. You are standing inside the perimeter."

Gerald pointed to a piece of chalk he had drawn on the floor. A circle.

"You are violating the perimeter."

Kaelen laughed. It was a dry, nervous sound.

"Are you a Core?"

"I am a Dungeon Core," Gerald said. "And you are trespassing on a non-public easement."

Thorne raised his staff.

"Enough talk. Let's clear the room before the Maw arrives."

The mage fired a bolt of arcane energy. It streaked toward Gerald.

Gerald did not dodge. He did not shield himself. He simply took a step back.

The bolt hit the wall behind him.

Dust fell. But the wall didn't crack. It didn't crumble. The stone absorbed the impact, glowing faintly with a dull, grey light.

Thorne stared.

"That wall... it took a Level 10 spell and didn't flinch."

Gerald looked at the scorch mark. He shook his head.

"Poor electrical insulation.

I noted the deficiency in my last audit.

The Gilded Maw's lightning traps are not properly grounded. This wall, however, is reinforced with a Class 4 grounding lattice, installed during my third renovation cycle.

"

Kaelen lowered his hammer completely.

"What are you doing?"

"Measuring," Gerald said. He pulled out a small, silver device. It looked like a laser level, but it glowed with a soft blue light. He aimed it at the breach.

A holographic line appeared in the air, stretching across the gap, and the line was red.

"Gerald," the woman from the previous hour—the System Auditor, now hiding in the shadows of the library stacks—whispered. Her voice was tight. "Don't provoke it.

The Maw is a Class B predator. It reacts to structural instability.

"

Gerald ignored her. He looked at the red line.

"The Gilded Maw," Gerald said, addressing the smoke, "is attempting to expand its footprint into this sub-basement. It is failing to maintain its own structural integrity. The pressure differential between its lair and this room is causing micro-fractures in the shared wall."

He turned to Kaelen.

"Your party is standing on unstable ground. If the wall collapses, you will be crushed."

Kaelen looked down. The floor beneath his boots was indeed vibrating more violently than before. Cracks were spider-webbing out from the breach.

"The Maw is pushing," Elara said, her face pale. "It's trying to widen the hole."

"It cannot," Gerald said. "Not while I am standing here."

He walked toward the breach.

"Stop!" Kaelen shouted. "You'll be killed!"

Gerald did not stop. He walked right up to the jagged edge of the hole. The smoke billowed around him. He could hear the chittering of goblins and the hiss of acidic spores from the other side.

He held up his clipboard.

"Notice of Violation," Gerald said. His voice was amplified by the System, resonating in the minds of everyone present.

"I, Gerald Hutchins, Core ID 8842-A, hereby cite the Dungeon Core known as 'The Gilded Maw' for the following infractions:"

He read from the paper. The words hung in the air, glowing gold.

"One: Unauthorized structural modification of a shared boundary wall without a variance permit."

"Two: Failure to maintain adequate ventilation in the breach zone, resulting in hazardous air quality."

"Three: Creation of an unsecured tripping hazard via debris accumulation."

The smoke stopped swirling. The chittering halted.

From the darkness of the breach, a massive eye opened. It was yellow, slit-pupiled, and filled with confusion.

A voice boomed from the hole, distorted and wet.

"Who... are... you?"

Gerald held up the tape measure. He clicked it. The metal tape extended, snapping into the gap, touching the Maw's own stone wall.

"I am your inspector," Gerald said. "And I have measured your violation at three inches of unauthorized expansion."

The Maw roared. A wave of force blasted out, knocking Kaelen and his party backward. Kaelen's shield snapped into place, absorbing the blow. He slid ten feet across the floor, boots scraping sparks.

"Shields up!" Kaelen yelled. "It's attacking!"

"It is not attacking," Gerald said calmly. "It is reacting to a Citation."

A golden envelope formed in the air, hovering before the breach. It was not paper. It was pure light.

The System had accepted the citation.

The Maw's eye widened. The yellow iris contracted.

"Appeal," the Maw hissed. "I appeal."

"Appeal window open," the System voice intoned, echoing in the chamber. "Duration: Thirty seconds. Participants must present valid structural logic."

Gerald looked at the Auditor. She was watching him, her face unreadable.

"I need you to help me," Gerald said.

The Auditor stepped out of the shadows.

"With what?"

"Proof," Gerald said. "The Maw will argue that the expansion is necessary for its own survival. It will cite the Prime Directive. It will claim that its dungeon is failing, and that expansion is the only solution."

"Can it do that?" Kaelen asked, struggling to his feet.

"It can try," Gerald said. "But it has to prove it. And I need to prove that its failure is due to poor maintenance, not structural necessity."

The Auditor tapped her tablet. She projected a hologram. It showed the Gilded Maw's internal layout. Red zones pulsed in the corners.

"Look," the Auditor said.

"The Maw's dungeon has three hundred and twelve active fire hazards. Its ventilation system is clogged with organic matter. It is not failing because it is small.

It is failing because it is dirty.

"

Gerald nodded.

"Good."

He turned back to the Maw.

"Present your defense," Gerald said.

The Maw's voice was louder now. Angry.

"MY CORE IS SHRINKING. THE EARTH PRESSURES ME. I MUST EXPAND TO SURVIVE. THE SYSTEM ALLOWS SELF-PRESERVATION."

Gerald looked at the Auditor.

"Is the earth pressure real?"

The Auditor checked her data.

"The surrounding geology is stable. The Maw's claim is false. It is hoarding resources, not saving itself."

Gerald smiled. It was a small, thin smile.

"Objection," Gerald said.

The System paused.

"State your objection," the System voice said.

"The Maw claims self-preservation," Gerald said. "But under Code Section 9, Paragraph 1, 'Self-Preservation' does not exempt a Core from Basic Hygiene Standards. A Core cannot claim survival necessity if its own negligence caused the hazard."

He pointed to the hologram the Auditor had projected.

"See this?" Gerald pointed to a clogged vent shaft in the Maw's lair. "This is a maintenance violation. It is not a structural necessity. It is a choice."

The Maw roared again. But this time, the sound was different. It was pained.

The golden envelope glowed brighter.

"Citation upheld," the System announced.

"Penalty calculation," Gerald said.

"No," the Maw shrieked. "No!"

Gerald closed his eyes. He visualized the penalty.

He didn't want to destroy the Maw, and he wanted to fix it. Or rather, he wanted to force it to fix itself.

"Penalty: Temporary Structural Lock," Gerald said.

The Auditor looked at him.

"That's not in the standard penalty list."

"It's a loophole," Gerald said. He opened his eyes. "The System allows for 'Corrective Measures' instead of 'Destructive Measures' if the Core demonstrates intent to comply. I am demonstrating intent to facilitate compliance."

The System processed this. The golden envelope turned green.

"Corrective Measure initiated," the System voice said.

A shockwave rippled out from the breach. But it wasn't destructive. It was constructive.

The jagged edges of the hole in the wall smoothed out. Stone flowed like liquid, knitting itself together.

The gap narrowed.

Four feet.

Three feet. Two feet.

The Maw screamed. It wasn't screaming in pain. It was screaming in frustration.

"No! I need space! I need air!"

"You have air," Gerald said. "I installed an intake fan in the ventilation shaft ten minutes ago. Check your logs."

The Maw fell silent.

The hole sealed completely. The wall was whole again.

Kaelen lowered his shield. He looked at Gerald.

"You just... fixed a dungeon?"

"I cited it," Gerald corrected. "And it complied."

The smoke cleared. The air smelled of clean stone.

Kaelen looked at his party.

"We're leaving."

"Wait," Gerald said.

Kaelen stopped.

"What?"

Gerald walked over to the spot where the breach had been. He picked up a single brick that had fallen from the ceiling during the sealing process. He held it up.

"This brick," Gerald said. "It is a relic of the violation. It is evidence."

He placed the brick in his pocket.

"You are free to go," Gerald said. "But please ensure you file your exit report at the front desk. The library closes at five."

Kaelen shook his head. He signaled to his party. They walked away, wary and confused.

Gerald turned to the Auditor.

"They'll be back," she said. "The Gilded Maw is furious. And there are other cores nearby.

The Iron Grotto. The Silt Warren.

They will hear about this.

"

"Let them hear," Gerald said.

He pulled out his clipboard. He wrote.

"What are you doing?" the Auditor asked.

"Planning," Gerald said.

He flipped to a new page.

"The Gilded Maw will appeal the Corrective Measure," Gerald said. "They will try to prove that the intake fan I installed is insufficient for their biomass. They will argue that I starved them."

The Auditor frowned.

"You installed a standard industrial fan. It's more than enough."

"But they will use the 'Biomass Expansion Clause'," Gerald said. "It's a loophole. If a Core's population exceeds a certain density, they are allowed to expand their footprint to prevent overcrowding. The Maw will claim their goblin population has exceeded the maximum capacity of their current footprint."

The Auditor's eyes widened.

"You're right. The Maw spawns fast. They could have pushed the population cap."

"Exactly," Gerald said. "So I need to prove that their overcrowding was caused by poor spawn management, not spatial limitation. I need to audit their spawn logs."

"I don't have access to their spawn logs," the Auditor said.

"You do," Gerald said. He pointed to the wall. "You're an Auditor.

You have root access to the System's diagnostic tools.

You can pull the spawn timestamps.

"

The Auditor hesitated.

"If I do that, I violate the Non-Interference Protocol. I could be de-listed."

Gerald looked at her. He didn't plead. He didn't beg. He just looked at her with the flat, expectant stare of a man waiting for a form to be signed.

"If you don't pull the logs," Gerald said, "the Maw will win the appeal.

It will expand. It will break into the library. It will kill adventurers.

And then, when the System audits me for failing to prevent it, I will be de-listed.

"

He paused.

"And if I am de-listed, who will check the library's fire suppression system?"

The Auditor looked at the sealed wall. She looked at the clean floor. She looked at Gerald's tired eyes.

She tapped her tablet.

"I'm pulling the logs," she said.

Gerald nodded. He turned away, walking toward the center of the dungeon.

The air was still. The hum of the Maw was gone.

But then, a new sound.

A rumble.

Deep below.

Beneath the library.

Beneath Gerald's dungeon.

The Auditor looked up. Her face went white.

"That's not the Maw," she said.

Gerald stopped. He listened.

The rumble was rhythmic. Thump. Thump. Thump.

Like a heartbeat.

Or like footsteps.

Huge footsteps.

"Gerald," the Auditor said. "The seismic sensors are picking up movement. Massive movement. Coming from the basement level below us."

Gerald looked at his spawn list.

One rat.

One leak.

Three mold spores, and he looked at the Auditor.

"How deep is the basement?" he asked.

"It's... it's not on the map," the Auditor said. "The city plans stop at Sub-Level 2. This is Sub-Level 3."

Gerald felt a chill that had nothing to do with the ventilation system.

"Who owns Sub-Level 3?" he asked.

The Auditor shook her head.

"No one. It's void space."

"Not anymore," Gerald said.

The floor beneath his feet cracked.

A hand burst through, which was made of roots and dirt. It was the size of a car.

Gerald took a step back.

He pulled out his tape measure.
