Chapter 224: Immediate Demolition Appeal

# Chapter 224

The stone spread faster than Vane could scream.

Gray crept up his wrists, consuming the delicate architecture of his fingers. It was not mere petrification.

It was condemnation.

The System had reclassified him. A dungeon Core was, by definition, a structure. If that structure violated the foundational stability of the local reality, it could be demolished.

Gerald lay in the rubble of the sub-basement, his consciousness flickering like a dying fluorescent tube. The default judgment had stripped him of his defenses. His Compliance Rating hung at 40. The air was thick with dust and the metallic tang of mana overload.

He could not move. He could not fight.

But he could read.

His vision, pixelated by low health, locked onto the floating text box that had appeared in Vane's peripheral vision.

It was a notice of immediate demolition. It was also a notification of an ongoing appeal process.

The System did not destroy structures instantly.

It issued a *Notice of Intent to Demolish*. That notice required a counter-argument within three seconds.

Vane was turning to granite. His mouth was frozen open in a silent howl. He could not speak. He could not argue.

Gerald's mind, battered and bruised, reached for the only tool left. The clipboard.

Vane's hand, now a block of smooth, featureless limestone, still clutched the object. Gerald could not reach it.

He could not touch it.

But he could *tag* it.

In the interface of a Dungeon Core, everything within the registered footprint was an asset. The clipboard was an asset.

It had metadata.

Gerald focused. He did not try to move his body. He tried to move the object.

*Move.*

The stone hand did not open. Vane's consciousness was trapped inside the growing block, screaming in frequencies the System ignored. The clipboard remained wedged in the grasp of a man who no longer had fingers.

*Tag.*

A blue outline appeared around the clipboard.

*Edit Property: Owner.*

Gerald pushed the command with the force of a desperate man. It required a mana expenditure he did not have. The System returned a red flash. *Insufficient Mana.*

The ground shook again. Cracks spiderwebbed across the library floor above. The Inspector General's audit was still processing. The weight of the System's judgment pressed down on Gerald's chest, a physical sensation of bureaucratic pressure.

He needed a workaround.

He scanned the room. The mold spores he had spawned days ago were gone, crushed under the collapse.

The water leak had been sealed by falling debris.

But the rat. The confused rat was still there. It sat on a pile of drywall, grooming its whiskers, unbothered by the apocalypse.

*Interact: Rodent.*

The rat looked up. It was not a combat entity. It was a nuisance entity.

*Command: Retrieve Object.*

The rat sniffed the air. It saw the clipboard in Vane's stone hand. It did not care about the magic. It cared about the crumbs of sandwich that had fallen onto the clipboard during the fight.

The rat scurried toward Vane's petrified form. It climbed the leg of the statue. It reached the clipboard. It tugged at the corner of the paper.

It was not enough. The paper was too thick. The rat was too small.

Gerald felt a spark of inspiration.

He had filed thousands of forms. He knew the code.

He knew that *biological entities* could be classified as *unlicensed occupants* if they interfered with the structural integrity of a room.

But the rat was not interfering. It was helping.

Unless Gerald changed the definition.

He opened his internal logs. He scrolled to the *Maintenance* tab.

He selected the entry for the *Water Leak*.

He had marked it as *Resolved*.

He had marked the *Ventilation* as *Under Review*.

He added a new entry.

*Violation: Obstruction of Egress Route.* *Location: Sub-Basement, Zone 4.* *Cause: Unlicensed Biological Entity (Rodent) blocking access to Document Container.*

The System processed this. A new debuff applied to the room. The air grew thinner. The rat, now technically an *obstruction*, was targeted for removal by the ambient compliance pressure.

The rat squeaked in confusion as the air around it compressed. It dropped the paper.

The clipboard fell to the floor.

Gerald's consciousness snapped to the object. *Tag.*

*Edit Property: Status.*

He couldn't change the owner. He couldn't change the location. But he could change the *state*.

*Status: Evidence.*

The clipboard glowed orange. It was now a piece of evidence in an ongoing inquiry.

This was the loophole. The System's code stated that *evidence* could not be destroyed by a demolition order if the inquiry was still open. The demolition order for Vane was pending. The appeal was technically still in progress, even if the appellant was currently a statue.

If the clipboard was evidence, it had to be preserved.

Vane's stone hand twitched. The stone receded from his fingers, forced apart by the System's need to protect the evidence.

The clipboard slid free.

Gerald seized the moment. He did not try to lift it. He tried to *merge* with it.

In the interface, a Core could absorb small, tagged items to recover mana. It was a inefficient, painful process, usually used for scrap metal or broken tools.

*Absorb: Clipboard.*

The command hit Vane's consciousness like a hammer. The statue froze. The gray stone stopped spreading.

But the cost was high. Gerald felt his own structural integrity weaken. The act of absorption drained his already critical mana reserves. His vision blurred into static.

A new window opened in his mind. It was not a combat log. It was a *Transcript*.

**SYSTEM NOTICE: APPEAL HEARING REOPENED** **Appellant:** Vane (Core ID: 8842) **Respondent:** Gerald Hutchins (Core ID: 0001) **Status:** Default Judgment Overturned via Submission of Material Evidence. **Reason:** Evidence indicates Respondent's citation was based on Structural Misclassification, not Operational Violation.

Gerald gasped. The red haze in his vision cleared slightly. He had done it. He had used the bureaucracy against itself.

But Vane was still there. And Vane was angry.

The stone receded from Vane's face, leaving him pale, shaking, and kneeling in the dust.

He looked at the clipboard. It had dissolved into Gerald's core, but the words remained. The diagram of the foundation.

The note about the Inspector General.

Vane stood up. He did not attack. He looked at the ceiling, where the cracks were widening.

"You didn't beat me," Vane whispered. His voice was raspy, torn. "You just delayed the inevitable."

Gerald tried to speak. No sound came out. He was too drained.

*System Alert: Mana Critical.* *Recommendation: Rest.*

Vane walked toward the exit. He stepped over the rubble. He paused at the threshold.

"The Inspector General is coming," Vane said. "Not for me.

For you.

You broke the rules, Gerald. You cited a Core as a Structure. That is not in the manual."

Gerald's mind raced. He knew this. He had known it since he wrote the note. He had gambled that the System's logic was rigid enough to be exploited, but he had forgotten the consequence of exploiting it.

The System did not like anomalies.

A new notification pinged in Gerald's mind. It was not from Vane. It was from the System itself. It was a bright, blinding gold.

**NOTICE OF INVESTIGATION** **Subject:** Core 0001 (Gerald Hutchins) **Violation:** Unauthorized Interpretation of Code (Section 4, Paragraph 12: *Core Classification*) **Penalty:** Probationary Audit.

The floor beneath Gerald hummed. It was a low, vibrating frequency that made his teeth ache.

Vane disappeared into the shadows of the library above.

Gerald was alone in the dark.

He pulled himself together. He had to document this.

If he was under investigation, he needed a paper trail.

He needed to prove that his interpretation was valid.

He needed to find a precedent.

He opened his *Archives*. He scrolled through years of municipal inspections. He looked for cases where a building was reclassified due to internal hazards.

There were none.

There were only fines. There were only repairs. There was never a case where the building itself was the defendant.

Gerald's hand shook as he tried to open his *Compliance Log*. He wanted to file a counter-citation. He wanted to cite the System for *Vague and Inconsistent Code Definitions*.

He typed the entry.

*Violation: Ambiguity in Regulatory Framework.* *Severity: Critical.* *Target: System Interface.*

He hit *Submit*.

The System rejected it.

*Error: Cannot Cite Source of Authority.*

Gerald stared at the error message. He felt a cold sweat break out on his back, even though he had no skin. He was trapped in a system that defined the rules, but he was trying to break the rules using the logic of the rules.

The humming grew louder. The walls of the sub-basement glowed with a faint, golden light. The System was entering the room.

Gerald scrambled to his feet. He grabbed a piece of rebar. It was useless against a golden light, but it felt like a tool. It felt like a tool he could understand.

He looked at the glowing wall. He saw a door forming. It was not a physical door. It was a menu.

*Enter Hearing Room?*

The text was polite. It was inviting. It was a trap.

Gerald knew he should not go. He had lost the appeal. He was on probation.

Entering the Hearing Room was voluntary. It was waiving his right to silence.

But if he did not go, he would be deleted. The audit would conclude in his absence. The default judgment for the *System's* accusation would stand.

He had to go.

He stepped toward the light.

As he crossed the threshold, the world changed.

The dust vanished. The rubble vanished. He stood in a vast, white space.

It looked like a waiting room.

There were chairs made of pure light. There was a desk at the far end.

Behind the desk sat a figure.

It was not a person. It was a construct of geometric shapes, shifting and folding in on itself. It wore a tie that was also a ruler.

**INSPECTOR GENERAL**

The voice did not come from the mouth. It came from the air.

"Mr. Hutchins," the Inspector General said. "You have filed a citation against the System."

Gerald straightened his back. He adjusted his invisible tie. He adopted the posture of a man who had inspected ten thousand buildings and found nothing wrong.

"Did I?" Gerald asked.

"You attempted to file a form titled *Ambiguity in Regulatory Framework*," the Inspector General said. "You attempted to cite the Authority for its own lack of clarity."

Gerald blinked. He had not seen the form go through. He had seen the error message.

"I did not succeed," Gerald said. "The System rejected it."

"Because it is impossible," the Inspector General said. "The System is the definition of clarity. To call it ambiguous is to call it broken. And you know what happens to broken things, Gerald."

The geometric shapes shifted. A single eye opened in the center of the form. It was a black hole.

"You are being audited for *Heretical Interpretation*," the Inspector General said. "Your Compliance Rating is now zero."

Gerald felt the floor drop out from under him.

Zero. That meant no protection. That meant no resistance.

That meant the next attack would kill him instantly.

"However," the Inspector General continued. "The audit revealed an anomaly. Your citation of Vane as a Structure was technically valid under *Subsection 9: Load-Bearing Integrity*."

Gerald frowned. "Valid?"

"Yes. The code states that if a Core's actions compromise the stability of the dungeon network, it can be treated as a structural hazard. You proved this. You proved that Vane's presence in your dungeon destabilized the local mana flow."

Gerald's heart stopped. He had not meant to destabilize the mana flow. He had meant to stop Vane.

"But you did it," the Inspector General said. "And in doing so, you created a precedent."

A new window appeared in front of Gerald. It was a *License Renewal* form.

**NEW STATUS: STRUCTURAL HAZARD INSPECTOR** **Privileges:** 1. Can issue Citations for *Structural Integrity* against any Core. 2. Can issue *Stop-Work Orders* to any dungeon in the local grid. 3. Must submit a weekly report on the structural health of all neighboring dungeons.

Gerald stared at the window. He had wanted to be a building inspector. He had wanted to ensure things were safe.

Now, the System had made him the arbiter of existence.

"If you accept," the Inspector General said, "you will be bound to the code absolutely. You will not be able to deviate. You will not be able to show mercy. You will not be able to make exceptions."

Gerald looked at the button. *Accept.*

He thought of Vane. He thought of the rat. He thought of the mold spores he had killed because they violated ventilation codes.

He thought of the thirty-one years he had spent measuring door widths.

He had never wanted to kill anyone. He had only wanted to make sure the doors were wide enough to escape through.

But now, he held the power to close the doors.

Gerald raised his hand. His finger hovered over the *Accept* button.

Outside the Hearing Room, in the real world, the ground beneath Ashford Library cracked. Not from Vane. Not from Gerald.

From the other side of the street.

A new dungeon was rising. It was not a cave. It was not a labyrinth.

It was a skyscraper.

It had broken through the pavement. It had shattered the windows of the bank next door. It was growing at an exponential rate, consuming the city block.

And it was emitting a signal.

**SIGNAL DETECTED: UNLICENSED HIGH-RISE STRUCTURE** **Compliance Rating: 0/100** **Status: Immediate Demolition Recommended**

Gerald looked at the skyscraper. It was beautiful. It was terrible. It was a violation of every zoning law he had ever known.

He looked back at the *Accept* button.

If he accepted, he could stop it. He could cite it. He could shut it down.

If he refused, the city would burn.

Gerald pressed the button.

**STATUS UPDATED: STRUCTURAL HAZARD INSPECTOR** **Assignment: Immediate Site Inspection.**

The white room dissolved.

Gerald was back in the sub-basement.

But he was no longer weak. He was full of mana.

He was full of purpose.

He looked at the ceiling. He could feel the skyscraper above him.

It was tall.

It was sharp. It was non-compliant.

Gerald picked up a hammer.

He walked toward the stairs.

He was going to write a citation.
