Chapter 10: Citation Requires Medium

# Chapter 10

The rat was not enough. Gerald had tried three times.

He spawned a dungeon rat, small and fast and cheap to maintain, and directed it toward the rival's entrance corridor with the citation loaded into its behavioral subroutine.

The System rejected it each time. The error message was terse and unhelpful: CITATION REQUIRES PHYSICAL MEDIUM. NOT CONSTRUCT.

Gerald sat with the error for a long time.

In his old life, this was the equivalent of a contractor showing up with a verbal complaint and expecting you to write it on a napkin. You couldn't.

The form had to be on the form, and the form had to be the right form.

He needed a physical object. Something the System would recognize as a document.

Not a thought.

Not a subroutine.

A thing.

His Core body pulsed in the floor of Room 3. He felt the dungeon as an inspector does a structure, except the building was his body and he was the load-bearing wall. He had limited material manipulation.

He could reshape walls, adjust floor tiles, grow stalactites, and could not, as far as he'd tested, create new matter from nothing.

But he could repurpose.

Room 7 had a decorative stone slab.

Useless. Purely aesthetic, placed there by whatever instinct had guided his early dungeon growth before he started thinking like an inspector. It was granite.

Flat.

Roughly the size of a clipboard.

Gerald reached for it through the dungeon's substrate and pulled. The slab cracked, shifted, rose from the floor on a column of reshaped stone until it stood upright like a lectern. He focused his Core's awareness on the surface and started carving.

The System recognized the act immediately.

It offered him a template. CITATION FORM 11-A. STANDARD FORMAT.

He accepted it without reading. He'd filled out ten thousand of these.

The words etched themselves into the granite in a pale luminescent script. He filled in the fields with the precision of a man who had once been sent back to the field office to retype a form because he'd used a ballpoint instead of a carbon copy.

Responding Core: Unidentified.

Designation pending.

Violation Category: Structural, Electrical, Fire Safety, Egress, Signage. Number of Violations: 11.

Severity: Critical (4), Major (5), Minor (2). Compliance Rating Adjustment: -11 points. Appeal Window: 72 hours from service.

He finished. The tablet hummed with System authority.

It was a citation.

A real one. A physical one.

Now he needed to get it to the rival's dungeon, and the rat was still available. Gerald spawned a second one, larger, with a flat back modified to carry weight. He bonded the citation tablet to the rat's spine with a material weld that would hold until the rat reached the rival's entrance threshold. Then he sent it.

The rat crossed Gerald's dungeon, down the main corridor, through the gap marking the end of his territory. It scurried across forty meters of bare earth. It reached the rival's entrance. It crossed the boundary.

The System pinged.

CITATION SERVED.

TARGET CORE IDENTIFIED: VORRATH. COMPLIANCE RATING REDUCED BY 11 POINTS.

ALL SPAWNED ENTITIES RECEIVE PROPORTIONAL DEBUFFS.

APPEAL WINDOW OPEN: 72 HOURS.

Gerald felt the debuff land on the rival's dungeon like a change in barometric pressure, and he could sense it through the proximity of their territories.

Vorrath's spawn entities, whatever they were, just got slower.

Weaker.

Their regeneration dropped. Their fire vulnerability spiked.

Eleven violations. Eleven points of debuff. Not enough to kill.

Enough to hurt. Enough to make a hero raid that dungeon and think they were getting lucky when the dungeon was just limping.

Gerald allowed himself one full breath of satisfaction. It lasted four seconds.

Then the System pinged again.

COUNTER-CITATION FILED.

SOURCE: VORRATH. TARGET: GERALD. VIOLATIONS: 3.

CATEGORY: ZONING.

Gerald stopped breathing.

Zoning. He knew that word.

He'd enforced it for thirty years.

Setbacks, easements, use classifications, dimensional overlays. The System's zoning rules were entirely different. He'd never encountered it before.

He'd never even seen the category appear on a form.

The counter-citation listed three violations. All zoning. All against him.

Violation 1: Dungeon footprint exceeds maximum permissible area for Core Classification Tier 1. Violation 2: Sub-level excavation encroaches on protected substrate layer (depth: 2.3 meters below permitted maximum). Violation 3: Adjacent unclaimed territory absorbed without proper annexation filing.

Gerald read them twice. His hands, if a crystal the size of a fist could be said to have hands, were shaking.

He checked.

He checked his own dungeon's dimensions against the System's zoning grid, which he'd never queried because he'd never had a reason to.

The grid existed. It was always there.

He'd simply never looked at it.

Violation 1 was accurate. His dungeon footprint was fourteen percent over the Tier 1 maximum. He hadn't known. He'd grown the dungeon organically, room by room, following his instincts and his code knowledge, but code knowledge about buildings was not the same as code knowledge about dungeon zoning.

Violation 2 was accurate. Room 11.

He'd excavated it to accommodate a thermal vent because the ventilation code required a certain airflow volume.

He'd gone too deep. Two point three meters below the permitted maximum for his tier.

Violation 3 was accurate.

He'd absorbed territory on the eastern edge to widen his egress corridor. He hadn't filed anything.

In his old life, you'd file an annexation with the planning department, and the System had an equivalent. He hadn't known it existed.

Three violations. His own Compliance Rating dropped by three points.

He felt it immediately.

A dull ache in his Core.

A thinning of the walls around him. His spawn entities, the three cave crabs and the colony of luminescent beetles, all felt slightly weaker.

Vorrath was citing him back. And Vorrath knew the zoning code.

Gerald had twenty-four hours to file an appeal before the debuffs became permanent. The appeal triggered a Hearing. Both Cores would argue structural logic.

If Gerald lost, the violations stood and his Compliance Rating stayed reduced. If he won, the debuffs reversed and he got a bonus.

He needed to win.

The Hearing opened exactly when the System said it would.

A neutral space. Gerald's awareness expanded into it and found Vorrath already there. The rival's Core was vast.

Gerald sensed its edges like the perimeter of a shopping mall.

Hundreds of rooms. Dozens of levels.

A Core that had been growing for decades.

Vorrath's presence in the Hearing space was a low rumble. A vibration that Gerald felt in his substrate.

You served me. Vorrath's communication was not language exactly. It was more like a structural stress report translated into meaning.

A citation. Eleven violations.

Yes.

Gerald replied.

And I stand by them. The spacing on your load-bearing pillars is four feet over code.

The water channel has no membrane. The egress path is blocked. These are not opinions.

They are measurements.

You are a small thing.

Vorrath said. A Tier 1 Core. You have three rooms that meet code.

I have two hundred that do not.

You cite me and I cite you and we are both in violation. This is not justice.

This is mutual destruction.

Gerald considered this. It was not wrong. In his old life, he'd seen developers do exactly this.

Cite each other into oblivion. Two buildings at war, both condemned, both empty, both bleeding money.

But.

Gerald said.

Your violations are critical. Four critical.

That's not a paperwork issue. That's a ceiling that's going to collapse. That's a water feature that's going to electrocute something.

That's a blocked exit that's going to trap something. I don't care how many rooms you have.

Critical is critical.

And mine? Vorrath asked. Are my zoning violations critical?

No. Gerald said. They're not.

They're administrative.

I can fix them. I can file the annexation.

I can backfill Room 11.

I can apply for a footprint variance.

Then why did I file them? Vorrath asked.

Because you don't know the code.

You know building code.

You don't know dungeon zoning code. You violated it because you didn't know it existed.

That's not malice. That's ignorance. And I filed them to teach you.

Gerald felt something cold move through him. Not anger.

Recognition, which was exactly what the planning department did to new contractors. File violations they didn't know existed, force them into the system, make them learn the rules before they could operate.

But Vorrath wasn't the planning department. Vorrath was a rival. And the Hearing was the forum where that rivalry would be adjudicated.

The System prompted: HEARING PHASE 1. APPELLANT ARGUES. RESPONDENT ARGUES. JUDGE DECIDES.

Vorrath went first.

It laid out its argument with the fluency of someone who had studied the zoning code for years. The Tier 1 maximum was not arbitrary.

It was calibrated to Core energy output.

A Tier 1 Core could not sustain a footprint beyond the maximum without drawing on protected substrate energy. Gerald's excavation into the protected layer was not just a code violation. It was a resource theft.

The System treated it as such.

Gerald listened, and he understood.

He'd seen this argument in real life. It was the same logic as environmental impact zones.

You can't build past the setback line because the land beyond it serves a function you don't own.

His turn. Gerald spoke.

The zoning code, he said, contains a provision for remediation. Section 14, subsection C.

A Core may file a variance application within the appeal window.

If the variance is granted, the violation is cured. The debuff is reversed. The citation is void.

Vorrath's rumble shifted. A frequency change. Surprise, maybe. Or irritation.

Section 14, Gerald continued.

It's in the code. I found it in the first six minutes of the Hearing.

You cited me for violations I didn't know existed, which is fair.

But the code also provides a cure mechanism. I am filing a variance for the footprint. I am filing an annexation for the eastern absorption.

I am backfilling Room 11 to permitted depth. All three violations are curable.

The citation should be voided.

The System paused. Then it responded.

VARIANCE APPLICATION: ACCEPTED.

ANNEXATION FILING: ACCEPTED. BACKFILL ORDER: ACCEPTED.

ALL THREE VIOLATIONS CURED. COUNTER-CITATION VOIDED. RESPONDENT DEBUFFS REVERSED.

Gerald felt his Compliance Rating climb back. The ache in his Core faded. His cave crabs perked up.

But the System added one more line.

APPEAL OUTCOME: RESPONDENT PREVAILS. BONUS AWARDED: ONE ZONING CODE ENFORCEMENT ABILITY UNLOCKED.

A new tool appeared in Gerald's mental inventory. ZONING CODE ENFORCEMENT.

He could now audit the zoning grid, and he could now see the boundaries; could now, if he chose, cite Vorrath for zoning violations.

Because Vorrath had them too.

Gerald could see it immediately now that the ability was unlocked. Vorrath's dungeon sprawled across three separate zoning overlays.

It had absorbed territory without filing, which had excavated below permitted depth in at least six locations. It was a zoning violation machine.

Gerald could cite Vorrath for zoning. He could do it right now. He could add twenty more violations to the original eleven. He could crush the rival.

He started the citation form. He filled in the first field.

Then the System pinged.

INSPECTOR GENERAL AUDIT: SCHEDULED. TARGET: ALL CORES IN REGION 7. TIMELINE: 48 HOURS. PREPARATION WINDOW: 48 HOURS.

Gerald stopped. The Inspector General.

He had read about it in the System's documentation, like a city-wide inspection cycle.

Periodic.

Unavoidable. A full audit of every dungeon in the region.

Forty-eight hours. He had forty-eight hours to bring his own dungeon into full compliance with every code category before the Inspector General arrived. Every category.

Not just the ones he'd been focusing on. All of them.

Including the zoning code he'd just learned existed.

And he had to do it while Vorrath was still active. Still hostile. Still two hundred rooms of dungeon with decades of accumulated violations that the rival was probably already working to cure before the audit.

Gerald looked at the citation form he'd half-filled. Twenty zoning violations against Vorrath. He could serve them. He could send another rat.

But if he served them now, Vorrath would appeal. The Hearing would take time. Time he didn't have.

And if Vorrath won the appeal, Gerald would know that his rival was better at arguing code than he was.

That was possible. Vorrath had been doing this for decades.

Or he could wait. Let the audit come. Let the Inspector General find Vorrath's violations. Let the System do the citing for him.

But the Inspector General might not find them.

Audits were broad. They checked for the most obvious things. A Core that had been around for decades would have learned to hide its violations from casual inspection.

Vorrath would have learned to make its violations look like code.

Gerald stared at the half-filled form. His cursor blinked in the first field.

Then a new message arrived.

Not from the System.

From Vorrath. Direct.

A Core-to-Core communication that Gerald had never received before.

You unlocked zoning enforcement. Vorrath said.

So now you can see what I've done.

So now you can cite me. So now you have to decide. Cite me and spend your 48 hours in a Hearing I will win because I have argued code for thirty years.

Or don't cite me and spend your 48 hours fixing your own dungeon because the Inspector General will find your violations and I will not.

Gerald read it twice.

Vorrath was offering him a choice. Cite and fight. Or fix and survive.

The cursor blinked.

Gerald's awareness hovered over the SEND button.
