Chapter 344: Breaking the Rule

# Chapter 344

The stone fingers crushed the cartilage of Gerald's larynx.

Air escaped in a thin, wet hiss. Vision narrowed to a pinprick of blue light.

The rogue Core's face was inches away, a mask of shifting tectonic plates and malice.

He had been waiting in the rebar. He had been waiting for the Inspector General to arrive, because he knew Gerald's own compliance rating was fragile.

But Gerald did not struggle. He did not claw at the stone.

His left hand, the one not currently being strangled, twitched.

It reached for his pocket. Not for a weapon.

He didn't have weapons, and he had a clipboard. And he had a pen.

The Inspector General's arrival was imminent. The air pressure in the room dropped. The walls groaned. The Auditor watched from the doorway, her expression shifting from triumph to confusion.

Gerald's fingers brushed the edge of his notebook.

**[INTERVENTION DETECTED.]** **[SOURCE: INSPECTOR GENERAL (CLASS OMEGA)]** **[STATUS: ACTIVE]**

The stone hand tightened. Gerald's neck popped. Black spots danced in his eyes.

He managed to pull the pen free. He jammed the tip into the stone hand. Not to hurt it. To scratch.

He wrote in the air.

*Violation 404: Unauthorized Structural Modification.*

The System flashed red.

The rogue Core laughed. It sounded like boulders grinding.

"You think a scribble stops me, inspector?

I am the foundation.

I am the bedrock. You cannot cite gravity.

"

Gerald couldn't speak. He couldn't breathe. He could only write.

He dragged the pen across the rough surface of the stone hand. *Subsection 12: Failure to Obtain Permit for Load-Bearing Alterations.*

The air shimmered. A translucent blue box materialized in front of the rogue Core's face. It wasn't a spell.

It wasn't a projectile. It was a form.

**[CITATION ISSUED]** **[TARGET: RIVAL CORE (UNREGISTERED)]** **[VIOLATION: ILLEGAL LOAD-BEARING INTEGRATION]** **[PENALTY: STRUCTURAL INSTABILITY - 40% REDUCTION TO PHYSICAL RESILIENCE]**

The stone hand shuddered.

The rogue Core's grip loosened.

Not because it wanted to. Because the code demanded it. The concrete fingers, which were technically part of the dungeon's structural integrity, were now classified as

"non-compliant attachments." The System's logic rewrote the physics of the grasp.

The friction coefficient dropped to zero.

Gerald slumped forward, gasping, sucking in air that tasted of dust and ozone.

The Auditor stepped forward.

"You're cheating. He's not a registered dungeon. You can't cite a wild entity."

Gerald rubbed his neck. It felt like a bruise had formed directly on the spine.

He straightened his tie, which was crooked. He fixed it.

"Article 7, Section 2," Gerald rasped. "Any entity that interfaces with the municipal grid is subject to inspection.

He touched my walls.

He integrated.

That is an interface.

"

The Auditor's eyes narrowed. She looked at the rogue Core. The tectonic plates of his face were cracking. Hairline fractures spread across his granite brow.

"He is destabilizing," she said. Her voice was quieter now. Uncertain.

"He is out of code," Gerald said.

A boom shook the library. Not the rogue Core's movement. Something else.

The floor of the sub-basement split open. Not with violence, but with precision. A perfect rectangle of tile lifted and slid aside. From the hole rose a figure.

It was not human. It was not monster. It was a suit.

A suit of armor made of pure, white light, shaped like a bureaucrat. It had no face. Only a badge. A golden shield with a single symbol: a stamp.

**[INSPECTOR GENERAL PRESENT]** **[AUDIT IN PROGRESS]** **[SILENCE REQUIRED]**

The air in the room froze. Dust motes stopped moving. The rogue Core froze mid-lunge, his stone arm hovering above Gerald's head. The Auditor froze in her stride.

Even Gerald felt his heartbeat slow to match the rhythm of the Inspector General.

The figure raised a hand. It held a clipboard. The same size and shape as Gerald's.

The Inspector General did not speak. It projected text into the minds of everyone present.

**[SUBJECT A: GERALD HUTCHINS. STATUS: CITIZEN. COMPLIANCE RATING: 84/100.

NOTE: INCREASED VENTILATION RECENTLY. ACCEPTABLE.]**

**[SUBJECT B: AUDITOR.

STATUS: SYSTEM EMPLOYEE. COMPLIANCE RATING: 92/100. NOTE: EXCESSIVE BUREAUCRATIC LATENCY.

TOLERABLE.]**

**[SUBJECT C: UNIDENTIFIED ENTITY. STATUS: UNLICENSED. COMPLIANCE RATING: NULL.]**

The Inspector General pointed a glowing finger at the rogue Core.

**[DETERMINATION: UNLICENSED DUNGEON CORE. ILLEGAL CONSTRUCTION. UNAUTHORIZED ZONING.]**

The rogue Core roared. It tried to move, to crush Gerald, to escape.

But the System had already applied the penalty.

The 40% reduction in resilience was not a debuff to his strength. It was a debuff to his cohesion.

His left arm detached.

It fell to the floor with a heavy *crunch*. The stone fingers scrambled for purchase, but they were just rocks now. Debris.

The rogue Core screamed. It was a sound of tearing metal.

The cracks in his face widened, and he was falling apart because he was not built to standard.

He was not up to code.

Gerald watched. He felt a strange sensation in his chest. It wasn't triumph.

It was satisfaction. The same feeling he got when he found a perfectly level door frame in a renovation project.

The Inspector General turned to Gerald. The white light of its face intensified.

**[INTERVENTION LOGGED.]** **[ACTION: CITATION ISSUED BY LOCAL AUTHORITY.]** **[VALIDITY: CONFIRMED.]** **[REWARD: PERMANENT PASSIVE -

"STRUCTURAL AUDIT" (PASSIVE). EFFECT: ALL CITED DEBUFFS APPLIED BY YOU ARE INSTANTANEOUS AND CANNOT BE APPEALED.]**

Gerald blinked. No appeals. That was new. That was powerful.

But the cost was written in the fine print.

**[PENALTY FOR INVOKING EMERGENCY JURISDICTION: AUDIT FREQUENCY INCREASED. INSPECTOR GENERAL WILL NOW CONDUCT UNANNOUNCED CHECKS ON YOUR DUNGEON WEEKLY.]**

Weekly.

Gerald's stomach turned, and he had barely managed to keep his Compliance Rating above 50. Now he would be under the microscope every week. If he failed an audit, the dungeon would be condemned.

Demolished.

He looked at the Inspector General. The figure was already fading, its work done. The rectangle of tile slid back into place. The silence broke.

The Auditor walked over to the remnants of the rogue Core. She kicked a piece of his arm. It crumbled into gravel.

"You just saved your neck," she said. "But you just made yourself a target.

The Inspector General doesn't like freelancers.

You went rogue, Gerald. You used emergency jurisdiction without a pre-approved form.

"

Gerald picked up his pen. His hand was shaking. He placed it in his pocket.

"I used Article 9," Gerald said. "Section 3."

"Article 9 is for natural disasters," the Auditor said. "Earthquakes. Floods. Not for rogue Cores who happen to be hiding in your walls."

"He was in the walls," Gerald said. "That is a structural defect. It is a safety hazard. I was addressing a safety hazard."

The Auditor smiled. It was a thin, dangerous smile.

"You are technically correct.

That is the most dangerous kind of correct.

But the Inspector General is not a person, Gerald. It does not care about your intent.

It cares about procedure. And you skipped step four.

"

"What step?"

"Filing the Preliminary Hazard Report. Form 114-A. You cited him without first documenting the hazard on a municipal form. You bypassed the queue."

Gerald felt cold.

"The queue?"

"The Inspector General has a backlog. By invoking emergency jurisdiction, you cut in line. You created a bureaucratic anomaly. The System remembers anomalies."

The rogue Core's remains dissolved into gray sludge. The System was cleaning up the violation.

"What happens now?" Gerald asked.

"Now?" The Auditor checked her watch. "Now you wait.

The Inspector General will audit you.

Not for your dungeon. For your paperwork. If you cannot prove that the hazard existed *before* you cited it, the citation is retroactively invalid.

The rogue Core's allies will get their stat bonuses back.

And they will remember who did this.

"

Gerald looked at the sludge.

"I need Form 114-A."

"It's in the digital archives. But you have to submit it within one hour. Before the audit begins."

"One hour."

"Go."

Gerald ran, and he did not run like a hero.

He did not run with power. He ran like a man who knows that if he does not finish the form, he will be fired. His feet slapped against the concrete.

His breath came in ragged gasps, and he burst out of the library's side exit. The sun was setting over Ashford. The sky was a bruised purple.

He pulled out his tablet. His fingers flew across the screen.

*Form 114-A: Preliminary Hazard Report.*

*Location: Ashford Municipal Library, Sub-Basement.*

*Description of Hazard: Unauthorized integration of foreign dungeon core into existing structural supports.*

*Date of Observation: Today.*

*Time of Observation: 4:42 PM.*

*Photographic Evidence: Attached.*

Gerald uploaded the image of the stone hand. The image was blurry. It was shaky. It was taken while he was being strangled.

He hit **SUBMIT**.

**[RECEIVED.]** **[PROCESSING...]**

He watched the progress bar. It moved slowly. Too slowly.

**[ERROR: IMAGE METADATA INCOMPLETE.]**

Gerald cursed. He opened the file properties. The timestamp was wrong. The camera had failed to sync with the System clock during the chaos.

He edited the timestamp manually.

**[WARNING: MANUAL EDIT DETECTED. FLAGGED FOR REVIEW.]**

He submitted again.

**[RECEIVED.]** **[PROCESSING...]**

**[VALIDATION FAILED.]** **[REASON: HAZARD MUST BE VERIFIED BY A THIRD PARTY PRIOR TO CITATION.]**

Gerald stared at the screen.

"No," he whispered.

"No, that's not how it works. I was the third party.

I was the witness.

"

But the System required verification. A separate entity. A separate eye.

He looked back at the library. The Auditor was still there. She was watching him.

She held up her tablet. She was submitting her own report.

Gerald's heart hammered. If she submitted a report that contradicted his, or if she failed to submit one that supported his, he would be liable for false citation.

He ran back toward the library. The doors were locked.

He pounded on the glass.

"Let me in!" he yelled. "I need your affidavit! I need you to verify the hazard existed before I cited him!"

The Auditor looked through the glass. She shook her head.

"You're too late," she mouthed.

Gerald pulled out his emergency key. He inserted it. He turned it.

The lock clicked, and he burst in.

The Auditor was standing in the lobby. She held up her tablet.

**[SUBMITTED.]**

Gerald grabbed her arm.

"What did you write?"

She looked at him. Her eyes were sad.

"I wrote that the hazard was self-manifesting. That the rogue Core emerged on his own.

That you acted in self-defense.

But I did not verify that the hazard existed prior to your citation. I could not. I arrived after you did.

"

Gerald dropped her arm.

**[SYSTEM NOTICE.]** **[CITATION 404 INVALIDATED.]** **[REASON: INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE OF PRE-EXISTING HAZARD.]** **[DEBUFFS REMOVED FROM RIVAL CORES IN REGION 4.]** **[PENALTY IMPOSED: GERALD HUTCHINS.]** **[FINE: 500 CORE POINTS.]** **[SECONDARY PENALTY: ALL FUTURE CITATIONS REQUIRE PRE-FILING APPROVAL.]**

Gerald felt the points drain. He checked his balance. He was near zero.

But worse was the secondary penalty. He could no longer cite on the spot. He had to ask permission. He had to wait.

In a fight, waiting was death.

The Auditor walked past him.

"You have to request a Citation Warrant now, Gerald. You have to file a motion. You have to wait for the System to grant you the right to write the form."

"How long does that take?"

"Twenty-four hours."

"Then I'm dead."

"Probably," the Auditor said. "But you're not dead yet. You're just... slowed down."

Gerald looked at his tablet. He opened a new document.

*Motion for Expedited Citation Warrant.*

He typed. Fast.

*I, Gerald Hutchins, Inspector Level 4, do hereby request a warrant for the immediate citation of Dungeon Core

"The Shattered Keep."*

*Reason: Imminent threat.*

*Evidence: See attached video.*

He attached the video from the rogue Core's attack. The stone hand. The cracks. The pain.

He hit **SUBMIT**.

**[REQUEST PENDING.]** **[ESTIMATED PROCESSING TIME: 14 HOURS.]**

Fourteen hours.

The ground shook.

Not in the library. In the city.

A scream echoed from the north. Then another.

Gerald ran to the window.

In the distance, across the city skyline, a tower of black smoke rose. It was the location of The Shattered Keep. A high-tier dungeon that had been dormant for years.

The rogue Core's allies. They felt the removal of their debuffs. They felt the System's weakness.

They were waking up.

Gerald looked at his tablet.

**[NOTICE: INSPECTOR GENERAL SCHEDULED AUDIT: TOMORROW, 9:00 AM.]**

He had fourteen hours before he could act, and he had less than that before the city burned.

Gerald opened his contact list. He scrolled down to a name he hadn't spoken to in six months.

*Martha. Union Rep. Local 402.*

He typed a message.

*I need a witness. I need a union rep to verify a hazard *right now*. Not after. Now.*

He hit send, and he waited.

The phone buzzed.

**[ACCEPTED.]**

A car horn honked outside.

Gerald looked out the window. A blue sedan pulled up to the curb. Martha stepped out. She was holding a clipboard.

She looked up at the library windows. She saw Gerald.

She didn't smile. She didn't wave. She just nodded.

Gerald ran for the door, and he had a witness. He had a form. He had a chance.

But as he stepped onto the sidewalk, a shadow fell over him.

Not from the sun.

From above.

A massive shape, made of wire and light, hovered over the library. It was the System's Enforcement Drone. It was tracking the pending warrant.

It was watching Gerald, which was waiting for him to make a mistake.

Gerald looked up. The drone's eye was a red lens.

He looked at Martha.

He looked at the burning skyline, and he took a breath.

"Let's go," he said.

He walked toward the car. His steps were steady. His hands were empty.

He had no weapons. He had no spells.

He had a pen. And he had a rule.

And for the first time in thirty-one years, he was about to break it.

What happened next was technically a crime.
