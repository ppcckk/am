Chapter 28: Sealed Structural Silence

# Chapter 28

Gerald's knuckles were white around the tape measure. He had found the resonance.

Fourteen inches from the floor, two feet left of the junction chamber's eastern wall, the plaster gave back a different sound.

Not hollow. Not solid. Something between.

Like a drum with a finger pressed to the membrane, and he marked the spot with a grease pencil. Then he marked the spot above it.

And below.

And to the left. A rectangle. Six inches by four.

He pressed his ear to the wall.

Silence. But not the silence of solid foundation. The silence of a gap that had been sealed.

Gerald pulled out his notebook. Form 7-C: Suspected Unauthorized Structural Modification.

He wrote the dimensions, and he wrote the location.

He wrote the date and time, using the System clock, because the System was the only clock he trusted now.

He needed to open the wall.

That was the problem. Gerald did not open walls. Gerald inspected walls.

He measured them, tapped them, noted their condition, and left them alone. Opening a wall was demolition.

Demolition required a permit.

And Gerald could not issue himself a permit. He had tried. Chapter fourteen.

The System had rejected it with a word: CIRCULAR.

So he could not open the wall. But he could do something else.

He could cite it.

Gerald pulled up his Citation interface. The complaint against him was still active. 4471-C.

Forty-six hours remaining, and he ignored it for now. He had a new target.

He placed his Citation cursor on the wall section. The System highlighted it in amber.

Unauthorized structural modification.

Penetrating attachment.

No permit on file. No inspection record.

No foundation assessment.

Violation category: Structural, Class 3.

Gerald issued the Citation.

The System confirmed. Citation 4471-C-1.

Counter-citation filed against the structure occupying coordinates within Gerald's dungeon footprint, and the citation was not against a Core. It was against a structure. That was the trick.

Gerald had never cited a structure before.

He had cited Cores, cited spawns, cited adventurers who brought open flames into his corridors. But a structure?

The System accepted it.

The Compliance Rating for the cited structure appeared in the air: 0/100. Of course it was zero. It had no ventilation, no egress, no fire suppression.

It was a hole in a wall.

Gerald issued a Stop Work Order, which was new. He had read about it in the System's regulatory text, Section 11.4: "Where a cited structure is found to be facilitating an active violation, the host Core may issue a Stop Work Order, which immediately halts all activity associated with the cited structure."

The Stop Work Order appeared in amber text, hovering over the wall section.

The complaint notification 4471-C flickered.

Then it went dark.

Gerald stared at it, and he waited.

The System did not re-issue the complaint. It did not re-notify.

The complaint was gone. Not dismissed. Not appealed.

Gone. Because the mechanism that had filed it was now non-compliant, and the System could not accept filings from non-compliant structures.

Gerald let out a breath he had been holding for twenty minutes.

He pulled up his dungeon map. The wall section was in the eastern corridor, between the junction chamber and the storage alcove he had converted to a ventilation shaft in chapter nineteen. The conduit, whatever it was, connected to the space beyond the library's foundation. CORE-9's territory.

Or was it?

Gerald opened the citation detail. 4471-C-1.

The System listed the structure's properties. Dimensions: 6 inches by 4 inches, 14 inches deep. Material: compressed foundation substrate, consistent with the host dungeon's existing wall composition.

Origin: not listed.

Not listed.

Gerald re-read it. The origin field was blank.

For a foreign structure, the System would list the creating Core.

CORE-9. CORE-14. The mold colony in the storm drain.

Something. But it was blank.

He checked the registration timestamp. The conduit had been created during Core Registration. At the exact moment Gerald's body had dissolved into dungeon footprint, and the System had carved it into his walls as part of the registration process.

Gerald sat down. He sat on the floor of his own dungeon, in the corridor, with his back against the wall that was not his wall, and he read the citation detail three more times.

The System had made the conduit, and the System had made it during his registration.

The conduit was not CORE-9's. It was Gerald's.

It had always been Gerald's. CORE-9 had simply used it. Had found it, perhaps, in the first hours after registration, when Gerald was still filing Form 11-B on a rat and had not yet mapped his own walls.

CORE-9 had found a hole in Gerald's wall that the System had carved for him, and cORE-9 had used that hole to file a complaint against Gerald.

Gerald's hands were steady. They were always steady.

Thirty-one years of writing citations had made his hands steady.

But his stomach was not steady. His stomach was doing something he had not felt since the day he had been asked to inspect the condemned parking structure on Fifth Street and discovered the contractor had forged his previous inspection reports.

He pulled up the complaint history.

All complaints filed against him. Eleven in all.

He had resolved seven, was currently responding to two, and two had been auto-dismissed because the filing structures had been cited.

This was the eighth auto-dismissal. He pulled up the details of the other two.

4102-C. Filed against him in chapter twenty-two.

Filing structure: a surface observation node attached to the exterior of his northern wall.

Cited.

Dismissed.

3847-C. Filed against him in chapter sixteen. Filing structure: a thermal differential sensor embedded in his ceiling.

Cited. Dismissed.

Gerald checked the origin field on both.

Blank.

All three conduits had been created during Core Registration. All three were part of Gerald's dungeon footprint. All three had been used by external parties to file complaints against him.

The System had built the holes. Others had used them.

Gerald stood up, and he walked to the junction chamber. The surveyor was still there. It had not moved.

Its retracted tape was still in its hand.

It was waiting for credentials that would never come.

Gerald looked at it.

"Where did you find the conduit?" he asked.

The surveyor tilted its head. Constructs did not answer direct questions unless the question was framed as a compliance inquiry. Gerald knew this. He had learned it the hard way in chapter nine, when he had asked a construct for its name and received a System error message.

"State the origin of your observation access point," Gerald said.

The surveyor's writing hand moved. It wrote on its clipboard: ORIGIN: PRE-EXISTING.

"Pre-existing relative to what?"

The surveyor wrote: PRE-EXISTING RELATIVE TO CORE-9 OCCUPANCY.

"Core-9's occupancy began when?"

The surveyor wrote: 47 HOURS AGO.

Gerald checked his records. CORE-9 had expanded into the adjacent parcel forty-seven hours ago; the conduit had been created during Gerald's registration, which was twenty-six days ago. So the conduit had existed for twenty-five days before CORE-9 arrived.

Who had used it before CORE-9?

Gerald pulled up the complaint history again. 3847-C.

Filed in chapter sixteen. That was nineteen days after registration. Before CORE-9 arrived.

Who had filed it?

The System listed the filing Core: CORE-14. The storm drain mold colony.

Gerald looked at his map.

CORE-14 was in the storm drain beneath Elm Street.

Four blocks away. The conduit was in Gerald's eastern wall.

The eastern wall faced the library's foundation. The library's foundation was adjacent to the storm drain's upper access point.

CORE-14 had found the conduit. Had used it.

Had filed a complaint against Gerald.

And Gerald had cited the conduit, and the complaint had been dismissed, but Gerald had never asked where the conduit came from. He had never checked the origin field. He had been too busy.

Gerald pulled up his full complaint history. All eleven complaints. He checked the origin field on every filing structure.

All blank. All created during Core Registration. All part of Gerald's dungeon footprint.

Eleven complaints. All filed through holes the System had carved in his walls. All used by other Cores.

Gerald sat back down, and he thought about the Inspector General audit.

It was scheduled for seventy-two hours from now. The audit would examine Gerald's Compliance Rating.

His rating was currently 67.

Up from

twelve, after thirty-one days of corrective measures. Seventy-two hours from now, the Inspector General would arrive, walk his corridors, check his egress signage, verify his fire suppression, and issue a final determination. If the rating held above sixty, Gerald would retain Core status. If it dropped below sixty, the System would dissolve his dungeon footprint and redistribute the parcel.

He looked at the conduit again. The surveyor's pen hovered, waiting for the next question.

"Are these openings still active?"

The surveyor wrote: YES. CONDUIT REMAINS OPEN. UNREINFORCED. NO SEAL.

Gerald felt something cold move through his awareness. Not fear. Gerald did not feel fear. Gerald felt the specific nausea of a man who has just discovered that the building he has been maintaining for thirty-one years has a load-bearing wall that was never in the original plans.

"Can other Cores still file complaints through this conduit?"

The surveyor wrote: YES.

Gerald pulled up the complaint timeline again. Eleven complaints.

Nineteen days between his registration and the first one.

CORE-14 had found the conduit.

CORE-9 had found it later. Both had used it.

Both had filed complaints that the System had processed as legitimate grievances against Gerald's dungeon.

The complaints had been dismissed. But the record of their existence remained.

The audit would see them.

The audit would see the conduit. The audit would see that Gerald's dungeon contained an unauthorized opening in its eastern wall.

Unauthorized openings in dungeon walls were a structural code violation.

Section 4.2.1. Prohibited penetrations.

The penalty was not a fine, and the penalty was a rating deduction proportional to the number of openings.

Gerald counted. Eleven complaints.

Eleven filing structures.

He had checked the origin field on eleven.

All blank. All pre-existing.

He pulled up the structural schematic of his eastern wall. He counted the penetrations.

Fourteen.

Three had never generated complaints. Three that other Cores had found and used for purposes Gerald could not yet determine.

The audit would find fourteen unauthorized openings. Section 4.2.1. Fourteen deductions.

His rating was 67. Fourteen deductions at the severity multiplier for prohibited penetrations would bring his rating to—

He did the math. He did it twice.

The number was not above sixty.

Gerald looked at the surveyor. "How do I seal a conduit?"

The surveyor wrote: SEALING REQUIRES A VARIANCE APPROVAL. VARIANCE APPROVAL REQUIRES A HEARING. HEARING REQUIRES THE CONDUIT TO BE ACTIVE DURING THE HEARING.

Gerald sat still.

To close the holes, he had to prove they should be closed.

To prove they should be closed, he had to argue their case before the System. To argue their case, they had to be open. To keep them open was to remain in violation.

To close them without approval was itself a violation, and the audit was in seventy-two hours.

Gerald opened a new form. Form 7-C: Petition for Variance.

He started filling it out.

His hands were steady. His mind was not.
