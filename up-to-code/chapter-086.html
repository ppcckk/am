Chapter 86: Adjudicating Shared Jurisdiction

# Chapter 86

The Inspector General stood in the doorway. It was not a person.

It was not a creature.

It was a shape that the System had assigned to the concept of oversight, and the shape looked like a clipboard that had learned to stand upright. It was seven feet tall. It had no face.

It had a seal on its chest that pulsed with a slow amber light.

Gerald had opened the door. He had not invited the IG in. He had simply opened it.

The IG stepped through. Its footsteps were precise.

Each one landed on a tile that Gerald had installed in chapter thirty-four after receiving a citation for non-compliant flooring materials.

The IG did not look at the exit sign. It did not look at the push plate. It looked at the walls.

"Inspector Hutchins." The voice came from everywhere and nowhere. It sounded like a printer warming up.

"You have filed audit objection 7741-IG-003.

I am here to adjudicate.

You will submit to inspection."

Gerald did not move. He held his clipboard against his chest. The four hundred and twelve violations were printed on its back in a font so small he needed reading glasses to parse them, but he did not need to read them right now. He needed to watch.

The IG moved through the entrance corridor. It paused at the first junction. A faint amber glow emanated from its seal as it scanned the space.

"Corridor width: compliant. Egress path: compliant. Ventilation: compliant." The voice paused. "This is unexpected."

Gerald said nothing. He had been expecting this. Every corridor in his dungeon was exactly forty-two inches wide.

Every junction had a turning radius that met the minimum clearance for wheelchair access. The ventilation system he had installed in chapter fifty-three used a dual-duct configuration that exceeded the System's minimum air exchange rate by thirty percent.

He had built this dungeon to the letter.

Not to the spirit. To the letter.

The IG continued deeper.

It moved through the spawn chambers. The three mold spores were in their designated containment cells, which were labeled with hazard placards that met OSHA standards for biological material storage.

The water leak was in a drainage channel with a proper trap and a cleanout access point.

The rat was in a sealed observation enclosure with a ventilation grate and a feeding port.

The IG scanned each one. Each time, the amber glow pulsed once, then settled.

"Compliant. Compliant. Compliant."

Gerald felt something he had not felt in eighty-five chapters. He felt proud. He pushed it down. Pride was not in the code.

The IG reached the central chamber. This was where Gerald kept his citation files.

Four hundred and twelve physical copies, filed in a steel cabinet that he had welded himself from angle iron and sheet metal, rated for fire resistance up to two hours.

The cabinet was bolted to the floor, and the floor was bolted to the foundation. The foundation was the original library sub-basement slab, poured in 1971, rated for a live load of fifty pounds per square foot.

The IG stopped at the cabinet. Its seal pulsed faster.

"You have issued four hundred and twelve Citations in the current audit cycle."

"Four hundred and thirteen," Gerald corrected. "I served Core 8892-C yesterday. It was a fire suppression violation.

The entity was physically present. I followed protocol."

The IG did not respond to the correction. It turned. The amber glow from its seal swept across the central chamber, then across the corridors, then back to the cabinet.

"I am initiating a full structural audit of this dungeon. You will not interfere."

Gerald set his clipboard down on the steel cabinet. He pulled a pen from his shirt pocket. He clicked it.

"Understood."

The IG began.

It moved through the dungeon in a grid pattern, scanning every surface, every joint, every fastener. The process took four minutes. Gerald counted them.

He had nothing else to do, and he stood in the central chamber and watched the IG work.

At the three-minute mark, the IG paused at the junction between the east corridor and the spawn chamber. It tilted its clipboard-shaped head.

"Anomaly detected."

Gerald looked up.

"Structural anomaly at junction 4-C. Load path discontinuity."

Gerald walked over, and he looked at the junction. The IG was correct. A discontinuity existed.

A steel plate he had installed in chapter sixty-one to reinforce the ceiling joists was not properly connected to the adjacent wall stud. The load path was interrupted.

The weight above was not being transferred cleanly to the foundation.

He had missed it.

The IG marked the spot with a thin line of amber light. "Violation logged. Structural code, subsection 7.1. Severity: moderate."

Gerald felt his stomach turn. He had missed a violation in his own dungeon. He had missed it in a space he had inspected himself, with his own hands, on at least six separate occasions.

He pulled out his pen. He wrote it down.

The IG continued.

It found two more anomalies. A nail spacing inconsistency in the west wall.

A gap in the vapor barrier near the drainage channel. Three violations total. Gerald's Compliance Rating ticked down from 94 to 91.

The IG returned to the central chamber. It faced Gerald.

"Audit complete. Compliance Rating: 91. This exceeds the minimum threshold of 60. You are not in danger of deactivation."

Gerald exhaled.

"However." The IG's seal pulsed once. "The purpose of this visit is not to assess your dungeon.

It is to adjudicate your audit objection.

You objected to the Inspector General's authority to audit your dungeon without prior notice. You cited System Regulation 44.2, subsection C, which requires forty-eight hours' notice for a scheduled audit."

"Yes."

"You are correct. I was summoned by an emergency escalation protocol, not a scheduled audit. The forty-eight-hour requirement does not apply to emergency escalations. Your objection is denied."

Gerald nodded, and he had expected this. He had not filed the objection to win it.

He had filed it to force the IG to come in person.

Because the IG, when it came in person, had to follow the same procedural rules as any inspector entering a space.

And Gerald had been watching.

"You conducted a full structural audit of my dungeon," Gerald said. "You scanned every surface. You logged three violations."

"Correct."

"You did not present jurisdictional credentials."

The IG stopped.

Gerald continued. "System Regulation 12.1, subsection A.

Any inspector entering a structure to conduct an audit must present valid jurisdictional credentials identifying the scope of their authority over that structure.

You did not present them.

You entered my dungeon and began scanning without identifying whether your authority extends to this structure."

The IG's seal pulsed. Slowly. Like a machine thinking.

"You are correct," the IG said. "I did not present credentials."

"Under System Regulation 12.1, subsection D, evidence gathered during an unauthorized entry is inadmissible in any subsequent enforcement action."

The amber lines on the walls flickered. The three violations the IG had logged were fading.

Gerald felt something cold settle in his chest. He had done it. He had used the IG's own rules against it.

The audit was void. The violations were void.

His Compliance Rating was back to 94, and the IG stood still for a long time. Its seal pulsed in a pattern Gerald had not seen before. Faster.

Then slower.

Then it stopped pulsing.

"Inspector Hutchins." The voice was different now.

Not flat. Almost... Careful. "You have raised a valid procedural objection.

The audit is void. Your Compliance Rating is restored."

Gerald waited.

"However." The IG took a step forward.

"You have raised this objection because you are aware that your dungeon occupies a structure that falls under municipal jurisdiction.

The Ashford Municipal Library sub-basement. A city-owned structure.

Subject to the Ashford Municipal Building Code. Subject to the authority of the City of Ashford Department of Building Inspection."

Gerald said nothing.

"You are correct that my jurisdictional credentials do not automatically extend to a structure governed by municipal code. The System's authority and municipal authority are concurrent, not subordinate. Neither overrides the other. Therefore, for me to audit your dungeon, I would need to coordinate with the municipal authority that has jurisdiction over this structure."

The IG paused.

"And that municipal authority is you."

Gerald felt the floor shift beneath him. Not physically. Professionally.

"I am still a municipal building inspector," Gerald said. It was not a question.

"You never resigned your position. You never filed a Form 7-C, Notice of Departure from Municipal Service.

You never notified the City of Ashford that you had vacated your post.

Your employment record is active. Your jurisdictional authority is active. Your obligation to enforce municipal building code is active."

Gerald looked at the door. The exit sign glowed green. The push plate was clean.

"The implication," the IG said, "is that you are subject to municipal conflict-of-interest statute 14.3.

A municipal inspector may not hold a financial or proprietary interest in a structure they are responsible for inspecting. You are the inspector of this structure.

You are also the owner of this structure.

You are also the dungeon core of this structure. These three roles are mutually exclusive under municipal law."

Gerald closed his eyes.

"You have two options," the IG said. "Option one: resign your municipal position.

File Form 7-C.

Relinquish your jurisdictional authority over this structure.

This will resolve the conflict of interest. My audit authority will then be exclusive and unchallenged.

I will conduct a full audit. You will be subject to System enforcement without the municipal jurisdictional shield you have been using to delay my authority for the past thirty-one chapters."

Gerald opened his eyes.

"Option two: dissolve your dungeon core registration. File Form Core-0.

Relinquish your proprietary interest in this structure.

You will remain a municipal inspector. You will retain your jurisdictional authority. But you will no longer be a dungeon core.

You will no longer have spawn entities.

You will no longer have a Compliance Rating. You will no longer have the ability to issue Citations."

The IG waited.

Gerald looked at his clipboard. Four hundred and twelve citations. Four hundred and twelve dungeons he had brought into partial compliance. Four hundred and twelve cores that could no longer hide behind the System's silence because he had made them visible.

He looked at the steel cabinet. The fire-rated welds. The angle iron. The bolts.

He looked at the mold spores in their containment cells. The water leak in its drainage channel. The rat in its observation enclosure.

He looked at the door.

"I need time," Gerald said.

"You have it." The IG's seal pulsed once. "System Regulation 12.1, subsection F. A subject under jurisdictional challenge has seventy-two hours to file a response. The clock starts now."

A number appeared in the air. Seventy-two hours. It was already counting down.

Seventy-one hours, fifty-nine minutes, fifty-eight seconds.

The IG turned. It walked toward the door. It stopped at the threshold.

"One more thing, Inspector Hutchins." The voice was flat again.

Bureaucratic. "The reason I came faster than scheduled was not the emergency escalation protocol. It was because Core 7741-A filed a complaint against you.

A formal complaint. It alleges that your Citations constitute harassment and that your dungeon's jurisdictional status is fraudulent."

Gerald said nothing.

"The complaint is valid.

If you cannot resolve the conflict of interest within seventy-two hours, the System will void every Citation you have issued.

All four hundred and thirteen. Every debuff.

Every Hearing you won. Every spawn you killed through compliance enforcement. Gone."

The IG stepped through the door. The door closed behind it. The exit sign glowed green.

Gerald stood alone in his dungeon. The clock counted down.

Seventy-one hours, fifty-nine minutes, twelve seconds, and he picked up his clipboard. He turned to a fresh page. He wrote at the top: Form 7-C. Notice of Departure from Municipal Service.

He stared at the words. He had never filed one.

He had never even considered one.

He had worked for the City of Ashford for thirty-one years, and had a pension that vested in nine months. He had a parking spot.

He had a name on a door on the second floor of City Hall.

He erased the words, and he wrote: Form Core-0. Notice of Dungeon Core Dissolution.

He stared at that too.

He erased it.

He sat down on the floor. The floor was bolted to the foundation.

The foundation was poured in 1971, which was rated for fifty pounds per square foot. Gerald weighed one hundred and sixty. He was within the load rating.

The clock counted down.

Seventy-one hours, fifty-six minutes, forty-one seconds.

Gerald opened his citation files. Four hundred and thirteen.

He started reading without knowing what to look for.

He was unsure whether a third option existed, and did not know if the System had a loophole for this.

He was unsure whether a Form 7-C-2, a Core-0-B, or a subsection in a regulation he had never needed to read actually existed.

He had never needed it because he had never been both the inspector and the inspected.

He read the first citation. Then the second. Then the third.

At citation forty-seven, he stopped.

Citation 47. Issued against Core 2201-F. Violation: unauthorized structural modification to a shared boundary wall.

Gerald read it again. Shared boundary wall. Core 2201-F's dungeon shared a wall with his. He had cited it for modifying that wall without a permit.

He pulled out his pen. He turned to a new page.

He wrote: Jurisdictional overlap.

Shared boundary wall. Municipal code subsection 3.1, joint liability.

If the wall was shared, then the structure was not exclusively municipal. It was not exclusively System. It was both. And if it was both, then the conflict-of-interest statute might not apply to the shared portion.

He looked at the clock. Seventy-one hours, fifty-one minutes.

He stood up. He walked to the east wall. He placed his hand on it. Behind it, somewhere, was Core 2201-F.

He needed to find every shared boundary. Every overlap. Every joint structure. If he could prove that his dungeon was not exclusively municipal, not exclusively System, but a hybrid structure with shared jurisdictional boundaries, he might be able to thread the needle.

He might be able to keep both, and he pulled out his tape measure.

The clock counted down.
