Chapter 146: Building Smiling

# Chapter 146

Gerald squeezed a wet clump of pulp from the clipboard, watching brown sludge drip onto the floor. Gerald stared at the empty space where his clipboard had been, then at the pile of wet, disintegrated paper sludge on the floor of the sub-basement library. Twenty-four hours. That was the window. That was the entire margin of error.

Brix wiped his mouth with the back of a gauntlet that cost more than Gerald's monthly pension. The dwarf didn't look at Gerald. He looked at the door.

"You forged a receipt," Brix said. His voice sounded like gravel grinding in a mixer.

"I submitted a record of service," Gerald corrected, though his hands were still trembling. He picked up the coffee-stained slip.

The ink was bleeding into the fibers, turning the words *Latte, Extra Shot* into a brown smear.

"The System recognizes municipal employment status during active incidents. It's a loophole in the jurisdictional overlap between city ordinances and Core law."

"It's a miracle," Brix said. "And miracles don't last long enough to print a defense brief."

Gerald looked at the counter. *23:59:12.*

The library above them was quiet.

Too quiet. Usually, the ventilation fans hummed a low B-flat.

Now, the air was dead still.

The silence pressed against Gerald's eardrums.

"We need witnesses," Gerald said. "The IG doesn't care about intent. It cares about documentation. If we can prove the lockdown was a containment failure on the part of the *previous* Core, not an active aggression by this one, the citation drops."

"The previous Core is dead," Brix said. "Its corpse is in the sewer. I didn't see any witnesses."

"Then we make witnesses."

Gerald walked to the center of the room. The floor was a grid of reinforced concrete, up to code.

The walls were painted a calming, non-reflective off-white.

No handrails in the middle of the room, which was a violation. Gerald made a mental note to file it later, if he survived the next hour.

He pulled out a pen. It was a cheap ballpoint, blue ink, standard issue. He wrote on the back of the receipt.

*Statement of Observation. Time of Incident. Parties Present.*

He didn't know what to write. He had no memory of the exact seconds leading up to the auditor's arrival. He had been too busy checking for water damage on the ceiling tiles.

Brix watched him scribble.

"You're making it up."

"I am reconstructing a narrative based on available evidence," Gerald said. "Evidence includes the coffee. The coffee proves I was here.

I was here to inspect. Inspection requires observation.

Observation requires time.

"

"And the auditor?"

"The auditor appeared because the Compliance Rating dropped below critical thresholds. The drop was caused by the structural instability of the previous dungeon's remnants. I found that.

I reported that.

The System flagged it. The auditor came to verify.

"

Brix laughed, which was a dry, hacking sound.

"You think the Inspector General will buy that? The IG is a literal manifestation of bureaucratic spite.

He knows you're lying.

"

"He knows I'm *creative*," Gerald said. "There is a difference."

Gerald turned to the spawn list. It floated in his peripheral vision, a translucent blue overlay.

**[Current Spawns: 3 Mold Spores (Level 1), 1 Confused Rat (Level 1), 1 Water Leak (Level 2 Hazard).]**

He needed something more. Something that moved. Something that could testify.

He tapped the air.

**[SUMMON: EGRESS SIGNAGE (Level 3).]**

A green plastic sign popped into existence, hovering at head height. It blinked. *EXIT.*

Gerald stared at it. Then he looked at Brix.

"Grab that," Gerald said.

Brix sighed. He reached out and grabbed the sign. The plastic crackled with static.

"Make it talk," Gerald said.

The sign remained silent.

Gerald closed his eyes, and he focused on the System interface, digging into the sub-menus he had spent months mastering.

Most Cores used their energy to build traps. Gerald used his energy to build compliance.

He had a passive skill tree dedicated to Administrative Manipulation.

He selected the sign.

**[ATTACH: WITNESS PROTOCOL (Level 1).]**

The sign flickered. A small, robotic voice emanated from it.

"Exit. Exit. Proceed with caution."

Gerald pointed at Brix.

"Ask it what it saw."

Brix looked at Gerald.

"This is insane."

"Ask it."

Brix held the sign up.

"What did you see?"

"Exit," the sign said.

Gerald frowned. He adjusted the settings. **[Override Voice Module: Testimony Mode.]**

The sign changed. The green light pulsed red.

"Structural integrity compromised in Sector 4," the sign said. "Rapid degradation detected. Core instability imminent."

Gerald nodded.

"Good. Keep recording. If anyone walks through that door, you repeat that statement."

Brix lowered the sign. He looked pale.

"You're wiring a sign to be a witness. You know that's illegal under the Dungeon Core Act, Section 9, Paragraph C?"

"It's not illegal," Gerald said. "It's unregulated.

The Act specifies that *biological* entities can testify.

It doesn't specify that *inanimate* objects cannot. And since the System governs the dungeon, everything in the dungeon is technically part of the Core. Therefore, the sign is me.

And I can testify for myself.

"

Brix shook his head.

"You're going to get us killed."

"Probably," Gerald said. "But not yet.

We have twenty-three hours left.

I need to check the perimeter. The IG might try to seal the exits before the hearing. If we can't get to the Surface, we can't appeal.

"

Brix stood up. He grabbed his axe.

"I'll hold the door. You check the vents."

Gerald nodded. He walked to the nearest ventilation duct.

It was a standard HVAC unit, up to code, and the filters were clean.

The airflow was laminar and consistent.

He opened the access panel.

Inside, something was moving.

It wasn't a rat. It wasn't a mold spore.

It was a spider. A large, black spider with eyes that glowed with a faint, violet hue.

Gerald froze. Violet was the color of the Inspector General.

The spider spun a web across the duct opening. The web was made of glowing threads.

Gerald backed away.

Brix heard the scuffle. He ran over, axe raised.

"What is it?"

"Violet webbing," Gerald said. "It's a tracking marker. The IG left it before he vanished."

Brix looked at the web. He swung his axe. The blade passed through the web without touching it.

"It's intangible," Brix said.

"No," Gerald said. "It's jurisdictional."

He pulled out his pen. He wrote on the wall.

**[NOTICE OF TRESPASS: Unauthorized Surveillance Device.]**

Nothing happened.

"The web isn't a device," Gerald muttered. "It's a status effect."

He looked at the spider. The spider looked at him. It didn't attack. It just watched.

**[STATUS: OBSERVED.]**

**[PENALTY: -10% Evasion for next 24 hours.]**

Gerald felt a weight on his shoulders. A physical heaviness. He stumbled.

Brix caught him.

"Whoa. Easy."

"It's fine," Gerald said, straightening his tie. "It's a fine. A physical fine."

"How do we remove it?"

"We don't. We work around it." Gerald looked at the web. "If we remove the spider, the penalty stays.

The System considers the observation complete. We have to remove the source of the observation.

"

"The spider?"

"No. The door."

Gerald looked up at the ceiling. The violet light from the auditor's departure was still faintly lingering in the corners.

"IG audits are remote," Gerald said. "But they require a line of sight. If we block the sightlines, the penalty doesn't apply. The System can't observe what it can't see."

Brix looked at the door.

"You want to brick it up?"

"No. That's a fire code violation. I'm not doing that."

"Then what?"

"We install a curtain."

Gerald pointed to the supply closet.

"Get the blackout drapes from the reading room. The heavy velvet ones. They're Class A fire retardant, but we'll claim they're for privacy."

Brix stared at him.

"You want to hide from an omnipresent eye with curtains?"

"I want to obscure the view," Gerald said.

"The code specifies 'unobstructed views' for emergency exits. It doesn't specify views for private offices. If we designate this room as a private office, we can legally obstruct the view.

And if the view is obstructed, the IG can't see the violations.

He can't cite them.

"

Brix rubbed his temples.

"Gerald. You are insane."

"I am pragmatic," Gerald said. "Move."

Brix moved. He ran to the closet and came back with armfuls of heavy blue velvet.

Gerald directed him.

"Hang them over the door. Over the vents. Over the windows.

If there is a hole, cover it.

"

Brix worked fast. He nailed the fabric into the frame. The room grew darker. The violet light seemed to dim, confused by the sudden lack of clear sightlines.

Gerald watched the spider. The spider stopped moving. The **OBSERVED** tag flickered.

**[STATUS: OBSERVED... OBSCURED.]**

**[PENALTY SUSPENDED.]**

Gerald let out a breath he didn't know he was holding.

"Good," Gerald said. "Now we wait."

They waited.

An hour passed, and the room was dark. The velvet smelled of old dust and lavender. Brix sat on a crate, sharpening his axe. Gerald paced.

At 12:00:00 remaining, the air in the room changed.

It got cold.

Not the chill of a draft. The chill of deep, institutional cold.

Brix stood up.

"He's here."

"No," Gerald said. "He's not here. He's watching."

The velvet curtains rippled. Not from a breeze. From pressure.

A voice spoke. It didn't come from the door. It came from everywhere. It was smooth, genderless, and bored.

"Citizen Hutchins."

Gerald stopped pacing. He straightened his spine.

"Inspector General," Gerald said. He spoke to the air.

"You have submitted a motion for an Appeal Hearing," the voice said. "Your arguments are... irregular."

"They are compliant," Gerald said. "I have attached the receipt. I have attached the witness statement from the Egress Signage. I have documented the environmental obscurity to protect sensitive municipal operations."

"Sensitive operations?" The voice sounded amused. "What sensitive operations are you conducting in a public library basement?"

"Privacy," Gerald said. "Under the Municipal Privacy Act, Section 4.2, citizens have a right to privacy in their workplace. I am a citizen. This is my workplace."

There was a pause.

"Interesting," the IG said. "You are invoking a civilian law to protect a dungeon core."

"I am invoking a law that supersedes Core law because I am currently a municipal employee. The jurisdiction is concurrent. Therefore, my rights as a citizen apply to my dungeon as a private workspace."

The pause lasted longer this time.

Gerald held his breath.

Brix gripped his axe.

"You are testing the limits of the System," the IG said. "I admire that. It is rare. Usually, Cores just try to kill me."

"I am not trying to kill you," Gerald said. "I am trying to file paperwork."

"I see," the IG said.

"Very well.

I will grant the Hearing. But I will not come to you.

You must come to me. The Surface. The Library Main Entrance.

Noon."

"The door is open," Gerald said.

"The door is locked," the IG said. "I have placed a temporary seal. You cannot leave until 11:59 AM. You must wait."

"Can I inspect the seal?" Gerald asked.

"No."

"Then I will assume it is up to code."

"You are a dangerous man, Gerald Hutchins," the IG said. "You follow rules so strictly that you weaponize them. This will be... challenging."

The cold vanished. The pressure lifted. The velvet curtains settled.

Brix slumped against the wall.

"Did you just argue with God?"

"I argued with a bureaucrat," Gerald said. "Same thing, but God has better dental."

They waited.

The hours dragged.

Gerald measured the room again. He checked the humidity. He noted that the velvet was starting to sag.

He wrote a note to himself to install support wires.

At 11:55 AM, the air shifted again.

The door clicked.

The lock disengaged.

Brix looked at Gerald.

"We ready?"

Gerald looked at his spawn list.

**[Current Spawns: 3 Mold Spores (Level 1), 1 Confused Rat (Level 1), 1 Water Leak (Level 2 Hazard), 1 Egress Sign (Level 3 Witness).]**

It wasn't much. It wasn't a army. It was a mess.

But it was organized.

Gerald picked up his clipboard. He picked up his pen.

"Ready," Gerald said.

He opened the door.

The stairs to the library were empty. The main floor was silent.

Gerald stepped out, and the library was not empty.

Standing in the center of the circulation desk was a figure made of pure, swirling violet light. It had no face. It had only a clipboard.

Behind the figure stood a dozen adventurers. They were not here to help. They were here to watch.

And beside the IG stood another Core, which was not a dungeon.

It was the Ashford Municipal Library itself.

The library had awakened.

The violet figure turned to Gerald.

"Welcome to the Hearing," the IG said. "Your opponent is not a rival dungeon. Your opponent is the building you inspect."

Gerald's pen snapped in his hand, and he looked at the walls. He looked at the load-bearing columns. He looked at the exit signs he had installed.

The library was smiling at him.
