Chapter 145: Forging the Paperwork

# Chapter 145

The stone ceiling groaned.

Gerald Hutchins pressed his palm against the rough-hewn granite. The vibration traveled up his arm, a low-frequency hum that rattled the fillings in his molars. Above him, two hundred feet of earth and bedrock separated him from the fluorescent hum of the Ashford Municipal Library's breakroom. Below him, the darkness of the sub-basement was pressing in, heavy and suffocating.

The timer on his HUD ticked down.

*00:57:15.*

Brix scrambled up the stairs behind him. The goblin's claws scraped against the limestone treads, leaving white streaks in the dust. He was breathing hard, a wet, rattling sound that echoed in the confined stairwell.

"Hurry," Brix gasped. "The air is changing."

Gerald ignored him. He reached the top of the staircase and pushed against the heavy iron door. It didn't budge. A thick band of violet energy wrapped around the frame, pulsing in time with a heartbeat that wasn't Gerald's.

The Inspector General had sealed the exit.

Gerald didn't panic. He didn't scream. He pulled a tape measure from his belt. The retractable metal blade snapped out with a sharp *clack* that sounded obscenely loud in the silence.

He measured the gap between the door and the jamb.

Three millimeters.

Less than the required half-inch clearance for a fire-rated egress door in a high-traffic public assembly space.

"Non-compliant," Gerald muttered. He clicked the pen on his clipboard.

The ink was dry, but the motion was soothing.

He wrote down the measurement. He noted the obstruction. He noted the lack of panic hardware.

*00:56:45.*

The violet energy pulsed brighter.

The stone beneath Gerald's feet heated up. The dungeon's ambient temperature was rising, a side effect of the IG's punitive lockdown protocol.

Heat caused expansion.

Expansion caused stress. Stress caused failure.

Gerald knew this. He had inspected thousands of doors. He knew that if you blocked an exit, you trapped the pressure. And if you trapped the pressure in a confined space with no ventilation, you created a hazard.

A hazard he could cite.

"Gerald," Brix whispered. He was huddled in the corner of the landing, his green skin pale. "Stop measuring. It won't work."

"Everything works," Gerald said, not looking up. "If you document it correctly."

He turned his back to the door. He looked at the wall to his left.

The stone was smooth, unadorned.

But Gerald saw the seams.

The mortar joints were uneven. The alignment was off by two degrees from the vertical axis established by the library's foundational blueprints, which Gerald had memorized during his initial registration.

The IG's lockdown wasn't just a magical seal. It was a physical restructuring of the dungeon's layout. It violated Section 402.1 of the Structural Integrity Code: *No alteration to load-bearing elements without a permit.*

The IG had issued no permit.

Gerald stepped away from the door. He walked to the center of the stairwell landing. He closed his eyes.

He reached for the System interface. Not with his hands, but with his mind. He focused on the concept of *Authority*. He focused on the specific language of the Code.

*Form 01-A: Emergency Request for Immunity.*

He hadn't filed it yet. He needed the evidence first.

He opened his eyes. He looked at Brix.

"Stand on the third step from the top."

Brix blinked. "What?"

"Stand on the third step. Do not move."

The goblin hesitated, then obeyed. He trembled, his long ears flattened against his skull.

Gerald raised his hand. He didn't cast a spell. He didn't summon a monster. He projected his intent.

*Violation: Obstruction of Egress Path.*

*Violation: Unauthorized Structural Modification.*

*Violation: Failure to Provide Adequate Ventilation in Enclosed Space.*

The air in the stairwell warped. The violet energy around the door flickered. It wasn't enough to break the seal.

It wasn't enough to open the door. But it was enough to trigger the System's internal logic.

A notification window appeared in Gerald's peripheral vision.

**[ALERT: EXTERNAL AUDIT DETECTED]** **[SUBJECT: DUNGEON CORE 44-B (ASHFORD LIBRARY SUB-BASEMENT)]** **[STATUS: ACTIVE CITATION PENDING]** **[RESPONSE TIME: 10 SECONDS]**

Gerald's heart hammered against his ribs, but his face remained blank. He watched the timer.

*00:56:10.*

Brix looked at the notification light reflecting in Gerald's eyes. "What are you doing?"

"Creating a jurisdictional conflict," Gerald said.

The System did not allow multiple Cores to occupy the same spatial coordinates without a treaty. The IG was currently *occupying* Gerald's dungeon via its lockdown protocol. By asserting his own Core authority over the same space, Gerald forced the System to resolve the overlap.

The only way to resolve it was to pause the lockdown. Just for a moment. Just long enough to serve a physical citation.

*00:55:50.*

The violet energy around the door sputtered. For a fraction of a second, it turned gray. The seal failed.

Gerald didn't try to open the door.

He threw the clipboard.

The hard plastic case spun through the air and struck the violet barrier. It bounced off, but the impact created a ripple. A crack appeared in the energy field.

Gerald ran.

He didn't run away.

He ran *at* the door. He slammed his shoulder into the iron slab. The metal was hot, searing his jacket, but he didn't feel it.

He felt the give.

The door groaned. It swung inward, just an inch.

Enough.

Gerald reached out and grabbed the edge of the door. He pulled. The hinges screamed. The violet energy flared back to life, trying to push him back, but Gerald was anchored by his own mass, by his thirty-one years of leaning against stubborn things.

"Push!" he shouted at Brix.

The goblin threw himself against the door. His claws dug into the wood. He pushed with all his weight.

The door opened three inches.

A beam of sunlight cut through the gap. It was blinding, golden, and smelled of old paper and coffee.

*00:55:30.*

A voice boomed in Gerald's head. It was not a voice. It was a presence. Cold, metallic, and infinitely superior.

**[CEASE AND DESIST.]** **[YOU ARE IN VIOLATION OF PROTOCOL 7-ZULU.]**

Gerald ignored the voice. He stepped through the gap.

He stood in the library basement. The floor was linoleum. The walls were beige. The smell of mildew was faint, replaced by the scent of the industrial cleaner used by the janitorial staff.

Gerald was out.

But the lockdown remained. The violet light still sealed the door behind him.

He was outside the dungeon, but he was not safe.

The IG could still reach him. The IG could still punish him.

Unless Gerald made himself part of the municipal infrastructure.

Gerald turned to the wall. He looked at the electrical panel. It was old, rusted, and labeled with handwritten tags in red marker.

*Main Breaker.*

*Lighting Circuit B.*

*HVAC.*

Gerald walked to the panel. He opened the cover.

The hinges were rusted stiff, but he forced them.

Inside, the wires were chaotic.

A mess of copper and insulation.

He didn't touch the wires. He didn't touch the breakers.

He pulled out a small, laminated card from his pocket. His employee ID. *Gerald Hutchins, Senior Inspector, City of Ashford.*

He taped the ID card to the inside of the electrical panel door.

Then he pulled out his stamp. The blue ink pad. The rubber stamp that said **VIOLATION NOTICE.**

He stamped the card.

*VOID.*

*PENDING INSPECTION.*

Gerald stepped back. He looked at the ceiling. He looked at the library above.

He could hear the muffled sounds of patrons. The squeak of a chair.

The rustle of a book.

He was no longer just a dungeon core.

He was a building inspector who had filed a report on a live electrical hazard in a public building.

And by the Code, he had to fix it.

The System paused.

The violet energy around the door flickered, confused. The IG's lockdown protocol was designed for dungeons. It was designed to punish Cores. It was not designed to handle a municipal safety intervention that overlapped with the Core's physical location.

If Gerald was performing a legally mandated inspection, he had right-of-way.

**[CONFLICT DETECTED.]** **[MUNICIPAL AUTHORITY VS. SYSTEM AUTHORITY.]** **[RESOLUTION: DEFER TO LOCAL STATUTE UNTIL APPEAL HEARING.]**

The timer froze.

*00:55:29.*

The countdown stopped.

Brix slumped against the wall, sliding down to the linoleum floor. He was panting, his chest heaving. He looked at Gerald with wide, terrified eyes.

"You... you stopped it."

Gerald straightened his jacket. He brushed the dust off his shoulder. He picked up his clipboard.

"I didn't stop it," Gerald said. "I suspended enforcement pending review."

He walked to the door. He closed it gently. The violet energy remained, but it was dim now, waiting for the appeal to begin.

Gerald sat down on the floor. His legs felt weak. He knew this weakness was not just physical.

It was the cost of using his professional authority as a weapon.

The System respected rules, but it hated improvisation. Every time Gerald bent the rules, he paid a price in stability.

His Compliance Rating flickered.

**[CURRENT RATING: 41/100]** **[PENALTY APPLIED: TEMPORARY LOSS OF STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY (-15%)]**

The walls of the sub-basement behind him were cracking. Fine hairline fractures spread across the stone. The dungeon was becoming less stable. If the rating dropped too low, the dungeon would collapse.

Gerald looked at Brix.

"We have to appeal," Gerald said.

Brix nodded. "I'm ready."

Gerald shook his head. He looked at the ID card taped to the electrical panel. He looked at the stamp.

"No," Gerald said. "You're not."

Brix's eyes narrowed. "What?"

The air in the basement grew cold.

The shadows in the corners lengthened. From the shadows, a figure emerged. It was not a goblin.

It was not a monster. It was a suit.

A tall, thin man in a gray suit, wearing a visor that glowed with a faint blue light. He held a briefcase made of obsidian.

The Inspector General's auditor.

The man walked into the room. He didn't look at Gerald. He looked at the electrical panel. He looked at the ID card.

"You have filed a complaint," the auditor said. His voice was smooth, like polished stone. "This is permissible. The Code allows for citizen oversight."

Gerald stood up. He kept his hands at his sides.

"I am filing a counter-complaint," Gerald said. "Against the IG for obstructing municipal operations."

The auditor smiled. It was a thin, cruel smile.

"Your counter-complaint is noted. However, your dungeon is currently undergoing an audit. You are required to submit your spawn logs, your layout plans, and your financial records for the past twelve hours."

Gerald froze.

Twelve hours.

He hadn't kept records for twelve hours. He had been fighting, running, and improvising. He had no logs.

"I have them," Gerald lied.

"Do you?" The auditor opened his briefcase. Inside were thousands of tiny, glowing scrolls. "Then produce them. Now."

Gerald's mind raced. He looked at Brix. Brix shook his head. He didn't have the records either.

If Gerald didn't produce them, the IG would cite him for *Record Keeping Violations*. That would trigger an immediate shutdown of the dungeon. The library would be condemned. Gerald would be erased.

Gerald looked at the timer. It was still frozen. But the auditor was waiting.

Gerald pulled out his pen. He opened a fresh page.

He didn't write numbers. He didn't write dates.

He wrote a single question.

*Who authorized the lockdown without a warrant?*

He held the paper out.

The auditor took it. He read it. The glowing scrolls in his briefcase flickered.

The auditor looked at Gerald. For the first time, his expression changed. Confusion.

"That is not a record," the auditor said.

"It is a procedural objection," Gerald said. "And objections must be addressed before records are requested. That is Section 12-B of the Appeal Protocol."

The auditor paused.

The System was thinking.

The logic was sound. It was a loophole.

A tiny, dangerous loophole.

If Gerald was right, the IG had to answer his question first.

If Gerald was wrong, the delay would be interpreted as non-compliance.

The auditor tapped his finger on the briefcase.

*Tap. Tap. Tap.*

"Very well," the auditor said. "We will address the objection. But you must answer this first."

The auditor pointed to Brix.

"Is this entity a registered spawn under your Core's jurisdiction?"

Gerald looked at Brix.

Brix was trembling. He looked at Gerald with desperate hope.

If Gerald said yes, Brix would be part of the dungeon.

He would be subject to the audit. He would be deleted if his records were incomplete.

If Gerald said no, Brix was a trespasser. He would be executed.

But there was a third option.

Gerald looked at the auditor. He looked at Brix. He looked at the electrical panel.

The wires inside the panel were exposed. The insulation was frayed.

"Gerald," Brix whispered. "Don't say anything."

Gerald clicked his pen.

"He is not a spawn," Gerald said.

The auditor raised an eyebrow. "Then who is he?"

Gerald looked Brix in the eyes.

"He is a witness," Gerald said. "And witnesses are protected by the Municipal Truth-in-Investigation Act. You cannot audit a witness. You can only interview them."

The auditor's visor flashed red.

**[FALSE ASSERTION DETECTED.]** **[SUBJECT 89-G IS CLASSIFIED AS HOSTILE ENTITY.]** **[ACTION: TERMINATE.]**

A blade of light formed in the auditor's hand.

Gerald didn't move. He didn't run. He stepped forward. He put himself between the auditor and Brix.

"I claim diplomatic immunity," Gerald said.

The blade stopped.

Diplomatic immunity.

It was a nonsense term. It didn't exist in the Code. It didn't exist in the System.

But Gerald had said it with such absolute, bureaucratic certainty that the System had to pause to verify the term.

**[SEARCHING DATABASE...]** **[TERM 'DIPLOMATIC IMMUNITY' NOT FOUND IN DUNGEON CORE CODE.]** **[CHECKING MUNICIPAL CODE...]** **[CHECKING FEDERAL CODE...]**

The pause lasted three seconds.

Three seconds was an eternity.

Gerald used those three seconds to reach into his pocket. He pulled out a single, crumpled receipt.

A receipt from the library cafe.

He slapped it onto the auditor's chest.

**[RECEIPT FOR PUBLIC SERVICES RENDERED.]**

The System scanned the receipt. It showed Gerald buying coffee. It showed the timestamp. It matched the time of the lockdown.

**[CITIZEN ENGAGED IN PUBLIC SERVICE DURING INCIDENT.]** **[STATUS: ACTIVE MUNICIPAL OFFICER.]** **[PROTECTION PROTOCOL: ENGAGED.]**

The blade dissolved.

The auditor stepped back. The visor dimmed.

**[AUDIT SUSPENDED PENDING LEGAL REVIEW.]** **[YOU HAVE 24 HOURS TO SUBMIT FULL RECORDS.]**

The auditor vanished.

The violet light around the door faded. The door swung open.

Brix fell to his knees. He vomited.

Gerald stood there, his hands shaking.

He looked at the receipt. It was stained with coffee.

It was worthless.

It was the only thing that had saved them.

He looked at the timer.

*00:00:00.*

The countdown had resumed.

But now it was counting up.

**[APPEAL HEARING SCHEDULED.]** **[LOCATION: THE SURFACE.]** **[TIME: 24:00:00 FROM NOW.]**

Gerald looked at the open door. He looked at the stairs leading up to the library.

He had bought himself a day.

But he had no records. And the IG would return.

Gerald looked at Brix.

"Get up," Gerald said. "We have a lot of paperwork to forge."
