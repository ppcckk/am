Chapter 151: Bureaucratic Violation

# Chapter 151

The pen tip hovered over the parchment. The ink did not flow. It pooled, thick and black, trembling in sync with Gerald's pulse.

"Nine minutes," the Obsidian Shard hummed. Its voice sounded like grinding granite. "You are stalling."

Gerald did not look up. He stared at the line of text he had written: *Integral Structural Component.*

It was a lie. A beautiful, bureaucratic lie.

But if the System accepted the premise that his dungeon was an extension of the 1920 library, then Appendix J, Section 4, Paragraph B applied.

Historical ventilation standards were laxer. The narrow shaft would be compliant. The Citation would be voided.

If he failed, the rat died.

"Your argument is incoherent," the Shard said.

It circled the desk, its crystalline facets catching the dim light of the sub-basement. "A dungeon is a separate entity.

You are not the library.

You are a parasite in its basement."

"Precedent," Gerald muttered.

The Shard paused. "What?"

Gerald finally looked up. His eyes were red-rimmed, but his voice remained flat, the tone of a man denying a permit extension to a contractor who had tried to bribe him with donuts.

"Case 74-B. The Gilded Hall of Mirrors.

Cited for non-compliant lighting in 1994.

Appealed on grounds of 'aesthetic heritage.' The System accepted that a dungeon built *adjacent* to a landmark could inherit landmark status if the structural load was shared.

I am sharing load. The library's foundation rests on my ceiling.

I am holding it up."

The Shard tilted its head. "That is a stretch."

"It is code," Gerald corrected. "Code is code. Even the weird code."

A red timer flashed in the corner of his vision. **04:12.**

Below them, the floor vibrated. A low, wet tearing sound echoed through the ventilation shafts. The rat squeaked, a high-pitched panic that cut through the silence.

"They are in the shaft," Gerald said. He did not look at the shaft. He looked at the Shard. "Your spawns."

"My constructs are merely... investigating," the Shard said. It did not look guilty. It looked bored.

"They are killing my tenant."

"Your tenant is a rodent. It has no rights. It is unregistered."

Gerald set the pen down. He picked up Form 11-B. He tore off the top corner.

"Section 3, Subsection C," Gerald said. "Biological entities within a dungeon footprint are considered 'Fixture Equipment' if they contribute to the hygiene or maintenance of the structure.

My rat maintains the mold balance.

Without it, the mold overtakes the air filtration. The library above would suffer black mold toxicity. Liability."

The Shard's facets dimmed. "You are invoking liability?"

"I am invoking code," Gerald said.

"If you kill the rat, you damage the library. You damage the landmark.

You violate the Historical Preservation Act of the System.

That is a Class A Felony. It overrides your combat privilege."

The Shard went still. The air in the room grew heavy.

"You are bluffing," the Shard whispered.

"Am I?" Gerald held up the torn form. "I have already filed the preliminary injunction.

It is in the queue.

If the rat dies, the injunction becomes active.

Your Compliance Rating drops by fifty points. You will lose your ability to spawn high-tier constructs for six months.

Your dungeon will be flagged for aggressive remediation."

The Shard's crystals flickered, rapid and erratic.

**02:45.**

The tearing sound grew louder. A claw, large and serrated, punched through the grate of the main ventilation intake. It pulled back, then thrust again. The metal screeched.

Gerald did not move. He watched the Shard.

"Call them off," Gerald said.

"You have no authority over my constructs."

"I have authority over the environment," Gerald said. "I am citing you for creating a nuisance hazard.

Noise.

Vibration. Potential structural compromise. Immediate abatement required."

"That is not a combat citation."

"It is a safety citation. And I am serving it now." Gerald slammed his hand on the desk. "Abate or appeal. Choose."

The Shard hesitated. It looked at the grate, where two more claws were now visible, prying the metal wide. It looked at Gerald. It looked at the timer.

**01:10.**

Gerald's hand shook again.

He gripped the edge of the desk. He thought about the 1920 foundation.

He thought about the weight of the books above.

He thought about the rat, hiding behind a support beam, its tiny heart beating against its ribs.

"Prove it," the Shard said. "Prove the library is in danger."

"I don't have to prove it," Gerald said. "The System presumes it. That is the nature of historical liability. The burden of proof is on the defendant."

The Shard laughed. It was a dry, cracking sound. "You think the System plays favorites?"

"The System plays rules," Gerald said. "I am the rule. You are the anomaly."

**00:59.**

The grate burst open. A construct, a spider-like thing made of obsidian glass and shadow, spilled into the room. It chattered its mandibles. It moved toward the beam where the rat hid.

Gerald did not shout. He did not cast a spell. He opened his mouth and spoke into the air, not to the construct, but to the System.

"Citation served. Violation: Uncontrolled Hazardous Movement in Historic Zone. Penalty: Immediate Stasis."

Silence.

The construct froze. Its legs locked mid-step. It hovered an inch off the ground, unable to move forward, unable to move back.

The Shard screamed. A sound of pure frustration. "You cannot stasis a combat construct!"

"Combat constructs are not permitted in Historic Zones during active preservation protocols," Gerald said. He pointed at the frozen spider.

"Section 12, Paragraph 4.

Safety over aggression.

The rule is absolute."

The timer hit **00:00**.

A green light pulsed across Gerald's vision.

**[APPEAL ACCEPTED.]** **[CITATION VOIDED.]** **[VIOLATION CITED: 12-4 (HAZARD IN HISTORIC ZONE).]** **[TARGET: OBSIDIAN SHARD CORE.]** **[PENALTY: CONSTRUCT STASIS - 10 MINUTES.]** **[PENALTY: COMPLIANCE RATING REDUCTION - 15 POINTS.]**

The spider collapsed. It did not die; it simply fell to the floor, inert.

The rat peeked out from behind the beam. It twitched its nose. It looked at Gerald, then at the frozen spider, then back at Gerald. It squeaked once, low and respectful.

Gerald exhaled. The tension in his shoulders did not leave, but it changed shape. It became something heavier. Responsibility.

The Shard glowed purple. It was furious.

"You used a loophole," the Shard hissed. "A small, stupid loophole."

"It was a regulation," Gerald corrected. "You forgot to read the fine print. Everyone does. That is why we have inspectors."

The Shard expanded. It loomed over Gerald, its mass increasing, the crystals sharpening into spikes.

"This is not over, Inspector. You have drawn a line in the sand. The other cores will notice.

They will come. And they will not play by your rules.

They will break the walls.

They will flood the basement. They will burn the library down to get to you."

"Let them try," Gerald said. He picked up his pen. "I have more forms."

The Shard pulsed with anger.

"You are a small thing. A moldy rat's keeper.

You are not a player.

You are a nuisance."

"I am a code enforcement officer," Gerald said. "And you are in violation."

The Shard vanished. The air returned to normal. The spider remained frozen on the floor, a statue of malice.

Gerald walked to the ventilation shaft. He pulled a small, rusted screwdriver from his pocket. He tightened the grate. Then he walked to the beam.

The rat approached him. It climbed onto his shoe.

Gerald sat down on the cold floor. He stroked the rat's fur. It was damp. It smelled of old paper and mildew.

"Good job," Gerald said to the rat. "You didn't bite them. That's good. Biting is unauthorized use of force."

The System pinged.

**[NEW COMPLIANCE RATING: 62/100.]** **[PASSIVE RESISTANCE UNLOCKED: HISTORICAL STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY (+5% Physical Damage Reduction).]** **[WARNING: AGGREGATE THREAT LEVEL INCREASING.]**

Gerald ignored the warning. He looked at the frozen spider. He needed to move it. It was blocking the egress route.

He stood up. He lifted the spider.

It was heavy, cold, and silent.

He carried it to the corner of the room.

He placed it on a wooden crate.

Then he went to his desk. He opened a new folder.

He wrote a title.

*Project: Fortification of Egress Routes and Defense of Historic Asset.*

He listed the threats. The Shard was one. But there were others.

The spider had not been alone. Four red dots.

Four constructs.

The Shard had sent four. Why send four if one was enough to kill a rat?

Because it was a test.

Gerald looked at the map of his dungeon. It was small. Four thousand square feet. A sub-basement.

If he wanted to survive, he could not just be compliant. He had to be hostile. But he could not be a dungeon. He had to be a building.

And buildings had defenses.

He opened the System menu. He searched for *Building Defenses.*

**[ACCESS DENIED.]** **[REASON: CLASS D CORE. DEFENSE MODULES REQUIRE CLASS B STATUS OR SPECIAL PERMIT.]**

Gerald frowned.

He searched for *Special Permits.*

**[SPECIAL PERMIT: ACTIVE DEFENSE INSTALLATION.]** **[REQUIREMENTS: CITATION HISTORY > 10.]** **[CURRENT CITATIONS: 1.]**

He needed nine more citations.

He needed to find someone else to cite. Someone who would let him.

He looked at the entrance to his dungeon. The door to the library stairs was open. A figure stood there.

It was not a monster.

It was a woman. She wore leather armor, stained with mud and blood.

She held a sword that was too big for her frame.

She looked nervous.

Gerald stood up. He adjusted his tie.

"Excuse me," he said.

The woman jumped. She raised her sword.

"Stay back!

I am a Paladin of the Silver Order!

I am here to cleanse the darkness!"

Gerald looked at her sword. He looked at her stance. He looked at the way she held the hilt.

"Ma'am," Gerald said. "You are standing in a designated egress zone."

The Paladin blinked. She lowered the sword slightly. "What?"

"Section 4, Paragraph 2," Gerald said. "Egress routes must remain clear of obstruction. Your sword is obstructing the path of travel.

Also, your stance is wide. You are blocking the swing radius of the fire extinguisher on the wall."

The Paladin looked at the wall.

There was a fire extinguisher there. It was new. Gerald had installed it yesterday.

It was bright red and shiny.

"I... I am here to fight," the Paladin said. She sounded confused.

"You are here to violate code," Gerald said. "I can issue a Citation for Obstructing Emergency Egress. It carries a fine of ten mana crystals. Payable immediately."

The Paladin looked at her party. Behind her, a mage and a rogue peeked into the room.

They looked terrified.

Not of Gerald. Of the frozen spider. And of the fact that Gerald was talking.

"He... he talks like a bureaucrat," the rogue whispered.

"He is a monster," the mage said. "A mimic. It's using words to confuse us."

Gerald pulled out a citation pad. He tore off a sheet.

"Sign here," he said. "Or pay the fine. Or leave. But do not block the exit."

The Paladin hesitated. She looked at the spider. She looked at the rat, which was now grooming its tail on the desk. She looked at Gerald.

"If I pay," the Paladin said slowly, "do we get a tour?"

Gerald paused.

A tour.

The System allowed tours. *Guided Tours of Historic Sites.*

If he gave a tour, he could control the flow of people. He could measure them. He could see what they touched. He could find violations.

He could get citations.

He needed nine.

"Only if you sign the liability waiver," Gerald said. "And you stay on the marked path. Do not touch the mold. The mold is sensitive."

The Paladin looked at her friends. She shrugged. She took the pen.

She signed.

**[CITATION RECEIVED: OBSTRUCTING EGRESS.]** **[FINE PAID: 10 MANA CRYSTALS.]** **[COMPLIANCE RATING: 65/100.]**

Gerald smiled. It was a small, thin smile. It did not reach his eyes.

"Follow me," Gerald said. "Mind the step. It is not up to code, but we are working on it."

He walked toward the stairs. The Paladin followed. The rogue and the mage followed.

Gerald did not look back. He did not check for the rat. He knew the rat was safe. The System had protected it.

But as he stepped into the stairwell, the lights flickered.

A new notification appeared. Not in his vision. On the wall.

Written in ice.

**[NEW CORE DETECTED: THE FROST WARDEN.]** **[LOCATION: DIRECTLY ABOVE.]** **[STATUS: HOSTILE.]** **[ACTION: FREEZING LIBRARY FOUNDATION.]**

Gerald stopped.

The library above was freezing. The books were cracking. The patrons were screaming.

His dungeon was losing heat. His Compliance Rating was dropping.

**[TEMPERATURE ALERT: BELOW MINIMUM STANDARD.]** **[VIOLATION: INADEQUATE HEATING.]**

He looked up. The ceiling was white with frost.

The Frost Warden was not citing him. It was killing him with cold.

Gerald looked at the Paladin. She was shivering. Her teeth chattered.

"Inspector," she stammered. "It is cold."

Gerald looked at the ice forming on his desk. He looked at the frozen spider.

He looked at the rat. It was curled into a ball, fur standing on end.

Gerald reached into his pocket. He pulled out his pen.

"Ma'am," he said. "Do you have any matches?"

The Paladin shook her head. She was afraid. Not of him. Of the cold.

Gerald looked at the ceiling. The ice was spreading. It was beautiful. And it was illegal.

He wrote.

Not an appeal.

A complaint.

*Re: Unpermitted Climate Control Modification in Historic Zone.*

He signed it with blood. He pressed his thumb to the parchment, cutting it on a shard of ice from the ceiling.

**[CITATION DRAFTED.]**

He threw it up.

It stuck to the ice.

The ice cracked.

And then, from the darkness above, a voice boomed. Deep. Cold. Ancient.

"Who dares?"

Gerald straightened his tie.

"The Inspector," he said. "And you are in violation."
