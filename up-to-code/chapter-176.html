Chapter 176: Obstruction in the Void

# Chapter 176

The lawyer's smile did not reach his eyes. It was a contractual smile, designed for closing deals and voiding warranties. He adjusted his cufflinks, which were made of a dark, non-reflective metal that Gerald recognized immediately as Void-Iron, typically reserved for high-security vault doors.

Gerald looked at the hammer in his hand. Then at the crack in the wall. Then at the dwarf, who was currently wiping concrete dust off his beard with a rag that looked suspiciously like a used diaper.

"You're obstructing an active inspection," Gerald said. His voice was flat, devoid of the panic that should have been rattling his core. "And you are failing to provide proper site safety signage for the demolition zone. Where is your PPE?"

The lawyer, whose name tag read *Silas Vane, Esq.*, stepped forward. He didn't walk so much as he glided, his shoes making no sound on the debris-strewn floor.

"Mr. Hutchins," Vane said.

"You are currently in breach of your fiduciary duty to the Library District.

The structural integrity of this sub-basement is critical to the zoning laws of Ashford. By swinging that hammer, you have initiated a sequence of events that voids your liability protection.

"

"Liability protection?" Gerald asked.

He dropped the hammer. It clattered against the floor, a sound that echoed too in the confined space.

"I have never had liability protection.

I have had a pension and a very bad back.

"

"Not anymore," Vane said. "The System has updated its Terms of Service. Section 4, Subsection C: *Core Actions with Civilian Impact.* If a dungeon core's actions cause collateral damage to municipal infrastructure, the core is personally liable for the repair costs. And the legal fees."

The dwarf snorted.

"He's right, Inspector.

You can't just smash things.

You'll get sued into oblivion.

And in the System, a lawsuit isn't just money. It's stat drain.

Permanent."

Gerald looked at Vane.

"Show me the statute."

Vane produced a document from thin air. It was thick, bound in leather, and smelled of sulfur.

"Here it is.

You have twenty-four hours to rectify the structural deficit you caused. If the library above collapses, you are liable for thirty-one years of damages. Plus interest.

"

Gerald took the document. He did not read it. He knew what it said. He had written regulations like this when he was a junior inspector, trying to cover the city's ass after a sewer backup flooded the Mayor's office.

"I need a permit for the demolition," Gerald said.

"There is no permit," Vane said.

"This is an emergency repair. But you must have a licensed structural engineer on site to certify the safety of the remaining load-bearing elements.

The dwarf here is a contractor.

He is not an engineer.

"

The dwarf, whose name was Brokk, crossed his arms.

"I know what I'm doing."

"You are not certified," Gerald said. "I need a stamp."

"You don't have one," Vane said. "And you don't have time to hire one.

The ceiling is sagging by three millimeters per hour.

If it hits five, the library's foundation shifts.

If it shifts, the school bus parked outside becomes a hazard.

"

Gerald closed his eyes. He could feel the dungeon's Compliance Rating dropping. It was currently at 12/100, a number that made his teeth ache.

Every crack he created lowered it further. Every violation was a fine he couldn't pay.

"Brokk," Gerald said. "Stop swinging."

Brokk lowered his pickaxe.

"What's the plan, boss? We got ten minutes before the roof gives way."

"We don't break the wall," Gerald said. "We reinforce it."

"With what?" Brokk asked. "We got rubble. We got dust. We got a lawyer who smells like expensive cologne and bad news."

Gerald looked at Vane.

"You said I'm liable for collateral damage. That means the damage is considered a *municipal asset* under this new clause?"

Vane raised an eyebrow.

"Precisely. The damage is now part of the city's infrastructure inventory. Which means it falls under the jurisdiction of the Building Inspector.

You."

Gerald picked up the hammer.

"So, if I classify the crack as a *Feature*, not a *Defect*, I can bypass the demolition permit?"

Vane's smile tightened.

"That is... a creative interpretation. But it requires a re-zoning of the dungeon's internal layout. You would need to designate the cracked area as a 'Public Access Egress Route.' Which requires compliance with ADA standards for width and gradient."

"Width is four feet," Gerald said. He looked at the crack. It was narrow. Too narrow.

"Then you're out of compliance," Vane said. "And the penalty for misclassifying a hazard is immediate Core Shutdown."

Gerald looked at Brokk.

"Can you widen the crack without breaking the wall?"

Brokk scratched his head.

"Widen it?

No.

I can chip away at the edges. Make it look wider than it is. Optical illusion.

Works great for tourists.

"

"Optical illusion doesn't satisfy code," Gerald said. "The System scans geometry, not appearance."

Gerald walked to the wall. He ran his hand over the spiderwebbing cracks. They were warm. The System was watching.

"I need to change the definition of 'Wall,'" Gerald said.

Vane laughed. "You can't change the definition of a wall, Mr. Hutchins. It is a fundamental concept."

"Code doesn't care about concepts," Gerald said. "It cares about labels. I can issue a Citation."

He raised his hand. The air around him shimmered. A semi-transparent blue box appeared in front of the wall.

**CITATION ISSUED: UNLICENSED STRUCTURAL ELEMENT.** **TARGET: LOAD-BEARING WALL.** **VIOLATION: FAILURE TO DISPLAY CERTIFICATION OF LOAD.**

Vane's smile vanished.

"You can't cite a wall."

"I can cite anything with a presence," Gerald said. "And because I cited it, it is now subject to inspection. And because it is subject to inspection, it is technically 'Under Review.' And under System rules, an 'Under Review' element cannot be demolished. It must be preserved in its current state until the review is complete."

Brokk's jaw dropped.

"You're saying if I hit it, I'm violating a legal hold?"

"Exactly," Gerald said. "You can't tear down a wall that's under subpoena."

Vane stepped forward.

"That is a loophole, Mr.

Hutchins. A dangerous one.

The Inspector General will see this as abuse of process.

"

"Let him see," Gerald said. "I have thirty years of experience finding holes in municipal code. This isn't abuse. It's advocacy."

He looked at the wall. The cracks were still there. But the System's penalty timer for the demolition had stopped. The hammer swing had registered, but the citation had frozen the status.

"Brokk," Gerald said. "Do not touch the wall. Do not touch the floor near the wall. Stand still."

"What about the ceiling?" Brokk asked.

"We need to support it," Gerald said. "But we don't have materials. We have... me."

Vane tilted his head.

"You?"

"I am a Dungeon Core," Gerald said. "My body is the dungeon. If I extend a limb—a pillar—into the space, it is part of my anatomy. And my anatomy is compliant, because I built it up to code."

Gerald closed his eyes. He focused on the spot where the ceiling sagged. He felt the cold, damp stone beneath his feet. He reached out with his mind, pushing against the fabric of his own dungeon.

It hurt. It felt like trying to grow a tooth in his foot.

But he had done it before. He had grown stairs to meet the minimum width requirement. He had grown handrails to satisfy the ADA.

A section of the floor rose up. It was rough, jagged, but solid. It grew upward, pushing against the sagging ceiling. It stopped two feet short.

"Too short," Vane said.

"I can extend it," Gerald said. He gritted his teeth.

The floor-pillar stretched upward.

It groaned. Dust rained down.

It touched the ceiling.

The sagging stopped.

**STRUCTURAL STABILIZATION COMPLETE.** **PENALTY CANCELED.** **NEW BONUS: PASSIVE RESISTANCE TO COMPRESSION (MINOR).**

Gerald exhaled. His core felt lighter.

Vane looked at the pillar. He looked at Gerald.

"That was reckless."

"It was compliant," Gerald said. "Now, let's talk about the school bus."

Vane checked his watch.

"The bus is still there. But the library is stable. For now."

"For now?" Gerald asked.

"The Inspector General has flagged your Core," Vane said. "He doesn't like your interpretations. He has dispatched an Auditor."

Gerald felt a chill run down his spine. An Auditor was worse than a lawyer. A lawyer argued with words. An Auditor argued with force.

"Where is he?" Gerald asked.

"Already here," Vane said.

The air in the sub-basement changed. The temperature dropped. The light from Gerald's bioluminescent moss flickered and died, replaced by a harsh, sterile white glow.

A door appeared in the middle of the room. It was not a door Gerald had built. It was not a door that existed in the physical space. It was a System construct.

It opened.

A figure stepped through. He was ten feet tall, made of polished marble. His face was a smooth mask with no features, save for a single, glowing red eye in the center of his forehead. He wore a suit tailored from the skin of defeated dungeon bosses, stitched together with gold thread.

He held a clipboard.

**INSPECTOR GENERAL AUDIT: COMMENCING.**

Brokk backed away, raising his pickaxe. Vane raised his hands, as if in surrender.

Gerald did not move. He looked at the Auditor. He looked at the clipboard.

"State your name and rank," Gerald said.

The Auditor did not speak. He simply raised the clipboard. A text box appeared in the air, large and red.

**CORE VIOLATIONS DETECTED:** **1. UNAUTHORIZED CITATION USAGE.** **2. MALICIOUS COMPLIANCE MANIPULATION.** **3. FAILURE TO REPORT SELF-AUDIT.**

**PENALTY: IMMEDIATE CORE SUSPENSION.** **REMEDY: VOLUNTARY DEMOLITION OF DUNGEON FOOTPRINT.**

Gerald looked at the red text. He looked at the Auditor's marble face.

"Voluntary demolition," Gerald said. "That's a choice."

"It is a recommendation," the Auditor said. His voice sounded like grinding stones.

Gerald looked at Brokk, and the dwarf was trembling. He looked at Vane.

The lawyer was smiling again.

A triumphant smile.

"If I refuse," Gerald said, "what happens?"

"The System will enforce the demolition," the Auditor said. "Your Core will be shattered. Your dungeon will be erased. The library above will lose its foundation support."

Gerald looked at the library's ceiling. He thought about the school bus. He thought about the thirty-one years he had spent measuring door widths.

He looked at the Auditor.

"I appeal," Gerald said.

The Auditor's red eye blinked.

"Appeal accepted," the Auditor said. "Hearing scheduled: Immediate.

Location: The Void.

Participants: Gerald Hutchins, Inspector General.

Witness: Silas Vane, Esq. Observer: Brokk, Unlicensed Contractor.

"

The floor beneath Gerald's feet vanished, and he fell.

He did not scream. He held his clipboard.
