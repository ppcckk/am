Chapter 200: Critical Structural Failure

# Chapter 200

Gerald counted three staples on the filing tab before realizing he had used the wrong form. It solidified.

Gerald stood on a floor of polished obsidian, the kind used in high-end municipal courthouses before the budget cuts of '08. Across from him, Ironspire Core-992 loomed, a towering construct of jagged iron and burning mana. The air smelled of ozone and old paper.

Gerald's hands were shaking.

Not from fear.

From the realization that he had filed the paperwork in triplicate instead of duplicate.

The System interface hovered in the air between them, a glowing red rectangle that pulsed with the rhythm of a failing heart.

**HEARING INITIATED.** **SUBJECT: PROCEDURAL FRAUD VIA OBSOLETE REGULATORY FRAMEWORK.** **PROSECUTION: CORE-992 (IRONSPIRE).** **DEFENSE: CORE-404 (HUTCHINS).** **STAKES: TOTAL CORE DELETION.**

Ironspire's voice boomed, vibrating through Gerald's spectral form.

"Your Honor, the defendant cites Section 44.2 of the Municipal Structural Act, revised in 1994. The System's current active build is the *Post-Metamorphosis Harmonized Code*, enacted in 2023.

The 1994 text was superseded.

By citing it, the defendant did not merely err. He committed fraud.

"

Gerald looked at the System's judge. It wasn't a person. It was a floating geometric shape, a dodecahedron made of pure light, rotating slowly. It had no face, but it had attention.

Gerald's mind raced, and he had memorized the 1994 codes because they were the only ones he knew by heart. The 2023 codes were a disaster. They were written by adventurers, not engineers. They prioritized

"dungeon flow" over "structural integrity." They allowed load-bearing walls to be made of compressed air if they had a mana rating above 50.

Gerald hated compressed air walls.

They leaked.

"I object," Gerald said. His voice was flat. Dry. The voice he used when a contractor tried to tell him that plywood was a valid substitute for fire-rated drywall.

The dodecahedron paused its rotation.

"State the basis."

"Relevance," Gerald said.

Ironspire sneered, a sound like grinding gears.

"Irrelevance is not a defense against fraud, Inspector. You used a dead law to kill my spawns."

"I used a law that was in effect when the wall was built," Gerald said. "The wall was constructed during the initial dungeon spawn event in 2019. Under the doctrine of *Retroactive Compliance Exemption*, structures built under prior codes are judged by the codes of their construction era, provided they have not undergone a Class-3 Renovation."

Silence.

Ironspire froze. The metal plates on his torso clicked together nervously.

"Class-3 Renovation," the Judge's voice echoed, devoid of tone. "Did the defendant perform a Class-3 Renovation on the cited wall?"

"No," Gerald said. "It was raw spawn. Unfinished. Unregistered."

Ironspire's mana flared.

"That is a technicality! The current code supersedes all!"

"Current code applies to *current* conditions," Gerald said.

"If I change a lightbulb, I must use current electrical codes. But if I check a foundation, I check it against the foundation codes.

The wall did not change.

My inspection did not change it. I only observed it.

"

The Judge's light shifted from red to amber.

Gerald felt a bead of sweat roll down his spine. He had gambled everything on a footnote in Appendix Z of the 1994 Manual. *Subsection 9: Preservation of Historical Non-Conformity.*

He had spent three nights reading it.

Ironspire was panicking. The dodecahedron rotated faster.

"Evidence," the Judge commanded.

"Submitted," Gerald said.

He pulled a scroll from his inventory. It wasn't magic.

It was a PDF he had scanned from the physical binder in the Library's archive room before the apocalypse.

He had printed it on actual paper because the System respected physical media more than digital entries, which was a quirk Gerald had discovered in Chapter 45. Digital files could be corrupted by mana surges.

Paper could not.

He handed it to the Judge.

The dodecahedron scanned the text.

Ironspire slammed a fist onto the obsidian floor. Cracks spiderwebbed outward.

"This is absurd! The System updates in real-time! The 1994 code is a ghost!"

"A ghost that protects the innocent," Gerald said. "And the negligent."

The Judge remained silent.

Gerald watched the light shift. Amber to green.

**RULING: DEFENSE MOTION GRANTED. JURISDICTIONAL PRECEDENCE ESTABLISHED.** **CITATION UPHELD.** **FRAUD CHARGES DISMISSED.**

Ironspire roared.

The white void shattered.

Gerald was back in the sub-basement, and he fell to his knees. The concrete floor was cold. Damp. Perfect.

The wall that had started it all was still there, but it was different now. The System's remediation order had taken effect.

The 3-degree tilt was gone.

The wall was plumb. Straight. Boring.

Beautiful.

Gerald exhaled.

His inventory window popped up.

**NOTICE: CORE-992 HAS FILED A SECONDARY APPEAL.** **SUBJECT: ADMINISTRATIVE RETALIATION.** **CLAIM: INSPECTOR HUTCHINS ENGAGED IN UNAUTHORIZED RESOURCE ALLOCATION DURING LEGAL PROCEEDINGS.** **DETAILS: USE OF PHYSICAL MEDIA (PAPER) TO SUBMIT EVIDENCE.

PAPER REQUIRES ORGANIC MATERIAL. ORGANIC MATERIAL REQUIRES BIOLOGICAL INPUT.

BIOLOGICAL INPUT REQUIRES SANITARY ZONES.

SANITARY ZONES ARE NOT PRESENT IN DUNGEON 404.** **PENALTY: IMMEDIATE HYGIENIC AUDIT. FAILURE TO COMPLY RESULTS IN CORE DELETION.**

Gerald stared at the screen.

Paper, and he had used paper.

Paper was made from trees. Trees were biological. The System classified biological matter as

"unsanitary" unless it was in a designated waste disposal zone.

Gerald didn't have a waste disposal zone, and he had a mold spore.

And a rat.

The rat was currently trying to eat the edge of the PDF Gerald had dropped.

"Stop," Gerald said. The rat ignored him.

He had to build a sanitary zone.

But he was a Dungeon Core. He couldn't just *build* it.

He had to spawn it.

And spawning required mana.

And he was out of mana. The hearing had cost him nearly everything.

He checked his stats.

**MANA: 12/5000.**

The audit would happen in ten minutes.

Gerald looked around his dungeon. 4,000 square feet of concrete, exposed pipes, and shadows.

He needed a bathroom.

But not a human bathroom. A *System-sanctioned* sanitary zone.

He opened the Construction Menu, which was greyed out.

**INSUFFICIENT MANA.**

Gerald grit his teeth. He couldn't build a bathroom. He couldn't afford the pipes. He couldn't afford the porcelain.

But he could repurpose.

The code, and he remembered a loophole. A tiny, ugly, bureaucratic loophole from the 1997 Plumbing Addendum.

*Section 12: Temporary Sanitary Facilities.*

"If a permanent fixture is unavailable, a temporary facility may be designated in any enclosed space with a floor area of at least ten square feet, provided it is separated from food preparation and living areas by a barrier of non-porous material."

Gerald looked at the corner of the room.

The corner where the water leak had dripped for thirty years, and the floor there was tiled. Old, cracked ceramic tile. Non-porous enough.

He didn't have walls.

But he had the *shadow*.

The System recognized shadows as

"structural voids" if they were cast by a light source and blocked by an opaque object.

Gerald looked at his own core, which was a cube of blue light.

If he dimmed the core to 10% brightness, and moved the three mold spores to cast a specific angle of shadow...

He could create a

"barrier."

It was insane.

It was inefficient, which was technically compliant.

Gerald moved.

He didn't walk; he projected his consciousness across the dungeon, shifting the mold spores. They resisted. They were chaotic. They wanted to grow on the wet patch.

"Move," Gerald commanded. "Left. Three inches.

Right. Two inches.

"

The mold drifted, confused by the sudden instruction, and he dimmed his core. The blue light faded to a dim twilight.

Shadows stretched.

The mold spores, now positioned correctly, blocked the light just enough to cast a hard, black rectangle against the corner.

**STRUCTURE DETECTED: SHADOW BARRIER.** **DENSITY: 0.4. INSUFFICIENT FOR SANITARY ZONE.**

Gerald cursed, and he needed denser shadows.

He looked at the rat.

The rat was fat.

If the rat was in the corner, its body would block the light.

But the rat was a

"Vermin Entity." Vermin entities were classified as "Biohazards." Placing a biohazard in a sanitary zone was a violation.

Unless the biohazard was *contained*.

Gerald opened his inventory again, and he had one item left.

A single, heavy-duty rubber stopper.

He had found it in the library's maintenance closet before the world ended. He had kept it because it was the only thing that fit the drain in the sub-basement sink.

He couldn't put the stopper on the rat.

But he could put the stopper in the *drain*.

The System's logic was literal.

*Sanitary Zone* = *Enclosed Space* + *Non-Porous Barrier* + *Proper Drainage*.

If he plugged the drain, the space wasn't properly drained. It was a stagnant pool.

Stagnant pools were *not* sanitary.

Gerald's hands hovered over the interface, and he had two choices.

One: Accept the audit, fail, and get deleted.

Two: Create a zone that was so technically compliant it was functionally useless, thereby satisfying the *letter* of the law while violating its *spirit*.

The System punished violations of the law.

It did not punish violations of the spirit.

Gerald dragged the rubber stopper into the corner. He placed it over the existing floor drain.

Then, he dragged the mold spores over it.

Then, he moved the rat onto the mold.

The rat squeaked.

**SANITARY ZONE DETECTED.** **STATUS: TEMPORARY.** **CONTAINMENT: FAILED (BIOHAZARD PRESENT).**

**AUDIT FAILURE IMMINENT.**

Gerald's heart hammered, and he had to fix the containment.

He needed a lid.

He had no lid.

He looked at the ceiling.

The ceiling was low. 8 feet.

If he lowered the ceiling.., and he couldn't build.

But he could *cite* the ceiling.

If the ceiling was cited for

"Insufficient Clearance," the System might lower it to meet the new requirement.

What was the minimum clearance for a sanitary zone?

4 feet, and the current clearance was 8 feet.

Too much.

If he cited it for

"Excessive Height," the System might not fix it. Excessive height wasn't a safety violation.

Unless...

Unless he linked it to *ventilation*.

High ceilings in small rooms created dead air pockets.

Dead air pockets were a ventilation violation.

**VIOLATION: INADEQUATE AIR CIRCULATION IN RESTRICTED ZONE.** **REMEDIATION REQUIRED: REDUCE VOLUME TO IMPROVE AIRFLOW.**

Gerald opened a new Citation window.

Target: The Ceiling above the corner.

Charge: Violation of Airflow Dynamics, Section 8.

Penalty: Immediate Structural Adjustment, and he submitted it.

The ceiling groaned.

Concrete cracked.

Dust rained down.

The ceiling dropped.

Slowly, agonizingly, the slab of concrete lowered itself until it was exactly four feet above the mold-covered stopper.

The rat was now trapped between the dropped ceiling and the floor.

It squeaked, panicked.

But the space was now enclosed, and the barrier was the shadow.

The floor was tile.

The ceiling was concrete.

The drain was stopped.

**SANITARY ZONE VALIDATED.** **STATUS: COMPLIANT.** **AUDIT PAUSED.**

Gerald slumped, and he had saved himself.

By creating a four-foot tall closet with a live rat inside it.

The System's Audit window appeared.

**AUDIT COMPLETE.** **HYGIENE RATING: 65/100.** **WARNING: BIOLOGICAL CONFINEMENT VIOLATION DETECTED.** **RAT (ENTITY-404-1) IS NOT AUTHORIZED FOR STORAGE.** **FINE: 500 MANA.**

Gerald didn't care, and he had passed.

But then, the ground shook.

Not from the System.

From above.

The Library was above him. The Library was collapsing.

Gerald looked up at the ceiling he had just lowered, and a crack ran through it.

Not a hairline crack.

A canyon.

Through the gap, he saw light.

Not the blue light of his core.

Golden light.

Heroic light.

A voice boomed down from the hole.

"Is there anyone down here? We're clearing the sub-basement! The Party Leader says there's a Dungeon Core causing the tremors! We're here to purge the corruption!"

Gerald looked at the crack.

Adventurers, and they were right above him.

And they were about to drop a mace on his head.

Gerald looked at his mana.

0.

He looked at his dungeon.

A 4-foot sanitary zone.

A rat.

A mold spore.

And a ceiling that had just dropped four feet, creating a new structural imbalance in the rest of the room.

The rest of the room was now tilted.

5 degrees.

5 degrees was a violation.

5 degrees was unsafe.

5 degrees required remediation.

But he had no mana to fix it.

Unless he cited *himself*.

If Gerald cited his own dungeon for

"Critical Structural Instability," the System would force a stabilization.

But the penalty for self-citation was a Mana Lockout, and he wouldn't be able to spawn anything.

He wouldn't be able to fight.

He would be a rock.

A rock that the adventurers would break.

The voice came again.

"I see a glow down there! Take the shot!"

Gerald made his choice, and he opened the Citation window.

Target: Gerald Hutchins.

Charge: Critical Structural Failure.

Submit.

The world went black.
