Chapter 386: Blank Page Problem

# Chapter 386

The white light did not kill him. It suspended him.

Gerald hung in a void of absolute stillness, his fists still raised, his body locked in the posture of a man who had just tried to punch through biology. The countdown timer hovered before his eyes, red and pulsing.

**Core Extraction in: 00:04**

He couldn't move. He couldn't breathe. He could only look at the number ticking down.

Then, the void ripped open.

Not with a flash, but with a sound like tearing wet paper. A hole punched through the white light, jagged and dark. Through it spilled air. Cold, stale, library-air smelling of old paper and dust.

A hand grabbed Gerald's collar. It yanked him backward with brutal efficiency.

Gerald slammed onto a hard wooden floor. The white light snapped off, leaving him in dim, flickering amber. He gasped, sucking in lungfuls of oxygen that tasted of mildew. He coughed, hacking up a speck of green slime that had coated his tongue.

He wasn't in the dungeon anymore, and he was in the sub-basement hallway. The real one. The one outside the mold-infested chamber.

Standing over him was Vane. The Inspector General's avatar had manifested physically for the first time. He looked like a bureaucrat carved from obsidian, wearing a suit that fit perfectly but seemed to have too many buttons.

Vane straightened his tie. He did not look pleased.

"That," Vane said, his voice smooth as oiled glass, "was an unauthorized intervention."

Gerald scrambled to his knees. He checked his hands.

They were intact, and he checked his chest. No extraction.

"You pulled me out," Gerald rasped.

"I paused the extraction," Vane corrected.

"The System protocol for Unlawful Containment requires the Core to be physically separated from the non-compliant structure. You were inside the structure.

Technically, you were the structure.

Extracting you would have destroyed the dungeon footprint.

"

"I saved the dungeon," Gerald said. He stood up.

His legs felt like jelly, but he forced them to hold weight.

He needed to look stable.

If he looked weak, Vane would audit him.

"You created a Code 14 violation," Vane said. He held up a tablet. It glowed with red text.

"Sealed environment. No ventilation.

No egress.

You trapped yourself inside your own containment zone. That is a Class A Self-Hazard. Penalty: Immediate Deactivation of the Core.

"

"I widened the corridor," Gerald said.

He pointed at the mold-sealed door behind him. It was solid stone now.

The green slime was gone, calcified into rock.

"The corridor is thirty-six inches wide. Compliant. I fixed the structural collapse risk."

Vane tilted his head.

"The corridor is compliant.

The room is not.

You have sealed a habitable space without ventilation, which is not a fix. That is a tomb.

"

"It's not habitable," Gerald said. "It's storage."

"Define storage."

"Cold. Dark. Sealed. No living entities."

Vane tapped the tablet.

"The System does not recognize 'storage' as a category for a Core's primary chamber. Your primary chamber is sealed.

Therefore, your primary chamber is dead. Therefore, your Core is dead.

"

Gerald's heart hammered against his ribs. He looked at the seal. The mold had hardened into a perfect circle of grey stone. It was beautiful, in a grotesque way.

"I can reopen it," Gerald said.

"You cannot," Vane said.

"The extraction protocol has already begun. The System has flagged the chamber as a Hazard Zone. You are locked out.

You cannot enter your own dungeon.

You are currently standing in limbo. If you do not provide a valid appeal within five minutes, the System will liquidate the footprint and reassign the location to the next available Core.

Which, coincidentally, is the sewer rat nest three blocks east.

"

Gerald felt a cold sweat break on his neck. He hated rats. They didn't follow code. They didn't file forms.

"I need to speak to the mold," Gerald said.

Vane blinked. "The mold is not a conscious entity. It is a procedural hazard."

"It reacted to my pressure," Gerald said. "It sealed the breach. It decided I was a threat.

That's a decision. That's a logic loop.

I can appeal the logic.

"

Vane checked his watch.

"You have four minutes and ten seconds. Appeal what?"

"I'm appealing the citation for Unlawful Containment," Gerald said. "I'm arguing that the containment was not unlawful. It was a necessary structural remediation."

Vane smiled. It was a terrible smile. It showed too many teeth.

"Very well," Vane said.

He gestured to the air.

"Hearing initiated.

You will argue against the System's automated logic. If you lose, you are deleted. If you win, the chamber remains, but you are still locked out until the Hazard Flag is lifted.

How will you prove the containment was necessary?

"

Gerald closed his eyes. He thought about the stress fractures. He thought about the load-bearing wall. He thought about the fact that if he hadn't widened the gap, the ceiling would have come down.

"The mold is reactive," Gerald said.

"It's a biological sealant. When I applied pressure to the weak point, it reacted by hardening to support the structure. It wasn't containing me.

It was supporting me.

"

Vane laughed. A dry, rasping sound.

"That is anthropomorphism, Inspector. The System does not recognize intent in non-sentient entities."

"It recognizes function," Gerald said. He opened his eyes.

He looked Vane in the eye.

"Code Section 7, Subsection 4: Temporary Structural Support. If a hazard is stabilized by a reactive material, it is considered a temporary patch, not a permanent containment. I am arguing that the mold is a temporary patch."

Vane's eyes narrowed.

"Cite the precedent."

Gerald paused. He didn't have a precedent. He had a gut feeling. A bad one.

"There is no precedent," Gerald said. "But there is a loophole."

Vane leaned in.

"Loopholes are frowned upon, Gerald."

"Section 12, Clause 9," Gerald said. "Exemption for Emergency Repairs. If a repair prevents immediate collapse, the material used is exempt from standard material certification requirements for a period of forty-eight hours."

Vane checked the tablet. He frowned.

"That is for municipal buildings," Vane said. "Not dungeon cores."

"The System is based on municipal code," Gerald said. "I built this dungeon on a municipal code framework.

You told me that in Chapter One.

You said I could use the code to shut down rival dungeons.

So the code applies. The exemption applies.

"

Vane was silent. The tablet glowed brighter. He was running the query.

Gerald held his breath.

"The query is... ambiguous," Vane said. "The System does not have a precedent for a Core using a biological agent as a structural patch."

"So it's new," Gerald said. "Apply the municipal standard. It's a patch.

It's temporary. It's holding up the ceiling.

Therefore, it's not a containment.

It's a support.

"

Vane tapped the screen.

"The System is accepting the argument. Provisionally."

Gerald exhaled. His knees buckled slightly, but he caught himself.

"However," Vane continued.

"The citation for Lack of Ventilation remains. You have sealed a chamber.

Even if it's a patch, the air exchange is zero, which is a Code 14 violation. Penalty: Debuff to all spawn regeneration rates by 50% for one week.

"

"That's fair," Gerald said. He could work with 50% regen. He could manage.

"Furthermore," Vane said, "because the chamber is sealed, it is now classified as a Dead Zone. No adventurers can enter.

No loot can be generated, and the chamber is effectively offline.

You have lost thirty percent of your revenue potential.

"

Gerald felt a sharp pain in his chest. That was real money. That was real XP.

"Can I reopen it?" Gerald asked.

"Not until the Hazard Flag clears," Vane said. "Which requires ventilation. Which requires opening the seal. Which requires breaking the structural support you just created."

Vane looked at him with pity.

"So you have to choose," Vane said. "Keep the structure, lose the revenue. Or break the seal, lose the structure."

Gerald stared at the sealed door. The grey stone was perfect. It was strong.

"I need more time," Gerald said.

"You have two minutes left on the appeal window," Vane said. "Use them."

Gerald looked at the tablet. He looked at Vane. He looked at the door.

He needed a third option, and he walked to the wall. He placed his hand on the stone. It was cold.

He could feel the vibration of the dungeon's heartbeat through it. Faint.

But there.

He closed his eyes. He reached out with his Core Sense. He couldn't enter the room, but he could sense the air pressure.

There was a draft.

Tiny. Imperceptible to a human. But to a Core, it was a scream.

"Air is leaking," Gerald said.

Vane looked up.

"Impossible. The seal is airtight."

"It's not," Gerald said. "The mold hardened, but it contracted. It left micro-fractures.

The air is moving through them.

Slowly. But it's moving.

"

Vane checked the sensors. His eyes widened.

"Sensors show a 0.04% air exchange rate," Vane said.

"0.04% is not zero," Gerald said.

"Code 14 requires zero air exchange for a full cycle. 0.04% is technically ventilation. It's bad ventilation. It's non-compliant ventilation.

But it's not zero.

"

Vane stared at him.

"That is splitting hairs," Vane said.

"That is law," Gerald said.

"I am amending the citation.

The violation is not Unlawful Containment. It is Inadequate Ventilation.

Penalty: Fine. Not Debuff.

"

Vane's face hardened.

"You are testing my patience, Inspector."

"I am testing your logic," Gerald said. "0.04% is a number.

The threshold is zero.

Therefore, I am compliant. Barely. But compliant.

"

Vane tapped the tablet. He looked at the data. He looked at Gerald.

"The System will accept this," Vane said.

"But the fine will be substantial. And the Hazard Flag remains.

You still cannot enter the chamber.

You still cannot spawn mobs in it.

"

"I can spawn them outside," Gerald said. "In the hallway."

"The hallway is not your dungeon," Vane said. "It is public easement. You cannot spawn there."

"I can spawn in the adjacent corridor," Gerald said. "The one that connects to the sewer."

Vane paused. "The sewer connection? That is a high-risk area. Rival Cores patrol there."

"Which is why I need to be able to defend it," Gerald said. "If I lose the chamber, I lose the defense.

If I can't defend the chamber, the rival Cores will take it.

They'll dig through the walls.

They'll breach the seal.

"

Vane looked at the sealed door. For the first time, he looked concerned.

"If the seal breaks," Vane said, "the structural integrity fails. The dungeon collapses. You lose everything."

"Then we need a guard," Gerald said. "A guard that can operate in the hallway. A guard that can keep the rivals away from the door."

Vane looked at Gerald. He looked at the sealed door.

"You are asking for permission to deploy a combat unit in a public space," Vane said. "That is a Code 22 violation. Public Nuisance."

"I'm asking for permission to deploy a Security Measure," Gerald said. "Code Section 5, Subsection 8: Authorized Security Presence for Hazardous Zones."

Vane laughed. It was a sharp, barking sound.

"You are inventing subsections, Gerald."

"I'm reading the fine print," Gerald said. "It's there. I saw it in the manual.

Page 402, line 14. 'Security Measures may be deployed to protect structural assets from external threats.'

"

Vane checked the manual. He flipped through the pages. His finger traced the lines.

He stopped, and he looked at Gerald.

"It is there," Vane said. Quietly.

"Then I'm citing the permission," Gerald said. "I am deploying a Security Measure to protect the Hazardous Zone."

Vane sighed. He rubbed his temples.

"Fine," Vane said.

"You may deploy one unit. One. If you deploy two, you are in violation.

If the unit causes public distress, you are in violation.

If the unit leaves the zone, you are in violation.

"

"One unit," Gerald said.

"Who is it?" Vane asked.

Gerald thought, and he had no mobs in the dungeon. He had three mold spores. A water leak.

A rat.

The rat was gone.

The mold was the seal.

The water leak was the air source, and he needed something that could stand still. Something that looked intimidating but didn't move. Something that could block a doorway.

He looked at the ceiling. The pipes were leaking. The water dripped onto the floor.

Drip. Drip. Drip.

Gerald looked at Vane.

"I need a construct," Gerald said. "Made of water."

Vane blinked. "Water is not a valid spawn material."

"Code Section 18: Elemental Constructs," Gerald said.

"If the Core has sufficient environmental control, it may spawn elemental entities.

I have control over the water. I have control over the temperature.

I can make ice.

"

"You have no ice," Vane said. "It is summer."

"I can lower the temperature," Gerald said. "It will cost mana. It will lower the efficiency of the dungeon. But I can make ice."

Vane looked at the thermometer on his tablet. It read 72 degrees.

"You will have to lower the temperature to freezing," Vane said. "That will trigger a Code 19 violation: Hypothermia Risk. Penalty: Spawn Debuffs."

"I know," Gerald said. "But I need the guard."

Vane looked at him for a long time.

"Do it," Vane said. "But if you freeze the pipes, you lose the ventilation. You lose the appeal."

"Then I won't freeze the pipes," Gerald said. "I'll freeze the air."

Vane shook his head.

"Impossible. You cannot freeze air without freezing the container."

"I'll freeze the moisture," Gerald said. "The humidity.

I'll condense it.

Turn it into frost. Build a wall of frost.

"

Vane checked the humidity sensors. 65%.

"Very well," Vane said.

"You have ten minutes to build this wall. If it falls, you are in violation.

If it melts, you are in violation.

If it moves, you are in violation.

"

Gerald nodded. He turned away from Vane.

He raised his hands. He focused on the moisture in the air. He pulled it. He squeezed it.

The air in the hallway grew cold.

Gerald's breath plumed in front of him.

The water droplets on the pipes shined. They hardened.

Gerald gritted his teeth. He pushed harder.

The frost crept along the walls. It formed intricate patterns. Ice ferns. Ice spirals.

It was beautiful, which was also freezing.

Gerald's fingers went numb. He couldn't feel his toes.

The frost reached the door. It covered the grey stone. It created a barrier.

It was five inches thick, which was solid.

Gerald slumped against the wall. He was shivering. His teeth chattered.

Vane watched him.

"It is done," Vane said. "The wall is compliant.

It is static, which is decorative.

It is not a door.

"

Gerald looked at the ice wall. It blocked the view of the seal. It blocked the view of the chamber.

"Can I enter now?" Gerald asked.

Vane checked the sensors.

"The Hazard Flag remains. The chamber is still sealed.

But the air exchange is now 0.08%. Improved.

"

"I can't enter," Gerald said.

"No," Vane said. "But you can defend."

Gerald closed his eyes. He was exhausted. He was cold. He was broke.

But he had a wall.

And he had a rule.

"Next audit," Vane said. "In six hours. I expect the Compliance Rating to improve. If it does not, I will not be so lenient."

Vane faded away. The obsidian suit dissolved into mist.

Gerald stood alone in the hallway.

The ice wall glittered in the amber light.

A shadow moved at the end of the corridor.

Gerald turned, and a goblin stood there.

It was large. It held a rusty axe. It looked at the ice wall.

It looked at Gerald.

It grinned, which took a step forward.

Gerald didn't have a mob. He didn't have a weapon.

He had a citation.

Gerald raised his hand. He pointed at the goblin.

"Stop," Gerald said. His voice was quiet. "You are trespassing on a Hazardous Zone. Violation: Code 22."

The goblin laughed. It charged.

Gerald didn't move. He waited.

The goblin reached the ice wall.

It swung its axe.

The axe hit the ice.

The ice didn't break.

The goblin stumbled. It slipped on the frost.

It crashed into the wall.

Gerald watched. He didn't help. He didn't intervene.

He just watched.

The goblin tried to stand. It slipped again.

It looked up at Gerald. It looked confused.

Gerald pulled a small notebook from his pocket. He opened it.

He clicked his pen.

"Violation noted," Gerald said. "Uncontrolled Aggression. Penalty: Citation."

The System flashed.

A red symbol appeared over the goblin's head.

**CITED.**

The goblin screamed. Its health bar dropped.

It wasn't a combat hit. It was a bureaucratic hit.

The goblin fell. It didn't move.

Gerald closed the notebook, and he looked at the ice wall.

It was starting to melt.

He had to fix the temperature.

He had to fix the ventilation.

He had to fix the dungeon.

But first, he had to survive the night.

Because the System hadn't just cited the goblin, which had cited Gerald.

**Citation Issued: Core.**

**Violation: Unauthorized Frost Construction.**

**Penalty: Core Temperature Lock. Cannot adjust environmental controls for 24 hours.**

Gerald looked at the melting ice, and he looked at his freezing hands.

He looked at the red light blinking in the vent.

The air was getting warmer.

The ice was dying.

And the goblin's corpse was melting into a puddle of blood and sludge.

A sound came from the other end of the hallway.

Heavy footsteps.

Not one goblin.

Many.

Gerald looked at the ice wall. It was three inches thick now.

He looked at the footsteps, and he looked at his notebook.

He had one page left.

He didn't have a pen.

He had a problem.

And he had twenty-four hours to fix it.

Before the ice broke.

Before the rivals broke.

Before the System broke him.

Gerald Hutchins stood in the cold, holding a blank page, waiting for the door to open.

The page was blank.
