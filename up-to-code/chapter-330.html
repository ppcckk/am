Chapter 330: Condemn the Library

# Chapter 330

The gavel's resonance still hung in the air, a low hum that vibrated in Gerald's marrow. Guilty. The word had barely left the Judge's mouth when the replacement stepped forward, her heels clicking against the obsidian floor with metronomic precision.

She held out a folder. It was crisp, white, and stamped with the City of Ashford seal.

"Please sign for the transfer of authority," the woman said. Her name tag read *Inspector A.

Thorne*, and she did not blink.

"Your tenure has been terminated due to non-compliance with Statute 99-C: Improper Use of Regulatory Authority."

Gerald looked at the folder. Then he looked at the Prosecutor, who was already retreating into the shadows, his clipboard clutched to his chest like a shield. The Judge's blank eyes bore into Gerald's skull.

Kael stood frozen near the witness stand. The adventurer's hand hovered over his sword hilt, trembling. If he drew steel, it would be an assault on a court officer. If he did nothing, Gerald ceased to exist.

"Wait," Gerald said. His voice was dry, lacking the tremor of fear but heavy with the weight of thirty-one years of denied permits. "I require a recess."

The Judge paused. The gavel hovered, suspended by invisible threads.

"Objection," the Prosecutor called from the darkness. "The defendant is stalling."

"Procedural motion," Gerald corrected.

He turned to Inspector Thorne.

"You claim I am in violation of Statute 99-C.

I have not been served with the specific citation detailing the infractions under that statute.

Without the citation, the charge is vague. Vagueness is a structural defect in the legal framework of this hearing.

"

Thorne's smile did not waver, but her left eye twitched. A micro-spasm. A crack in the porcelain mask.

"The citation is attached to your file," she said, tapping the folder.

"Which is currently in your possession," Gerald said. "And therefore not in my possession.

Under Subsection 4 of the Municipal Due Process Protocol, evidence must be presented directly to the accused prior to adjudication.

You have hidden the evidence, which is an obstruction of justice. By me?

No. By you."

The Judge lowered the gavel. The air in the courtroom grew cold. The shadows behind the Judge rippled.

"Show me the citation," Gerald said. He did not raise his voice. He simply extended his hand, palm up. A universal gesture for *submit*.

Thorne hesitated. The System, bound by its own rigid logic, could not override a procedural demand for evidence when the evidence was physically held by the opposing party. The Judge slammed the gavel once, a sharp crack that split the silence.

"Present the evidence," the Judge commanded.

Thorne's fingers tightened around the folder. She opened it. A single sheet of parchment floated out, drifting toward Gerald.

He caught it. He did not look at the text immediately.

He looked at the margin, and the paper was thin, translucent. Backlighting from the courtroom lanterns revealed a watermark in the fiber.

*Draft.*

Gerald's lips thinned.

"This is a draft," he said. "It is not a certified citation.

It lacks the Inspector General's seal.

Therefore, it is inadmissible.

"

Thorne's face paled, the blood draining from her cheeks.

"It is a certified—"

"It is a draft," Gerald repeated. He tapped the paper with a fingernail.

"See the edge?

The serrated tear from the perforation line, and a certified citation is printed on continuous stock. This was torn from a bound ledger.

You are using an unapproved form, Inspector Thorne. That is a Class 2 Administrative Error.

"

The Prosecutor hissed.

"He is splitting hairs."

"I am splitting the hair of the law," Gerald said. He turned to the Judge. "I move to dismiss the charge of Endangerment of Public Infrastructure, as the primary evidence is procedurally void. I also move to hold the Prosecutor in contempt for introducing inadmissible evidence into a criminal proceeding."

The Judge stared at Gerald. The white orbs seemed to focus, drilling into the core of his being.

"Motion granted," the Judge said.

Thorne dropped the folder. The papers scattered across the floor.

Gerald picked up the draft citation. He read it quickly. *Violation: Unauthorized alteration of dungeon topology without environmental impact study.*

Gerald's heart skipped a beat. He had altered the topology.

He had widened the egress corridor in Sector 4 to accommodate the increased flow of adventurers.

He had done it without filing a Form 11-E, the Environmental Impact Study. He had been so focused on the immediate hazard of the collapsing ceiling that he had skipped the preliminary paperwork.

It was a violation. A real one.

But the charge was *Endangerment*. Not *Procedural Deficiency*.

"Your Honor," Gerald said, his voice steady.

"The charge is Endangerment. The evidence suggests Procedural Deficiency.

These are not synonymous.

One requires harm; the other requires paperwork. You cannot convict me of harm based on a lack of paperwork.

"

The Judge tilted its head. The shadows coiled tighter around the Prosecutor.

"Argument accepted," the Judge boomed. "Charge of Endangerment dismissed. Remaining charges: Fraudulent Permitting and Obstruction of Justice."

Thorne stepped forward, her composure shattered. She grabbed Gerald's arm. Her grip was cold, hard, like stone.

"You cannot win," she whispered. "The Inspector General is watching.

If you continue to litigate, you will be audited.

Not just this hearing.

Everything. Your entire core history.

Every violation. Every shortcut.

"

Gerald shook her hand off. He straightened his tie, a reflex born of habit than dignity.

"Let them audit," Gerald said. "I have nothing to hide.

My files are in order.

My citations are valid. And my egress routes are clear.

"

He turned to Kael.

"Kael. Sign the permit."

Kael blinked. "What?"

"The transfer permit," Gerald said, pointing to the scattered papers. "Sign it for Thorne."

Kael stared at him.

"You're losing?"

"I am conceding the jurisdictional dispute," Gerald said.

"If Thorne has a permit, she is technically the acting authority. If she is the authority, she can issue the Citation to the neighboring dungeon, *Gorgon's Pit*.

She has been trying to cite them for months but lacks the standing because their lair is technically 'wild terrain' outside municipal boundaries.

Thorne's permit covers wild terrain. Mine does not.

"

Kael looked at Thorne. Thorne looked at Kael, her expression hungry.

"If I sign," Kael said slowly, "they get the citation?"

"Yes," Gerald said. "And Gorgon's Pit loses its regeneration buff. Which means you can kill their boss in three hits instead of thirty."

Kael's eyes widened. He looked at the sword in his hand, then at the paper on the floor.

"It's a trade," Gerald said. "My existence for a tactical advantage. Do it."

Kael knelt. He picked up the pen that had fallen from Thorne's hand. He signed his name on the transfer line.

The System flashed red.

**TRANSFER INITIATED.**

A wave of nausea hit Gerald. It was not pain, exactly.

It was the sensation of being erased.

His vision blurred.

The courtroom dissolved into static.

Then, clarity.

Gerald was standing in his dungeon. The sub-basement of the Ashford Municipal Library. But the lights were flickering. The air smelled of ozone and burning plastic.

Thorne stood before him. She was no longer in a suit. She was armored, clad in plates of polished obsidian. She held a hammer in one hand and a stamp in the other.

"Welcome to the demotion," Thorne said. Her voice was no longer oily. It was metallic.

Gerald looked down at his hands. They were smaller. Translucent. He could see the veins in the concrete floor beneath him.

**STATUS: DEMOTED.** **AUTHORITY: LIMITED.** **PERMISSIONS: NONE.**

"I have revoked your ability to issue Citations," Thorne said. "You are now merely a Structure. You cannot act.

You cannot fight. You can only exist.

"

Gerald looked at his spawn list. The rat was gone. The mold spores were dying. The water leak had stopped, because the pumps had been shut off by the new authority.

"You can't just take my rights," Gerald said. "I appealed."

"You conceded," Thorne said. "Concession is finality. You signed. You lost."

She raised the hammer. She brought it down on a load-bearing column in Sector 1.

The column cracked. Dust rained down.

"Violation," Gerald said.

Thorne paused.

"What?"

"You are violating Structural Integrity Code 12," Gerald said.

His voice was faint, but it carried. The System recognized the observer, even if the observer had no power.

"You are damaging a load-bearing element without a permit.

This is a Class 1 Catastrophic Failure.

"

Thorne frowned. She looked at the crack. It was small. Barely a spiderweb.

"Who cares?" she said. "You have no authority. You cannot cite me."

"I don't need to cite you," Gerald said. "The System cites you automatically upon detection of a Class 1 violation. It doesn't require my input. It only requires my presence as a witness."

Thorne's face twisted. She swung the hammer again, harder.

The column shattered.

The floor beneath Gerald lurched. Sirens wailed, not from speakers, but from the walls.

**SYSTEM ALERT: CATASTROPHIC STRUCTURAL FAILURE DETECTED.** **AUDIT PROTOCOL ACTIVATED.**

The air shimmered. A door appeared in the wall, made of pure light. It was not a dungeon door. It was an office door.

Behind it stood a figure in gray robes. The Inspector General.

Thorne froze. Her hammer lowered.

"Gerald Hutchins," the Inspector General said. The voice was not loud, but it drowned out the sirens. "You have triggered a Code Black Audit."

Gerald tried to speak, but his throat was tight. He had no mouth, technically. He was a concept. A footprint.

"He set it up," Thorne shouted. "He provoked me!"

"Provoke or not," the Inspector General said, stepping through the door. The light dimmed around them. "A Class 1 failure is a Class 1 failure. The Core responsible is suspended."

Thorne looked at Gerald. For the first time, there was fear in her eyes. Not fear of death, but fear of irrelevance.

"Wait," Thorne said. "If I am suspended, who runs this dungeon?"

Gerald looked at the Inspector General. He looked at Thorne. He looked at the cracked column.

"I did," Gerald said. "But I am suspended."

"Then it belongs to the State," the Inspector General said. "Until the audit is complete."

"Who is the State?" Gerald asked.

The Inspector General turned to Gerald. The face was blank, like the Judge's, but the eyes were sharp, calculating.

"You," the Inspector General said.

Gerald blinked.

"Your concession was invalid," the Inspector General stated. "Kael is an adventurer.

Adventurers do not have municipal signing authority.

Therefore, the transfer permit was void ab initio. From the beginning. It never happened.

"

Thorne gasped. The air rushed back into Gerald's lungs. The opacity of his form solidified. The colors of the dungeon returned.

"However," the Inspector General continued, "your attempt to evade the audit by provoking a violation is noted. That is a strike against you."

"I was observing," Gerald said. "I was not provoking.

I was stating a fact.

The column cracked.

I identified the code section.

"

"A semantic distinction," the Inspector General said. "But a valid one. You are reinstated, Gerald Hutchins. Your authority is restored."

Thorne stepped back. She looked smaller now. Human.

"Then why call an Audit?" Gerald asked.

"Because," the Inspector General said, "while you were arguing, I scanned the surrounding grid. Do you know what I found?"

Gerald shook his head.

"Another core," the Inspector General said. "Hidden beneath the library's foundation. Not a dungeon.

A municipal facility. The City of Ashford has been storing mana-crystals in your sub-basement.

Unlicensed.

Uninsured. And leaking radiation.

"

Gerald felt a chill that had nothing to do with temperature. Mana-radiation. It caused spontaneous mutation. It caused dungeon cores to go feral.

"Who is the core?" Gerald asked.

"The City Council," the Inspector General said.

"They are the ones who hired Thorne. They are the ones who filed the motion to replace you.

They need a compliant core to hide the crystals.

You were too stubborn. Thorne was pliable.

"

Gerald looked at Thorne. She was weeping silently, tears of black ink running down her face.

"They will destroy you," the Inspector General said. "If they find out you know, they will erase you.

Completely.

Not a death.

An erasure. Your name will vanish from the System logs.

You will never have existed.

"

Gerald looked at his spawn list. The rat was still there.

The mold was still there.

His dungeon was small, dirty, and barely compliant. But it was his.

"What do I do?" Gerald asked.

The Inspector General stepped closer.

"You have a new option," the General said.

"You can file a Whistleblower Protection Claim. But it requires you to surrender your dungeon to the State for preservation.

You become a custodian, not an owner.

You lose your Citations. You lose your autonomy. But you live.

"

"Or?" Gerald asked.

"Or you cite the City Council," the Inspector General said. "You cite the politicians for illegal storage of hazardous materials.

It is a suicide mission, and they have the power to delete you.

But if you win... you gain total immunity from municipal replacement. You become a protected entity.

"

Gerald looked at the cracked column. He looked at Thorne, who was fading, her form becoming less real.

"I measure door widths," Gerald said.

The Inspector General waited.

"I check egress routes," Gerald continued. "I determine if a load-bearing wall is load-bearing."

He walked over to the cracked column. He placed his hand on it.

"I do not improvise," Gerald said. "I follow the code."

He opened his mouth and spoke to the air, to the System, to the invisible eyes of the world.

"I, Gerald Hutchins, Municipal Building Inspector, hereby issue a Citation to the City of Ashford, Department of Public Works, for violation of Code 77-A: Improper Storage of High-Density Mana Crystals in Residential Zoning."

The air screamed.

Thunder rolled through the sub-basement. The walls shook. The lights exploded.

In the darkness, Gerald heard a voice. Not the Inspector General. Not Thorne.

It was the voice of the City Mayor, echoing from miles away, carried by the ley lines.

*"You have no jurisdiction here, Hutchins."*

Gerald smiled. It was a small, dry smile.

"I have the permit," Gerald said.

He pulled a form from his pocket. It was old, dog-eared, and signed by the Mayor himself, ten years ago, approving the library's foundation.

And on the back, in tiny print, was a clause. A loophole. A building code he had memorized thirty years ago.

*Clause 14: In the event of hazardous material storage, the Inspector retains full authority to condemn the site immediately.*

He didn't need to win a fight. He didn't need to kill a dragon.

He just needed to condemn the building.

And if he condemned the library, he condemned the City Hall's secret stash.

The floor beneath him turned to sludge. The ceiling collapsed.

Gerald closed his eyes.

Next time, he would check the basement first.
