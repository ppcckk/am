Chapter 222: Ten Minutes to Comply

# Chapter 222

Darkness swallowed the room.

Total, absolute blackness.

The Emergency Stop had severed the connection to the grid. The hum of the ventilation fans died. The light strips flickered and vanished.

Silence followed, heavier than the dark.

Gerald lay on the concrete floor. His shoulder screamed.

The blood from the sword cut pooled warm against his cheek.

He tasted copper. He did not blink. His eyes adjusted, straining for any shift in luminosity, any shape against the void.

Nothing.

The adventurers had stopped moving.

He heard the scrape of a boot against tile. Then another. The leader's breathing changed, becoming shallow and rapid.

"Where is the light?" Her voice echoed, bouncing off the low ceiling. "Why is it dark?"

Gerald did not answer.

He counted seconds. One.

Two.

Three.

In the absence of power, the dungeon's automated compliance systems failed. The ventilation dampers closed.

The emergency lights failed to activate because their battery backup had been flagged as non-compliant for age and charge capacity.

Gerald had known this.

He had filed the deficiency report three days ago. He had not replaced the batteries.

He had not had the budget.

He had relied on the grid.

The grid was now dead.

"Get the torches!" someone shouted from the darkness.

Flames erupted. One, then three.

The adventurers held up glowing crystal staffs and oil-soaked rags.

The sudden light cast long, jagged shadows against the unfinished drywall. The leader stood over Gerald. Her face was twisted in confusion, not triumph.

The sword in her hand dripped onto his shirt.

"Kill him," she said. "Before he does something stupid."

"Wait," Gerald said. His voice was a rasp. "You are violating section 4.2."

The leader frowned. She looked down at him. "What?"

"Section 4.2," Gerald repeated. He struggled to push himself up.

His arms shook.

The pain in his shoulder was a white-hot spike. "Improper use of open flame in a confined, non-ventilated space. Carbon monoxide accumulation is imminent.

This is a life-safety violation."

The leader laughed. It was a harsh, barking sound. "You think we care about your little rules? You're bleeding out, old man."

Gerald ignored her. He looked past her sword. He looked at the ceiling.

The air in the sub-basement was still.

The fans had stopped.

The only oxygen source was the adventurers' lungs and the burning torches. The carbon monoxide would begin to accumulate.

The System monitored air quality. It had always monitored air quality.

But the System required power to enforce the penalty.

Unless.

Unless the violation itself triggered the audit.

Gerald closed his eyes. He focused on the Core.

His mind, usually cluttered with forms and regulations, cleared.

He reached out to the invisible network that bound the dungeon together. He could feel the structural integrity of the walls. He could feel the water pipes groaning under pressure.

He could feel the electrical grid, dead and silent.

He felt the adventurers' hearts beating. Fast. Strong.

He felt the air turning thick.

"Get him up," the leader commanded. "Drag him to the core chamber."

Two guards grabbed Gerald's arms. They lifted him.

His feet scraped the floor.

The pain made his vision swim. He kept his eyes closed. If he opened them, he might see the despair.

He could not afford despair. He needed to calculate.

The torches were close. Too close. The heat radiated onto his face.

"Move," the guard grunted.

Gerald let them drag him.

He let his head hang.

He let his body go limp. It was easier that way.

Fighting back would waste energy he did not have. Compliance was his weapon. He had to be perfect.

He had to be the problem.

They dragged him down the corridor. The floor was slick with his blood. The guards slipped once. They cursed.

"Watch your footing," Gerald muttered. "Slip hazard. No warning signs."

"Shut up," one guard said.

"Warning signs are required for wet surfaces," Gerald said. "Liability."

The leader kicked him in the ribs. "Stop talking."

Gerald stopped talking.

He focused on the sensation of the floor. The concrete was cold. The crack in the plaster near the entrance was still there.

He had noted it in his last inspection. *Structural integrity compromised by thermal expansion.*

Thermal expansion.

The torches. The heat. The dead ventilation.

The air was heating up. The temperature in the room was rising. The System tracked temperature.

*Alert: Ambient temperature exceeding safe operational limits.*

The message pinged in Gerald's mind. It was faint.

Weak.

The System was struggling to process the violation without power. But the violation existed. The violation was real.

The guards stopped. They had reached the central chamber.

The Core was embedded in the wall, a pulsing blue orb behind a pane of glass.

It was cracked.

The glass had been shattered earlier by the sword strike.

"Break the glass," the leader said. "Extract the core."

Gerald opened his eyes.

The blue light pulsed weakly. It was dying. Without power, the Core's regeneration cycle had halted. It was vulnerable.

The leader raised her sword.

Gerald looked at the torch held by the nearest guard. The flame flickered. A draft?

No. The ventilation was off.

The flame was consuming the oxygen in the immediate vicinity.

The air was toxic.

Gerald coughed. It was a dry, hacking cough. The guard next to him coughed too.

The leader waved her hand, clearing the air, but the smoke was getting thicker.

The torches were burning inefficiently. The lack of oxygen was causing incomplete combustion.

Carbon monoxide.

The System registered the toxicity level.

*ALERT: AIR QUALITY CRITICAL. VENTILATION FAILURE CONFIRMED.*

*COMPLIANCE RATING: 14%.*

*STATUS: CRITICAL VIOLATION.*

The message flashed in Gerald's mind.

It was brighter this time. Stronger. The violation was undeniable.

The evidence was in the air itself.

The leader stepped forward. She thrust the sword toward the Core.

Gerald saw the glint of steel. He saw the cracks in the glass. He saw the blue light of the Core dimming further.

He did not try to move his body. He could not.

He tried to move the System.

He pushed with his mind. Not with strength. With authority.

He was the Inspector.

The violation was clear. The hazard was present. The enforcement action was mandatory.

*ACTION: ENFORCEMENT PROTOCOL INITIATED.*

*TARGET: UNAUTHORIZED OPEN FLAME.*

*METHOD: SUPPRESSION.*

The sword stopped.

It stopped mid-air, an inch from the glass.

The leader's arm jerked.

She tried to push forward.

The air in front of the Core had changed. It was no longer just air.

It was thick. Viscous. Like walking into a wall of gelatin.

She grunted, straining. The muscles in her neck bulged.

"What is this?" she hissed.

The guard with the torch stumbled back. The flame sputtered.

The air around the Core had become dense with condensed moisture. The System, desperate to cool the overheated room and combat the fire hazard, had initiated an emergency cooling cycle. It had pulled moisture from the pipes, from the walls, from the air.

It had created a localized fog. A suffocating, freezing mist.

The torch went out.

The other torches flickered and died.

Total darkness returned.

But now it was wet. Cold. The mist clung to their skin. It filled their lungs.

The leader gasped. She dropped the sword. It clattered to the floor. She clawed at her throat.

"The air..." she wheezed. "I can't breathe."

Gerald lay on the floor. The mist washed over his face.

It was cold.

It was wet. It was life-saving.

He inhaled deeply. The moisture soothed his burned lungs. The pain in his shoulder dulled.

The guards were choking. They fell to their knees. Their armor clanked against the tiles.

The leader slumped against the wall.

Her eyes were wide, staring at the blue glow of the Core. The glow had brightened.

The cold mist was stabilizing the temperature.

The Core was recovering.

Gerald pushed himself up. His arms trembled. He stood. He swayed.

He looked at the leader. She was on her hands and knees, gasping.

"You have cited me," Gerald said. His voice was clearer now. The mist carried it well.

The leader tried to speak. A wet cough wracked her body.

"But you," Gerald said, pointing at the dead torches, "have cited yourselves. And the System has accepted the citation."

He walked toward the leader. Slowly. Steadily.

The air was clearing. The moisture was draining away, evaporating into the heat of the bodies.

The danger was passing, but the damage was done.

The adventurers were weakened.

Their stamina bars would be depleted. Their breathing would be impaired for hours.

Gerald picked up the leader's sword. It was heavy. He dragged it across the floor. The sound scraped against his nerves.

"Leave," he said.

The leader looked up. Her face was pale. Sweat beaded on her forehead.

She looked at Gerald. She looked at the sword in his hand.

She looked at the Core, now pulsing with a steady, healthy rhythm.

She spat on the floor.

"This isn't over," she wheezed.

Gerald leaned on the sword. He felt the weight of it. He felt the weight of the dungeon. He felt the weight of the thirty-one years.

"Check your exits," Gerald said. "They are blocked by debris from the ceiling collapse. I filed a report."

The leader froze.

Above them, a chunk of plaster fell. It hit the guard's helmet. Then another. The ceiling groaned.

The thermal shock had weakened the structure. The emergency cooling had contracted the metal supports. The load-bearing walls were stressed.

Gerald had not fixed the ceiling. He had only cited the ventilation. But the System punished all violations simultaneously.

The thermal stress was a secondary violation.

The structural instability was a tertiary violation.

The ceiling was coming down.

Gerald walked away. He did not run. He walked to the elevator. He pressed the button.

The elevator did not move. The power was still out.

He turned back. The leader was scrambling toward the stairs. Her guards were following.

Gerald watched them go. He watched the ceiling buckle.

He reached into his pocket. He pulled out his clipboard.

He wrote: *Incident Report: Structural Failure due to Unauthorized Thermal Manipulation. Responsibility lies with Adventurer Party 'The Blade of Justice'. Core Damage: Minimal. Civilian Casualties: Zero.*

He signed it.

He dropped the paper on the floor.

The ceiling crashed down. Dust billowed. The stairs were blocked.

The adventurers were trapped.

Gerald stood in the dust. He looked at the Core. It was safe. For now.

But he was alone. And he was out of power. And the Inspector General was coming.

A new alert flashed in his mind.

*AUDIT INITIATED. INSPECTOR GENERAL EN ROUTE.*

*TIME TO COMPLY: 10 MINUTES.*

Gerald looked at the rubble. He looked at the dead lights.

He had to rebuild. He had to fix the ventilation. He had to fix the ceiling.

In ten minutes.

He started running.
