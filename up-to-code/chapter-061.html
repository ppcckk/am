Chapter 61: Reclassified for Destruction

# Chapter 61

The clipboard did not match the wall.

Gerald had reclassified the east corridor as a public assembly space, which meant it needed a minimum of 44 inches clear width at all pinch points. He'd measured it at 39. He'd measured it twice more with the System's built-in calipers, which were worse than his old Stanley tape but at least didn't require him to hold his own end.

39 inches. Three short.

He'd built this dungeon with his own hands. Every corridor, every chamber, every handrail. He'd built it to code. The code he'd been working from was the one the System had provided in his initial Compliance Audit, the one that treated a dungeon as a "private recreational facility." Private recreational facilities had a minimum corridor width of 36 inches.

But Core 2847 had just reclassified his dungeon as a registered municipal inspection office, and a municipal inspection office was a public building. And public buildings, under every building code Gerald had ever read, required 44 inches minimum clear width at all accessible routes.

He had five minutes before the first of the forty-three Cores reached his boundary.

Gerald set the clipboard down. He walked to the east corridor.

He ran his hand along the wall where the handrail met the drywall.

He'd installed that handrail himself, at 34 inches, per ADA requirements for a residential facility. For a public building, it needed to be at 34 inches on one side and 28 inches on the other. A second handrail.

A second set of brackets.

A second set of anchors, and he did not have time for anchors.

He had time for something else.

Gerald pulled up the System interface. The new one. The one that had appeared the moment he'd typed ACCEPT, the one that looked like a municipal permitting portal but rendered in glowing blue text on the dungeon wall. He scrolled through the classification options.

Public building.

Yes. That was the default. But public buildings had sub-classifications.

He found them nested three levels deep in a menu he'd have to scroll through on his knees because the text was small and the System thought accessibility meant the interface itself was accessible than the building.

Public assembly. Public office. Public institutional. Public residential.

Public residential.

A municipal inspection office was not a public assembly space, which was not a public institutional space.

It was, functionally, a workspace. A place where one person performed administrative labor.

The closest real-world analogue was a home office.

Gerald selected Public Residential.

The corridor width requirement dropped to 36 inches, and he still had three inches to spare.

He exhaled. He picked up the clipboard.

He walked to the main chamber, where the forty-three names pulsed on the wall in red text, each one ticking down a proximity counter.

The closest was Core 4412, a mid-tier fire dungeon three hundred meters east. Its proximity counter read 00:04:17. Four minutes and seventeen seconds until its boundary overlapped with Gerald's.

Gerald had been expecting a fight. He'd been expecting spawn lists, combat rounds, the whole LitRPG apparatus he'd spent sixty chapters avoiding. What he'd gotten instead was a permitting portal and a reclassification menu.

He should have known better. The System was a bureaucracy. Bureaucracies didn't fight. They processed.

The permitting portal had a tab labeled "Enforcement Actions." Gerald clicked it.

Three options appeared.

Citation. He knew this one. It required physical presence at the target's boundary and applied stat debuffs proportional to violations found.

Stop Work Order.

New. He hadn't used this before.

The description was terse: "Halts all construction and spawning activity within the cited Core's jurisdiction for a period of 72 hours.

Requires service at the boundary. Target may appeal within 24 hours."

Seal. This one was underlined in red.

The description read: "Physically closes the cited Core's entrance to all entities.

Duration: indefinite.

Requires a signed permit denial form (Form 27-C) and proof that the target's entrance violates a specific code provision."

Seal was the nuclear option. It didn't debuff. It didn't slow.

It closed the door. But the requirement was brutal.

You had to prove a specific code violation at the entrance itself.

Not inside the dungeon. Not in the spawn list. The entrance.

The door.

The threshold.

Gerald checked his citation history.

Forty-three citations. None of them had included entrance-level violations. He'd been citing interior conditions because interior conditions were easier to measure.

Ventilation, egress, fire suppression. All interior.

All measurable from inside his own dungeon, and he had no entrance-level data on any of the forty-three.

The proximity counter on Core 4412 ticked down. 00:03:42.

Gerald walked to his own entrance.

The entrance to his dungeon was a standard library sub-basement door. Concrete floor.

Steel frame. A push bar. He'd installed it himself, to code, with a self-closing mechanism and a panic bar rated for a public occupancy load of fifty.

He looked at the door. He looked at the door frame. He looked at the threshold.

The threshold was 0.5 inches high.

A public building required a maximum threshold height of 0.5 inches. His threshold was exactly 0.5 inches. Compliant. Barely.

He turned to the wall. He pulled up Core 4412's public profile.

For any registered Core, the System supplied basic entrance data, mirroring how a property assessment database lists frontage measurements.

It was not detailed. It was not enough for a Seal, and it was enough for a Stop Work Order, which didn't require a specific violation.

It just required that construction was occurring.

And spawning was construction. Gerald had established that in Chapter 34, when he'd cited Core 1192 for "unpermitted structural additions" and the System had ruled that spawning an entity constituted a structural addition to the dungeon's biological infrastructure.

Gerald opened the Stop Work Order form. It was a page.

Target designation.

Violation category. Service method. Signature.

He filled it out. Core 4412. Violation: unpermitted biological construction. Service method: boundary delivery.

He signed it.

The System prompted: "Service requires physical presence at the target's boundary. Distance: 312 meters. Estimated walk time: 4 minutes."

He had three minutes and forty-two seconds.

Gerald walked, and he did not run. Running was not permitted in a public building. He'd installed a sign at the entrance that said exactly that, because he was Gerald Hutchins and he had been walking through municipal buildings for thirty-one years and he did not run.

He walked through his dungeon. He walked through the east corridor, which was 39 inches wide and technically compliant under Public Residential classification.

He walked through the main chamber, past the rat nest, which was now native fauna and therefore not a code violation.

He walked through the water leak, which was now resolved and therefore not a code violation.

He walked through the handrails he'd installed, the sprinklers he'd installed, the egress signage he'd installed.

He walked to the boundary.

Core 4412's boundary was a shimmer in the air, a translucent wall of heat that marked the edge of its jurisdiction. On the other side, Gerald could see fire. Actual fire. A corridor of magma channels that pulsed orange and black.

A goblin stood at the boundary. Not a scout. A warrior.

Heavy armor. A sword that glowed with heat enchantment.

It had been waiting for him.

Gerald held up the Stop Work Order.

"Service," he said.

The goblin's eyes went wide. It knew what that word meant in the System's language. It tried to step forward.

The boundary stopped it, and the boundary was Core 4412's own jurisdiction. The goblin was inside it.

And the Stop Work Order had been served at the boundary, which meant it had been served at the edge of Core 4412's jurisdiction, which meant it had been served on Core 4412.

The fire stopped.

Not the ambient fire. The spawning. Gerald watched the magma channels flicker.

New spawn points that had been forming along the corridor's edges simply stopped; the entities that had been mid-formation dissolved back into raw mana.

The dungeon's population count, visible on Gerald's clipboard, dropped from 217 to 189.

Twenty-eight spawns, frozen mid-construction.

The goblin looked at Gerald. Gerald looked at the goblin.

"Seventy-two hours," Gerald said. "You can appeal. Form 27-C. You have twenty-four hours to file."

He turned and walked back, and he made it to his main chamber with eleven seconds to spare. The next proximity counter, Core 3301, hit zero.

Core 3301's boundary overlapped his. Gerald felt it as a pressure change, a drop in air quality.

Core 3301 was a poison dungeon. Its atmosphere was toxic by design. And it was now touching his atmosphere.

Gerald's Compliance Rating flickered. 71. 70. 69, and the System was re-evaluating. His dungeon was now a public building. Public buildings had air quality standards. His air quality was about to fail.

He pulled up the permitting portal. He scrolled to the reclassification menu.

Public Residential.

He kept it. But he added a note: "Air handling systems under renovation. Temporary variance requested."

The System processed it.

A variance form appeared. Form 19-D.

Temporary Variance Request, and he had to fill it out; he had to specify the duration, the mitigation measures, and the compensating features.

Mitigation measures. He had sprinklers. He had ventilation fans he'd installed in Chapter 12. He had a sealed door at the boundary.

Compensating features. He had a sealed door at the boundary. That was it.

He filled out the form. He listed the sealed door.

He listed the ventilation fans, and he listed the sprinklers.

He requested a 48-hour variance.

The System approved it. His Compliance Rating stabilized at 69.

One point down. Because of a neighbor.

Gerald sat down. He looked at the clipboard. Forty-two names remaining.

Core 4412 was now grayed out, frozen for seventy-two hours. The others were still coming.

He opened the permitting portal again. He scrolled to the Enforcement Actions tab. He stared at the Seal option.

Form 27-C. Proof of entrance violation.

He needed entrance-level data, and he had none; he had a tool he hadn't used in a long time. He had a clipboard.

He had a tape measure.

And he had thirty-one years of knowing what a door frame looked like when it was wrong.

He could walk to each boundary. He could measure each entrance. He could build a case.

But he only had one body. And he could only be at one boundary at a time.

Gerald looked at the rat. The rat looked back. It was grooming its paw. It did not care about building codes.

Then the System pinged.

Not a proximity counter. Not a Compliance Audit. A new notification, rendered in red text that pulsed like a heartbeat.

COUNTER-CITATION FILED.

Gerald stared at it. He hadn't been cited. Not since Chapter 3. Not since he'd learned to cite others.

The counter-citation was from Core 0001.

Gerald didn't know what Core 0001 was.

It wasn't on his list of forty-three. It wasn't on any list. He'd seen the number once, in Chapter 22, in a footnote in the System's registration database.

Core 0001. The first Core.

The oldest Core, and the Core that had been registered before the current System framework existed.

The counter-citation was being served. Right now. In his main chamber.

Gerald looked up, and a figure stood in his dungeon. It was not a goblin.

It was not a warrior. It was a woman in a gray suit with a clipboard of her own. Her face had the standard blankness of System entities, yet her posture was distinct.

She stood with her weight on her left leg, right hand on her hip, clipboard held at chest height.

She looked like an inspector.

"Mr. Hutchins," she said. "I'm here to serve a citation."

Gerald looked at her clipboard. He looked at his own.

"Who are you?" he asked.

"I'm the thing that wrote the framework Core 2847 wrote the audit on," she said. "I'm the original. I'm the first Core. And you just modified my System without a permit."

She held out the citation.

Gerald looked at the violation category.

Unpermitted alteration of System infrastructure.

Penalty: permanent removal from Core status.

Gerald looked at the rat. The rat looked back. The rat had been in his dungeon since Chapter 1. The rat had never filed a permit.

The woman in the gray suit waited.

Gerald reached for his clipboard.
