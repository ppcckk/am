Chapter 152: Served by a Rat

# Chapter 152

The citation hung in the air, a fragile scrap of parchment pinned to a sheet of glacial ice that was rapidly expanding across the library's foundation.

The Frost Warden's voice vibrated through the concrete. It wasn't a sound so much as a pressure change, a sudden drop in barometric weight that made Gerald's ears pop.

Above them, the library groaned. Timber splintered. Glass shattered in display cases three floors up.

Gerald didn't look up. He looked at his clipboard.

The Paladin, Seraphina, had stopped shivering. She had stopped breathing too, her chest locked tight against the sudden drop in ambient temperature. Her armor plates were rimed with white.

"Inspector," she whispered. The word came out as a cloud of vapor. "The ceiling."

Gerald ignored the ceiling.

**[SYSTEM ALERT: CITATION SERVED.]** **[TARGET: THE FROST Warden (Tier 4 Dungeon Core).]** **[VIOLATION: UNPERMITTED THERMAL ALTERATION (CLASS B).]** **[PENALTY: STAT DEBUFF APPLIED. TARGET POWER REDUCED BY 15%.]**

The text pulsed in neon blue against the frost.

For a second, nothing happened.

Then the ice on the ceiling stopped growing.

It didn't melt. It didn't retreat. It just... stopped. The aggressive, predatory creep of the frost halted, leaving a jagged, crystalline fringe hanging over Gerald's head.

The voice from above returned, but the boom was gone. It sounded irritated. Petulant.

"You cannot cite the weather," the Frost Warden said. The voice was closer now. Directly above the ceiling tiles. "I am the weather."

Gerald uncapped his pen. He clicked it twice. The sound was obscenely loud in the silence.

"Section 4, Paragraph 12," Gerald said. His voice was steady.

Dry.

The voice of a man reading a parking ticket to a speeding motorist.

"All thermal manipulation within municipal limits requires a variance permit. You did not file one."

"I am a dungeon core," the Warden replied. "We do not file."

"Neither do rats," Gerald said. "But we cite them for carrying plague."

A crack echoed through the basement. Not from the ice. From the Warden.

Gerald felt a tug in his gut. The physical sensation of a legal challenge. The System recognized the dispute.

**[APPEAL INITIATED.]** **[HEARING SCHEDULED: IMMEDIATE.]** **[MODE: STRUCTURAL LOGIC.]**

The basement lights flickered and died.

Total darkness.

Then, blue light erupted from Gerald's chest. His Core. It pulsed, a rhythmic, sickly throb. The Paladin backed away, her sword drawn but pointing at the floor, useless against a concept.

Gerald closed his eyes.

When he opened them, the basement was gone, and he stood in a void. Pure white, infinite, and silent.

Opposite him stood the Frost Warden.

It wasn't a monster. It wasn't a giant ice golem.

It was a geometry.

A shifting, fractal lattice of sharp angles and cold blue light, towering thirty feet high. It had no face, only facets.

Gerald stood on a simple, flat gray platform. He held his clipboard.

"Welcome to the Hearing," Gerald said.

The lattice shimmered. **

"I am the cold. I am the end. You are a bug."**

"I am an inspector," Gerald corrected. "And you are non-compliant."

The lattice shifted, forming a spike aimed at Gerald's head. **

"Show me your authority."**

Gerald stepped forward. He didn't raise his hands. He raised his pen.

"Exhibit A," Gerald said. He pointed at the space between them. "The structural integrity of the Ashford Municipal Library."

The lattice paused. **

"Irrelevant."**

"Relevant," Gerald said. "You are modifying the load-bearing foundation.

That is a Class A violation.

But we are arguing Class B.

Unpermitted climate control.

"

Gerald walked around the lattice. He didn't move like a hero. He moved like a man walking a site perimeter, looking for cracks.

"Your thermal output is unregulated," Gerald continued. "You are dropping the temperature by four degrees per minute. That exceeds the variance allowed for historic zones by a factor of forty."

**"The cold is natural,"** the lattice boomed. **"It preserves."**

"It fractures," Gerald said.

Gerald stopped in front of the lattice. He looked up at a sharp, jagged protrusion.

"Look at that joint," Gerald said. He pointed.

The lattice didn't look. It couldn't look.

"I see a stress point," Gerald said. "Your thermal expansion is uneven. The core contracts faster than the outer shell. You are building a bomb out of ice."

The lattice flickered. **

"I am stable."**

"You are brittle," Gerald said. "And you are violating the Minimum Structural Integrity Standard for all subterranean entities. Section 9."

Gerald tapped his pen against his clipboard.

**[SYSTEM NOTICE: APPELLANT ASSERTING STRUCTURAL FAILURE.]**

The void shuddered.

The Frost Warden tried to expand. It tried to fill the white space with ice, to crush Gerald with the sheer weight of its domain.

But the System had rules. And Gerald had just introduced a new one.

"Objection," Gerald said.

The word hit the lattice like a physical blow.

**"You have no standing to object to my form,"** the Warden raged.

"I have standing because my dungeon is below you," Gerald said. "And if you break, I fall. That makes it a shared liability."

Gerald stepped closer. He was now inches from the fractal spikes. The cold burned his skin, numbing the nerves in his fingers.

He didn't flinch. He adjusted his tie.

"Rule 14-B: Mutual Structural Dependency," Gerald recited. "If a Core's actions threaten the physical stability of a registered neighbor's footprint, the neighbor may demand a stabilization hold."

The lattice froze.

**"What?"**

"I am demanding a hold," Gerald said. "Stop the expansion. Or I cite you for Endangerment, and the Inspector General takes your license."

The Warden vibrated. The sound was a low, subsonic hum that made Gerald's teeth ache.

**"You are bluffing."**

Gerald looked at the void. He looked at the System's interface, which was no longer a notification, but a gavel hovering in the white space.

"I am never bluffing," Gerald said. "I am reading the code."

He turned to the void.

"System. Enforce Rule 14-B."

The gavel slammed down.

It didn't make a sound. But the lattice screamed.

A crack appeared in the fractal structure. Then another.

The Frost Warden tried to pull back, to retract its heat-siphoning tendrils, but the System held it in place. It was being inspected. For the first time in its ancient existence, it was being inspected.

Gerald watched the cracks spread.

"Turn down the heat," Gerald said.

"Literally. Restore the ambient temperature to Code Standard 68 Degrees Fahrenheit. Within sixty seconds.

Or I file for a Cease and Desist.

"

The lattice shuddered. The blue light dimmed.

The cracks widened.

**"I... cannot,"** the Warden rasped. **"The cold... it is my essence."**

"Then you are defective," Gerald said. "And defective units are recalled."

Gerald raised his pen, and he wrote.

**[SECOND CITATION: DEFECTIVE CORE INFRASTRUCTURE.]** **[PENALTY: CORE STABILITY REDUCED BY 50%.]**

The void shattered.

Gerald was back in the basement.

The silence was absolute.

The ice on the ceiling was receding. Not melting. It was being pulled back, sucked up into the ceiling joists like a tide going out.

The library above was quiet. The screaming had stopped.

Gerald stood alone. The Paladin was gone.

She hadn't fled. She had been ejected. The System had cleared the area of non-essential personnel during the Hearing.

Gerald looked down at his clipboard. The second citation was signed. In blood. His blood.

He wiped his thumb on his suit jacket. The stain was dark, almost black.

He felt sick. Not from the cold.

From the adrenaline.

His stomach churned. He hated improvising. He hated the chaos of the Hearing.

It had gone too fast. He hadn't checked the load-bearing calculations properly.

He had relied on intuition, which was a fireable offense in his own mind.

He walked to his desk. He sat down.

He picked up a ruler. He measured the thickness of the ice still clinging to the window frame.

Three inches. Non-compliant.

He wrote it down.

A new notification pinged.

**[HEARING WON.]** **[REWARD: INSPECTOR'S LENS (Passive).]** **[EFFECT: You can now identify hidden structural flaws in enemy dungeons from a range of 50 meters.]**

Gerald stared at the text.

An eye.

A tool. He had wanted a hammer.

He had wanted to hit something. Instead, he got better at looking.

He stood up. He walked to the center of the room.

The rat was still there. Curled in a ball. Alive.

Gerald poked it with the end of his ruler.

"Get up," he said.

The rat squeaked. It uncurled. It looked at him with one black eye.

Gerald felt a pull. A connection.

He looked through the lens.

The air shimmered, and he could see it now. The flaws.

In the walls, faint red lines pulsed. Stress fractures. Caused by the previous battles. Caused by his own hasty renovations.

In the floor, a yellow patch glowed. Moisture intrusion.

And above him, through the ceiling, a web of blue lines. The Frost Warden's domain.

But the web was broken, and the Warden was weak. Debuffed.

And Gerald could see something else.

A door.

Not a physical door. A System construct.

It hovered in the air, visible only through the Lens. A glowing white rectangle in the upper right corner of the ceiling.

**[ACCESS POINT: THE FROST WARDEN'S VAULT.]**

Gerald froze, and he had never seen an Access Point before.

He walked toward it. The air grew colder with every step, but less aggressive. Curious.

He reached the wall beneath the glowing rectangle, and he placed his hand on the concrete.

It was warm.

Too warm.

Something was heating the wall from the other side.

Gerald pulled his hand back, and he looked at the notification.

**[NOTICE: NEARBY CORE DETECTED.]** **[NAME: THE PYRE.]** **[STATUS: AGITATED.]** **[DISTANCE: 12 METERS ABOVE.]**

Gerald looked up.

The Frost Warden wasn't the only one up there.

The fire was coming down, and he ran to his desk. He grabbed his Form 11-B. He grabbed his stamp.

He stamped the rat.

**[LICENSED.]**

He stamped the spider.

**[LICENSED.]**

He stamped himself.

**[ACTIVE.]**

Then he ran for the exit, and he didn't make it.

The floor beneath him split.

Not with ice.

With lava.

Bright, orange, molten rock bubbled up from the cracks in the foundation. The heat hit Gerald like a wall.

He didn't scream. He didn't run.

He looked at the lava, and he looked at the ceiling.

He looked at the two glowing lines in the air. Blue and Orange.

Two cores.

One cold. One hot.

And they were fighting.

Right above his head.

Gerald straightened his tie, and he pulled out his pen.

He didn't write a citation for trespass.

He didn't write a complaint.

He wrote a merger request.

**[PROPOSAL: JOINT VENTURE FOR THERMAL STABILIZATION.]**

The lava bubbled. The ice formed on the other side of the room, creeping in to meet the fire.

Steam hissed.

A new voice spoke. Not from above. From the lava.

Deep. Guttural. Smoky.

"**Burn,**" the voice said.

Gerald stepped forward. The heat singed his eyebrows.

"Burn?" Gerald asked.

"**Yes,**" the voice said. "**Burn the cold.**"

Gerald looked at the steam. It was obscuring his vision.

He looked at the System interface.

**[WARNING: CONFLICTING ENVIRONMENTS.]** **[ADVICE: EVACUATE.]**

Gerald ignored the advice, and he looked at the Frost Warden's broken web above.

He looked at the Pyre's boiling floor below.

He had two violations.

He had one opportunity.

Gerald raised his pen, and he didn't sign the merger.

He crossed it out.

He wrote:

**[CITATION: ILLEGAL COMBUSTION IN NON-INDUSTRIAL ZONE.]**

He threw it at the lava.

The lava swallowed the paper.

For a second, nothing.

Then the lava turned black.

It solidified. Instantly.

The Pyre had been frozen.

By the heat.

Gerald blinked.

The Frost Warden hadn't frozen the lava, and the System had.

**[VIOLATION: REACTION INSTABILITY.]** **[PENALTY: THERMAL SHOCK.]**

The floor shook.

Cracks spread across the entire basement.

Not just the floor. The walls.

Gerald looked at the ceiling, and the white rectangle was gone.

In its place was a red eye.

Huge.

Staring down.

**[INSPECTOR GENERAL NOTIFICATION.]** **[SUBJECT: GERALD HUTCHINS.]** **[STATUS: UNDER REVIEW FOR RECKLESS ENDANGERMENT.]**

Gerald's face went pale, and he had just frozen a Tier 4 Fire Core.

And the IG was watching.

The red eye blinked.

A message appeared on every wall in the basement.

**[YOU HAVE 10 MINUTES TO FILE YOUR DEFENSE OR BE LIQUIDATED.]**

Gerald looked at the rat.

The rat looked back.

It held a piece of burning paper in its mouth.

Gerald looked at the paper, which was his merger request.

The rat had eaten it.

Gerald stared at the small, confused creature.

"Did you just..." Gerald started.

The rat squeaked.

It dropped the charred paper.

It scurried into a crack in the wall.

Gerald was alone.

With ten minutes.

And a System that wanted to kill him for doing his job too well.

He had nine minutes and fifty seconds to survive.
