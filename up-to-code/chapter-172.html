Chapter 172: Violations Not Committed

# Chapter 172

The pen hovered. It did not tremble. It waited, suspended in the gray air of the void, waiting for a sentence that might cost Gerald Hutchins his existence.

Gerald looked at the bureaucrat. The figure was indistinct, a smudge of charcoal and ink, but the suit was sharp.

The tie was knotted with geometric precision.

Gerald knew suits. He knew how cheap fabric wrinkled at the elbows and how expensive fabric refused to fold. This one refused to fold.

"Explain yourself," the bureaucrat said again. The voice sounded like pages turning in an empty library.

Gerald adjusted his glasses.

They weren't there. He felt the phantom weight of them on his nose, the ghost of the bridge pressing into skin that no longer existed.

He straightened his spine.

It was a reflex, deeper than muscle, deeper than bone. It was the posture of a man who had stood in doorways for thirty-one years, waiting for someone to tell him the door width was incorrect.

"I did not act out of heroism," Gerald said.

The pen dipped. A drop of black ink fell, sizzling against the white floor of the inquisition.

"Core 412 was attempting to breach the foundation of the Ashford Municipal Library," Gerald continued. "Its spawn list contained three entities rated Class C for structural instability.

The breach would have compromised the load-bearing integrity of the sub-basement.

I issued a Citation for Unpermitted Excavation.

The penalty was applied automatically by the System. The resulting structural collapse eliminated the threat."

The bureaucrat blinked. Or the space where eyes should be shifted.

"You cite the code," the bureaucrat said. "You speak of permits and load-bearing walls. You do not speak of saving lives.

You do not speak of fear. You speak of *paperwork*."

"Paperwork is the architecture of order," Gerald replied.

"It is a cage," the bureaucrat countered.

"The System was not designed for cages, Mr. Hutchins. It was designed for chaos.

For growth.

For conflict. You have disrupted the ecosystem by treating a dungeon like a municipal ordinance.

You have made your dungeon... boring."

"Boring is safe," Gerald said.

"Boring is dead."

The chair beneath Gerald shifted. The white light around him dimmed, turning a sickly, bruised purple. The air grew heavy, smelling of ozone and rot.

This was not an argument anymore. This was a test.

**AUDIT PHASE 2: ADVERSARIAL SIMULATION.** **OBJECTIVE: SURVIVE 60 SECONDS.** **THREAT LEVEL: MAXIMUM.** **WARNING: CODE COMPLIANCE IS NOT A DEFENSE. IT IS THE SUBJECT OF SCRUTINY.**

Gerald looked up. The ceiling of the void tore open.

It was not a hole in the ceiling. It was a mouth.

Spikes of black obsidian rained down.

They were not random.

They were arranged in a spiral, a geometric pattern that mimicked the growth cycle of a crystal dungeon. Core 412's legacy.

The Inspector General had pulled data from the dead Core to build the simulation.

Gerald did not move. He did not dodge.

He raised his hand. Not to block. To point.

"Violation 104-A," Gerald shouted. "Improper drainage gradient."

The spikes hit him. Or they should have.

Instead, they curved.

The obsidian tips bent away from his hand, sliding off his invisible barrier like water off a duck's back. The System text flashed in his peripheral vision, not in gold, but in red.

**CITATION ISSUED: DRAINAGE FAILURE.** **EFFECT: KINETIC DEFLECTION (TEMPORARY).**

The bureaucrat leaned forward. "That is a loophole. You are using the code against the physics of the simulation."

"I am using the code," Gerald said. "Physics is secondary to compliance."

Another wave of spikes came.

This time, they were accompanied by fire. Blue, unnatural flames roared from the ceiling, licking toward Gerald's feet.

The heat was intense, blistering, but Gerald did not flinch.

He had built fire suppression systems in his own dungeon, even when it was just a concrete box. He had measured the sprinkler coverage. He had checked the water pressure.

He remembered the layout. He remembered the spacing.

"Violation 77-C," Gerald muttered. "Inadequate clearance between combustible materials and ignition sources."

He stomped his foot.

The floor beneath him, which was just light, flickered. For a microsecond, it became concrete. Then it became gravel. Then it became a fire-rated floor assembly with a one-hour fire-resistance rating.

The blue flames died. They sizzled out, starved of oxygen, suppressed by the sudden shift in atmospheric composition triggered by the citation.

The bureaucrat's pen stopped moving.

"You are adapting," the bureaucrat said. "You are interpreting the intent of the code, not just the letter."

Gerald breathed hard. His hands were shaking.

Not from fear.

From the exertion of forcing the System to recognize his narrative.

It required focus. It required him to believe that the rules were real, even when the reality was a lie.

"Intent is irrelevant," Gerald said, though his voice wavered. "The code is binary. Compliant or non-compliant. There is no gray area."

"Then let us see if you can handle a violation that has no code."

The void shifted again. The obsidian and fire vanished. In their place stood a figure.

It was not a monster. It was not a dungeon core avatar.

It was a man. Or the approximation of one.

He wore a tattered adventurer's tunic. He held a sword that glowed with a sickly green light. His eyes were hollow, empty of pupil, filled with swirling data streams.

**SUBJECT: HERO UNIT 001.** **STATUS: CORRUPTED.** **ORIGIN: THE BLACK MARKET OF THE SYSTEM.**

Gerald knew this type. He had read about them in the forums before the apocalypse. Adventurers who hacked their own stats.

Who bypassed the leveling caps. Who broke the rules to win.

The hero lunged.

Gerald had five seconds.

He had no spawn list. He had no minions. He had no dungeon.

He had only himself, and the memory of a building code.

The hero's sword swung. It was fast. Too fast. It cut through the air with a shriek that sounded like tearing metal.

Gerald did not dodge. He did not block.

He closed his eyes.

He thought about egress routes.

He thought about emergency exits.

He thought about the requirement that every space over 1,000 square feet must have two marked exits, and that those exits must be unobstructed at all times.

The hero's sword was an obstruction.

"Violation 12-B," Gerald whispered. "Obstruction of emergency egress path."

The sword stopped.

It did not hit a shield.

It did not hit armor. It hit a wall of pure, rigid law. The System enforced the citation.

The sword, representing an illegal blockage, was physically repelled.

It bounced off the invisible barrier with a clang that rang through the void.

The hero stumbled back. For the first time, the hollow eyes showed something like surprise.

**CITATION ACCEPTED.** **EFFECT: OBSTRUCTION REMOVED.** **COOLDOWN: 10 SECONDS.**

Gerald opened his eyes. The hero was regenerating, the green light pulsing faster. The simulation was not over. The Inspector General was watching.

"You cannot cite them for not having a permit," the bureaucrat said. "They have no authority to grant. They are beyond the code."

"They are within the range of my audit," Gerald said. "And they are unsafe."

He reached into his pocket.

He had no pocket. But he felt the weight of the citation pad. He pulled it out.

It was empty.

He looked at the hero. The hero looked at him.

Gerald realized then that the Inspector General was not trying to delete him.

The Inspector General was trying to break him.

To make him break the rules. To make him improvise.

To make him fight like a hero, not an inspector.

If Gerald fought with fists, he would fail.

If Gerald fought with code, he would survive.

But the code was failing. The hero was too fast. The citations were taking too long to process. The System's processing speed was lagging under the strain of the audit.

Gerald looked at the bureaucrat.

"I need a witness," Gerald said.

The bureaucrat paused. "A witness?"

"Per Section 4, Paragraph 9 of the Municipal Inspector's Handbook," Gerald said, his voice gaining strength. "A citation for immediate danger requires a third-party verification to bypass the standard appeal window.

If I can prove immediate danger, I can issue a Summary Abatement Order.

No hearing. No appeal. Immediate removal."

The bureaucrat's pen twitched.

"Summary Abatement," the bureaucrat said.

"That is a nuclear option, Mr. Hutchins.

It requires you to prove that the target poses an imminent threat to public safety.

And in this void, there is no public."

"There is you," Gerald said.

The void went silent.

The hero charged again. This time, the sword was covered in fire. This time, the hero was screaming, a digital roar of corrupted data.

Gerald stepped forward.

He did not run. He walked.

He walked toward the hero.

He walked until the sword was inches from his face.

He looked up at the bureaucrat.

"Inspector," Gerald said. "Look at this entity.

It is unregistered.

It is undocumented.

It is using fire without a permit. It is using steel without a safety inspection.

And it is standing in a space that has no egress."

He turned back to the hero.

"You are not a hero," Gerald said. "You are a violation."

He slammed his hand against the hero's chest.

Not a punch. A stamp.

A mental stamp.

**SUMMARY ABATEMENT ORDER ISSUED.** **REASON: IMMINENT PUBLIC HAZARD.** **ACTION: PURGE.**

The hero froze. The green light shattered. The body dissolved into code, into data, into nothingness.

The void cleared.

Gerald stood alone.

The bureaucrat sat across from him. The pen was still.

The white light returned.

Gerald waited for the text. He waited for the confirmation. He waited for the fine, or the reward, or the deletion.

The text appeared.

**AUDIT COMPLETE.** **RESULT: PASS.**

Gerald exhaled. His legs gave out. He sat back down.

**NOTE:** **YOUR METHOD OF DEFENSE IS... UNCONVENTIONAL.** **THE SYSTEM DOES NOT KNOW HOW TO CLASSIFY YOU.** **YOU ARE NOT A DUNGEON CORE. YOU ARE NOT A HERO. YOU ARE NOT A MONSTER.** **YOU ARE A REGULATORY ENFORCEMENT ENTITY.** **THIS STATUS IS TEMPORARY.** **BUT IT HAS OPENED A NEW PATH.**

The bureaucrat stood up. He buttoned his suit jacket.

"You have survived, Mr. Hutchins," the bureaucrat said.

"But you have drawn attention.

You have proven that Code can be weaponized. Other Cores will hear of this. They will fear you.

Or they will try to learn from you."

The bureaucrat smiled. It was a thin, cruel smile.

"And some of them are not interested in learning. They are interested in silencing you."

The void faded. The chair dissolved. The floor returned.

Gerald found himself sitting in the sub-basement of the Ashford Municipal Library.

The concrete was cold. The air smelled of damp and old paper.

The hole in the floor was gone.

But there was a note on the ground where the chair had been.

A single piece of paper, printed on heavy, cream-colored stock.

Gerald picked it up.

It was not a System notification. It was a physical letter.

**FROM:** THE BUREAU OF UNLICENSED REALITY **TO:** CORE 001 **SUBJECT:** NOTICE OF PRE-EMPTIVE CLOSURE

**DEAR MR. HUTCHINS,**

**WE HAVE REVIEWED YOUR RECENT ACTIVITY.** **YOUR METHODS ARE DISRUPTING THE NATURAL ORDER OF THE SYSTEM.** **YOU ARE CREATING A PRECEDENT THAT THREATENS THE ECONOMY OF ADVENTURER INCOME.** **IF YOU ISSUE ONE MORE CITATION, WE WILL BE FORCED TO RECLASSIFY YOUR DUNGEON.**

**RECLASSIFICATION MEANS: NO MORE CITATIONS.** **ONLY MONSTERS.**

**WE WILL PROVIDE YOU WITH THE BEST MONSTERS MONEY CAN BUY.** **YOU WILL NOT BE ABLE TO STOP THEM WITH PAPERWORK.**

**WELCOME TO THE DARK SIDE, INSPECTOR.**

**SIGNED,** **THE ARCHITECTS**

Gerald read the note twice.

His hands were still shaking.

He looked at the citation pad in his other hand.

It was full.

Not empty. Full.

Every page was filled with violations he hadn't committed yet. Future violations. Predicted violations.

And at the bottom of the last page, in handwriting that looked like his own, was a single line:

**THEY ARE COMING. AND THEY HAVE THE PERMITS.**
