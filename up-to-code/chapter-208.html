Chapter 208: Citation of the Night

# Chapter 208

The tape moved with a sound like Velcro tearing across a gymnasium floor.

Adventurer Kaelen stopped mid-stride. His sword, still dripping with the ichor of a recently dispatched slime, hovered uselessly in the air.

He watched the red figure unfold itself from the shadows of the corridor.

It was not a monster. It was a bureaucrat.

The creature adjusted its clipboard. The paper rustled with a dry, threatening snap.

Kaelen glanced at his party members. Elara, the rogue, had vanished into the shadows, but the tape figure did not seem to care about stealth. It cared about documentation. It cared about the violation.

"Is that... is that the Inspector?" Kaelen whispered.

"It's the Inspector General," the tape figure said. Its voice was not loud. It was resonant, vibrating through the stone floor and up into the soles of Kaelen's boots. "And you are trespassing in a Zone of Administrative Suspension."

Kaelen looked at the red counter floating above the hallway. *50,000.* The number pulsed like an infected wound.

"We didn't do anything!" Kaelen protested. "We just walked in!"

"Ignorance of the code is not a defense," the tape figure said.

It pointed a sticky, crimson limb at Kaelen's sword. "Unregistered magical weapon in a non-combat zone.

That's a Class 4 Hazard.

Your loot rights have been revoked."

Kaelen dropped his sword. It clattered against the stone, the sound echoing in the oppressive silence.

Behind the tape figure, Gerald Hutchins stood frozen.

The Inspector General had cited him. Personally. For interfering with a sealed evidence room. The penalty was immediate and absolute: a dungeon-wide suspension of all spawn generation and resource accumulation until the infraction was rectified.

Gerald looked at his own hands. They were shaking. Not from fear. From rage.

He had moved the Vault. He had solved the problem. He had followed the letter of the law regarding spatial displacement. He had not broken a rule; he had exploited a gap in the geometry.

But the Inspector General did not operate on geometry. It operated on jurisdiction.

The tape figure turned its head. It had no eyes, only two dark smudges where eyes should be, filled with the static of unfiled paperwork.

"Gerald Hutchins," the figure said. "You have one hour to vacate the premises.

Failure to comply will result in total demolition.

Not of the dungeon.

Of the core."

Gerald took a step forward. The floor cracked further under his weight.

"I did not interfere," Gerald said. His voice was flat, the tone he used when telling a contractor their permit was invalid. "I relocated a hazard. Section 12, Paragraph 4 allows for emergency mitigation of unstable structural elements."

"Section 12 applies to natural disasters," the Inspector General said. "The Architect was not a natural disaster. He was a suspect."

"He was a threat."

"He was evidence."

The red counter ticked down. *49,999.* Then *49,998.*

Each second of hesitation cost Gerald a fragment of his dungeon's integrity. The walls were beginning to dissolve into gray mist. The handrails he had installed so were turning to dust.

Kaelen watched, paralyzed. He had fought dragons. He had slain lichs. He had never fought a man who could kill him by pointing at a form.

Gerald closed his eyes. He reached into the System interface. Not the combat menu. The administrative panel.

He needed a loophole. He needed a precedent.

His mind raced through thirty-one years of municipal code. He remembered a case from 1994. The Ashford Water Department had attempted to demolish a historical marker for being "structurally unsound." The city council had ruled that historical significance constituted a protected status, overriding standard safety assessments if no immediate danger was present.

Was the Vault a historical marker?

No. It was a prison.

But was the Architect a prisoner?

The Inspector General shifted its weight. The sound of tape stretching was nauseating.

"Time is expiring," the figure said.

Gerald opened his interface. He navigated to the *Appeals* tab. Usually, this was for minor citations.

A misplaced exit sign. A blocked hallway.

But there was another option. Buried deep in the sub-menu of *Jurisdictional Disputes*.

**Option 7: Petition for Reclassification of Entity.**

If the Architect was not a suspect, but a *fixture*, then moving him was not interference. It was renovation.

Gerald clicked the option.

The System processed the request. The red counter froze.

*Processing...*

The tape figure paused. Its clipboard lowered slightly.

"That is not a valid claim," the figure said. "The Architect is a sentient Core."

"He is a fixture," Gerald said.

He spoke faster now, his voice rising in pitch, matching the cadence of a man delivering a closing argument. "He was embedded in the wall. He was load-bearing.

To remove him is to collapse the structure.

Therefore, he is part of the infrastructure."

"The System does not recognize sentient entities as infrastructure," the Inspector General said.

"Does the System recognize *infrastructure*?" Gerald asked.

The tape figure hesitated. It looked at the wall where the Vault had been. The stone was still there, solid and unmoved. The Vault was gone, but the space it had occupied remained as a void, a pocket of non-existence.

"You created a void," the figure said.

"I created a cavity," Gerald corrected. "A void is unpermitted. A cavity, for utility access, is permissible under Section 8, Paragraph 2, provided it is labeled and sealed."

Gerald raised his hand. He pointed at the empty space.

"I am labeling it," he said. "I am sealing it."

He pulled a form from his inventory. *Form 44-C: Designation of Non-Occupied Utility Space.*

He wrote. His pen moved across the paper with frantic speed. *Purpose: Storage of hazardous materials. Access: Restricted. Hazard Class: High.*

The Inspector General watched. The red counter did not move.

"If the Architect is gone," the figure said, "the cavity is empty. There is no hazard."

"The hazard is the instability caused by his removal," Gerald said. "I am mitigating that instability by designating the space as a sealed void. It is not evidence. It is a structural anomaly."

The tape figure tilted its head. The static in its eyes swirled.

"Show me the seal," it said.

Gerald walked toward the wall. The floor was disintegrating faster now, chunks of stone falling away into the abyss below. He stepped carefully, balancing on the remaining tiles.

He reached the spot where the Vault had been. He placed his hand on the cold stone.

*Seal,* he thought.

The System interface flashed.

**ERROR: Cannot seal a space containing a displaced entity.**

Gerald gritted his teeth. He couldn't lie to the System. It knew the Architect was there, somewhere in the depths.

But he could lie about *where*.

He pulled up the map of his dungeon. He highlighted a sector in the sub-basement, a small, unexcavated pocket of rock.

*Move the cavity there,* he commanded.

The System resisted. Moving a sealed void was a violation of conservation laws.

But Gerald was a dungeon core. He was the building. He could change the building.

He focused on the map. He visualized the walls shifting. He imagined the stone flowing like water, rearranging itself to accommodate the void.

It hurt. The pain was sharp, localized in his chest, as if he were being squeezed by giant hands.

The Inspector General stepped closer. The red tape reached out, touching Gerald's shoulder.

"Stop," the figure said. "This is unauthorized structural alteration."

"It is a repair," Gerald gasped. "The structure is compromised. I am repairing it."

"You are moving the evidence."

"I am moving the hole," Gerald said. "The evidence is irrelevant if the hole is contained."

He pushed the pain away. He poured his will into the map. The sector lit up red.

The floor beneath Gerald buckled. The wall in front of him rippled.

For a second, nothing happened.

Then, the stone groaned. It shifted. The empty space where the Vault had been closed up, becoming solid rock again.

And in the distance, deep in the dark, a new void opened. A black, circular hole in the earth, sealed with a glowing red rune.

Gerald fell to his knees. He vomited dust and bile onto the floor.

The Inspector General moved to the new location. It inspected the sealed void. It checked the seal. It checked the label.

*Non-Occupied Utility Space. Hazard Class: High.*

The tape figure stood before the sealed door. It tapped the stone with its clipboard.

The sound was hollow.

"Acceptable," the figure said.

The red counter above the hallway dropped. Not to zero. But to a manageable number.

*10,000.*

Still too high. But survivable.

Gerald wiped his mouth. He stood up. His legs felt like jelly.

"You are under probation," the Inspector General said. "No further relocation of sealed entities. No further citations of other Cores without prior authorization."

"Understood," Gerald said.

"You have one hour to submit a plan for rectifying the remaining violations. If you fail, I will return."

The tape figure dissolved. It melted into the shadows, leaving behind only the smell of adhesive and ozone.

Kaelen stepped forward. His face was pale.

"Did you just... argue with the government?"

"I argued with the code," Gerald said. He picked up his pen. It was bent.

"That's terrifying," Kaelen said. "More terrifying than the dragon."

"The dragon was simple," Gerald said. "It wanted to eat you. This wants to fine you into oblivion."

He looked at the counter. *10,000.*

He looked at his inventory. He had no more forms. No more loopholes.

He had saved his dungeon. But he had lost his freedom.

He was on probation.

And the debt was still there.

A notification popped up in his vision.

**New Violation Detected:** *Inadequate Lighting in Sector 4.*

**Penalty:** *Vision Reduction for All Spawns.*

Gerald sighed. He pulled out a fresh pad of paper.

"Kaelen," he said. "Do you have a flashlight?"

"No," Kaelen said. "Why?"

"I need to cite myself," Gerald said. "And I need to prove I didn't."

He started writing. The pen scratched against the paper, a harsh, rhythmic sound in the silent dungeon.

Outside, the sun was setting over Ashford. The library closed for the night. The city slept.

But underground, Gerald Hutchins was awake. And he was working late.

He had to find the light. He had to find the code. And he had to do it before the Inspector General returned.

He clicked his pen.

The next citation was already waiting.
