Chapter 123: Builder Destroys Proof

# Chapter 123

Margaret Vane kicked the rat aside with her polished black heel. She did not look at the rooftop gravel, the broken grate, or the small, trembling rat that clutched a stone tablet to its chest. She looked at the library entrance.

Margaret Vane adjusted her glasses. They were rimless, expensive, and likely bought from a vendor who understood the aesthetic of unyielding authority.

She checked her watch.

Then she checked the library hours posted on the glass door. Then she checked her watch again.

"Twelve minutes early," she muttered.

She opened her briefcase. Inside, there were no weapons. There were no potions. There was only a stack of parchment so thick it could have served as a shield against low-level projectile spells, and a quill that glowed with a faint, cold blue light.

Vane stepped over the threshold. The air in the lobby was stale, smelling of old paper and damp stone. Her nose wrinkled. She pulled a handkerchief from her pocket and pressed it to her mouth.

"Unauthorized entry," she said aloud. Her voice was flat, devoid of inflection. "Or is this a public access point? The signage is non-compliant."

She walked to the circulation desk. The librarian, a young man named Timothy who had been reading a fantasy novel behind the counter, looked up. He blinked.

"Can I help you?"

"Where is Gerald Hutchins?" Vane asked. She did not look at Timothy. She was looking at the floor, measuring the distance between the carpet edges and the baseboards.

"Gerald? The inspector? He's in the basement."

"Which basement?"

"The… the sub-basement. Under the fiction section."

Vane's eyes narrowed.

"Code requires all sub-basement access points to be clearly marked with illuminated egress signage. There is none here. This constitutes a Class 4 Visibility Violation."

She dropped a single, glowing citation onto the desk. It burned a small, precise hole through the wood.

"Lead the way," she said. "And try not to violate the occupancy limits while you do it."

Timothy swallowed hard. He pointed toward a narrow staircase hidden behind a bookshelf of travel guides. Vane followed, her heels clicking against the linoleum with the rhythmic precision of a metronome.

***

Gerald Hutchins lay on his back, staring at the blue sky.

His rat-body felt brittle. The bones were too thin, the muscles atrophied from the lack of proper nutrition and sunlight.

But the mind—the mind was sharp, which had to be.

The tablet was heavy. He dragged it across the gravel. *23 Hours, 58 Minutes.*

A chime echoed in the dungeon. Not a warning. A notification.

**NEW VISITOR DETECTED.** **CLASS: ADMINISTRATIVE.** **STATUS: UNINVITED.**

Gerald froze. His claws dug into the gravel.

He knew that status, and he had issued it to himself a hundred times. *Administrative.* It meant the System was watching.

And the Inspector General's representative was already in the building.

He couldn't stay on the roof. The exposure was a violation of Thermal Regulation Standards. He needed to get back down.

But the grate was broken. The ladder was inside.

He scurried toward the edge of the roof.

The drop to the alley below was twelve feet. For a human, it was a jump. For a rat in a failing body, it was a death sentence.

Or at least, a severe injury that would complicate the hearing.

He hesitated. The timer ticked down. *23 Hours, 57 Minutes.*

He could wait. He could hide. But hiding was a violation of Transparency Protocols. If he didn't appear for the summons, the default judgment would be immediate dissolution of the Core.

He jumped, and the fall was faster than he expected. The impact jarred his teeth.

He rolled, tucking the tablet against his side.

A rib cracked. He felt the hot flare of pain, then the dull ache of a System debuff: *Structural Integrity Compromised. -15% Agility.*

He scrambled up, limping toward the service door. It was locked.

Of course it was, and he tried to push it.

It didn't budge.

He looked at the hinges. They were rusted.

A new violation. *Improper Maintenance of Emergency Exit Hardware.*

He kicked the door. It groaned. The lock mechanism was old, municipal-grade steel.

He knew this lock. He had inspected it three times in the last decade.

He knew exactly where the tension points were, and he didn't kick it again. He jammed a claw into the gap between the door and the frame, finding the latch. He twisted.

It wasn't about force.

It was about geometry.

The latch clicked. The door swung open.

He slipped inside, into the darkness of the stairwell. The smell of damp concrete hit him. It was comforting. It was home.

But it was no longer safe.

At the bottom of the stairs, a figure stood. Margaret Vane was waiting. She held a clipboard. Her pen hovered over a blank line.

"Mr.

Hutchins," she said. She didn't look surprised. She looked bored.

"You're late."

"Technically," Gerald squeaked, his voice raspy, "I am exactly on time for the pre-hearing inspection. The summons specified the time of the hearing, not the arrival."

Vane looked down. Her eyes were ice.

"You are a rat."

"I am a Core," Gerald corrected. "The rat is the vessel. The vessel is currently experiencing a minor structural failure. I am filing a maintenance report."

"Cute." Vane stepped closer. "Let's get this over with. The Inspector General does not have time for bureaucratic theater. You are accused of engaging in Unsanctioned Structural Modification, Unauthorized Use of Force against a rival Core, and Filing of Frivolous Claims."

Gerald straightened his spine. The cracked rib twinged, but he ignored it.

"On what basis?"

"Tablet 4-A," Vane said. She held up a copy of his own evidence. "You claim The Gilded Labyrinth was expanding illegally. You claim you acted under Code 0.0."

"Correct."

"The problem, Mr. Hutchins, is that Code 0.0 does not exist."

Gerald stopped.

"What?"

"It is a myth," Vane said, her pen tapping against the clipboard.

"A legend told by junior Cores to comfort themselves in the dark.

There is no Code 0.0. There is only Code 1 through Code 999.

And you violated all of them.

"

Gerald felt a cold sweat break out over his fur. He had written that line.

He had cited it, and he had believed it. Had he made it up? Had the stress of the inspection caused him to hallucinate a legal loophole that didn't exist?

"No," Gerald said. "It's in the preamble."

Vane raised an eyebrow.

"Preamble? The System does not have a preamble.

It has rules.

Hard, numerical rules.

"

"The System has a hierarchy," Gerald said. He remembered. He had read it in the fine print of Chapter One, the moment he had registered. *Preservation of Life takes precedence over Preservation of Property.* It wasn't a code number. It was a foundational axiom.

Vane smiled. It was a thin, cruel smile.

"Ah.

So you are arguing for a moral interpretation of the law.

Dangerous territory, Mr. Hutchins.

The System does not do morality. It does compliance.

"

She turned and walked toward the elevator.

"Follow me.

The hearing is in the Board Room.

And try to keep up. Your agility debuff is going to make the stairs difficult.

"

Gerald limped after her.

His mind raced. If Code 0.0 wasn't a written rule, it was an implicit one.

And implicit rules were harder to enforce, and they required interpretation.

And interpretation was Gerald's domain.

***

The Board Room was a small, windowless chamber deep in the library's sub-basement. It smelled of ozone and stale coffee.

A long table dominated the space, surrounded by empty chairs.

At the head of the table sat a holographic projection of a giant, glowing eye.

The Inspector General.

Vane sat on the left. Gerald sat on the right. The rat-sized chair was comically small for him, but he had dragged it over. He placed the original Tablet 4-A on the table between them.

"Proceed," the eye boomed. Its voice vibrated in Gerald's teeth.

Vane stood. "The Core known as Gerald Hutchins is charged with three counts of Malfeasance. First, he issued a Citation to a Core of higher tier, causing significant economic damage to the local dungeon economy.

Second, he physically destroyed a load-bearing anchor in a shared foundation, risking the integrity of the municipal infrastructure. Third, he filed a self-exoneration request citing a non-existent code.

"

She gestured to the tablet.

"I move for immediate dissolution of the Core. His Compliance Rating is currently 42. He is a liability."

The eye blinked.

"Hutchins. Respond."

Gerald looked at the tablet. He looked at Vane. He looked at the eye.

"The accusation of Malfeasance is incorrect," Gerald said. His voice was steady. "It was a Compliance Enforcement Action."

"You attacked a Core," Vane said.

"I served a Citation," Gerald corrected.

"The damage to the anchor was a side effect of the Core's own non-compliant structure. I did not break it. The System did.

When I applied the Torque, the anchor failed because it was already fractured.

I merely revealed the violation.

"

Vane scoffed. "Semantics."

"No," Gerald said. "Jurisprudence."

He reached into his fur, and he pulled out a second item. Not a tablet. A small, folded piece of paper.

It was a printout of the System's Terms of Service, Section 4, Paragraph 12.

*"In cases of conflicting directives between structural preservation and administrative procedure, the Core shall prioritize the prevention of total collapse. Total collapse is defined as the irreversible loss of the dungeon's physical footprint."*

Gerald unfolded the paper.

"This is the definition of Code 0.0.

It's not a myth. It's an emergency override clause.

You don't number it because it's not a code you can choose to follow. It's the baseline for survival.

"

Vane's eyes widened slightly. She looked at the paper. She looked at the eye.

"The clause is ambiguous," Vane said. "It requires a threshold event."

"The anchor bolt was failing," Gerald said. "The Gilded Labyrinth was pouring concrete into a void that was already unstable.

If I had waited for a permit, the library above would have collapsed, which is a threshold event. Total collapse of the host structure.

"

The eye rotated. It scanned the paper. It scanned the dungeon map. It scanned the timestamp.

"The host structure," the eye said. "Is the library."

"Correct," Gerald said.

"And the Gilded Labyrinth was threatening the library."

"Correct."

"Then the action was justified," the eye said.

Vane slammed her hand on the table.

"Objection! The Core Hutchins did not attempt to negotiate.

He did not attempt to mediate, and he acted unilaterally. This sets a precedent for vigilante justice.

"

"Compliance is not justice," Gerald said. "It's order.

And order requires that the strongest foundation be preserved first.

The library is a Class A Public Facility, and the Gilded Labyrinth is a private, unregistered entity. The hierarchy is clear.

"

The eye paused. The hum of the room grew louder.

"Verdict," the eye said. "Case dismissed on the primary charge. However, the Core Hutchins is found guilty of Procedural Negligence for failing to file the emergency permit *after* the fact within the allotted time window."

Gerald felt a wave of relief wash over him, followed immediately by a sting of anger. He had filed it. He had filed it on the roof.

"The Core claims he filed on the roof," Vane said. "The System logs show no transmission until this moment."

"I was in transit," Gerald said. "The roof is a Class C Public Area. Transmission there is spotty."

"Excuse," the eye said. "That is a Class 2 Communication Failure. Penalty: 500 Core Points. And a temporary lock on your Citation authority for 48 hours."

Gerald slumped in his chair. 500 points. That was most of his recent gains. And a lock on Citations. He couldn't cite anyone for two days.

"Is that all?" Vane asked. She looked satisfied. She had stopped him, even if he hadn't been destroyed.

"No," Gerald said. He looked up at the eye. "I have a counter-claim."

Vane froze. "You cannot counter-claim against an Inspector General's representative."

"I'm not claiming against her," Gerald said. "I'm claiming against the System."

He pointed to the tablet.

"Tablet 4-A shows that The Gilded Labyrinth has been violating the Noise Ordinance for six months. They have not been cited.

Yet. Under the Equal Application of Code statute, if I am penalized for acting without a permit, the Core that violated the code without being cited should be penalized for the System's failure to enforce it.

"

The room went silent. The eye turned to Vane.

"Inspector Vane," the eye said. "Have you ever cited The Gilded Labyrinth for noise violations?"

Vane hesitated.

"They are… difficult to locate."

"Difficulty is not an exemption," the eye said. "If Hutchins can cite them, you should have. The System failed to monitor. This is a System Error."

Vane's face went pale.

"That… that is not possible. The monitoring algorithms are infallible."

"The algorithms missed it," Gerald said.

"Because they were looking for combat violations. They weren't looking for civil complaints. I filed a Form 7-C (Nuisance Complaint).

It was rejected by an automated filter because I wasn't a registered citizen.

But I *am* a Core. And Cores are citizens of the System.

"

The eye blinked. Once. Twice.

"Error logged," the eye said. "Re-evaluation of monitoring parameters required. Inspector Vane, you are hereby issued a Formal Reprimand for Negligence of Duty. You will undergo retraining."

Vane stood up. Her hands were shaking.

"This is absurd. The Core manipulated the logic."

"He used the Code," the eye said.

"That is his job. Dismissed. Both parties.

Next."

The hologram vanished. The room plunged into darkness.

Gerald sat there, breathing hard. He had won. He had survived.

But then, a new message appeared on his HUD.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** **MONITORING PARAMETERS UPDATED.** **NEW VIOLATION DETECTED: UNLICENSED CITATION AUTHORITY.** **SOURCE: GERALD HUTCHINS.** **REASON: CITATION ISSUED TO CORE OUTSIDE JURISDICTION (LIBRARY BOUNDARY).**

Gerald frowned. He hadn't cited them outside the boundary. The anchor was inside the library's foundation.

He looked at the map.

The Gilded Labyrinth's expansion had pushed its roots *under* the library's foundation, technically outside the physical building but within the municipal easement.

Gerald had cited them for the easement violation.

But the new parameter update meant the System now recognized *easements* as a distinct legal category from *buildings*.

And for easements, Citations required a *Land Surveyor's Seal*.

Gerald was not a Land Surveyor. He was a Building Inspector.

**STATUS: CITATION INVALIDATED.** **CORE PENALTY: 1000 CORE POINTS.** **COMPLIANCE RATING: 38/100.**

Gerald's heart stopped. He had lost more than he had gained. The reprimand for Vane didn't cancel his penalty. The System was retroactively applying the new rules.

He had been right. But he had been wrong about the classification.

Vane walked to the door. She paused.

"You're dead, Hutchins," she said quietly. "Your rating is dropping. If it hits 30, the System initiates a forced dissolution to protect the local ecosystem. You have less than forty-eight hours to raise your rating by twelve points."

She opened the door. Light flooded in.

"How?" Gerald asked.

Vane didn't look back.

"Build something. Something that can't be destroyed by a citation. Something that is code-perfect beyond doubt."

She left.

Gerald sat alone in the dark. His rating was 38. He needed 50.

He looked at his spawn list. Three mold spores. A water leak. A rat.

He couldn't build a new room in forty-eight hours. He couldn't recruit new spawns.

But he could change the code.

Not the System's code. His dungeon's code.

He looked at the mold spores. They were breeding. They were spreading.

*Biological Hazard.*

If he contained the mold, he could install a HEPA filtration system. That was an Electrical Code compliance project. It would grant him Lightning Resistance.

But it would cost resources he didn't have.

Unless, and he looked at the rat. The rat was carrying the tablet. The tablet was stone.

*Mineral Processing.*

He could grind the tablet into dust. Use it to line the walls. Create a fire-resistant barrier.

It would mean losing his evidence. His proof.

But if he didn't do it, he died.

Gerald looked at the rat. The rat looked back, confused.

Gerald raised his claw, and he didn't want to. His hands shook. He was a builder, not a destroyer.

But the timer was ticking. *47 Hours, 59 Minutes.*

He brought the claw down.
