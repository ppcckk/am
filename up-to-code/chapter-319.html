Chapter 319: Citation Served

# Chapter 319

The pen tip hovered over the air, trembling with the kinetic energy of a man who had not exercised since the Clinton administration.

The mechanical dragon froze. Its steel-jawed maw remained open, a vent of superheated plasma building in its throat, but the System interface between them had locked the action sequence. A translucent blue box, identical to the ones Gerald used to block unauthorized construction permits, hovered directly in front of the creature's snout.

**CITATION SERVED.** **Target: Proxy Unit 7-Alpha (Tier 4 Dungeon Core).** **Violation: Open Flame Discharge in Zone A (Residential/Classroom).** **Penalty: Immediate Thermal Lockout. Structural Integrity Debuff applied.**

The dragon's internal fans whined. Smoke poured from its nostrils, not because it was angry, but because its cooling system had just been forcibly disabled by a bureaucratic decree.

The beast crashed to the floor, its legs locking together as the System enforced the penalty, which was no longer a predator. It was a violation.

Gerald exhaled, a short, sharp puff of air that fogged in the sudden drop in ambient temperature caused by the thermal lockout. He clicked his pen. The sound was louder than the dragon's crash.

"You are in breach of Section 14, Subsection C," Gerald said, his voice echoing off the freshly painted drywall. "Uncontrolled combustion sources require a Class B fire extinguisher within fifteen feet. You do not have one. I checked."

He reached into his vest pocket and pulled out a crumpled piece of paper. It was the physical manifestation of the citation, a hard copy generated by the System's compliance engine. He tucked it under the dragon's claw. The creature was immobilized, its eyes dimming from a furious red to a confused, low-power amber.

"Wait here," Gerald said. "I need to process the appeal window."

The dragon did not wait. It tried to move. Its servo-motors sparked, grinding against the locked joints. A new message blinked rapidly in Gerald's peripheral vision.

**WARNING: TARGET ATTEMPTING UNAUTHORIZED MOVEMENT.** **ACTION: SECONDARY SANCTION REQUIRED.**

Gerald stared at the warning, and he hated secondary sanctions. They required more paperwork.

Specifically, they required a Form 11-C, *Request for Escalated Containment*, which he had never filled out because he had never needed to escalate beyond a simple citation before.

The last time he had dealt with a hostile entity, it had been a rat that he had shooed away with a broom, citing it for loitering in a non-zoning area.

This was a proxy for a Tier 4 Core. If he messed this up, the appeal hearing would be public. He would have to argue against a Core that likely had an AI legal assistant.

But Gerald had thirty-one years of experience arguing with developers who claimed their illegal basements were

"creative interpretations of zoning laws." He could handle an AI.

He tapped the air. The System interface expanded, filling his field of view with lines of code and regulatory text.

It was a mess.

The dungeon's internal network was struggling to maintain the thermal lockout on the dragon while simultaneously processing the citation.

The Compliance Rating, which had spiked to 100 moments ago, was already dipping. 98. 96, and the dragon was draining his resources just by existing in violation of them.

Gerald needed to isolate the threat. He couldn't let the dragon sit in the middle of the lobby. It was blocking the egress route.

He had inspected that route twice this morning. He had measured the width of the doorframe.

It was exactly thirty-two inches.

The dragon's wingspan was forty.

"Move," Gerald said.

The dragon ignored him.

"Move, or I will cite you for obstruction of a public thoroughfare. The fine is double."

The dragon's head tilted.

It processed the threat. It knew the fine was real.

It had seen what the citation had done to its mobility.

Slowly, painfully, it dragged itself backward toward the wall.

Gerald watched it go, his clipboard held ready. He wasn't afraid of the dragon.

He was afraid of the paperwork.

He needed to document the obstruction before he could remove the entity, otherwise the System would view his removal attempt as an act of aggression, triggering a combat protocol.

Gerald did not want a combat protocol. He wanted a compliance protocol.

A new notification popped up. Not from the System. From the dungeon network.

**Incoming Call: Core 442-B (The Obsidian Spire).** **Status: Unanswered.**

Gerald ignored it. He focused on the dragon. The proxy unit had stopped moving.

It lay on the floor, its eyes fixed on the ceiling. The smoke had stopped.

The fans had stopped, which was dead weight.

Gerald approached it.

He didn't touch it. He never touched unpermitted structures.

He leaned in, squinting at the serial number etched into the dragon's flank, which was small, barely visible under a layer of soot.

"Serial number seven-seven-omega," Gerald muttered. "That's a batch issue. They're prone to overheating. I noted that in the municipal database under 'Industrial Heat Sources, Non-Compliant.'"

He tapped the air again. **FILE REPORT: DEFECTIVE MANUFACTURING.**

The System hesitated. Usually, a dungeon core could not file a manufacturing defect report against another core's asset.

That was cross-domain interference.

But Gerald had found a loophole, and the dragon was currently *within* his jurisdiction. And it was *defective* because it violated his building codes.

**REPORT ACCEPTED.** **ACTION: ASSET RECALL INITIATED.**

The dragon's body shuddered. Blue light wrapped around it, not a magical barrier, but a containment field formed of pure regulatory force. The System was recalling the unit because it was unsafe to operate in a compliant environment.

Gerald stepped back. The dragon dissolved. It didn't explode.

It didn't leave loot. It simply ceased to be present in his dungeon, pulled back to its home core by a systemic mandate.

The lobby was empty.

Gerald looked at his clipboard, and the paper was blank. The System had absorbed the citation. His Compliance Rating stabilized at 94.

A small hit, but manageable.

He had survived the encounter without throwing a punch.

But the silence that followed was heavy.

The library's sub-basement hummed. The water leak he had fixed three chapters ago was silent. The mold spores, now licensed and contained in a sealed ventilation unit, were quiet. Even the rat, now serving as a designated pest control agent with a tiny ID badge, watched from the base of the handrail.

Gerald felt the weight of the victory, which was hollow. He had cited a dragon. A small, mechanical one, but a dragon nonetheless.

The System had validated his authority. And that authority came with a new problem.

By citing a proxy, he had identified the parent Core. The System had logged the interaction. The Obsidian Spire, Core 442-B, knew he was here. They knew he could hurt them.

And they would not send a small proxy next time.

A new message appeared.

This one was not blue, which was red. Bold.

Unmistakable.

**SYSTEM NOTICE: INSPECTOR GENERAL AUDIT SCHEDULED.** **TIME TO COMMENCE: 48 HOURS.**

Gerald's stomach dropped. An Inspector General audit was not a random check.

It was a deep-dive, and they would look at everything. Every spawn. Every wall.

Every line of code.

If they found even one loose screw, one non-compliant wiring junction, one unpermitted extension, they could revoke his Core Registration.

He had forty-eight hours to ensure his dungeon was perfect. Not just up to code. *Over* up to code. He needed to be so compliant that the System itself would hesitate to audit him.

He looked around the lobby.

It looked clean. It looked safe. But it wasn't enough.

The dragon had entered. That was a security breach.

The System would cite him for inadequate perimeter security, and he needed a security system that was also a compliance feature.

He tapped the air. **SEARCH: PERIMETER SECURITY OPTIONS.**

The list was long. Traps. Walls. Monsters.

*Traps:* Illegal in residential zones without a sign permit (Form 22-A). *Walls:* Already present. Compliant. *Monsters:* Require a Biological Hazard Permit (Form 9-C), which takes three weeks to process.

He didn't have three weeks. He had two days.

His eyes landed on an item at the bottom of the list. **Automatic Sprinkler System.**

He had one, which was installed. It was compliant with fire code.

But it was passive. It only activated when a fire was detected.

He needed an active deterrent.

He looked at the sprinkler head. Then he looked at the dragon's burn marks on the wall. The fire had been intense. Hot enough to scorch the paint but not ignite the drywall.

He had a theory.

Gerald walked to the utility closet. He opened the door.

Inside, the water pipes ran vertically, and he traced the line to the main valve. He grabbed a wrench. He didn't want to shut the water off.

He wanted to change the pressure.

He turned the valve. The pipe groaned. He increased the pressure to the maximum safe limit allowed by the municipal water code. Then he added a second pump, a small device he had scavenged from a broken vending machine in the library's lobby, wiring it into the system with a splice that he covered in a neat, labeled junction box.

He wasn't trying to flood the dungeon. He was trying to create a mist.

A fine, high-pressure mist that would fill the air. It would look like fog.

It would obscure vision.

It would cool the air, helping with the ventilation rating. And if a fire started, it would suppress it instantly.

But more importantly, it would be a *feature*, and he ran a test. He triggered the sprinklers manually.

A hiss filled the room.

A white cloud erupted from the nozzles.

The air grew cool and damp. The mold spores on the walls seemed to perk up, their spore counts dropping as the humidity balanced out.

**COMPLIANCE BONUS: ATMOSPHERIC CONTROL ESTABLISHED.** **VENTILATION RATING: +5.**

Gerald wiped his hands on his pants. It wasn't a weapon. It was a comfort measure.

That was the key. He wasn't building a dungeon to kill heroes.

He was building a dungeon that was so pleasant, so meticulously maintained, that the System would consider it a model citizen.

He sat at his desk. The desk was a repurposed reference table. He pulled out a fresh sheet of paper.

He wrote.

Not a citation. A letter.

To the Inspector General.

*Dear Inspector General,*

*Per your recent notice, I am submitting my pre-audit self-assessment. Attached is a comprehensive breakdown of my current compliance metrics, including a detailed log of the neutralization of a non-compliant proxy unit via administrative citation. I have attached the physical evidence (the burn marks, which I have preserved as a teaching tool for my spawn units regarding fire safety).*

*I request that the audit be conducted by a specialist in small-scale municipal compliance, as my dungeon's footprint aligns more closely with library sub-basements than traditional dungeon architecture.*

*Sincerely,* *Gerald Hutchins* *Core ID: 88-402-ASH* *Inspector, Class 4*

He signed it. He stamped it. He sent it.

The System accepted it. **PRE-AUDIT SUBMISSION RECEIVED.**

Gerald leaned back in his chair. He felt a flicker of hope. If he could frame his dungeon as a civic asset, the Inspector General might treat him differently. Less like a target, more like a colleague.

But then the air in the room changed.

The mist swirled. The lights flickered.

Not from a power surge. From an intrusion.

The air pressure dropped. The doors to the lobby blew inward. Not with force, but with precision.

The hinges had been cut.

Cleanly. By someone who knew exactly where the structural weaknesses were.

Gerald stood up. His clipboard was in his hand.

Standing in the doorway was not a monster. It was not a robot.

It was a man.

Dressed in a black suit, with a tie that matched the darkness of the dungeon's deepest corridors. He held a briefcase. He looked like a lawyer.

Or a auditor.

The man stepped inside, and he did not look at Gerald.

He looked at the walls. He touched the handrail.

He ran a finger along the baseboard.

"Good work on the drywall," the man said. His voice was smooth, devoid of accent. "Very clean. Very... sterile."

Gerald tightened his grip on the clipboard.

"Can I help you?"

The man turned. He smiled. It was not a nice smile. It was the smile of a man who had found a loophole you didn't know existed.

"Gerald Hutchins," the man said. "I'm not here to cite you."

He opened his briefcase. Inside was not a document. It was a small, glowing crystal. It pulsed with a rhythm that matched Gerald's heartbeat.

"I'm here to offer you a job."

Gerald blinked.

"A job? I am a Core."

"You are a Core who thinks like a bureaucrat," the man said. "And the System is full of criminals who think like criminals. We need someone who can read the fine print. Someone who can find the violation in a fireball."

The man stepped closer. He placed the crystal on the desk.

"The Inspector General isn't coming in forty-eight hours, Gerald. He was delayed. By me."

Gerald felt a cold sweat break out on his neck.

"That is illegal."

"It's not illegal," the man said. "It's an extension.

You applied for it, in your letter.

'Request that the audit be conducted...' You didn't specify the date. The System interprets open-ended requests as a request for negotiation.

"

The man leaned in.

"I can get you a permanent position.

On the Inspector General's staff. You won't have to manage your own dungeon.

You can audit others.

You can shut down entire kingdoms of monsters with a single form.

"

Gerald looked at the crystal. It was beautiful. It was power. It was the ultimate compliance tool.

"And if I refuse?" Gerald asked.

The man's smile widened.

"Then the audit happens tomorrow morning. And we both know you have at least three unpermitted electrical junctions in your east wing. I counted them when I walked in."

Gerald looked at the east wing. He had fixed those junctions. He had labeled them. He had used the correct gauge wire.

But had he filed the Form 11-E for *Electrical Maintenance Records*?

His mind raced. He remembered. He had fixed the wiring. He had not filed the maintenance log.

The man saw the realization in Gerald's eyes.

"Tick tock, Inspector," the man said. "You have until sunrise."

The man picked up his briefcase. He walked to the door. He paused.

"Oh, and Gerald? Don't try to cite me. I'm not a dungeon core. I'm a contractor."

He stepped out. The doors swung shut.

Gerald stood alone in the mist. The compliance rating on his vision blinked.

**WARNING: UNDOCUMENTED MAINTENANCE ACTIVITY DETECTED.** **PENALTY ACCRUAL: STARTING...**

Gerald looked at the clock. 11:42 PM, and he picked up his pen. His hand was shaking.

He hated this.

He hated the uncertainty.

He hated the improvisation.

But he knew the code. He knew the forms. And he knew that if he didn't file the maintenance log in the next six minutes, he would lose everything.

He wrote.
