Chapter 302: Jurisdiction Shift

# Chapter 302

The ceiling groaned. Not a settling sound, not a shift in the tectonic plates beneath Ashford, but a grinding shriek of metal on metal.

Gerald Hutchins looked up.

The support beams, thick as ancient oaks and forged from dark, pulsing iron, were bowing inward. They were not collapsing yet.

They were compressing, and the System was enforcing the consequence of his status change. He was no longer protected by the Code. The dungeon, now a wild and unregulated entity, was reacting to the absence of its Core's regulatory presence with structural decay.

Dust rained down. A fine, choking powder that tasted of copper and old blood.

Gerald did not scream.

Screaming wasted oxygen. Oxygen was a resource he could no longer mandate.

He stepped back, his heels clicking against the slick stone floor, and the sound was too loud. In a dungeon, noise attracted predators. In a municipal building, noise attracted inspectors.

He was not an inspector. He was prey.

A chunk of plaster fell from the archway, missing his left shoulder by inches. It shattered on the floor.

Gerald stared at the debris. He recognized the failure mode.

It was a load-bearing fracture caused by lateral pressure, and the ceiling was being pushed by something outside the room.

The Auditor hadn't just locked the door. He had turned the dungeon into a pressure cooker.

*Survive,* the notification had said.

Survival was a code violation if the exit route was blocked.

Gerald scanned the walls. The library's sub-basement had been his domain. He knew every inch of this 4,000-square-foot footprint. He knew where the water pipes ran, where the electrical conduits were buried, and where the air vents were located.

The main door was gone. The Auditor had taken it.

The secondary egress was in the corner, behind a collapsed shelving unit that had once held the city's zoning archives. It was sealed by a pile of rubble that looked suspiciously organized.

Gerald walked toward it. His legs felt heavy. The air in the room was thinning.

The ventilation system, once compliant with HVAC standard 101-A, had been severed when his status changed. The System was suffocating him to force a panic response.

Panic was inefficient, and he reached the rubble.

He ran his hand along the jagged edge of a broken beam. He didn't pull. He didn't lift.

He looked at the joint where the beam met the floor, which was a standard mortise-and-tenon. Or it should have been. In a dungeon, the materials were often warped by mana. This joint was fused by a hard, resinous substance.

*Goblin glue.*

A low chittering sound echoed from the shadows beyond the rubble.

Gerald froze. He didn't look at the sound.

He looked at the glue, which was yellowish, viscous, and smelled of sulfur. It was a binding agent used by low-level dungeon cores to reinforce traps. It was also a Class 3 Fire Hazard.

The System had removed his protection, but it had not removed his knowledge.

He reached into his pocket. His fingers brushed against a small, folded square of paper.

He had pulled it out of his breast pocket just before the Auditor's pen had touched his forehead. A single sheet. A duplicate of a Form 11-C: *Emergency Structural Remediation Permit*.

It was blank. Unfilled. Invalid.

But it was paper.

And paper was fuel.

The chittering grew louder. A shape emerged from the dust. A goblin, small and wiry, with eyes that glowed a faint, toxic green. It held a rusty dagger in one hand and a bucket of the same yellow glue in the other.

It saw Gerald. It did not see a Core. It saw a man. A soft, fleshy thing without the shimmering aura of mana that usually protected the dungeon's heart.

The goblin grinned. It showed too many teeth.

Gerald stepped back. He kept his eyes on the joint.

He calculated the tensile strength of the glue against the force of gravity.

The rubble was heavy, but the joint was the weak point.

If he could break the joint, the rubble would slide. If the rubble slid, the egress would open.

If he could ignite the glue, the heat would expand the air, breaking the seal further. But fire in a confined space was suicide. Unless the space was vented.

The air vents were gone. The ducts were blocked by the Auditor's magic.

But the electrical conduits were still live. The System had not stripped the wiring; it had only stripped Gerald's authority to regulate it. The copper was still there, humming with residual mana.

Gerald looked at the conduit box on the wall. It was open. The cover had been ripped off during the audit. The wires inside were exposed, frayed, and sparking slightly.

The goblin took a step forward. It raised the bucket.

"Hey!" Gerald shouted. The word was sharp, cutting through the dust.

The goblin paused. It tilted its head. It was confused.

Dungeon creatures expected fear. They expected begging.

They did not expect authoritative volume control.

Gerald pointed at the conduit box.

"That is a Class A electrical hazard. You are violating Section 402.1 of the Residential Electrical Code."

The goblin blinked. It looked at the box, then back at Gerald. It did not understand the words, but it understood the tone. It was the tone of a man who was about to write a detailed report.

The goblin hesitated.

Gerald used the hesitation. He grabbed a loose fragment of the plaster dust, a chunk that was still warm, and threw it at the goblin's face.

It wasn't an attack. It was a distraction.

The goblin flinched, swatting at the dust. Its hand went to its eyes.

Gerald lunged. Not at the goblin. At the conduit box.

He didn't have a tool. He didn't have a wire cutter. He had his hands.

He grabbed the exposed copper wire. The current bit into his palm, a bright, white-hot shock that traveled up his arm and stopped his heart for a fraction of a second. He gritted his teeth.

The pain was specific, which was measurable. It was data.

He pulled. The wire snapped.

A spark showered down. It landed on the yellow glue.

The glue didn't catch fire. It hissed. It bubbled. A thick, black smoke rose.

The goblin shrieked. The smoke was acrid, burning its sensitive lungs. It dropped the bucket. It clawed at its throat, stumbling backward into the shadows.

The smoke rose toward the ceiling, and the ceiling was still compressing. The metal beams were screaming.

Gerald looked at the smoke. He looked at the ceiling.

He had created a hazard. He had violated the fire code. He had exposed himself to toxic fumes.

He had done it on purpose.

The System notification didn't come. There was no citation. There was no fine.

Because he was no longer subject to the Code.

The smoke hit the compressed air of the room. The pressure built. The yellow glue, heated by the spark and the chemical reaction, softened. The structural integrity of the rubble pile compromised.

The goblin, blinded and choking, lunged at Gerald again. It swung the dagger.

Gerald sidestepped. It was a clumsy move, unpracticed, desperate. The dagger slashed across his forearm. A line of blood welled up, bright and red.

Pain. Real, biological pain.

He ignored it. He kicked the base of the rubble pile. Not the glue. The stone beneath it.

The glue was soft now. The stone shifted. The rubble groaned.

The goblin recovered its sight. It saw the movement. It saw the gap opening. It screamed a war cry and charged.

Gerald didn't run. He grabbed a heavy, iron bracket from the floor and swung it at the goblin's leg.

*Crack.*

The goblin went down, howling. Its leg was broken. It crawled backward, dragging its dagger, trying to escape the smoke.

Gerald turned back to the rubble, and he pushed. His muscles burned. His lungs burned.

The air was thick with sulfur and soot.

The rubble shifted. A gap opened. Just enough for a man to squeeze through.

Beyond the gap was the hallway. The hallway was dark. The hallway was unsafe.

But it was outside.

Gerald squeezed through. The stone edges scraped his back, tearing his shirt, abrading his skin. He didn't care. He tumbled out onto the dusty floor of the library's sub-basement corridor.

He lay there for a moment, gasping. The air here was stale, but breathable.

He rolled over. He looked back at the gap. The goblin was trying to squeeze through. Its green eyes glowed in the darkness.

Gerald picked up a piece of rubble. A sharp, jagged stone.

He didn't throw it. He jammed it into the gap, wedging it against the shifting masonry. It acted as a doorstop. The rubble pile stabilized, sealing the opening.

The goblin slammed against the stone, shrieking in frustration.

Gerald stood up. He wiped the blood from his arm on his pants. His hands were shaking. His heart was hammering against his ribs like a trapped bird.

He looked down the hallway, which was long.

It was dark. There were other doors.

Other dungeons. Other dangers.

He was alive, and he was uninjured, mostly.

He was alone.

And he had no authority.

He walked. He didn't run.

Running drew attention, and he walked with the measured pace of a man checking a meter. His footsteps echoed in the silence.

He needed to get out of the sub-basement. He needed to get to the surface. He needed to get to the City Hall.

If he was fired, he was a citizen. Citizens had rights. Citizens had access to public records. Citizens could file complaints with the State Board of Occupational Safety.

The System couldn't audit a civilian. But it could audit a business.

Gerald's dungeon was a business. It was a registered entity in the municipal database. If he could prove that the Auditor's actions constituted an illegal seizure of private property and an act of terrorism against a municipal asset, he could appeal to the human government.

It was a long shot. It was bureaucratic. It was the only option he had.

He reached the stairwell. The door was locked.

He kicked it. It didn't budge.

He looked at the lock. It was a standard mortise lock. Easy to pick.

He didn't have a pick, and he looked at his pocket. The blank Form 11-C was still there.

He unfolded it. He looked at the corner of the paper. It was stiff.

He tore the corner off. It was a sharp, triangular flap of cardstock.

He jammed it into the keyhole. He twisted.

The lock clicked.

Gerald stared at the door, which was a trick. A cheap, hacky trick.

It was not code-compliant.

It was improvised.

It worked.

He pushed the door open.

The stairs led up to the street level. The exit door was visible at the top.

But between him and the exit, the hallway had changed, and the walls were gone. The floor was gone.

In their place was a chasm. A deep, dark void that seemed to go down forever. The bridge that had spanned it was made of floating stones, each one hovering in mid-air, disconnected from the ground.

The System was testing him. It was asking if he could adapt. If he could improvise.

Gerald looked at the floating stones. They were spaced four feet apart.

He could jump four feet.

He could not jump four feet while wearing wet, bloody shoes and carrying the weight of thirty-one years of anxiety.

A voice echoed from the chasm.

"Mr. Hutchins."

It wasn't the Auditor. It was a different voice.

Smooth.

Polite.

Dangerous.

A figure rose from the darkness below. A woman. She wore a suit that was too sharp, too black. Her eyes were the color of ice.

"You are out of your jurisdiction," the woman said.

Gerald looked at her.

"Who are you?"

"I am the Liaison," she said. "The System's representative to the non-Core population."

She stepped onto the first floating stone. It didn't wobble. It held her weight.

"You broke the rules," she said. "You used improvised tools. You ignored safety protocols. You endangered a sentient entity."

She pointed at the sealed gap behind him. The goblin was still pounding on the stone.

"That goblin has rights," the Liaison said. "You hurt it."

Gerald looked at his bleeding arm. He looked at the goblin's shadow.

"It was attacking me," Gerald said. His voice was flat.

"Self-defense is a defense," the Liaison said. "But you didn't retreat. You escalated.

You created a fire hazard. You violated the chemical storage regulations.

"

She smiled. It was a cold, professional smile.

"I am here to offer you a settlement."

Gerald frowned.

"A settlement?"

"Reinstatement," the Liaison said. "You will regain your status as Core. You will regain your protection. You will regain your authority."

"And the cost?"

"You will accept the Auditor's citation," she said.

"You will admit to incompetence. You will submit to a permanent audit. Your dungeon will be remodeled.

You will lose control of the spawn list.

You will become a manager, not a ruler.

"

Gerald looked at the chasm. He looked at the Liaison.

"And if I refuse?"

"Then you remain a civilian," the Liaison said.

"And I leave you here. The goblin will get out eventually. The ceiling will come down.

The air will run out.

"

Gerald looked at the blank Form 11-C in his hand, and he looked at the Liaison.

"I don't have the authority to sign," Gerald said.

The Liaison raised an eyebrow.

"You are a Core. You have all authority."

"I am a civilian," Gerald said.

"I am not in the system.

I cannot sign a contract. I cannot accept a citation.

I cannot be audited.

"

The Liaison's smile faltered.

"That is a technicality," she said.

"It is a code," Gerald said.

He turned away from the chasm. He walked back toward the sealed door.

The Liaison watched him. She did not move.

"You are making a mistake," she said.

Gerald stopped. He looked back at the sealed gap. The goblin was still pounding.

"I am making a choice," Gerald said.

He pulled a pen from his pocket. It wasn't the Auditor's pen. It was his own. A cheap, ballpoint pen he had used for thirty-one years.

He unfolded the blank Form 11-C, and he wrote at the bottom.

*REJECTED.*

*APPEAL FILED.*

*GROUNDS: IMPROPER SERVICE OF PROCESS.*

He handed the paper to the Liaison, and she took it. She looked at it. Her eyes narrowed.

The paper glowed.

The System accepted it.

The Liaison looked up, her face pale.

"You just appealed to the Human Court," she whispered.

"I appealed to the rules," Gerald said.

The floor beneath the Liaison shook. The floating stones cracked.

The Liaison vanished.

Gerald stood alone in the dark.

The pounding on the door stopped.

Silence.

Then, a new sound.

The sound of a stamp. Heavy. Final.

**SYSTEM NOTIFICATION:** **APPEAL ACCEPTED.** **CASE MOVED TO JURISDICTION: HUMAN LEGAL SYSTEM.** **COURT SESSION: 09:00, TOMORROW.** **DEFENDANT: GERALD HUTCHINS.** **PROSECUTION: THE SYSTEM.**

Gerald looked at the notification, and he looked at the chasm. The stones were falling.

He ran.
