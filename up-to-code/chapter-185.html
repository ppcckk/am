Chapter 185: Compliant Lie

# Chapter 185

The Void was not empty. It was cluttered.

Gerald Hutchins landed on a surface that felt like polished obsidian, cold and slick against his knees. He did not gasp. He did not scream. He adjusted his glasses, which had survived the transition through the collapse of local physics, and straightened his tie.

The air tasted of ozone and old paper.

Around him, the darkness churned with shapes that defied Euclidean geometry. Pillars of light twisted into knots.

Stairs spiraled into infinity, leading nowhere.

But Gerald ignored the surrealism. He looked down at his clipboard. The paper was still crisp.

The pen still worked.

Across the vast, shifting expanse, a figure stood waiting.

It was not a monster. It was a bureaucrat.

The Inspector General's representative wore a suit made of shifting gray static. Its face was a smooth, featureless oval, except for two glowing points where eyes should be. It held a gavel carved from a shard of a collapsed star.

"Gerald Hutchins," the entity said. The voice did not come from a mouth.

It vibrated directly into Gerald's auditory cortex.

"You have been served with a citation for Custodial Negligence of a Class-4 Anomaly. How do you plead?"

Gerald checked his watch. 9:00 AM. On the dot.

"Not guilty," Gerald said. His voice was steady, though his hands shook slightly.

He pressed the pen against the clipboard to stop the trembling.

"The object in question was impounded pursuant to Section 9, Subsection C of the Inter-Dimensional Asset Management Protocol.

I have chain-of-custody documentation ready for your review."

The static-suit tilted its head. "Chain of custody is irrelevant if the asset is unstable. The Void Hearing is not for legal argument.

It is for containment assessment. If the asset cannot be contained, it is erased."

Gerald looked at the ball in his hand.

The *Anomaly*. It was a sphere of swirling chaos, no larger than a tennis ball, but it pulled at his vision. It looked like a mistake in the code of reality.

A glitch.

"Containment," Gerald said. "You mean destruction."

"Efficiency," the entity corrected. "The asset is a recursive paradox loop. It threatens the structural integrity of the dungeon grid. Why did you not report it to the central server?"

"Because I am a municipal inspector, not a server admin," Gerald said. He flipped his clipboard open.

"I found the anomaly in a residential zone.

It was leaking mana into a load-bearing wall. I fixed the leak. I quarantined the source.

I did not report it because the reporting form requires a Level 5 clearance, which I do not have."

The entity's eyes narrowed. The glowing points dimmed.

"Then you have created a shadow file," the entity said. "That is a violation of Transparency Code 12. The penalty is immediate erasure of the Core."

Gerald felt a cold knot tighten in his stomach. This was the trap. The System loved traps. It loved them because they generated drama, and drama generated energy.

But Gerald did not play drama. He played procedure.

"Show me the Transparency Code," Gerald said.

The entity paused. The static around its suit flickered. "The law is absolute. All anomalies must be logged."

"Not under the Municipal Exemption Clause," Gerald said. He pointed to a paragraph on his clipboard. He had written it in the margins during the transport, using a shorthand that only he understood. "Paragraph 4, Subsection B: *Local infrastructure threats that are resolved within the jurisdiction of a registered Core prior to escalation do not require central logging, provided the Core maintains a physical record of the resolution.*"

The entity stared at the clipboard. The text glowed faintly gold.

"That is an obscure regulation," the entity said. "It applies only to non-magical infrastructure failures."

"This is a magical infrastructure failure," Gerald said.

"The mana leak damaged the structural integrity of the dungeon walls.

That is infrastructure. Therefore, the exemption applies.

I have the repair logs. I have the materials requisition forms. I have the receipt for the sealant."

He held up a small, crumpled piece of paper. A hardware store receipt. *Two gallons of epoxy, one can of spray foam.*

The entity looked at the receipt. It looked at the Anomaly. It looked at Gerald.

"The Anomaly is still active," the entity said. "Your logs show a repair, not a containment."

"Show me," Gerald said.

He stepped forward. The ground beneath him rippled. He walked toward the center of the Void, where a small, floating platform hovered. The Anomaly was placed there by invisible forces.

Gerald approached the platform. He did not touch the Anomaly. He pulled a tape measure from his pocket.

"Step back," Gerald said.

The entity did not move.

Gerald measured the platform. He measured the diameter. He measured the distance from the edge of the Void to the nearest stable anchor point. He measured the height of the railing.

There was no railing.

"Violation," Gerald said. "Section 8, Subsection D: *All platforms in unstable zones require guardrails of at least thirty-six inches in height.* You have none."

The entity's static surged. "This is a hearing, not an inspection."

"It is both," Gerald said. "You cannot enforce a rule that you are violating. If the platform is non-compliant, any action taken upon it is void. Including your attempt to erase the asset."

The entity went silent. The gray suit tightened. The glowing eyes turned white.

"You are trying to stall," the entity said. "The asset is destabilizing. Look."

Gerald looked. The Anomaly was pulsing faster. Cracks appeared in the obsidian floor around it. The reality of the Void was fraying at the edges.

"It is going to blow," the entity said. "And it will take your Core with it. Because you have accepted custody. You are now the owner of the problem."

Gerald looked at the cracks. They were spreading.

If the Anomaly exploded, it would release a shockwave of raw magic.

That magic would tear apart the spatial coordinates of Gerald's dungeon. His library would collapse. His employees—Kaelen, the rat, the water leak—would cease to exist.

He had one minute.

Gerald looked at the tape measure. Then he looked at the entity.

"Who owns the platform?" Gerald asked.

"The Void Bureau," the entity said. "Why?"

"Because you built it," Gerald said. "And you built it wrong. So you are liable for the damages."

The entity tilted its head. "Liability does not apply to cosmic phenomena."

"It applies to negligence," Gerald said.

"And you were negligent. You placed a unstable asset on a non-compliant platform.

That is a chain of liability.

Therefore, the erasure order is invalid until the platform is brought up to code."

The entity's eyes flashed red. "This is absurd."

"It is the code," Gerald said. "And the code is the only thing that matters."

Gerald reached into his pocket. He did not pull out a weapon. He did not pull out a spell. He pulled out a small, yellow tag.

He had used it on the goblin in Chapter 1. He had used it on the dragon in Chapter 100. He had never used it on an abstract concept.

He stuck the tag on the floating platform.

**Citation: Guardrail Violation.** **Fine: 500 System Credits.** **Status: Pending Payment.**

The System paused.

The entire Void froze. The swirling chaos stopped. The cracks in the floor halted their spread.

A message appeared in the air, written in burning letters.

**VIOLATION DETECTED. INSPECTOR GENERAL REPRESENTATIVE CANNOT EXERCISE AUTHORITY IN NON-COMPLIANT ZONES. HEARING SUSPENDED.**

Gerald let out a breath he didn't know he was holding. He hadn't won. He had just delayed. But delays were how you survived.

He looked at the Anomaly. It was still pulsing. But the pulse was slower now. The System was holding the chaos in check because the environment was illegal.

Gerald picked up the Anomaly. It was heavy. Warmer than before.

He looked at the entity. The static-suit was vibrating with rage.

"I have five minutes to fix the railing," Gerald said. "Or I have five minutes to pay the fine. Which do you prefer?"

The entity did not answer. It pointed at the edge of the Void.

"Leave," the entity said. "The hearing is adjourned. You are fined 500 credits. And you are ordered to report the Anomaly to Central Command within twenty-four hours."

Gerald nodded. He put the Anomaly in his pocket. He turned and walked toward the exit portal.

Behind him, he heard the entity shout something. He didn't listen.

He stepped through the portal.

The darkness vanished. The weight of gravity returned.

Gerald landed on the library floor. Kaelen was there, waiting. The boy looked terrified.

"Gerald?" Kaelen whispered. "You're back."

Gerald checked his watch. 9:05 AM.

Five minutes in the Void. Five minutes out. It was consistent.

He looked around the library. The shelves were still standing. The lights were on. The air smelled of dust and old paper.

But something was wrong.

The air felt heavy. The mana density in the room had increased. Gerald could feel it in his teeth. A metallic tang.

He looked at his pocket. The Anomaly was still there.

He pulled it out. The ball was glowing brighter. The cracks in its surface were widening.

"Gerald," Kaelen said. "What did you bring back?"

Gerald looked at the ball. He looked at the library walls. He looked at the ceiling.

He realized what he had done.

He had cited the Void. He had fined the Inspector General.

But the Anomaly wasn't contained. It was *charged*.

The citation had added System energy to the object. It had stabilized it, but it had also energized it. The Anomaly was no longer a glitch. It was a battery.

And Gerald was holding it.

He felt a pull in his chest. His Core. His own dungeon core, hidden beneath the library, was resonating with the ball.

The ball was trying to merge with him.

Gerald tried to drop it. His hand didn't move. His fingers were locked around the sphere. The muscles in his arm were rigid.

Kaelen stepped forward. "Gerald, drop it!"

"I can't," Gerald said. His voice was tight.

The ball pulsed. A wave of energy shot out from it.

It hit the nearest bookshelf.

The books didn't fall.

They *changed*. The words on the spines scrambled.

The titles became meaningless gibberish.

Kaelen screamed. He grabbed Gerald's arm.

"Let go!" Kaelen shouted.

Gerald gritted his teeth. He tried to force his hand open. He couldn't. The System was binding him.

*Asset Integration in Progress.*

The message appeared in his mind. Not on a clipboard. In his thoughts.

*If integration completes, Host Body will be overwritten.*

Gerald looked at Kaelen. The boy's face was twisting with fear and effort as he pulled at Gerald's arm.

Gerald had to stop it. He couldn't fight physics. He couldn't fight magic.

He had to fight bureaucracy.

He needed a form. He needed a signature. He needed a reason to stop the process.

*System Rule: Asset Integration can be halted if the Host Core files a Motion for Recalibration.*

*Requirement: The Host Core must present a flaw in the Asset's origin.*

Gerald stared at the ball. What was the flaw? What was the origin?

He had found it in a wall. A load-bearing wall.

Why was it in the wall?

Someone had put it there. Someone had hidden it.

And if it was hidden, it wasn't registered.

If it wasn't registered, it had no origin.

If it had no origin, it couldn't be integrated. It was an orphaned asset.

Gerald stopped struggling. He went limp. He let Kaelen fall back.

He closed his eyes. He reached for his mental clipboard. He didn't need the physical one. He needed the System interface.

*Motion to Halt Integration.*

*Grounds: Asset lacks Origin Certification.*

*Proof: Physical inspection of Asset surface reveals absence of Manufacturer's Mark.*

Gerald opened his eyes. He looked at the ball.

It was smooth. Perfect.

No brand.

No maker. No date.

"Origin Certification Missing," Gerald said aloud.

The ball pulsed. The energy wave stopped.

The binding on his hand loosened. Gerald dropped the ball. It hit the floor and rolled under a table.

Kaelen scrambled away. "What happened?"

Gerald breathed. Hard, shallow breaths. His heart hammered against his ribs.

He looked at the ball under the table. It was still glowing. But it was dormant.

He had stopped the integration. But he hadn't destroyed the Anomaly.

And now, the System knew he had halted the process.

A new message appeared in his mind.

**Motion Accepted. Integration Halted.** **Penalty for Unregistered Asset: Immediate Audit of Host Core.** **Audit Begins in 10 Minutes.**

Gerald looked at his watch.

Ten minutes.

The System was going to tear apart his dungeon. It was going to look at every wall, every pipe, every rat. It was going to look at *him*.

He looked at Kaelen. The boy was shaking.

"Gerald," Kaelen said. "What do we do?"

Gerald stood up. His legs felt weak. He picked up his clipboard.

"We don't run," Gerald said. "We inspect."

He looked at the walls of the library. They were solid. But were they?

He had fixed the leak. He had cited the goblin. He had fined the Void.

But he had never inspected his own Core.

He had assumed it was safe. He had assumed that because he was careful, he was compliant.

He was wrong.

The Anomaly had been in the wall. Why?

Because the wall was weak.

Because the Core was weak.

Gerald walked to the center of the room. He placed his hand on the floor.

He felt the hum of his Core beneath the concrete. It was there. But it was quiet. Too quiet.

He had forgotten to file his own Compliance Report.

He had been so busy citing others, he had neglected his own paperwork.

The System was auditing him. And he had thirty-one years of unfiled reports.

Gerald's face went pale.

He didn't have ten minutes to prepare a defense. He had ten minutes to commit fraud.

He looked at Kaelen.

"Find me the original blueprint," Gerald said. "The one from 1974."

Kaelen blinked. "The library was built in 1982."

"The foundation was laid in 1974," Gerald said. "And the foundation has a flaw."

He ran.

He didn't run fast.

He ran with purpose. He ran to the back office.

He tore through the cabinets.

He found the dusty folder.

He opened it.

The blueprint was there. But one line was missing. A line that shouldn't be there.

A line connecting the foundation to the Void.

Gerald stared at the paper.

He hadn't found the Anomaly in a wall.

He had found the exit to the Void in his own foundation.

And he had been using it to transport himself.

Without a permit.

The clock ticked.

9:55 AM.

Five minutes left.

Gerald took out his pen. He uncapped it.

He didn't draw a new wall. He didn't build a barrier.

He drew a door.

He drew a door on the blueprint, right over the exit to the Void.

And he labeled it.

*Emergency Exit.*

*Compliant.*

He signed it.

It was a lie. A massive, dangerous, illegal lie.

If the System accepted it, he was safe.

If the System rejected it, he would be erased.

Gerald held the paper. His hand didn't shake.

He waited for the Audit.
