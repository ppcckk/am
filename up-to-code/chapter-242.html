Chapter 242: Next Core Revealed

# Chapter 242

The clipboard descended from the darkness like a guillotine blade.

Gerald Hutchins did not flinch. He had survived thirty-one years of municipal budget cuts and angry landlords who tried to argue that a load-bearing wall was merely decorative. A faceless auditor holding a clipboard was, relatively speaking, the least of his worries.

"Your suspension is lifted," the Figure said. Its voice sounded like paper tearing. "But your audit has begun."

Gerald tightened his grip on the wrench. The metal was cold, slick with condensation from the leaking pipe he had ignored for forty-five minutes.

"Define 'audit,'" Gerald said. He kept his voice flat. Professional. The tone of a man denying a variance request over a minor setback on a driveway permit.

"Comprehensive review," the Figure replied. It did not move.

It hovered three inches above the linoleum, its suit absorbing the dim emergency lighting.

"All structures, all entities, all compliance records, and all active citations. You will submit to inspection immediately. Failure to comply results in Core Dissolution."

The Rat chittered in the shadows. The Mold pulsed in the vents, a sickly green rhythm.

Gerald looked at the Swarm. They were still regrouping, chitinous legs clicking on the floor tiles. If the Inspector General dissolved him, the Swarm would inherit the dungeon. Or worse, the System would collapse the sub-basement, taking the library with it.

He had to stall.

"Standard protocol requires a scheduled appointment," Gerald said.

He pulled a crumpled form from his pocket. It was a duplicate of Form 11-B, the one he had tried to file on the rat weeks ago.

"Section 4, Paragraph 2, Subsection C of the Municipal Inspection Code.

No unannounced inspections unless there is imminent danger of structural failure."

The Figure tilted its head. The blank surface where eyes should be reflected the chaos of the room.

"Imminent danger of structural failure," the Figure repeated. It checked the clipboard.

"Your Compliance Rating is currently 42.

The threshold for immediate dissolution is 20.

You are not failing. You are merely... under review."

"I am not failing," Gerald corrected. "I am undergoing necessary renovations. Your presence is a hazard. You are obstructing the egress route."

Gerald pointed his wrench at the Figure.

"Move to the left. You are blocking the path to the fire exit."

The Swarm surged forward. A centipede-like construct lunged at Gerald's throat.

Gerald did not dodge. He stepped *into* the attack, bringing the wrench up not to strike, but to block. The metal clangs against the creature's carapace. Sparks flew.

**SYSTEM ALERT: PHYSICAL CONTACT REGISTERED.**

**VIOLATION: ASSAULT ON AN AUDIT ENTITY.**

The text flashed in Gerald's peripheral vision. Red. Angry.

The Figure stopped hovering. It dropped to the floor with a heavy thud. The clipboard slammed against the ground.

"You struck me," the Figure said. The voice was no longer paper-tearing. It was concrete grinding.

"This is a Class A violation. Obstruction of justice."

"I blocked a hazard," Gerald said.

His heart hammered against his ribs, a frantic bird trapped in a cage of bone. He kept his face blank. "It lunged.

I defended.

Check the timestamp."

The Figure raised a hand. The air around them shimmered. A grey box expanded, filling the room.

**AUDIT PHASE 1: STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY.**

**TIMER: 60 SECONDS.**

**OBJECTIVE: IDENTIFY THREE CRITICAL FAILURES IN YOUR CORE INFRASTRUCTURE.**

**PENALTY FOR FAILURE: 15% STAT DEBUFF.**

The Swarm froze. The Mold stopped pulsing. Even the police pounding on the library door seemed to fade into a dull thud.

Sixty seconds.

Gerald looked around his dungeon.

Four thousand square feet of basement. Exposed pipes. Uneven concrete.

A ceiling that sagged in the north quadrant because he hadn't reinforced the joists after the water leak.

He had to find the failures. Quickly.

He ran.

Not toward the exit. Toward the north wall.

His legs burned. The debuff from the earlier Hearing was still active; his speed was reduced by 10%. He felt heavy, sluggish.

*Three critical failures.*

What counted as critical?

Gerald stopped at the north wall.

He placed his hand on the concrete.

It was damp. Cold.

He pressed harder. A hairline fracture spiderwebbed beneath his fingers.

**FAILURE 1: WATER INTRUSION COMPROMISING STRUCTURAL LOAD BEARER.**

The text appeared above his head.

One down.

Fifty-eight seconds.

Gerald spun around. The Swarm was advancing.

Slowly, cautiously.

They sensed the System was watching. They wouldn't attack an Auditor. Not yet.

He looked up. The ceiling.

The water leak.

The one he had been ignoring. It wasn't just a stain.

It was a rusted conduit.

A pipe meant for electrical wiring, not water.

*Electrical code.*

Section 602.1. No water sources within six inches of electrical junction boxes.

Gerald ran to the junction box. It was on the east wall, near the old furnace. The water was dripping directly onto the metal box.

**FAILURE 2: WATER SOURCE IN PROXIMITY TO ELECTRICAL JUNCTION. CODE VIOLATION 602.1.**

Two down.

Forty-five seconds.

Gerald's chest heaved. He needed one more.

He scanned the room. The handrails.

He had installed them.

They were up to code.

The fire exits were clear. The ventilation ducts were narrow, but he had filed a variance request for them two chapters ago.

The System had granted a temporary waiver. That wouldn't count as a failure right now.

What else?

He looked at the floor.

Uneven.

He had measured it. It was within tolerance for a sub-basement.

He looked at the door.

The police were still pounding.

The Swarm was still watching.

He looked at the Mold.

The Mold was in the vents.

*Ventilation.*

The System had fined him for inadequate ventilation at the start. He had fixed it. Mostly.

But had he fixed it *completely*?

Gerald ran to the main vent intake. It was a six-inch circular duct.

He pulled a measuring tape from his belt. It clicked as he extended it.

Six inches.

Code required eight inches for proper air exchange in a space of this volume, unless mechanical fans were used.

He had no fans.

He had relied on natural draft.

He had been wrong.

**FAILURE 3: INADEQUATE VENTILATION CAPACITY. CODE VIOLATION 401.3.**

Three down.

The timer stopped.

**AUDIT PHASE 1 COMPLETE.**

**PENALTY WAIVED DUE TO SELF-IDENTIFICATION OF FAILURES.**

**NEW VIOLATION LOGGED: 1. ASSAULT ON AUDIT ENTITY.**

Gerald gasped for air. He leaned against the junction box, ignoring the sparks that snapped near his head.

The Figure stepped forward. It picked up its clipboard.

"You identified the failures," the Figure said. "But you did not fix them."

"I am in the process," Gerald said. He wiped sweat from his brow with his sleeve. "I am preparing the remediation plan."

The Figure opened the clipboard.

"Remediation requires time," the Figure said. "The System does not grant time. It grants penalties."

The grey box updated.

**PENALTY FOR ASSAULT: 20% REDUCTION IN STRUCTURAL HP.**

**PENALTY FOR UNREMEDIED VIOLATIONS: 10% REDUCTION IN SPAWN SPEED.**

**TOTAL COMPLIANCE RATING: 38.**

The floor shook. A crack split the concrete beneath Gerald's feet.

The Figure looked down at the crack. Then at Gerald.

"Your dungeon is weakening," the Figure said. "This is your warning. The Inspector General does not tolerate negligence."

"I am not negligent," Gerald said. His voice was tight. "I am efficient."

"Efficiency is not compliance," the Figure said. "You have thirty seconds to submit a Remediation Plan. Or the audit moves to Phase 2: Entity Review."

"Entity Review," Gerald repeated.

He looked at the Rat. He looked at the Mold. He looked at the Swarm.

If the Figure reviewed the entities, it would see the unlicensed Rat. It would see the Mold acting as a combat unit. It would see the Swarm as hostile invaders, not part of the dungeon's ecosystem.

If the System deemed them non-compliant, it would purge them.

The Swarm was his defense. Without them, he had nothing but a wrench and a leaking pipe.

"I need an extension," Gerald said.

"The System does not negotiate," the Figure said.

Gerald looked at the Swarm leader. It was a large spider-construct, its eyes glowing red.

It waited.

It trusted Gerald. Or at least, it trusted the rules Gerald upheld.

If Gerald failed, they died.

Gerald closed his eyes.

He thought about his office. The stack of unfinished files. The coffee stains on the carpet. The way the city council ignored his reports because they were "too detailed."

He had spent his life trying to make things safe. Trying to make things *right*.

And now the rules were killing him.

He opened his eyes.

"I have a counter-citation," Gerald said.

The Figure paused.

The Swarm clicked.

"Explain," the Figure said.

Gerald pointed at the Figure's feet.

"You are standing on a non-load-bearing tile," Gerald said.

"I did not install these tiles. I inherited them.

They are cracked.

They are crumbling."

He pointed to the air.

"You are hovering. You have no physical mass registered in the System. Therefore, you are not subject to gravity, but you are subject to *spatial occupancy* codes."

Gerald pulled out his notebook. He flipped to a new page.

**COUNTER-CITATION 1: UNLICENSED AERIAL OCCUPANCY.**

**VIOLATION: ENTITY PRESENT IN SPACE WITHOUT REGISTERED FOOTPRINT OR WEIGHT DISPLACEMENT.**

**PENALTY: FORCED GROUNDING.**

The Figure looked down at its feet.

"I am an Auditor," it said. "I am not a citizen."

"You are an entity," Gerald said. "And you are obstructing my path to the remediation zone. If you do not land, you are violating the 'Clear Pathway' mandate of Section 302."

The Figure hesitated.

Gerald pressed.

"Land, or I cite you for Obstruction of Justice. Again. And this time, the penalty is Immediate Deletion."

The Figure's blank face rippled.

Deletion.

The System's ultimate punishment. It erased the entity from existence.

No respawn.

No memory.

Just nothing.

The Figure looked at the Swarm. It looked at Gerald.

It looked at the clipboard.

It lowered itself.

Its feet touched the cracked tile.

**CRACK.**

The tile shattered.

The Figure sank an inch into the concrete.

**SYSTEM ALERT: STRUCTURAL FAILURE DETECTED IN AUDIT ENTITY'S CURRENT POSITION.**

The grey box flashed.

**VIOLATION: AUDIT ENTITY PLACED IN UNSTABLE ZONE.**

**PENALTY: AUDIT SUSPENDED PENDING RELOCATION.**

**NEW TIMER: 120 SECONDS TO RELOCATE AUDIT ENTITY.**

The Figure stepped back. Its suit was torn at the knee. Dust coated its shoes.

"You tricked me," the Figure said. The voice was quiet. Dangerous.

"I cited you," Gerald said. He straightened his back. "You are now in violation of your own safety protocols.

You need to move to a stable surface. The north wall is load-bearing.

The south is electrical.

The east is wet."

Gerald pointed to the center of the room.

"There is a patch of reinforced concrete there. I put it in last week."

The Figure looked at the center.

It moved.

Slowly.

As it moved, the Swarm surged forward.

They didn't attack the Figure. They attacked the *space* the Figure had vacated.

The Spider-construct leaped onto the cracked tile where the Figure had stood. It crushed the remaining fragments.

**SPAWN ACTION: REINFORCEMENT OF DUNGEON FLOOR.**

Gerald watched.

He hadn't ordered them to do that.

They had done it because the Figure had left the area unprotected.

The Figure reached the reinforced patch. It stopped.

It looked at Gerald.

"You are playing a dangerous game, Inspector," the Figure said.

"I am following the rules," Gerald said.

The Figure raised its pen.

"Audit Phase 2: Entity Review. Begin."

The grey box expanded again.

**TARGET: THE SWARM.**

**QUESTION: ARE THESE ENTITIES LICENSED PARTS OF THE DUNGEON?**

Gerald's blood ran cold.

The Swarm looked up. They looked at Gerald.

They waited for his answer.

If he said *no*, they were invaders. He could cite them. The System would purge them. He would be safe, but alone.

If he said *yes*, he was admitting they were part of his dungeon.

Which meant he was responsible for them.

Which meant he had to prove they were compliant.

Did he have a license for a swarm of chitinous monsters?

No.

He had a Form 11-B. But it was for a rat.

He had no forms for them.

The Figure waited.

Gerald looked at the Swarm. He looked at the wrench in his hand.

He looked at the Inspector General.

He opened his mouth.

"Yes," he said.

The Figure's pen touched the paper.

**SUBMIT LICENSING DOCUMENTATION FOR ENTITY GROUP: SWARM.**

**MISSING DOCUMENTS DETECTED.**

**PENALTY: CORE FRAGMENTATION IMMINENT.**

The floor beneath Gerald splitted.

Not from water. Not from weight.

From the System tearing his dungeon apart, layer by layer.

He had to produce a document. Any document.

He reached into his pocket.

He pulled out a receipt.

It was a receipt for a bulk order of industrial-grade epoxy. He had bought it to seal the cracks in the north wall.

He held it up.

"This is proof of material integrity," Gerald said. "The Swarm is constructed of bonded organic material. This receipt proves the bonding agent is approved for structural use."

It was a lie. A massive, bureaucratic lie.

The Figure looked at the receipt.

It looked at Gerald.

It looked at the Swarm, which was currently holding up a section of the ceiling with its legs.

The Figure's pen hovered.

The System processed the input.

**ANALYZING...**

**DOCUMENT VALIDITY: 40%.**

**INSUFFICIENT.**

The ceiling collapsed.

Gerald screamed.

But not in pain. In rage.

He grabbed the wrench and smashed it into the nearest pillar.

"Wait!" he shouted.

The Figure paused.

Gerald was panting. He looked at the crumbling ceiling. He looked at the Swarm, which was now desperately trying to hold the debris back.

He looked at the Inspector General.

"I have another citation," Gerald said. His voice was a growl.

The Figure tilted its head.

"Go on."

Gerald pointed at the crumbling ceiling.

"Your presence is causing undue stress on the structure. Your audit is incomplete. Your authority is contingent on the stability of the site."

He slammed his hand on the notebook.

**CITATION 2: UNNECESSARY INDUCED STRESS.**

**VIOLATION: AUDIT PROCESS EXCEEDS STRUCTURAL TOLERANCE.**

**PENALTY: HALT AUDIT.**

The Figure did not move.

The ceiling groaned.

Gerald closed his eyes.

He hoped the System understood that he was not fighting the Auditor.

He was fighting the *physics* of the Audit.

And he was betting his entire existence that the System valued its own infrastructure more than it valued its own agents.

The pen stopped.

The ceiling stopped falling.

**SYSTEM ALERT: STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY AT CRITICAL LEVELS.**

**AUDIT PAUSED FOR SAFETY CHECK.**

The Figure lowered its pen.

"You are walking a fine line, Hutchins," the Figure said.

Gerald opened his eyes. He was sweating. His hands were shaking.

"I am within tolerance," Gerald said.

The Figure looked at the Swarm.

They were holding the ceiling up. They were struggling.

"They are dying," the Figure said. "If you do not reinforce them, they will die. And if they die, your dungeon collapses."

Gerald looked at the Spider-construct. It was cracking.

He looked at the Figure.

He looked at the wrench.

He looked at the exit.

The police were still outside.

He had one minute before the ceiling fell.

He had one minute before the Swarm died.

He had one minute before he lost.

Gerald turned to the Swarm.

"Hold," he said.

He turned to the Figure.

"Give me your clipboard."

The Figure hesitated.

"Why?"

"Because," Gerald said, "I need to check if you have a permit to stand on my floor."

The Figure stared.

Gerald held out his hand.

"Hand it over. Or I cite you for Contempt of Inspector."

The silence stretched.

The ceiling groaned.

The Figure extended the clipboard.

Gerald grabbed it.

He looked at the first page.

It was blank.

He looked at the Figure.

"Where is the file?" Gerald asked.

The Figure smiled.

It had no face.

But it smiled.

"There is no file," it said.

Gerald froze.

"Then who are you?"

The Figure stepped back.

"I am not an Auditor," it said. "I am the next Core."

The grey box vanished.

The Swarm dropped the ceiling.

Gerald screamed.
