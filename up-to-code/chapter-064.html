Chapter 64: Form Against Floor

# Chapter 64

Gerald's pen moved. The pen was the only thing moving.

Everything else was either too fast or too slow.

The pale thing rose. The rat crouched. The suppression heads spat water in short, failing bursts.

Gerald wrote.

Form 11-B. Unlicensed Biological Entity. Section 4, Subsection 2. Any entity not registered to a Core that occupies space on registered property without a valid permit.

He filled in the entity description. This was the hard part.

The thing beneath the floor had no shape, which had no edges, and it moved like something that had forgotten how to stop. Gerald wrote: Amorphous.

Pale. Approximately 2 feet visible above floor plane.

No discernible extremities.

No eyes.

No mouth, and he paused. The rat was watching him. The rat's eyes were flat and gray and not entirely rat-shaped.

"It's getting wider," the rat said.

Gerald looked. It was. The pale mass had spread. It pressed against the crack's edges, widening it and splitting the floor like tree roots on pavement.

He kept writing. Occupied area: expanding. Estimated footprint: 3 square feet and growing.

He couldn't measure it properly. The thing was too fast for a tape measure.

He estimated, and he hated estimating. But he wrote the number.

He signed. He filed. The System processed.

REJECTED.

The word appeared in his vision like a stamp pressed by an invisible hand. Red. Final. Gerald stared at it.

The System's rejection note appeared below: Entity does not meet biological classification criteria. Entity classified as STRUCTURAL. See Form 7-C for structural modifications.

Gerald stopped writing. His hand was still in the air. The pen dripped ink onto the floor.

The pale thing rose another inch. The crack widened. One of the suppression heads cracked and fell. Water sprayed across the concrete.

"You got rejected," the rat said.

"I got misclassified." Gerald's voice was flat.

He could feel it in his chest, that old familiar wrongness. The feeling of filing the wrong form.

The feeling of a clerk sending you back to the window.

The feeling of standing in line for forty minutes only to be told you were at the wrong counter.

His hands wanted to stop. His hands wanted to sit down.

His hands wanted to go home and make a sandwich and not think about this anymore.

His stomach was doing that thing it did when he realized he had filled out the wrong version of a form and had to start over.

It was not panic. It was worse than panic.

It was the specific nausea of a man who had made a procedural error and could not fix it by going back.

The pale thing was at three inches. The floor was buckling. The crack had spread to four feet across.

Gerald looked at the thing. He regarded it with the same blank stare he gave a wall.

Not as a living thing.

Not as a threat. As a structure. What was it doing?

It was rising, which was occupying space. It was modifying the floor plane.

It was changing the geometry of his dungeon without a permit.

Form 7-C. Unpermitted Structural Modification.

He tore out the 11-B. He started a new sheet. His handwriting was worse now.

His hand was shaking harder. But the form was simple.

He knew this form, and he had written four hundred of these in his career.

Unpermitted deck addition. Unpermitted basement conversion.

Unpermitted load-bearing wall removal. The form was always the same.

Entity description: Amorphous. Pale.

Rising from floor plane.

Modifying structural substrate. No permit on file. No registered Core associated.

Occupied area: 4 square feet. Expanding.

Violation: Unpermitted structural modification. Section 9, Subsection 1.

He signed. He filed.

The System processed.

CITATION ISSUED.

The pale thing stopped.

Not slowed.

Stopped. It froze mid-rise.

The pale mass hung in the air like something caught between one breath and the next.

The crack stopped widening. The floor stopped buckling. The water from the broken suppression head pooled around Gerald's shoes.

Gerald exhaled. His knees were not reliable.

The rat stood. It looked at the pale thing. It looked at Gerald.

"That worked," the rat said.

"It's a citation. It's not a solution. It's a stop order. It has to come to a hearing or comply within the window or..."

He didn't finish. He didn't know what "or" meant.

He had never seen what happened when a non-Core entity failed to comply with a citation.

He had never cited a non-Core entity before, and he had cited dungeon cores. He had cited goblins.

He had cited a sentient fog that had been blocking an egress route. He had never cited a thing that had no name and no shape and no obvious connection to the System.

A new notification appeared. Not the usual blue.

This one was gray.

Like old paper. Like something that had been sitting in a filing cabinet for a long time.

PRE-CODE STRUCTURE DETECTED.

Gerald read it twice.

PRE-CODE STRUCTURE DETECTED.

ENTITY CLASSIFICATION: LEGACY. ENTITY PREDATES CURRENT CODE FRAMEWORK.

CITATION ACCEPTED UNDER TRANSITIONAL PROVISIONS.

GRANDFATHERING PROCESS INITIATED.

"What does that mean?" the rat asked.

Gerald knew. He knew exactly what it meant.

He had seen it a hundred times in municipal work.

A grandfathering clause.

When new code came in, existing structures were exempt. They didn't have to meet the new standard.

They were allowed to stay as they were.

But grandfathering wasn't permanent. A grandfathered structure could be brought up to code at any time.

And when it was, it had to meet current standards.

All of them. Every single one.

The pale thing was being grandfathered. It was being brought up to Gerald's dungeon's current code.

And Gerald's dungeon's current code was the one he had built, and the one with handrails and brackets and suppression heads. The one that was currently missing most of its handrails and brackets because the Agent had pulled them out. The one that was a 4,000-square-foot sub-basement with a Compliance Rating of 34 and a containment perimeter that was held together by hope and three functioning suppression heads.

The pale thing was being rebuilt to Gerald's specifications. Gerald's current, incomplete, damaged specifications.

"Oh," Gerald said.

The pale thing started to change.

It wasn't rising anymore. It was reshaping.

It was taking on structure, which was becoming compliant. It was becoming part of the building.

And as it became part of the building, it introduced new elements. Elements Gerald hadn't accounted for.

The pale thing had mass.

It had weight, which was loading the floor; was adding structural stress that Gerald's current code didn't account for.

His Compliance Rating flickered.

34/100.

31/100.

29/100.

The rat looked at the numbers. It looked at Gerald.

"It's part of your dungeon now," the rat said.

"It's a grandfathered structure. It's subject to my code."

"And your code is failing."

"My code is in transition."

"Your code is failing."

Gerald looked at the pale thing. It was settling. It was taking a shape.

A rough shape. A shape that looked like it was trying to be a load-bearing column.

It was becoming a column.

A pale, wrong, unpermitted column that was now technically grandfathered into his structure.

His dungeon was gaining a new structural element.

And that element was introducing stress that his other systems weren't designed to handle. The column was wide.

Wider than a standard column, which was taking up floor space that his egress routes needed. It was blocking sight lines that his fire code required. It was, Gerald realized with a cold clarity that felt like swallowing ice, introducing new violations simply by existing.

28/100.

The notification came. Gray again. Like old paper.

INSPECTOR GENERAL AUDIT ACCELERATED. REASON: PRE-CODE STRUCTURE DETECTED. AUDIT ETA: 6 HOURS.

Six hours.

Gerald looked at his dungeon. His dungeon was a sub-basement that was missing handrails, missing brackets, missing half its suppression heads, and now contained a pre-System entity that was becoming a load-bearing column. His Compliance Rating was 28 and falling. And the Inspector General was coming in six hours.

He looked at the rat.

"How long has that thing been down here?" he asked.

"I don't know," the rat said. "Before me. Before the System. Before the library."

Gerald sat down. He sat on the floor, in the water, with his clipboard in his lap.

He uncapped his pen, and he capped it.

He uncapped it again.

Six hours to bring a pre-System entity up to current code. Or the Inspector General would condemn his dungeon. And Gerald knew what condemnation looked like.

He had condemned buildings. He had condemned a condemned building once by accident, and it had taken three weeks and two lawyers to fix.

He looked at the pale column, which was settling into shape. It looked almost right. Almost like a column.

Almost like something that belonged in a building.

He opened a new form. Form 14-A.

Structural Modification Permit Application, and he was going to permit the thing. He was going to make it legal. He was going to bring it up to code.

But he had to know what it was first. He had to measure it. He had to understand its load. He had to calculate the stress it was introducing.

He had six hours, and he looked at the column. The column looked back.

It didn't have eyes.

It didn't have a face.

But Gerald felt it looking at him the way a building looks at an inspector. Waiting.

Patient. Knowing that the inspector will eventually have to look at the foundation.

And the foundation was where the problem was.

Gerald stood. He walked to the nearest wall.

He put his hand on it, and he felt the vibration. Not the pale thing's vibration. Something else.

Something deeper.

Something that moved through the concrete like a pulse.

The walls were cracking.

Not the pale thing.

Not the column. The walls. The library's walls.

The real library's walls. The walls above him.

The walls that held up a building with three stories and a roof and a foundation that was,, not what anyone had thought it was.

Gerald walked to the column, and he put his hand on it. It was warm.

It lacked the warmth of concrete. It had the warmth of skin.

The pale thing was not just a column. It was a foundation.

It was the thing beneath the floor, and the thing that had been down here before the library. Before the System. Before Gerald.

It was the thing the library was built on.

And it was moving.

Gerald's pen stopped. He looked at the column. He looked at the floor. He looked at the walls.

The ceiling cracked. A chunk of concrete fell. It landed on the floor and shattered into gray powder.

Gerald looked up. The ceiling was the library floor above him. The library floor was cracking. The library was cracking.

He had six hours. But he didn't have six hours. He had minutes.

The rat said, "What do we do?"

Gerald looked at the ceiling. He looked at the column. He looked at his clipboard.

He uncapped his pen, and he started a new form.

And the ceiling cracked again.
