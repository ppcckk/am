Chapter 122: Serving the Summons

# Chapter 122

The latch clicked open.

Cold air rushed out, smelling of ozone and old paper.

Gerald's rat-mind scrambled forward. His tiny claws scrabbled on the dusty metal. The box was heavy, sealed with a lead gasket that felt cold against his whiskers.

Behind him, the basement door groaned.

Wood splintered. Concrete dust rained down.

*Bang.*

Another blow. The frame twisted.

Gerald didn't look back. He couldn't look back.

His consciousness was a frayed wire in a storm, barely tethered to this twitching, fur-covered vessel.

If he lost focus, the Core would shatter. The rat would die. He would be deleted.

He pushed the lid open.

Inside, stacked in neat, crumbling columns, were tablets. Not digital. Physical. Slate.

Gerald froze.

A rat's brain processed images as movement.

He saw the shapes. He felt the texture.

But he had to force his human intellect through the animal's sensory filter.

It was like reading a contract through a keyhole.

One tablet glowed faintly.

*Code 12.4: Prohibition of Unauthorized Sub-Basement Expansion.*

Gerald's heart hammered against his ribs. Or the rat's heart.

It felt the same.

Panic.

Pure, unadulterated panic.

He had spent thirty years ensuring sub-basements didn't expand. He had fined developers for digging an inch too deep.

Now, his own dungeon was expanding.

The door behind him buckled. A sliver of daylight pierced the gloom.

"Clear!" a voice shouted. "Push it!"

The door gave way.

Gerald shoved the slate back into the box. He slammed the lid. He turned.

The demolition crew stood in the doorway. Three men in orange vests. Hard hats. Hammers.

But they weren't looking at him.

They were looking at the ceiling.

Above them, the library floor was buckling. The weight of the dungeon, shifting as Gerald's consciousness moved into the rat, had thrown off the load distribution. The joists above were snapping.

One by one. Like dry bones.

The foreman pointed up. "Shit. We're losing the header."

Gerald scurried under the table.

The men didn't notice the rat. They were too busy watching the structural failure.

This was it.

Gerald understood structures.

He knew that when a header failed, the load transferred to the adjacent walls. Those walls here were non-load-bearing partitions. Cheap.

Hollow-core doors.

The ceiling would collapse.

And if the library collapsed, the Municipal permit would be revoked.

And if the permit was revoked, the System would deregister the Core.

Gerald needed to stabilize the load. Now.

He couldn't build. He had no mana. He couldn't spawn blocks. He was a rat in a basement full of men with hammers.

But he had the box.

He had the Code.

Gerald bit the latch of the box. Hard. His jaws ached. The metal resisted.

*Crack.*

The latch broke. The box slid forward, scraping across the concrete.

The foreman turned. "Hey! What's that?"

Gerald didn't flee. He stood on his hind legs. He placed his front paws on the box. He looked at the foreman.

He projected.

Not magic. Not mana. Intent.

*You are violating Ordinance 44-B: Unsafe Conditions.*

The foreman blinked. He rubbed his helmet. "Did you hear that?"

"Hear what?" asked the younger worker.

"Like... a buzz."

The ceiling groaned. A chunk of plaster fell, cracking on the floor between the men.

Gerald seized the moment.

He didn't attack. He inspected.

In his mind, he pulled up the structural schematic of the basement. The System interface, usually a glowing blue overlay, was dim. Flickering. It looked like a corrupted file.

But the lines were there.

The load path. The shear walls. The anchor bolts.

The anchor bolts were loose.

Gerald ran.

He darted between the foreman's legs. The man yelped, kicking backward.

Gerald hit the wall.

He stopped.

He looked at the base of the wall. A steel plate. Rusting. The bolt head was stripped.

*Improper installation. Torque specification not met.*

Gerald's Core pulsed. A spark of authority, weak but real.

He couldn't tighten the bolt. He didn't have hands. He didn't have tools.

But the System enforced its own rules.

If a violation was identified, and the Core lacked the resources to fix it immediately, the System could impose a *Temporary Remediation Protocol*.

It was a loophole. Section 9, Subsection D. Emergency Stabilization.

Gerald focused on the bolt.

He imagined the torque wrench. He imagined the counter-clockwise turn. He imposed that logic onto the physical reality of the room.

*This bolt is non-compliant. it must be adjusted.*

The air around the bolt shimmered.

The foreman saw it. "What the hell?"

The bolt turned.

Not by magic. By physics. The System applied a force vector to correct the variance.

The bolt tightened.

The ceiling groan stopped.

Silence.

The three men stared at the wall.

"That was weird," the younger one said.

"Structural settling," the foreman muttered, wiping sweat from his forehead. "Old pipes.

Vibrations.

Come on. Let's check the other side."

They moved past Gerald.

Gerald remained still. His breath came in short, sharp gasps. The rat's body was shaking.

He had used the loophole.

He had forced the System to perform maintenance.

But it had cost him.

A notification flashed in his peripheral vision.

**REMEDIATION PROTOCOL TRIGGERED.**

**COST:** 15% Core Integrity.

**WARNING:** Frequent use of Emergency Stabilization will be flagged by the Inspector General.

Gerald ignored the warning.

He had bought time. The library wasn't collapsing. The permit wasn't revoked.

But he was still in the basement.

And the hearing was in two days.

He needed the box. He needed the evidence inside it. But he couldn't carry it. It was too heavy.

He needed to leave.

But the exit was blocked by the demolition crew.

And worse.

A shadow moved in the corner of the room.

Gerald's rat-eyes widened.

Not a man.

A construct.

It was made of rebar and drywall. A crude golem, shaped like a person, but jagged. Its eyes were two glowing red LEDs.

It stepped out from behind a stack of old books.

It wasn't the demolition crew's. It wasn't his.

It was a scout.

From the neighboring dungeon. *The Gilded Labyrinth.*

The golem tilted its head. It scanned the room. It found Gerald.

*Target Acquired,* a mechanical voice buzzed.

Gerald scrambled back.

The golem raised a arm. A piece of rebar extended, sharpening into a point.

Gerald had no mana. He couldn't spawn walls. He couldn't spawn traps.

He was a rat.

He looked at the door. The foreman was walking away, distracted by the ceiling.

The golem advanced.

Gerald looked at the box.

He looked at the golem.

He looked at the loose floorboard near the drain.

*Drainage Code 7.1: Floor Slope and Drainage Requirements.*

The drain was clogged. The floor sloped *toward* the drain, but the drain was blocked by debris.

Gerald didn't think. He acted.

He kicked the debris pile with his hind legs.

It wasn't enough.

The golem swung.

The rebar spike whistled past Gerald's ear. It sparked against the concrete.

Gerald ducked. He ran toward the drain.

The golem followed. It was clumsy. Its feet, made of jagged drywall shards, caught on the uneven floor.

Gerald slammed his paws against the debris. He pushed.

He poured his Core's remaining focus into the friction coefficient of the debris.

*Increase drag. Increase mass.*

The debris shifted. It slid into the drain opening.

Water, stagnant and black, bubbled up.

The floor became slick.

The golem stepped into the puddle.

Its drywall feet softened.

It slipped.

It crashed down, rebar arms flailing. It tangled in the loose wires of the old electrical outlet.

*ZAP.*

The golem spasmed. Sparks flew. Its LED eyes flickered.

Gerald didn't stop. He ran to the box.

He couldn't carry it.

But he could open it.

He bit the broken latch again. The lid fell open.

He grabbed one tablet. It was small. Small enough for a rat to drag.

*Code 12.4: Prohibition of Unauthorized Sub-Basement Expansion.*

He dragged it across the floor. The golem was twitching, short-circuiting.

The demolition crew was turning back. They heard the spark.

"Hey! Fire!" the foreman shouted.

Gerald dragged the tablet toward the ventilation shaft.

It was a standard HVAC return. Eighteen inches square. Just big enough for a rat.

He shoved the tablet into the duct.

Then he climbed in.

The duct was dark. Smelled of dust and rust.

Gerald crawled. The tablet scraped behind him.

Above, he heard shouts. "The golem is sparking! Grab the extinguisher!"

Gerald kept crawling.

He reached the grate. It was screwed shut.

He bit the screw. His teeth ached. He pulled.

The screw loosened.

He kicked it free.

The grate fell.

Gerald squeezed through.

He was in the main ductwork of the library.

He was safe. For now.

But the tablet was heavy. And the duct was narrow.

He crawled through the darkness, dragging the slate.

He needed to get to the surface. He needed to file this with the City Council. Or the System. Or both.

Then, a voice.

Not in his ears. In his mind.

It was smooth. Polite. Bureaucratic.

*"Citizen Core 404. You have filed an emergency remediation request.

This request has been accepted.

However, please note that such actions require a Form 12-C: Justification of Structural Instability.

You have not filed Form 12-C."*

Gerald froze in the duct.

*"Failure to file Form 12-C within twenty-four hours will result in an automatic Compliance Violation of the highest severity."*

The voice paused.

*"Furthermore, the Inspector General has flagged your Core for 'Unsanctioned Use of Physical Media.' You have removed a sealed archival record without authorization."*

Gerald felt a cold dread settle in his stomach.

He had saved the dungeon.

He had cited the golem.

He had escaped.

But he had committed a new crime.

A crime of paperwork.

*"Please submit Form 12-C immediately. Or prepare for Audit."*

The voice faded.

Gerald lay in the dark duct, the rat body trembling.

The tablet was in front of him.

He had one day.

One day to write a justification for why a dungeon core had to break building code to save a library.

One day to argue with the System that sometimes, rules were the problem.

Gerald closed his rat-eyes.

He drafted the memo.

*To: The Inspector General.*

*From: Gerald Hutchins, Core Unit 4.*

*Re: Emergency Structural Intervention and Archival Retrieval.*

*1. Intent:*

*To preserve the structural integrity of the Ashford Municipal Library and prevent the illegal expansion of the adjacent dungeon, The Gilded Labyrinth.*

*2. Action Taken:*

*Remediation of anchor bolt failure via System-enforced torque application.*

*Retrieval of sealed evidence regarding non-compliant sub-basement expansion.*

*3. Justification:*

*Standard protocols require a permit for structural work. No permit existed for the emergency. The delay required to obtain a permit would have resulted in catastrophic failure. the action was not a violation of Code 12.4, but a necessary application of Code 0.0: Preservation of Core Integrity.*

*4. Evidence:*

*Attached: Tablet 4-A. Proof of The Gilded Labyrinth's illegal expansion.*

*5. Request:*

*Dismissal of violation charges.*

*Granting of provisional immunity for actions taken under Code 0.0.*

*Signed,*

*Gerald Hutchins.*

He opened his eyes.

The duct ended.

A grate. Sunlight.

He pushed the grate. It was stuck.

He pushed harder.

It gave way.

Gerald fell out onto the roof of the library.

He lay there, panting, the rat's body weak. The tablet beside him.

Above him, the sky was blue.

Below him, the library was quiet.

But in his mind, a timer was counting down.

**TIME REMAINING TO FILE: 23 HOURS, 59 MINUTES.**

And something else.

A ping.

Not from the System.

From the library below.

The front door opened.

A woman walked in. She wore a suit. She carried a briefcase.

She didn't look like an adventurer.

She looked like a lawyer.

She looked like the Inspector General's representative.

Gerald's heart sank.

She wasn't here to help.

She was here to serve the summons.
