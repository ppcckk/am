Chapter 104: Double Rate

# Chapter 104 The rubble was already falling.

Gerald Hutchins did not flinch. He had spent thirty-one years watching load-bearing walls fail, and the sight of a sub-basement ceiling coming down in a shower of jagged limestone chips was no longer frightening. It was merely expensive.

He stood in the center of the collapse zone, arms crossed, clipboard clutched against his chest like a shield. The dust cloud was thick enough to taste, gritty and alkaline. His mana bar, already hovering at a perilous 12%, dipped into the red. A small, frantic squeak from his left boot told him the rat was still alive, though he suspected the creature was currently experiencing a level of existential terror that went beyond mere survival instinct.

The mist was gone, which was the point.

But the void remained. And the Void was hungry.

A shadow detached itself from the darkness beyond the shattered wall. It was not mist.

It was not gas, which was a form. Tall, slender, composed of swirling, semi-solid smoke that coalesced into the shape of a man in a tattered suit. The eyes were burning embers of necrotic green.

"Invalidation," the entity said.

Its voice sounded like gravel grinding in a mixer. "You have voided the partnership, Inspector.

The contract is void.

But the debt remains."

Gerald checked his clipboard. He did not look at the monster. He looked at the debris field.

"Section 4.2.1," Gerald said, his voice dry and flat. "Unlicensed demolition. You have no permit for this structural alteration."

The entity tilted its head. "I am the Void.

I do not require permits.

I require stability, and the mist was the stabilizer. You removed it.

Now the boundary fails. I enter."

Gerald finally looked up. He adjusted his glasses. They were smudged with limestone dust. He wiped them on his sleeve, a reflex so deeply ingrained it happened even when his body was technically a dungeon core footprint than a human form.

"Then you are trespassing," Gerald said. "And you are creating a hazardous condition. I am issuing a Citation."

The Void laughed. It was a wet, rattling sound.

"You cited me three chapters ago.

You cited the Goblin King. You cited the Fire Elemental. You are a nuisance, Hutchins.

A bureaucratic anomaly.

But I am not a dungeon. I am the absence of structure.

You cannot cite the void.

You can only fill it."

Gerald felt the physical pull of the void, and it was a suction, a vacuum trying to drag his mana into itself.

If he lost his mana, he lost the dungeon.

If he lost the dungeon, the library above collapsed.

And if the library collapsed, he would be fined for negligence.

He could not let that happen, and he looked at the debris. The limestone dust was everywhere. It coated the floor, the walls, the entity itself.

The System had just told him the mist was a *Non-Permanent Atmospheric Anomaly*. That was a reclassification. It was a legal definition. And in the System, definitions were power.

If the mist was an anomaly, it was not a structure. If it was not a structure, it had no load-bearing capacity. But the debris?

The debris was solid. The debris was *material*.

Gerald looked at the entity. The entity was made of smoke. Smoke was particulate matter suspended in air. It was technically a *hazardous atmospheric contaminant*.

He opened his inventory, and he did not have a fan. He did not have a vacuum. He had *Limestone Dust*.

He had *Water*.

He had *Unstable Gravel*.

He also had something new.

A drop from the previous fight with the Contractor. A *Sealant Canister*. Industrial grade.

Designed for patching leaks in high-pressure pipes, and he looked at the Void; the entity was drifting closer, drawn to the vacuum of Gerald’s depleted mana.

"You say you are the absence of structure," Gerald said. "I say you are an unauthorized particulate suspension exceeding OSHA permissible exposure limits."

The Void paused.

It was confused, which had faced heroes who used swords. It had faced wizards who used fireballs.

It had not faced a man who cited it for air quality violations.

"I am the end of all things," the Void said.

"You are a violation of Code 7-B: Ventilation of Confined Spaces," Gerald said. "The space is confined.

The air is not moving, and the particulate count is high. I am ordering immediate abatement."

The Void surged forward. "Abatement is my domain. I eat structure. I eat form."

It reached Gerald. The cold was absolute. It touched his clipboard. The clipboard's wood rotted, becoming gray sludge.

Gerald did not move. He did not fight. He activated the *Sealant Canister*.

He did not aim at the Void. He aimed at the debris field behind the Void. The pile of shattered limestone.

He poured the sealant into the cracks of the rubble. The canister hissed, releasing a fast-hardening foam. The foam expanded, binding the loose gravel and dust together. It created a solid, sealed mass.

The Void screamed.

Not in pain, but in frustration. It tried to flow into the rubble, to consume it, to turn it back into void.

But the sealant had changed the nature of the material, which was no longer loose aggregate. It was a *composite structure*.

And a composite structure required a permit.

Gerald pointed a finger at the now-sealed pile of rubble.

"I have just performed emergency structural shoring," Gerald said. "Under the *Emergency Repair Exemption*, I am authorized to create temporary load-bearing supports without prior approval, provided they are inspected within forty-eight hours."

The Void halted. The green eyes flickered.

"You cannot shor up the void," the entity hissed. "The void is the space between the supports."

"Then you are obstructing a protected egress path," Gerald said. "And you are interfering with an active repair site."

The System flashed.

[NOTICE: ACTIVE REPAIR SITE ESTABLISHED] [PROTECTED ZONE: RADIUS 10 FEET] [UNAUTHORIZED ENTITY DETECTED] [ISSUE CITATION?]

Gerald nodded. "Issue Citation. Severity: Critical. Violation: Obstruction of Emergency Egress during Active Repair."

A red stamp appeared in the air. *VIOLATION*.

The Void roared. It tried to phase through the protected zone, but the System’s compliance enforcement was absolute.

The zone was defined by the act of repair, and the Void was not repairing.

The Void was consuming. The Void was non-compliant with the repair protocol.

The citation landed. A chain of red numbers wrapped around the Void’s torso.

[DEBUFF APPLIED: STRUCTURAL INSTABILITY -50% MANA REGENERATION] [DEBUFF APPLIED: FORCED CONGEALMENT - SPEED REDUCED BY 40%]

The Void slowed. Its edges blurred, becoming less defined, more like a thick fog than a solid entity; it was being forced to *condense*.

To become more solid. To become *material*.

And if it was material, it had mass.

And if it had mass, it could be weighed.

And if it could be weighed, it could be moved.

Gerald looked at his mana. 8%, and he needed to get rid of the Void. He could not kill it. You cannot kill the void. But you can *displace* it.

He looked at the sealed pile of rubble. It was heavy. It was stable. It was in the wrong place.

The hole was still there. He looked at it again. The breach where the Void had entered.

He needed to plug the hole. The Void blocked the path.

He looked at the *Water* in his inventory. He had three gallons.

He looked at the *Limestone Dust*. He had fifty pounds.

He looked at the *Sealant*. He had used the canister, but the pump was still active.

He had an idea. It was stupid. It was inefficient. It required mixing cement in a confined space while a demon watched you.

It was the only thing that complied with Code 4.3: *Concrete Placement in Wet Conditions*.

"Gerald," the rat squeaked. It sounded tired.

"Quiet," Gerald said. He opened his inventory and dragged the water and dust into the *Crafting* slot. He added the residual sealant.

*CREATE: FAST-SET MORTAR (BATCH 1)*

The System processed. *MANA COST: 15%*.

Gerald winced. 15% was too high. He had 8%.

He had to be cheaper, and he looked at the Void; the entity was struggling against the debuffs, its form flickering. It was trying to expand, to regain its status as *atmospheric*, but the Citation was forcing it to behave as a *solid object*.

Gerald looked at the debris. The limestone. The dust.

He did not need to make mortar. He needed to make a *slurry*, and a wet, heavy, non-compliant fluid that would fill the breach and weigh down the Void.

He changed the recipe.

*DEFINE: MIXTURE = WATER + LIME + GRAVEL = NON-STRUCTURAL AGGREGATE*

The System paused.

[WARNING: THIS ACTION MAY CREATE SLUDGE] [WARNING: SLUDGE IS NOT LOAD-BEARING] [PROCEED?]

Gerald pressed yes.

His mana dropped to 2%.

The mixture appeared in his hands. It was gray, slimy, and smelled of wet earth. It was not concrete. It was mud.

He walked toward the Void.

The Void laughed again, but it was a nervous laugh. "You bring mud to a void? You think you can fill the abyss with dirt?"

"I am not filling the abyss," Gerald said. "I am creating a barrier."

He threw the mud.

It splattered against the Void, which did not hurt. It did not kill. But it coated the entity.

The mud stuck to the semi-solid smoke, adding weight.

The Void tried to rise, but the mud pulled it down; the Citation’s *Forced Congealment* effect made the mud stick harder.

The Void stumbled. It was now a muddy, gray blob, weighed down by its own new mass.

Gerald walked closer. He reached into his inventory and pulled out the *Handrails*. The ones he had installed in Chapter 4, which were now technically *removable safety barriers* during active repair.

He snapped them off their mounts. The System allowed it. *Temporary Removal for Maintenance*.

He jammed the metal rails into the mud at the edge of the breach.

"Section 5.1.4," Gerald said, panting. "Barrier Installation."

He tied the rails together with wire from his pocket. A makeshift fence.

The Void thrashed, trying to slip through the gaps, but the mud was thick, and the Citation was dragging it down. It was stuck in the breach.

Gerald looked at his mana. 1%, and he had one action left.

He could not fix the breach. He could not seal the wall. He had no more materials.

But he had a *Violation* that had not been served, and he looked at the Void. The Void was physically present. It was within the protected zone. It was obstructing egress.

Gerald opened the *Hearing* protocol. Not to argue. To *submit*.

*SUBMIT: VOLUNTARY COMPLIANCE PLAN*

The Void froze.

"What are you doing?" it gurgled.

"I am surrendering," Gerald said.

The System flashed.

[SUBMISSION ACCEPTED] [STATUS: UNDER REVIEW] [CONDITIONS: CORE MAY NOT USE OFFENSIVE MANA]

This was a trap. The Void knew it. If Gerald surrendered, the Void could take his mana. But Gerald had a clause.

He had filed a *Form 11-B: Unlicensed Biological Entity* on the rat in Chapter 1, and that form was still active. The rat was technically a *protected witness* in a pending administrative case.

And Gerald had just cited the Void for *Obstruction of Justice*.

If the Void attacked Gerald, it violated the *Witness Protection* clause of the active case.

The Void hesitated. It looked at the rat. The rat looked back, trembling, but standing on a patch of clean floor.

"If you strike me," Gerald said, "you strike the witness. And the Inspector General will audit your entire dungeon dimension."

The Void snarled. The green eyes burned.

"You bluff," the Void said. "You have no witness. The rat is a nuisance."

"The rat is a registered entity," Gerald said. "And I am the inspector. You are under observation."

The Void loomed over him, and the cold was unbearable. His vision blurred. The mana bar was empty.

He was running on residual adrenaline and stubbornness.

He looked at the mud. He looked at the rails.

He looked at the Void.

"I am not bluffing," Gerald said. "I am filing a report."

He pulled out his clipboard. He wrote.

He did not write a spell. He did not write a command. He wrote a description.

*REPORT: UNAUTHORIZED ENTITY OBSERVED IN RESTRICTED ZONE. NO IDENTIFICATION. NO PERMIT. WITNESS: RAT, SERIAL NUMBER 4921.*

He held the clipboard up.

The Void stared at the paper. It was just paper. But in the System, paper was law.

The Void could not move. If it moved, it broke the seal. If it broke the seal, it was liable.

But Gerald was dying. His body, the dungeon core, was fading. The walls were crumbling further. The void was pressing in from all sides.

He needed help. He needed someone to serve the citation. He needed a *Process Server*.

He looked at the ceiling.

A ventilation shaft was present.

Small. Dirty.

Non-compliant.

But it led to the sewer.

And the sewer was patrolled by the *Sanitation Guild*, and the Sanitation Guild had been fined for non-compliant plumbing in Chapter 89. They owed Gerald a favor. Or rather, they owed the System a fine.

Gerald had no mana to send a message.

But he had a *whistle*. A municipal safety whistle, used to signal evacuation.

He put it to his lips. He blew.

Nothing happened. No sound came out.

He had no air, and he had no lungs. He was a core.

But he had *intent*.

He focused on the whistle. He poured the last of his existence into it.

*EMIT: WARNING SIGNAL (CODE: RED)*

A faint, high-pitched tone pierced the dust. It was weak. Pathetic.

But it was heard.

From the ventilation shaft, a head popped up. A dwarf. Wearing yellow coveralls. Holding a wrench.

"Gerald?" the dwarf shouted. "Is it you? The System pinged us. Said we had a *Priority One Contamination Event*."

The dwarf looked at the Void. The Void looked at the dwarf.

The dwarf didn’t look at the Void. He looked at Gerald.

"You’re alive," the dwarf said. He looked relieved. "Thank god. The paperwork was going to be a nightmare."

"Serve it," Gerald said. His voice was barely a whisper.

The dwarf pulled a red stamp from his pocket. *CITATION: SANITATION*.

He marched forward, through the mud, through the debris, and slapped the stamp onto the Void’s chest.

*VIOLATION: BIOHAZARD UNCONTAINED.*

The Void shrieked. It was a sound of pure bureaucratic horror. It was not just an entity of destruction. It was now an *unsanitary condition*.

The System responded.

[DEBUFF APPLIED: PURGE PROTOCOL INITIATED] [TARGET: UNAUTHORIZED BIOHAZARD] [METHOD: VENTILATION]

The ventilation fans, which Gerald had barely installed in Chapter 5, kicked to life. Not because they were powerful, but because they were *compliant*, and the System used compliant systems to purge non-compliant entities.

A hurricane of air blasted into the sub-basement. It caught the Void. The mud held it, but the wind pulled at it. The Void was being sucked toward the shaft.

The dwarf grabbed Gerald’s clipboard. "I’m taking custody," he yelled. "You’re on administrative leave, Hutchins. Don’t touch anything."

The Void was dragged, screaming, into the ventilation shaft. It was small. It was weak. It was being flushed.

Gerald slumped against the wall. His mana was zero. He was dark. He was blind.

But he was still standing, and he felt the rat climb onto his shoulder. The rat nuzzled his neck.

Then, a new message appeared in the darkness.

It was not a System notification. It was a direct message. From an unknown sender.

*SENDER: INSPECTOR GENERAL* *SUBJECT: AUDIT NOTICE* *TEXT: You have resolved a Class-A Void breach using non-standard compliance methods.

This is unusual. I am coming to observe.

Prepare for Inspection.

Bring your forms. And Gerald? Stop citing the Void.

It’s cheating.*

Gerald’s core pulsed. He was not saved. He was in trouble.

But he had a new tool, and he looked at the dwarf, who was already writing up the incident report with practiced ease.

"Hey," Gerald said.

The dwarf looked up. "Yeah?"

"Did you charge them for the cleanup?"

The dwarf grinned, showing a missing tooth. "Double rate. Weekend surcharge."

Gerald smiled. It was a small, dry smile.

He closed his eyes.

And then the floor beneath him turned to glass.
