Chapter 387: Jump into Dark

# Chapter 387

The first arrow took the ice wall.

It splintered.

The second arrow took the vent cover. The metal shrieked and buckled inward, jamming the flap open.

Warm, humid air rushed in, mixing with the freezing mist.

The temperature lock held, but the physical breach did not. The ice was weeping, dripping onto the stone floor in rhythmic, damning puddles.

Gerald stared at the blank page, and he couldn't write. His fingers were numb. The pen was gone, lost in the scuffle with the first scout.

The footsteps grew louder. Heavy boots. Leather straps clinking. The sound of a dozen bodies moving in synchronized, aggressive rhythm.

Gerald looked at the melting puddle. It wasn't just water. It was thermal energy leaving his system. Every drop was a point of Compliance Rating bleeding out.

He couldn't fight them. Not without weapons. Not without health.

He could only inspect them.

But they weren't inside yet, and they were behind the door. The door was oak.

Solid oak.

Unfinished. No fire rating.

Gerald's eyes narrowed, and he pressed his hand against the oak. It was cold, but not frozen.

The ice wall was failing.

The structural integrity of the door relied on the frame, which relied on the masonry.

The masonry was expanding from the cold, but the ice was melting, reducing that expansion. The tension was wrong.

He pulled his hand back. His knuckles were white.

*Unfinished door.*

*No fire rating.*

*No egress signage.*

He didn't need to cite them for aggression. He could cite the door.

But a Citation required presence. The goblins were outside.

Unless.

Gerald looked at the jammed vent. The arrow had punctured the duct. Dust motes danced in the sliver of light from outside.

If he could get a piece of the door into the room, he could serve it.

But how?

The door handle turned. The wood groaned.

Gerald grabbed the loose vent cover. It was heavy steel. He dragged it to the center of the room, placing it directly in the path of the door's swing.

It was a stupid move. It wasn't code. It wasn't strategy. It was desperation.

The door burst open.

Three goblins flooded in. They were larger than the first. Iron skins.

Rusty swords. Their eyes glowed with the red hue of hostile Core control.

They didn't see the vent cover. They were looking at Gerald.

The first goblin charged. Its foot caught the edge of the steel plate. It stumbled, flailing, and slammed into the wall. The impact shook the room.

The second goblin leapt over the plate. Its sword swung at Gerald's head.

Gerald ducked. The blade whistled over his ear, shaving a lock of hair.

The third goblin hesitated. It looked at the fallen comrade, then at the vent, then at Gerald.

Gerald scrambled back, his back hitting the cold stone. He was trapped.

The leader goblin laughed. It was a wet, gargling sound. It raised its sword for the kill.

Gerald looked at the fallen goblin. The vent cover was wedged under its arm. A piece of the oak door was embedded in its shoulder, shattered from the impact of the wall.

A piece of the structure.

*Presence.*

Gerald didn't attack. He pointed.

"Violation," Gerald said. His voice was steady. Dry.

The goblin paused. Confused.

"Improper Egress," Gerald continued. "Blocking public thoroughfare. Penalty: Citation."

The System flashed.

**CITATION ISSUED.**

**TARGET: GOBLIN ALPHA.**

**VIOLATION: OBSTRUCTION OF EGRESS.**

**EVIDENCE: STRUCTURAL DEBRIS (DOOR FRAGMENT) ATTACHED TO ENTITY.**

The red symbol appeared. It wasn't on the goblin's head. It was on the door fragment stuck to its armor.

The debuff hit.

The goblin's movement speed plummeted. Its strength stat dropped by forty percent. It tried to lift its sword, but the arm hung limp, dead weight.

The other two goblins froze, and the System had recognized the debris as part of Gerald's dungeon infrastructure. By wearing it, the goblin had become a conduit for a violation within Gerald's jurisdiction.

Gerald gasped. Air. Sweet, warm air.

The lock was still active. He couldn't adjust the temperature. But he could adjust the *space*.

He looked at the jammed vent. The duct was narrow. Too narrow for a goblin.

He grabbed the loose steel plate. He kicked it toward the leader. It skidded across the floor, hitting the leader's shins. The goblin fell, pinned by its own debuffed strength and the weight of the plate.

The other two goblins looked at each other, and they were confused. Their Core wasn't issuing commands. The Citation had disrupted the local network.

Gerald didn't hesitate. He ran.

Not away. Toward the vent.

He crawled into the duct, and the metal was freezing. His skin burned against it. He dragged himself forward, elbows scraping, knees bruising.

The space was tight.

Claustrophobic.

He heard shouting behind him. The goblins were recovering. The leader was struggling to stand.

Gerald reached the end of the duct. It opened into the main corridor of the library sub-basement. The corridor was empty. Silent.

He pulled himself out. He stood up. He brushed dust from his suit.

He was safe.

For now.

But the temperature lock was still active.

The air in the corridor was warm. The ice in the room behind him would be gone within the hour. The goblins would come out.

They would be stronger. The Citation debuff was temporary.

It lasted ten minutes, and he had ten minutes.

Gerald looked down the long, dark hallway. His dungeon was a maze of compliance. Every turn was a potential violation. Every shadow was a potential inspection.

He needed to reinforce the room. He needed to seal the breach.

He walked toward the electrical panel. It was his weak point. His strong point.

The panel was mounted on a load-bearing wall. The wiring was messy. Exposed. A code nightmare.

But it was also his source of power, and he opened the panel. The breakers hummed. The lights flickered.

Gerald stared at the wires. Copper. Insulated. Grounded.

He couldn't fix the ice. He couldn't stop the warmth.

But he could change the environment, and he looked at the lighting controls. The dungeon's ambient light was controlled by the same grid that powered the ventilation fans.

If he cut the power to the lights, the fans stopped.

If the fans stopped, the air circulated less.

It didn't help. It made it worse.

He needed cold, and he needed heat exchange.

Gerald closed the panel. He looked at his notebook.

He had one page. He had no pen.

He looked at the goblin corpse lying in the hallway. It was melting. Fast.

He walked back to the corpse. He sat down next to it.

He watched it dissolve, and he watched the blood pool spread.

He thought about Form 11-B. Unlicensed Biological Entity.

He thought about thermal dynamics.

He thought about the Citation, and the Citation had been served to the door fragment.

What if he served it to something else?

Gerald looked at the water leak. The one that had been there since Chapter 1. It was dripping from a crack in the ceiling. A constant, annoying drip.

It was a violation. Poor drainage. Moisture accumulation. Mold risk.

But it was also a coolant.

Evaporation cools, and the leak was small. But the water was cold. Coming from the city mains.

Gerald stood up. He walked to the leak. He looked up.

The pipe was copper. Corroded.

He couldn't fix the leak. Not without tools.

But he could redirect it, and he looked at the floor. The water pooled there.

He looked at the goblin corpse. The sludge.

He looked at his own hands. Numb. Blue.

He had a new idea. It was terrible. It was inefficient. It was against his professional ethics.

But it was the only way, and he went back to the electrical panel. He didn't open it. He stood in front of it.

He looked at the junction box on the wall. The one that powered the room's lighting.

He pulled the cover off.

Inside, there were wires. Live wires.

He looked at the ice wall, which was gone. The room was bare stone.

The goblins were pushing the door open from the outside. The wood was cracking.

Gerald grabbed the live wire.

He didn't touch it. He used the tip of his pen. No, he didn't have the pen.

He used the edge of his notebook, and he wrapped the paper around his hand.

He reached into the junction box. He touched the live wire with the paper-wrapped finger.

The paper scorched. The heat traveled up his arm.

He didn't let go, and he dragged the wire. He pulled it out of the box. The insulation frayed. Sparks flew.

He ran to the leak.

He held the live wire above the dripping water.

The water hit the exposed copper.

The current surged.

The air crackled.

The water boiled.

Steam erupted.

The temperature dropped.

Not because of cooling. Because of the phase change. The energy required to turn the water into steam was pulled from the surrounding air.

The room froze.

Instantly.

The goblins bursting through the door were hit by a wall of superheated steam, followed by a sudden vacuum. The rapid expansion blew them back into the hallway.

The steam condensed on the cold stone walls. Frost formed. Thick, white, aggressive frost.

The temperature lock tried to hold. But the physical reality of the phase change was overriding the System's environmental control.

Gerald dropped the wire. The spark jumped to the floor.

The lights went out.

Darkness.

But the air was cold. Freezing.

Gerald sat in the dark. He listened.

The goblins were screaming. Not in pain.

In confusion, and the steam had scalded their eyes. The cold had stiffened their joints.

The Citation debuff on the leader had expired. But the new environmental debuff had taken its place.

**NEW VIOLATION DETECTED: IMMEDIATE HAZARD.**

**ACTION: CORE EMERGENCY SHUTDOWN.**

The System didn't wait for a Citation. It acted.

The lights didn't come back on. The ventilation fans stopped.

The room became a tomb.

Gerald breathed. He felt his lungs burn.

He had survived.

But he had caused a blackout, and the entire dungeon was offline. No lights.

No doors unlocking.

No spawn points active.

He was blind.

And he was alone, and he heard a sound. Not footsteps.

A tap.

On the ceiling.

Above his head.

Gerald froze, and the tap came again. Rhythmic. Deliberate.

*Tap. Tap. Tap.*

It wasn't a goblin. Goblins didn't tap.

It was mechanical, and he looked up into the darkness.

A red light blinked in the corner of the ceiling.

A camera.

Not one of his.

A System camera.

A small, blinking red dot.

It watched him.

Gerald stared at it, and he couldn't hide. He couldn't run. He was in the center of his own dungeon, in the dark, with a live wire at his feet.

The red light pulsed.

Then, a voice came from the speaker above. It wasn't the robotic System voice.

It was human.

Calm.

Professional.

"Inspector Hutchins."

Gerald's stomach dropped.

"Mr. Vane," Gerald said. His voice cracked. "I was handling an infestation."

"An infestation," the voice said. "By causing an electrical fire and triggering a thermal shock event."

"It was a necessary mitigation," Gerald said. "Code 7.3 permits emergency thermal regulation."

"Code 7.3," the voice said, "requires pre-approved equipment. You used a junction box."

Silence.

The red light blinked faster.

"You are being audited, Gerald," the voice said. "The Inspector General is on site."

Gerald looked around. The darkness seemed to press in.

"Where?" Gerald asked.

"Below," the voice said.

The floor beneath Gerald's feet vibrated.

Not a tremor.

A rumble.

The stone cracked.

A section of the floor sank.

Gerald scrambled back. He grabbed the doorframe.

The floor collapsed.

Not into a room.

Into a shaft.

A drop.

Gerald hung by his fingers.

Below him, in the dark, something moved, which was large. It was metallic. It had many legs.

It climbed up.

Gerald pulled himself up. He looked down.

The machine was rising. It had a camera. The same red light.

It wasn't an attack, which was a summons.

The voice from the speaker said, "We are coming up. Please remain calm. And do not attempt to cite this unit. It is not in your jurisdiction."

Gerald looked at the machine. It was huge. It was covered in compliance stickers.

Every bolt was labeled. Every wire was grounded.

It was perfect, which was terrifying.

And it was coming for him.

Gerald looked at the notebook in his hand.

He had no pen.

He had no allies, and he had a dungeon that was broken, freezing, and blind.

The machine reached the top of the shaft. It stopped. It hovered at eye level.

The red light scanned Gerald's face.

"State your current compliance status," the machine said.

Gerald looked at the machine. He looked at the dark corridor behind it.

He looked at his own hands. Trembling.

He didn't know what to say.

But he knew he had to say something.

Because if he didn't, the machine would audit him.

And if the machine audited him, it would find the ice. The live wire. The corpse.

The violations.

Gerald opened his mouth.

"Compliance Rating..." he started.

The machine tilted its head.

"Rating?" the machine asked.

Gerald looked at the notebook. The blank page.

He thought about the Citation. The goblin. The door.

He thought about the System, and he thought about his job.

He had spent his life measuring things. Checking things. Finding faults.

But he had never checked himself.

Not really.

The machine waited.

The red light pulsed.

Gerald realized, with a cold, sinking certainty, that he had been hiding something.

Something fundamental.

Something that violated the core code, and he looked at the machine.

"My rating," Gerald said, "is irrelevant."

The machine froze.

"Illogical statement," the machine said.

"It's not illogical," Gerald said. "It's a loophole."

The red light flared.

"Explain," the machine said.

Gerald looked at the dark corridor, and he had twenty-four hours left on the lock.

He had a machine that could audit him.

He had a blank page.

And he had a decision to make.

He could submit to the audit.

Or he could run.

But where could he run?

He looked at the machine's legs. They were hydraulic. Strong.

He looked at the live wire on the floor, and he looked at the leak.

He looked at the notebook.

He tore the page out.

He held it up.

"Violation," Gerald said.

The machine stopped moving.

"Violation?"

"Obstruction of inspection," Gerald said. "You are blocking the egress route. Penalty: Citation."

The red light flickered, and the machine did not fall.

It did not de-buff.

It laughed. A mechanical, grinding sound.

"Citation rejected," the machine said. "Target immunity: Inspector General Authority."

Gerald's heart stopped.

Immunity, and he had never considered it.

The System protected its inspectors.

He had just cited a god.

The machine extended a claw. It was sharp. Metal.

"Compliance failure," the machine said. "Core termination initiated."

Gerald stepped back, and he hit the wall.

There was nowhere to go.

The claw reached for his neck.

Gerald looked at the blank page.

He looked at the claw, and he looked at the System's red light.

He had one move left.

He had to change the definition of the dungeon.

He had to change the definition of *himself*.

He didn't have a pen.

But he had a live wire, and he grabbed the wire.

He didn't touch the claw.

He touched the machine's chassis.

The spark jumped.

The machine screamed.

Not in pain.

In feedback.

The red light exploded.

Darkness.

Gerald stood in the dark, and the machine was dead.

Or asleep.

He didn't know.

He walked to the edge of the shaft.

He looked down, and the drop was still there.

But something was at the bottom.

A light.

A single, white light.

And a voice.

Not the machine.

Not the System.

A human voice.

"Gerald?"

It was his wife, and he hadn't heard from her in three years.

"Gerald, can you hear me?"

Gerald looked down.

He had to go down.

He had to go into the dark, and he had to face the Inspector General.

He had to find out why she was there.

He looked at his hands. They were shaking.

He looked at the notebook, and he looked at the dark.

He stepped onto the edge.

And he jumped.
