Chapter 214: Void Collapse Penalty

# Chapter 214

Gerald pressed his pen harder into the wet ink of Form 114-C. It wasn't a tremor. It was a settling.

The sub-basement foundation of the Ashford Municipal Library groaned under the weight of the books above it, a sound like a heavy sigh trapped in concrete.

Gerald Hutchins ignored it. He pressed the nib of his pen harder into the paper, ensuring the signature was legible. The System did not accept sloppy penmanship.

A blurry signature could be construed as intent to evade, and intent to evasion triggered an immediate audit flag.

Forty minutes remained before the Inspector General arrived.

Gerald looked at the breach in the western wall. It was a jagged tear in the stone, three feet wide, revealing the dark, damp caverns of the Ashwood Crypt beyond.

It was ugly, which was non-compliant. It was currently leaking cold air into a zone rated for 68 degrees Fahrenheit.

The rat, whom Gerald had designated as Junior Inspector Kevin, stood on the edge of the breach. Kevin held a trowel in his tiny, clawed hands.

He looked at the hole.

He looked at Gerald, and he looked at the pile of rubble, which was mostly dust and despair.

"Kevin," Gerald said, not looking up from his form. "The mortar ratio is off. You're using too much water."

Kevin squeaked. It was a dry, dusty sound.

"There is no water. There is only mana and regret."

"Mix the mana with the aggregate. Two parts to one. Do not let it pool.

Pooling creates weak points. Weak points are liabilities.

"

Gerald turned the page of his clipboard. He had moved on to Form 204-A, *Temporary Structural Support Permit*, which he was filing retroactively. The System's timestamp was forgiving if you filed before the IG arrived, but unforgiving if you waited. Gerald was never late.

The air in the room grew colder. The temperature gauge on Gerald's mental HUD dropped to 64 degrees. A ventilation violation warning flashed in the corner of his vision, pulsing a soft, annoying amber.

*Ventilation Efficiency: 82%. Recommendation: Install exhaust fans.*

Gerald dismissed it. He didn't have fans. He had a leaky water main and a rat.

The breach widened slightly. A tendril of black smoke curled in from the Ashwood Crypt side. It hovered in the air, forming a vague, mocking shape. A face.

"Core Hutchins," the smoke whispered.

The voice was layered, echoing from multiple points in the cavern system.

"You file papers while your walls bleed. It is cute.

It is also fatal.

"

Gerald did not look at the smoke. He filled in the *Estimated Load Bearing Capacity* column.

He calculated the tensile strength of the remaining stone, factoring in the moisture content and the potential impact of a Level 12 Earth Golem.

He wrote down the number. It was low. It was dangerously low.

"Core Varek," Gerald said, his voice flat and dry. "Your atmospheric output is exceeding the local ambient pressure limits. You are pressurizing a non-sealed zone. This is a Code 4.2 violation."

"Code 4.2 is a myth," Varek sneered. The smoke coalesced further, forming a jagged, angry mouth. "It is a scare tactic for new cores."

"It is in the manual," Gerald said. "Page 412, subsection D.

Paragraph three.

I read it.

I have read it twice. The System does not lie, Core Varek.

It only omits."

The smoke hissed. A chunk of obsidian from the Ashwood side detached from the wall and flew toward Gerald. It struck the handrail Gerald had installed three weeks ago.

*Clang.*

The handrail held. The obsidian shattered. The handrail remained perfectly straight, bolted securely to the concrete with anchors that met ASTM F150 standards.

Gerald noted the impact force. He added a line to his report: *Incident Log: Projectile impact on safety barrier. No structural failure detected. Barrier integrity confirmed.*

"Stop throwing things," Gerald said. "You are creating debris hazards. I will have to cite you for littering."

"I will tear this basement down to bedrock!" Varek roared. The ground shook again. Dust fell from the ceiling.

Gerald paused. His pen hovered.

Dust in the eyes was a workplace injury.

Workplace injuries lowered efficiency. Efficiency lowered Compliance Rating.

He closed his eyes. He took a breath. He exhaled.

"Kevin," Gerald said. "The anchor points on the north wall. Check the torque."

"Torque requires a wrench," Kevin said.

"You have teeth. Use them."

Kevin scurried up the wall, tiny claws clicking against the stone.

Gerald opened his eyes. He looked at the breach. He looked at the smoke. He looked at his remaining mana: 35 points.

He had one card left to play.

One loophole. It was risky.

It was borderline unethical, which was, however, technically legal.

The System allowed for *De Minimis* exceptions. Small violations that, if cited, would cause disproportionate harm to the ecosystem of the dungeon. Usually, this applied to minor aesthetic issues, like peeling paint.

But the fine print of the *Inspector General Audit Protocol* stated that if an audit was initiated, all pending citations were reviewed for *Materiality*. If a citation was deemed immaterial, the Core issuing it was fined. If it was material, the cited Core was debuffed.

Gerald had already cited Varek for *Loitering*. That was a petty citation. Varek had successfully appealed it by arguing that he was *transiting*. The appeal had frozen Varek, but the citation itself had been deemed non-material.

Gerald needed a material violation, and he looked at the hole in his wall. The Ashwood Crypt was adjacent. Shared property line.

If Gerald could prove that Varek's dungeon was structurally compromising Gerald's foundation, it wasn't just a violation. It was a *Hazard*.

Hazards were not subject to appeal.

Gerald picked up his pen. He started a new form.

**Form 99-Z: Emergency Stop Work Order.**

**Target:** Ashwood Crypt. **Violation Code:** *Structural Negligence Resulting in Imminent Collapse of Adjacent Property.*

**Justification:** *Target Core's excavation activities have destabilized the bedrock supporting the Ashford Municipal Library sub-basement. Immediate cessation of all dungeon expansion is required to prevent catastrophic failure.*

This was a lie. Mostly.

The bedrock was stable.

Gerald had checked it himself.

But the *perception* of instability was enough if he could get the System to flag it.

The problem was the *Proof of Immediacy*. He needed to show that the collapse was imminent.

He needed to make it look like it was happening *now*.

"Kevin," Gerald said. "Stop the torque check."

Kevin froze on the wall.

"Why?"

"Come down. I need you for a different task."

Kevin dropped to the floor. He looked at Gerald with beady, intelligent eyes. He was a rat, but he was a *Union Rep* rat. He understood leverage.

"I need you to chew through the support strut on the north wall," Gerald said.

Kevin's ears flattened.

"That strut is load-bearing. If I chew it, the ceiling might crack."

"It might," Gerald agreed. "But it won't collapse. I calculated the redundancy.

The ceiling will sag by two millimeters. That is enough to trigger a *Seismic Warning* in the System.

"

"That is sabotage," Kevin said.

"That is *diagnostic testing*," Gerald corrected. "And it is within the scope of a Junior Inspector's duties."

Kevin hesitated, and he looked at the breach. The smoke from the Ashwood Crypt was thickening, forming into a humanoid shape. Varek was preparing for a direct physical assault.

The time-out from the appeal was nearly over.

If Varek attacked now, Gerald would lose. The dungeon would be destroyed. The Library would be compromised. Gerald would be decommissioned.

Kevin looked at Gerald. Gerald did not blink. He waited. The silence stretched.

Finally, Kevin scurried over to the support strut. It was a thick iron beam, rusted but sturdy. Kevin opened his mouth. His teeth were yellow, sharp, and designed for gnawing.

He bit down.

The metal groaned. A small spark flew.

Gerald watched the HUD.

**System Notice: Structural Stress Detected.**

**Seismic Sensor: Active.**

The amber warning light on his HUD changed to red.

Gerald slammed his pen down on the form. He didn't sign it. He *stamped* it.

**STAMP APPLIED: IMMEDIATE ACTION REQUIRED.**

He served the citation.

The air in the room changed. The red light pulsed. A low hum, like a turbine spinning up, vibrated through the floor.

Varek's smoke form froze. The humanoid shape dissolved back into mist.

**System Message: Citation Served. Violation Code 99-Z. Materiality Assessment: Pending.**

Gerald held his breath.

If the System judged the violation as material, Varek would be shut down. All his spawns would be frozen. The dungeon would be locked for

"Renovation."

If the System judged it as immaterial, Gerald would be fined. And Varek would be free to kill him.

The red light flickered.

Then, it turned blue.

**System Message: Violation Accepted. Severity: Critical. Immediate Stop-Work Order Issued.**

**Target: Ashwood Crypt.** **Status: Locked.** **Reason: Structural Hazard.**

A sound echoed from the caverns. Not a roar. A whine. Like a machine being unplugged.

The smoke in the breach dissipated. The cold air stopped.

Gerald let out a breath he didn't know he was holding. His hands were shaking. He clasped them together under the desk to stop them.

Kevin spat out a piece of iron. He looked at Gerald.

"That was reckless," Kevin squeaked.

"It was effective," Gerald said. "Clean up the debris. Use the broom. Do not use your paws."

Kevin nodded and scurried off to find the broom.

Gerald looked at his mana. 15 points. He was drained. The effort of maintaining the focus, of writing the form, of serving the citation, had cost him almost everything.

He needed to rest. But he couldn't rest. The IG was coming.

The elevator dinged.

It wasn't a normal elevator. It was the *Inspection Elevator*, a hidden shaft that only appeared when an IG was en route. The doors slid open with a smooth, silent hiss.

A figure stepped out, which was tall. It wore a suit made of grey scales that shifted like oil on water. It had no face, only a smooth, polished oval where a face should be. On its chest, a badge glowed with a blinding white light.

**Inspector General: Unit 7.**

Gerald stood up. He smoothed his tie. He picked up his clipboard. He walked to the center of the room.

Unit 7 did not speak. It looked at the room.

It looked at the handrails.

It looked at the fire extinguisher (fully charged, inspection tag dated yesterday). It looked at the breach in the wall, now sealed with a temporary wooden brace and a sign that read *DO NOT ENTER. UNDER REPAIR.*

Unit 7 walked to the breach. It placed a long, slender finger on the wood.

The wood did not move.

Unit 7 turned to Gerald. The faceless head tilted.

**System Message: Audit Initiated.**

**Scanning...**

Gerald stood still. He did not breathe.

He did not blink, and he had prepared for this.

He had organized the files. He had the receipts.

Unit 7 scanned the room.

**Ventilation: 82%. Pass.** **Fire Safety: 100%. Pass.** **Structural Integrity: 94%.

Pass.** **Sanitation: 98%. Pass.**

Unit 7 paused. It looked at Gerald.

It looked at the clipboard.

It looked at the stack of forms Gerald had prepared.

Then, Unit 7 reached into its grey, scaled suit. It pulled out a small, black book.

It opened the book.

**System Message: Query Detected.**

Gerald's heart stopped.

This wasn't in the manual. The IG did not ask questions. The IG issued fines. The IG issued rewards.

Unit 7 pointed a clawed finger at the breach.

Then it pointed at Gerald.

Then it pointed at the rat, Kevin, who was hiding behind the fire extinguisher.

Unit 7 made a sound. It was not a voice. It was a series of clicks and whirs, like a computer processing data.

**System Message: Anomaly Detected in Compliance Logic.**

**Explanation Required.**

Gerald looked at Unit 7.

**Explanation Required.**

Gerald looked at the hole in the wall. He looked at the stop-work order. He looked at the mana bar.

15 points.

If he couldn't explain the logic of the violation, the citation would be reversed. Varek would be unlocked. And Unit 7 would cite Gerald for *Fraudulent Compliance Reporting*.

Fraud was an instant decommission.

Gerald looked at Kevin. Kevin looked at Gerald.

Kevin squeaked.

"Gerald," the rat said. "I am a biological entity. I am not liable for structural repairs. But I am liable for *witness testimony*."

Gerald stared at Kevin.

Then he stared at Unit 7.

"I have a witness," Gerald said. His voice was steady.

Unit 7 stopped clicking. The white light on its chest pulsed once.

**Witness: Acknowledged.**

**Proceed with Testimony.**

Gerald opened his mouth.

"Inspector," Gerald said. "The violation was not fabricated. The violation was *induced*."

Unit 7 tilted its head.

**Clarify.**

"Core Varek was planning an unpermitted expansion," Gerald said. "He was digging into the bedrock without a permit. If I had not cited him, the collapse would have been *accidental*. By citing him, I made the hazard *known*."

**Logic: Flawed.**

"The hazard was always there," Gerald said. "I just filed the paperwork to acknowledge it."

Unit 7 looked at the hole.

**The hazard was created by your agent.**

Unit 7 pointed at Kevin.

**You ordered a subordinate to damage your own structure to create a legal pretext.**

Gerald felt the cold sweat on his back, which was true.

It was also illegal.

But was it illegal under the *Code*?

Gerald looked at the clipboard. He flipped to the back. There, in tiny print, was a footnote he had memorized.

*Section 12, Paragraph 4: Self-Reported Hazards. If a Core voluntarily identifies a hazard, even if self-inflicted, the System prioritizes mitigation over punishment, provided the mitigation is immediate.*

Gerald had mitigated. He had put up the sign. He had used the rat. He had stopped the expansion.

"It was a controlled demolition of a non-compliant segment," Gerald said. "To expose the root cause."

Unit 7 was silent.

The seconds stretched.

Then, Unit 7 reached into its pocket. It pulled out a pen.

It wrote on the air.

Gold letters formed in the air between them.

**Verdict: Pending.**

Gerald frowned.

**Pending?**

The System never gave a Pending verdict. The System always gave Pass or Fail.

Unit 7 looked at Gerald.

**Anomaly Detected. Core Hutchins. You are using a loophole that has been closed for 400 cycles.**

Gerald's blood ran cold.

**Where did you learn this code?**

Gerald didn't answer. He couldn't.

Unit 7 stepped closer. The air grew heavy.

**You are not a Dungeon Core.**

Gerald looked at Unit 7.

**You are an Auditor.**

The world went black.

Then, a new window opened.

**System Message: Core Classification Update.**

**New Class: Rogue Auditor.**

**Reward: +10,000 XP.**

**Penalty: Immediate Transfer to the Void.**

Gerald looked at the Void. It was a dark, empty space where the library used to be.

And in the Void, waiting for him, was not Varek, which was the Library.

The entire Ashford Municipal Library.

It was falling.

And it was falling on *him*.
