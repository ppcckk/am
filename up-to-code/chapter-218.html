Chapter 218: Falling Through

# Chapter 218

The Auditor Prime did not blink. Her eyelids had been removed during her initial calibration, a cosmetic choice that made the stare permanent and uncomfortably dry.

Gerald stared at the broken seal of the subpoena. It sat on the laminate surface, cracked down the middle. A piece of red wax, no larger than a thumbnail, had chipped off and rolled into the crease of the desk.

He placed his index finger on the crack.

The Auditor's pen hovered. Ink dripped. A black teardrop fell onto the white paper, blooming into a stain that looked suspiciously like a Rorschach test for bureaucratic failure.

"Your motion lacks standing," the Auditor said. Her voice sounded like a printer jamming.

"You are a Class-4 Core.

I am the Inspector General's delegate. You do not have the jurisdiction to file a motion to reopen a sealed case.

"

Gerald did not look up, and he was looking at the grain of the laminate. It was a standard-issue municipal desk.

Particleboard with a melamine finish.

Cheap. Flammable.

"Subsection 14, Paragraph C," Gerald said. He pointed at the stain. "Alteration of official record during active proceedings. You are contaminating the evidentiary trail."

The Auditor paused. The pen stopped moving.

"It is ink," she said.

"It is a violation of document integrity," Gerald corrected. He tapped the desk.

"This desk is not fire-rated.

It is not rated for high-traffic filing.

If you are going to conduct an appeal, you must do so on a compliant surface. Otherwise, the entire hearing is void.

"

The Auditor's head tilted. The movement was jerky, mechanical.

"You are citing me for furniture?"

"I am citing you for procedural negligence," Gerald said. "If you continue to write on a non-compliant medium, I will be forced to request a recess. And you know what happens during a recess."

The Auditor's eyes narrowed. The timer in the corner of Gerald's vision, now invisible but felt like a pressure behind his eyes, ticked down.

Four minutes, and she looked at the desk. Then at Gerald.

"You think you can stall me with code?"

"I think," Gerald said, picking up the chipped wax, "that you are unprepared. I am prepared. I have inspected this room.

It has no emergency exit signage on the north wall. The ventilation is insufficient for the number of entities present.

And your desk is a fire hazard.

I am filing a Citation of Immediate Hazard. Right now.

"

He didn't have a form. He didn't have a stamp. But he had the Core.

He pushed his will into the air.

The air shimmered. A red holographic text box appeared in the space between them.

**VIOLATION: UNSAFE WORKING CONDITIONS.** **TARGET: INSPECTOR GENERAL DELEGATE.** **PENALTY: STATIONARY LOCKDOWN.**

The Auditor laughed. It was a harsh, scraping sound.

"You cannot cite an Auditor. We are above the code."

"Nobody is above the code," Gerald said.

"Code is the foundation. Without it, reality collapses.

You are part of the system, and the system is built on rules. You are breaking them.

"

The red box flared.

The Auditor stiffened. Her feet, planted on the dark floor, did not move. She tried to step forward, but her boots slid an inch, then stopped.

Friction increased. The floor beneath her had changed.

The smooth, obsidian-like surface of the void dungeon had roughened.

Grit had appeared.

Sandpaper texture.

"What have you done?" she hissed.

"I improved traction," Gerald said. "Safety requirement for high-risk zones. You are trying to write a judgment while standing on a slippery surface.

That is an OSHA violation. Well, the Dungeon OSHA.

But the principle holds.

"

She struggled. Her legs trembled. The friction was immense.

She could not walk, and she could not run.

And she could not leave.

Gerald checked his mental clock. Three minutes and forty-five seconds.

He had bought himself time, but he had trapped himself with her.

He needed to fix the dungeon, and he needed to fix the Auditor.

He needed to fix everything.

Gerald closed his eyes.

He reached into the deep sub-basement of his mind, where the blueprints lived.

The red lines were gone. The black line to the Auditor was still there, vibrating.

But there were other lines.

Faint, ghostly outlines of the dungeon he had been building. The one with the handrails. The one with the proper ventilation shafts. The one that had a Compliance Rating of 47.

He pulled those lines into the present.

The darkness around them shuddered.

Concrete pillars erupted from the floor. Steel beams groaned into existence, welding themselves together with sparks that flew harmlessly in the void.

The Auditor screamed as a beam materialized inches from her left ear.

"Cease construction!" she shouted. "You are interfering with a divine process!"

"I am upgrading the facility," Gerald said.

He opened his eyes. He looked tired. His face was gray, the skin hanging loose.

The cost of manifesting matter was draining his Core energy rapidly.

He walked past her. He didn't run. He measured his steps.

One foot. Two feet.

He walked to the north wall.

There was a door there. It was a simple wooden door. It had a sign on it.

*EXIT*.

But the sign was crooked.

Gerald stopped. He looked at the sign.

"The exit sign is not illuminated," he said.

The Auditor was trying to free herself.

Her hands were clawing at the air, but the System was holding her down.

The Citation was working. It wasn't stopping her magic, but it was stopping her movement.

And it was distracting her.

"That is irrelevant," she spat.

"It is not irrelevant," Gerald said. He pulled a wrench from his inventory. A standard 10mm socket wrench. He had carried it since Chapter 1.

He reached up. He tightened the screws on the exit sign.

Click.

Click.

Click.

The sign lit up. Green. Bright. Compliant.

The dungeon's ambient light shifted. The oppressive gloom lifted slightly.

**COMPLIANCE RATING INCREASED.**

**+2 VENTILATION.** **+1 LIGHTING.** **+5 EGRESS SAFETY.**

The red holographic box above the Auditor flickered.

**HAZARD MITIGATED.** **CITATION VOIDED DUE TO IMMEDIATE CORRECTION.**

The sandpaper texture on the floor vanished.

The Auditor lunged, and she was fast. Faster than Gerald expected.

She crossed the distance in two strides, her hand outstretched, fingers sharp like quill pens.

Gerald did not dodge. He did not have the stamina.

He turned his head.

The hand caught his shoulder.

Pain, white and hot, exploded through his right side.

The Auditor grabbed his core shell.

Gerald gasped.

His inventory menu flashed in his mind.

**DAMAGE DETECTED.** **CORE INTEGRITY: 84%.**

"You are slow," the Auditor whispered. "You are a bureaucrat. You are not a warrior."

She squeezed. The metal of his shell groaned.

Gerald gritted his teeth. He felt the pressure. It was like being crushed by a filing cabinet.

But he was looking at her hand.

Where her fingers touched his shell, a small spark jumped.

Static electricity.

Gerald remembered the blueprint.

*Chapter 114: Electrical Grounding.*

He had installed a grounding wire in the foundation. It was connected to the earth. Deep earth. The bedrock of the library.

He had forgotten to disconnect it, and the Auditor was not just touching him. She was touching a grounded conductor.

And she was using magic.

Magic is energy.

Energy seeks ground.

"Gerald," the Auditor said, smiling. "Let us see how much you can endure."

She poured power into her grip.

Gerald watched the spark.

It grew.

It arced from his shoulder to her wrist.

Then to her elbow.

Then up her arm.

The Auditor's smile froze.

Her eyes widened.

"Is that..."

"Lightning resistance," Gerald said through gritted teeth. "I have lightning resistance. You do not."

He had spent three weeks installing copper grounding straps in the walls of his dungeon. He had done it because the System fined him for 'Improper Electrical Infrastructure.'

He had done it because he hated fines.

Now, it was a weapon.

The electricity surged through the Auditor, and she shrieked. A high-pitched, digital screech.

Her form flickered. Glitching.

She pulled her hand back, clutching her arm. Smoke rose from her sleeve.

"System Error," she hissed. "System Error."

Gerald stumbled back. He held his shoulder. His arm hung limp, numb.

**STATUS: PARALYZED (TEMPORARY).**

He ignored it, and he looked at the door.

The exit sign was still glowing.

The timer was gone.

The dungeon was stable.

The Auditor was on her knees, and she looked up at him. Her face was cracked. Digital artifacts swirled around her eyes.

"This is not over," she said.

"It never is," Gerald said. He picked up his clipboard. It was empty. He wrote.

**NOTICE OF APPEAL DENIED.** **REASON: TARGET VIOLATED SAFETY PROTOCOLS DURING HEARING.** **PENALTY: REVOKED AUTHORITY TO ENTER THIS ZONE.**

He wrote it on the back of the subpoena, and he held it up.

"Serve," he said.

The paper flew from his hand. It hit the Auditor's chest.

She screamed.

Not in pain. In frustration.

The System recognized the document.

The Auditor dissolved, and she shattered into a million pixels, scattering into the air like confetti.

The dungeon fell silent.

Gerald slumped against a concrete pillar.

He waited for the timer to reappear.

It didn't.

Instead, a new message popped up in his vision.

**INSPECTOR GENERAL AUDIT SCHEDULED.** **DATE: TOMORROW.** **PREPARATION: MANDATORY.**

Gerald closed his eyes, and he was alone.

His arm was numb.

His core was at 84%.

And somewhere in the dark, a rat was chewing on a wire.

He needed to fix the wire, and he needed to fix the dungeon.

He needed to survive the audit.

He pulled out a pencil.

He sharpened it against the concrete.

The shavings fell to the floor.

White. Clean.

Compliant.

***

Three miles above the dungeon, in the real world, the Ashford Municipal Library was quiet.

The janitor, Sarah, pushed her mop bucket down the hallway. She was tired. It was 11:30 PM. Her shift ended at midnight.

She passed the sub-basement door, which was locked. It had always been locked.

But today, there was a smell.

Not mildew. Not rot.

Ozone.

And something else.

Paper. Freshly cut paper.

Sarah stopped. She leaned against the door.

She could hear a sound.

Click. Click. Click.

Like a stamp, and she frowned. She pressed her ear to the wood.

The clicking stopped.

Then, a voice.

Muffled. Distant. But clear.

"Next item," the voice said. "Load-bearing wall. Check for termite damage."

Sarah pulled away, and she looked at the door.

The lock was new. A heavy, industrial padlock. It hadn't been there yesterday.

She walked back to her cart, and she pulled out her phone.

She dialed 911.

"911, what is your emergency?"

"There's a man in the library basement," Sarah said. "He's talking to himself. And... I think he's building walls."

"Sir, the library is closed. There is no one inside."

"I heard him," Sarah said. "He said something about a citation."

The dispatcher sighed.

"Ma'am, if you hear noises, it could be the pipes. The old building settles."

"It sounded like a stamp," Sarah said.

There was a long pause on the line.

"Is there anyone else there?" the dispatcher asked.

"No," Sarah said.

"Okay. We'll send a patrol car. Just to check."

Sarah hung up, and she looked at the door again.

The wood was warm.

She touched it.

Hot.

She pulled her hand back.

A small burn mark was on her finger, and she ran.

***

Back in the dungeon, Gerald stood before his blueprint.

The Auditor was gone.

But the cost remained.

His arm was still numb.

And his dungeon had changed, and the concrete pillars were real. They were not holograms. They were physical matter, pulled from the earth and shaped by his will.

The energy cost had been immense.

He checked his Core Energy.

12%.

He was almost empty.

If he fought now, he would fail.

If he slept now, he might not wake up, and he had to rest.

But the System had given him a new problem.

The Inspector General was coming tomorrow.

And Gerald knew what the Inspector General looked like.

He had seen the file.

Chapter 1, Page 4.

*Inspector General: Entity 001. Formerly Gerald Hutchins.*

Wait.

No.

That wasn't right.

Gerald frowned. He went back to the file.

*Inspector General: Entity 001. Formerly Arthur Penhaligon.*

Arthur Penhaligon. The previous City Inspector.

Arthur had disappeared in 2015.

Gerald had taken his job.

Gerald had taken his desk.

Gerald had taken his files.

And now, the Inspector General was Arthur?

Gerald looked at the black line in his mind.

It wasn't vibrating anymore, which was pulsing.

Slowly.

Like a heartbeat.

It led to the exit portal.

But the exit was gone.

The only way out was through the dungeon.

Through the monsters.

Through the traps.

Through the code.

Gerald sat down on the floor, and he pulled out his clipboard.

He opened it to a fresh page.

He wrote:

**TO: INSPECTOR GENERAL.** **FROM: GERALD HUTCHINS, CORE ID 404.** **RE: PRE-AUDIT COMPLIANCE REVIEW.**

He wrote the checklist.

*1. Fire Suppression: Installed.* *2.

Egress: Verified.* *3.

Ventilation: Upgraded.* *4. Electrical: Grounded.* *5. Structural: Reinforced.*

*6. Personnel: One rat. One leak. One broken arm.*

He paused, and he added:

*7. Threat Assessment: High.*

*8. Countermeasures: Active.*

He underlined 'Active'.

A new notification popped up.

**CITATION ISSUED: SELF.** **REASON: UNAUTHORIZED MODIFICATION OF CORE STRUCTURE.** **FINE: 500 MANA.**

Gerald laughed, which was a dry, rasping sound.

He couldn't pay the fine.

He had 12% mana.

He had 500 mana worth of fine.

He was bankrupt.

Unless.., and he looked at the wall.

The wall where the Auditor had been.

The wall where the lightning had arced.

There was a scorch mark.

But beneath the scorch mark, there was something else.

A seam.

A hidden door.

It wasn't in his blueprint.

It wasn't in the System's blueprint, which was old.

It was from the library's original construction.

Gerald crawled toward it.

His numb arm dragged behind him.

He touched the seam, which was cold.

He pushed.

The door didn't open.

But a voice came from the other side.

It was not the Auditor, which was not the System.

It was a human voice.

Tired.

Resigned.

"Hutchins?" the voice said. "Is that you?"

Gerald froze.

"Arthur?" he whispered.

The handle turned.

The door opened.

And Gerald fell through.
