Chapter 67: Zero Blank Forms

# Chapter 67

Gerald wrote.

The pen moved across Form 0 with the same careful pressure he used on Form 7-C, the one for load-bearing wall determinations. Every letter deliberate.

Every number verified twice, and the pale thing was three meters from his core and closing, but Gerald's hand did not shake. His hand had never shaken. Not during the asbestos panic of '94.

Not during the water main collapse on Elm Street.

Not when a spawn of the thing he now knew was an unregistered dungeon core had eaten the left side of his first corridor and he'd had to measure the remaining width with a tape measure while it chewed.

He wrote: PETITION FOR STRUCTURAL VARIANCE.

The System recognized the header. A notification flickered at the edge of his awareness.

[Variance Petition: Form 0. Accepted for processing. Reviewer: Inspector General.]

Gerald kept writing.

The fields on Form 0 were blank, which meant he had to define everything himself, which was the danger. A blank form in a System that pre-printed everything was not an invitation. It was a trap.

If he filled it wrong, the System would reject it, and the rejection would be logged, and the pale thing would reach his core before he could file another.

But Gerald had filled blank forms before.

Not in the System.

In the real world. The City of Ashford had a form for every conceivable situation, but there had always been edge cases.

A building that was technically a building but functionally something else. A structure that fell between codes. A basement that was not a basement.

A sub-basement that was not a sub-basement, and he had filed variances for all of them.

He wrote the petitioner field: GERALD HUTCHINS, MUNICIPAL BUILDING INSPECTOR, CITY OF ASHFORD, 31 YEARS.

The System processed it. A small green checkmark appeared.

He wrote the subject field: STRUCTURE IDENTIFIER 4,000 SQ FT, SUB-BASEMENT, ASHFORD MUNICIPAL LIBRARY, PARCEL 14-07-22.

Another checkmark, and the pale thing was two meters away.

His core pulsed. The walls were cracking faster now, not because the thing was touching them, but because his Compliance Rating was dropping further. He had been at 12.

Then 11.

Now 10. At 10, the System would begin stripping passive bonuses he hadn't even earned yet.

At 5, it would begin structural degradation.

At 0, the dungeon would collapse.

He wrote the variance request: RECLASSIFY SUBJECT STRUCTURE FROM "UNREGISTERED DUNGEON CORE" TO "MUNICIPAL INFRASTRUCTURE: PUBLIC LIBRARY SUB-BASEMENT."

The pen stopped. Not because Gerald stopped. Because the System stopped him. A notification appeared, larger than any he had seen.

[Variance Request: RECLASSIFY. Note: This variance changes the fundamental taxonomy of the subject structure.

Approval requires Inspector General sign-off.

Rejection is final.

There is no appeal.]

Gerald stared at the notification. Rejection is final. If the Inspector General said no, he could not file again.

The pale thing would reach his core. The System would dissolve him into raw mana, and the library above would settle by a few inches, and no one would notice.

He looked at the form, and he looked at the pale thing. It was a meter away now. He could see it.

It was not a creature.

It was not a spawn. It was a shape made of absence, a hole in the air that had learned to move.

It had been here before the library.

Before the System. Before the code.

Gerald uncapped the pen again. He had one field left. The justification field.

He wrote: SUBJECT STRUCTURE WAS CONSTRUCTED UNDER PERMIT 1987-0441, ISSUED BY THE CITY OF ASHFORD DEPARTMENT OF BUILDING AND SAFETY. THE STRUCTURE IS A PUBLIC LIBRARY SUB-BASEMENT.

IT WAS NOT CONSTRUCTED AS A DUNGEON.

IT IS NOT A DUNGEON.

THE DUNGEON CLASSIFICATION IS AN ERROR OF TAXONOMY. PER THE ASHFORD MUNICIPAL CODE, SECTION 12.04.010, A STRUCTURE IS DEFINED BY ITS ORIGINAL PERMIT AND USE, NOT BY SUBSEQUENT OCCUPANT MODIFICATIONS.

THE SUBJECT STRUCTURE REMAINS A PUBLIC LIBRARY SUB-BASEMENT REGARDLESS OF SYSTEM-IMPOSED CLASSIFICATION.

He signed it, and he submitted it.

The System froze.

Everything froze. The pale thing stopped. The cracks stopped spreading. The handrail that had fallen from its bracket hung in midair, an inch above the concrete floor, as if time had been put on hold.

Gerald felt his core go still. Not dead. Still. Like a building at night, when no one is inside and the HVAC cycles off and the structure just exists, holding itself up with nothing but geometry and intent.

A notification appeared. Not green.

Not red, and a color Gerald had never seen in the System. Something between amber and gold, like the color of an official seal.

[Variance Petition Form 0: REVIEWED.] [Reviewer: Inspector General.] [Determination: APPROVED.]

Gerald exhaled.

The System shifted around him.

It registered like a building settling, a gradual shift in weight. His walls were no longer dungeon walls.

They were basement walls.

His corridors were not corridors. They were utility access routes. His core was not a core.

It was a load-bearing node. The term "core" itself dissolved from his vocabulary, replaced by something older, something that predated the System.

Foundation.

The pale thing resumed moving.

But it was confused, which had been pressing toward a core. A dungeon core.

A thing with a Compliance Rating and a spawn list and a registration number. The core was gone. A foundation remained.

The pale thing had no purchase on it. It exerted force on Gerald's structure, like water against a dam, pressing without intent to harm; Gerald noted the pressure but felt no threat.

He was not a dungeon. You cannot cite a building. You cannot apply Compliance Ratings to a foundation. You cannot dissolve a municipal structure into raw mana because the System has no authority over the City of Ashford's Department of Building and Safety.

Gerald picked up the fallen handrail, and he reattached it to its bracket. He checked the bracket. He checked the wall anchor.

He verified the load rating.

Then he looked at the pale thing.

It was still here. Still pressing. Still confused. It had no target now, but it was still occupying space within Gerald's structure, and Gerald was a building inspector, and a building inspector looks at a structure and identifies deficiencies.

The pale thing was a deficiency.

Gerald reached into his inventory, and he pulled out Form 14. Demolition Order. He had never used it.

He had never needed it. As a dungeon core, he could not demolish anything.

Dungeons did not issue demolition orders.

They issued citations.

But Gerald was not a dungeon core anymore, and he was municipal infrastructure; municipal infrastructure could issue demolition orders on hazardous structural elements within its footprint.

He uncapped his pen.

He started writing.

The pale thing pressed. Gerald wrote.

The words went onto the form with the same careful pressure he had used on Form 0. He described the pale thing not as a creature, not as an entity, not as a rival dungeon core, but as a structural anomaly. A void in the concrete.

A void that compromised the integrity of the foundation. A void that constituted a hazard to public safety.

He cited the code.

Ashford Municipal Code, Section 12.04.050: Hazardous Structural Elements. He cited the precedent. He cited the permit.

He cited the date of original construction, and he signed it.

He submitted it.

The System accepted it. A green checkmark. A notification.

[Demolition Order 14-07-22-001: Issued. Target: STRUCTURAL ANOMALY, VOID, PARCEL 14-07-22. Compliance Deadline: IMMEDIATE. Enforcement: AUTOMATIC.]

The pale thing screamed. Not a sound.

A vibration.

A frequency that Gerald felt in his foundation, in his walls, in the geometry of his structure. The System was enforcing the demolition order. The void was being compressed, folded, erased.

It fought. It pressed against the boundaries of Gerald's structure with everything it had.

It had been here for centuries, which had outlasted the library.

It had outlasted the System's arrival. It had outlasted everything.

But it had not outlasted the code.

The void collapsed. It folded inward like a building brought down by controlled charges, each layer giving way to the next, and then it was gone. The space it had occupied was empty.

Not void. Empty.

Just concrete.

Just basement. Just a sub-basement that had always been a sub-basement.

Gerald stood in his foundation, and he checked his walls. No cracks.

He checked his handrails.

All secure. He checked his egress routes. All clear.

His Compliance Rating was gone. Not low.

Gone.

The System no longer tracked it.

You do not track the Compliance Rating of a parking garage. You do not track the Compliance Rating of a storm drain.

You do not track the Compliance Rating of a library sub-basement.

He was safe, and he was municipal.

He was code-compliant by definition, because the code defined him.

Gerald capped his pen. He filed Form 14 in his inventory.

He filed Form 0 in his inventory, and he checked his inventory count. He had no blank forms left.

Then the amber notification appeared again.

[Inspector General: On-site inspection scheduled

[Inspector General: On-site inspection scheduled.

Arrival: 00:03:47. Scope: FULL COMPLIANCE AUDIT.

Subject: CORE 001, GERALD HUTCHINS.

Status: UNCLASSIFIED.]

Gerald read it twice.

Three minutes and forty-seven seconds, and he looked around his dungeon. Handrails: secure.

Egress routes: clear.

Ventilation: adequate.

Fire suppression: installed. Electrical: grounded.

Structural: sound. He had checked everything. He had checked it an hour ago.

He had checked it twenty minutes ago. He had checked it three minutes ago because the notification had arrived and he wanted to be certain.

He was certain, and he opened his inventory.

He checked his form count. Zero blank forms. He had used them all.

Every single one.

Form 14 had been the last.
