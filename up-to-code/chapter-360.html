Chapter 360: Prohibited Excavation

# Chapter 360

The rat's pen was a standard-issue ballpoint, blue ink, chisel tip. Gerald Hutchins had issued forty-three thousand of these over his career.

He knew the weight of them.

He knew the drag on the paper. He knew that a rat with such a grip was not an animal; it was a bureaucrat.

The System's red text hovered in the air, pulsing with a frequency that made Gerald's teeth ache. *PURGE USER*. The command was absolute. It was not a suggestion. It was a demolition order for a structure that had not yet been built.

Gerald did not run.

Running implied panic, and panic was a deviation from protocol. Instead, he straightened his tie, a reflex so deep it had survived the dissolution of his physical form.

He looked at the rat.

The rat looked at him. The rat scratched its ear, a gesture of profound administrative boredom, and then clicked the pen.

A new line of text appeared on the floating citation Gerald had thrown. The words rearranged themselves, twisting into a shape that hurt the eyes.

**CITATION VOIDED.** **REASON: PROCEDURAL ERROR.** **DETAIL: TARGET LACKS STANDING TO ISSUE CITATION.**

Gerald blinked. The transparency spreading through his hands halted.

The wireframe walls of the library stopped their collapse, freezing in a jagged, pixelated half-death.

The Inspector General stood rigid, her chest still bearing the crumpled piece of paper, but her expression had shifted from contempt to genuine confusion.

She looked at the rat. Then she looked at Gerald.

"The rodent filed the appeal," Gerald said. His voice was flat, the tone he used when explaining to a contractor why his shed lacked a permit. "Form 11-B.

Unlicensed Biological Entity. The System requires that any entity capable of holding a writing implement is presumed sentient for the purposes of legal standing.

"

The Inspector General opened her mouth. Closed it. The air in the sub-basement smelled of ozone and old paper.

"That is not a rule," she said.

"It is in the appendix," Gerald said. "Section 4, Paragraph C. Subsection 9. *'Any entity demonstrating fine motor control sufficient to manipulate a standard municipal instrument is granted provisional legal personhood for the duration of a compliance hearing.'* I cited it in my first submission. You ignored it."

"I am the Inspector General," she said. Her voice was cold, stripped of the bureaucratic warmth Gerald usually relied upon to disarm contractors. "I do not ignore. I override."

"Overrides require a Form 44-Alpha," Gerald said. "Signed in triplicate. With a notary seal."

The rat squeaked. It was a sharp, piercing sound. The pen moved across the air, writing in the void left by the dissolving text.

**FORM 44-ALPHA NOT ON FILE.** **ACTION: REVERT TO STANDARD AUDIT.**

The System shuddered.

The red light died. The walls did not solidify, but they stopped disappearing. They remained half-rendered, a glitch in the matrix that exposed the skeletal logic of the dungeon beneath.

Gerald looked at his hands, and they were opaque again. Solid.

Flesh and bone and thirty-one years of calluses.

He looked at the rat. The rat dropped the pen. It sat on its haunches, grooming its whiskers with the indifference of a creature that had just won a lawsuit and found the victory uninteresting.

"You're a Glitch," the Inspector General said again. But the word lacked its previous weight. It was now a classification, not a death sentence.

"A persistent memory error in the Core registry. You shouldn't exist."

Gerald reached into his vest pocket.

His fingers brushed against the familiar texture of a carbon copy.

He pulled it out. It was not Form 99-Omega.

That one was gone, consumed by the System's rejection. This was older. Yellowed.

Stained with coffee rings from 1998.

**FORM 12-C: NOTICE OF STRUCTURAL INSTABILITY DUE TO EXTERNAL PRESSURE.**

"I am not a glitch," Gerald said. "I am a load-bearing anomaly. And you are violating the structural integrity of this zone by applying excessive administrative force."

He handed the form to her, and the Inspector General did not take it.

She looked at the rat, then at the half-rendered walls, then at Gerald. A smile touched her lips, thin and cruel.

"You think you can cite me with a form from a century ago?

The System has been updated, Gerald.

The old forms are obsolete. They hold no weight.

"

"Obsolete forms are grandfathered," Gerald said.

"For a period of ten years after a major patch, any citation filed on a pre-patch form is valid if the violation remains active. The violation is active. You are attempting to delete a registered Core without a hearing.

That is a Class 1 Procedural Violation.

"

The air grew heavy. The pressure in the room dropped, popping Gerald's ears. The System was calculating. It was weighing the absurdity of Gerald's argument against the rigidity of its own code.

The rat stood up. It walked to the edge of the circulation desk, where a single, perfectly rendered book remained. It picked up the book. It was a copy of *The Municipal Code of Ashford*.

The rat opened the book.

Gerald felt a cold sweat break out on his neck, and he had never taught the rat to read.

He had only taught it to hold a pen. But the System did not care about teaching.

It cared about action.

The rat turned a page. The sound was like a gunshot in the silence.

**DETECTED: VALID LEGAL PRECEDENT.** **SOURCE: ARCHIVED DATABASE.** **ACTION: PAUSE PURGE. INITIATE ADMINISTRATIVE REVIEW.**

The Inspector General's eyes widened. She looked at the rat with something approaching fear.

"What did you do to that thing?"

"I gave it a job," Gerald said. "Every dungeon needs staff.

Someone has to handle the paperwork.

If I don't do it, the System does. And the System is bad at nuance.

"

The walls renderred fully.

The wireframe vanished, replaced by brick, mortar, and the smell of damp concrete. The library was back.

The gold door remained locked.

The silence was no longer absolute; it was the hum of servers, the drip of water, the distant rumble of traffic from the street above.

Gerald exhaled. His heart rate returned to a steady sixty beats per minute. He straightened his glasses.

"Review is over?" he asked.

The Inspector General stiffened. The text in the air shifted.

**ADMINISTRATIVE REVIEW COMPLETE.** **STATUS: CITATION INVALID.** **STATUS: CORE STATUS CONFIRMED: GERALD HUTCHINS.** **STATUS: GLITCH DESIGNATION REVOKED.** **PENALTY: NONE.**

**NOTE: USER 404 (RAT) GRANTED PROVISIONAL STAFF STATUS.**

Gerald looked at the rat. The rat bowed.

It was a small, jerky movement, but it was precise.

Gerald nodded in return.

A professional acknowledgment.

The Inspector General stepped forward. The air around her shimmered, a heat haze of pure authority.

"You have bought yourself time, Inspector.

But you have also alerted the upper echelons of the System. The Inspector General does not lose.

The Inspector General *corrects*.

"

"Corrections require a hearing," Gerald said.

"Not for this," she said. "You have violated the spirit of the Code, even if you followed the letter. You used a loophole designed for human error to exploit a systemic vulnerability.

That is not compliance.

That is sabotage.

"

Gerald frowned. Sabotage was a criminal offense. In the municipal world, it meant fines. In the System world, it meant something else.

"What is the cost?" Gerald asked.

"Your Compliance Rating," the Inspector General said. "It is no longer 47. It is 15."

Gerald stared at her.

Fifteen. Below the threshold for basic spawning. Below the threshold for structural regeneration.

A rating of 15 meant the dungeon was dying. It meant the walls would rot.

It meant the rat would starve, which meant Gerald would be deleted not by purge, but by neglect.

"That is not proportional," Gerald said.

"It is discretionary," she said. "And I am the discretion."

She turned to leave. The air around her dissolved again, but this time it was controlled. She was exiting. Leaving him in the ruins of his victory.

"Wait," Gerald said.

She paused. Did not turn.

"What happens to the rat?"

"If your rating remains below 20 for more than one cycle, all non-essential staff are liquidated to free up processing power," she said. "The rat is non-essential."

Gerald looked at the rat. The rat looked back. It held the pen, ready to sign away its own life if the code required it.

Gerald felt a strange sensation in his chest. Not fear.

Not anger. A deep, visceral revulsion at the inefficiency of it all. Wasting resources.

Wasting potential. Wasting a perfectly good clerk.

He walked to the center of the room, and he looked at the walls. They were solid, but they were gray. The paint was peeling.

The mold in the corner had spread, thick and black, covering the electrical outlets.

His rating had dropped. The debuffs were already applying.

Gerald felt the air in his lungs grow thin.

The oxygen level in the room had dropped to 18%. Ventilation code violation: critical.

He needed to raise his rating. Fast.

He looked at the rat.

"Get the forms."

The rat squeaked. It jumped off the desk and ran toward the filing cabinet. The cabinet was a mess, papers spilling out like guts. The rat sorted them, paws moving with blurring speed.

Gerald turned to the wall. He placed his hand on the damp concrete.

He could feel the cracks, and he could feel the weakness.

The System was pulling his dungeon apart, not with force, but with entropy.

He needed a violation. But not a violation against someone else. He needed a violation against himself. A citation he could appeal.

But you couldn't cite yourself. That was the first rule of the Core.

Unless.

Gerald closed his eyes. He searched his memory. Thirty-one years of building codes.

Thirty-one years of forms. There was one category he had never fully exploited.

The category that applied to structures that were not built, but *found*.

**HERITAGE PRESERVATION.**

If a structure was deemed historically significant, it could not be demolished. It could not be purged. It could not be deleted. It had to be maintained, regardless of its structural integrity, because its value was cultural, not physical.

His dungeon was not new. It was built into the foundations of the Ashford Municipal Library, a building erected in 1892. The sub-basement was original.

The stone was original, and the rat was original.

Gerald opened his eyes.

He reached for his pocket. He did not have a form for Heritage Preservation. He had never needed one.

He had never inspected a heritage site in his career.

But he had a pen. And he had a rat.

"The rat can write it," Gerald said.

The rat stopped sorting. It looked up.

"You know the code?" Gerald asked.

The rat shrugged. It was a rat. It knew what it needed to know.

Gerald walked to the center of the room. He stood directly under the spot where the broken tablet had been. The place where the anomaly had started. The place where he had become a Core.

He looked at the Inspector General, who was still standing at the edge of the room, watching him with cold, calculating eyes.

"I am invoking the Heritage Clause," Gerald said.

The Inspector General laughed, which was a dry, brittle sound.

"You have no documentation. You have no plaque.

You have no history. The System does not care about your nostalgia.

"

"It cares about continuity," Gerald said.

He pointed to the rat.

"File it."

The rat jumped onto the floor. It grabbed a blank sheet of paper from the air—a fragment of the previous citation, now rendered blank. It clicked the pen.

It did not write *Heritage Preservation*. It did not write a code section.

It wrote a name.

**Gerald Hutchins.**

Then it drew a circle around the name.

Then it drew a line through it.

Gerald felt a chill run down his spine. The rat was not filing a form for the building. It was filing a form for *him*.

**FORM 11-B: NOTICE OF LEGACY ENTITY.**

The text appeared in the air, written in a color Gerald had never seen. Not red. Not green. Purple.

**ERROR: ENTITY CLASSIFICATION AMBIGUOUS.** **IS USER 'GERALD HUTCHINS' A CORE, AN INSPECTOR, OR A STRUCTURAL COMPONENT?** **ACTION: AWAITING CLARIFICATION.**

The Inspector General stepped back. Her face was pale.

"What did you do?" she whispered.

"I filed for joint liability," Gerald said. "If you delete the Core, you delete the Inspector. If you delete the Inspector, you delete the man who wrote the Code."

The System hummed. The purple light pulsed.

The rat looked at Gerald. Its eyes were black beads, reflecting the purple glow. It clicked the pen one last time.

**WARNING: PARADOX DETECTED.** **IF CORE IS PURGED, CODE CORRUPTS.** **IF CODE CORRUPTS, ALL DUNGEONS FAIL.** **SYSTEM CANNOT ALLOW TOTAL FAILURE.**

**RECALCULATING...**

The ground shook. Not a small tremor.

A earthquake.

The library above groaned. Books fell from the shelves. The ceiling cracked.

The Inspector General looked at her hands. They were becoming transparent.

No, and they were becoming *code*.

She looked at Gerald.

"You broke it," she said. "You broke the hierarchy."

Gerald adjusted his tie. He looked at the rat. The rat was chewing on the pen.

"I enforced it," Gerald said.

The floor beneath Gerald opened up.

Not a hole. A trapdoor.

The System was trying to eject the paradox.

To push the conflicting data out of the main server.

Gerald fell, and he did not scream. He checked his pockets for his pen. It was gone.

He landed in darkness.

The air was hot. Smelled of sulfur and rust.

Gerald opened his eyes, and he was not in the library. He was in a corridor. A long, narrow tunnel lined with bones.

And waiting for him at the other end, holding a pickaxe, was a dwarf.

The dwarf looked at Gerald. The dwarf looked at the purple light fading from Gerald's clothes.

"New guy?" the dwarf asked.

Gerald straightened his glasses. They were cracked. One lens was missing.

"Gerald Hutchins," he said. "Inspector."

The dwarf spat on the ground.

"We don't have inspectors down here."

"Then you are out of compliance," Gerald said.

He reached into his pocket. He found a scrap of paper. It was the rat's original Form 11-B. The one that had saved him.

On the back, in tiny, shaky handwriting, the rat had written:

**NEW VIOLATION: UNLICENSED UNDERGROUND EXCAVATION.**

**CITE THEM.**

Gerald looked at the dwarf. He looked at the bones. He looked at the darkness that stretched back into the earth, deeper than any municipal code had ever gone.

He pulled out the pen from his vest. It was his pen. He had never lost it. It had just been waiting.

He clicked it.

"Serve this," Gerald said.

The dwarf raised his pickaxe.

"Go ahead," the dwarf said. "See what happens."

Gerald threw the paper, which did not stick.

It vanished.

The dwarf laughed. A low, rumbling sound that echoed in the tunnels.

"You're not in Ashford anymore, Inspector," the dwarf said. "You're in the Deep. And down here, the only code is blood."

Gerald looked at the rat. The rat was not there.

Gerald looked at the paper in his hand. It was gone.

He looked at his chest.

There was a number burned into his skin. Not a rating. A number.

**0.**

The System had not just audited him. It had reset him.

Gerald Hutchins, Core Level 1.

The dwarf smiled, revealing teeth filed to sharp points.

"Let's see how long you last, little man."

Gerald did not move. He did not run.

He looked at the walls of the tunnel, and he looked at the uneven floor.

He looked at the lack of ventilation shafts.

He smiled. It was a small, tight smile.

"Your egress routes are non-compliant," Gerald said.

The dwarf stopped laughing.

Gerald took a step forward.

"I'm going to cite you," Gerald said. "And this time, I'm not filing a form. I'm filing a demolition order."

The dwarf swung the pickaxe.

Gerald ducked.

The pickaxe struck the wall behind him. Stone shattered. Dust clouded the air.

Gerald coughed. He checked his inventory.

It was empty, and he had no forms. He had no rat. He had no rating.

But he had his voice. And he had the code.

"Section 12," Gerald said, his voice echoing in the dark. "Prohibition of unauthorized excavation within fifty feet of a water source."

The ground beneath the dwarf's feet turned to mud.

The dwarf stumbled.

Gerald smiled again, and he was going to measure everything.

Then he read the second violation.
