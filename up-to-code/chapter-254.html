Chapter 254: Fourteen Minutes of Chaos

# Chapter 254

**SYSTEM NOTIFICATION** **TARGET:** CORE-8842 [ASHFORD MUNICIPAL SUB-BASEMENT] **STATUS:** PENDING AUDIT **TIMER:** 00:14:59

The silence in the sub-basement was heavy. It pressed against Gerald's eardrums like deep water.

Fourteen minutes.

That was all the time the System had granted him before the Inspector General descended. Not days. Not hours. Fourteen minutes to transform a four-thousand-square-foot pit of dirt and anxiety into a fortress of bureaucratic invincibility.

Gerald's hands shook. They were not shaking from fear.

They were shaking because he had been writing for nine minutes straight without blinking, and his eyes had begun to water.

He ignored the sting. He kept the pen moving. The ink flowed onto the parchment-like paper the System had provided, which smelled faintly of ozone and old library books.

He needed to file a Motion for Summary Judgment against the Audit itself.

This was a loophole he had discovered in Chapter 112, buried deep within the *Inter-Dimensional Zoning Ordinance, Section 9, Subsection C, Paragraph 4*. It had taken him three months to find. He had never used it. It required a precedent case to be cited, a specific type of structural anomaly to be present, and a witness who was technically part of the dungeon infrastructure but legally distinct from the Core.

The kid.

The adventurer who had filmed him. The one whose phone battery had died in the corner of the room, leaving behind a cracked rectangle of glass and lithium.

Gerald scribbled the last line of the form.

*WHEREAS, the subject dungeon maintains a Code Compliance Rating of 98.4, exceeding the statutory minimum of 90 for Class-1 Residential Integration;*

*AND WHEREAS, the pending Audit constitutes an Unwarranted Intrusive Inspection under the Privacy of Core Structures Act;*

*BE IT RESOLVED that Gerald Hutchins, Core-8842, requests an immediate Stay of Execution pending a Preliminary Compliance Hearing.*

He signed it. His signature was small, cramped, and illegible, exactly as it had been on every permit denial he had issued for thirty-one years.

He held the form up.

The air shimmered. Red text bloomed in the darkness, hovering inches from his face.

**FORM 77-C RECEIVED.** **PROCESSING...**

Gerald waited.

He could feel the dungeon around him. The mold spores were vibrating.

The water leak had slowed to a drip, sensing the shift in atmospheric pressure.

The single rat was hiding behind a load-bearing pillar, clutching a stolen crumb of bread with a intensity that Gerald respected.

**ERROR.** **PRELIMINARY HEARING REQUIRES PHYSICAL PRESENCE OF WITNESS.** **WITNESS: DETECTED.** **LOCATION: SECTOR 4 (RUINS).**

Gerald exhaled. The witness was still there.

The phone.

The System counted it.

A recording device, when left unattended in a dungeon, became a temporary extension of the dungeon's sensory network. A "Passive Observer."

If he could prove the phone had seen a violation committed by the Inspector General's advance scout, he could invoke the *Contamination Clause*.

But there was no scout.

Void-Star had recalled its Construct.

Gerald looked at the hole in the floor. The debris was still there. Black pixels, fading into grey dust.

He needed a violation. A real one. Something the IG would see when it arrived, something so egregious that the audit itself would be deemed invalid due to "Hostile Pre-Conditioning of the Site."

He had fourteen minutes to create a code violation that was also a trap.

He grabbed his clipboard.

"Come with me," he muttered to the empty room.

He walked to the corner where the phone lay. He picked it up. It was cold.

Dead. He placed it on the center of the main walkway, right in front of the newly installed ADA-compliant handrail.

Then he went to the ventilation shaft.

This was the problem.

The System had fined him for inadequate ventilation years ago. He had fixed it. He had installed a high-efficiency particulate air (HEPA) filtration system, rated for industrial use.

It was beautiful.

It hummed with the quiet dignity of a well-oiled machine.

But Gerald needed it to fail.

Not completely. Just enough.

He pulled out his multi-tool. He adjusted the fan blades. He tightened the belt until it squeaked. He loosened the intake vent cover until it rattled.

*Clatter. Clatter. Clatter.*

It sounded like a warning.

He checked his watch. Nine minutes left.

Now, the structural issue.

The IG would look for load-bearing integrity.

Gerald's dungeon was solid. Concrete foundations, reinforced steel beams he had salvaged from the library's renovation. It was up to code.

Too up to code.

He needed to introduce a "De Minimis" violation. A minor one. One that the System would flag, but not enough to drop his rating. Just enough to force the IG to open the *Detailed Structural Review* protocol.

Why? Because the Detailed Review required the IG to physically interact with the infrastructure. To touch the walls. To test the beams.

And Gerald had prepared the beams.

He ran to the north wall.

He pulled out a can of industrial adhesive.

Not glue. Epoxy.

Strong, fast-setting epoxy that cured in thirty seconds.

He coated a section of the wall where the concrete had been patched. He applied it thick. Then he sprinkled it with a fine dust he had collected from the mold spores.

*Calcium carbonate.*

It created a frictionless layer. A slick surface that looked like dry concrete but acted like ice.

If the IG touched it during the inspection, it would slip.

A slip during an official inspection was a liability. It was an unsafe work environment.

Gerald stepped back. He checked the handrail. He checked the exit sign. He checked the fire extinguisher.

He looked at the phone.

"Recording," he said. "Even if you're dead. You're still recording my intent."

The System chimed.

**TIME REMAINING: 00:05:00**

Gerald sat on the floor. He opened his notebook to a fresh page. He wrote the transcript he hoped the IG would generate.

*Inspector: Why is the ventilation rattling?* *Core: It is within acceptable decibel limits for residential integration.* *Inspector: The intake cover is loose.* *Core: It is designed to be easily removable for emergency egress, per Section 44.* *Inspector: You have altered the load-bearing coefficient.* *Core: I have improved it. The epoxy increases tensile strength by 12%.*

He was lying. He knew he was lying. But in the eyes of the System, if the paperwork matched the claim, and the claim was technically defensible, it was truth.

Gerald Hutchins did not lie. He recontextualized.

The air in the room grew cold. The lights flickered. Not a glitch. A signal.

The Inspector General was arriving.

It did not manifest as a monster. It did not spawn a beast.

It manifested as a presence.

A pressure. The weight of every rule ever written, condensed into a single point of focus.

A figure stepped out of the shadows near the entrance.

It was tall.

It wore a suit made of shifting geometric shapes, black and white. Its face was a smooth, featureless mask of obsidian.

It held no weapon.

It held a clipboard.

**INSPECTOR GENERAL** **CLEARANCE: OMEGA**

The figure walked toward Gerald. Its footsteps made no sound.

Gerald stood up. He straightened his tie. He picked up his own clipboard.

"Good evening," Gerald said. His voice was steady. "I have filed for a Stay of Execution."

The IG did not speak. It projected text.

**STAY DENIED. AUDIT PROCEEDS.**

"Then proceed," Gerald said. "But be aware. I have filed a Form 45-A regarding the conduct of the previous tenant, Void-Star. And I have preserved evidence of its trespass."

The IG paused. Its head tilted.

**EVIDENCE INSUFFICIENT. VOID-STAR HAS WITHDRAWN ITS CLAIM.**

"It has withdrawn its claim on the dungeon," Gerald corrected. "It has not withdrawn its liability for the structural damage it caused. Look at the floor."

The IG walked to the hole. It crouched. It touched the debris.

**DAMAGE: MINOR. NON-CRITICAL.**

"Minor for you," Gerald said. "But it compromised the integrity of Sector 4. I have repaired it with Class-A Epoxy. It is now stronger than before."

The IG stood. It walked to the ventilation shaft. It listened.

*Clatter. Clatter. Clatter.*

**NOTATION: MECHANICAL FAILURE IMMINENT.**

"It is within tolerance," Gerald said. "I have installed a secondary backup system."

He pointed to the corner. There was no secondary system. There was only a bucket catching a leak.

The IG ignored the bucket. It walked to the north wall.

Gerald held his breath.

This was the moment. The IG had to touch the wall to verify the epoxy claim.

If it touched the slick spot, it would slip.

A slip would trigger a *Safety Protocol Override*.

The audit would be paused. Gerald would have time to file an injunction against the IG itself for creating a hazardous condition.

The IG reached out. Its hand, made of sharp angles, brushed the wall.

It did not slip.

Gerald's heart stopped.

The IG pulled back. It looked at its hand. Black dust coated its fingers.

**CONTAMINATION DETECTED.**

Gerald frowned. The dust wasn't supposed to be slippery. It was supposed to be frictionless.

The IG turned to Gerald. The obsidian face reflected Gerald's pale, terrified expression.

**YOU ATTEMPTED TO CREATE A SLIP HAZARD.**

Gerald said nothing. He couldn't deny it. The intent was in the form. In the *recontextualization*.

**VIOLATION: OBSTRUCTION OF AUDIT.** **PENALTY: IMMEDIATE DEMOLITION.**

The floor beneath Gerald shook. The concrete cracked. The handrails groaned.

**DEMOLOITION IN T-00:00:05**

Gerald looked at the phone. The phone was dead. It could not save him.

He looked at the IG.

"Wait," Gerald said. "I have a counter-claim."

The IG paused. The shaking stopped.

**SPEAK.**

"You are conducting an inspection without a valid Environmental Impact Assessment," Gerald said. "You have introduced foreign material—your own geometric residue—into a protected residential zone. That is a violation of the *Preservation of Dungeon Integrity Act*, Section 12."

The IG went still.

Gerald's pen hovered over his notebook. He wasn't bluffing. He had read that section last week.

It was obscure. It was rarely cited.

But it was real.

If the IG had left residue on the wall, it had contaminated the site. The audit was invalid.

The IG looked at its hand. The black dust was still there.

It looked at Gerald.

For the first time, the IG spoke. Its voice was the sound of a thousand pages turning at once.

"Your interpretation is... narrow."

"It is correct," Gerald said. "And I have the transcript."

He held up his notebook. He had written the dialogue. He had timestamped it. The System recorded everything.

The IG stared at the notebook. The silence stretched. The mold spores stopped vibrating. The rat stopped eating.

Then, the IG raised its clipboard.

**NOTATION: CONTAMINATION CONFIRMED. AUDIT SUSPENDED.** **ACTION: CLEANUP REQUIRED.**

Gerald let out a breath he didn't know he was holding. His knees buckled. He sat down hard on the floor.

He had won.

But the IG did not leave. It remained there, standing in the center of the room. It looked at the hole in the floor. It looked at the phone.

It walked to the phone. It picked it up.

**DEVICE ANALYZED.**

The IG held the phone up. Text appeared in the air, green this time.

**DEVICE OWNER: ADVENTURER CLASS: RANK 12.** **STATUS: ACTIVE.** **LOCATION: SECTOR 7 (LIBRARY MAIN FLOOR).**

Gerald blinked. Sector 7? The library?

The phone hadn't been dead. It had been disconnected. The System had cut the link, but the owner was still nearby.

The IG dropped the phone. It turned to Gerald.

**THE WITNESS IS NO LONGER PASSIVE. THEY ARE NOW A COMBATANT.**

Gerald felt a cold knot form in his stomach.

**WARNING: EXTERNAL INTERFERENCE DETECTED.** **THREAT LEVEL: CRITICAL.**

Above them, the ceiling of the sub-basement shuddered. Dust rained down.

The library was under attack.

And the IG wasn't leaving. It was waiting.

**AUDIT RESUMES IN T-00:00:10.**

Gerald looked at his pen. He looked at the IG.

He had to choose. Fight the IG, or run to the library.

He couldn't do both.

Unless.

Unless he cited the library itself.

Gerald grabbed his clipboard. He wrote a single word.

**EMINENT.**

He slammed the form onto the floor.

**MOTION: EMERGENCY EVACUATION ORDER FOR SECTOR 7.**

The System chimed.

**ORDER ACCEPTED.**

But the IG didn't move. It just watched him.

"You are creating chaos," the IG said. "Chaos is a code violation."

"Chaos," Gerald said, standing up, "is an act of God. Or a dragon. I don't distinguish."

The IG stepped toward him.

Gerald backed away, towards the exit.

He had bought ten seconds.

He had to make them count.

He ran.
