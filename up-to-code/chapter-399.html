Chapter 399: Conduit Citation

# Chapter 399

The pen snapped.

Gerald Hutchins did not flinch. He simply set the broken ballpoint on the rusted railing and watched the ink stain his thumb. The staircase spiraled down into a void that his blueprints insisted did not exist. The air grew colder with every step, thick with the coppery tang of old blood and the sharp bite of ozone.

Behind him, the knight stood frozen at the threshold. The armor clanked as he shifted his weight, a nervous habit that Gerald had noted in the file he'd mentally compiled during the encounter in the upper levels.

Gerald ignored the knight. His eyes scanned the wall.

**[ASHFORD SUB-LEVEL 9: RESTRICTED AREA.]**

The sign was not painted. It was etched into the stone with a precision that suggested high-pressure water jetting or magic.

Gerald reached out, and the stone was damp. Condensation. He touched it.

**[Ambient Humidity: 88%.] [Structural Integrity: Compromised.] [Violation: Lack of Vapor Barrier.]**

A notification pinged in his peripheral vision. Not a citation. A warning.

**[Warning: Unsanctioned Descent. Core Integrity at 15%. Continued descent may trigger Structural Collapse Event.]**

Gerald stopped, and he pulled a small, leather-bound notebook from his breast pocket. The same one he had carried for thirty-one years of municipal inspections.

He clicked his mechanical pencil.

The pen had failed, but the pencil remained.

He wrote: *Sub-Level 9. Humidity 88%.

No vapor barrier.

Stone damp.

Cause: Unknown.*

He looked at the timer floating in the air.

**[Hearing Starts in: 59 Minutes, 12 Seconds.]**

Forty-two minutes of walking. Eighteen minutes of panic. The ratio was acceptable.

The stairs ended abruptly. Not at a floor, but at a sheer drop. A black chasm yawned in the darkness, three meters wide and seemingly infinite. A bridge spanned it.

The bridge was made of glass.

Thick, reinforced glass panels, bolted to steel cables that disappeared into the darkness above.

Gerald stepped onto the first panel. It did not flex.

He took out his measuring tape. He extended it against the edge of the glass.

**[Material Analysis: Tempered Silicate. Thickness: 12mm. Load Rating: Unknown.]**

He looked down through the glass. The chasm was not empty. Below the glass, suspended in the dark, hung massive chains.

And attached to those chains were cages. Thousands of them.

Inside each cage, a single, faintly glowing rune pulsed.

Gerald felt a nausea rise in his throat.

Not fear. Disgust. The layout was wrong.

The load distribution was asymmetrical.

The center of gravity was pulled toward the left, where the chains were thicker.

He looked up. The ceiling of this cavernous space was lined with pipes. Massive, industrial conduits running parallel to the bridge.

He checked the pipes. No insulation. No thermal regulation.

**[Violation: Thermal Bridging. Energy Loss: 40%.] [Violation: Improper Conduit Support. Sagging detected.]**

Gerald frowned. This was not a dungeon. This was a utility shaft.

He walked to the middle of the bridge. The glass creaked. A subtle sound, but audible in the silence.

He stopped. He placed his hand flat against the glass.

"System," he said. His voice echoed, flat and dead. "Query: Origin of Structure."

The interface blinked.

**[Origin: Pre-System Infrastructure.] [Status: Non-Compliant.] [Ownership: City of Ashford, Public Works Division.]**

Gerald blinked. He looked at the interface again.

**[Ownership: City of Ashford, Public Works Division.]**

The words hung in the air, glowing a soft, bureaucratic green.

Gerald Hutchins had worked for the City of Ashford for three decades. He knew every blueprint, every zoning ordinance, every buried utility line.

Sub-Level 9 was not part of the library. It was part of the city's stormwater management system. Specifically, the abandoned overflow valve for the north sector.

It had been sealed in 1998.

And it had been cited for non-compliance in 1999.

Gerald smiled. It was a thin, tight expression.

He pulled out his notebook again, and he wrote: *Pre-System Infrastructure. City ownership. Sealed 1998.

Cited 1999. Status: Unresolved.*

He turned around. The knight was still at the top of the stairs, peeking over the railing.

"Come down," Gerald said.

"I cannot," the knight called back. "The light... it burns my eyes."

"Then wait," Gerald said. "I have work to do."

He walked to the end of the bridge.

There was a door.

Not a rusted iron door like the one above. This was a heavy, steel fire door.

Rated for four hours of exposure, which was locked.

Gerald checked the handle. It was a standard panic bar.

He pulled. It held.

**[Door Status: Locked from Exterior.] [Key Required: Level 3 Maintenance Access.]**

Gerald looked at the door. He looked at the chasm. He looked at the cages below.

The System was trying to kill him.

Not with monsters. Not with magic. With bureaucracy.

The Hearing was not just a trial. It was an audit. And Gerald was being audited by his own past.

He realized something.

The dungeon core, *Gerald Hutchins*, had inherited the liabilities of the physical structure he now occupied.

If the library sub-basement was his body, then Sub-Level 9 was his gut. His intestines. And it was full of waste.

The cages below were not prisoners. They were filters.

Old, clogged, non-compliant filters.

If they collapsed, the structural integrity of his core would fail. The

"Structural Collapse Event" warning was not a threat. It was a countdown.

Gerald needed to clear the blockage, and he needed to cite the filters.

But he could not serve a citation to a filter. A filter was not a sentient entity. It was an object.

The System rules stated: *Citations require the target to be physically present to 'serve'.*

But they also stated: *Every dungeon has a hidden Compliance Rating.*

And: *Building a dungeon up to specific code categories grants permanent passive resistances.*

Gerald looked at the pipes on the ceiling. The thermal bridging.

If he fixed the thermal bridging, he would gain Lightning Resistance.

If he cleared the filters, he would gain... what?

He didn't know. But he knew that if he didn't clear them, the pressure would burst the chains. The cages would drop. The runes would detonate.

He looked at the timer.

**[Hearing Starts in: 54 Minutes, 03 Seconds.]**

He had fifty-four minutes to fix a city utility that had been dead for twenty years.

Gerald walked back across the glass bridge. He moved quickly, his shoes clicking on the tempered surface. He reached the far side of the chasm. Here, the pipes were accessible.

He found a junction box. It was labeled **VALVE CONTROL: NORTH SECTOR.**

He opened it. Inside, there were no wires. There were levers. Three of them.

One was labeled **IN.** One was labeled **OUT.** One was labeled **BYPASS.**

The **BYPASS** lever was rusted in place.

Gerald examined the mechanism. The rust was not just surface corrosion. It was structural oxidation. The metal had weakened to the point of fracture.

If he forced it, it would break.

If it broke, the valve would fail, and he needed a new lever.

He looked around. The cavern was empty, save for the chains and the cages.

He looked at the knight's sword, leaning against the wall near the stairs. The knight had left it behind when he retreated.

Gerald walked back to the stairs. He picked up the sword. It was heavy. Broad.

He returned to the junction box, and he jammed the sword into the rusted **BYPASS** lever.

He leaned on it.

The sword bent.

He grunted. He put his foot against the wall and pushed.

The sword snapped. The lever did not move.

Gerald stared at the broken hilt, and he closed his eyes.

He thought about the form he had filed in 1999. **Form 44-A: Notice of Violation, Sanitation Systems.**

He had cited it for improper backflow prevention.

The city had ignored it.

The System had not, and the System was the city that never slept.

Gerald opened his eyes. He looked at the broken sword.

He pulled out his notebook.

He wrote: *Improper Tool Usage. Violation: Failure to use correct equipment for maintenance.*

He looked at the System interface.

**[Self-Citation Accepted.] [Penalty: Stamina Debuff -10%.] [Correction Required: Replace Tool.]**

Gerald frowned. He had just debuffed himself.

But it had worked. The System had acknowledged the error.

And now, it was waiting for the correction, and he looked at the chasm. The cages. The runes.

He looked at the ceiling pipes.

He had lightning resistance potential. He had physical damage reduction potential.

But he needed to clear the blockage first, and he looked at the cages below. They were chained to the ceiling. The chains were the support structure.

If he cited the chains...

He looked at the chains.

**[Object: Chain Assembly.] [Material: Iron. Age: 20 Years.] [Status: Fatigued.]**

He could cite them for Fatigue Failure.

But he couldn't reach them, and he looked at the glass bridge.

The glass was the walkway. The chains were the support.

If he cited the glass for improper support attachment...

He looked at the bolts holding the glass to the chains.

He zoomed in with his System vision, and the bolts were loose.

**[Violation: Loose Fasteners.]**

He could cite the bolts.

If he cited the bolts, they would loosen further. The glass would shift.

He could use that shift to reach the junction box from above.

No. That was inefficient.

He needed to bypass the valve, and he looked at the pipes. The thermal bridging.

If he insulated the pipes, he could reduce the energy loss.

But he had no insulation.

He looked at the cages. The runes.

What were the runes?

He looked closer, and they were not runes.

They were meters.

Pressure gauges.

The cages were not holding prisoners. They were holding *air*.

Compressed air, and the stormwater overflow had been converted into an air compression system.

The System had repurposed the infrastructure.

The air was meant to cool the core.

But the filters were clogged. The air was hot. The thermal bridging was causing condensation.

Condensation was rusting the chains.

Rust was weakening the supports.

If the supports failed, the cages would drop. The pressure would release.

Explosion.

Gerald understood now, and the dungeon was not trying to kill him. It was trying to vent.

He needed to open the **BYPASS**.

He needed a new lever.

He looked at his pen. The broken one.

He looked at his pencil, and he looked at his tie.

His tie was silk. Useless.

He looked at his belt. Leather. Useless.

He looked at his shoes, and he looked at the sword hilt.

He picked up the broken sword hilt. It was steel.

He jammed it into the slot of the **IN** lever.

He turned it.

The valve groaned.

The pipes shuddered.

**[Valve Opened.] [Pressure Releasing.]**

A hiss of air erupted from the pipes.

The temperature in the cavern dropped instantly.

The condensation on the walls evaporated.

The humidity dropped to 50%.

**[Violation: Lack of Vapor Barrier. Resolved.]**

**[Compliance Rating: 15% -> 18%.] [Debuff Removed: Reduced Regen.]**

Gerald exhaled. His shoulders dropped.

He looked at the timer.

**[Hearing Starts in: 48 Minutes, 11 Seconds.]**

He had gained a little time.

But the hearing was still coming.

And he had not served the Citation, and he had not cited the rival core.

He had fixed his own plumbing.

He looked at the interface.

**[Notice: Inspector General Requesting Audit.] [Topic: Unauthorized System Modification.]**

Gerald froze, and he had opened the valve.

He had modified the system.

Without permission.

The System did not allow unauthorized modifications.

He had just committed a felony.

A dungeon core felony.

**[Violation: Unauthorized Modification of Core Infrastructure.] [Penalty: Immediate Suspension of Spawning Privileges.]**

The notification appeared in bold red letters.

**[Spawning Privileges Suspended.]**

Gerald looked at the interface.

His spawn list, which had been building up mold spores and rats, went blank.

He had no monsters, and he had no defenses.

He was alone in Sub-Level 9.

And the Hearing was forty-eight minutes away.

The knight shouted from the top of the stairs.

"Gerald! Something is coming!"

Gerald looked up.

From the darkness beyond the bridge, shapes were emerging.

Not monsters.

Inspectors.

Three figures in grey suits. Their faces were blank, smooth marble.

They held clipboards, and they floated toward him.

**[Inspector General Agents: Three.] [Objective: Confiscate Core.]**

Gerald looked at the broken valve.

He looked at the agents.

He looked at his notebook, and he had no spawn. He had no weapons.

He had only his code.

He stepped forward.

"I invoke Form 77-B," Gerald said. His voice was steady.

The agents paused.

**[Form 77-B: Request for Temporary Stay of Execution Pending Review.]**

The lead agent tilted its head.

**[Review Required. Processing.]**

Gerald looked at the timer.

**[Hearing Starts in: 47 Minutes, 55 Seconds.]**

He had bought time.

But he had also attracted attention, and the agents were waiting.

And behind them, the darkness was shifting.

Something larger was moving in the shadows of Sub-Level 9.

Something that had been sleeping in the filters.

The pressure release had disturbed it.

The air hissed.

The chains rattled.

A low, grinding sound echoed from the bottom of the chasm.

Gerald looked down, and the cages were moving.

Not falling.

Rising.

They were pulling themselves up.

Toward him.

Toward the bridge.

Toward the core.

Gerald looked at his Compliance Rating. 18%, which was not enough.

He looked at the agents. They were frozen in their review process.

He looked at the rising cages.

He looked at the broken valve, and he had one option.

He could cite the agents.

But they were not a dungeon. They were the System.

You cannot cite the regulator, and he could appeal the suspension.

But that would take time he did not have.

He could fix the valve.

But the valve was open. The pressure was gone.

He needed to close it.

But the lever was broken, and he looked at his belt.

He unbuckled it.

He held the leather strap in his hand.

It was not steel. It was not a sword.

But it was a strap, and he looked at the valve.

He looked at the rising cages.

He looked at the timer.

**[Hearing Starts in: 47 Minutes, 50 Seconds.]**

Gerald Hutchins tightened his belt, and he looked at the System interface.

**[Query: Can a non-metallic object be used to secure a pressure valve?] [Answer: Dependent on Tensile Strength.]**

He looked at the cages. They were ten meters away.

He looked at the agents. They were watching.

He looked at the knight.

"Knight!" Gerald shouted. "Your sword!

The hilt!

It is steel! It is broken! It is useless!

"

The knight did not answer.

Gerald looked at his own belt, which was leather.

It was useless.

But the buckle was brass.

Brass was metal.

Brass was non-corrosive.

Brass was conductive, and he looked at the pipes.

The pipes were carrying energy.

If he shorted the pipes...

The energy would arc.

The arc would weld the valve shut.

The heat would melt the chains.

The cages would drop.

The explosion would kill him.

Or it would kill the thing in the cages, and he had to choose.

He looked at the agents.

He looked at the cages.

He looked at his belt buckle.

He pulled it off, and he held the brass buckle in his hand.

He looked at the interface.

**[Warning: Electrical Hazard.]**

Gerald stepped toward the pipes.

The agents stepped forward.

**[Stop.]**

Gerald did not stop, and he raised the buckle.

He brought it down on the live conduit.

Sparks flew.

The air crackled.

The lights in the cavern exploded.

In the darkness, Gerald Hutchins smiled, and he had a citation.

And he had a plan.
