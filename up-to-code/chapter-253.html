Chapter 253: Midnight Audit

# Chapter 253

The phone trembled in the kid's hand, the screen glowing with a frantic cascade of chat messages that scrolled faster than a human eye could track. To the streamer, Gerald Hutchins was a glitch in the matrix, a NPC with impossible hit points who had just survived a Tier 3 predator by citing building regulations. To the audience, he was content. To the System, he was a target.

Void-Star's message hung in the air, a spectral red font overlaying the dark sub-basement. It was a direct ping from a dungeon core of a magnitude Gerald could barely comprehend. The notification pulsed with a rhythmic, menacing heat, burning against the damp chill of the library's foundations.

Gerald adjusted his tie. His fingers were steady, though the knuckles were white. He looked at the kid, who was still filming, capturing the spectral text from a low angle.

"Turn the camera off," Gerald said. His voice was flat, the tone he used when telling a contractor that their permit was expired three months ago.

"Are you kidding?" The kid lowered the phone but didn't stop recording. "This is huge. They're saying you're a raid boss. People are trying to queue up to fight you."

"Queuing implies an appointment," Gerald said. "This is an unsolicited home invasion. Stop recording."

The kid shrugged, a gesture that suggested he did not care about liability waivers. "Chat says you're cool. Chat says you're a vibe."

Gerald turned his attention to the red text. He didn't look at Void-Star's avatar or the sector designation.

He looked at the metadata.

Every System notification, even hostile ones, carried a header. Void-Star's message had been sent via the Global Broadcast Channel, a feature reserved for Cores with a Compliance Rating above eighty.

Void-Star was not just a predator. It was a regulator. Or it believed itself to be one.

The sludge creature in the drain had retreated, but the threat remained. The red text flickered again.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** **FROM: CORE 'VOID-STAR' (SECTOR 9).** **MESSAGE: Your ventilation shafts are non-compliant. I will adjust them for you. Expect a draft.**

Gerald's stomach tightened. A "draft" from a reality-warping Core meant structural destabilization. It meant the air pressure in his dungeon would shift, potentially crushing his spawn or, worse, opening a breach to the outside world.

He couldn't fight Void-Star.

Not physically. Not yet.

He was a sub-basement with three mold spores and a confused rat.

But he could file a complaint.

"Kid," Gerald said. "Do you have a microphone?"

The kid frowned. "Yeah. Why?"

"Read this into your chat. Verbatim.

Do not paraphrase." Gerald pulled a small, folded piece of paper from his pocket.

It was a standard Form 11-C, *Objection to Unsolicited Structural Modification*, but he had modified it.

He had added a footnote in red ink.

"What is it?" the kid asked.

"Read it," Gerald commanded.

The kid squinted at the paper. "Uh. 'To the Inspector General, regarding Core Void-Star. Pursuant to Subsection 44.B of the Inter-Realm Infrastructure Accord, any Core modifying the atmospheric pressure of a neighboring dungeon without prior written variance approval is committing an Act of Environmental Sabotage.'"

Gerald nodded. "Go on."

"'Void-Star is attempting to breach the containment seal of Sector 7-Alpha, my dungeon, under the guise of ventilation improvement. This is a Class 4 Violation. I demand an immediate injunction.'"

The kid looked up. "Is this real? Can I actually do that?"

"You are a witness," Gerald said. "The System recognizes public broadcasts. Your stream is a public record. By reading this, you are serving a subpoena."

The kid shrugged again, but this time there was a hint of awe. "Okay. Cool."

The kid turned the phone toward himself, framing his face and Gerald's stern profile in the background. He cleared his throat. "Yo, chat.

Listen up. Gerald here wants me to read some legal stuff about Void-Star.

It's like, really aggressive.

If Void-Star is watching, he's gonna get sued."

The chat exploded. Emojis of scales of justice flooded the screen. Someone spammed the word *LITIGATION*.

Gerald closed his eyes. He focused on the System interface, which was currently inaccessible to him due to his depleted mana, but he could feel the weight of the notification. He waited for the response.

It came ten seconds later.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** **FROM: INSPECTOR GENERAL.** **MESSAGE: Citation received. Temporary freeze on environmental modifications for Sector 9. Void-Star is under review. You have 24 hours to present evidence of prior non-compliance by Void-Star, or the freeze is lifted and Void-Star retains control.**

Gerald exhaled.

It wasn't a victory. It was a stay of execution.

And he had no evidence.

Void-Star was a Tier 3 Core; its compliance rating was likely perfect, or at least high enough to pass standard audits. Gerald needed to find a crack. A violation so egregious, so mundane, that it would override Void-Star's power.

He opened his eyes. The kid was gone. The stream had ended, likely because the kid had noticed the red light of a raid party approaching the library entrance. Gerald was alone with the drain, the mold, and the silence.

He walked to the center of the dungeon. The concrete floor was cold. He pulled out his tape measure.

If he couldn't prove Void-Star was currently violating the code, he had to prove that Void-Star's *history* contained a violation that the System had missed. A blind spot.

Gerald had spent thirty-one years finding those blind spots.

He walked to the north wall. He measured the distance from the floor to the ceiling.

Twelve feet.

He measured the width of the corridor.

Four feet, six inches. Standard.

He checked the handrails. Steel, galvanized, spaced at four inches apart to prevent head entrapment. Compliant.

He moved to the electrical box. It was a mess of wires, but he had organized them.

He checked the grounding.

Solid copper to the earth. Compliant.

He was running out of time. Twenty-four hours.

The System's audit rules were strict. You could not cite a violation that had been previously addressed. You had to find a violation that was *latent*. Something that existed in the structural DNA of the dungeon but had never been inspected.

Gerald thought about Void-Star.

Sector 9. Reality-warping.

That meant Void-Star manipulated space.

It expanded its footprint by folding dimensions.

The code for dimensional folding was obscure. It was covered in Appendix Zeta, a section of the manual Gerald had memorized but never encountered in practice.

*Dimensional stability requires a minimum of 0.5% mass displacement to prevent paradox collapse.*

Void-Star didn't have mass. It was pure energy and intent. How did it maintain stability?

Gerald walked back to the drain. The sludge creature was gone.

But the drain itself was the key.

Drains were connected to the city's sewer system.

The sewer system was a shared resource. If Void-Star was manipulating space, it was manipulating the pressure gradients of the entire grid.

He pulled out his phone. He couldn't access the System menu, but he could access the city's public infrastructure records. He had saved them locally years ago, just in case of a power outage. It was a habit from the old days, before the apocalypse.

He typed in the coordinates for Sector 9. He cross-referenced them with the Ashford sewer map.

Sector 9 was located beneath the old industrial district. Directly above the main water treatment plant.

Gerald stared at the map. Void-Star's dungeon was anchored to the water table. Its reality-warping effects would cause micro-fluctuations in the water pressure. Not enough to burst pipes, but enough to create air pockets.

Air pockets in a sewer system were a hazard. But more importantly, they were a *ventilation failure*.

The code stated that all subterranean structures must maintain a positive pressure differential to prevent the ingress of toxic gases. If Void-Star was creating air pockets in the sewers, it was effectively creating negative pressure zones in its own dungeon.

It was a violation. But was it significant enough?

Gerald needed to prove it was *ongoing*. He needed data.

He walked to the library's ventilation shaft. It was a narrow duct, barely large enough for a cat. Gerald squeezed inside.

It was a tight fit. His suit jacket tore on a jagged piece of metal.

He ignored the pain.

He crawled forward, guided by the faint hum of the city's infrastructure. He reached the junction where the library's air supply met the main municipal duct. He pulled out his anemometer, a small device he had scavenged from a construction site.

He held it up to the vent.

The needle twitched. It showed a standard flow. But then, a spike.

A sudden drop.

Then a rise.

Rhythm.

Void-Star was breathing. Its reality-warping was synchronized with the city's air circulation system. It was using the library's HVAC system to stabilize its own dimensional folds.

That was illegal. Using public infrastructure to subsidize private dungeon operations was a Class 1 Violation. It was theft of utility.

Gerald pulled himself out of the duct. He brushed dust off his knees. He straightened his tie.

He opened his mouth to speak, to address the System directly, but a new voice interrupted him.

It came from the shadows near the elevator shaft.

"Nice trick, Inspector."

Gerald turned. A figure stepped into the light. It was tall, clad in armor that looked like it was made of obsidian glass. The face was hidden behind a helmet with no visor, just a smooth, black surface.

It was not a goblin. It was not an adventurer.

It was a Construct. A high-level construct, likely sent by Void-Star.

"I am the Enforcer," the Construct said.

Its voice was synthesized, devoid of emotion. "Void-Star requests you withdraw your citation. In exchange, it offers to upgrade your ventilation system.

Permanently."

Gerald looked at the Construct. He looked at the armor. It was beautiful. And it was heavy.

"You are wearing non-standard armor," Gerald said. "No reflective strips. No visibility markers. And you are blocking a fire exit."

The Construct tilted its head. "I am here to negotiate."

"Negotiations are not permitted in active fire zones," Gerald said.

He pointed to the elevator shaft.

"This area is designated a temporary egress route. You are obstructing it.

That is a second violation."

The Construct took a step forward. The ground trembled. "Withdraw the citation. Or I will remove you."

Gerald felt the weight of the threat. He felt the fear, cold and sharp in his chest.

But he looked at the Construct's feet.

They were massive. They were crushing the floor tiles.

He looked at the ceiling. He looked at the load-bearing beams above the elevator.

Gerald didn't have mana. He didn't have spawn. But he had knowledge.

"You are standing on a weak point," Gerald said.

The Construct paused. "What?"

"Your armor weighs approximately four hundred pounds," Gerald said. "The floor here is rated for two hundred. The previous tenant removed the sub-floor supports to install a server rack. The load-bearing capacity is compromised."

The Construct looked down at its feet.

Gerald took a step back.

"You are causing a structural failure. If you take one more step, the floor will collapse.

You will fall into the sub-basement's foundation.

The debris will block the egress route. That is a third violation. Obstruction of emergency services."

The Construct hesitated. For the first time, it looked uncertain.

It was a logic machine.

It processed threats.

It calculated risk. But it had not accounted for the mundane, boring truth of municipal engineering.

"Step back," Gerald said. "Or I will add 'Reckless Endangerment of Public Infrastructure' to the citation. That triggers an automatic shutdown protocol for the sender."

The Construct remained still. The silence stretched.

Then, the Construct's helmet flickered. A new message appeared in the air, not from the System, but from Void-Star.

**VOID-STAR:** *Why do you cling to rules, Inspector? The world is chaos. Order is an illusion.*

Gerald looked at the red text. He looked at the Construct.

"Order is not an illusion," Gerald said. "It is the difference between a building standing and a building collapsing."

He raised his hand. He didn't cast a spell. He pointed at the Construct's left foot.

"Your weight distribution is uneven. You are favoring the left. That puts 300 pounds on a single tile.

That tile is cracked. I can see the fissure."

The Construct looked down. A thin crack ran across the floor tile beneath its left foot.

"Step back," Gerald repeated. "Now."

The Construct moved its left foot. Just an inch.

A loud *crack* echoed through the dungeon.

The floor tile didn't just break; it shattered. The Construct's foot sank through the concrete, down into the void below. It let out a metallic screech as its leg was caught in the rubble.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** **VIOLATION LOGGED:** **CORE 'VOID-STAR' SUB-UNIT: STRUCTURAL DAMAGE TO PUBLIC INFRASTRUCTURE.** **ACTION: IMMEDIATE CITATION ISSUED.** **STATUS: VOID-STAR COMPLIANCE RATING DROPPED TO 78.**

The Construct froze.

It was trapped. It couldn't move its leg. It couldn't fight.

It was immobilized by physics, not magic.

Gerald walked over to the edge of the hole. He looked down. The Construct's leg was dangling into the darkness.

"Get out of my dungeon," Gerald said. "And tell Void-Star that if it wants to argue the merits of its architecture, it can file a Form 45-A. In triplicate."

The Construct struggled, trying to pull its leg free. But the debris had jammed it. It was stuck.

Suddenly, the red text from Void-Star vanished. The air shimmered. The Construct dissolved, its form breaking apart into black pixels. It was being recalled by its master.

But before it vanished, a final message appeared.

**VOID-STAR:** *You have bought yourself time, Inspector. But the Inspector General is coming. And the audit is not just for me.*

Gerald felt a chill run down his spine. The Inspector General. The apex predator of bureaucracy.

If the IG came, it would inspect *everyone*. And Gerald's dungeon, while compliant, was small.

Weak.

It had no elite spawns. It had no defenses. It had only rules.

Would rules be enough against the ultimate auditor?

Gerald looked at the hole in the floor. He looked at the phone left behind by the kid, still recording.

He had to prepare. He had to fortify. Not with walls, but with paperwork.

He pulled out his notebook. He wrote.

He didn't know if it would work. He didn't know if the IG could be cited. But Gerald Hutchins had never met a regulation he couldn't weaponize.

He turned to the camera.

"Make sure you get the handrails," he said. "And the exit signs. If they try to remove them, it's a capital offense."

The phone battery died. The screen went black.

Gerald stood alone in the dark, surrounded by the ruins of a god's enforcer.

The System chimed.

**AUDIT SCHEDULED.** **TIME: 00:00.**

It was midnight.
