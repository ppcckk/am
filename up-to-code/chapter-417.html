Chapter 417: The Building Judges

# Chapter 417

The lever snapped off in his hand.

Not broke. Snapped.

The metal sheared clean from its housing, leaving a jagged stump of iron that bit into his palm.

Blood welled, dark and immediate, dripping onto the cracked tile. Gerald stared at the severed mechanism. He did not feel pain.

He felt the specific, cold nausea of a job gone wrong.

Above them, the library groaned. It was a sound like a ship's hull under pressure, deep and resonant, vibrating through the soles of his boots.

The ceiling tiles rained down, fine white dust choking the air.

Mira coughed, shielding her face with an arm. She looked at him, eyes wide, searching for a plan.

Gerald looked at the broken lever. Then he looked at the door.

The lock held. The steel plate remained flush against the frame.

But the wood around it was splintering, and the structure was twisting.

The System had locked the door, but it had not accounted for the fact that the door was load-bearing in a way the Inspector General had forgotten to check.

"Move," Gerald said.

"Gerald, the ceiling—"

"The egress route is compromised. Subsection 9.4, paragraph three. Emergency lighting must remain operational during structural deformation." He kicked the door.

It didn't open. It bowed inward, the hinges screaming.

"And I am out of lighting."

He turned away from the door. He ran toward the far corner of the sub-basement, where the water leak had formed a shallow pool near the electrical panel. Mira followed, stumbling over debris.

The floor tilted.

Books fell from shelves above, crashing through the floorboards and into the darkness of the dungeon. They landed with heavy thuds, burying the mold spores that had been trying to root in the damp earth.

Gerald reached the panel. He ripped off the cover. The wires were exposed, humming with the erratic energy of the realignment. Sparks showered down, singing the air.

"This is suicide," Mira shouted over the roar of collapsing timber. "You're going to fry the core."

"No," Gerald said. He pulled a screwdriver from his pocket. It was a small, cheap thing, meant for adjusting blinds, not rewiring a metaphysical entity. "I am filing a variance."

He jammed the screwdriver into the main junction box, and the System did not have a category for 'electrocution.' It had categories for Fire, Structural, and Electrical. But Gerald had spent thirty-one years learning that the boundaries between them were negotiable. If you could prove the electrical fault was caused by a structural deficiency, the electrical hazard became secondary. The primary violation was the building's failure to maintain its integrity.

And the building's integrity was Gerald's job.

He twisted the screwdriver.

Sparks flew. The hum grew to a shriek. The light in the room didn't come back on.

Instead, a new message flashed in his vision, burning red against the darkness.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** **UNAUTHORIZED INTERVENTION DETECTED.** **TYPE: ELECTRICAL SHORT CIRCUIT.** **SEVERITY: CRITICAL.** **ACTION: PURGE.**

The floor dropped, and they fell.

Not far, but enough to jar their bones.

The sub-basement was sinking. The library above was shifting its weight, realigning its foundations to escape Gerald's citation.

The Inspector General was trying to isolate the core by moving the physical location of the dungeon, effectively zoning Gerald out of existence.

But Gerald had anchored himself, and he landed on the pile of books that had fallen through the ceiling. A first edition of *The History of Ashford Zoning Laws*. Appropriately heavy.

Mira landed beside him, gasping. She grabbed his shoulder.

"It's working. The floor is moving."

"It's retreating," Gerald said. He stood up.

His knees shook, and he ignored them.

"The System is trying to sever my connection to the municipal grid by shifting the physical coordinates. It's a legal maneuver disguised as physics."

"What do we do?"

"We let it move."

Gerald walked to the center of the new, shifting floor. He pulled out his clipboard. It was the only thing he had left that wasn't damaged. The paper was singed at the edges.

"Form 11-C. Request for Temporary Relocation due to Environmental Hazard."

He wrote.

The pen scratched violently against the paper. The ground beneath him lurched again.

Dust billowed.

Somewhere above, a section of the library roof collapsed into the void they had created. The air pressure dropped. Gerald's ears popped.

Mira watched him write.

"You're insane."

"I am compliant."

The rumbling stopped. The silence was sudden and absolute.

The dust settled.

The sub-basement was no longer beneath the library, which was somewhere else. The walls were different.

The air smelled of ozone and old stone.

**SYSTEM MESSAGE:** **RELOCATION COMPLETE.** **NEW LOCATION: SECTOR 4, SUB-LEVEL 9.** **STATUS: UNLICENSED SITE.** **NOTE: NO ADVENTURERS PRESENT.** **NOTE: NO RESOURCES AVAILABLE.**

Gerald looked up. The walls were black obsidian.

Smooth.

Polished. There were no handrails. No exit signs.

No ventilation grates, which was a private dungeon. One of the high-tier ones. The kind that catered to heroes who wanted to kill things for loot, not citizens who wanted to stay alive.

Mira stepped forward, looking around.

"Where are we?"

"Sector 4," Gerald said. "The Merchant's Quarter. This is the dungeon of Valerius the Vile."

Mira's face went pale.

"The guy who burned down the orphanage in Chapter 12?"

"The same."

Gerald looked at the obsidian walls. He looked at the smooth, frictionless floor. He looked at the absence of any safety features. His nose twitched.

It was a perfect violation.

No fire suppression. No emergency lighting.

No ADA-compliant pathways, and the floor was a slip hazard. The walls were a tripping hazard. The entire dungeon was a Class 1 Hazard waiting to happen.

And Gerald was standing in it.

**SYSTEM MESSAGE:** **WARNING: FOREIGN ENTITY DETECTED IN PRIVATE DUNGEON.** **IDENTIFY OR BE PURGED.**

A low growl echoed from the shadows. Not a beast. A voice. Deep, resonant, coming from everywhere and nowhere.

"Who dares enter my sanctum?"

Valerius the Vile.

Gerald didn't answer. He took out his pen. He wrote on the Form 11-C.

*Violation 1: Lack of Egress Signage. Subsection 12.1.* *Violation 2: Slippery Floor Surface.

Subsection 8.4.* *Violation 3: Inadequate Illumination.

Subsection 6.2.* *Violation 4: Absence of Fire Extinguishers.

Subsection 15.1.*

He tore the page off the clipboard. He held it up.

"I am Gerald Hutchins," he said. His voice was steady. "I am here to serve you a Citation."

The air crackled. Lightning arced from the walls. A giant eye opened in the obsidian ceiling. It was purple, slit-pupiled, and furious.

"You think you can cite me?" Valerius laughed. It was a dry, rasping sound. "I am Level 85.

I have slain gods. I have eaten heroes.

And you bring me a piece of paper?

"

"It is a legal document," Gerald said. "And you are in violation of the Municipal Code of Ashford, which the System recognizes as the baseline standard for all constructed habitats within its jurisdiction."

"The System protects me," Valerius sneered. "I am registered. I am licensed."

"You are licensed to operate a dungeon," Gerald corrected. "You are not licensed to operate a death trap."

The eye narrowed.

"Kill them."

The obsidian floor cracked. From the fissures, shadows rose. Not goblins.

Not rats, and they were made of pure shadow, sharp-edged and silent. They moved with speed that blurred the air.

Mira drew her sword.

"Gerald, move!"

Gerald did not move.

He stepped forward. He walked right into the swarm of shadow-beasts. They swiped at him.

Their claws passed through him, and he was not there.

Not physically.

He was a Core. And in a Core's dungeon, the Core is everywhere.

But this was Valerius's dungeon. Gerald had no power here. No spawns.

No walls.

Except for the code, and he focused on the shadows.

He looked at their form. They were amorphous. Unstable.

They lacked defined boundaries.

"Violation," Gerald said. "Biological entities in enclosed spaces must maintain a minimum footprint of twenty square feet to prevent accidental collision hazards. These entities are non-compliant."

The System paused.

The shadows froze.

**SYSTEM QUERY:** **DEFINE 'MINIMUM FOOTPRINT' FOR NON-CORPOREAL ENTITIES.**

Gerald smiled. It was a thin, painful expression. He pulled out a tape measure from his belt. He clicked it open.

"I define it," he said, "by the space they occupy. And they are occupying space."

He walked up to the nearest shadow. He pressed the tape measure against it. The tape didn't touch the shadow. It passed through.

But the System registered the intent. It registered the measurement.

**SYSTEM RESPONSE:** **INTERPRETATION ACCEPTED.** **CITATION ISSUED.** **TARGET: UNLICENSED SHADOW SPAWNS.** **VIOLATION: OBSTRUCTION OF PATHWAY.** **PENALTY: TEMPORARY DISSOLUTION.**

The shadows shrieked. They unraveled. They didn't die. They simply lost their cohesion, turning into harmless wisps of smoke that drifted apart.

Valerius's eye widened.

"What?"

Gerald walked to the next shadow. He measured it.

**CITATION ISSUED.** **TEMPORARY DISSOLUTION.**

The wisps scattered.

The eye burned brighter.

"Stop! Stop it!"

"I cannot stop," Gerald said. "I am conducting an inspection."

He measured the wall.

**VIOLATION: LACK OF LOAD-BEARING SUPPORT.** **PENALTY: STRUCTURAL WEAKENING.**

The obsidian wall cracked. A fissure ran up its surface.

Valerius screamed, and the sound was psychic, a blade in Gerald's mind. Gerald gritted his teeth. He felt blood trickling from his nose.

He was paying for it. The cost of citing a Level 85 Core was not just administrative.

It was metaphysical.

Every citation he issued in a hostile dungeon drained his own compliance rating.

He was borrowing stability from his own foundation to punish theirs.

His rating was dropping. He could feel it. A cold numbness spreading through his limbs.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** **CORE INTEGRITY WARNING.** **COMPILANCE RATING: 30/100.** **CRITICAL THRESHOLD IMMINENT.**

If he hit 20, he would lose his ability to spawn anything. He would be a hollow shell. A dead building.

Mira grabbed his arm.

"Gerald, you're bleeding from your ears."

He wiped the blood. It was black.

"One more," he said.

He walked to the center of the room. He pointed at the ceiling.

"Violation: Absence of Emergency Ventilation."

The eye stared at him. Valerius was panicking. The structural weakening was spreading.

The dungeon was cracking. The shadows were gone.

The traps were disabled by the citation penalties.

"Please," Valerius whispered.

Gerald looked at the ceiling. He measured the distance between the cracks.

"No," Gerald said.

He pulled out his pen. He wrote the final line.

*Violation 5: Refusal of Cooperation. Subsection 22.1.*

He tore the page off, and the System did not issue a Citation.

It issued a Demolition Order.

**SYSTEM MESSAGE:** **DEMO LITION INITIATED.** **TARGET: DUNGEON CORE 'VALERIUS THE VILE'.** **REASON: UNCORRECTABLE VIOLATIONS.** **ESTIMATED TIME TO COLLAPSE: 10 SECONDS.**

The floor heaved. The walls buckled. The eye in the ceiling blinked out, extinguished like a lightbulb.

Gerald grabbed Mira.

"Run."

They ran toward the edge of the dungeon. The edges of Valerius's domain were dissolving into white static. The boundary between Sector 4 and Sector 5 was breaking down as the dungeon ceased to exist.

"Gerald!" Mira shouted. "The portal!"

There was no portal. There was only the void.

Gerald stopped. He looked at the void. He looked at Mira.

"I can't go back," he said. "My dungeon is still under lockdown. If I go back, the Inspector General will purge me."

"So what do you do?"

Gerald looked at the collapsing world around them. He looked at his clipboard. The Form 11-C was fluttering in the wind of the demolition.

He had no home. He had no dungeon. He had no safety net.

But he had his pen, and he wrote one last line.

*Request for Temporary Residency.*

He held it up to the void.

The System paused.

The destruction stopped.

For a second, just a second, the white static hung in the air.

Then, a new message appeared.

**SYSTEM MESSAGE:** **REQUEST DENIED.** **REASON: INSUFFICIENT SECURITY CLEARANCE.** **ALTERNATIVE OPTION: DETENTION CENTER DELTA.**

A door appeared in the void. It was plain. Grey steel. No handle.

Mira looked at him.

"That's a prison."

"It's a cell," Gerald said. "With a bed. And a table."

"And no rights."

"No rights," Gerald agreed.

He looked at the door. He looked at the void. He looked at Mira.

"If I go in there, I lose my autonomy."

"If you don't," Mira said, "you die."

Gerald nodded. He stepped toward the door.

But before he touched it, the air behind him shimmered. A figure stepped out of the static.

It was not Valerius. It was not the Inspector General.

It was a woman in a grey suit, holding a briefcase. Her face was blank. Her eyes were the color of old concrete.

She looked at Gerald. She looked at the clipboard.

"Mr. Hutchins," she said. "You have been summoned."

"By whom?" Gerald asked.

"By the Union," she said.

Gerald froze.

The Union. The group of Cores that had tried to sue him in Chapter 300. The ones he had dismissed as irrelevant.

They were not irrelevant. They were waiting.

"Come," she said. "Or be deleted."

Gerald looked at Mira. She nodded. Slowly. Sadness in her eyes.

He walked through the door, which did not open into a cell.

It opened into a courtroom.

And the Judge was not the Inspector General.

The Judge was the Building.
