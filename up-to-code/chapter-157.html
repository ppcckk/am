Chapter 157: Welcome to the Audit

# Chapter 157

Darkness tasted like copper and old dust.

Gerald hit the floor. Not with a thud, but with a sickening slide, his momentum carrying him across a surface that felt wrong.

Too smooth.

Too cold. It was polished concrete, poured in a single uninterrupted slab. A violation of expansion joint requirements for anything over forty feet, but Gerald didn't have the bandwidth to care about that yet.

His vision swam. The blinding white of the doppelganger's explosion had left purple afterimages dancing in his retinas. He blinked them away, forcing his eyes to focus on the immediate foreground.

One hand reached for purchase. His fingers scrabbled against grit.

Grit.

He was back in his own dungeon.

The hole in the wall was gone.

The wall itself was gone. In its place stood a jagged maw of exposed rebar and shattered drywall, weeping gray sludge.

But the darkness beyond it was silent.

No buzzing ballasts. No bone-clipboard stamping. Just the low, rhythmic drip of the water leak in the north corner.

Gerald pushed himself up. His arms trembled.

The loss of Physical Damage Reduction meant his bones felt hollow, brittle as dry twigs.

He tested his weight.

His left knee buckled.

**[STATUS: INJURED. MOBILITY REDUCED BY 40%.]**

A notification flashed in his peripheral vision, red and urgent. He ignored it.

He wasn't alone.

Three feet away, the confused rat sat on its haunches. It twitched its nose, sniffing the air where the doppelganger had been. It did not look at Gerald.

It looked at the hole. Then, with a speed that belied its small stature, it darted into the shadows of the sub-basement, disappearing into the wall cavity.

Gerald watched it go.

He wanted to cite it for unsanctioned dispersal. He wanted to issue a Form 11-B. His hand twitched, but no clipboard materialized.

The System was quiet.

No audit notices. No warnings.

That was worse.

Silence meant the Inspector General was watching.

Gerald stood.

His spine cracked. He looked at the hole. The rebar jutted out like broken teeth.

This was not a breach. This was a structural failure.

And structural failures meant liability.

He limped toward the center of the room. The fluorescent lights in his own dungeon were dead. The power grid here was primitive, reliant on a single, undersized transformer he had built with three copper wire scraps and a prayer.

*Electrical Code Violation: Single point of failure.*

He should have installed a breaker panel. He should have run a secondary feed. He hadn't. He had relied on the ambient mana flow, assuming the System's passive compliance rules would cover him.

He had been wrong.

The doppelganger had used the electrical noise of the neighbor's dungeon to mask its entry. The flickering lights had been a frequency jammer, disrupting Gerald's passive mana shielding. That was a loophole. A new one.

Gerald touched the cold concrete floor. He could feel the vibration. A low hum traveled through the floor, coming from the east. The neighbor's dungeon.

*Who are you?*

The question echoed in his mind. The doppelganger had worn his face. It had held a clipboard made of bone. It had cited him for *Existential Non-Compliance*.

What was existential non-compliance?

Gerald closed his eyes.

He forced his mind to shift gears. He was not a hero.

He was an inspector. Inspectors do not panic. Inspectors document.

He opened his eyes. He walked to the nearest wall.

The drywall was exposed.

He ran his hand along the studs. They were 2x4s. Standard.

But the spacing was fourteen inches on center.

Fourteen inches.

Code required sixteen.

A small violation. Negligible for structural integrity in a low-load environment.

But in a dungeon, every inch of load-bearing capacity mattered.

If the neighbor was using the electrical frequency to weaken the structural resonance of Gerald's walls, a fourteen-inch stud spacing was a fatal weakness. It created a harmonic frequency that could be exploited.

Gerald nodded. He understood now. The doppelganger hadn't just broken the wall. It had used the wall's inherent code violation as a weapon.

He needed to reinforce the structure. Immediately.

He turned toward the storage alcove. It was a shallow recess in the wall, barely enough to hold a mop bucket.

Inside, he kept his supplies.

A coil of galvanized steel wire.

Three bags of quick-set concrete mix. A roll of rebar mesh.

He grabbed the wire. His hands shook. He unspooled it, the metal singing in the quiet.

*Action.*

He wrapped the exposed rebar in the hole. He tightened the wire, pulling it until his knuckles turned white. He was bracing the breach.

Not to close it. To stabilize it.

If the neighbor tried to push through again, the bracing would hold.

It would buy him time.

While he worked, the System chimed.

**[NEW VIOLATION DETECTED: STRUCTURAL REINFORCEMENT WITHOUT PERMIT.]**

Gerald paused. He looked at the wire. He looked at the hole.

He was reinforcing his own dungeon. This was maintenance. Maintenance did not require a permit. Repair did.

He pulled out a mental ledger. He had never filed a permit for his own repairs. He had assumed the Core registration granted implicit authority for self-maintenance.

He was wrong. The System was pedantic. It was cruel.

He had to file the permit. Now. Before the reinforcement set.

He closed his eyes and focused on the mental interface. The menu was a mess of red alerts. He navigated to the *Applications* tab. He scrolled past *Dungeon Expansion*, past *Spawn License*, past *Fire Safety Certification*.

He found it. *Form 44-C: Emergency Structural Bracing.*

He filled it out in his mind. *Location: East Wall, Sector 4. Material: Galvanized Steel. Reason: Imminent Breach.*

He hit submit.

**[APPLICATION PENDING. APPROVAL TIME: 48 HOURS.]**

Forty-eight hours.

The wire in his hand snapped.

Gerald stared at the frayed end. He had pulled too hard. He had no replacement wire. He had no time.

*Alternative material.*

He looked around.

The sub-basement was bare. No furniture.

No fixtures.

Only the mold spores, which were growing in a patchy green ring around the water leak.

Mold.

Biological matter.

He could not use mold for structural bracing. It was organic. It would rot.

But it could be used as insulation.

Insulation was not bracing.

Gerald gritted his teeth. He dropped the broken wire.

He walked to the water leak.

The mold was thick here.

Slimy. Foul.

He touched it. His finger came away black and sticky.

**[CONTACT: HAZARDOUS BIOLOGICAL MATTER. DEBUFF IMMINENT.]**

His health bar flashed red. He ignored it.

He scraped a handful of the mold and pressed it into the gap between the studs. It was absurd. It was ineffective. It was all he had.

He packed the mold into the crack. It oozed out. He packed more.

He was creating a dam. Not to stop the breach, but to slow the frequency.

The mold was damp.

Water conducted electricity. Water disrupted mana flow.

If the neighbor was using electrical frequency, the water in the mold might absorb it.

It was a guess. A desperate, unprofessional guess.

But it was physics.

He stepped back. He looked at the patch of black slime. It looked like a tumor on the wall.

Then, the hum returned.

It started low, in the floor. A vibration that rattled his teeth.

The lights in his dungeon flickered. Once. Twice.

They came back on. Dim. Yellow. Sickly.

From the east, through the braced hole, a sound emerged. Not a voice. A stamp.

*Thump.*

*Thump.*

*Thump.*

Gerald held his breath.

The figure stepped through the hole.

It was the doppelganger.

The man in the suit of filing cabinets. But it was different.

Smaller.

The suit was tighter. The face was sharper.

It held a new clipboard. This one was made of glass.

"You are in violation," the doppelganger said. Its voice was the sound of tearing paper.

Gerald straightened his back. It hurt. He ignored the pain.

"State the violation," Gerald said. His voice was dry.

"Failure to maintain proper humidity levels," the doppelganger said. It pointed the glass clipboard at the mold patch. "Your biological containment is failing. You have introduced unlicensed organic decay into a structural element."

Gerald looked at the mold. It was spreading. Black veins crawled up the drywall.

"I am repairing," Gerald said.

"You are contaminating," the doppelganger corrected. "And you have no permit."

"I applied," Gerald said.

"Your application was denied."

Gerald's stomach dropped. "Denied?"

"By me," the doppelganger said. It smiled.

The jagged grin returned.

"I am the Inspector General's proxy in this sector.

Your repairs were deemed non-compliant with hygiene standards. Your dungeon is now classified as a Biohazard Zone."

The System flashed.

**[STATUS UPDATE: DUNGEON CLASSIFIED AS BIOHAZARD.]** **[ADVENTURER ENTRY: LOCKED. REWARDS: NULLIFIED.]** **[SPAWN RATE: INCREASED BY 200% TO FACILITATE CLEANUP.]**

Gerald felt a cold sweat break out on his neck.

Adventurers couldn't enter. His revenue stream was gone. His ability to attract outside help was gone.

And the spawn rate?

He listened.

From the corners of the room, from the drains, from the cracks in the ceiling, came the sound of scratching.

Not one rat.

Many.

The mold spores were multiplying. They were turning into something else. Something fast.

Gerald looked at the doppelganger.

"Why?" Gerald asked.

The doppelganger tilted its head. "Because you cite others. You disrupt the ecosystem.

The System balances itself. You are a cancer.

We are the antibodies."

It raised the glass clipboard.

**[CITATION ISSUED: IMMEDIATE CONDEMNATION.]** **[COUNTDOWN: 10 MINUTES.]**

Gerald froze.

Condemnation.

It wasn't a fine. It wasn't a debuff.

It was a shutdown order.

If the countdown hit zero, his dungeon would be dissolved. Erased. His core would be scattered into the void.

He had ten minutes to prove his dungeon was habitable.

He had ten minutes to clean a Biohazard Zone while being swarmed by spawn.

The first mold spore detached from the wall. It expanded, stretching into a gray, amorphous blob. It skittered across the floor toward him.

Gerald raised his hand. He didn't have a weapon. He didn't have a shield.

He had a code.

He looked at the blob. He looked at the water leak.

*Electrical Code.*

He looked at the doppelganger.

*Structural Code.*

He looked at the ten-minute timer.

*Time Code.*

Gerald ran.

Not away. Toward the water leak.

He grabbed the coil of wire he had dropped. He ran to the leak. The mold blob was gaining on him. It was fast, slimy, leaving a trail of black slime.

Gerald reached the puddle. He jammed the end of the galvanized wire into the standing water.

Then he grabbed the other end.

He looked at the doppelganger.

The doppelganger stopped. It watched.

Gerald closed his eyes. He focused on the single, undersized transformer in the north corner. The one that had failed. The one that was still hot.

He couldn't restart it.

But he could short it.

He could create a ground fault.

It was a violation. A massive, dangerous, life-threatening violation.

But it would create a surge.

A surge that would travel through the water.

Through the mold.

Through the floor.

Gerald shouted into the dark.

"I am citing this water leak for improper drainage!"

He threw the wire into the transformer.

The air popped. Blue light arced across the room.

The mold blobs shrieked. They convulsed, their bodies turning gray and cracking.

The doppelganger raised its glass clipboard to block the surge. The glass cracked.

Gerald fell to his knees. His vision went white.

The timer stopped.

**[APPEAL FILED: IMMEDIATE DANGER TO PUBLIC SAFETY.]** **[HEARING: RESCHEDULED.]**

The doppelganger lowered its clipboard. It looked at Gerald.

It looked at the smoking transformer.

It looked at the dead mold.

Then, it looked at the hole in the wall.

Beyond the hole, the neighbor's dungeon was silent.

The doppelganger turned. It walked back through the hole.

The wall healed. The rebar retracted. The drywall knitted itself together.

Gerald lay on the floor. He couldn't move. His body was numb.

He waited for the darkness to take him.

But the darkness didn't come.

A voice spoke. Not from the hole. From behind him.

A voice that was not the doppelganger's.

A voice that sounded like old paper and rust.

"You filed a Form 44-C," the voice said.

Gerald turned his head. It took effort.

Standing in the corner, where the storage alcove should have been, was a new figure.

It was small. It looked like a clerk, but made entirely of staplers. Thousands of staples, fused together into a humanoid shape.

It held a red stamp.

"I approved your application," the Stapler said.

Gerald's heart stopped.

"You approved the repair?" Gerald whispered.

"No," the Stapler said. It raised the stamp. "I approved your *appeal*."

It stamped the air.

**[VERDICT: COUNTER-CITATION UPHELD.]** **[NEW MECHANIC UNLOCKED: THE RIGHT TO SILENCE.]**

The Stapler dissolved into a cloud of staples.

They hit the floor.

Clatter. Clatter. Clatter.

Gerald lay in the sea of metal.

He couldn't move.

But he could think.

The doppelganger was gone. The neighbor was still there.

And Gerald had a new power.

*The Right to Silence.*

What did that mean?

He tried to speak. His voice worked.

"System," he rasped. "Explain."

No response.

He tried to stand. His legs worked. Barely.

He looked at the wall. It was whole. The mold was dead. The water leak was still there, but the water was still.

He looked at the hole.

It was open again.

And through it, he saw a single, white envelope.

It lay on the floor of the neighbor's dungeon.

It was addressed to *Gerald Hutchins*.

He crawled toward it.

He reached out. His fingers brushed the paper.

The paper was warm.

He pulled it into his dungeon.

He unfolded it.

The handwriting was neat. Precise.

It read:

*Dear Mr. Hutchins,*

*Your citation against the Biohazard Zone was valid. But you failed to note that the moisture in Sector 4 is not water.*

*It is blood.*

*Please schedule your next inspection at your earliest convenience.*

*Sincerely,*

*The Inspector General*

Gerald looked at the water leak.

He looked at the mold, which was dead and black.

He looked at the water itself.

It was red.

Dark, viscous red.

It had always been red.

He just hadn't been able to see it in the dark.

And now, the neighbors knew he knew.

A new sound rose from the east.

Not a stamp.

A scream.

A thousand voices, screaming in unison.

Gerald stood. He held the letter.

He walked to the hole.

He stepped through.

The neighbor's dungeon was bright. Fluorescent lights buzzed overhead.

And standing in the center of the room, waiting for him, was not the doppelganger.

It was a woman. She wore a hard hat. She held a sledgehammer.

She smiled.

"Welcome to the audit," she said.

Gerald raised his hand. He had no code. He had no citation.

He had only the letter.

He held it up.

"I am here to inspect," he said.

The woman swung the sledgehammer.
