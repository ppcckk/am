Chapter 181: Clearance Level Zero

# Chapter 181

The tape tore with a sound like a wet sheet ripping in half.

Gerald stepped back. The water lapped at his knees, cold and smelling of sulfur.

He watched the gap in the wall widen.

A thick, black ooze pushed through the breach, followed by a clawed hand. The hand was pale, translucent, and covered in sores. It gripped the edge of the masonry.

"Do not touch the structural elements," Gerald said.

The goblin-like creature pulled itself through.

It was hunched, its spine curved like a question mark. It hissed, spraying a mist of bile onto Gerald's clipboard.

Gerald wiped the slime off with his sleeve.

He did not flinch. He simply noted the contamination.

"State your business," Gerald said.

"I am Vex," the creature gurgled. "I represent the Underworks Core.

You have blocked the arterial flow.

The pressure is building.

The walls will burst. You will be fined for negligence."

Gerald looked at the water rising around his shins. He looked at the creature. He looked at the Inspector General, who stood in the corner, taking notes with a quill made of bone.

**VIOLATION: UNAUTHORIZED ENTRY.** **SEVERITY: HIGH.** **SUBJECT: VEX.**

"The entrance is not a door," Gerald said. "It is a breach in a load-bearing partition. That is a Class 4 Structural Hazard.

By entering, you have compounded the violation. You are now liable for the damage to the sub-floor."

Vex blinked.

Its yellow eyes narrowed. "You cite me? I am the opposing counsel in the Hearing.

You cannot cite the counsel."

"I can cite anyone who violates code while standing on my property," Gerald said. "And you are standing in a flood zone.

That is a separate violation.

Slippery surfaces require non-slip mats and warning signage. You have neither. You are creating a hazard for yourself, not just for me."

Vex snarled. It lunged forward.

Gerald did not move. He held up a hand.

"Stop."

The System flashed red.

**SYSTEM ALERT: CITATION ISSUED.** **CHARGES: FAILURE TO MAINTAIN SAFE ACCESS, UNAUTHORIZED STRUCTURAL BREACH, CONTAMINATION OF PUBLIC RECORDS.** **PENALTY: MOBILITY DEBUFF -30%. STAT LOCK: AGILITY.**

Vex froze mid-lunge. Its legs locked.

It toppled forward, splashing into the knee-deep sludge.

It struggled, its limbs moving as if through thick syrup.

It could not lift its arms above its chest.

**STATUS: DEBUFF APPLIED.**

Gerald checked his clipboard. He wrote down the time. He wrote down the severity. He signed it with a flourish.

"The Hearing begins now," Gerald said.

The air in the dungeon shimmered. The walls of the sub-basement dissolved, replaced by a sterile, white void. The Inspector General sat at a high desk.

Gerald sat at a lower desk. Vex sat on the floor, trapped in its debuff, unable to stand.

"Proceeding with Hearing Case ID 8940-B," the Inspector General intoned. "Complaint filed by Underworks Core against Hutchins Dungeon Core for Obstruction of Public Infrastructure. Respondent: Hutchins. Defense: Compliance."

Vex spat water.

"You have dammed the sewage line. The pressure is building. If it bursts, the entire district floods.

This is not just your dungeon.

This is city infrastructure. You are endangering lives."

"Objection," Gerald said.

"The premise is flawed. The 'sewage line' you refer to is an unpermitted extension of your dungeon's waste disposal system. It connects to my foundation without a backflow preventer.

This is a cross-connection violation. Section 14.2 of the Municipal Plumbing Code."

The Inspector General looked at Gerald. "Counselor Hutchins, do you have documentation?"

"I have a schematic," Gerald said.

He pulled a folded blueprint from his pocket.

It was soaked, but the ink had held. "This shows the connection point.

It lacks a valve. It lacks an air gap. It is a direct link between a hazardous waste source and a potable water source.

If the pressure builds, it will not just flood my dungeon. It will contaminate the municipal water main running beneath this library."

Vex's face went gray. "That is... possible."

"It is certain," Gerald said.

"By building up to code, I have installed a backflow preventer. It is a brass device, rated for high pressure. It is currently holding the line.

If you attempt to push more fluid through, the device will trigger an emergency shutoff.

It will lock the gate."

"And if the gate locks?" the Inspector General asked.

"Then your dungeon is cut off from its waste processing," Gerald said. "Your spawn entities will suffocate in their own waste within forty-eight hours. Your Compliance Rating will plummet to zero. You will be decommissioned."

Vex slammed a fist onto the floor.

The water rippled. "You are bluffing. There is no brass device.

We scanned your layout. There is only a rat and a leak."

"There is also a sump pump," Gerald said.

"Installed last Tuesday.

Model HP-400. It is connected to the grid.

It activates automatically when water levels exceed six inches. It does not require power from my mana reserves. It runs on municipal electricity.

Which means it is not part of my dungeon's ecosystem. It is part of the city's utility grid."

The Inspector General paused. Its quill hovered.

"Is this accurate?" the entity asked.

Gerald looked at the ceiling. "I submitted Form 11-C for Utility Integration three days ago. It was approved by the Substation. You can check the logs."

The Inspector General made a gesture. A holographic screen appeared in the air. It showed a timestamp: *Tuesday, 14:02. Approved.*

Vex stared at the screen.

Its locked limbs twitched. The debuff was still active, but its mind was racing. "If the pump runs, it will drain the water.

It will lower the pressure.

But it will not solve the blockage. The blockage is in the pipe.

The pipe is cracked.

You broke it."

"I broke it to save the structure," Gerald said. "But you are responsible for the pipe.

It is your infrastructure.

You have failed to maintain it.

You have created a hazard. I have cited you for that.

And I have cited you for the obstruction."

"This is a trick," Vex said. "You are using the System to avoid responsibility."

"The System rewards compliance," Gerald said. "You are penalized for non-compliance. The law is clear."

Vex gritted its teeth. Water poured from its mouth.

It looked at the Inspector General.

"I request a recess. I need to consult with my Core."

"Request denied," the Inspector General said.

"The Hearing must proceed. The conditions are volatile.

The water is still rising.

If the Compliance Rating of the Respondent drops below 20, the Hearing is suspended, and the Respondent is subject to immediate demolition of non-compliant structures."

Gerald looked at his status.

**COMPLIANCE RATING: 31/100.**

It was close. Too close.

He looked at the water. It had reached his thighs. The sump pump was humming, but it was struggling. The intake was clogged with slime.

"Vex," Gerald said. "Your blockage is organic.

It is made of decaying matter.

That matter is decomposing.

It is producing gas. That gas is expanding.

If it reaches the crack I made, it will ignite. Or it will burst the pipe entirely."

"So?" Vex asked.

"So, you need to clear the blockage. Now. Before the gas pressure becomes critical."

"I cannot," Vex said. "My minions are elsewhere. My spawn are engaged in combat with a hero party in Sector 4."

"Then you are negligent," Gerald said. "Negligence of a hazard is a Class 1 Violation. The penalty is immediate shutdown."

Vex laughed. A wet, gurgling sound.

"You cannot shut me down.

I am a Core. I am immune to citations."

"You are immune to citations from other Cores," Gerald said. "But the System is the ultimate authority. And I have appealed to the ultimate authority."

Gerald reached into his pocket.

He pulled out a small, red stamp. It was a **System Seal of Urgency**.

He had earned it by completing three consecutive compliance audits with perfect scores.

It was a single-use item.

"I invoke the Emergency Containment Clause," Gerald said.

The Inspector General straightened. "Explain."

"Clause 19-A," Gerald said. "In the event of imminent environmental disaster caused by a rival Core, the aggrieved party may petition for immediate remediation. The cost of remediation shall be deducted from the offender's Mana Reserve. If the offender refuses or fails to act, the System shall perform the remediation itself."

The void went silent.

Vex stared at Gerald. "You would use the System to clean my pipes?"

"I would use the System to enforce the code," Gerald said. "You have three options.

One: You send minions to clear the blockage.

Two: You pay the penalty for negligence, which will bankrupt your dungeon's economy.

Three: I invoke the clause, and the System uses a Void Drain to suck out the blockage. The Void Drain will also suck out your mana reserves for the week."

Vex's eyes widened. The Void Drain was brutal. It stripped raw energy.

"If you do that," Vex said, "my dungeon will collapse. The pressure differential will crush my central chamber."

"Then do it yourself," Gerald said. "You have ten minutes."

The Inspector General looked at the clock on the wall. It ticked loudly.

"The Respondent has invoked the clause," the entity said. "The Offender has ten minutes to comply. Failure to comply results in automatic remediation and a permanent Compliance Strike."

Vex looked at the water. It looked at Gerald. It looked at the Inspector General.

"I need a scribe," Vex said. "I need to coordinate with the Sector 4 party."

"You have your mind," Gerald said. "Use it. You have nine minutes and fifty seconds."

Vex closed its eyes. Its skin turned a darker shade of gray. It was reaching out, mentally, to its network.

Gerald could feel the strain in the air. The dungeon shook.

Dust fell from the ceiling.

**SYSTEM ALERT: REMOTE COMMAND INITIATED.** **LATENCY: HIGH.**

"Come on," Gerald muttered.

He looked at the water. It was touching his waist. The sump pump was whining, a high-pitched sound that grated on his nerves. The intake was clogged with a large, fleshy mass that had washed through the crack.

Gerald looked at the mass. It was a rat. A dead rat.

The same rat that had spawned when he first became a Core.

It had been swept up by the flood.

He looked at Vex. "Your minions are slow."

"They are adapting," Vex said through gritted teeth. "They are using the sludge as a lubricant. They are moving the blockage, not removing it."

"That is a temporary fix," Gerald said. "The pressure will return."

"Shut up," Vex hissed. "You are causing stress. Stress reduces cognitive function."

Gerald smiled. It was a thin, dry smile. "Stress is a byproduct of non-compliance. If you had built the pipe to code, you would not be stressed."

Vex screamed. It was a sound of pure frustration.

Suddenly, the water level dropped.

It fell an inch.

Then two. The sump pump's whine lowered in pitch. The flow increased.

The fleshy mass in the intake was pulled through, dissolving into the dark water.

**STATUS: FLOW RESTORED.** **PRESSURE NORMALIZING.**

Vex slumped. The debuff remained, but the panic was gone. The immediate threat was passed.

The Inspector General looked at the readings. "The violation has been mitigated. The Hearing is adjourned. Case 8940-B is closed with a Conditional Dismissal."

Vex opened its eyes. "Conditional Dismissal?"

"You have admitted to the blockage," the Inspector General said. "You have taken remedial action. However, you have not paid the fines for the initial violation. The fines are deducted from your Mana Reserve."

Vex winced. "How much?"

"Enough to halt your next evolution tier," the Inspector General said.

Vex looked at Gerald with pure hatred. "You won."

"I followed the rules," Gerald said.

The void dissolved.

Gerald was back in the sub-basement.

The water was still ankle-deep.

The sump pump was humming steadily. The crack in the wall was still there, but it was no longer gushing.

It was weeping.

Gerald sighed. He wiped his hands on his shirt. He looked at the Inspector General, who was fading into the shadows.

"Will they appeal?" Gerald asked.

"Perhaps," the entity said. "But you have established a precedent. You have shown that the Underworks Core is vulnerable to logistical pressure. That is more dangerous than any fire code citation."

Gerald looked at his clipboard. He had a new entry.

**VIOLATION: FAILURE TO MAINTAIN INFRASTRUCTURE.** **PENALTY: ECONOMIC SANCTION.**

He uncapped his pen. He wrote a note at the bottom.

*Next inspection: Check the electrical grounding in Sector 4. If their lightning resistance is tied to their waste management, then grounding them is a compliance issue.*

He stood up. His knees ached. The water had soaked his socks. He felt heavy.

**SYSTEM ALERT: NEW OBJECTIVE.** **REPAIR FLOOD DAMAGE.** **REWARD: +5 COMPLIANCE POINTS.** **PENALTY FOR FAILURE: -10 COMPLIANCE POINTS.**

Gerald looked at the floor. The wood was swelling.

The sub-floor was warping.

He knew what he had to do. He had to install a drainage grate. He had to replace the drywall.

He had to file a Form 12-A for Water Damage Remediation.

It was a lot of work.

But Gerald Hutchins did not mind work. He minded chaos.

And as he bent down to pick up a piece of wet drywall, he saw something in the crack. A small, glowing object. It was caught in the tape.

He reached in. His fingers brushed it.

**ITEM FOUND: THE CORE FRAGMENT OF THE FIRST DUNGEON.**

Gerald froze.

He had heard rumors. The First Dungeon was a myth.

A story told by old Cores to scare young ones.

It was said to have been destroyed by the System's first Inspector General. It was said to be impossible to find.

But here it was. In a crack in a wall. In a sewer.

Gerald held the fragment. It pulsed with a dark, rhythmic light.

He looked at the Inspector General. The entity was gone.

Gerald looked at the fragment. He looked at his empty hand.

If he kept it, he could unlock the highest tier of System knowledge. He could rewrite the building code itself. He could make his dungeon impervious to citations.

But if he kept it, the System would know. The Inspector General would audit him again. And this time, they might not be interested in fines.

Gerald weighed the fragment in his hand. It was warm.

He looked at the door. The door to the library stairs.

He could take it upstairs.

He could hide it in his apartment.

He could study it.

Or he could throw it back into the crack. He could let it wash away.

His hand trembled.

**SYSTEM QUERY: DO YOU WISH TO REGISTER THIS ITEM?** **WARNING: THIS ITEM HAS NO CLEARANCE LEVEL.** **ACCEPT?**

Gerald looked at the accept button.

He looked at the fragment.

He looked at the water, still lapping at his feet.

He made a decision.
