Chapter 216: Closing the Loophole

# Chapter 216

Gerald dragged the ballpoint pen across the signature line with a wet scrape. Gerald's hand trembled. Not from fear, but from the violent tremor of a man whose soul had been ripped from its chassis and stuffed back in a little too quickly. The ink from his ballpoint smeared a black blot across the white paper.

Sixty seconds.

The clock on the library wall ticked. A mechanical, indifferent sound.

The doors rattled. Not just rattled. They shuddered against their hinges.

Gerald didn't look up. He looked at the form. *Misuse of Jurisdictional Loopholes.*

He had always hated that term. Jurisdictional Loophole.

It sounded like a legal fiction, a slip in the fabric of reality that let bad actors hide from the truth.

But Gerald knew the truth. The truth was that the System was a code, and every code had gaps. He had spent thirty-one years finding them for the City of Ashford.

He hadn't invented the loophole, and he had simply pointed at it.

The front door burst open.

The bell above the door didn't chime. It shattered.

Varek stepped in first.

The Goblin King was seven feet of scarred muscle and bad decisions, wearing armor that looked like it had been salvaged from a junkyard and polished with spit. Behind him shuffled a procession of things that had no business being in a public library. A skeleton in a tuxedo.

A slime mold wearing a tie. A man made entirely of floating books.

And behind them all, a figure in a suit that cost more than Gerald's annual salary.

The man held a briefcase. He did not look like a monster. He looked like he belonged at a firm that billed by the six-minute increment.

"Mr.

Hutchins," the man said.

His voice was smooth, like oil on water.

"I am Retainer Three.

I represent the Coalition of Non-Compliant Entities. You have thirty seconds remaining to file your objection, or we move for immediate summary judgment.

"

Gerald capped his pen. He capped it with a soft *click*.

"You're in a public building," Gerald said. He didn't raise his voice.

He didn't need to.

The silence in the lobby was heavy enough to crush bone.

"This is a library. You are blocking the circulation desk."

Varek snarled.

A low, guttural sound that vibrated in Gerald's teeth.

"We are here to watch you die, little core.

The Inspectorate has spoken.

Your class is deleted. Your dungeon is void.

"

"Your dungeon is a hazard," Gerald corrected. He picked up his clipboard. "And you are loitering."

Retainer Three smiled. It was a thin, paper-cut smile.

"You have no standing to issue citations within a Void Zone, Mr.

Hutchins, which was ruled in Case 004. The void is outside municipal jurisdiction.

Therefore, your power is null.

"

Gerald looked at the clock. Forty seconds.

He looked at the statue. The stone golem he had summoned was gone, dissolved back into the ether, but the spot where it had stood was still scuffed. A single chunk of granite remained on the floor.

He walked toward the retainer.

"Where are you going?" Varek growled, stepping forward. His claws clicked on the tile.

"To serve the citation," Gerald said.

Varek laughed. It was a wet, hacking sound.

"You can't serve it.

You're just a man. A flesh-sack. The Core is gone.

"

"The Core is the building," Gerald said. He stopped five feet from the retainer. "And I am the Inspector."

"You are a glitch," the retainer said. "A rounding error. The Inspectorate has already drafted the deletion order. It's in the queue."

"Is it?" Gerald raised an eyebrow. He flipped a page on his clipboard. "Because I have a counter-motion."

The retainer's smile faltered.

"Counter-motion? There is no such procedure."

"There is if you know where to look." Gerald pointed his pen at the library entrance.

"Section 14, Paragraph B of the Municipal Access Act. Public spaces cannot be closed by private entities without a hearing.

You locked the doors before entering.

That is a violation of the Right to Exit.

"

The retainer blinked.

"That is... irrelevant to the Core Class deletion."

"It is relevant to the standing of the plaintiff," Gerald said. He turned to Varek. "Did you lock the door, Varek?"

Varek growled.

"I broke it."

"Breaking is not locking," Gerald said. He turned back to the retainer.

"However, the Coalition is responsible for the actions of its members.

You are in breach of contract with the City by obstructing a public thoroughfare.

This void zone? It's technically adjacent to the Library's foundation.

My jurisdiction extends to the foundation.

"

The retainer's eyes narrowed. He looked at Varek. Varek looked confused.

"He's playing semantics," Varek muttered. "Kill him. Just kill him."

Varek lunged.

Gerald didn't move. He didn't dodge. He didn't raise a shield. He simply held up his clipboard.

Varek's hand passed through the clipboard.

It passed through Gerald's chest.

The Goblin King stumbled, off-balance, and crashed into the circulation desk. Books flew. A stack of encyclopedias erupted into a cloud of dust.

"What the hell?" Varek roared, pulling himself up from the rubble. He swung again. His claw passed through Gerald's shoulder.

Gerald looked down at his own body. He was fading. Translucent.

"Ah," Gerald said. "I see."

"What?" The retainer's voice lost its smoothness. It became sharp. "What is happening?"

"I invoked the Right to Exit," Gerald said. "And the System honored it."

"You're disappearing," the retainer said. "Good. Let the wind take you."

"No," Gerald said. "I am not disappearing. I am *vacating the premises*."

He pointed to the air.

A blue box materialized.

**Notice of Vacatur: Case 001.** **Status: Pending Jurisdictional Challenge.** **Note: Defendant is currently outside the physical bounds of the contested zone due to Municipal Access Act Section 14B.** **Action Required: Plaintiff must re-enter the jurisdiction to proceed with deletion.**

The retainer's face went pale.

"What is this?"

"It's a procedural pause," Gerald said. He was still solid enough to hold his pen.

"You can't delete what isn't there.

And I'm not there. I'm technically in the street.

"

"You're in the lobby!" Varek shouted.

"I'm in the lobby of a building that has been deemed a public hazard by its own inspector," Gerald said. "Which means it is under quarantine. Quarantined zones are sealed off from outside entry by non-essential personnel. You are non-essential."

Varek tried to step toward Gerald, and he couldn't. An invisible wall stopped him.

Not a barrier of force.

A barrier of *policy*. The air shimmered with the text: **RESTRICTED AREA**.

"You're abusing the system," the retainer hissed. "You're using a building code to fight a metaphysical deletion."

"It's the same code," Gerald said. He checked his watch.

"Ten seconds left.

You need to serve the deletion order within the jurisdiction.

But you can't enter. Because I cited you for trespassing on a quarantined site.

"

The retainer's hands shook. He opened his briefcase. He pulled out a scroll. It glowed with red light.

"Fine," the retainer said. "We appeal to the Inspector General. We demand a Writ of Immediate Execution."

"You can't," Gerald said. "Appeals require a physical presence in the court. The court is here. And you are outside the court's perimeter because I filed a Form 11-C: *Temporary Exclusion of Hostile Entities During Active Inspection.*"

Varek roared and punched the air. His fist bounced off nothing, but the impact sent a shockwave that knocked over a lamp.

"He's not real anymore!" Varek yelled. "He's a ghost!"

"I am a Core," Gerald said. "And this library is my dungeon. And my dungeon has rules."

The retainer looked at the clock. Five seconds.

He looked at Gerald. He looked at the invisible wall. He looked at the scroll in his hand.

"I don't have time to file an injunction," the retainer whispered.

"You don't have the standing," Gerald said. "You're a lawyer for a monster. Monsters don't have standing. They have HP."

The retainer's face twisted. He slammed the scroll down on the circulation desk. The paper burst into flame.

"Fine," the retainer said. "Let the Inspectorate handle it. They'll override your petty bureaucracy."

"Let them try," Gerald said.

The clock hit zero.

The world snapped.

The Library vanished.

The shelves, the desk, the shattered glass—all of it dissolved into gray mist.

Gerald was standing in the Void again. But it wasn't empty.

The ground beneath him was paved. Perfect, smooth, black asphalt.

Streetlights lined the path. They buzzed with a low, electric hum.

A sidewalk stretched out before him. It was perfectly level. ADA compliant.

And standing on the sidewalk, waiting for him, was a single figure, which was not Varek. It was not the retainer.

It was a woman. She wore a gray suit. Her hair was pulled back in a bun so tight it looked painful. She held a clipboard identical to Gerald's.

She did not look like a monster. She looked like a bureaucrat who had been awake for seven hundred years.

"Gerald Hutchins," she said. Her voice was dry. Like paper sliding over paper.

Gerald straightened his tie. He looked around. The Void was gone.

This was a street. A long, endless street lined with buildings that looked like municipal offices.

"Who are you?" Gerald asked.

"I am Auditor Prime," she said. "I have been reviewing your case."

"And?"

"You are guilty," she said.

Gerald felt a chill run down his spine.

"Of what?"

"Of creating a public nuisance," Auditor Prime said.

She stepped forward. Her heels clicked on the asphalt.

"You utilized a municipal code to create a jurisdictional paradox.

You trapped a Coalition agent in a loop of legal impossibility, which is not clever, Mr. Hutchins.

It is illegal."

Gerald looked at his clipboard. The paper was blank.

"I was following the rules," Gerald said.

"You were weaponizing the rules," Auditor Prime corrected. "That is a violation of the Spirit of the Code. And the Spirit of the Code is not a loophole you can cite your way out of."

She raised her pen.

"I am revoking your Inspector Status."

Gerald's heart stopped.

Without Inspector Status, he was just a Core. A weak, sub-basement Core with a 47 Compliance Rating.

He looked down at his hands. They were translucent again.

"You can't," Gerald said. "I have a pending appeal."

"You do not," Auditor Prime said. "I denied it before you filed it."

She signed the paper.

A red stamp appeared on Gerald's chest. **STATUS REVOKED.**

Gerald gasped. He fell to his knees. The asphalt bit into his skin.

Pain. Real, physical pain.

"Why?" he whispered.

"Because you broke the one rule that matters," Auditor Prime said. She looked down at him with pity. "You made the System look foolish. And the System does not tolerate embarrassment."

She turned to leave.

"Wait," Gerald said. He struggled to stand. "If I'm not an Inspector... how do I fight?"

"You don't," Auditor Prime said.

"You die.

The Coalition will find you. Varek is already coming.

He has his own lawyers now. And this time, they won't be bound by your little games.

"

She walked away. The streetlights flickered.

Gerald looked up.

At the end of the street, a black car was pulling up. It was sleek. Silent. It had no license plate.

The window rolled down.

Varek's face appeared in the glass. He was smiling.

"Time's up, Hutchins," Varek said. "Get in the car."

Gerald looked at his empty clipboard. He looked at the gray suit of the Auditor, shrinking into the distance.

He looked at the car, and he reached into his pocket.

He still had his pen.

And in his other pocket, folded up small, was a single piece of paper. A form he had been working on for three chapters. A form he hadn't even submitted yet.

**Application for Variance: Change of Zoning from Residential to Industrial Hazard.**

He unfolded it.

The car door opened.

Gerald stood up. He didn't get in.

He held out the form.

"Serve it," he said.

Varek's smile vanished.

"No," Varek said. "That's not... that's not a valid form."

"It is," Gerald said. "If you want the ride, you have to sign for the liability waiver. And the liability waiver says you agree to abide by all City ordinances."

Varek stared at him.

The Auditor stopped walking. She turned around.

"You're bluffing," Varek said.

"Am I?" Gerald asked. "Check the fine print."

Varek leaned out of the car. He squinted at the paper.

Gerald watched the Goblin King's eyes dart back and forth. Watching him try to find the loophole.

But Gerald had written it.

And it was perfect.

Varek looked up. His eyes were wide.

"You trapped us," Varek whispered. "The whole Coalition."

"I cited you," Gerald said. "And now, I'm suing you for emotional distress caused by the presence of a non-compliant dungeon core in a residential zone."

The car door slammed shut.

The engine revved.

But it didn't move.

Because the road was gone.

The asphalt cracked. Split.

And from the cracks, vines of green light erupted.

Not vines.

Bars.

Jail bars.

Made of light, and they slammed down around the car. Around Varek. Around the retainer who was standing next to the car, his suit torn.

Auditor Prime watched. She did not move to help.

She pulled out her own clipboard.

She wrote one line.

Gerald read it from the bottom of the page.

**Case 002: Core Hutchins vs. The System Inspectorate.**

**Charge: Contempt of Court.**

**Penalty: Immediate Integration into the Audit Staff.**

Gerald looked at the Auditor, and she looked at Gerald.

She smiled.

It was a thin, paper-cut smile.

"Welcome to the job, Mr. Hutchins," she said.

The bars tightened.

Gerald screamed.

But it wasn't a scream of pain.

It was a scream of a man who had just realized that he had lost the game he had been playing.

And the new game had no rules he understood.

The bars closed, and the blackness took him.

And the last thing Gerald saw, before the dark, was the retainer picking up his briefcase.

And walking toward him.

With a smile.
