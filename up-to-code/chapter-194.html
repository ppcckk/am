Chapter 194: Perjury Collapses Dungeon

# Chapter 194

The stamp hit the paper with the sound of a gavel.

**FORM 77-C: INTRA-GOVERNMENTAL COMPLIANCE AUDIT.** **SUBJECT: GERALD HUTCHINS, CORE ENTITY.** **STATUS: PROBATIONARY (REVOCABLE UPON SUBMISSION OF EVIDENCE).** **TIME LIMIT: 00:04:59 REMAINING.**

Four minutes. Gerald looked at the red numbers pulsing in the corner of his vision.

He did not look up at the crowd of inspectors blocking the marble corridor.

They stood in a perfect row, their suits identical, their faces blanked by the System's rendering engine. They held clipboards. They held hammers.

"Move," the lead inspector said. His voice had no inflection. It was the sound of a printer jamming.

Gerald adjusted his tie.

The fabric was gone. He was a geometric shape of light and intention, but he still felt the phantom weight of a Windsor knot.

He looked at the golem, or what was left of it.

The dust was settling into fine, gray powder on the new marble floor. The gold leaf on the walls shimmered, mocking the grayness of the intruders.

"You are trespassing," Gerald said. "This is a private easement."

"It is a public infrastructure hazard," the inspector replied. He stepped forward.

The floor did not creak.

It did not settle.

It held.

Gerald opened his notebook. The pages were blank, but the words appeared as he looked at them.

**JURISDICTIONAL AWARENESS ACTIVE.** **SCOPE: LOCAL MUNICIPAL BOUNDARIES.** **CLAIM: THE INSPECTOR GENERAL IS DEFINED BY STATUTE 401-B.** **STATUTE 401-B: ALL GOVERNMENT EMPLOYEES MUST FILE ANNUAL WORKPLACE SAFETY REPORTS.**

Gerald flipped a page. "Where is your safety report?"

The inspector stopped. The hammer in his hand lowered an inch. "We do not file reports. We are the System."

"Wrong," Gerald said. His voice was dry. It cut through the hum of the dungeon's new resonance.

"You are agents of the System. The System is defined by the Inspector General.

The Inspector General is an employee.

You are his subordinates. Subordinates do not skip paperwork."

A murmur rippled through the line of inspectors. Their blank faces flickered. For a microsecond, they looked like confused clerks caught drinking on the clock.

"I am issuing a Citation," Gerald said.

He pulled a pen from his pocket. It was a fountain pen.

It leaked black ink onto his light-formed hand.

He didn't care. "Violation: Failure to Maintain Workplace Safety. Penalty: Temporary Suspension of Inspection Authority."

The lead inspector raised his hammer. "You cannot cite the state."

"I can," Gerald said. "I have jurisdiction.

I am the Auditor.

I define the room.

If you are in my room, you are subject to my rules."

**SYSTEM NOTICE: CITATION DRAFTED.** **TARGET: INSPECTION UNIT 7.** **SEVERITY: MINOR.** **PENALTY: 10% REDUCTION IN STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY.**

The inspector's suit frayed at the shoulder. The hammer cracked.

Gerald felt a pull in his chest. Not pain. Pressure.

Like a dam holding back a flood. The dungeon groaned.

The gold leaf peeled from the walls, falling like dead leaves.

"Stop," the second inspector said. He stepped out of line. He was shorter than the others.

He wore a tie that was too loose.

A violation. Gerald noted it instantly. *Improper attire for a municipal official.

Code 12-A.*

"Sir," the second inspector said. "We can negotiate."

"Gerald Hutchins," the first inspector snapped. "Return the Key."

Gerald looked at the key in his other hand. It was no longer cold. It was warm. It vibrated with a low frequency that matched the heartbeat of the dungeon.

"The Key is evidence," Gerald said. "It is attached to the counter-claim. The Inspector General is a municipal employee.

Therefore, he must submit to a workplace safety audit. If he refuses, he is in violation of the very laws he enforces."

The first inspector lunged.

Gerald did not dodge. He held his ground. He raised his hand.

**ACTION: SERVE SUMMONS.**

The inspector hit an invisible wall.

Not a barrier.

A boundary. The edge of Gerald's dungeon footprint.

The inspector bounced off, sliding across the marble. The other inspectors stopped. They looked at their leader.

They looked at the crack in the hammer. They looked at Gerald.

"They are listening," the second inspector whispered.

Gerald looked at the ceiling. The cracks in the reality were spreading, but the System was patching them with gold. The dungeon was becoming beautiful. It was becoming expensive.

"Who is listening?" Gerald asked.

"The Board," the second inspector said. "The High Council of Cores. They have heard your claim. They have seen the loophole."

A notification flashed in Gerald's vision. It was not red. It was gold.

**HIGH COUNCIL NOTICE: HEARING SCHEDULED.** **VENUE: THE OBSIDIAN HALL.** **DATE: IMMEDIATE.** **ISSUE: THE LEGAL STATUS OF THE INSPECTOR GENERAL.** **STAKES: TOTAL JURISDICTIONAL REVOKE OR SUPREME CORE STATUS.**

Gerald frowned. "Immediate? I haven't prepared the exhibits."

"The Board does not wait for preparation," the second inspector said. "They wait for leverage. You have it."

Gerald looked at the first inspector.

The man was struggling to stand. His structural integrity was dropping. The suit was tearing.

The face was pixelating.

"Go back," Gerald said. "Tell them I will attend. But I am bringing my own counsel."

The first inspector snarled. "You have no counsel. You are a rat in a basement."

"I am a building inspector," Gerald said. "And I am about to inspect the foundations of heaven."

The air tore open again. This time, it did not sound like terror. It sounded like a court order.

The inspectors vanished. The corridor remained.

The marble was perfect.

The gold leaf was pristine. The dust of the golem was gone.

Gerald stood alone.

He looked at the key. He looked at the notebook.

**CORE STATUS: STABLE.** **COMPLIANCE RATING: 88/100.** **NOTE: AESTHETIC OVERLOAD. REMOVE GOLD LEAF TO RESTORE FUNCTIONALITY.**

Gerald ignored the note. He opened a new page in his notebook.

He wrote.

Not notes.

A brief.

**CASE 94-B: EXHIBIT A.** **The Inspector General has failed to file Form 11-B (Unlicensed Biological Entity) for the last 300 years.** **Exhibit B: The Inspector General has issued over 40,000 Citations without a valid license number.** **Exhibit C: The Inspector General is a construct. Constructs cannot be employed under Civil Service Statute 4. They are property.**

Gerald wrote for three minutes. The dungeon hummed around him. The walls breathed. The mold in the corner turned to silver.

A new notification appeared.

**SYSTEM NOTICE: COUNTER-CLAIM FILED.** **BY: THE INSPECTOR GENERAL.** **CLAIM: GERALD HUTCHINS IS A ROGUE CORE. REQUEST: IMMEDIATE DELETION.** **REASON: UNAUTHORIZED EVOLUTION.**

Gerald stared at the text. Deletion. Not demotion.

Not fine. Deletion.

He felt a chill that had nothing to do with the temperature. He thought of his mother. He thought of his pension. He thought of the forty years he had spent measuring door widths so that no one would ever get stuck.

He looked at the door to the Obsidian Hall.

It had appeared in the wall. It was black. It was huge.

It had no handle.

Gerald walked toward it.

**INVENTORY CHECK:** **ITEM: THE KEY.** **STATUS: EVIDENCE.** **ITEM: NOTEBOOK.** **STATUS: BRIEF.** **ITEM: SELF.** **STATUS: TARGET.**

He reached the door. He touched the surface. It was cold. Colder than the key.

"Open," Gerald said.

The door did not open.

**ERROR: JURISDICTIONAL MISMATCH.** **THE OBSIDIAN HALL IS BEYOND MUNICIPAL LIMITS.** **ACCESS DENIED.**

Gerald's blood ran cold. Or what passed for blood in a core.

He had been so focused on the legal argument. He had forgotten the geography.

The Obsidian Hall was not in Ashford.

It was not in the Municipal Library. It was in the Void.

"You need a passport," a voice said.

Gerald turned.

A man stood at the end of the corridor. He was tall. He wore a coat made of shadows. His face was a smooth, featureless oval.

"Who are you?" Gerald asked.

"I am the Courier," the man said. "I deliver subpoenas that cross dimensional boundaries."

"I didn't call for a subpoena."

"You filed a claim," the Courier said. "That is a call.

The System responds to claims.

It does not care about your feelings, Mr.

Hutchins. It cares about the paperwork."

The Courier held out a black envelope.

"Sign this," the Courier said.

"What is it?"

"An acknowledgment of receipt for the Obsidian Hall summons. Without it, you cannot enter. Without entry, you forfeit the case. Without the case, the Inspector General deletes you."

Gerald looked at the envelope. He looked at the key.

He looked at the notebook.

He had one option. He could refuse to sign. He could try to build a portal. He could try to argue with the Courier.

But he knew the rules. He had written the rules.

He reached for the envelope.

**WARNING: SIGNING ACKNOWLEDGES JURISDICTION.** **JURISDICTION TRANSFER: FROM MUNICIPAL TO COSMIC.** **COST: ALL LOCAL PROTECTIONS REVOKED.**

Gerald hesitated. His hand hovered over the black paper.

If he signed, he lost his dungeon. He lost his home. He lost the protection of the local code. He would be naked in the face of the Inspector General.

If he did not sign, he was deleted.

The Courier waited. The featureless face did not blink.

Gerald thought of the goblin he had cited for trespassing. He thought of the mold he had cultivated. He thought of the rats.

He thought of the fire exit signs he had installed. They were bright green. They were correct.

He pulled a pen from his pocket.

"I need a lawyer," Gerald said.

The Courier tilted his head. "The System provides one."

"Good," Gerald said. "Send it to the Obsidian Hall. I will not sign until my counsel arrives."

The Courier paused. The shadows around him shifted.

"That is not in the manual," the Courier said.

"Page 4, Line 12," Gerald said. "Right to representation in all administrative hearings. It is a fundamental right of the Core."

The Courier looked at the envelope. He looked at Gerald.

"Wait," the Courier said.

He vanished.

Gerald stood in the empty corridor. The gold leaf glowed. The marble reflected his own face.

He was alone.

But he was not defeated.

He opened his notebook to a fresh page.

**BRIEF FOR COUNSEL.** **THE INSPECTOR GENERAL IS NOT A CORE.** **THE INSPECTOR GENERAL IS A BUREAUCRACY.** **BUREAUCRACIES CANNOT BE DELETED.** **THEY CAN ONLY BE REORGANIZED.**

A new notification appeared. It was small. It was in the corner.

**SYSTEM NOTICE: AUDITOR STATUS CONFIRMED.** **NEW ABILITY: ADMINISTRATIVE PARALYSIS.** **DESCRIPTION: TARGETS SUBJECT TO EXCESSIVE PAPERWORK REQUIREMENTS WILL SUFFER A 50% REDUCTION IN ACTION SPEED FOR 10 SECONDS.**

Gerald smiled. It was a sharp, dangerous smile.

He looked at the door to the Obsidian Hall.

He was ready.

The air behind him cracked.

Not with a tear. With a snap.

Gerald turned.

The first inspector was back. But he was not alone. Behind him stood a figure made of pure light.

It was huge. It had many eyes.

It had no mouth.

The Inspector General.

He did not speak. He pointed a finger at Gerald.

**SYSTEM NOTICE: EXECUTION ORDER SIGNED.** **TARGET: GERALD HUTCHINS.** **REASON: UNAUTHORIZED JURISDICTIONAL EXPANSION.** **METHOD: COMPLETION.**

The light surged.

Gerald raised his hand.

"Objection," he said. "The target is currently in a hearing room."

The light stopped.

It hovered. It trembled.

Gerald looked at the Inspector General.

"I am still in the library," Gerald said. "This is not the Obsidian Hall. You cannot execute me here. You have no jurisdiction."

The Inspector General's many eyes blinked.

The light flickered.

Gerald looked at the clock.

The hearing had not started.

But the execution had.

And it had failed.

For now.

But the Inspector General did not leave. He waited.

And in the silence, Gerald heard a new sound.

Not footsteps.

Not whispers.

Laughter.

It came from the walls.

The gold leaf was laughing.

The marble was laughing.

The dungeon was laughing at him.

Gerald looked down at his hands. They were no longer glowing. They were cracking.

**CORE INTEGRITY: 40%.** **CAUSE: EXISTENTIAL CONTRADICTION.**

Gerald looked at the Inspector General.

He looked at the crack in his own hand.

He looked at the notebook.

He realized then. The Inspector General had not come to kill him.

He had come to make him sign.

And Gerald had just given him the reason.

The dungeon was collapsing. Not from the outside. From the inside.

Because Gerald Hutchins had lied.

He did not have a lawyer.

He never had.

He was alone.

And the System knew it.
