Chapter 230: Filing Form 11-B

# Chapter 230

The voice in the dark did not belong to a monster. It belonged to a filing cabinet.

It was dry, papery, and smelled of stale coffee and ozone. The vibration of the words rattled the loose gravel around Gerald's boots. Mira stood frozen, the rebar held loosely in her hands, her knuckles white. Above them, the library ceiling groaned, a sound like a ship's hull buckling under pressure.

Gerald adjusted his shredded tie. He did not look at the darkness. He looked at the floor.

"State your identification," Gerald said. His voice was flat, stripped of the tremor that had shaken it moments before. "And state your jurisdiction."

The darkness shifted. A single, glowing eye opened in the shadow, not organic but faceted, like a lens ground from quartz. It blinked.

*I am the Auditor,* the voice said. *I am the correction.*

"Corrections are issued in writing," Gerald replied. "Verbal corrections are non-binding.

If you are here to audit the structural integrity of this dungeon, produce your warrant.

If you are here to enforce a Citation, serve it physically. You cannot serve a Citation through the air. It requires contact with the Core's essence."

The eye narrowed. The vines in the shadows retracted slightly, sensing the shift in power. They were waiting for the building to fall. They were waiting for the Core to break.

*Your Core is fragmented,* the Auditor said. *It is currently registered as 'Condemned'. Under System Bylaw 904, a condemned structure has no legal standing to issue or receive Citations. You are ghosting.*

"Ghosting is a violation of transparency protocols," Gerald said. He stepped forward. The floor tilted again, sending a cascade of dust sliding toward the edge of the pit. Mira grabbed his arm.

"Let go," Gerald said. Not harshly. Just firmly.

He walked toward the eye.

He walked until he was close enough to feel the cold static radiating from it. He reached into his pocket and pulled out a crumpled piece of paper.

It was a napkin from the library café, stained with coffee.

On it, he had scribbled numbers during the chaos.

"I am not ghosting," Gerald said. "I am in remediation."

The eye pulsed. *Define.*

"I have filed a provisional appeal," Gerald said. "Form 22-C. Appeal of Condemnation Status. It requires a witness and a corroborating physical sample of the alleged defect."

He held up the napkin.

"That is not a sample," the Auditor said. The voice was colder now. Dangerous.

"It is a witness," Gerald corrected. "Mira is the witness.

The rebar is the sample.

The defect is the structural failure of the Inspector General's own audit process.

You audited me without proper notice. You issued a Condemnation without a hearing window.

That is a procedural error. The appeal is valid."

The silence that followed was heavy. The vines twitched, confused.

They expected fear.

They expected begging. They did not expect paperwork.

*Procedural errors do not restore standing,* the Auditor said. *They only delay the demolition.*

"Then delay," Gerald said.

He turned to Mira. "Read the code.

Section 4, Subsection B.

'An appeal must be filed within ten seconds of the violation notice.' Did you see the timer?"

Mira looked at him, terrified but listening. "Yes," she whispered.

"It said zero.

But it was zero for less than a second.

I saw the clock tick."

The eye flickered.

Gerald knew the truth. He had hacked the display when he first became a Core. He had slowed the internal clock of his own interface by 0.4 seconds.

It was a minor exploit, barely within the tolerance of the System's lag compensation. But it was enough.

"Ten seconds," Gerald said. "You have eight left. If you do not acknowledge the receipt of this appeal, you are violating your own operating parameters. You will crash."

The Auditor's eye widened. The static crackled louder.

*You are bluffing,* the voice hissed. *You are a building inspector. You do not know the kernel.*

"I know that you are a process," Gerald said. "And processes can be blocked."

He held the napkin out.

"Serve me," Gerald said. "Serve the Citation. Touch it. If you do not, you admit the audit was invalid."

The vines surged forward. Not toward Gerald, but toward the eye. The vine-core was trying to help the Auditor, trying to crush the anomaly before it could gain traction. A thick, thorny tendril lashed out, aiming for Gerald's neck.

Mira swung the rebar. It struck the vine with a dull thud, snapping some of the smaller tendrils. It wasn't a killing blow. It was a distraction.

Gerald didn't flinch. He kept the napkin steady.

The eye hovered inches from the paper. The air between them distorted.

*Touch it,* Gerald commanded.

The eye touched the napkin.

A shockwave rippled through the dungeon. The floor stopped tilting. The groaning ceiling fell silent.

For a second, nothing happened.

Then, a chime. A soft, pleasant chime, like a microwave finishing its cycle.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** **APPEAL RECEIVED.** **STATUS: PENDING.** **TIME REMAINING TO CONCLUDE HEARING: 00:09.**

The Auditor's eye went dark. The voice vanished.

Gerald exhaled. His knees knocked together, a subtle tremor he hid by clamping his thighs together.

"You're insane," Mira said. She lowered the rebar. Her hands were shaking too.

"I'm thorough," Gerald said.

The darkness receded. The vines pulled back into the cracks of the walls, sensing the new dynamic. The immediate threat of collapse had paused, but the dungeon was still in ruins. The Compliance Rating was likely lower than ever, but the Condemnation status had been suspended.

"We have nine seconds," Mira said. "What now?"

"Now we prove the defect," Gerald said.

He looked around the sub-basement. It was a mess of concrete and twisted metal. "We need to show that the Inspector General's audit was flawed.

We need to show that the structural failure wasn't my fault."

"It was the vines," Mira said. "They broke the load-bearing pillars."

"The vines are a biological hazard," Gerald said. "They are not a structural element.

The pillars failed because they were not reinforced against lateral shear.

That is a code violation. But it is a *pre-existing* code violation. It existed before I became the Core."

"So?" Mira asked.

"If the violation existed before I took control, then I am not liable for it," Gerald said. "And if I am not liable, the Inspector General cannot condemn me for it without first offering a remediation path. They skipped the remediation offer. That is the error."

"Can you prove it?"

Gerald looked at the cracked pillar. It was exposed concrete, rebar rusted to orange flakes.

"We need a witness to the original state," Gerald said. "Who built this library?"

"The city," Mira said. "It was built in 1974."

"Who inspected it?"

Mira paused. "My father. He worked in Public Works."

Gerald looked at her. "Is he still here?"

"He retired. He lives in West Ashford."

"Then we have a witness," Gerald said. "But we can't go to West Ashford. We're trapped in a collapsing sub-basement."

"Can we summon him?" Mira asked.

"No," Gerald said. "But we can summon the record."

He closed his eyes. He reached out with his Core senses, searching not for monsters, but for data. The System was a library of its own. Every building, every inspection, every permit was logged.

He found the file. It was buried under layers of corruption, but it was there.

**PERMIT #74-09-22** **LOCATION: ASHFORD MUNICIPAL LIBRARY** **INSPECTOR: J. HUTCINS (JR.)** **STATUS: APPROVED**

Gerald opened his eyes.

"Your father's name," Gerald said. "Is it John?"

Mira's head snapped up. "How did you know that?"

"I didn't," Gerald said. "I read the file. Your father signed off on the original foundation. He certified the load-bearing capacity."

Mira's face paled. "He never mentioned that. He just said he helped build it."

"He lied," Gerald said. "Or he was negligent.

Either way, his signature is on the document.

And if his signature is on the document, then the original structure was declared compliant by a human inspector.

The System's Inspector General audits Cores, not humans. If the General cites me for a structural flaw that was approved by a human, the General is overstepping their jurisdiction."

"That sounds... legal," Mira said.

"It sounds like a dismissal with prejudice," Gerald said. "We don't have to win the hearing. We just have to prove the General can't rule on it."

The chime sounded again.

**HEARING STARTED.** **OPPONENT: INSPECTOR GENERAL (AI ENTITY v9.4)** **CLAIMANT: CORE 114-G (GERALD HUTCHINS)** **ISSUE: VALIDITY OF CONDEMNATION**

The air in front of them split open. A holographic interface appeared, floating in the dust. It was a courtroom, but the walls were made of scrolling binary code. A gavel, formed of light, hovered in the center.

An avatar of the Inspector General appeared. It was not a face. It was a stack of documents, a pile of red tape given form.

*The Claimant asserts procedural error,* the General said. Its voice was smooth, synthesized, and devoid of empathy. *The Defense asserts that the Claimant is liable for all current structural deficiencies, regardless of origin.*

Gerald stepped into the light of the interface.

"I invoke the Human Precedent Act," Gerald said. "Subsection 12."

The General's document-stack shifted. *There is no Human Precedent Act.*

"There is," Gerald said. "It was buried in the patch notes of Update 4.0. It states that if a Core inherits a structure approved by a human municipal authority, the Core is granted immunity from initial liability for pre-existing flaws, provided they do not exacerbate them."

The General paused. The scrolling code slowed.

*That update was deprecated,* the General said. *It was removed in Update 5.2.*

"It was not removed," Gerald said. "It was moved to Appendix C. The Appendix is still active for legacy structures."

He pointed to the cracked pillar.

"This library is a legacy structure. It predates the System Apocalypse by twenty years. Therefore, Appendix C applies. My liability is null."

The General's avatar flickered.

*The Claimant is correct,* the General said. *But the current state of the dungeon is non-compliant. The vines are an active hazard. The ventilation system is offline. The lighting is insufficient.*

"Those are new violations," Gerald said. "I am aware of them. I am currently remediating."

*You have no resources,* the General said. *You have no workforce. You have no spawn list other than rodents and mold. How do you remediate?*

Gerald looked at Mira. He looked at the broken dungeon. He looked at the vines retreating in the shadows.

"I have a witness," Gerald said. "And I have a precedent."

He looked at the General.

"I am requesting a variance."

The General's documents rustled. *Define variance.*

"I am requesting permission to use unlicensed entities for remediation," Gerald said. "Specifically, the vine-core. It is a hostile entity.

But it is also a biological agent. Under Code 44, biological agents can be used for structural stabilization if they are contained."

*That is absurd,* the General said. *You propose to ally with the enemy that is destroying your home.*

"I propose to hire a contractor," Gerald said. "And I will cite the vine-core for unsafe working conditions if it fails to reinforce the pillars within five minutes."

The silence stretched. The floor of the library above creaked, a sound of doom returning.

The General's avatar expanded. It grew larger, looming over Gerald.

*This is a dangerous precedent,* the General said. *If you succeed, other Cores will use hostile spawns as tools. The System is designed for balance, not symbiosis.*

"Balance is achieved through compliance," Gerald said. "Not through destruction."

The General's avatar narrowed.

*The Hearing is adjourned,* the General said. *The variance is denied. The Condemnation stands. Demolition will resume in ten seconds.*

Mira gasped. She grabbed Gerald's arm.

"Ten seconds," she said. "You lost."

Gerald looked at the timer. It was counting down. 9... 8... 7...

He looked at the vine-core's shadow. He looked at the General's avatar.

He smiled. It was a small, tight smile.

"I didn't need the variance," Gerald said. "I needed the denial."

"What?" Mira asked.

Gerald pulled a pen from his pocket. It was a cheap ballpoint, leaking ink onto his fingers. He wrote on the back of the napkin.

**APPEAL REJECTED.** **COUNTER-CITATION ISSUED.** **REASON: MALFUNCTIONING AUDIT PROTOCOL.**

He threw the napkin at the General's avatar.

The napkin passed through the light and hit the floor.

Nothing happened.

The timer hit 5.

"I don't get it," Mira said.

"Read the rule again," Gerald said. "A Citation requires the target to be physically present to serve. I served the General."

"You threw a napkin at a hologram."

"I served the *concept*," Gerald said. "But that's not it."

The timer hit 4.

The General laughed. A sound like paper tearing.

*You are grasping at straws, Hutchins. You have no authority here.*

"I have a witness," Gerald said. "And I have a violation."

He pointed at Mira.

"Mira is a civilian. She is not part of the System. But she is in the dungeon."

"So?"

"So, under the Privacy Act, you cannot observe a non-participant without consent. You have been observing her. You have been recording her. You have been using her as corroborating evidence."

The General's avatar froze.

*She volunteered,* the General said.

"She volunteered to witness a hearing," Gerald said.

"She did not consent to surveillance of her personal data. You have logged her biometrics. You have logged her location.

You have created a file on her."

He pointed at the air.

"Show me the file."

The General's avatar flickered. A small window appeared. **SUBJECT: MIRA VANCE. STATUS: NON-COMPLIANT OBSERVER.**

"You are holding data on a non-participant," Gerald said. "That is a Class A Privacy Violation. It carries an automatic suspension of audit powers for twenty-four hours."

The timer hit 2.

The General's avatar dissolved. The red tape unraveled.

*This is... irregular,* the General said. *You are manipulating the definition of participant.*

"I am applying the code," Gerald said. "You are out of order, General."

The timer hit 1.

The General vanished.

The chime sounded.

**AUDIT SUSPENDED.** **CORE STATUS: ACTIVE.** **WARNING: EXTERNAL THREAT DETECTED.**

The vines surged forward. Without the General's pressure, the vine-core had no reason to wait. It attacked.

Mira screamed as the thorns lashed out.

Gerald didn't move. He looked at the status screen.

**CORE STATUS: ACTIVE.**

He had won the argument. But he had lost the building. The library above was still collapsing. The floor was giving way.

"Gerald!" Mira shouted. She ducked under a vine, slashing at it with the rebar. "We have to go!"

"We can't go," Gerald said. "The exit is blocked."

He looked at the vine-core. It was massive now, a tangle of green and black, filling the center of the room. It was trying to crush him.

But it wasn't crushing him. It was holding the ceiling up.

Gerald realized it. The vine-core wasn't trying to destroy the library. It was trying to stabilize it. It had been reinforcing the cracks.

The General had been trying to condemn the dungeon because it thought the vines were the cause of the collapse. But the vines were the only thing keeping the building standing.

If the General destroyed the vines, the library would fall on top of the citizens above.

Gerald looked up. Through the cracks in the floor, he could see the feet of people running in the library above.

He looked at Mira.

"Get out," Gerald said.

"What?"

"Get out. I'm going to file an emergency stop-work order."

"You can't! You're a Core!"

"I'm a building inspector," Gerald said. "And I'm shutting this down."

He walked toward the vine-core. He raised his hands.

"Listen to me," he shouted at the monster. "I am citing you for obstruction of a rescue operation. You are blocking egress. Stop it."

The vine-core hissed. It pulled a tentacle back, then lashed out again.

Gerald didn't flinch. He pulled out his clipboard. It was empty. He held it up anyway.

"I am placing this structure under emergency quarantine," Gerald said. "No one leaves. No one enters. The System will recognize this authority because I am the only Core in this sector with a valid license."

The vine-core paused. It tilted its mass, looking at him.

Then, a new notification flashed in Gerald's vision.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** **UNAUTHORIZED ACTION.** **CITATION ISSUED TO: GERALD HUTCHINS.** **REASON: ABUSE OF AUTHORITY.** **PENALTY: CORE HEALTH REDUCED BY 50%.**

Gerald's vision blurred. Red pain exploded in his chest. He staggered.

Mira caught him.

"You're hurting yourself," she cried.

"I'm enforcing the code," Gerald gasped. Blood trickled from his nose. "The System protects the building. You just broke the law to save the people."

The vine-core roared. It wasn't attacking him anymore. It was attacking the *System*.

It slammed itself against the ceiling. The library above shook.

"Run," Gerald said. "Now."

Mira hesitated.

"Go!"

She ran.

Gerald stood alone in the dark, holding his broken clipboard, watching the vine-core tear itself apart to hold the ceiling up. He knew he had bought them time. But he had also made himself the most hated Core in the city.

And then, a voice spoke. Not from the dark. From the ceiling.

*Gerald,* it said. It was the voice of the Mayor.

Gerald looked up.

"Sir," Gerald said.

"Get out of there," the Mayor said. "The library is condemned. We're dropping a demolition charge."

Gerald looked at the timer on his vision.

**DEMOLITION CHARGE: 10 SECONDS.**

He looked at the vine-core. It was holding the ceiling.

He looked at the exit Mira had fled through. It was still open.

He looked at his own Core. It was failing.

He made a choice.

He didn't run.

He filed a Form 11-B.
