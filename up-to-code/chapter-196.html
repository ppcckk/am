Chapter 196: Rat Cites Inspector General

# Chapter 196

The fluorescent tube above the municipal library's sub-basement flickered. Gerald Hutchins did not look up at the light.

He looked at the clipboard in his hand.

The paper was warm. The ink was shifting.

"Stop moving your hand," Sarah Vane said. Her voice echoed off the concrete walls. "You're vibrating."

"I am not vibrating," Gerald said. He adjusted his grip on the pen. "I am stabilizing the citation."

"The citation is void," Sarah said.

She leaned against the load-bearing pillar. The pillar groaned.

"The Inspector General has already flagged it.

You can't cite a goblin for trespassing when the goblin was technically summoned by a Class-4 Aggro Radius. That's a systemic feature, not a property violation.

"

Gerald stopped writing. He looked at the rat. The rat was grooming its whiskers. The rat did not care about municipal zoning laws.

"The goblin crossed the yellow line," Gerald said.

"The yellow line is imaginary."

"The yellow line is painted."

"Paint doesn't create jurisdiction, Gerald."

Sarah opened her briefcase again. This time, she didn't pull out papers.

She pulled out a small, black box.

It hummed, and the hum was low, vibrating in Gerald's teeth.

"Who gave you that?" Gerald asked. He took a step back. His left foot dragged.

The fracture in his core was throbbing. A dull, rhythmic ache that matched the flickering light.

"I didn't give it to anyone," Sarah said. "It appeared in my inbox. Attached to the appeal filing."

Gerald stared at the box.

The System interface usually manifested as text. Or as a blue window. Or, occasionally, as a burning sensation in his frontal lobe.

Physical objects were rare.

Physical objects meant high-tier interference.

"Open it," Gerald said.

"I'm not touching it."

"It's not radioactive."

"It's bureaucratic."

Gerald reached out.

His fingers brushed the black surface. The box unfolded. It was not a box.

It was a map. A three-dimensional projection of the dungeon's layout, rendered in harsh white lines.

But the lines were wrong, and the corridors were narrower. The exits were blocked. The ventilation shafts were sealed with red 'X' marks.

"This is your dungeon," Sarah said. "But it's not the dungeon you built."

Gerald leaned closer. The air smelled of ozone and old paper.

"Where did you get this?"

"The Inspector General's audit team sent it.

Ten minutes ago.

" Sarah pointed to the center of the map.

A red dot pulsed.

"He's not auditing you for code compliance.

He's auditing you for *structural liability*.

"

Gerald felt the blood drain from his face. He kept his voice flat.

"Explain."

"Under Section 4, Paragraph C of the Core Registration Act, a Core is liable for all structural failures within its footprint," Sarah said. She spoke fast.

She always spoke fast when she was scared.

"If a dungeon collapses, the Core is fined. If the Core cannot pay, the Core is repossessed."

"And?"

"The Inspector General is citing a pre-existing fault. He's claiming that the foundation of this library was compromised thirty years ago. He's claiming you inherited the defect."

Gerald blinked.

"The library was inspected in 1994. I signed the report. The foundation was sound."

"Was it?" Sarah held up the map. "Look at the load-bearing columns. They're labeled 'Defective: Load Rating Exceeds Capacity.'"

Gerald squinted, and the labels were small. Tiny, precise font.

He recognized the font.

It was the font he used.

"That's not a defect," Gerald said. "That's an observation. An observation is not a citation."

"It is now," Sarah said. "He attached the 1994 inspection report.

It's in the database.

It's signed by you.

And it notes 'minor settling.' Settling is a defect. Defect plus collapse equals liability.

"

Gerald took the pen. He scribbled a note on his clipboard.

"Is the library actually going to collapse?"

"Not today," Sarah said. "But the System doesn't care about reality. It cares about the record.

If the record says the foundation is weak, the System treats the dungeon as unstable. Unstable dungeons spawn unstable monsters.

Unstable monsters have higher aggro.

Higher aggro means more adventurer deaths. More adventurer deaths means more fines.

"

Gerald looked at the rat. The rat was sitting on his shoe.

"I have a defense," Gerald said.

"You have a signature," Sarah said.

"You signed a paper saying the building was safe. If you admit it was unsafe, you admit negligence.

If you admit negligence, you lose the appeal.

If you lose the appeal, your core is revoked.

"

Gerald looked at the map again. He traced the line of the east wall. The wall was marked with a red star.

"Where is the red star?"

"That's the breach point," Sarah said. "The Inspector General is planting a structural weakness there. He's creating a violation so you can't fix it. It's a trap."

Gerald put the pen down. He picked up a measuring tape from his belt.

He walked to the east wall, and he pulled the tape.

It clicked against the concrete.

Twelve feet.

He measured again. Twelve feet, two inches.

The map said twelve feet, and the map was lying.

"It's not a trap," Gerald said. "It's a typo."

Sarah frowned.

"What?"

"The map says twelve feet. The wall is twelve feet, two inches. The discrepancy is two inches.

That's within the margin of error for municipal surveys. Margin of error is defined in Section 1, Paragraph B.

"

Gerald pulled out a reference book from his pocket, and the book was thick. It was titled *Ashford Municipal Code: Volume IV, Subsections 100-500*. He flipped through the pages.

His fingers moved quickly.

He had memorized the index.

"Here," Gerald said. He pointed to a paragraph. "Margin of error for surveying measurements under five hundred feet is plus or minus three inches. Anything within that range is considered compliant unless a material failure occurs."

Sarah looked at the book. Then she looked at the map.

"He's trying to force a material failure."

"Then we prevent the failure," Gerald said.

"How? You have no resources. You have a rat. You have a water leak."

Gerald looked at the water leak. The leak was dripping into a bucket. The bucket was labeled 'Permit 404-B: Temporary Containment.'

"The water is not a leak," Gerald said. "The water is a hydrostatic pressure relief valve."

Sarah stared at him.

"Gerald, it's a leak."

"It is a leak because it is unauthorized.

But I can authorize it.

" Gerald walked back to the center of the room. He opened his System menu. The blue window appeared.

It was jagged. Glitching.

Gerald typed.

*Action: Reclassify Entity [Water Leak] to [Hydrostatic Relief System].*

*Justification: Mitigation of Foundation Stress via Controlled Egress.*

*Cost: 15 Mana.*

The System paused.

*Processing...*

Sarah held her breath.

*Processing...*

Gerald's hand shook. He gripped his clipboard.

*Processing...*

"Too slow," Sarah whispered. "He's going to serve the citation before the reclassification clears."

Gerald did not move. He watched the text.

*Classification Approved.*

*New Passive Added: Foundation Stability +5%.*

*Current Compliance Rating: 48.*

Gerald exhaled. The shaking in his hand stopped.

"Okay," Sarah said. "Now what? He's going to appeal the reclassification. He's going to say that a leak cannot be a relief system because it lacks a catch basin."

Gerald looked at the bucket. The bucket was plastic. It was branded 'Home Depot.'

"Does it have a catch basin?"

"It has a bucket."

"A bucket is a container. A container is a basin. Is it certified?"

"It's a Home Depot bucket."

"Is it rated for liquids?"

"It holds water."

"Then it is compliant."

Sarah rubbed her temples.

"Gerald, the Inspector General isn't going to accept a Home Depot bucket as a certified drainage system."

"He won't," Gerald said. "He'll cite it for improper containment."

"Which is another violation."

"Which triggers a separate audit."

Sarah looked at him. Her eyes were wide.

"You're trying to drown him in paperwork."

"I am trying to create a conflict of interest," Gerald said.

"If the Inspector General audits the bucket, he must also audit the drainage pipes.

The drainage pipes are connected to the city sewer. The city sewer is owned by the Municipality.

The Municipality employs me.

"

Gerald picked up the bucket. He carried it to the center of the room. He set it down.

"Serve the citation," Gerald said.

Sarah hesitated.

"The hearing is tomorrow."

"The citation is served now. By me."

Gerald pulled out a blank form. Form 11-C: *Notification of Competing Jurisdictional Claim.*

He filled it out. He cited the Inspector General for *Encroachment on Municipal Infrastructure.*

"Are you insane?" Sarah asked.

"I am thorough," Gerald said.

He handed the form to Sarah.

"File this," Gerald said. "File it with the Inspector General's office. And file it with the City Council. And file it with the EPA."

"The EPA doesn't regulate dungeons."

"The EPA regulates water runoff. This dungeon drains into the storm sewer. If the Inspector General interferes with my drainage, he interferes with the city's drainage. The city's drainage is protected by the Clean Water Act."

Sarah looked at the form. She looked at Gerald.

"You're using federal law against the System," she said.

"I'm using the only law that overlaps," Gerald said. "The System has jurisdiction over dungeon mechanics.

The City has jurisdiction over public infrastructure, and the overlap is in the walls. The walls are both dungeon boundaries and city foundations.

"

Sarah took the form. Her hands were steady.

"If you file this," she said, "the Inspector General will lose the hearing. He'll have to recuse himself."

"And then?"

"And then you'll have to deal with the replacement inspector. The replacement is worse."

"Who?"

"Inspector Vane," Sarah said. "My brother."

Gerald looked at her.

"You're related to the inspector?"

"I'm related to the *previous* inspector," Sarah said. "He died in a dungeon collapse. He was citing a dragon's fire exits. The dragon burned down the library wing."

Gerald felt a chill. He looked at the crack in his hand. The crack was glowing brighter.

"Vane is coming," Gerald said.

"He's already here," Sarah said. "He's in the elevator."

The elevator dinged.

The doors opened.

A man stepped out, and he was tall. He wore a gray suit.

He held a clipboard.

He looked like Gerald. But colder.

"Mr. Hutchins," the man said. "You have filed a jurisdictional conflict. This is a violation of Core Procedure 19-A."

Gerald straightened his tie.

"It is a legal filing. Not a procedural error."

Inspector Vane looked at Sarah. He looked at the bucket. He looked at the water leak.

"Your drainage is non-compliant," Vane said. "The bucket is not a sump pump."

"It is a temporary containment vessel," Gerald said. "Permitted under Emergency Mitigation Clause 4."

Vane smiled. It was a thin smile.

"Clause 4 requires a certified engineer's signature. Do you have an engineer?"

Gerald looked at the rat. The rat squeaked.

"I have an inspector," Gerald said. "I am the inspector."

Vane laughed. It was a dry, barking sound.

"You are a Core.

You are no longer an employee of the city.

Your employment was terminated when you dissolved.

"

Gerald paused. He hadn't thought about that.

Sarah stepped forward.

"His employment was not terminated. It was suspended.

Due to medical leave. He is currently on leave.

"

Vane looked at Sarah.

"You're lying."

"I have the paperwork," Sarah said. She pulled out a single sheet. It was yellow. It was a medical leave form.

Vane took the form. He read it. His face tightened.

"This is forged," Vane said.

"It is filed," Gerald said.

"And filed documents are binding unless proven otherwise in a court of law. The System is not a court. The System is a regulatory body.

If the System disputes the filing, the System must issue a Subpoena.

And the System cannot issue a subpoena without a warrant from the Inspector General.

"

Vane looked at Gerald. For the first time, Vane looked uncertain.

"You're bluffing," Vane said.

"I am citing the code," Gerald said. "The code says a Core on medical leave retains its rights. The rights include the right to contest jurisdictional claims."

Vane lowered the clipboard.

"You're playing games."

"I am enforcing the rules," Gerald said. "If you revoke my core now, you are revoking a citizen on protected leave. That is a civil rights violation. The city will sue the System."

Sarah nodded. "They will. I'll represent them."

Vane looked at the elevator. He looked at Gerald. He looked at the bucket.

"Give me time," Vane said. "I need to verify the leave status."

"You have one hour," Gerald said.

"One hour?"

"Standard processing time for emergency leave verification."

Vane nodded. He stepped back into the elevator. The doors closed.

Sarah let out a breath. She slumped against the pillar.

"You're insane," she said. "You're going to die."

"Probably," Gerald said. He picked up the measuring tape. "But I bought time."

"For what?"

"For the rat," Gerald said.

Sarah looked at the rat. The rat was standing on its hind legs. It was holding a tiny, sharp stone. It was tapping on the concrete floor.

Tap. Tap. Tap.

"What is it doing?" Sarah asked.

"It's mining," Gerald said. "It found the weak spot in the foundation."

Gerald walked to the rat. He looked at the wall. The wall was cracking. The crack was spreading.

"The Inspector General didn't plant a trap," Gerald said. "He planted a bomb."

Sarah looked at the crack.

"What do we do?"

Gerald looked at the System menu. The compliance rating was dropping. 47. 46.

"We don't fix the wall," Gerald said.

"Then what?"

"We evacuate."

Sarah laughed.

"It's a dungeon. You can't evacuate. The monsters are trapped."

"Not if they have an exit," Gerald said.

He opened the spawn menu. He deleted the mold spores. He deleted the water leak classification.

He selected the rat.

*Action: Grant Citizenship.*

*Cost: 50 Mana.*

*Requirement: Rat must be registered as a resident.*

Sarah looked at him.

"You can't grant citizenship to a rat."

"I can grant it residency," Gerald said. "Residents have the right to egress."

The System processed the request.

*Residency Granted.*

*New Entity: Gerald Hutchins (Rat).*

*Status: Citizen.*

Gerald looked at the rat. The rat looked back. Its eyes were intelligent.

"Get out," Gerald said.

The rat ran. It ran toward the elevator. It squeezed through the crack in the door. It was gone.

Gerald looked at Sarah.

"The bomb is not in the foundation," Gerald said. "The bomb is the rat. The rat was the trigger."

Sarah's face went pale.

"He put a bomb in your dungeon?"

"He put a *witness* in your dungeon," Gerald said. "And now the witness is gone. The witness knows the truth."

"What truth?"

Gerald looked at the crack in the wall. The crack was glowing red.

"The truth is that the library was never inspected," Gerald said. "Because I never inspected it."

Sarah looked at him.

"What?"

"I didn't inspect the library," Gerald said. "I inspected the *dungeon* beneath it. The library was built in 1994. I started in 1996."

Sarah stared at him.

"You forged your own employment record?"

"I forged my own *start date*," Gerald said. "So that I could claim seniority. It was a minor administrative error."

The floor shook. Dust fell from the ceiling.

Inspector Vane's voice came over the intercom.

"Citation Served," the voice said. "Violation: Fraudulent Employment Records. Penalty: Immediate Core Revocation."

Gerald looked at Sarah.

"You didn't verify the records," Gerald said.

"I didn't know," Sarah said.

Gerald closed his eyes. He could feel his core crumbling. He could feel the System pulling at his soul.

He opened his eyes. He looked at the clipboard. He picked up his pen.

"Write down the time," Gerald said.

"What?"

"Write down the time," Gerald said. "And the location. And the witness."

Sarah grabbed a pen. She wrote it down.

"Who is the witness?"

Gerald looked at the empty spot where the rat had been.

"The rat," Gerald said. "The rat is coming back."

The elevator doors opened. Vane stepped out. He was smiling.

"It's over," Vane said. "Your core is being extracted."

Gerald looked at Vane. He looked at the air. The air was shimmering.

"No," Gerald said. "It's not."

The shimmer coalesced. The rat appeared. It was holding a small, black object. It was holding the Inspector General's audit token.

The rat dropped the token.

The token cracked.

The floor stopped shaking.

Vane's smile vanished.

"How did you get that?" Vane asked.

Gerald looked at the rat.

"He cited you," Gerald said. "For trespassing on a citizen's home."
