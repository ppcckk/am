Chapter 367: Appeal Denied

# Chapter 367

The pen did not hesitate. Gerald pressed the nib into the slate until the graphite snapped.

He wrote.

The armor floated forward. The gear inside the helmet spun faster, a metallic ratchet sound echoing off the damp concrete.

"Thirty seconds elapsed," the armor said. "Appeal denied. Citation stands."

Gerald's hand shook. Not from fear.

From the indignity of a broken tool, and he reached into his vest pocket, retrieved a spare mechanical pencil, and uncapped it. The plastic cap fell to the floor. He did not pick it up.

"You are citing a Core for being too efficient," Gerald said. His voice was flat. It sounded like a man reading a lease agreement.

"The System requires struggle," the armor said. "Your dungeon provides none. Therefore, it is not a dungeon. It is a storage unit."

"I am not appealing the classification," Gerald said. "I am appealing the service."

The gear stopped spinning.

"Explain," the armor said.

"I served you." Gerald pointed at the empty suit of armor.

"You are standing in a designated egress route. You are blocking emergency exit 4-B.

Under Municipal Ordinance 74.2, obstruction of an egress path is a Class 2 misdemeanor.

I have been trying to serve you a citation for thirty seconds. You did not let me speak. That is non-compliant behavior.

"

The armor tilted its head. The empty helmet stared at the clipboard.

Dale groaned on the floor. He tried to sit up, clutching his ribs.

Gerald ignored him. He looked at the slate. The text was changing again.

**STATUS: PENDING.** **ISSUE: PROCEDURE VIOLATION.**

"Your audit is invalid," Gerald said. "You have not provided a physical presence sufficient for service.

You are floating.

You have no address.

You cannot be served.

"

The gear clicked.

"I am the Auditor," the armor said. "I am everywhere."

"You are currently occupying airspace above a public right-of-way," Gerald said. "I am invoking the Right of Immediate Remediation."

Gerald slammed his hand onto the slate, and he didn't write. He tapped.

A new command appeared.

**COMMAND: ENFORCE ZONING.**

The air in the sub-basement warped. The mold spores in the corner stopped drifting. The water leak in the ceiling ceased dripping.

The suit of armor jerked.

"Error," the armor said. "Zoning conflict."

Gerald walked past the armor. He walked past Dale. He walked to the center of the room. He opened his clipboard to a fresh page.

"I am declaring this zone a Commercial Residential Hybrid," Gerald said. "Under the new designation, floating entities are prohibited. You are grounded."

The armor dropped.

It hit the concrete with a clang that sounded like a church bell. The gear fell out of the helmet and rolled across the floor.

Dale looked up, eyes wide.

"Gerald?"

Gerald did not look at him. He looked at the slate.

**NEW MECHANIC: ZONING OVERRIDE.** **COST: 40% OF CURRENT MANA POOL.** **DURATION: 10 MINUTES.** **WARNING: LOCAL REALITY UNSTABLE.**

The walls bleeded.

Not blood. Ink.

Thick, black ink oozed from the cracks in the foundation. It spread across the floor, forming lines. Grids. Coordinates.

"What are you doing?" Dale whispered.

"I am rezoning," Gerald said. He grabbed a roll of red tape from his belt. He started taping the floor.

"If I cite the Auditor, I lose my license. But if I cite the *space* the Auditor is in, I win."

"You can't zone reality, Gerald."

"I can zone anything I can measure," Gerald said.

He tore the tape. He placed it in a perfect square around the fallen armor.

"This is now a Restricted Storage Area.

No movement allowed.

No combat allowed. No magic allowed.

"

The ink on the walls pulsed.

The System hated this. Gerald could feel it in his teeth. A low hum, like a high-voltage wire.

But the System also hated messes. And Gerald was making the mess official.

The armor tried to rise. It could not move. The zoning lines glowed red. The gear on the floor spun uselessly, trapped inside the perimeter.

**VIOLATION: UNAUTHORIZED MOVEMENT IN RESTRICTED ZONE.** **PENALTY: STAT LOCK.**

The armor froze.

Gerald exhaled. His shoulders dropped two inches.

He turned to Dale.

"Get up."

Dale scrambled to his feet. He looked at the frozen armor, then at the ink spreading across the ceiling.

"You broke the game," Dale said.

"I fixed the code," Gerald said.

"It's not a code. It's a system."

"It is a set of rules," Gerald said. "And rules have loopholes. I found one."

He walked to the door. He opened it.

The library above was dark. The emergency lights were off. The main exit was boarded up.

Gerald stared at the wood.

"Where did that come from?" he asked.

"I don't know," Dale said. "It just appeared."

Gerald stepped over the threshold. He kicked the board. It splintered.

Underneath, there was another board. And another.

The hallway was sealed.

"The Auditor," Gerald said.

"He locked us in?"

"He locked the zone," Gerald said. "We are now inside the Restricted Storage Area. The zoning applies to us, too."

Dale's face went pale.

"Can we move?"

Gerald looked at the red tape on the floor. The lines were fading, but the walls were still covered in ink.

"We have to leave the square," Gerald said. "But the door is outside the square."

He looked at the armor. The gear was still spinning.

"We need a key," Gerald said.

"We don't have keys," Dale said. "We are in a dungeon."

"Gerald, look at the ceiling."

Gerald looked up, and the ink was forming words.

**PERMIT DENIED.**

The ceiling lowerred.

It wasn't just the ink. The concrete itself was pressing down. at first, then faster.

"The zoning override is failing," Gerald said. "The Auditor is appealing the appeal."

"Then we fight him," Dale said.

"You can't fight a zoning dispute with a sword," Gerald said.

He ran to the desk in the corner of the basement. It was a relic, a heavy oak table that had survived the library's construction. Gerald grabbed a pen. He grabbed a form.

**FORM 11-C: EMERGENCY EGRESS REQUEST.**

He wrote fast. His handwriting was cramped, precise.

Dale backed away. The ceiling was six inches lower.

"Gerald, move!"

Gerald signed the form. He stamped it.

He held it up to the ceiling.

"I am filing an emergency exception!" he shouted at the ink. "Life safety override! Section 99.4!"

The ceiling stopped.

For three seconds, it hung there.

Then the ink exploded.

It sprayed outward, hitting Gerald in the chest. It hit Dale. It hit the armor.

Gerald looked down at his chest. The ink was forming a tattoo.

**CITATION ISSUED: GERALD HUTCHINS.** **VIOLATION: UNAUTHORIZED MODIFICATION OF SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE.** **PENALTY: STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY REDUCED BY 90%.** **DEBUFF: PHYSICAL DAMAGE TAKEN INCREASED BY 500%.**

Gerald felt a crack in his ribcage. Not from impact. From existence.

He gasped. He grabbed the edge of the desk.

"The audit is over," he said. His voice was wet.

"The Auditor is gone," Dale said. He pointed at the armor.

The suit was empty. The gear was still there, but it was dark. The ink had consumed it.

But the ceiling was still lowering.

"It wasn't an audit," Gerald said. "It was a demolition order."

"What?"

"I cited the Auditor.

The Auditor cited me. But I cited the *space*.

" Gerald coughed. Blood on his lips.

"I made the space non-compliant. So the System is fixing it."

"Fixing it how?"

"By removing the non-compliant parts."

"The floor," Gerald said.

The red tape vanished. The zoning lines dissolved.

And the floor beneath them turned to dust.

Dale fell.

Gerald caught the desk. The desk cracked.

"Jump!" Gerald yelled.

Dale scrambled back, landing on the only patch of solid ground near the stairs.

Gerald held on. The desk splintered. He let go.

He fell, and he did not scream. He counted.

One.

Two.

Three.

He landed on a pile of rubble. The impact shattered his left wrist. He felt it break. He felt the bone pierce the skin.

He looked up, and the basement was gone.

Above him, the library floor was intact. But below him, there was no sub-basement. There was only a hole.

And in the hole, rising from the dust, was a staircase, which was perfect. Straight. White marble. Polished to a shine.

It led down into the dark.

Gerald dragged himself to the edge of the hole. He pulled himself up using his good arm.

Dale was there. He grabbed Gerald's collar.

"You're bleeding," Dale said.

"I am compliant," Gerald said.

"You're dying."

"I am under review."

Gerald looked at the staircase.

The slate buzzed.

**NEW ALERT:** **SOURCE: SYSTEM INSPECTOR GENERAL.** **NOTICE: AUDIT COMPLETE.** **RESULT: FAILURE.** **CONSEQUENCE: DEMOLITION OF PREVIOUS STRUCTURE.** **NEW START: GERALD HUTCHINS.** **LEVEL: 1.** **DUNGEON TYPE: ABYSSAL ARCHIVE.** **COMPLIANCE RATING: 0/100.** **NOTE: YOUR PREVIOUS CITATIONS HAVE BEEN ERASED. YOUR AUTHORITY HAS BEEN REVOKED. YOU ARE NO LONGER AN INSPECTOR. YOU ARE A DEFENDANT.**

Gerald read the text. He read it again.

Then he laughed, which was a dry, rattling sound.

"They erased my citations," he said.

"That's good," Dale said. "That means you can start over."

"It means I have no leverage," Gerald said.

He looked at the white stairs.

"They built me a new dungeon," he said. "A clean slate."

"Can you go up?" Dale asked.

Gerald looked at the library above. The hole was sealed with a new slab of concrete. It was already drying.

He looked down at the abyss.

"No," Gerald said.

He pulled himself up. He stood on the edge of the abyss. The wind coming from below smelled of old paper and ozone.

"I am filing a complaint," Gerald said.

"With who?"

"With the Architect," Gerald said. "Because this staircase is not up to code."

He stepped off the edge, and he did not fall.

He walked down the white stairs.

Dale screamed.

But Gerald was already gone.

He descended into the dark, his broken wrist dangling, his clipboard clutched to his chest.

The first landing was fifty feet down.

There, waiting for him, was not a monster, which was a desk.

And sitting behind the desk was a woman.

She wore a suit identical to Gerald's. Her face was hidden behind a mask of polished steel.

She looked up.

"Inspector Hutchins," she said. "Welcome to the Appeals Court."

She held out a hand.

In her hand was a stamp, which was red.

"Sign here," she said. "Or I will cite you for loitering."

Gerald looked at the stamp. He looked at the void behind her.

He raised his pen.
