Chapter 97: Annexation Form

# Chapter 97

The Inspector General's argument was losing, and Gerald could see exactly why.

The hearing space existed as a flat grey plane beneath a featureless sky. Gerald stood at the designated third-party marker, a small circle of pale light on the floor, and watched the Inspector General pace in front of the adjudication node. The Inspector General appeared as a tall figure in a long coat with too many pockets, each one labeled with a number that changed when Gerald looked at it directly.

"The error was externally induced," the Inspector General said. Its voice had the quality of a filing cabinet being opened in an empty room.

"Core Vennick's compliance rating was accurate at the time of audit.

The variance in recorded value was caused by Citation #47, which altered the underlying data mid-audit. The error is therefore not an error. It is an artifact of concurrent enforcement action."

The adjudication node pulsed amber, and the System did not accept this framing. Gerald could see why.

The Inspector General was arguing that its own measurement was correct but its own record was wrong.

That was not a defense. That was an admission wearing a hat.

Core Vennick stood at the respondent marker, a squat mass of green light that occasionally rippled. It had not spoken. It did not need to. The Inspector General was defeating itself with the enthusiasm of a man trying to explain a parking ticket by describing the weather.

Gerald studied the audit documentation the System had made available to all parties. It hung in the air between the markers like a projected slide, dense with numbers and timestamps. He scanned it as usual, checking the inspection date, the license number, and the methodology box.

The methodology checkbox was ticked. Standard Compliance Audit, Methodology 7-C.

Gerald frowned. He pulled up the methodology definition, and it scrolled into view, a dense block of System text describing the audit procedure. He read the first three paragraphs, then the last two, then went back to the second paragraph.

He read it again.

Methodology 7-C required that the auditor establish a baseline structural assessment before measuring compliance values. A structural assessment.

Not a compliance measurement.

A structural assessment, and the two were different things. Gerald had spent thirty-one years explaining this distinction to contractors who wanted to skip the structural phase and jump straight to the electrical rough-in.

The Inspector General had not performed a structural assessment. It had gone straight to compliance measurement. The audit was therefore invalid from its first data point.

Not because the Inspector General had made an error. Because the Inspector General had not followed its own procedure.

Gerald raised his hand.

The adjudication node turned blue. Third-party speaking privileges activated.

"Third party wishes to file a Procedural Objection," Gerald said.

The Inspector General stopped pacing. Core Vennick's green mass went still.

"Procedural Objection is not a recognized action type in this hearing category," the adjudication node said. Its tone was flat and final, the way a clerk says "the form is complete" when it means "leave my desk."

Gerald pulled up the hearing rules. They were in the System's procedural appendix, subsection 14. He had read them three times in the four minutes since the hearing opened. Subsection 14, paragraph 2: "Any party with standing may challenge the procedural validity of any action taken by any other party during the hearing period."

The Inspector General's audit was an action taken during the hearing period, and the audit was the subject of the hearing. The audit was the thing being appealed. So the audit was an action taken during the hearing period.

"The audit methodology is the subject of this hearing," Gerald said. "I am challenging its procedural validity. Paragraph 2."

Silence. The adjudication node pulsed once, twice.

"Procedural Objection accepted. Grounds: methodology non-compliance. Review initiated."

The Inspector General turned to look at Gerald. Its face was a smooth oval with no features except a pair of narrow slits where eyes should be. Those slits narrowed.

"You are aware," the Inspector General said, "that if your objection is sustained, the precedent applies retroactively."

"I am aware."

"Every audit conducted under Methodology 7-C since its implementation will be subject to challenge. Every debuff applied under those audits will be contestable."

"I am aware."

The Inspector General's coat pockets all opened at once. Inside each one was a small red light. They pulsed in sequence, like a countdown.

"You are aware that Methodology 7-C has been in use for nine hundred and forty-three System days."

"I am aware."

The adjudication node pulsed amber, then green.

"Objection sustained.

Methodology 7-C found non-compliant with System Procedural Standard 14.2.1. All audits conducted under Methodology 7-C are reclassified as Procedurally Invalid. Original appeal is moot.

Error is reclassified as Methodology Variance.

No Reciprocal Audit triggered."

The grey plane shuddered. The audit documentation dissolved. The Inspector General's red lights went dark.

Gerald felt the Third Party Liability Notice dissolve. The feeling resembled a deadline extension: not quite relief. The absence of pressure. The body's slow recognition that it can stop holding its breath.

Core Vennick's green mass pulsed once and faded. The respondent was dismissed. The Inspector General stood still in the center of the grey plane, looking at nothing.

"Thank you," it said. The words sounded like they cost something.

"You're welcome," Gerald said. "I'd recommend switching to Methodology 9-A. It includes a structural pre-assessment phase. You'll lose some speed but you won't lose the audit."

The Inspector General nodded once. The grey plane dissolved.

Gerald was back in his dungeon.

The sub-basement. The handrails he had installed along the east corridor; the single confused rat, which had relocated to a nest behind the new ventilation unit.

His Compliance Rating sat at 31, a number he had been meaning to improve for three weeks.

The notification came four seconds later.

SYSTEM-WIDE NOTIFICATION: Precedent established.

Methodology 7-C reclassified as Procedurally Invalid.

All Cores who received debuffs under Methodology 7-C audits now hold standing to file Retroactive Appeal. Appeal window: 30 System days.

Total affected Cores in Eastern District: 14. Total affected Cores in Central District: 22. Total affected Cores in Northern District: 9.

Gerald read it. He set down his clipboard. He picked it up again and set it down again, which was his version of putting his hands on his hips and looking at the sky.

Fourteen Cores in his district. Twenty-two in the Central District.

Nine in the Northern District.

Forty-five Cores, all of them now able to challenge every debuff ever applied to them by the Inspector General. Every fire-safety debuff. Every ventilation debuff.

Every structural debuff.

Gone, if they chose to appeal.

And Gerald had just handed them the key, and the second notification came eleven seconds later.

RETROACTIVE APPEAL FILED: Core Vennick has filed a Retroactive Appeal against debuffs applied under Methodology 7-C, audit date 147.2. Appeal granted. Core Vennick's fire-safety debuff is reversed. Core Vennick's Compliance Rating adjusts from 44 to 67.

Gerald stared at the number.

Sixty-seven. Core Vennick was no longer a mid-tier Core with fire vulnerability, and it was a healthy Core with no fire vulnerability.

The three elite spawns Gerald had killed during the Citation #47 hearing were not dead because of a fire-safety debuff. They had been killed because of a debuff that no longer existed.

They were still dead. Gerald could not un-kill them. But the legal basis for their deaths was gone.

The third notification came before Gerald could finish processing the second.

RETROACTIVE APPEAL FILED: Core Kesswick has filed a Retroactive Appeal against debuffs applied under Methodology 7-C, audit date 203.7. Appeal granted. Core Kesswick's structural debuff is reversed. Core Kesswick's Compliance Rating adjusts from 38 to 61.

Core Kesswick.

Gerald had cited Core Kesswick for a missing load-bearing support in its main corridor.

The citation had been valid. The structural debuff had been valid.

But the audit that recorded the compliance value was now invalid, which meant the debuff was invalid, which meant the citation was built on a foundation that no longer existed.

Every citation Gerald had issued under Methodology 7-C audits was now vulnerable.

He started counting. He had issued forty-seven citations.

He could not remember which ones had been based on Methodology 7-C audit data.

He would need to check each one. Forty-seven citations. Each one a potential reversal.

Each reversal a restored Core with no memory of why it had been weakened.

The fourth notification came.

RETROACTIVE APPEAL FILED: Core Dunmarr has filed a Retroactive Appeal against debuffs applied under Methodology 7-C, audit date 189.1. Appeal granted. Core Dunmarr's electrical debuff is reversed. Core Dunmarr's Compliance Rating adjusts from 29 to 58.

The fifth. The sixth. They came in a steady stream, each one a Core getting stronger, each one a door Gerald had closed now being pried back open.

He was still counting when the sixth notification ended and a seventh appeared. This one was different.

The formatting was different, and the header was not RETROACTIVE APPEAL FILED. It was different.

STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY REVIEW INITIATED: Core Gerald Hutchins has established a precedent challenging audit methodology. In accordance with Procedural Standard 14.2.1, all Cores who have issued enforcement actions under challenged methodologies are subject to Structural Integrity Review of their own compliance infrastructure.

Core Gerald Hutchins is flagged for immediate review.

Review window: 72 System hours.

Current Compliance Rating: 31. Minimum passing threshold: 60.

Gerald read it twice.

A Structural Integrity Review was not an audit. It was not a citation. It was a full examination of the Core's own infrastructure, conducted by the System directly, with no opportunity for appeal.

If he failed, his dungeon would be condemned. Not debuffed.

Condemned.

Closed. Sealed. The rat and the mold spores and the water leak and the handrails he had installed would all be sealed under a compliance lock that no Core could override.

He had seventy-two hours to raise his Compliance Rating from 31 to 60.

He looked at his dungeon.

The east corridor, up to code. The ventilation unit, barely adequate.

The fire suppression system, which was a bucket of sand in a corner because he could not afford a proper sprinkler installation and the System did not recognize sand as fire suppression.

The electrical system, which was a lantern he had mounted to the wall at the legally required height.

Thirty-one. He needed twenty-nine more points.

The notification blinked.

STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY REVIEW: Pre-review assessment complete. Deficiencies identified: 19. Estimated remediation cost: 4,200 Core Credits. Current Core Credit balance: 112.

Gerald set down his clipboard. He picked it up. He set it down.

He opened his citation log. Forty-seven entries.

He started reading them.

Not to find the ones he could defend.

To find the ones he could reverse voluntarily. If he reversed his own citations, the Cores would not need to appeal.

If they did not appeal, the precedent would not apply to them. If the precedent did not apply to them, they would stay weak.

And if they stayed weak, they would not notice that Gerald was now the weakest thing in the district.

He was on entry fourteen when his dungeon shook. Not a tremor.

A shift.

Something heavy moving through the east corridor. He turned toward the sound and saw the wall at the far end of the corridor bowing inward. Not cracking.

Bowing.

As if something on the other side was pressing against it with slow, deliberate force.

The notification appeared.

CORE KESSWICK HAS DETECTED STRUCTURAL WEAKNESS IN ADJACENT CORE. PROXIMITY PROTOCOL INITIATED. Core Kessick will attempt annexation of adjacent Core within 72 hours.

Gerald opened the annexation form.
