Chapter 383: Violation Reported

# Chapter 383

The clipboard was the first thing to go.

It didn't fall. It didn't slide. It was simply not there, where it had been a second ago, and then it was everywhere, a chaotic scatter of black plastic and white paper across the concrete floor of the sub-basement.

Derek watched the papers settle. He didn't move. He didn't breathe. His heart hammered against his ribs with a violence that made his vision blur, a frantic drumbeat that felt entirely too loud in the silence of the library's lowest level.

He was supposed to be checking the water pressure in the boiler room. He was supposed to be wearing his City Workers Guild badge, visible over his coveralls. He was supposed to be safe.

But the air had changed.

It tasted like ozone and old paper. It tasted like fear.

Derek looked at the fire extinguisher he had been inspecting. The red canister sat upright, harmless, mundane. But the tag, the small metal rectangle with the union logo, was gone. In its place was a void, a small patch of darkness that seemed to swallow the light from the fluorescent tube overhead.

The mimic had moved.

Derek stepped back. His boot scraped against the linoleum.

"Hello?" he whispered.

The word felt stupid. Absurd.

You did not greet a threat that had just vanished into thin air.

You ran. You called security. You filed an incident report.

But Derek couldn't run.

His legs felt heavy, anchored by the sudden, crushing weight of realization. He wasn't just in a dungeon.

He was in *the* dungeon.

The one everyone in Ashford whispered about. The one that had turned the library into a fortress of compliance and concrete.

And he had broken something, and he had brought an unauthorized item into a regulated zone. A mimic, disguised as city property, had been planted by his own union brothers. They had used the loophole. They had used the trust people placed in the uniform.

Derek looked down at his hands. They were shaking. He clenched them into fists, forcing the tremor to stop, but the coldness remained. It started in his fingertips and crept up his wrists, a numbness that spread with terrifying speed.

The System interface flared in his peripheral vision. Not a notification. A warning.

**VIOLATION DETECTED: UNAUTHORIZED MATERIAL CARRIAGE.** **PENALTY: TEMPORARY MOBILITY SUPPRESSION.**

Derek tried to lift his right foot. It didn't move.

He tried to lift his left. It stayed planted.

Panic, cold and sharp, spiked in his chest. He was a builder.

He built things, and he didn't get stuck in them.

He didn't get trapped by invisible walls of code and regulation.

He looked toward the shadows where Gerald Hutchins had been sitting. The Core was gone. Vanished, just like the tag.

But the clipboard remained. Or rather, the memory of it.

Derek realized, with a sick lurch of his stomach, that he was being audited. Not by a person. By the space itself.

He tried to speak, to call out for help, but his voice was thin, reedy.

"Help," he managed.

The silence swallowed the word.

Then, a sound.

A wet, organic squelch.

Derek froze. The sound came from above. From the ceiling.

He tilted his head back, fighting the stiffness in his neck.

A single mold spore, large and pulsing with faint green light, drifted down from the dark rafters. It hung suspended in the air, hovering just above his head.

It wasn't attacking. It was waiting.

Derek held his breath. He counted the seconds. One.

Two. Three.

The spore dipped lower. It touched his coverall, right over his chest pocket.

And then it dissolved.

It sank into the fabric, into his skin, into him.

Derek gagged. He tried to swipe it away, but his arms were leaden. The numbness had reached his elbows. His fingers twitched, useless claws.

The System flashed again.

**INFECTION DETECTED: BIO-LOGICAL CORRUPTION.** **STATUS: QUARANTINE INITIATED.**

The lights in the sub-basement flickered. The hum of the ventilation system stuttered, then died.

In the absolute dark, Derek heard something else. A voice. Dry, deadpan, and impossibly calm.

"Mr. Miller."

Derek's heart stopped, and he knew that voice. Every inspector in Ashford knew that voice. It was the voice that had fined the city for improper signage.

The voice that had shut down the downtown mall for three weeks over a fire exit obstruction.

It was Gerald Hutchins.

But Gerald was a Core. Gerald didn't speak. Gerald didn't stand in the dark.

"Please remain stationary," the voice said. "You are in a non-compliant state. Movement will exacerbate the violation severity."

Derek wanted to scream. He wanted to run. He couldn't do either.

He could only listen.

"Who planted it?" the voice asked. It wasn't a question directed at Derek.

It was a statement of fact, framed as an inquiry.

"The union. The City Workers Guild. They have a new representative.

Or they have a new strategy.

"

A light clicked on.

Derek squinted, his eyes adjusting to the sudden brightness.

Gerald Hutchins stood there, and he looked exactly as he had in life, or perhaps more so.

He was taller. His suit was crisp, pressed, immaculate.

He held no clipboard. He held only a pen.

He looked at Derek. He did not look angry. He looked disappointed.

"You brought a fire extinguisher into a room designated as Class B hazardous storage," Gerald said. "The tag was forged. I can smell the ink. It smells like cheap toner and desperation."

Derek tried to speak, to explain, to beg for mercy. But his throat was tight.

Gerald stepped closer. He didn't touch Derek. He didn't need to.

"The union thinks they can exploit the public access protocols," Gerald said. "They think because you wear the badge, you are invisible to the code.

They are wrong.

The code applies to all matter entering this jurisdiction. Including employees. Including spies.

"

Gerald tapped the pen against his palm.

"You are now a violation, Mr. Miller. And I am the inspector."

Gerald turned away. He walked toward the center of the room, where the floor had begun to ripple like water.

"Derek," Gerald said, without looking back. "Do you know what happens when a non-compliant entity is removed from a compliant structure?"

Derek didn't answer. He couldn't.

"It is excised," Gerald said. "Cleanly. Efficiently. Without damaging the surrounding integrity."

The floor beneath Derek's feet softened. He felt himself sinking. Not into dirt. Into light.

He looked down. His boots were disappearing. The linoleum was turning into a viscous, white gel.

He looked up at Gerald. Gerald was already fading, his form becoming translucent, merging with the walls of the dungeon.

"File 88-B," Gerald's voice echoed, coming from everywhere and nowhere. "Subject: External Influence. Status: Resolved."

The gel rose to Derek's knees. Then his waist.

He screamed.

But the sound was dampened, absorbed by the compliance of the walls.

And then, darkness.

***

Gerald opened his eyes, and he was back in the center of the room.

The fire extinguisher stood upright, pristine. The tag was gone.

The union logo was no longer etched in his mind, or perhaps it was, but it felt distant now.

Irrelevant, and he looked at his hands. They were steady.

He looked at the floor. The gel had receded. The linoleum was solid, cold, and clean.

Derek Miller was gone.

Not dead. Gerald had not killed him.

That would have been a violation of the moral code, even if the System didn't explicitly forbid murder.

Derek had been *cited*.

His presence had been deemed a hazard to the structural integrity of the dungeon. He had been evicted.

Pushed out into the world above, stripped of his memories, or perhaps just stripped of his agency.

Gerald didn't know which. He didn't care.

What he cared about was the source.

The mold spore, which was still there. Floating in the air near the ceiling.

But it was no longer drifting aimlessly.

It was pulsing. Rhythmic. Communicating.

Gerald raised his hand. He didn't point. He didn't cast a spell. He simply held his hand up, palm open.

The spore stopped pulsing. It hovered, waiting.

Gerald focused. He didn't try to attack it. He tried to read it.

He reached out with his mind, not with magic, but with the part of himself that understood patterns. He understood load-bearing structures. He understood stress points. He understood that a single crack, if ignored, could bring down a skyscraper.

The spore was a crack, which was a message.

*You are watched,* the pattern said. *Not by the System. By the others.*

Others?

Gerald's mind raced.

The Inspector General? No.

The audit had passed, and he had met the standards. He had cleaned the ventilation. He had installed the handrails.

He was up to code.

Then who?

The union?

They had tried the mimic, which had failed. Why send a spore?

A spore was passive. A spore was a warning.

*They are coming,* the pattern continued. *Not with force. With law.*

Law.

Gerald felt a chill that had nothing to do with the temperature.

Law. The System was law.

But there were other laws.

Human laws. Municipal laws.

The union had legal standing. They had contracts. They had leverage.

If they could plant a mimic, they could plant a lawsuit. They could sue the dungeon for emotional distress. They could sue the city for negligence. They could tie Gerald up in court, in a real court, where the System didn't apply.

Where Gerald was just a man. A 54-year-old man with no body.

He looked around the sub-basement. It was perfect. It was compliant. It was safe.

But it was vulnerable.

Because it existed in the real world.

Gerald closed his eyes, and he needed to move. He needed to expand.

A 4,000-square-foot dungeon was a target.

A small, contained entity could be isolated. Could be legislated against.

He needed to be bigger, and he needed to be everywhere.

He focused on the walls. The concrete. The steel beams. The plumbing.

He pushed his awareness outward, and he felt the library above him. He felt the streets of Ashford. He felt the other dungeons, distant and hostile, like rival businesses in a mall.

But he also felt the pipes. The sewers. The electrical grids.

The infrastructure of the city, and the city was his dungeon.

And if the city was his dungeon, then every violation in the city was his to cite.

Every broken streetlight. Every pothole. Every faulty wiring.

Every corrupt official, and the System had given him a range. He had assumed the range was physical. Proximity.

But the System was a network. A grid.

Gerald opened his eyes.

A notification flashed in his mind.

**NEW MECHANIC UNLOCKED: MUNICIPAL EXTENSION.** **RANGE: CITY BOUNDARIES.** **COST: MAINTENANCE FEES.**

Gerald stared at the message.

Maintenance fees.

What did the System charge for expanding his jurisdiction?

He looked at his Compliance Rating. It had dropped.

47/100.

Why?

He looked at the mold spore. It was gone.

He looked at the door.

The door to the library. The door to the world.

The handle was turning.

Gerald didn't move. He couldn't move.

He was a Core, and he was a location.

He was a set of rules.

But the person coming through the door was not a Core.

The door creaked open.

A woman stepped in. She was young, sharp-featured, wearing a suit that cost more than Gerald's annual salary. She held a briefcase.

She looked around the empty room. She looked at the fire extinguisher. She looked at the place where Derek had been.

She smiled. It was a cold, professional smile.

"Gerald Hutchins," she said. "My name is Elara Vance. I represent the City Workers Guild Legal Team."

She walked to the center of the room. She placed her briefcase on the floor.

"We have filed a Class Action against the Ashford Municipal Library," she said. "And against its current... management structure."

Gerald felt the System tense.

*Violation,* it whispered. *External Legal Threat.*

Elara opened the briefcase. Inside was not paper. Inside was a device.

A transmitter. A jammer.

She pressed a button.

The air in the room distorted. The lights flickered.

"We are alleging wrongful termination," Elara said. "Wrongful displacement of Derek Miller. And unauthorized alteration of public infrastructure."

She looked up. She seemed to look right through Gerald.

"Are you going to cite us, Mr. Hutchins?"

Gerald tried to respond. He tried to issue a Citation.

*Target: Elara Vance.* *Violation: Trespassing.*

But the System returned an error.

**ERROR: TARGET IS OUTSIDE JURISDICTIONAL BOUNDARIES FOR CITATION.** **STATUS: LEGAL ENTITY, NOT DUNGEON ENTITY.**

Gerald froze, and she was right.

He couldn't cite her. She wasn't a Core. She wasn't a monster.

She was a citizen.

A lawyer, and she was outside his power.

Unless...

Unless he changed the jurisdiction.

Unless he made the whole city his dungeon.

But he hadn't paid the maintenance fees yet. He didn't have the resources.

Elara pulled out a phone. She dialed a number.

"I'm here," she said into the phone. "The Core is inactive. I'm starting the injunction."

Gerald felt his power slipping. The walls of his dungeon, usually so solid, felt brittle. The ventilation hum died. The light dimmed.

If she got the injunction, the city would seal the library. Board it up. Declare it condemned.

He would be buried. Not by monsters. By concrete and red tape.

He had to act. Now.

He had to find a violation she couldn't ignore.

He had to find something in the city that was broken, something that the System recognized, and make it his responsibility.

He reached out. He grabbed the nearest pipe. The water main.

It was old. Rusting.

A violation.

But it was outside his dungeon, and he needed a bridge.

He looked at Elara. She was tapping her foot. Impatient.

She was standing on a tile. A loose tile.

A tripping hazard.

In a public space.

Gerald focused. He pushed his awareness into the tile. He felt the grout, cracked and crumbling.

He didn't cite Elara. He cited the *tile*.

**CITATION ISSUED: TILE #44-B.** **VIOLATION: LOOSE SURFACE IN PUBLIC ACCESS ZONE.** **PENALTY: STRUCTURAL INSTABILITY.**

The tile beneath Elara's foot gave way, and she stumbled. She caught herself on the briefcase, but she didn't fall.

She frowned. She looked down at the hole in the floor.

"What?" she muttered.

Gerald felt the System surge. Not a big surge. A trickle. But it was enough.

Because the violation was real. And because he had cited it, the System recognized his authority over that specific point of infrastructure.

He had a foothold, and he pulled.

He pulled the tile up. The concrete beneath it cracked. The steel rebar groaned.

Elara stepped back. She looked around, confused.

"Earthquake?" she asked.

Gerald didn't answer. He couldn't.

He focused on the crack. He widened it. He reached through the crack, into the earth, into the foundation of the library.

He felt the foundations of the neighboring buildings.

The bank. The post office. The union headquarters.

He felt them all.

And he felt the union's secret server room, three blocks away.

There were files there. Files on the mimic. Files on the other Cores. Files on how to break him.

He could reach them.

But if he reached them, he would be attacking a civilian infrastructure. A violation of the highest order.

It would drop his Compliance Rating to zero.

It would delete him.

Unless.

Unless he cited them first, and he scanned the files.

They were stored on outdated hardware. Non-compliant servers. No fire suppression in the server room. No backup generators.

Violations.

Dozens of them.

Gerald smiled. A ghost of a smile, felt only by the concrete around him.

He didn't attack the server, and he cited the server room.

**CITATION ISSUED: UNION HEADQUARTERS, SUB-LEVEL 2.** **VIOLATION: INADEQUATE FIRE SUPPRESSION.** **VIOLATION: MISSING EGRESS PATH.** **VIOLATION: UNLICENSED DATA STORAGE.**

The System processed it.

The debuff hit.

Three blocks away, the lights in the union headquarters went out.

The sprinkler system failed to activate.

The doors locked.

Elara's phone rang. She answered it, her face draining of color.

"What do you mean the doors are locked?" she hissed. "The fire alarm is pulling?"

Gerald felt the connection snap into place, and he had a legal entity on the line. And he had just cited their building.

He couldn't cite her. But he could cite the building she worked for.

And she was holding the phone.

The phone was connected to the building, and the building was cited.

The citation echoed up the line.

Elara dropped the phone. It clattered on the broken tile.

Her nose bleeded.

Gerald felt the System stabilize, and he had won the first move.

But Elara was picking up the phone again. She was wiping the blood from her lip.

She looked at the darkness. She looked at the fire extinguisher.

"You think this is over?" she said, her voice trembling but fierce.

She opened the briefcase again, and she took out a second device. Larger. Black.

"This is a Class-A Nullifier," she said. "Approved by the City Council. It disables all unregistered magical phenomena within a fifty-foot radius."

She pointed it at the center of the room.

At Gerald.

"Say goodbye, Hutchins."

Gerald felt the air around him solidify. The System interface went gray.

He was being erased, and he had seconds.

He needed a new loophole.

He looked at the phone. The black device.

It was electronic, which had a battery.

A lithium-ion battery.

Gerald remembered the rules. Electrical code compliance grants lightning resistance.

He didn't have lightning resistance. He had never built up that category.

But he could change that, and he could change the category of the room.

He focused on the wiring in the walls. The old, frayed copper.

He didn't try to stop the nullifier.

He tried to overload it, and he pulled the voltage from the city grid. He pulled it from the library's main line.

He pushed it into the walls.

The lights exploded.

Sparks showered the room.

Elara screamed as the nullifier sparked in her hand.

Gerald felt the surge hit him.

It burned. It tore at his core.

But it was lightning.

And for the first time, he didn't just take it, and he absorbed it.

**CATEGORY UPDATED: ELECTRICAL COMPLIANCE.** **RESISTANCE GRANTED: LIGHTNING.**

The nullifier died.

Elara fell back, coughing.

Gerald stood in the smoke, his form flickering, but present, and he looked at her.

He looked at the door.

He looked at the city beyond.

He had survived.

But he had changed, and he was no longer just a building inspector.

He was a power source.

And the union had just declared war.

Gerald picked up the broken phone.

He dialed the number for the City Council.

"Hello," he said. His voice was static, but clear. "This is Gerald Hutchins. I'm calling to report a violation."

He hung up.

And in the shadows, the mold spore returned.

It landed on the briefcase.

It waited.
