Chapter 87: Shared Structure

# Chapter 87

Gerald's tape measure clicked against the east wall at the 4.2-meter mark. The reading was clean.

He marked it with a pencil line and moved to 4.4, and the wall was concrete, poured in place, approximately thirty centimeters thick. He could feel the System's boundary register as a faint vibration under his palm, like a phone buzzing inside a wall.

He measured 4.4. Then 4.6. Then 4.8.

The vibration intensified at 5.1 meters, and he pressed his ear to the concrete. On the other side, he could hear the wet sound of something breathing.

Core 2201-F's spawn.

Probably the cave worms. He'd cited them twice for inadequate burrow ventilation.

Gerald marked the spot. He pulled out his clipboard and wrote: Joint boundary confirmed, east wall, 5.1m to 12.3m. Approximately 7.2 linear meters of shared structure. Subsection 3.1 applies.

He checked the clock. Sixty-nine hours, forty-three minutes.

He moved to the north wall. This was the one he'd never measured properly.

His dungeon's northern edge abutted the library's foundation, but he'd always assumed the boundary was clean.

Municipal on one side, System on the other.

A simple line, and he extended the tape. Zero at the northeast corner. He walked the length, muttering numbers as was his habit.

Two meters. Four.

Six.

At eight meters, his hand hit something that wasn't concrete, which was warm. Slightly soft. It yielded under his fingers like dense clay, then sprang back.

He pressed harder.

The material had a faint pulse, rhythmic, like a heartbeat slowed to half speed.

Gerald stepped back.

He looked at the wall. It looked like concrete. It felt like concrete.

But at the eight-meter mark, it was not concrete, and he pulled out his inspection hammer and tapped the spot.

The sound was wrong.

Too dull. No ring.

He tapped again, harder. The surface cracked, and underneath it, something green and fibrous pulsed once, then went still.

He stared at it.

Core 2201-F's wall was concrete. He knew because he'd measured it, cited it, argued about it in a Hearing. This was not 2201-F's wall.

He pulled up his System interface and queried the boundary registry, and the response came back in a format he'd never seen.

Not a citation.

Not an audit notice. A Survey Notification.

BOUNDARY SURVEY INITIATED.

CORE 4409-B. STATUS: PENDING ADJUDICATION.

SHARED BOUNDARY DETECTED: NORTH WALL, 8.0m TO 14.7m.

ESTIMATED JOINT LENGTH: 6.7m. ALL SHARED BOUNDARIES TEMPORARILY FROZEN PENDING SURVEY COMPLETION. ESTIMATED SURVEY DURATION: 48 HOURS.

Gerald read it twice. Then a third time.

The Survey had frozen all his shared boundaries. Including the east wall.

Including the one he'd just spent three hours measuring and documenting.

The loophole he'd been building toward, the joint liability argument, the only thread that might keep him from being dissolved into pure System jurisdiction.

Gone. Frozen.

Inaccessible until the survey completed, and he had sixty-nine hours on his own clock. Forty-eight on the survey. If the survey completed before his clock ran out, the frozen boundaries would thaw, and he could use them. If his clock ran out first, he would be dissolved, and the survey would be moot.

But if something happened to the boundaries during the freeze. If they were damaged, severed, or altered while frozen. Then the survey would record them as non-shared. And the joint liability argument would be dead permanently.

Gerald walked back to the east wall. He placed his hand on the concrete at the 5.1-meter mark.

The vibration was still there, but it felt different.

Muted. Like something behind the wall was pressing harder on the other side.

He pressed back.

The concrete flexed.

Gerald stepped away. He looked at the wall. A hairline crack ran along the joint where his concrete met the material on the other side. It hadn't been there ten minutes ago.

Core 2201-F was pushing.

Not attacking. Pushing.

Trying to widen the crack.

Trying to create a gap in the shared boundary while it was frozen and the System couldn't register it as a violation. If the gap widened enough, the boundary would read as severed. Not shared.

Not joint. Two separate structures with a gap between them.

Gerald pulled out his pen, and he wrote on a fresh page: 2201-F attempting boundary severance during Survey freeze.

Violation type: new. No existing citation covers this.

The System doesn't yet have a rule for what constitutes damage to a frozen boundary.

He stared at the sentence. He underlined it.

Then he wrote: I need to make the boundary physically impossible to sever before 2201-F succeeds.

He looked at the crack. It was growing.

Slowly, but visibly.

The concrete on his side was being compressed. The material on 2201-F's side was expanding. The joint was being pulled apart like taffy.

Gerald went to his storage, and he pulled out the quick-set concrete patch he used for minor repairs. He moved it to the east wall and started stuffing it into the crack.

The material resisted.

It wanted to expand. Gerald pressed harder. The patch held.

He applied a second layer. A third.

The pushing on the other side intensified. He could feel it through the patch. A slow, grinding pressure, like a hydraulic press set to low.

He worked for forty minutes. The patch held. The crack stopped growing.

Gerald sat back on his heels and wiped his hands on his pants. His knees ached. He was fifty-four years old and he was doing masonry in a dungeon while a countdown clock ran above his head.

He stood up and walked to the north wall.

The green material at the eight-meter mark was still there.

Core 4409-B. He'd never encountered it.

Never cited it. Never even knew it existed.

He queried the System for Core 4409-B's profile. The response was minimal.

Core 4409-B.

Compliance Rating: 91. Spawn count: 1,247. Threat level: not applicable.

Owner status: Inspector General, Office of System Compliance.

Gerald read that line four times, and the Inspector General had a dungeon; the Inspector General's dungeon shared a wall with his.

He thought about the forty-seven citations. The ones he'd been reading before he'd started measuring.

Forty-seven citations issued against him.

He'd assumed they came from the System's automated audit system; the standard enforcement mechanism for cores that fell below compliance thresholds.

He went back to his desk. He pulled up the citation list.

He looked at the issuer field on each one.

Citation 1.

Issuer: System Automated Audit. Citation 2.

Issuer: System Automated Audit. Citation 3. Citation 4.

Citation 47. Issuer: Inspector General, Office of System Compliance.

He scrolled back. Citation 46.

Issuer: Inspector General.

Citation 45. Citation 44. Citation 43.

The first twelve citations were automated. From citation 13 onward, every single one was manually issued. By the Inspector General. By the Core whose dungeon shared a wall with his.

Gerald sat down. He put his pen down. He picked it up. He put it down again.

The Inspector General had been citing him personally. For months. Building a case. And the Inspector General's own dungeon was structurally connected to his.

That meant the Inspector General had a conflict of interest. That meant the citations might be void. That meant Gerald's entire compliance history, the 12 out of 100 rating, the fines, the forced upgrades, all of it, might be the product of a Core using the System's enforcement apparatus to build a case against a neighbor.

A neighbor whose dungeon it was trying to dissolve.

Gerald looked at the clock. Sixty-three hours, twelve minutes.

He looked at the north wall.

The green material pulsed. Slow.

Steady.

Like a heartbeat, and he looked at the east wall. The patch held.

But the pressure on the other side hadn't stopped.

It had just paused.

Waiting.

Gerald pulled out his clipboard. He turned to a new page. He wrote: The Inspector General has been citing me manually since citation 13.

The Inspector General's dungeon shares a boundary with mine. The Inspector General is trying to dissolve my Core.

The Inspector General is also trying to sever the shared boundary with 2201-F, which would eliminate my joint liability defense.

He underlined that. Then he wrote: The Inspector General is both my adversary and my neighbor. And the System does not yet have a rule for what happens when an Inspector General has a conflict of interest.

He checked the clock again. Sixty-three hours, nine minutes.

He pulled out a fresh citation form. Form 11-C. Conflict of Interest, Regulatory Body.

He'd never used this form.

He'd seen it in the municipal code. Section 9.4.

Applicable when a regulatory official has a personal, financial, or structural interest in the outcome of an enforcement action.

He filled it out. His handwriting was steady. It was always steady.

Citation issued to: Inspector General, Office of System Compliance. Violation: Conflict of interest in enforcement action. Supporting evidence: Shared boundary, manual citation issuance, structural connection between complainant and respondent.

He signed it. He put it in the outgoing pile.

Then he picked up his tape measure and walked to the north wall, and he placed his hand on the green material. He could feel the pulse.

He could feel the boundary. And he could feel, faintly, something on the other side pressing back.

Not pushing. Pressing. Like a hand held flat against a wall from the other side. Like someone waiting to see what he would do.

Gerald pulled his hand back. He looked at the clock.

Sixty-three hours, four minutes.

The green material pulsed once. Then went still.

And Gerald heard, faintly, through the wall, the sound of a pen scratching on paper.

It was not his pen.
