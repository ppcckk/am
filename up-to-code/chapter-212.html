Chapter 212: Citation Served

# Chapter 212

The clipboard was the first thing to fall.

It hit the shattered concrete with a dry slap, sliding across the debris toward Gerald's central crystal. Dust choked the air, thick and chalky. Kaelen coughed, waving a hand in front of his face, his armor coated in white powder.

Above, the young woman leaned further into the hole. She looked less shocked than annoyed. Her badge, small and metallic, caught the dim light filtering down from the library.

"I'm going to need to see your permit," she said. Her voice didn't waver.

Gerald's consciousness thrummed with panic. The System warnings flashed red across his mind, a strobing barrage of failure.

**Compliance Rating: 25/100.** **Critical Failure: Structural Integrity.** **Shutdown Sequence Initiated.**

He had seconds. Maybe ten.

The darkness wasn't just metaphorical anymore; it was creeping in at the edges of his awareness, swallowing the corners of the room.

If he didn't stabilize the structure, he died. If he stabilized the structure without addressing the intruder, he died.

The woman above was not a Core. She was flesh and blood. A City Inspector.

Gerald's mind snagged on the word.

Inspector.

The System recognized municipal authority. It recognized hierarchy. And it recognized that a human official entering a private zone was, by definition, a violation of trespassing statutes, unless said official was operating under a sanctioned warrant.

She had no warrant.

But she had a badge. And badges were issued by the City.

Gerald reached out, not with physical limbs, but with the raw, grinding tension of his dungeon's will. He targeted the hole in the ceiling. The jagged edges of the plaster. The exposed rebar.

*Non-compliant egress.*

That was one citation. Easy. But it didn't stop the collapse.

He needed a stopper. A plug. Something that could bear the weight of the library above and keep the ceiling from coming down completely.

Kaelen was staring up, sword raised, ready to fight the woman if she dropped down.

"Don't," Gerald's voice echoed in Kaelen's mind, sharp and cold. "Stand down."

"She's coming down!" Kaelen hissed.

"She's a liability," Gerald replied.

The woman descended.

She didn't jump. She lowered herself carefully, grabbing the edge of the hole, pulling herself down with practiced ease.

She landed in the dust with a heavy thud, boots crunching on broken stone.

She brushed off her blazer, then pulled a pen from her pocket.

"State your name and business," she said. She didn't look at Kaelen. She looked at the crystal. "And identify the property owner."

Gerald focused. He couldn't speak with a mouth. He had no mouth. But he had a presence.

*Gerald Hutchins. Inspector. Municipal Code Compliance.*

The words appeared in the air, formed from the floating dust motes. They hung there, shimmering and grey.

The woman paused. She squinted at the text. Then she looked at her own badge. Then back at the text.

"Inspector Hutchins?" she asked. "That's not possible.

You retired.

You died.

I inspected your sub-basement three days ago."

The dust text shifted.

*Active. Retained.*

A flicker of recognition crossed her face. Not fear. Bureaucratic irritation.

"Unregistered entity," she muttered. She opened her clipboard. She wrote.

Gerald felt the System react to her action. The pen moving against paper created a friction point in the ambient mana field. It was a small thing, insignificant to a mage, but profound to a Code Compliance Core.

*Administrative Process Initiated.*

Gerald seized it.

The loophole was buried in Subsection 4.2 of the System's Interaction Protocols: *Inter-Agency Cooperation.*

If a government official was performing a lawful inspection, the dungeon was legally required to provide access. If the dungeon failed to provide safe access, the official was entitled to invoke *Emergency Structural Stabilization.*

Gerald wasn't trying to stop her. He was trying to make her help him.

He needed her to sign the paper. Not to cite him. To authorize a temporary structural patch.

"The ceiling is failing," Gerald projected. The words were faster now, more urgent. "Immediate hazard. You are within the hazard zone."

The woman looked up. The ceiling groaned again. A chunk of lath fell, missing her head by inches.

"Right," she said. She didn't run. She didn't scream.

She looked at the crack, then at Gerald's crystal. "Is this a load-bearing failure?"

*Affirmative.*

"Show me the stress points."

Gerald pulsed his energy. He directed the mana flow toward the main beam above the crystal. It glowed faintly, a sickly green.

The woman stepped closer. She reached out, touching the glowing beam with her bare hand.

Nothing happened. No spark. No shock.

She frowned. "No residual charge. That's bad. It means the support has already snapped internally."

She looked at her clipboard. Then she looked at Gerald.

"I'm issuing a Stop Work Order on the entire library structure," she said. "Effective immediately. All occupancy is suspended."

Gerald froze.

A Stop Work Order was a death sentence.

The library was his anchor. If the library was closed, the mana flow from the city's ley lines into his dungeon would be dampened by the municipal lockdown protocols. His rating would drop.

He would suffocate.

"No," Gerald projected. The word was huge, shaking the dust. "Countermand. I am the owner."

"You are a violation," she said. She wrote something down. "I'm citing you for unauthorized excavation."

**Citation Received.** **Penalty: -5 Compliance.** **Current Rating: 20.**

The room tilted. Kaelen grabbed a pillar to stay upright.

"Boss!" Kaelen shouted. "Do something!"

Gerald's mind raced. He had been cited.

By a human.

It shouldn't have been possible. Core Cites only worked on Cores.

Unless.

The System didn't care if the issuer was a Core or a human. It only cared if the issuer had *Authority.*

She was a City Inspector. The City had jurisdiction over the building.

Gerald needed to challenge the jurisdiction.

He needed to prove he was operating under a federal exemption.

Dungeons were classified as *Supernatural Entities.* They fell under the Federal Department of Arcane Regulation (FDAR). Municipal codes did not apply to FDAR-registered entities unless they posed a threat to public health or safety.

Was the ceiling a threat to public health?

Yes.

But was it a threat *right now?*

If he could prove the library was empty, the threat was theoretical. Theoretical threats could be deferred.

"Occupancy count," Gerald projected.

The woman paused. "What?"

"Current occupancy of the library. Above me."

She checked her notes. "Zero. It's closed for renovations."

*Then the hazard is theoretical.*

Gerald pushed his awareness upward, through the rubble, through the floorboards. He scanned the library.

Empty.

Dusty.

Silent.

*Hazard Level: Low.*

*Jurisdiction Conflict.*

The air shimmered. A new notification pinged in Gerald's mind.

**Jurisdictional Dispute Detected.** **Municipal Authority vs. Federal Arcane Authority.** **Resolution Pending: Hearing Required.**

He had started a legal battle. And he had no lawyer. He had no time.

The ceiling cracked again. A waterfall of plaster poured down.

Kaelen yelped, shielding his face. "It's coming down!"

Gerald looked at the woman. She was calm. Too calm. She was watching the ceiling, not the crystal.

She knew.

She knew the dungeon was dying.

"You have to evacuate," she said. Her voice was softer now. "You have to let me seal the hole."

"I can't seal it," Gerald projected. "I lack materials."

"I have materials."

She reached into her blazer pocket. She pulled out a small, metallic cylinder. A foam sealant canister.

Industrial grade. Used for patching minor structural breaches in municipal buildings.

Gerald stared at it.

If she sprayed it, it would harden. It would plug the hole. It would provide temporary structural support.

But it would also be an *External Modification.*

The System hated external modifications. They were often viewed as cheating or tampering.

But *Emergency Maintenance* was an exception.

"If you apply the sealant," Gerald projected, "you are performing Maintenance on Federal Property."

The woman hesitated. Her pen hovered over the paper.

"I don't know if that's legal," she whispered.

"It is," Gerald insisted. "Subsection 4.2. Emergency Stabilization overrides municipal jurisdiction if the entity in question is FDAR-registered."

She looked at the cylinder. Then at the ceiling, which was sagging visibly now.

"Prove you're registered," she said.

Gerald didn't have a registration number. He had never registered with the FDAR. He had been too busy fixing handrails.

But he had a *Case Number.*

His first Citation. The one he had served on the Goblin King.

**Case ID: 88-Alpha-Zulu-9.**

He broadcast the number.

The woman's eyes widened. She typed it into a small device on her wrist.

"88-Alpha-Zulu-9," she read. "That's the Ashford Dungeon Core. The one that cited the Goblin King."

*Affirmative.*

"That's a Level 3 Core," she said. She looked at him. "You're a big deal."

"I am a nuisance," Gerald corrected.

The ceiling gave a final, terrible groan. The main beam splintered.

"Do it!" Kaelen screamed.

The woman uncapped the sealant. She aimed it at the hole.

"Clear the area," she commanded.

Kaelen scrambled back.

She sprayed the foam. It expanded rapidly, white and thick, filling the crack in the ceiling. It hardened in seconds, forming a rigid plug.

The groaning stopped.

Silence.

The dust settled.

**Structural Integrity: Restored (Temporary).** **Compliance Rating: 20.** **Penalty for Unauthorized Modification: Pending.**

Gerald watched the foam harden. He felt the weight of the library above settle onto the new plug. It held.

He was alive.

But the penalty was hanging over him. *Unauthorized Modification.*

If he didn't justify it, the System would strip the foam. The ceiling would collapse. He would die.

He needed to justify it.

He needed to prove that the modification was *required* for compliance.

And he needed to do it before the Inspector General noticed the spike in activity.

The woman capped the canister. She looked at the crystal.

"You're not dead," she said. "So, what now?"

Gerald's mind cleared. The fog of death was receding.

"Now," he projected, "you file a Form 11-B."

"Form 11-B?"

"Unlicensed Biological Entity. That's me. I'm not registered. I'm not authorized to exist."

She blinked. "You want me to shut you down?"

"I want you to *legalize* me," Gerald corrected. "If you cite me for being unlicensed, you have to give me a window to apply for a permit. That window is seventy-two hours."

She frowned. "That's a lot of paperwork."

"I'll pay for it," Gerald said.

"Pay?"

"I have mana. I have dungeon loot. I have a rat."

Kaelen, still crouched, raised a hand. "I have gold."

The woman looked at Kaelen. Then at the crystal. Then at her clipboard.

She smiled. It was a small, tired smile.

"Gold," she said. "I can work with gold."

She pulled a fresh sheet of paper from the clipboard.

"I'm not citing you," she said.

"I'm opening a Case for Permit Application. You have seventy-two hours to submit a full compliance dossier. If you don't, I'll condemn the building.

And by extension, you."

She wrote.

**Case ID: 99-GH-01.** **Status: Pending Permit.** **Deadline: 72 Hours.**

Gerald felt the System accept the status change.

**Compliance Rating: 20.** **Debuff: Vulnerability to Legal Shutdown.**

He had bought himself three days.

But he had also created a new problem.

The woman stood up. She brushed the dust off her pants.

"Who's going to sign the application?" she asked.

Gerald hesitated.

"I am," he said.

"I need a human signature," she said. "You don't have hands."

Gerald looked at Kaelen.

Kaelen stood up, brushing plaster off his armor. He looked confused.

"Me?" Kaelen asked.

"Yes," Gerald projected.

"Me?"

"You are my representative."

Kaelen looked at the woman. He looked at the crystal. He looked at the sealant foam, still fresh and white in the ceiling.

"I don't know how to fill out forms," Kaelen said.

"You're going to learn," Gerald said. "Fast."

The woman turned to leave. She climbed back up through the hole, pulling herself up with a grunt.

"Stay in the basement," she called down. "If you leave, I'm calling the cops."

Her boots disappeared into the library above.

Silence returned to the sub-basement.

Kaelen slumped against a wall. "We're not dead."

"We are," Gerald said. "Legally speaking. We are on probation."

"What do we do now?"

Gerald focused on the new case file hanging in his mind. **99-GH-01.**

Seventy-two hours.

He needed a full compliance dossier. Ventilation plans.

Fire suppression specs.

Egress routes. Load-bearing calculations.

He had never done this for himself. He had only inspected others.

"I need to read the manual," Gerald said.

"The manual?"

"The System's Code of Regulations. Volume 4."

"I don't have Volume 4," Kaelen said.

"You do," Gerald said. "You're holding the clipboard."

Kaelen looked at the clipboard lying on the floor. The woman had dropped it.

He picked it up.

On the back of the clipboard, in small, neat handwriting, was a list of required documents.

*1. Fire Suppression Plan.* *2.

Ventilation Schematic.* *3.

Occupancy Load Calculation.* *4.

Proof of Liability Insurance.*

Kaelen looked at the list. Then at Gerald's crystal.

"Insurance?" he asked. "For a dungeon?"

"Dungeons are businesses," Gerald said. "If an adventurer dies in your dungeon, you're liable."

Kaelen swallowed. "We have no money for insurance."

"Then we get some."

"How?"

Gerald pulsed his energy, illuminating the dark corners of the room.

"We start selling permits."

"Selling permits?"

"To other dungeons."

Kaelen stared. "You can't sell permits. You're not a government."

"I am a Core," Gerald said. "And I have a license to inspect."

He looked at the ceiling, at the white foam plug.

"We're not just surviving," Gerald projected. "We're monetizing compliance."

Kaelen shook his head. "This is insane."

"It's efficient."

A noise came from the shadows. A scratching.

The rat emerged. It looked at the foam in the ceiling. It looked at Kaelen. It looked at the crystal.

It squeaked.

Gerald focused on the rat.

*Subject: Rodent.* *Status: Unlicensed.* *Action: Issue Citation.*

A small, blue light formed around the rat.

**Citation Served.** **Violation: Unlicensed Biological Entity in Commercial Zone.** **Fine: 1 Mana Crystal.**

The rat vanished. The System took its mana.

Gerald felt a tiny surge of power. The fine had been collected.

It wasn't much. But it was a start.

Kaelen watched the space where the rat had been. He looked at Gerald.

"You just fined a rat," Kaelen said. "To pay for insurance?"

"To start a business," Gerald corrected.

He turned his attention to the new case file.

**Deadline: 71 Hours, 59 Minutes.**

He had to draft a plan. He had to draft a blueprint. He had to draft a defense.

And he had to do it before the next Inspector General audit.

Which was scheduled for tomorrow morning.

Gerald's crystal pulsed.

*Work begins now.*

Kaelen sighed. He opened his mouth to argue, then closed it. He picked up the pen.

"Okay," he said. "Where do I start?"

Gerald projected a schematic. It was a simple square.

"Start with the fire exit," Gerald said. "Make it wide enough for a dragon to turn around in."

Kaelen looked at the small, cramped dungeon.

"There's no room," Kaelen said.

"Then we dig," Gerald said.

Kaelen stared at him.

"We're going to dig a hole," Gerald projected, "right into the next dungeon over."

Kaelen's eyes widened.

"You're going to invade?"

"No," Gerald said. "I'm going to annex."

He turned his awareness outward, scanning the walls of his dungeon. He could feel the other Cores. They were close. Too close.

He could feel their fear.

They knew he was still alive.

And they knew he was angry.

The first thing he would do was send a Citation to the neighbor. Not for safety. Not for structure.

For *Noise.*

He needed a pretext to break their wall. And noise was the easiest thing to prove.

Kaelen looked at the pen in his hand. He looked at Gerald.

"You're going to get us killed," Kaelen said.

"I'm going to get us promoted," Gerald said.

He focused on the wall. He found the weakest point. A hairline fracture in the stone, vibrating with the ambient mana of the neighboring dungeon.

Gerald pushed.

The fracture widened.

And from the other side, a voice echoed back.

*"Who's there?"*

It was a new voice. Deep. Baritone. Confident.

*"I am the Keeper of the Ashwood Crypt,"* the voice boomed. *"Identify yourself."*

Gerald didn't answer.

He cited the wall for excessive vibration.

**Citation Served.**

The wall shuddered.

And then, it exploded inward.
