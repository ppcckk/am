Chapter 110: Ceiling Form Precedent

# Chapter 110 The rat was chewing on the negative terminal of a salvaged car battery.

Gerald watched it with the flat, unblinking patience of a man who had watched thirty-one years of contractors pretend they didn’t know what they were doing. The rat, whose name Gerald had tentatively assigned as "Specimen One" in his mental log, paused.

It looked at the battery.

It looked at Gerald’s single, unblinking eye. Then it took another bite. The wire sparked.

A small shower of orange light rained down onto the damp concrete floor.

"Stop," Gerald said.

The rat did not stop.

Gerald felt the familiar, nauseating lurch of his compliance instinct screaming for order, for a permit, for a laminated sign that said *AUTHORIZED PERSONNEL ONLY*. But he had no mana. He had no gold. He had forty-seven hours and twelve minutes before the System’s Auditor arrived to measure the gap between his soul and the code.

Torvald, the dungeon core from the old textile mill three blocks over, had drained Gerald’s mana pool dry during their last skirmish. Torvald was a brute-force core, all spikes and poison, no nuance. But Torvald had money. Gerald had a clipboard, a dying battery, and a rat that was technically an illegal biological import under Ashford municipal ordinance 44-C.

Kael stood by the entrance to the sub-basement, tapping his foot against the cracked floor tiles. He was a human adventurer, the first one to willingly enter Gerald’s dungeon, mostly because he’d gotten lost looking for a shortcut to the library’s parking garage and found himself trapped in a room that smelled of mildew and despair.

"You’re looking at the rat like it owes you money," Kael said.

"It does," Gerald said. "It owes me time."

Gerald moved toward the corner where the water leak was pooling, and the leak was the source of his 12/100 rating. It was also, if he was reading the fine print correctly, his only renewable resource.

He pulled the System interface into his view. The text was jagged, flickering with low-energy static.

**STATUS: CRITICAL** **RESOURCES: 0 Mana, 4 Copper, 1 Rat, 1 Battery (8% charge)** **ACTIVE VIOLATIONS: 14** **LIST OF VIOLATIONS:** 1. Lack of Fire Extinguishers (Severity: High) 2.

Improper Ventilation (Severity: Medium) 3.

Unlicensed Biological Entity (Severity: Low) 4. Electrical Hazard (Severity: High) 5. Structural Instability (Severity: Critical)

Gerald ignored the first three. He focused on number four. *Electrical Hazard.*

In the standard dungeon economy, electricity was a hazard to be avoided. Lightning damage was high.

Fire damage was high.

Most cores built their dungeons out of stone and wood, relying on monsters to do the killing.

They didn’t wire their halls. They didn’t install outlets.

They didn’t care about the National Electrical Code.

Gerald cared about it more than he cared about his own heartbeat.

"Kael," Gerald said. "Do you know what a ground fault circuit interrupter is?"

Kael blinked. "A... a safety switch?"

"It prevents electrocution by detecting the imbalance between the current flowing out of the live wire and the current returning through the neutral wire," Gerald said, his voice monotone. "If there is an imbalance, it cuts the power. Instantly."

"Great," Kael said. "Can we install one?

Do you have copper wire?

Do you have rubber insulation? You have a rat and a battery that is leaking acid."

"I don't need to install one," Gerald said. "I need to cite one."

Kael stared at him. "You can’t cite a GFCI. You can only cite violations."

"Read the mechanic again, Kael. *A Code Compliance Core can issue Citations to any dungeon within range... applying temporary stat debuffs proportional to the severity and number of violations found.*"

"Right. So if I find a dungeon without fire exits, I debuff their speed."

"Precisely. And what is the primary function of a GFCI?"

"Saving lives?"

"No," Gerald said. "It is a *required component* for any circuit that supplies power to a wet location. Or, in a dungeon context, any area where fluid hazards are present."

Gerald pointed to the water leak.

"My dungeon has a fluid hazard. Therefore, any electrical system I attempt to install in this area *must* have a GFCI.

If I install a circuit without one, I am violating code.

But here is the loophole."

Gerald pulled out his clipboard. He started writing, the pen scratching against the quiet damp.

"The System defines 'Installation' as the active creation of a functional entity. If I do not *install* the circuit, but rather *pre-emptively cite* the absence of a required safety component in a *potential* installation, the violation is not active. It is prospective."

"You're going to cite a hole in the wall?" Kael asked.

"I am going to cite the *lack of a required component* in the blueprint," Gerald said. "And because I have no mana to build anything, the 'potential' is infinite. I am citing the *concept* of a wired room that does not exist."

"That makes no sense," Kael said.

"It makes perfect sense. It’s called 'Zoning Pre-Compliance.' I did it in '04 when a developer tried to put a spa in a residential zone without a variance. I cited the *intent* to violate the code before they broke ground. The citation held because the *requirement* existed, even if the structure did not."

Gerald turned to the System window. He didn’t try to build a wire. He didn’t try to build a switch. He simply focused on the water leak and the battery.

*Target: Potential Electrical Circuit in Zone 4 (The Leak).* *Violation: Missing Ground Fault Circuit Interrupter.* *Severity: High.* *Action: Issue Citation.*

The System hesitated. The text flickered.

This was not how Citations worked.

You cited things that existed.

You cited monsters. You cited traps.

You did not cite a hypothetical wire in a wall that wasn’t there.

Gerald pushed. He pushed with the sheer, grinding weight of his professional indignation. He remembered the look on the face of the architect who had tried to tell him a load-bearing wall was just "decorative." He remembered the smell of asbestos in the old high school. He remembered the thirty-one years of saying *no*.

The System accepted.

**CITATION ISSUED: #2204** **TARGET: INTERNAL SYSTEM (ZONE 4)** **VIOLATION: ABSENCE OF MANDATORY SAFETY DEVICE** **PENALTY: STAT DEBUFF APPLIED TO USER.**

Gerald gasped. A sharp pain shot through his chest. His vision blurred.

**DEBUFF: SYSTEM INTEGRITY -10%** **CURRENT RATING: 2/100**

"What did you do?" Kael yelled, rushing forward.

"I debuffed myself," Gerald wheezed. "But look."

He pointed to the System log.

**BENEFIT GENERATED: PRE-CITATION CLEARANCE** **EXPLANATION: By acknowledging the violation of a non-existent system, the Core has demonstrated full knowledge of required safety protocols for said system. The System recognizes the Core's intent to comply with future installations.** **UNLOCKED MECHANIC: 'PRE-INSTALLATION WAIVER'** **EFFECT: For the next 24 hours, any electrical, plumbing, or structural installation created by this Core is exempt from standard material costs and mana requirements, provided it meets the cited code standards.**

Kael’s jaw dropped. "You... you broke the game."

"I didn't break it," Gerald said, wiping sweat from his forehead. "I filed a variance request."

The pain in Gerald’s chest faded, replaced by a strange, humming warmth. He could feel the dungeon now.

Not just as a list of stats, but as a physical space.

He could feel the water leak. He could feel the air currents. And for the first time, he could feel the *possibility* of the walls.

"We have twenty-four hours of free materials," Gerald said. "But only for things we have already cited as missing."

Kael looked at the list of violations. "Ventilation. Egress. Fire Suppression."

"And electrical," Gerald said. "We cite the lack of wiring, we get the waiver for the wiring. We cite the lack of handrails, we get the waiver for the handrails."

"But you said you had no mana."

"I don't need mana to build if the System provides the materials via the waiver. The waiver covers the *cost*. I still need to assemble it. But I have Kael."

Gerald looked at the adventurer. "Can you climb?"

"I'm an Explorer class. Yes."

"Good.

The ventilation violation is in the upper shaft. You need to install an exhaust fan.

The System says we need airflow.

The waiver says we don't have to pay for the fan. But we have to *put it in*. The System will provide the fan.

You will have to mount it."

"You want me to install an industrial fan in a dungeon?"

"I want you to install it *to code*," Gerald said. "It needs to be on a dedicated circuit. It needs to have a back-draft damper. And it needs to be grounded."

Kael stared at him. "Gerald, this is insane. You're asking me to do electrical work in a place that is currently trying to kill me with mold."

"If you do not do it, the Auditor comes in forty-seven hours and deletes us.

If you do it, we get a 20-point boost to our Ventilation score, which removes the Regen Debuff.

If we have Regen, you can survive the next monster fight. If you survive the next monster fight, you can bring me loot.

If you bring me loot, I can buy copper wire."

Kael looked at the rat. The rat was now asleep.

"Fine," Kael said. "But if I die, I’m haunting you."

"Noted," Gerald said.

Kael moved to the center of the room. He focused.

He pulled on the System interface, not as a user, but as a worker.

The System recognized his intent. A shimmer of blue light appeared in his hands. A fan.

A standard, municipal-grade exhaust fan.

Kael gritted his teeth. He lifted it. It was heavy.

"Where do I put it?" Kael grunted.

"Up there," Gerald said, pointing to a dark hole in the ceiling. "But first, we need a conduit. We need to run wire from the battery to the fan."

Gerald closed his eyes. He focused on the electrical hazard violation.

*Target: Potential Wiring Path.* *Violation: Lack of Conduit.* *Action: Issue Citation.*

**CITATION ISSUED: #2205** **TARGET: INTERNAL SYSTEM (ZONE 4)** **VIOLATION: UNPROTECTED WIRING** **PENALTY: STAT DEBUFF APPLIED TO USER.**

**DEBUFF: PHYSICAL DAMAGE TAKEN +15%** **CURRENT RATING: 0.5/100**

Gerald slumped. His skin felt like it was bruising from the inside. But the waiver was active. The System materialized a coil of copper wire and a plastic conduit tube at Kael’s feet.

"Run it," Gerald whispered. "Ground it to the water pipe. The water pipe is metal. It will act as a ground."

Kael scrambled. He ran the wire along the damp wall.

He clamped the conduit, and he mounted the fan. He connected the wires to the battery.

*Click.*

The fan shuddered. Then, it started spinning.

A rush of air blew through the sub-basement. The dampness didn't vanish, but the stagnant, rotting smell faded, and the mildew spores floating in the air were sucked upward and out.

**VENTILATION UPDATE: 12/100 → 45/100** **DEBUFF REMOVED: REGENERATION PENALTY** **BONUS: +5% STAMINA REGEN FOR ALL NATIVE ENTITIES**

Kael gasped, sucking in the cleaner air. He looked at Gerald. "It works. You crazy old man, it actually works."

Gerald smiled. It was a thin, painful smile. "It is not crazy. It is compliant."

He checked the clock. Forty-six hours remaining.

"Now," Gerald said, pointing to the crack in the ceiling. "We cite the lack of fire suppression."

"Fire suppression?" Kael asked. "We don't have water pressure. We have a leak."

"We cite the leak as a fire suppression system," Gerald said.

Kael blinked. "That's... that's absurd. A leak is not a sprinkler system."

"It is a water source," Gerald said. "If we install a pipe network that distributes the leak to specific points, it becomes a wet-pipe system. We cite the lack of that network. Then we build it using the waiver."

"That’s not how fire codes work."

"It is how *dungeon* codes work," Gerald said. "The System cares about function, not form.

A wet-pipe system keeps the structure wet, preventing combustion.

We have water.

We need to distribute it."

Kael looked at the leak. He looked at the fan. He looked at Gerald.

"You want to flood the ceiling?"

"I want to *protect* the ceiling," Gerald corrected. "Cite the violation, Kael. Cite the lack of a fire suppression network. Do it."

Kael hesitated. Then, he closed his eyes and focused.

**CITATION ISSUED: #2206** **TARGET: INTERNAL SYSTEM (ZONE 1)** **VIOLATION: ABSENCE OF FIRE SUPPRESSION** **PENALTY: STAT DEBUFF APPLIED TO USER.**

**DEBUFF: FIRE RESISTANCE -50%** **CURRENT RATING: 0.1/100**

Gerald groaned. His skin prickled with heat, even though the room was cold. The vulnerability was immediate. If a monster with fire breath attacked now, it would incinerate him.

But the waiver activated.

**SYSTEM NOTICE: MATERIALS PROVIDED FOR FIRE SUPPRESSION INSTALLATION.**

A bundle of PVC pipes appeared. A pressure pump appeared. A series of sprinkler heads appeared.

"Install it," Gerald said. "Fast. Before the debuff stacks."

Kael moved with surprising speed. He started connecting pipes. He ran them along the ceiling.

He tapped into the main leak. The water gurgled, then flowed through the new network.

It dripped from the sprinkler heads.

The ceiling began to darken with dampness.

**FIRE SUPPRESSION UPDATE: 0/100 → 30/100** **RESISTANCE BONUS: +10% FIRE RESISTANCE (ACTIVE)**

"Better," Gerald said.

"Much better," Kael agreed. He wiped sweat from his brow. "We’re doing well. We’re actually fixing it."

Gerald looked at his Compliance Rating.

**12/100 → 15/100**

It was still abysmal. But it was rising.

Then, the air in the room changed.

It wasn't a sound, which was a *pressure*. A sudden, sharp drop in the atmospheric tension.

The fan seemed to stutter.

The water in the pipes stopped flowing.

Kael froze. "Gerald?"

Gerald felt it too. A coldness. A silence that was louder than the fan.

The System window flashed red.

**NOTICE: INSPECTOR GENERAL PROXIMITY DETECTED.** **STATUS: PRE-AUDIT SCAN INITIATED.** **TARGET: CORE HUTCHINS.**

"He's early," Gerald whispered.

"No," Kael said, his voice shaking. "He's not early. He's *here*."

The door to the sub-basement didn't open. It dissolved.

The wood simply ceased to exist, replaced by a shimmering, golden barrier. Through the barrier walked a figure.

It was not human, which was a construct of pure light and geometric shapes, shifting and refracting.

It had no face, only a single, glowing symbol on its chest: a checkmark.

**AUDITOR: UNIT 734.** **GREETING: GREETINGS, CORE.** **QUERY: WHY IS YOUR COMPLIANCE RATING INCREASING WHILE YOUR STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY IS CRITICAL?**

Gerald straightened his back. He picked up his clipboard. His hands were steady now.

"Because," Gerald said, "I am currently undergoing a mandatory renovation. And I have filed all the necessary permits."

The Auditor tilted its head.

**QUERY: YOU HAVE NO MANA.** **QUERY: YOU HAVE NO GOLD.** **QUERY: YOU ARE USING A VIOLATION LOOPHOLE TO GENERATE FREE MATERIALS.** **QUERY: IS THIS COMPLIANT?**

Gerald looked at the Auditor. He looked at Kael, who was cowering behind a pipe; looked at the rat, which was now hiding inside a pipe casing.

Gerald smiled. A real smile this time.

"Yes," Gerald said. "It is compliant. Because I cited the violation *first*."

The Auditor paused. The golden light intensified.

**QUERY: DEFINE 'FIRST'.**

Gerald stepped forward. He held up his clipboard.

"I cited the lack of a GFCI before I built the circuit. I cited the lack of a fire suppression system before I built the pipes. I cited the lack of ventilation before I built the fan.

Therefore, the violations were *procedural*, not *structural*. The materials were generated as a *corrective action*, not a *construction project*."

Gerald took a breath.

"Under Ashford Municipal Ordinance 12-A, subsection 4, 'Corrective Actions taken in response to a cited violation are exempt from permit fees and material costs.' The System has its own code. I am applying it."

The Auditor was silent.

The air grew heavier. The golden light started to pulse.

**CALCULATING...** **CALCULATING...**

**RESULT: LOGIC FLAW DETECTED.**

Gerald’s heart stopped.

**EXPLANATION: THE WAIVER MECHANIC REQUIRES A VALID CITATION. A VALID CITATION REQUIRES AN EXISTING STRUCTURE OR SYSTEM. A NON-EXISTENT SYSTEM CANNOT BE CITED FOR A MISSING COMPONENT.** **YOUR CITATIONS #2204, #2205, AND #2206 ARE VOID.** **REVERTING CHANGES.**

The fan stopped. The pipes crumbled into dust. The wire dissolved.

Kael screamed as the water from the leak splashed back, soaking his boots.

Gerald fell to his knees. His rating plummeted.

**0.1/100**

**WARNING: CORE INTEGRITY AT 5%.** **IMMEDIATE DELETION PROTOCOL INITIATED.** **TIME TO DELETION: 10 SECONDS.**

The Auditor raised a hand. A beam of pure white light started to form.

Gerald looked at the rat. The rat looked at him.

Gerald looked at the clipboard. There was one thing left. One thing he hadn't cited. One thing that was undeniably, physically present in his dungeon.

The crack in the ceiling, and he didn't have mana to fix it. He didn't have materials.

But he had a rule. A rule he had never used because it was too small, too petty, too mundane for a dungeon core.

But it was real.

Gerald focused on the crack.

*Target: Structural Integrity.* *Violation: Lack of Load-Bearing Support.* *Action: Issue Citation.*

But he didn't just cite it. He did something he had never done in thirty-one years.

He *appealed*.

**ACTION: FILE APPEAL AGAINST CITATION #2207** **ARGUMENT: THE STRUCTURE IS NOT BROKEN. THE STRUCTURE IS COMPRESSING UNDER LOAD FROM THE LIBRARY ABOVE. THIS IS NORMAL SETTLING.** **EVIDENCE: PHOTOGRAPH OF CRACK.** **REQUEST: CLASSIFY AS 'NON-DEFECTIVE SETTLEMENT' THAN 'STRUCTURAL FAILURE'.**

The System paused.

**APPEAL RECEIVED.** **HEARING INITIATED.** **OPPOSING PARTY: INSPECTOR GENERAL UNIT 734.**

The white beam of light halted. It hovered inches from Gerald’s face.

The Auditor’s form flickered.

**QUERY: WHY SHOULD THE CORE BE ALLOWED TO APPEAL?** **QUERY: THE AUDITOR HAS NO INTEREST IN ARGUMENT.** **QUERY: THE AUDITOR HAS NO INTEREST IN DEBATE.**

Gerald looked up. He met the empty, glowing face of the machine.

"Because," Gerald said, his voice shaking but clear.

"You are an Auditor. But you are also an Inspector. And Inspectors have to follow the Code of Conduct.

Article 4, Section 1: *All Core Entities have the right to contest a citation through a formal Hearing.*"

Gerald stood up. His legs were weak, but he stood.

"You cannot delete me until the Hearing is complete. If you delete me now, you are violating the System's own Due Process protocols. You are citing *yourself* for a violation."

The Auditor froze.

The silence stretched. Five seconds. Four. Three.

Two.

The golden light dimmed.

**APPEAL ACCEPTED.** **HEARING SCHEDULED: IMMEDIATE.** **DELETION SUSPENDED.** **WARNING: IF YOU LOSE THE HEARING, DELETION IS INSTANT.**

Gerald slumped against the desk. He had bought himself a few minutes.

But he had no evidence, and he had no witnesses; he had only a crack in the ceiling and a lie about settling.

And the Auditor was not a judge. It was a machine. It did not care about lies. It cared about *truth*.

Gerald looked at Kael. Kael was looking at the door.

"Gerald," Kael whispered. "What happens if you lose?"

Gerald looked at the System window.

**HEARING STARTING IN: 10...** **9...** **8...**

Gerald looked at the rat. The rat was chewing on the wire again.

He picked up the wire.

"I'm going to need a new precedent," Gerald said.

He slammed the wire into the crack in the ceiling.

The ceiling had a form for this.
