Chapter 323: Violation of Code

# Chapter 323

The hammer fell.

It did not swing. It dropped. Gravity was the only engine the Demolitionist required. The head, a block of fused granite and iron the size of a compact car, smashed into the asphalt of Ashford Avenue with enough force to liquefy the roadbed.

Shockwaves rippled outward. Glass in storefronts three blocks away shattered in unison.

From the perspective of the Consortium's advance scout, a shivering goblin crouched behind a crushed sedan, the scene was apocalyptic. The water rising from the library's sub-basement churned with high-voltage runoff, casting a sickly green light over the debris. The entity standing there was not a monster. It was a walking violation.

Gerald Hutchins stood ankle-deep in the floodwater. He was not screaming. He was not fleeing. He was holding a clipboard made of reinforced polycarbonate, which he had printed from his own dungeon's fabrication queue just seconds prior.

The goblin watched, mouth agape. It expected fear. It expected rage. It expected a hero's monologue.

Instead, Gerald tilted his head. He looked at the crater. He looked at the Demolitionist. He looked at the structural damage radiating outward in a perfect, terrible circle.

Then, Gerald pulled a pen from his pocket. Clicked it.

The goblin's blood turned to ice. It knew that click.

***

Back inside the library, the air smelled of ozone and wet drywall. Gerald's vision overlaid the physical world with the System's interface. Red text flashed across his retinas, highlighting every breach, every fracture, every unauthorized discharge of kinetic energy.

*Target: The Demolitionist.* *Status: Hostile Entity.* *Classification: Unpermitted Structural Modification.*

Gerald wiped rain from his glasses. His hands were steady. They had been shaking for thirty years, ever since he realized that improvisation led to collapse, but they had stopped shaking the moment he saw the hammer strike.

A hammer strike was not combat. A hammer strike was an unauthorized demolition permit.

The Demolitionist took a step forward. The ground buckled under its foot.

Ten feet tall, clad in armor that looked like it had been baked in a kiln, it radiated heat.

Not magical heat. Thermal inefficiency. Poor insulation.

Gerald stepped onto the flooded sidewalk, and the water was knee-deep now. The System warned him of electrical hazard, but Gerald ignored the warning.

He had already cited the power grid for inadequate grounding.

The hazard was their problem.

He raised the clipboard.

"Citation!" Gerald shouted. His voice was not loud, but it cut through the thunder. "Violation of Municipal Code Section 14.2, Subsection C! Unauthorized Excavation of Public Right-of-Way!"

The Demolitionist paused. Its head, a hollowed-out sphere of brick, tilted. A low rumble emanated from its chest.

"You are operating without a valid permit," Gerald continued, his tone flat, bureaucratic, and lethal. "And you have failed to provide adequate dust suppression measures.

Look at the particulate matter in the air, which is exceeding OSHA limits for silica exposure.

This is a health and safety violation.

"

The goblin scout peeked out from behind the sedan. It had never seen a Core argue with a boss monster. It had never seen a Core *cite* a boss monster.

The Demolitionist raised its hammer again.

Gerald did not flinch. He tapped the air, interacting with the System interface only he could see.

*Initiate Citation Protocol: Structural Hazard.* *Severity: Critical.* *Penalty: Immediate Stop-Work Order.*

The air around Gerald shimmered. The System, bound by its own rigid rules, recognized the legal validity of the claim. The Demolitionist had indeed created a hazard. The water, the debris, the lack of signage—all were violations.

The hammer stopped in mid-swing, which did not stop because of strength. It stopped because the System froze the entity's motor functions, citing non-compliance with active site safety standards.

The Demolitionist roared. It tried to bring the hammer down. The muscles in its armored back bulged, tearing the mortar joints. It was fighting the System itself.

Gerald watched the struggle with professional detachment.

"Note to file," Gerald muttered, scribbling on his clipboard. "Target exhibits resistance to regulatory enforcement. Recommend escalation to Inspector General level."

The water lapped at Gerald's waist. The flood was still rising. If he did not stop the source of the breach, the library would become a reservoir. And a reservoir was a drowning hazard.

He needed to close the breach. But he could not leave his post. The Demolitionist was held in place by the Citation, but the Citation was temporary. The System's audit window was closing.

Gerald looked at the crater. The water was pouring out of the library's foundation. The breach was a hole in the dungeon's perimeter.

*New Mechanic Detected: Permeable Boundary.*

The System was allowing the water to escape because the dungeon's

"containment field" was rated for liquid, but not for *flooded* liquid. The rating was too low.

Gerald had a choice. He could reinforce the walls, which would take hours. He could try to fight the Demolitionist, which would result in death.

Or he could change the definition of the dungeon.

Dungeons were defined by their boundaries. But boundaries were legal constructs as much as physical ones. If he re-zoned the area, he could change the rules.

"System," Gerald said aloud. "File application for Conditional Use Permit. Zone: Flood Control Infrastructure."

The System paused. The text hovered in the air.

*Query: This dungeon is registered as Residential/Commercial Hybrid. Re-zoning requires public notice and a thirty-day waiting period.*

"Invoke Emergency Ordinance 77-B," Gerald said. "Imminent threat to public welfare."

*Query: Justification?*

Gerald pointed at the Demolitionist, who was currently vibrating with the effort of breaking the Stop-Work Order. He pointed at the flooded street. He pointed at the library.

"Risk of structural collapse. Risk of electrocution. Risk of drowning.

These are imminent. Thirty days is not an option.

"

The System processed this. It was a loophole. A small, ugly, bureaucratic loophole that Gerald had found in the fine print of the System's Terms of Service, section 4, paragraph 9. Emergency re-zoning allowed immediate modification of dungeon attributes if the Core could prove

"unavoidable necessity."

He had proven it. He had the citations. He had the water.

*Approval: Conditional. Zone Change Accepted.* *New Attribute: Permeability. Dungeon walls are now considered semi-permeable to water up to 50% capacity.*

The effect was instant.

The water rushing out of the library's foundation slowed. Then, it reversed.

The dungeon's new zoning status allowed it to absorb the floodwater, treating it as a natural resource than a breach. The rising tide in the street drained. The water flowed backward, into the cracks in the library, into the sub-basement, into the dungeon's new

"flood control" channels.

Gerald felt the shift. His dungeon was no longer just a building. It was a retention pond.

The Demolitionist roared. The water draining from the street meant the library was no longer sinking. The immediate threat of collapse was gone. But the enemy remained.

And the Citation was fading.

The hammer moved again. Slowly. The System's enforcement was weakening as the audit window closed.

Gerald stepped back. He needed to get the Demolitionist into the dungeon. He needed to trap it.

But you cannot trap a god of demolition by building walls. You trap it by giving it something to destroy that is already worthless.

Gerald looked at his dungeon's layout. He had spent the last few chapters reinforcing the library. He had made it strong. He had made it safe.

He needed to make it unsafe. Specifically, he needed to make it a code violation so egregious that the System would want it destroyed more than it wanted the Demolitionist preserved.

But he couldn't. The System rewarded compliance.

Unless...

Gerald looked at the goblin scout. It was still hiding behind the car. It was terrified. It was a witness.

"Hey," Gerald said, pointing at the goblin.

"You. You're in a public right-of-way. You're loitering.

That's a misdemeanor.

"

The goblin blinked.

"I... I am just watching?"

"You are obstructing emergency services," Gerald said. "Move, or I cite you for trespassing."

The goblin scrambled out, running toward the library. It thought Gerald was letting it escape.

It was wrong.

Gerald was herding it, and he wanted the goblin inside. He wanted a biological witness.

As the goblin ran, Gerald typed furiously.

*Add Spawn: Unlicensed Biological Entity.* *Designation: Witness.* *Location: Zone 4, Flood Channel.*

The goblin slipped, fell, and was sucked into the drain. It screamed, sliding down the pipe into the dark depths of the library's basement.

Gerald had planted a witness. Now he needed the Demolitionist to see it.

He looked up. The hammer was halfway down. The Stop-Work Order was gone.

Gerald didn't run. He didn't dodge. He stood his ground and raised his clipboard high.

"Objection!" he shouted. "Relevance! The target is not the library! The target is the *non-compliant infrastructure*!"

He pointed at the library.

"Section 14.2, Subsection D! Demolition of Non-Compliant Structures is Permitted Under Emergency Ordinance!"

The System froze.

Gerald was arguing that the library *itself* was the violation. That the Demolitionist was not attacking him, but performing a legal demolition of a condemned building.

It was a lie. A beautiful, intricate, bureaucratic lie.

The System checked the library's Compliance Rating. It had dropped to 12. It was failing. It was a hazard.

*Query: Is the structure condemned?*

Gerald pulled out a red tag. A real red tag. He had printed it from the dungeon's printer. He slapped it on the wet brick.

*Tag: CONDEMNED. DO NOT ENTER.*

The System scanned the tag. It verified the Core's authority to issue it.

*Query: Demolition Authorized?*

The Demolitionist's hammer hung in the air. Its head turned toward Gerald. It understood the command. It understood the permission.

*Yes,* the System said.

Gerald smiled. It was a small, tight smile.

"Go ahead," he said. "Demolish it."

The Demolitionist smiled back. It brought the hammer down.

But it did not hit the library.

It hit Gerald, and the System had authorized the demolition of the *structure*. Gerald was standing in front of it. Gerald was not part of the structure.

But Gerald was the Core. And the Core was the structure.

The hammer connected with Gerald's chest.

The world went white.

Gerald felt the impact. He felt his ribs turn to powder. He felt his body dissolve.

But he did not die.

Because he had prepared for this, and he had not filed a Form 11-B for himself. He had filed a Form 11-B for his *body*.

His body was an unlicensed biological entity.

And unlicensed entities were not protected by the System's combat immunity rules. They were subject to immediate removal.

But Gerald had a counter-clause.

*Clause: Biological Entity is Essential for Core Function.*

*Status: Indispensable.*

The System could not remove an indispensable entity without terminating the Core.

The hammer bounced off.

Gerald fell backward, gasping. His chest was intact, but bruised. The force had been absorbed by the System's paradox.

The Demolitionist roared. It was confused. Its command had failed.

Gerald scrambled up. He was winning. He had beaten the god with a form.

But then he heard it.

A new sound. Not a horn. Not a roar.

A click.

From behind him. From the library.

The red tag on the brick had changed.

It no longer said CONDEMNED.

It said: *UNDER REVIEW.*

Gerald turned.

Standing in the doorway of the library was not a goblin, which was not a monster.

It was a man in a grey suit. He held a briefcase. He looked like every building inspector Gerald had ever worked with, except he had no face. Just a smooth, blank oval where features should be.

*New Entity Detected.* *Name: The Auditor.* *Affiliation: System Inspector General.* *Objective: Final Verdict.*

The Auditor stepped out into the rain. He did not look at the Demolitionist. He looked at Gerald.

"Mr. Hutchins," the Auditor said.

His voice was like paper tearing.

"You have cited a god. You have re-zoned a dungeon in mid-combat. You have used a red tag to invoke emergency demolition authority."

Gerald gripped his clipboard. His knuckles were white.

"I followed procedure," Gerald said.

"Procedure," the Auditor said, tilting his headless face, "is not the same as justice."

The Auditor opened his briefcase.

Inside was not a document, which was a mirror.

And in the mirror, Gerald saw not his reflection.

He saw his dungeon.

But it was not a library.

It was a tomb.

*Verdict: Core Terminated for Abuse of Process.*

The Auditor closed the briefcase.

The Demolitionist raised its hammer again. This time, there was no citation. There was no loophole.

There was only the end.

Gerald looked at the mirror. He looked at the tomb.

He realized, with a cold, sinking horror, that the System had not been helping him, which had been setting him up.

The hammer fell, and Gerald heard himself scream.
