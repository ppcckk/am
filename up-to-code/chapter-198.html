Chapter 198: Snap the Pen

# Chapter 198

Kael pressed his thumb against the gauge, watching the needle tremble at twelve percent. Kael wiped his forehead with a rag that was already gray with dust. He checked his gauge.

The pressure in the main corridor had dropped by twelve percent in the last ten minutes, which was wrong. This was physically impossible for a dungeon of this size, even with the new Void Sentinel expansion.

"Report," Kael said into his headset.

Static hissed back.

Then, a voice, tinny and distant.

"Ventilation shafts are clear.

No blockages.

But the airflow... it's wrong, boss. It's not pulling. It's being pushed.

"

Kael frowned. He kicked a loose panel loose.

Behind it, the fan blades spun with a rhythmic, mechanical precision that lacked any organic wobble.

He pulled out his own clipboard, and he didn't care about monster stats. He cared about why his dungeon was losing pressure.

If the pressure dropped below forty percent, the structural integrity of the upper floors would compromise. The ceiling tiles would buckle. The expensive, hand-carved stalactites would crash down on his customers.

Kael walked toward the source of the change. He moved through the lobby, past the confused tourists who were still trying to figure out which door was the exit and which was the entrance. He ignored them. He had a violation to find.

He followed the draft. It was cold. It smelled like old paper and damp concrete.

It led him to the sub-basement level, the area that was supposed to be off-limits for maintenance until next quarter. The door was unlocked.

Kael pushed it open, and a man sat on the floor.

Not a monster. Not a goblin. Not a slime.

Gerald Hutchins.

Kael stopped, and he lowered his clipboard. He had heard rumors. Everyone had.

The inspector who became a dungeon.

The man who had cited the Void Sentinel into submission. Kael had expected a monster.

A beast made of stone and spite.

Instead, he saw a fifty-four-year-old man in a rumpled suit, holding a pen like a weapon.

Gerald didn't look up. He was drawing lines on a piece of parchment that glowed with a faint blue light. His handwriting was microscopic.

"You're trespassing," Gerald said. His voice was dry. It sounded like gravel crunching under tires.

Kael raised his hands.

"I'm here for the pressure drop. You're disrupting my ventilation system."

"I am fixing it," Gerald said. "Your intake vents are installed at a height that violates the municipal code for commercial basements. Section 8.4, subsection C.

You are recirculating stale air. It is a health hazard.

"

Kael blinked. "This is a dungeon. We have fireballs. We don't care about air quality."

"Code 114 says you do," Gerald said.

He pointed his pen at Kael.

"I am installing a secondary exhaust system. It requires a three-foot clearance from the load-bearing pillars.

Your pillars are two feet from the wall. You are non-compliant.

"

Kael felt a headache forming behind his eyes.

"Why are you doing this? You're the neighbor. You're supposed to be fighting us, not renovating us."

Gerald looked up. His eyes were tired.

There were dark circles under them that looked like bruises, and he wasn't fighting. He was working.

"Because if your dungeon collapses," Gerald said, "it takes the library foundation with it.

And if the library collapses, I have to explain to the city council why I failed to inspect my own sub-basement. I have a pension, Mr.

Vane.

I intend to keep it.

"

Vane. That was his name. Kael realized with a jolt that the man wasn't just a neighbor. He was a bureaucratic nightmare.

Kael looked at the wall. Gerald had already installed a small, metal grate.

It was ugly, which was out of place.

It hummed with a low, annoying frequency. But the draft coming through it was strong.

"Remove it," Kael said. "It violates the aesthetic guidelines of my dungeon. My customers want mystery. They don't want HVAC."

Gerald stood up. He was shorter than Kael, but he stood straighter. He held the clipboard against his chest like a shield.

"I have issued a Citation," Gerald said.

Kael laughed. It was a nervous, barking sound.

"You can't cite me.

I'm not in the System's active registry for that zone. I'm in Sector 4.

You're in Sector 1.

"

"The System doesn't care about sectors," Gerald said. "It cares about proximity. The Void Sentinel opened a tunnel.

That tunnel connects our spaces.

By the principle of adjacent property liability, your ventilation exhaust now crosses a shared boundary. You are venting into a residential zone.

"

Kael's blood went cold. He looked at the grate. He looked at the map on Gerald's clipboard.

It was true. The System's lines had shifted. The gray area between them had become a shared easement. And Gerald had found the loophole.

"Call your lawyers," Kael said.

"I am the lawyer," Gerald said.

"And I am the inspector. And I am the core. It is a conflict of interest, but the System has not flagged it as a disqualifier.

It is, technically, a promotion.

"

Kael tried to step forward.

To intimidate.

To use his size. He was a Core of the Iron Scale, a predator.

He was used to being feared.

But Gerald didn't flinch. He just held out his clipboard.

"I have prepared a Form 7-A," Gerald said. "Retrofitting of Shared Infrastructure.

It requires your signature.

It also requires you to pay for the installation costs. Which I have already deducted from your mana pool via the shared boundary tax.

"

Kael checked his internal HUD. His mana bar was blinking red. A small, persistent drain.

"How much?" Kael whispered.

"Forty thousand mana units," Gerald said. "Plus interest."

Kael felt sick. Forty thousand units was the cost of a new elite spawn. Or a new trap. Or a bribe for the Inspector General.

"You're bleeding me dry," Kael said.

"I am bringing you up to code," Gerald corrected. "The penalty for non-compliance is closure. I am offering a settlement. Pay the fee, install the fan, and I will withdraw the citation."

Kael looked at the fan. It was ugly. It was loud. It was expensive.

But the air was fresh. The pressure gauge in the hallway was stabilizing.

"If I sign," Kael said, "do you leave my tunnels alone?"

"For now," Gerald said. "But I will be inspecting your fire exits next week. I noticed they are marked with green light, but the path to the door is obstructed by decorative statues. That is a Class B violation."

Kael felt his jaw tighten. He hated this. He hated the logic. He hated that the logic worked.

He walked to the wall. He pulled a stylus from his belt. He signed the parchment.

The paper flashed gold.

**CITATION WITHDRAWN.** **PENALTY PAID.** **NEW STATUS: COMPLIANT.**

Kael looked at Gerald.

"You're insane."

"I am thorough," Gerald said.

He picked up his clipboard.

"Also, your statue in the east hall is not anchored.

It will tip over during an earthquake.

I have filed a preliminary notice. You have thirty days.

"

Gerald turned and walked back toward the library entrance. He moved with a stiff, careful gait, as if every step required calculation.

Kael watched him go. He looked at the fan. He looked at his mana bar.

He was poorer. He was annoyed. He was, for the first time in ten years, safe from a structural collapse.

He hated it.

***

Back in the library, the silence was heavy.

Gerald sat at his desk. It was a small, metal table pushed against the wall of his

"office," which was actually just a corner of his dungeon near the boiler room.

He stared at the clipboard, and the signature had worked. The Void Sentinel had watched it happen.

He could feel the Prism's attention. It was a cold weight on his shoulder.

The Prism didn't care about ventilation.

It cared about stability.

Gerald had just demonstrated that stability could be bought. That rules were currency.

But the cost was high.

His mana was critically low. If a raid party came now, he couldn't spawn a rat, let alone a mold spore. He had to wait for regen. He had to wait for the dungeon to heal.

And during that wait, he was vulnerable.

The intercom crackled.

"Gerald?" Sarah's voice was shaky. "Are you okay? The readings in Sector 4 went wild, then dropped. What did you do?"

"I filed paperwork," Gerald said.

Sarah was silent for a moment.

"You fought the neighbor."

"I cited him. There is a difference. Fighting is messy. Citing is precise."

"Did it work?"

"He paid. He's compliant. For now."

Gerald rubbed his eyes. His hands were shaking. Not from fear. From exhaustion.

He looked at the map on the wall. The Void Sentinel's tunnel was still there. Sealed, but present.

And now, a new line had appeared. A thin, blue line connecting Gerald's core to the shared boundary.

**NEW MECHANIC UNLOCKED: INFRASTRUCTURE TITHING.**

Gerald read the text.

*By enforcing code compliance on neighboring structures within a Shared Boundary, the Core may levy a daily mana tax proportional to the severity of the enforced upgrades.*

Gerald stared at the words, and he hadn't just saved money. He had found a revenue stream.

Vane had paid forty thousand. That was a one-time fee. But the tax?

The tax was ongoing.

Every day, Vane would have to pay a percentage of his mana generation to maintain the

"compliant" status. If he stopped paying, the citation would reactivate. The penalties would double.

Gerald smiled. It was a thin, tight expression.

He picked up his pen. He opened a new file.

**PROJECT: SECTOR 5 RETROFIT.**

Sector 5 was the home of the Slime King. A creature of chaos and acid. A creature who believed that walls were suggestions.

Gerald thought about the acid. He thought about the corrosion. He thought about the lack of drainage systems.

He wrote.

The water leak in the corner dripped. The rat squeaked.

Gerald didn't stop. He wrote until the light in the room changed from blue to red.

He needed more power. He needed to expand. But he couldn't expand without more mana. And he couldn't get mana without more citations.

He was trapped in a loop of his own making.

A loud bang echoed from the library above.

Dust fell from the ceiling.

Gerald froze. His pen hovered over the paper.

That wasn't Vane. Vane was in Sector 4. That sound was from the main lobby.

Someone was breaking in.

Gerald looked at the clock. It was 2:00 AM. The library was closed.

He stood up. His knees cracked. He walked to the edge of his dungeon footprint, near the library floorboards.

He could feel the vibrations. Heavy boots. Multiple sets.

Not adventurers. Adventurers moved with purpose. These moved with panic.

Gerald pressed his hand against the floor. He sent a pulse through the dungeon. He scanned the upper level.

Three figures. Human. They were wearing masks. They carried torches that burned with a black flame.

They weren't here to raid for loot, and they were here to burn the library down.

Gerald looked at his mana bar. It was at 12%.

He looked at the ceiling. The water leak had stopped, but the mold was growing thick and black in the corners.

He looked at the new blue line on his map, and he had a choice.

He could hide. He could stay in his sub-basement, let the library burn, and hope the fire didn't spread to his dungeon.

Or he could intervene.

If he intervened, he would have to fight. Fighting required mana. He didn't have enough.

But he had something else, and he had the building plans.

He knew where the gas lines were. He knew where the electrical panels were. He knew where the weakest points in the library's structure were.

He knew that if he triggered the fire suppression system, the chemical agents would react with the black torches.

It would be messy. It would be illegal.

It would definitely require a lot of paperwork.

Gerald closed his eyes. He imagined the form he would have to file. Form 9-Z: Emergency Structural Compromise.

He hated it. He hated the mess. He hated the risk.

But the library was his territory. And his territory was currently being violated.

Gerald took a deep breath. He opened his eyes.

He picked up his pen, and he didn't reach for his sword. He didn't reach for his claws.

He reached for his clipboard.

He wrote.

*NOTICE OF VIOLATION: UNLICENSED COMBUSTION SOURCE.*

*CHARGE: ARSON.*

*ENFORCEMENT: IMMEDIATE.*

Gerald slammed the paper against the floor.

The System registered the citation.

But there was no target in the sub-basement, and the targets were upstairs.

Gerald looked at the blue line. The shared boundary.

He couldn't cite them directly. They weren't dungeon cores.

But he could cite the building.

And if he cited the building, he could shut down the power.

He could turn off the lights. He could trigger the alarms.

He could make the building itself fight back.

Gerald focused his will. He pushed his mana into the electrical grid. It hurt. It felt like burning his own nerves.

The lights in the library flickered.

The intruders stopped. They looked up.

One of them raised his black torch.

Gerald gritted his teeth. He poured more mana into the grid. He overloaded the circuit breaker.

**SYSTEM ALERT: ELECTRICAL OVERLOAD.** **PENALTY: STRUCTURAL DAMAGE TO SURROUNDING DUNGEON.**

The ceiling above Gerald groaned. Cracks spiderwebbed through the concrete.

He had to stop. If he pushed further, he would destroy his own dungeon.

But if he stopped, the intruders would reach the gas main.

He looked at the mold spores. They were dormant. They were small.

He looked at the water leak. It was gone.

He looked at the rat. The rat was hiding.

Gerald had one option left, and he could release the mold.

If he released the mold into the air vents, it would spread to the upper floors. It would choke the intruders. It would trigger the System's biological containment protocol.

But releasing the mold would drop his Compliance Rating. It would cite him for bio-hazard.

It would cost him everything.

The intruder above stepped forward. He kicked the floorboard.

Gerald felt the vibration in his core, and he looked at the pen in his hand.

He looked at the ceiling.

He made his choice.

He snapped the pen.

"System," Gerald whispered. "Execute Emergency Protocol 1."

The floor beneath him shook.
