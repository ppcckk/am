Chapter 129: Sworn Testimony

# Chapter 129

The citation did not arrive as a physical document. It materialized in the air between Gerald and Marcus Thorne, glowing with the sickly green hue of System enforcement.

Gerald squinted at the floating text. The font was Sans-Serif. It was legible, which was already an improvement over the usual dungeon UI.

**SYSTEM NOTICE: LICENSE REVOCATION INITIATED.** **CLAIMANT: DIRECTOR MARCUS THORNE (CITY OF ASHFORD).** **RESPONDENT: GERALD HUTCHINS (CORE ID: 894-ALPHA).** **ALLEGATION: UNAUTHORIZED INFRASTRUCTURE MODIFICATION AND FRAUDULENT COMPLIANCE FILING.** **STATUS: PENDING JUDICIAL REVIEW (VIRTUAL).**

Thorne tapped his cane against the dry concrete patch. The sound echoed sharply, cutting through the dripping water.

"You filed a Form 11-B for a rat," Thorne said, his voice dropping to a conversational whisper. "Then you filed a Form 4-C for a water leak that you caused yourself by drilling into the aquifer to cool your magma vents. You are not a dungeon core, Gerald. You are a liability."

Gerald didn't look up from the notice. He was calculating the timeline.

Judicial reviews in the System operated on a strict ninety-second countdown.

If he didn't counter-file, his core would be forcibly deregistered. His dungeon would collapse. The library above would lose its foundation.

And Thorne would have a new parking garage where the reading room used to be.

Gerald's hands moved. They didn't reach for a weapon. They reached for the interface.

He swiped left. A translucent overlay appeared, cluttered with tabs he had spent the last four hundred chapters organizing. *Ventilation Logs.* *Load-Bearing Stress Tests.* *Biological Waste Disposal Certificates.*

*Form 22-A: Petition for Stay of Execution.*

The System rejected it.

**ERROR: SUBJECT LACKS LEGAL STANDING TO FILE FOR STAY WITHOUT CERTIFIED LEGAL COUNSEL.**

Thorne smiled. It was the smile of a man who had read the bylaws.

"See, that's the problem with you, Hutchins. You think the code is a shield. It's a sword. And you're holding it by the blade."

Gerald ignored him. His fingers flew across the holographic keyboard. He wasn't trying to argue the facts. He was arguing the jurisdiction.

Thorne was acting as a City Official. The System did not care about city ordinances. The System cared about Dungeon Compliance.

Unless Thorne was acting as a Core.

Gerald paused.

He looked at Thorne's chest. There was no visible core interface.

No floating stats.

No mana pool.

But Thorne had said he was here to take the city.

*To: System Inspector General.* *Subject: Immediate Jurisdictional Conflict.* *Body: Claimant Marcus Thorne is asserting authority over Dungeon Core operations while holding municipal office. Per System Rule 7.4, 'Dual Allegiance Clause,' this constitutes a conflict of interest. Claimant's authority is invalid until cleared by the Inspector General.*

The green notice flickered.

Thorne's smile vanished.

"You're bluffing," Thorne said. "The Dual Allegiance Clause is a myth. A rumor spread by lower-level cores to stall hearings."

"It's not a myth," Gerald said, his voice flat. "It's in the appendix.

Section 12, subsection D.

I read it.

Twice."

The System processed the query. The timer on the revocation notice froze at twelve seconds.

**NOTICE PAUSED. REASON: JURISDICTIONAL QUERY PENDING. ESTIMATED REVIEW TIME: 30 SECONDS.**

Thorne stepped forward, his cane hovering inches from Gerald's face. "You don't have the power to invoke that clause. You're a municipal employee. You're not a Core Lawyer."

"I am a Building Inspector," Gerald said. "And in this dungeon, I am the only authority. Which means you have to prove you aren't violating the Dual Allegiance Clause. You have to prove you are acting solely as a Core."

Thorne's eyes narrowed.

"And if I can't?" Gerald asked.

"Then you're just a bureaucrat with a god complex," Thorne spat.

"No," Gerald said. "Then you're a criminal."

The thirty seconds ticked down.

Gerald watched Thorne. The man was sweating. Not from the humidity, but from the realization that he had walked into a trap designed by a man who had spent thirty-one years finding loopholes in zoning laws.

Thorne raised his hand. A pulse of dark energy rippled outward. It wasn't an attack.

It was a scan. He was trying to read Gerald's compliance rating directly, looking for a weakness to exploit outside the legal framework.

Gerald didn't dodge. He held his ground.

The energy washed over him. Gerald felt the familiar tingling of a diagnostic sweep.

**SCAN COMPLETE.** **TARGET: GERALD HUTCHINS.** **COMPLIANCE RATING: 88/100.** **VIOLATIONS:** *- Minor electrical irregularity in Sector 4 (Grounding strap missing).* *- Egress route obstruction in Sector 7 (Stacked pallets).*

Thorne laughed.

"88? You think that's good? I have a Compliance Rating of 99.

I have zero violations.

I am the perfect Core."

Gerald looked at the scan results. Then he looked at Thorne.

"99," Gerald said. "In a Class-4 Dungeon."

"It's a masterpiece," Thorne said. "Flawless."

"It's a lie," Gerald said.

He pulled up a different tab. *Historical Audit Logs.*

"Every Core has a hidden penalty for 'Perfection.' It's not a bonus. It's a red flag. The System flags Cores with zero violations for over-scrutinization because it implies the Core is suppressing natural decay or manipulating spawns to avoid penalties. It's called 'Static Entropy Suppression.' It's a Class A felony."

Thorne went pale.

"You're making that up."

"I found it in the appeal records of Core 404," Gerald said.

"A dungeon that collapsed because it refused to let its walls rot. The Inspector General cited it for 'Unnatural Structural Integrity.' You're doing the same thing, Marcus. You're artificially maintaining your walls to keep your rating high.

You're cheating entropy."

The System chimed.

**JURISDICTIONAL QUERY RESOLVED.** **DETERMINATION: CLAIMANT MARCUS THORNE IS ACTING IN MUNICIPAL CAPACITY.** **VIOLATION: DUAL ALLEGIANCE.** **PENALTY: REVOCATION OF CLAIM.** **COUNTER-CITATION ISSUED: CORE 894-ALPHA HAS NOTIFICATION OF SUSPECTED STATIC ENTROPY SUPPRESSION.**

Gerald blinked.

The citation against Thorne had vanished. But a new notification had appeared in Gerald's peripheral vision.

**NOTICE: INSPECTOR GENERAL AUDIT SCHEDULED.** **DATE: IMMEDIATE.** **REASON: ALLEGATIONS OF SYSTEM MANIPULATION (REF: CORE 404 PRECEDENT).**

Thorne looked at the new notification on Gerald's interface. He grinned. It was a cold, predatory thing.

"You just called the Inspector General on yourself, Hutchins. You wanted a witness. You got an executioner."

Gerald looked at the timer.

**AUDIT BEGINS IN: 10 SECONDS.**

The water around Gerald's knees boiled. Not from heat, but from the pressure of the System focusing its attention on him.

"I didn't call for a witness," Gerald said, his voice trembling slightly. "I called for a distraction."

Thorne turned, confused. "What?"

Gerald wasn't looking at Thorne anymore. He was looking at the far wall of the dungeon. The one that had been flooded. The one where the water pressure was rising.

He had filed a Form 11-B for the rat. He had filed a Form 4-C for the leak. But he had also filed a Form 9-Z.

*Form 9-Z: Request for Emergency Structural Relief.*

He had filed it an hour ago.

The System had rejected it.

But the System had not canceled it.

And the Inspector General was now watching.

Gerald had not asked the System to fix the leak. He had asked the System to *record* the leak as a critical failure.

A critical failure triggered an automatic emergency response protocol.

The walls of the dungeon groaned. Not the groan of stone, but the groan of metal under stress.

The pipes in the ceiling, which Gerald had inspected last week and certified as "Adequate," burst.

Not because they were weak.

Because Gerald had removed their pressure relief valves during the inspection, claiming they were "non-compliant with modern noise ordinance standards."

He had filed the removal as a *violation fix*.

The System had accepted the fix.

But the System had not checked if the pipes would burst without them.

The water erupted. A torrent of high-pressure sewage and clean water sprayed across the dungeon floor.

Thorne yelped, jumping back. His expensive suit was instantly soaked.

Gerald stood his ground. He was drenched, shivering, but upright.

He held up his hand.

**SYSTEM ALERT: EMERGENCY STRUCTURAL FAILURE DETECTED.** **CORE 894-ALPHA IS ACTIVELY MITIGATING DAMAGE.** **AUDIT PAUSED FOR EMERGENCY RESPONSE.**

The Inspector General's presence receded. The audit window was suspended.

Thorne wiped sludge from his face.

He was furious.

But he was also trapped. The water was rising.

The emergency protocol meant Gerald had control of the floodgates now.

"You're insane," Thorne hissed. "You'll destroy your own dungeon."

"I'll file a variance," Gerald said.

He pulled out a wet, soggy piece of paper from his pocket. It was a physical backup. A relic from his old life.

*Form 9-Z: Variance Request.* *Reason: Catastrophic Infrastructure Failure caused by external interference (See: Marcus Thorne).* *Requested Relief: Temporary immunity from Compliance Rating penalties for duration of flood.*

He slammed the paper onto the floating System interface.

It didn't work. Paper didn't work.

But the *intent* did.

Gerald pushed his mana into the paper. He poured every ounce of his will into that single sheet of cellulose.

*This is a violation by the Claimant. The damage is not my fault.*

The System hesitated.

Then, a chime.

**VARIANCE ACCEPTED.** **REASON: EXTERNAL SABOTAGE DOCUMENTED IN REAL-TIME.** **CORE 894-ALPHA GRANTED TEMPORARY COMPLIANCE EXEMPTION.** **DURATION: UNTIL FLOOD SUBSIDES.**

Thorne stared. "You… you used the sabotage as a defense."

"I used the sabotage as evidence," Gerald corrected.

He looked at Thorne.

"You have thirty seconds to leave before I cite you for creating a hazardous working environment. And Marcus?"

Thorne paused at the edge of the dry patch.

"If you stay, I will cite you for *my* violations. And since you're here, you're *present*."

Thorne sneered. "You can't cite a man for a dungeon's violations."

"Watch me."

Gerald pointed to the water swirling around Thorne's feet.

"Slip-and-fall hazard. Non-compliant flooring. Lack of drainage. I am citing you, Marcus Thorne, for these conditions."

The System pinged.

**CITATION ISSUED.** **TARGET: MARCUS THORNE.** **VIOLATIONS: 3.** **PENALTY: TEMPORARY DEBUFF TO AGILITY AND STAMINA.**

Thorne stumbled. His cane slipped. He caught himself, but barely. His legs wobbled.

He looked at Gerald with pure hatred.

"This isn't over, Hutchins. The Inspector General will come back. And when he does, he won't care about variances. He will care about the fact that you caused a flood."

"I didn't cause it," Gerald said. "The pipes did."

"You removed the valves."

"I filed the paperwork."

Thorne disappeared into the shadows, leaving a trail of muddy footprints.

Gerald slumped. The water was still rising. His exemption was temporary. The audit was still pending.

He looked at his interface.

**NEW MESSAGE: FROM UNKNOWN USER.** **CONTENT: "Impressive trick, Inspector. But you've exposed your hand. Now they all know how you fight."**

Gerald frowned.

*Unknown User.*

He tried to open the message. It didn't open.

Instead, the entire dungeon lit up.

Every torch, every bioluminescent moss patch, every crack in the ceiling flared bright white.

**SYSTEM BROADCAST: GLOBAL AUDIT INITIATED.** **TARGET: ALL CORES IN ASHFORD ZONE.** **REASON: SUSPECTED NETWORK CONSPIRACY.** **STATUS: LOCKDOWN.**

Gerald's heart stopped.

Not just his audit.

Everyone's.

The neighboring dungeons. The high-level raid groups.

The merchant cores.

The slum dungeons. All of them were being audited.

And if they were audited, they would be looking for scapegoats.

Gerald heard a noise above. The library.

People were screaming.

The System had locked the library doors.

And Gerald was the only one who knew how to open them.

Because he had built the library's foundation.

And he had a key.

A single, rusted key, hanging from his belt. The Master Key to the Municipal Building.

He looked at the water. He looked at the key.

If he used the key, he could open the library. But the System would see him accessing a non-dungeon structure. That was a zoning violation.

A Class C felony.

It would revoke his exemption.

It would restart the audit.

But if he didn't use the key, the people inside would suffocate. Or starve. Or whatever the System did to citizens during a lockdown.

Gerald gripped the key.

His hand shook.

He looked at the notification.

**AUDIT RESUMES IN: 5 SECONDS.**

He had to choose.

Save the city, and lose his dungeon.

Or save his dungeon, and let the city burn.

Gerald closed his eyes.

He thought of the thirty-one years.

He thought of the forms.

He thought of the promise.

*The building code is not a suggestion.*

It was a promise.

Gerald opened his eyes.

He turned toward the stairs leading up to the library.

He walked toward the door.

He did not use the key.

Instead, he reached into his inventory and pulled out a single item.

*Form 11-B: Unlicensed Biological Entity.*

He had never deleted it.

He had kept it.

He had been keeping it for a reason.

He had not cited the rat.

He had *registered* it.

And he had made it a witness.

Gerald held the form up.

"I call a witness," he said to the empty air.

The form glowed.

A small, confused rat appeared on the floor. It squeaked.

**WITNESS ACCEPTED.** **TESTIMONY PENDING.**

The System paused.

For a witness, the System required testimony.

And a witness could only testify if they were sworn in.

Gerald knelt in the water.

"Speak," he whispered to the rat.

The rat looked at him.

It opened its mouth.

And instead of a squeak, a voice came out.

Thorne's voice.

*"The code is a lie."*
