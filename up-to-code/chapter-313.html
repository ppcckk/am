Chapter 313: The Inspector General

# Chapter 313

The marble floor was cold enough to radiate heat away from Elena's skin.

She scrambled up from the polished surface, her boots skidding on the high-gloss finish. Her hand flew to her mouth, suppressing a gag reflex that had nothing to do with the smell and everything to do with the view.

Gerald Hutchins lay face-down on the pristine white expanse, and he did not move.

A pool of crimson was spreading beneath him, blooming like a Rorschach test against the unblemished geometry of the room. His left arm hung at an unnatural angle, the shoulder joint dislocated, the bone visible through the torn sleeve of his cardigan.

Elena stared at the injury. She had seen worse. She had seen adventurers gutted by chimeras and burned alive by fire elementals. She had seen men die in their own traps.

But this felt different.

This felt administrative.

The room around them was perfect.

Too perfect, and the walls were seamless marble. The lighting came from nowhere, a soft, ambient glow that cast no shadows.

The air smelled of lemon polish and ozone.

There was no dust. There were no cracks. There were no exits.

Elena looked at the door, which had been there when they entered. A heavy oak slab with a brass handle.

Now it was gone.

Smooth wall. Unbroken marble.

Panic, sharp and bright, spiked in her chest. She turned back to Gerald. He was breathing. Shallow, ragged gasps that fogged in the sterile air.

"Gerald," she whispered.

She crawled toward him. Her knees ached against the hardness of the floor. She reached for his uninjured arm, shaking it.

"Gerald, wake up."

His eyelids fluttered.

For a second, they were clear. Confused. Human.

Then they clouded over, reflecting the white ceiling above.

**[USER NOT RESPONSIVE.]**

The text appeared in the air, floating in a polite, sans-serif font. It hovered near Gerald's head like a halo of bad news.

Elena slammed her hand onto the floating words. They passed through her fingers like smoke.

"System!" she shouted. "He's hurt! He needs a healer!"

The text did not change.

**[MEDICAL TRIAGE IS NOT A CORE FUNCTION.]** **[PLEASE FILE A COMPLIANCE EXCEPTION FOR EXTERNAL ASSISTANCE.]**

Elena froze.

File?

She looked around the room. No desk. No papers. No ink.

Just marble.

And Gerald.

The old man groaned, a wet, rattling sound. His fingers twitched, trying to find purchase on the smooth floor.

"Ventilation..." Gerald mumbled.

Elena blinked.

"What?"

Gerald's head lolled to the side. His eyes were open now, staring at a point in space that Elena could not see.

"Ventilation is... excessive."

He coughed. Blood speckled the white floor.

"It's too high. The exchange rate is... inefficient."

Elena grabbed his good hand.

"Gerald, focus. We're trapped. The door is gone."

Gerald didn't answer her. He was looking at the ceiling.

He raised his good hand. His fingers traced a shape in the air.

"Air returns are... insufficient," he muttered. "The pressure differential is creating a vacuum seal."

A low hum vibrated through the marble floor.

It wasn't a roar. It was a whine. The sound of a HVAC system running at maximum capacity.

Elena felt the air thicken. It became harder to breathe. Not because of lack of oxygen, but because the atmosphere was becoming dense, heavy.

**[WARNING: ATMOSPHERIC PRESSURE EXCEEDING OSHA STANDARDS FOR HUMAN OCCUPANCY.]**

The text flashed red. Then it corrected itself to a soothing blue.

**[OCCUPANT IS NON-HUMAN. REGULATION WAIVED.]**

"Non-human?" Elena shouted. "I'm human! He's human!"

**[CORE INTEGRITY COMPROMISED. SUBJECT CLASSIFIED AS 'DUNGEON ASSET'. ASSETS DO NOT REQUIRE COMFORT.]**

Gerald's face went slack.

Something shifted in his expression. The pain vanished. The confusion vanished.

In its place was a look of profound, bureaucratic irritation, and he sat up.

The movement was wrong. His shoulder snapped back into place with a sickening crunch. Gerald didn't flinch. He didn't even wince.

He stood up.

His cardigan was torn. His trousers were stained with blood. But he stood with the posture of a man who had just realized the coffee in the breakroom was decaf.

"Gerald?" Elena asked, backing away.

Gerald looked at her. His eyes were no longer cloudy. They were sharp. Critical.

He looked at the walls, and he looked at the lighting fixtures, which were embedded flush with the ceiling.

He looked at the floor.

Then he looked at the air.

"This room," Gerald said.

His voice was dry. It lacked the tremor of fear.

It lacked the rasp of injury.

It sounded like a man reading a rejection letter.

"Is non-compliant."

Elena stared at him.

"We're trapped, Gerald. The Inspector General put us here."

"Correct."

Gerald walked to the center of the room. He stopped. He placed his hands on his hips.

"The Inspector General," Gerald said, "has a fundamental misunderstanding of jurisdiction."

Elena frowned.

"Jurisdiction?

We're in his dungeon.

His basement.

His rules."

Gerald shook his head.

"Look at the floor, Elena."

She looked.

White marble. Veined with grey.

"It's tile," Gerald said. "Or stone. But it's not set with the correct expansion gaps."

He kicked the floor.

Nothing happened. The tile didn't crack. It didn't shift.

"But the walls," Gerald continued, pointing to the smooth marble surfaces. "Are load-bearing. They support the weight of the Olympus Core above us."

"So?"

"So, if the floor is not anchored to the foundation, and the walls are not anchored to the floor, the structure is floating."

Gerald turned to the empty air.

"System," he said. "I am invoking Article 4, Section C of the Inter-Core Structural Liability Act."

The air shimmered.

**[PROTOCOL INVOKED.]** **[WAITING FOR AUDIT.]**

Elena's heart hammered against her ribs.

"Gerald, what are you doing?"

"Measuring," Gerald said.

He pulled a pen from his pocket, which was a cheap ballpoint. Blue ink.

He scribbled something in the air.

The System responded instantly.

**[AUDIT INITIATED.]** **[CHECKING LOAD DISTRIBUTION.]**

A red laser line projected from the ceiling, scanning the room. It swept over the walls, the floor, the corners.

**[ERROR.]** **[LOAD BEARING WALLS NOT ANCHORED TO FOUNDATION.]** **[STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY: 12%.]** **[VIOLATION: CRITICAL.]**

Gerald nodded. He looked satisfied. It was the look a man gets when he finds a cockroach in the kitchen. It is a look of confirmation.

"As I suspected," Gerald said. "He didn't build this room. He grew it. He used magic to create the shape, but he didn't use engineering to secure it."

Elena looked at the red laser. It was pulsing.

"He's going to kill us," Elena said. "The pressure is getting higher. I can feel it in my ears."

"No," Gerald said. "He's going to be shut down."

Gerald raised his hand. He pointed at the wall.

"Citation," he said.

The word didn't come from his mouth. It came from the air itself.

**[CITATION ISSUED.]** **[TARGET: OLYMPUS SUB-BASEMENT.]** **[VIOLATION: UNANCHORED STRUCTURAL ELEMENTS.]** **[PENALTY: IMMEDIATE DECOMMISSION.]**

The room shook.

A crack appeared in the marble floor.

Then another.

The perfect white surface fractured. Dust billowed up, coating the pristine air.

The ceiling lights flickered.

The hum of the ventilation system stuttered, then died.

**[SYSTEM FAILURE.]** **[CORE INTEGRITY COMPROMISED.]** **[EVACUATION PROTOCOLS ENGAGED.]**

The walls melted.

Not dissolve. Melt. The marble turned to sludge, dripping down like hot wax.

Elena screamed. She covered her head with her arms.

But Gerald didn't move, and he stood in the center of the chaos, watching the destruction with critical eyes. He took notes in the air with his blue pen.

"The expansion joints," he muttered. "They're too narrow. He didn't account for thermal shock."

A chunk of ceiling fell. It missed Gerald by inches.

He didn't look up.

Elena lunged for him. She grabbed his good arm and pulled.

"We have to move!"

"Gerald, move!"

He resisted, and he was writing.

**[CITATION CONFIRMED.]** **[APPEAL WINDOW OPEN: 30 SECONDS.]**

Gerald looked at the number floating in the air.

"Thirty seconds," he said. "That's not enough time to appeal a structural violation of this magnitude."

"Gerald!"

"He's going to try," Gerald said. "He always tries."

The walls were gone now, and the room was a ruin of dripping stone and swirling dust.

But in the center of the collapse, a new shape was forming.

It was smaller than Gerald.

It was made of the same white marble, but it was twisted. Contorted. It looked like a man made of broken columns.

The Inspector General, which had no face. Just a smooth, blank surface where features should be.

It stood amidst the rubble of its own perfect dungeon.

It raised a hand.

**[APPEAL FILED.]** **[REASON: FORCE MAJEURE.]** **[CLAIM: THE DESTRUCTION WAS CAUSED BY EXTERNAL AGENT (G. HUTCHINS). NOT BY STRUCTURAL DEFECT.]**

Gerald's eyes narrowed.

"Force majeure," he repeated. The word tasted like ash in his mouth. "He's blaming me."

"It's true," Elena gasped, coughing in the dust. "You destroyed it. You cited him."

"I cited a violation," Gerald said. "The violation existed before I entered. If the wall was not anchored, it was always going to fall. The citation merely accelerated the inevitable."

The marble figure tilted its head.

**[ARGUMENT ACCEPTED FOR REVIEW.]** **[HEARING COMMENCING.]**

The dust settled, and the room was gone.

They were standing on a platform of pure white light, suspended in a void of black nothingness.

There was no floor. No ceiling. No walls.

Just the platform, and the two of them, and the faceless marble man.

And a new System message, large and gold.

**[MINI-GAME: STRUCTURAL LOGIC PUZZLE.]** **[OBJECTIVE: PROVE THE VIOLATION EXISTS.]** **[TIME LIMIT: 60 SECONDS.]**

Elena looked at Gerald.

"A puzzle? You can't solve a puzzle when you're being dragged to hell!"

Gerald adjusted his glasses. They were cracked, but he put them back on.

"It's not a puzzle," Gerald said. "It's a diagram."

He reached out and touched the air.

A blueprint appeared, which was the blueprint of the Olympus Sub-Basement.

It was complex. Intricate. A maze of magical conduits and physical supports.

Gerald traced a line with his finger.

"Here," he said. "This support beam. It's made of enchanted granite. It has a tensile strength of four thousand pounds."

The marble figure remained still.

"And here," Gerald pointed to a joint. "This connection. It's held by mana-fused glue."

He tapped the joint.

**[ANALYSIS: GLUE BOND STRENGTH: 200 POUNDS.]**

"Two hundred," Gerald said. "Against four thousand. That's a failure point.

It's not a matter of opinion. It's math.

"

The marble figure stepped forward.

**[COUNTER-ARGUMENT: THE DUNGEON WAS DESIGNED TO FLEX. THE GLUE IS ELASTIC.]**

Gerald paused, and he looked at the blueprint.

He looked at the figure.

He looked at Elena.

For the first time, Gerald looked unsure.

"Flex," he said. "You're claiming elastic deformation."

**[CORRECT.]**

Gerald frowned. He pulled out his pen. He started sketching rapidly in the air.

"Show me the modulus of elasticity," Gerald demanded.

The System flashed a number.

**[YOUNG'S MODULUS: 2 GPa.]**

Gerald stared at the number.

His hand trembled.

Just slightly.

Elena saw it, and she knew that tremor. It wasn't fear.

It was doubt.

Gerald Hutchins did not doubt. He measured. He checked. He verified.

But this number...

"It's too low," Gerald whispered.

"What?" Elena asked.

"The glue," Gerald said. "If the modulus is 2 GPa, the dungeon *can* flex. It *can* survive the stress."

He looked at the blueprint, and he looked at the cracks in the walls.

He had destroyed the room. He had cited the violation.

But if the room could flex...

Then the violation wasn't structural, which was aesthetic.

And aesthetic violations didn't warrant decommission.

Gerald's face went pale.

He had made a mistake.

He had cited a dungeon that was safe.

And now the Inspector General was going to win the appeal.

**[APPEAL LIKELY TO SUCCEED.]** **[RECOMMENDATION: WITHDRAW CITATION.]**

Gerald stared at the text.

If he withdrew, the dungeon would be restored, and he and Elena would be trapped in a perfect, inescapable bureaucracy forever.

If he didn't withdraw, he would be cited for false accusation.

**[PENALTY FOR FALSE CITATION: PERMANENT CORE DELETION.]**

Elena grabbed his arm.

"Gerald, withdraw it."

Gerald didn't move, and he was looking at the blueprint again.

At the line where the glue met the granite.

He was counting.

Not the load.

The age of the glue.

"How old is this glue?" Gerald asked.

The System paused.

**[DATA UNAVAILABLE.]**

"Check the core timestamp," Gerald said. "When was this dungeon built?"

**[3,000 YEARS AGO.]**

Gerald's eyes widened.

Three thousand years.

Glue degrades. Even mana-glue.

If the modulus was 2 GPa now...

What was it then?

Gerald scribbled a formula in the air, and he didn't look at the Inspector General.

He looked at Elena.

"Run," he said.

"What?"

"Run to the edge of the platform. Now."

"Gerald—"

"The calculation is wrong," Gerald said, his voice rising.

"The glue didn't fail because it was weak. It failed because it was *brittle*. It had crystallized.

Over three thousand years, the mana-dust settled.

The bond became rigid.

"

He pointed at the blueprint.

"I need to prove the crystallization. I need a sample."

"We don't have a sample!"

Gerald looked at his hand, and he looked at the blood on his cardigan.

He looked at the white marble dust covering his clothes.

He reached into his pocket and pulled out a small, crumpled receipt from a hardware store.

"I have a sample," he said.

He held it up, which was a scrap of sandpaper.

He had used it earlier to smooth a rough patch on his own dungeon wall, before he had been dragged here.

He rubbed the sandpaper against the marble platform.

White dust fell.

He caught the dust in his hand, and he held it up to the light.

"Analysis," Gerald said.

"Silica content: 90%. Binder content: 5%. This is not fresh glue.

This is fossilized resin.

"

The System paused.

**[SAMPLE ACCEPTED.]** **[RE-ANALYZING...]**

The marble figure stepped back.

It seemed to shrink.

**[ERROR IN ORIGINAL ASSUMPTION.]** **[GLUE IS NOT ELASTIC. IT IS VITRIFIED.]** **[STRUCTURAL FAILURE WAS INEVITABLE.]** **[CITATION UPHELD.]**

The platform beneath them vanished, and they fell.

But Gerald didn't scream.

He was smiling.

A small, tight, professional smile.

"Next time," he said, as they plummeted into the darkness, "check the expiration date."

And then the darkness swallowed them.

But not before a new message flashed in Gerald's vision, bright red and final.

**[CORE STATUS: RESTORED.]** **[LOCATION: UNKNOWN.]** **[WARNING: THE INSPECTOR GENERAL IS NOW AWAKE.]**

The Inspector General is awake.
