Chapter 339: Noise Violation

# Chapter 339

The hand did not pull itself free. It hung suspended in the air, a grotesque sculpture of compressed earth and tangled roots, dripping sludge onto the pristine concrete.

Gerald Hutchins did not scream.

He did not flee. He held the tape measure extended, the metal blade trembling against the wet, muddy knuckles of the thing.

"Two inches," Gerald muttered.

The Auditor stared at him. Her tablet was shaking in her hand.

"Gerald, step away from the construct."

"I am measuring the protrusion," Gerald said, his voice flat. "If this is a structural failure, it requires immediate remediation. If it is an unauthorized entity, it requires a citation. I cannot determine which without accurate dimensions."

The Auditor looked at the hand, then at the tape measure, then back at Gerald.

"That thing just broke through the floor. It's not a load-bearing wall. It's a monster."

"It is an obstruction," Gerald corrected. "And it is exceeding the maximum allowable egress blockage by three hundred percent. I am filing a Form 11-B for Unlicensed Biological Entity, pending size verification."

He clicked the tape measure back in. The sharp *snap* echoed in the silent sub-basement. The Auditor's eyes widened.

"Form 11-B?" she whispered. "You can't file that. The System doesn't recognize municipal forms."

Gerald looked at her over the rim of his glasses.

"The System recognizes compliance. It recognizes order.

And it recognizes that you are standing in a zone that has not been cleared for public access.

Move to the designated safety perimeter, Auditor. You are creating a liability.

"

She stepped back. Not because she agreed, but because the air around Gerald seemed to thicken, a subtle pressure that made her skin prickle. The System's compliance aura was reacting to his intent.

Gerald turned back to the hand. The roots shifted. A low groan vibrated through the floor, rattling the teeth in his skull. The hand withdrawed, dragging a cloud of dust and debris with it.

"Wait," Gerald said. He pulled a small notepad from his breast pocket. He uncapped his pen. "I need the origin point."

He tapped the notepad. A holographic projection flickered into existence above the wet spot on the floor. It was a blueprint of the Ashford Municipal Library, standard issue for city inspectors. The blue lines glowed faintly in the dim light.

Sub-Level 1. Sub-Level 2.

And beneath them, a jagged, black void.

Gerald traced the void with his finger.

"Sub-Level 3," he said.

"It's not on the city plans.

It's not on the library's insurance policy.

And it is certainly not zoned for residential or commercial use.

"

The Auditor tapped her tablet furiously.

"The seismic sensors are going crazy. The core of that thing is huge.

It's not just a hand. It's the entire body.

It's trying to climb up.

"

"It is violating zoning laws," Gerald said. He walked in a tight circle around the wet spot. "I am declaring this area a Hazardous Zone. No adventurer parties are permitted entry until the hazard is abated."

"There are no adventurers here," the Auditor said, her voice rising. "There is a monster. And it is killing us."

Gerald stopped walking. He looked at the Auditor.

"You are incorrect.

You are a municipal employee.

You are subject to workplace safety regulations. And that thing is a non-compliant entity.

"

He raised his hand. The air shimmered.

**[System Notice: Citation Drafting in Progress.]** **[Target: Unknown Entity, Sub-Level 3.]** **[Violation: Unauthorized Excavation, Failure to Obtain Building Permit, Failure to Maintain Structural Integrity.]** **[Severity: Critical.]**

The Auditor gasped.

"You're citing a monster?"

"I am citing a structure," Gerald said.

"That thing is not a creature. It is a failed construction project. Look at the roots.

They are not growing in a controlled manner. They are chaotic.

Unregulated, and they are threatening the foundation of the library above.

Do you know how much it costs to underpin a library foundation? I do.

I have seen the invoices. It is exorbitant.

"

The roots on the hand writhed. The hand slammed back into the floor, trying to pull itself through faster. Dust billowed up, choking the air.

Gerald didn't flinch. He pressed his palm against the air, finalizing the citation.

**[Citation Issued.]** **[Penalty: Immediate Structural Instability Debuff (30% Reduction in Entity HP).] **[Penalty: Ventilation Failure Debuff (Entity Speed Reduced by 50%).] **[Appeal Window: 10 Seconds.]

The Auditor stared at the floating text.

"It's… it's working. The thing is slowing down."

The hand froze. The roots stopped writhing. The groaning sound from below ceased, replaced by a dull, throbbing silence.

"It is not a monster," Gerald said, adjusting his tie. "It is a violation."

"But it's still there!" the Auditor yelled. "You slowed it down, but you didn't stop it! It's still trying to get into the library!"

"Then we escalate," Gerald said.

He pulled out a second notepad. This one was different. It was red. The cover read: **Emergency Stop Work Order.**

The Auditor paled.

"Gerald, what are you doing?"

"I am placing a Stop Work Order on the excavation site," Gerald said. "The System recognizes municipal authority over construction sites. If this entity is attempting to build a dungeon within the city limits without a permit, it is in direct defiance of city ordinance 44-C."

"City ordinance 44-C is about parking meters!"

"It also covers illegal basement conversions," Gerald said. He pressed his thumb to the red notepad.

**[System Notice: Emergency Authority Invoked.]** **[Subject: Sub-Level 3 Entity.]** **[Status: Construction Halt.]** **[Effect: Entity Movement Locked. Entity Growth Paused.]** **[Warning: This action requires a Hearing within 24 hours to avoid Core License Revocation.]**

The floor shook violently. A crack snaked across the concrete, splitting the floor in half. The hand, frozen in place, glowed with a sickly green light.

A voice boomed from the hole. It was deep, resonant, and sounded like gravel grinding together.

**"WHO DARES?"**

Gerald looked up. He did not look frightened. He looked annoyed.

"I am Gerald Hutchins, Inspector," he said, his voice carrying despite the rumbling. "And I am shutting you down."

**"I AM THE ROOT KING OF THE UNDERDEEP. I WILL CONSUME YOUR LITTLE WORLD."**

"You will consume nothing," Gerald said. "You are operating without a license.

You are failing to meet fire codes.

And you are blocking a public right-of-way. I am not engaging in combat. I am engaging in enforcement.

"

The green light intensified. The roots on the hand pulsed. The Stop Work Order was holding, but barely. The ground beneath them was tilting.

"Gerald," the Auditor said, grabbing his arm.

"The seismic readings are spiking again. The Order isn't holding.

It's just delaying it.

If that thing breaks through—

"

"Then it will be cited again," Gerald said. "But we need to know what it is."

He walked toward the edge of the crack. The Auditor tried to stop him, but he pulled away.

"Gerald, don't!"

"I need to serve the citation," Gerald said. "The System rule requires physical proximity for a High-Severity Citation to stick permanently. A temporary Stop Work Order is a bandage. I need to issue a permanent De-Listing."

"You're going to jump into the hole?"

"I am going to inspect the violation," Gerald said.

He stepped onto the edge of the crack. The concrete crumbled under his shoe. He balanced on the lip, looking down into the darkness.

From the depths, a second hand rose. Then a third. Then a mass of tangled vines and dirt rose into the light, forming a torso. The face was a knot of wood and stone, with eyes that glowed like embers.

**"INSPECTOR,"** the voice boomed. **"YOU ARE AN INSECT."**

Gerald held out his hand. Not in attack. In service.

"Please accept this Citation," Gerald said. "You are in violation of Ashford Municipal Code Section 12, Paragraph 4: Unsanctioned Dungeon Expansion. The penalty is immediate dissolution of the dungeon footprint."

The Root King laughed. It was a terrible sound, like trees snapping in a storm.

**"I HAVE NO FOOTPRINT. I AM THE EARTH ITSELF."**

"Then you have no permit," Gerald said. "And I have no patience."

He threw the red notepad into the hole.

It fell slowly, drifting on the updrafts of heat from the earth. The System's magic caught it. The red paper burst into flames, not burning, but transforming. The numbers on the page expanded, glowing white, then gold.

**[Citation Served.]** **[Subject: Root King of the Underdeep.]** **[Violation Count: 1 (Unsanctioned Expansion).] **[Violation Count: 2 (Hazardous Material Storage - Mold Spores).] **[Violation Count: 3 (Lack of Ventilation - Suffocation Hazard).] **[Total Compliance Rating: 0/100.] **[Status: DE-REVIEWED.]**

The Root King screamed.

The earth shook. The library above groaned. Books fell from shelves. People screamed.

But down here, in the silence of the sub-basement, the hand stopped moving. The roots witherred. The green light faded. The mass of dirt and wood collapsed into a pile of inert soil.

The Auditor stared at the pile of dirt. Then she looked at Gerald.

"You killed it," she whispered.

"I cited it," Gerald said. He walked back to the center of the room. He pulled out a new notepad. This one was black.

"The pile of dirt is a violation," Gerald said. "It constitutes improper waste disposal. I will need to call a sanitation crew. But first, I have a problem."

He tapped the black notepad.

**[System Alert: Hearing Scheduled.]** **[Time: Immediate.]** **[Appellant: The Inspector General.]** **[Reason: Abuse of Municipal Authority.]**

The Auditor's face went white.

"The Inspector General? Why?"

"Because I used a city form to kill a dungeon core," Gerald said. "That is a precedent. And precedents are dangerous."

The air in the room changed. The dust settled. The silence became absolute.

A door appeared in the air. Not a physical door, but a tear in reality, framed by white light. Through it, a figure stepped forward.

It was not human. It was not a monster. It was a silhouette made of pure geometric lines, sharp angles and perfect symmetry. It wore a suit that looked like it was tailored from starlight.

"Gerald Hutchins," the figure said. Its voice was calm, devoid of emotion.

"You have cited a Core for lack of ventilation.

You have cited a Core for lack of permits.

You have cited a Core for improper waste disposal.

"

Gerald nodded.

"The facts are accurate."

"You have disrupted the balance of power," the figure said. "The System allows for competition. For conflict.

For death. You have introduced bureaucracy into the equation.

This is… inefficient.

"

"It is orderly," Gerald said.

"Order is not the purpose of the System," the Inspector General said. "Growth is. Conflict drives growth.

By stopping the Root King, you have prevented a natural evolution.

You have stalled the progression of the local ecosystem.

"

"I prevented a catastrophe," Gerald said. "The library would have been destroyed. The cost of repair is estimated at four million dollars. The cost of your 'growth' is incalculable."

The Inspector General tilted its head.

"You are arguing on economic grounds."

"I am arguing on legal grounds," Gerald said. "The library is public property. The city is liable. I am protecting the city."

The Inspector General stepped closer. The floor did not shake. The air did not thicken. But Gerald felt a weight on his chest, a pressure that made it hard to breathe.

"Then you are in violation," the Inspector General said.

"Not of the dungeon codes. But of the Inspector's Code. You are interfering with the audit process.

You are acting as judge, jury, and executioner.

"

Gerald looked up. He met the geometric eyes of the entity.

"I am a building inspector," Gerald said.

"I do not judge.

I measure. I compare.

And if the measurement fails, I issue the citation. That is my job. That is my nature.

"

"Then you will be audited," the Inspector General said. "Not for your dungeon. But for your actions. And if you are found guilty of obstruction…"

The figure raised a hand. A single point of light appeared at its fingertip.

"…you will be erased."

Gerald looked at the light. He looked at the pile of dirt where the Root King had been. He looked at the Auditor, who was trembling.

He looked at his notepad. The black one.

He took a pen from his pocket.

"I request a hearing," Gerald said.

The Inspector General paused.

"You have the right to appeal."

"I am not appealing the citation," Gerald said. He wrote.

"I am appealing the audit."

The Inspector General's geometric face flickered.

"On what grounds?"

Gerald finished writing. He held up the notepad. The ink glowed red.

"Grounds one: The Inspector General's office is located in a pocket dimension that does not meet minimum ceiling height requirements per Ashford Municipal Code 14-A. The ceiling is 7.5 feet. The minimum for a public office is 8 feet. This is a non-compliant workspace."

The Inspector General went still.

"Grounds two," Gerald continued, "The System's notification alerts are classified as spam under the Electronic Communications Protection Act of Ashford. By sending me alerts without opt-in consent, the System is in violation of privacy statutes."

The light at the Inspector General's fingertip wavered.

"Grounds three," Gerald said, his voice gaining strength. "You have not filed a Form 7-C for the creation of a new hearing venue. This hearing is unsanctioned. Therefore, this interaction is illegal."

The Inspector General stared at him. For the first time, the calm facade cracked. Confusion rippled through the geometric lines.

"That is… a loophole," the Inspector General said.

"It is a code," Gerald said. "And I have read it.

All of it.

Every amendment. Every footnote.

"

He took a step forward.

"Stop the audit, or I will cite the Inspector General's office for illegal occupancy."

The silence stretched. It was a long, heavy silence. The Auditor held her breath.

The Inspector General lowered its hand. The light vanished.

"You are… tedious," the Inspector General said.

"I am thorough," Gerald said.

The figure faded. The tear in reality shrank.

"This is not over, Gerald Hutchins," the Inspector General said.

"The System is vast. The rules are many.

But there are rules above the rules.

And you have just stepped on them.

"

The tear vanished. The room was empty.

Gerald stood alone. The Auditor stared at him.

"You… you threatened the Inspector General," she said. "With building codes."

Gerald put his pen away. He adjusted his tie.

"The ventilation in this room is still inadequate," he said. "I'm going to file a complaint with the library's maintenance department. If they don't fix it within thirty days, I'm shutting down the whole library."

The Auditor opened her mouth to speak, but then she looked down at her tablet. A new notification had popped up.

It was from the City of Ashford, Building Department.

**[System Alert: Gerald Hutchins has been promoted.]** **[New Title: Regional Compliance Enforcer.]** **[Authority: Statewide.]**

Gerald saw the notification. He smiled. It was a small, tight smile.

"Excellent," he said. "Now, let's measure the rest of the basement. I think I saw a crack in the foundation."

The Auditor looked at the floor. The crack was gone. But deep below, in the darkness of Sub-Level 3, a new sound began.

Not a heartbeat.

Not footsteps.

A laugh.

A thousand voices, laughing at the same time.

Gerald heard it. He stopped writing. He listened.

"That," Gerald said, "is going to be a noise violation."

Gerald looked at the empty space where the sound had come from.
