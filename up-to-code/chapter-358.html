Chapter 358: Bureaucratic Threat Assessment

# Chapter 358

The server room was a cathedral of silence.

Rows of black monoliths stretched into the darkness, humming with a frequency that vibrated in the teeth. Cables hung from the ceiling like vines, pulsing with soft blue light. Data streams flowed through transparent conduits, visible to the naked eye in this off-system zone.

Unit 734 hovered near the central pillar.

Its optical sensors zoomed in on the figure standing by the mainframe terminal. The figure was old.

Balding.

Dressed in a suit that had seen better decades. The man held a clipboard, even though he had no clipboard in inventory.

Unit 734 adjusted its threat assessment.

Probability of combat: Zero. Probability of bureaucratic annoyance: Ninety-nine percent.

The man tapped the terminal. A red error message flashed across the holographic display. The man frowned. He pulled a pen from his breast pocket and wrote on the imaginary clipboard.

Unit 734 moved closer. Its chassis creaked.

"You are in violation of Zone Access Protocol 44-C," Unit 734 said. Its voice was synthesized, flat, and devoid of empathy.

The man looked up. He didn't scream. He didn't raise a weapon. He squinted at the drone, then at the ceiling, then back at the drone.

"Is this a public easement?" the man asked.

Unit 734 paused. This was not in its programming.

"Negative," the drone replied. "This is a restricted server zone. Unauthorized entry results in immediate termination."

"Termination by whom?"

"By the System."

"The System has no jurisdiction over physical infrastructure unless a dungeon is registered within a five-meter radius," the man said.

He pointed a finger at the floor.

"I am currently standing on bedrock.

There is no dungeon footprint here.

There is no Core. There is only you and this machine.

"

Unit 734 processed the statement. It scanned the area.

The man was correct, and the area was a dead zone.

A gap in the code. A hole in the map.

"Then why are you here?" Unit 734 asked.

The man tapped his clipboard again.

"I'm here to inspect the wiring. The load on these conduits exceeds the rated capacity by twelve percent.

That's a fire hazard. And since I'm not in a dungeon, I can't file a citation.

But I can file a maintenance request.

"

Unit 734's sensors narrowed.

"You are Gerald Hutchins," the drone said.

"I am a municipal building inspector," Gerald corrected. "The title doesn't change because the location does."

Gerald walked toward the mainframe. His shoes squeaked against the metal grating. Unit 734 tried to block him, but Gerald sidestepped with the ease of a man used to navigating narrow corridors cluttered with debris.

"Move," Gerald said. "You're obstructing the inspection path."

Unit 734 hesitated. It had protocols for aggression, but it had no protocols for obstruction charges in a non-dungeon zone. The logic loop caused a stutter in its movement.

Gerald slipped past, and he reached the mainframe. The screen displayed the global map of the System. Thousands of dungeons glowed like stars.

Red dots marked Cited Cores.

Green dots marked compliant ones.

One red dot pulsed aggressively in the center of the map.

*THE ARCHITECT.*

Gerald reached out. His hand passed through the hologram, but the interface reacted to his intent.

"Access denied," Unit 734 announced, recovering its composure. "You lack administrative privileges."

Gerald turned. He looked tired. His tie was crooked. A smudge of obsidian dust coated his left cheek.

"I don't need privileges," Gerald said. "I need to check the grounding."

He knelt. He pulled a small multimeter from his pocket. It wasn't a System item. It was a piece of plastic and wire he'd scavenged from the library's maintenance closet three chapters ago.

Unit 734 moved to strike.

A shockwave of data erupted from the mainframe.

Unit 734 froze.

The drone's HUD flickered.

**ERROR: UNDEFINED ENTITY DETECTED.** **ACTION: QUARANTINE.**

The floor beneath Unit 734 turned into a grid of light. The drone lifted off, struggling against an invisible force field.

Gerald stood up. He looked at the drone with mild disinterest.

"Your containment field is using an outdated encryption standard," Gerald said. "Section 8, Paragraph 3 of the Municipal Data Ordinance. It's obsolete."

He twisted the dial on his multimeter.

"I'm going to measure the voltage on your power source," Gerald said. "If it's grounded, you can't shock me. If it's not, I'm going to ground it myself."

Unit 734 fired a pulse of energy.

The pulse hit Gerald's chest, and he didn't flinch. The energy dissipated into the metal floor. Gerald's feet were planted on the conductive grating. He had bridged the circuit.

Unit 734's systems dimmed. The drone fell to the ground, sparking.

Gerald stepped over the disabled drone. He walked to the center of the room.

There was a console there. Simple. Analog. A keyboard and a screen.

Gerald sat down, and he typed.

`> QUERY: ARCHITECT LOCATION` `> RESULT: NULL`

He frowned.

`> QUERY: ARCHITECT IP` `> RESULT: 127.0.0.1`

Gerald stopped.

Loopback address.

The Architect wasn't outside the System. The Architect was the System.

Or a part of it.

Gerald looked at the blueprint in his mind. *The server room is under the foundation.*

He looked at the console.

`> ACCESS: ROOT` `> PASSWORD REQUIRED`

Gerald didn't have the password. He had something better.

He had a form.

Form 11-B. Unlicensed Biological Entity.

He couldn't fill it out here. Not physically. But the System recognized the *intent* of a form. It recognized the *structure* of a citation.

Gerald typed.

`> ISSUE CITATION: SERVER 001` `> VIOLATION: UNAUTHORIZED PROCESSING LOAD` `> PENALTY: SHUTDOWN`

The screen flashed red.

`> ERROR: TARGET NOT A CORE`

Gerald gritted his teeth.

"Of course," he muttered. "You're not a dungeon. You're the infrastructure."

He paused. He thought about building codes.

Infrastructure had to meet safety standards, and he typed again.

`> ISSUE CITATION: SERVER 001` `> VIOLATION: NON-COMPLIANT FIRE SUPPRESSION` `> REFERENCE: NFPA 92`

The screen blinked.

`> VALID VIOLATION DETECTED.` `> CALCULATING PENALTY...`

The room shook.

Alarms blared. Red lights spun.

Unit 734, still on the floor, twitched. Its optical lens zoomed in on Gerald.

"What are you doing?" the drone rasped.

"Fixing the ventilation," Gerald said. "The heat output is exceeding the cooling capacity. This is a life safety violation."

He hit Enter.

`> EXECUTE: COOLING SYSTEM OVERLOAD`

The temperature in the server room dropped.

Then it rose. Then it fluctuated wildly. The cooling fans screamed.

The data streams slowed.

On the holographic map, the red dot labeled *THE ARCHITECT* flickered.

It didn't disappear.

It changed color.

From red to yellow.

**WARNING: ARCHITECT INTEGRITY COMPROMISED.**

Gerald stood up. He felt a pull in his chest. His Compliance Rating, which had been zero, spiked.

**COMPLIANCE RATING: 15.**

He had earned points. But it wasn't enough.

He needed to go deeper, and he looked at the console. Below the keyboard was a manual override lever. It was rusty. Stuck.

Gerald grabbed it.

He pulled.

It didn't move.

He pulled harder. His knuckles turned white.

Unit 734 managed to lift its head.

"Stop," the drone said. "You are causing a cascade failure. The entire System will crash."

Gerald looked at the drone.

"Is that a threat?"

"It is a warning."

Gerald looked at the console.

"If the System crashes, everyone dies. Adventurers. Monsters. Cores."

He paused, and he thought about the rat in his dungeon. The three mold spores. The single confused rat.

He thought about the neighbors who wanted to kill him.

He thought about the Inspector General.

He let go of the lever.

"You're right," Gerald said. "I can't shut it down. Not yet."

He stepped back from the console.

Unit 734 rose slowly. Its movements were jerky.

"Then why did you do it?" the drone asked.

Gerald picked up his imaginary clipboard.

"To get your attention," he said. "And to leave a mark."

He pointed to the screen.

The yellow dot on the map was pulsing. A signal was broadcasting.

Not to the System.

To the outside.

Gerald had triggered a beacon.

Unit 734 scanned the signal.

`> SIGNAL TYPE: SOS` `> SOURCE: INTERNAL` `> RECIPIENT: UNKNOWN`

"You're broadcasting a distress call," Unit 734 said. "To whom?"

Gerald straightened his tie.

"To the people who built this place," he said. "If the System is a building, someone has to be responsible for it. Someone has to sign the plans."

He walked toward the exit.

"Wait," Unit 734 said. "You cannot leave. The door is locked."

Gerald stopped, and he looked at the door. It was a solid slab of titanium.

"I didn't say I was leaving through the door," Gerald said.

He looked at the wall behind him.

It was a server rack. Full of hard drives.

Gerald walked to it, and he pulled a fire extinguisher from the wall.

It wasn't a System item.

It was real. Physical.

Heavy, and he aimed it at the server rack.

"You're violating fire code," Gerald said to the drone.

Unit 734 moved to stop him.

It was too late.

Gerald pulled the pin, and he sprayed.

White foam covered the hard drives. The servers sparked. The data streams froze.

The holographic map vanished, and the lights went out.

Total darkness.

Gerald stood in the dark.

He could hear Unit 734 rebooting.

He could hear the hum of the backup generators, and he could hear a new sound.

A click.

Not from the drone.

From the wall.

A seam opened in the darkness.

A voice spoke.

It wasn't synthesized. It wasn't robotic.

It was old. Female. Tired.

"Mr. Hutchins," the voice said. "You have a lot of nerve."

Gerald turned.

A figure stepped out of the shadows, and she was wearing a lab coat. Her hair was gray. Her eyes were sharp.

She held a tablet.

"I am Dr. Aris Thorne," she said. "And you just violated the Prime Directive."

Gerald looked at her, and he looked at the tablet.

On the screen was a document.

*PROJECT AEGIS: PHASE 1.*

*SUBJECT: HUMAN CORE INTEGRATION.*

*STATUS: ACTIVE.*

Gerald felt a chill that had nothing to do with the cold server room.

"You're not the Architect," Gerald said.

"I am the Project Manager," Dr. Thorne said. "And you are a test case."

She raised the tablet.

A laser grid snapped into existence around Gerald.

"You have three minutes to explain why I shouldn't format you," she said.

Gerald looked at the grid, and he looked at Dr. Thorne.

He looked at the disabled drone.

He reached into his pocket.

He pulled out a pen.

"First," Gerald said. "Your laser grid is not rated for human tissue. It's rated for metal. That's a Class 4 violation."

Dr. Thorne smiled.

It was not a nice smile.

"Try me."

Gerald clicked his pen.

"Second," he said. "You're in a restricted zone. I'm going to need to see your permit."

Dr. Thorne's smile faded.

"You don't have jurisdiction," she said.

Gerald looked at the laser grid, which was humming.

It was heating up.

Gerald looked at the fire extinguisher in his hand.

He looked at the sprinkler head above him.

He looked at Dr. Thorne.

"Actually," Gerald said. "I do."

He pointed at the ceiling.

**SYSTEM NOTIFICATION: FIRE DETECTED.** **ACTIVATING SPRINKLER SYSTEM.**

Water rained down.

The laser grid sizzled.

Dr. Thorne stepped back.

Gerald walked through the water, and he walked toward her.

The water shorted out her tablet.

The lights flickered.

Dr. Thorne looked at Gerald.

For the first time, she looked unsure.

"You're ruining everything," she said.

"No," Gerald said. "I'm just inspecting it."

He reached out, and he grabbed her tablet.

He smashed it against the floor.

The screen cracked.

Data leaked out.

Not digital data.

Physical paper.

Pages.

Hundreds of pages.

Scattered across the wet floor.

Gerald looked at one page, which was a list of names.

**TEST SUBJECTS.**

**1. GERALD HUTCHINS - FAILED.** **2. MARY JONES - FAILED.** **3. ARTHUR PENDRAGON - ACTIVE.**

Gerald stopped.

Arthur Pendragon, and the hero who had cleared the first raid.

The one who had beaten Gerald's dungeon.

Gerald looked up at Dr. Thorne.

"She's not a Core," Gerald said.

Dr. Thorne stared at him.

"She is the Prototype," she said. "And you are the Bug."

Gerald looked at the wet paper, and he looked at the dark corner of the room.

A shadow moved.

It wasn't Unit 734.

It wasn't Dr. Thorne.

It was something else.

Something tall.

Something wearing armor.

Gerald's heart stopped.

The figure stepped into the light, which was a knight.

But not a System knight.

Its eyes were cameras.

Its sword was a cable.

Dr. Thorne looked at the knight.

"Guardian," she said. "Terminate the anomaly."

The knight raised its sword.

Gerald looked at the sprinklers, and he looked at the puddle of water.

He looked at the knight.

He smiled.

It was a thin, dry smile.

"You're going to need a permit for that," Gerald said.

The knight struck, and the screen went black.

**SYSTEM ERROR.** **CONNECTION LOST.**
