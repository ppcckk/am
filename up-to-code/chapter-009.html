Chapter 9

The wall was moving.

Gerald watched it from his spawn room. A slab of packed earth, six feet tall, creeping forward through the boundary tunnel like a tongue. The rival's dungeon was growing. Physically growing. Pushing through the shared substrate that separated his territory from the adjacent one. The gap where the earth had thinned showed a sliver of carved stone beyond. Dark stone. Worked stone. The kind of stone that took years to cut.

He checked the timestamp. Thirty-nine hours remained.

The wall crept another inch.

Gerald looked at his mana counter. Zero. He needed four mana to build the remaining signs. He'd spent the last hour searching for ways to generate mana without killing anything. The System offered three sources: kills, exploration, and compliance milestones. He had no kills. No exploration. And the compliance milestones were locked behind the very signs he couldn't afford to build.

Circular. Classic. The kind of problem that got buildings condemned because the owner couldn't afford the inspection fee to prove they were compliant enough to get a loan to pay the inspection fee.

He opened the System interface and started reading. Not the compliance categories this time. The other menus. The ones he'd ignored because they weren't relevant to what he was doing.

Permits.

There was a menu called Permits. It had been there since he'd first become a Core. He'd never opened it. He'd been too busy. He'd been too busy the way he'd always been too busy, which was to say he'd been busy doing the work in front of him instead of reading the paperwork that might have made the work easier.

He opened it.

BUILDING PERMIT APPLICATION: A Core may apply for a temporary resource grant to complete a compliance category. Grant amount: proportional to the scope of the declared work. The Core must complete the declared work within the permit window or face immediate Category Termination for the cited category.

Gerald read it twice. Then he read it a third time, slower.

A permit. You apply, you get resources, you complete the work, the permit closes. If you don't complete the work, the category you applied for is terminated. Which meant the audit would find a zero in that category. Which meant death.

But if you did complete the work, you got the resources for free.

He'd been doing this his whole career. People applied for permits. He approved them. He inspected the work. If it passed, they got their certificate of occupancy. If it didn't, they got a violation notice and a fine and a hearing date. The permit system was the oldest trick in municipal governance. It existed because nobody could pay for compliance upfront. The system assumed you'd do the work if you had the materials. It assumed good faith. It assumed that the threat of termination was enough to make people honest.

The System had built a permit system into the Core interface. Of course it had. The System was a bureaucracy. Gerald had always known that. He'd just never thought to use it.

He filled out the application. Declared work: four signage placards. Category: Signage. Scope: four units.

The System processed it.

PERMIT APPROVED. Temporary mana grant: 12. Permit window: 14 hours. Declared work: 4 signage placards. Failure to complete results in Signage Category Termination.

Twelve mana. He needed four. He had eight to spare.

Gerald built the signs. One by one. NO ENTRY at the tunnel entrance. FIRE EXTINGUISHER at the spawn room wall. ELECTRICAL HAZARD at the single exposed conduit running along the ceiling. STRUCTURAL LOAD LIMIT at the ceiling itself, where the earth pressed down with no visible support.

COMPLIANCE UPDATE: Signage compliance rating: 78 percent.

Seventy-eight. Above twenty. He wasn't dead. Not yet.

He had eight mana left. He looked at the wall. It had moved another foot. The rival's dungeon was close now. Close enough that Gerald could see the rival's interior through the gap where the earth had thinned.

It was a big dungeon. Multiple chambers. Stone floors. Carved pillars. Water features. The rival had been running for years. Gerald could tell from the depth of the excavation, the number of rooms, the complexity of the layout. A mature Core. A dangerous one.

He looked at it the way he looked at a building. The way he looked at everything. The way he'd looked at everything for thirty years, since the first time a contractor had told him a load-bearing wall was decorative and he'd put his hand on it and felt the whole floor above it flex.

The rival's dungeon had problems.

The pillars were load-bearing, but the spacing was wrong. Too wide. The ceiling between them sagged visibly, a slow curve that would accelerate over time until it failed. The water feature ran through a channel with no waterproofing membrane. The electrical conduit was bare, no insulation, running directly through the water channel at a crossing point that should have been elevated or rerouted. The fire extinguisher placard was present but the extinguisher itself was missing, mounted on a bracket with nothing on it. The egress path was blocked by a decorative archway that narrowed the passage below minimum width.

Gerald counted the violations. He counted them the way he counted them in his old life. Methodically. One by one. Writing them down in the mental clipboard he'd carried for thirty years.

Eleven violations. Structural: four. Electrical: three. Fire safety: two. Egress: one. Signage: one.

He could cite the rival.

The problem was the physical presence requirement. Citations had to be served in person.
