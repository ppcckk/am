Chapter 100: Class C Liability

# Chapter 100

The rubble did not fall; it floated.

Gerald Hutchins stood in the gap where the wall had been, his measuring tape dangling uselessly at his side. The debris of the partition hung in a suspended cloud of gray dust and broken stone, defying gravity for exactly four seconds before slamming onto the floor of the adjacent chamber with a sound like a collapsing library shelf.

Silence followed. Then, the smell hit him. Sulfur, stale beer, and the distinct, coppery tang of blood that had dried on stone for longer than Gerald liked to think about.

He stepped forward. His boot crunched on the debris. He did not look at the monster that had been occupying the space. He looked at the floor.

"Uneven substrate," Gerald muttered, pulling a pen from his shirt pocket. "Three-inch variance in elevation across a twelve-foot span. Trip hazard. Class C violation."

He wrote it on the clipboard. He did not look up.

From the shadows emerged a figure. It was humanoid, clad in armor made of chitinous plates and rusted chainmail.

Its face was a mask of tusk and fury, but its eyes widened as they locked onto Gerald, which was Kessick. Not the dungeon core itself—that was a metaphysical presence—but the avatar, the physical manifestation of the entity’s will, forced into existence by the System’s rules when an Inspector crossed a threshold.

Kessick raised a jagged axe.

"Stop," Gerald said.

His voice was flat, devoid of adrenaline. "You are standing on a non-compliant surface.

Move to the left.

There is a stable patch of stone near the eastern wall."

Kessick roared. It was a guttural, wet sound, vibrating with magical intent. The axe swung down.

Gerald did not dodge. He did not have the stats to dodge. He had a Compliance Rating of 84 and a passive resistance to blunt trauma derived from his Structural Code compliance. He raised his clipboard.

The axe struck the clipboard, which did not break. The System interpreted the clipboard not as a tool, but as an Official Document of Authority.

The impact was absorbed by the *Administrative Shield* granted to Inspectors when citing violations.

The force traveled up the axe handle and into Kessick’s arms.

The avatar grunted, stepping back, its boots sliding on the uneven floor Gerald had just noted.

Kessick stumbled. Gerald seized the moment.

"You are operating without a Foundation Permit," Gerald said. He pointed the pen at the creature. "According to Municipal Statute 44-B, a structure lacking a Foundation Permit is not a building.

It is an obstruction. It is refuse."

Kessick snarled, raising the axe again. "I am a Lord of the Deep! I have slain a hundred heroes!"

"You have no permit," Gerald repeated. "Therefore, you have no rights. Therefore, I am not fighting you. I am removing trash."

Gerald opened his menu. He navigated to the *Enforcement* tab. It was grayed out, locked behind a new mechanic the Inspector had unlocked: *Immediate Eviction Authority*.

He selected it.

A prompt appeared: *WARNING: Eviction requires physical removal of the Core’s anchor. If the Core resists, the Inspector must manually dismantle the anchor. Failure results in permanent loss of Core status.*

Gerald ignored the warning. He looked at Kessick. The avatar was not the anchor. The anchor was deeper.

Gerald walked past the stunned avatar.

Kessick lunged, but Gerald was already in motion. He moved with the efficient, jerky gait of a man who had walked ten thousand miles of municipal hallways. He stepped over the debris, ignoring the chitinous plates scraping against his shoulder.

He walked toward the center of the room, where a pulsating, black crystal sat embedded in the floor.

The Core’s Anchor.

Kessick screamed, a sound of genuine fear. "Do not touch it! It is sacred!"

"It is unpermitted," Gerald said.

He reached for the crystal.

A shockwave blasted him backward. Gerald slammed into a stalagmite.

Pain exploded in his ribs.

His HP dropped from 100 to 40. He gasped, tasting iron.

He pulled himself up. His vision swam.

The dungeon’s lighting flickered, responding to the Core’s distress.

Shadows lengthened, forming into grasping hands, and the dungeon itself was fighting back.

Gerald looked at the hands. He looked at the crystal.

He realized he could not pull the crystal out. It was fused to the bedrock. And he did not have the strength to break it.

He needed leverage. He needed a mechanic.

He opened his System log. He scrolled past the combat stats. He scrolled past the inventory. He found the *Zoning* tab.

*Zoning* contained a sub-option: *Reclassify Surface Type*.

Gerald’s eyes narrowed. He had never used it. It was meant for minor adjustments, like changing a dirt floor to a gravel path for drainage purposes. It required a specific material input and a lot of mana.

He looked at the crystal. It was obsidian. Black glass.

He looked at his inventory. He had three pounds of limestone dust from his own dungeon’s ventilation shafts.

*Query: Can limestone dust neutralize obsidian under high pressure?*

*System Response: No. Chemical incompatibility.*

Gerald frowned. He needed a different angle.

He looked at Kessick. The avatar was charging, axe raised, eyes burning with violet magic.

Gerald looked at the ceiling.

The ceiling of Kessick’s chamber was low. Too low. The fire code required a minimum clearance of eight feet for public assembly spaces. Kessick’s room had a clearance of six feet, three inches.

Gerald opened his menu again. He went to *Structural Modifications*.

He selected *Ceiling Lowering*.

*Cost: 500 Mana. Time: Instantaneous.*

He didn’t have 500 Mana. He had 120.

He checked the cost again. It was dynamic. It scaled with the difficulty of the modification. Lowering a stone ceiling in a hostile dungeon was high-difficulty.

But he didn’t need to lower the whole ceiling. He just needed to lower the part above Kessick.

He targeted the space directly above the charging avatar.

*Cost: 80 Mana.*

Gerald activated it.

The ceiling groaned. Dust rained down. A massive slab of rock, previously held by ancient magic and structural integrity, shifted. It dropped two feet.

It did not hit Kessick. It hit the space in front of him, creating a new, lower ceiling segment that acted as a barrier.

Kessick crashed into the new stone overhang. The impact shattered the avatar’s helmet. The creature staggered, blood spraying from its nose.

Gerald didn’t celebrate. He walked toward the crystal again.

He pulled out his measuring tape. He measured the gap between the crystal and the newly lowered ceiling.

"Insufficient clearance for manual extraction," he said. "Requires mechanical assistance."

He looked at the avatar. Kessick was getting up, groaning, reaching for its axe.

Gerald opened the *Citation* interface, and he drafted a new Citation.

*Violation: Failure to Maintain Adequate Headroom.*

*Penalty: Temporary Reduction in Height.*

He applied it to Kessick.

The effect was instantaneous and cruel. The avatar’s body compressed. Its legs shortened.

Its torso flattened. Kessick screamed as its bones ground together, its height dropping from seven feet to three.

The axe slipped from its grasp, clattering to the floor.

The System’s Compliance debuffs were brutal. They were not just numbers; they were physical realities.

Gerald looked at the shrunken, wheezing creature. It was no longer a threat. It was a nuisance.

He turned back to the crystal, and he had one move left. He needed to move the crystal. He couldn’t lift it. But he could move the floor it sat on.

He went to *Zoning* again.

*Reclassify Floor Type: From Solid Rock to Unstable Gravel.*

*Cost: 100 Mana.*

He had 40 Mana left, and he looked at his HP. 40%.

He looked at the crystal.

If he couldn’t pay the Mana cost, he could pay it in Health, and the System allowed for *Biometric Currency Exchange* for desperate situations. It was a loophole the Inspector had hinted at, a backdoor for Inspectors who were willing to bleed for compliance.

Gerald selected *Pay with HP*.

*Warning: This transaction will reduce Maximum HP by 10% permanently. Proceed?*

Gerald hesitated. Permanent stat loss. It was bad. It was against the protocol of efficiency.

But the Inspector had said: *The System does not give you the right of appeal if you fail to evict.*

Gerald confirmed.

Pain lanced through his chest. It felt like a heart attack. He doubled over, gasping. His vision went dark for a second.

When it returned, the floor beneath the crystal had turned to loose gravel.

The crystal sank.

Gerald scrambled forward. He kicked the gravel aside. He grabbed the black crystal. It was cold, vibrating with a frequency that made his teeth ache.

He pulled. It moved. Inch by inch.

Kessick, now the size of a child, crawled toward him, clawing at his ankles.

Gerald kicked the creature away. "Stay back. You are trespassing on a construction site."

He pulled the crystal free.

The dungeon shook. The walls cracked. The lighting died, replaced by emergency red glow from the System’s safety features.

*Eviction Successful,* the System message flashed in gold text. *Core Kessick has been dissolved. Loot distributed to Inspector.*

Gerald collapsed. He lay on the gravel, the crystal in his hand, his breath coming in ragged hitches.

He waited for the loot screen. He expected gold. He expected items.

Instead, the screen showed a notification.

*New Violation Detected.*

*Violation: Unauthorized Demolition of Adjacent Property.*

*Source: Inspector Gerald Hutchins.*

*Status: Under Review.*

Gerald stared. He had evicted a squatter. He had cited them. He had won.

And the System was now citing *him*.

A new window popped up.

*Hearing Scheduled: Immediate.*

*Opponent: The Inspector General.*

Gerald sat up. His heart hammered against his ribs, weak and fast.

The air in the room changed. The dust settled. The red lights stopped blinking and turned a sterile, bright white.

A figure stepped out of the shadows. It was not an avatar. It was not a monster.

It was the Inspector General, and he was tall, dressed in a suit that seemed woven from pure light. His face was blank, a smooth oval of porcelain, and he held a clipboard that was larger than Gerald’s entire body.

"Hutchins," the Inspector General said. His voice did not come from a mouth; it came from everywhere. "You have completed the eviction."

"I did," Gerald said. He stood up, trembling. "Kessick was a squatter.

No Foundation Permit.

I followed protocol."

"You followed protocol," the Inspector agreed. "But you missed subsection 9, paragraph C of the Inter-Core Zoning Act."

Gerald frowned. "There is no such paragraph. I read the entire act. Twice."

"There was no such paragraph," the Inspector said. "Until you performed the eviction. Your action created new law."

The Inspector raised his clipboard.

"By dissolving Kessick, you have left a void in the Dungeon Grid. A void attracts instability. Instability attracts predators. You have created a hazard."

Gerald looked at the space where Kessick’s dungeon had been. The walls were gone. Beyond them lay a swirling, chaotic mist of raw mana; it was a tear in the fabric of the System.

"I fixed the ventilation," Gerald said.

"You opened a wound," the Inspector corrected. "And now, you are responsible for closing it."

The Inspector pointed at the mist.

"You have twelve hours to stabilize the breach.

If you fail, the collapse will trigger a Cascade Failure. It will destroy your dungeon. It will destroy the Library above it.

And it will revoke your Core Status permanently."

Gerald looked at the mist. He looked at his HP bar. 30%. He looked at his Mana bar. 0%.

He had no resources. He had no allies. He had a rat in his backpack and a clipboard.

"How?" Gerald asked.

The Inspector smiled. It was a terrible, thin smile.

"You are an Inspector, Hutchins. You know how to fix things. But this is not a repair. This is a renovation."

The Inspector handed him a new document, which was a Blueprint.

It was not for a dungeon. It was for a *Bridge*.

"To stabilize the void, you must build a structure that connects your dungeon to the void's center. You must build a bridge over an abyss that does not exist."

Gerald looked at the blueprint. It was complex. It required materials he did not have. It required engineering principles he barely understood.

And it required a Foundation Permit.

"You cannot build without a permit," Gerald said.

The Inspector shook his head.

"That is the catch. You cannot get a permit because the land is unregistered. You cannot register the land because it is not real. You are trapped in a bureaucratic paradox."

Gerald stared at the blueprint. He stared at the mist.

If he did nothing, he died.

If he tried to build without a permit, he violated code.

He looked at the rat in his backpack. The rat squeaked.

Gerald looked at the Inspector.

"I need to consult with my attorney," Gerald said.

The Inspector vanished.

Gerald was alone.

The mist crept toward him. Tendrils of gray fog reached out, tasting the air.

Gerald looked at the blueprint. He looked at his empty Mana bar.

He opened his inventory, and he selected *Limestone Dust*.

He selected *Unstable Gravel*.

He looked at the *Reclassify* tab.

He had a new idea. A dangerous, stupid, illegal idea.

He could not build a bridge.

But he could cite the mist.

If the mist was a structure, it needed to comply.

If it did not comply, he could cite it.

If he cited it, he could force it to stabilize, and he walked toward the edge of the void. He raised his clipboard.

"Violation," Gerald said. "Failure to maintain boundary integrity."

The mist hesitated.

Gerald held his breath.

The mist condensed. It formed a wall.

But then, a second figure stepped out of the mist, which was not Kessick.

It was larger. It was wearing armor made of bone, and on its chest, glowing in purple runes, was a symbol Gerald recognized.

The symbol of the Adventurer’s Guild.

Not a hero. A contractor.

"Mr. Hutchins," the figure said, its voice smooth and oily. "The Guild is very interested in your new construction project."

Gerald lowered his clipboard.

"Who are you?"

"I am your new partner," the figure said. "And we need to talk about your liability insurance."
