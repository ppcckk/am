Chapter 205: Paperwork Explodes

# Chapter 205

Gerald's pen hovered over the first line of the citation form.

The crowd surged. Adventurers in mismatched armor, heroes with glowing swords, and monsters bound by blood-oaths packed the void, their collective noise a physical pressure against Gerald's eardrums. The woman with the iron crown stood ten feet away, her code-sword humming with unstable data.

"Move," Gerald said.

The voice was flat. Dry. It carried the specific, grating tone of a man who had waited in line at the Department of Motor Vehicles for six hours and lost.

The woman stepped forward. The ground beneath her cracked.

"You have no jurisdiction here, Inspector.

This is a chaotic zone. The System does not recognize municipal authority in the Unbounded Void."

Gerald did not look at her. He looked at the nearest adventurer, a massive brute swinging a hammer that trailed black smoke.

Gerald clicked his pen. The sound was sharp, like a bone snapping.

"Class A Violation," Gerald said.

"Improper storage of hazardous materials. You are carrying a weapon that emits necrotic radiation within ten feet of a population center.

No shielding.

No signage. No permit."

The brute blinked. "What?"

"I am citing you," Gerald said. He tapped the air. A translucent form materialized, glowing with harsh amber light. "Pay the fine, or cease your activity immediately."

The brute laughed. It was a wet, ugly sound. He swung the hammer.

The hammer stopped three inches from Gerald's nose.

Not because of a shield. Not because of magic.

Because the System recognized the citation.

The hammer didn't hit Gerald. It hit an invisible wall of regulatory force.

The brute's arms snapped back with a sickening crunch.

The necrotic energy sizzled, then died, smothered by a sudden drop in local mana density.

The air turned stale. The adventurer gasped, clutching his broken arms, staring at the floating form that burned brighter than the void.

One down.

Gerald turned to the woman with the iron crown. "Your platform."

She frowned. "What about my platform?"

"Load-bearing failure," Gerald said. He pointed his pen at the bone-and-steel structure supporting her feet. "You are standing on a composite material that exceeds its tensile limit by forty percent.

The bone is calcified beyond structural integrity. The steel is corroded.

You are exceeding the maximum occupancy load for a temporary elevation platform."

The woman's eyes narrowed. "This is a throne. It is not a platform."

"It is a platform," Gerald said. "And you are violating Code 14-C: Structural Safety of Temporary Edifices."

He clicked again.

The citation form appeared. The System processed it.

The platform groaned.

A hairline fracture appeared in the central steel strut. Then another. The bone base shifted, grinding against the void-grid.

The woman's expression changed from arrogance to alarm.

She looked down.

"You can't just—"

"The structural load is unsupported," Gerald said. "Evacuate the area."

The platform collapsed.

It didn't fall fast. It buckled slowly, deliberately, as if the laws of physics had been amended mid-fall. The woman stumbled, her code-sword clattering to the ground. She hit the grid hard, rolling to her knees.

The crowd froze.

Silence rushed back in, heavier than the noise had been.

Gerald straightened his tie.

He adjusted his clipboard. He looked at the hundreds of faces staring at him. Heroes.

Villains. Monsters.

All of them frozen, waiting for the next move.

"The next violation," Gerald said.

A hero stepped forward.

A young man with a shield that glowed blue. "Wait.

You can't just cite everyone. We're here to fight the Core. We're here to win."

"You are here to violate the perimeter," Gerald said. "This is a private easement.

You are trespassing.

There is no permit for a hostile incursion of this magnitude. You are also violating noise ordinances. The sonic resonance of your weapons exceeds the decibel limit for a residential zone."

The hero lowered his shield. "Residential? This is a dungeon."

"It was a library basement," Gerald said. "It is still zoned as such. Until the zoning board approves a change of use—which I have not filed, because you do not have the paperwork—you are in a residential zone."

The hero looked at the woman on the floor. She was struggling to stand, her crown askew.

"Who are you?" the hero asked.

"Gerald Hutchins," Gerald said. "Inspector."

The woman on the floor looked up. Her eyes were cold. "You think this stops us? You think citations stop war?"

"I think they slow you down," Gerald said.

"And in a war, speed is currency. You just lost your footing.

You lost your weapon.

You lost your momentum."

He turned to the crowd. "I will cite every single one of you.

One by one.

I will check your armor for rust.

I will check your spell components for expiration dates. I will check your party composition for illegal unions.

I will find a violation in every single one of you."

A murmur rippled through the crowd. Not of anger. Of doubt.

Adventurers looked at their gear. One checked his sword for nicks. Another wiped sweat from his brow, worried he had contaminated his mana crystals.

The woman with the iron crown stood up. She picked up her sword. The code-sword flickered, unstable.

"You are bluffing," she said. "The System does not audit individuals in combat. It audits Cores."

"The System audits everything with a footprint," Gerald said. "And you have footprints."

He clicked his pen one more time.

A new form appeared. This one was different. It was red.

**HEARING REQUEST: INDIVIDUAL COMPLIANCE REVIEW.** **TARGET: THE MOB.** **SUBJECT: AGGREGATE LIABILITY FOR UNLICENSED HEROIC ACTIVITY.**

The air shimmered. The void around the crowd twisted. Not into monsters. Not into traps.

Into walls.

Gray, concrete walls rose from the floor, enclosing the adventurers. They were not solid.

They were translucent, like glass, but they blocked movement.

They blocked magic. They were *containment barriers*.

The System was enforcing the citation. It was treating the mob not as combatants, but as unpermitted construction workers. And it was shutting down the site.

The woman's eyes widened. "What is this?"

"Stop-Work Order," Gerald said. "The site is unsafe. All operations must cease until a licensed inspector approves the structural integrity of the engagement."

The adventurer with the blue shield tried to punch the wall.

His fist passed through. The wall was intangible to physical force, but it anchored them in place.

They could not move forward.

They could not move back.

They were pinned.

Gerald walked forward. His footsteps echoed on the grid.

He walked past the frozen adventurers. He walked past the woman, who was glaring at him with hatred so pure it could burn.

He walked toward the center of the void, where the Architect had been.

The space was empty. But there was something there. A single, floating icon.

It was a stamp.

Gerald reached out. His fingers brushed the icon.

It burned.

Not with heat. With data.

Gerald flinched, pulling his hand back. His fingertips were blackened, charred by raw information.

The stamp floated toward him. It rotated.

**APPROVAL GRANTED.** **INSPECTOR STATUS: UPGRADED.**

A notification pinged in his mind. It was loud. Deafening.

**NEW MECHANIC UNLOCKED: ADMINISTRATIVE PREEMPTIVE STRIKE.**

Gerald stared at the text.

He looked at the crowd. They were still pinned. The walls were tightening.

The woman with the iron crown screamed. "Break the cage! Break it!"

Her heroes tried. They failed. The System held them.

Gerald looked at his hand. The burns were fading. The data was settling into his bones.

He understood what had happened.

The Hearing wasn't over.

The Hearing had just begun.

And the System was on his side.

But why?

Gerald turned back to the woman. "Why did you let me win?"

She didn't answer. She was struggling against the walls. The iron of her crown was bending, warping under the pressure of the containment field.

"Answer me," Gerald said.

The void shuddered.

A sound came from above. Not a roar. Not a scream.

A laugh.

It was deep. Resonant. It vibrated in Gerald's chest.

The clouds of the void parted.

A face appeared in the sky. Huge. Made of stars and static.

The Inspector General.

The face looked down at Gerald. At the pinned mob. At the broken woman.

"Interesting," the voice boomed.

The walls around the adventurers vanished.

The crowd gasped, stumbling forward. The woman scrambled to her feet, raising her sword. The heroes raised their weapons. The monsters snarled.

The balance of power had shifted. The cage was gone. The momentum was gone. But the weapons were back.

Gerald looked up.

"You cited a god," the Inspector General said. "You cited the System."

Gerald straightened his tie. "I cited a violation."

"And you won."

"I followed the code."

"The code is not meant to stop war," the Inspector General said. "It is meant to maintain order. You have introduced chaos into the regulatory framework."

Gerald felt a chill. Not fear. Recognition.

He had broken something.

"I have not broken it," Gerald said. "I have used it."

"You have created a precedent," the Inspector General said. "If a Core can cite a hero, then a hero can cite a Core. If a Core can cite a god, then a god can be fined."

The stars in the Inspector General's face shifted. They formed a frown.

"However," the voice continued, "the precedent is valid. The citation was legally sound. The structural deficiency was real."

Gerald relaxed. Just slightly.

"But," the Inspector General said, "there is a cost."

The void darkened.

The grid beneath Gerald's feet turned red.

**PENALTY ASSESSED.** **SOURCE: UNAUTHORIZED MODIFICATION OF SYSTEM LOGIC.** **COST: ONE CORE INTEGRITY POINT.**

Gerald felt a tear in his soul.

It was a physical sensation, like a rib being pulled out.

His Compliance Rating, which had been rising, dropped.

**CURRENT RATING: 89/100.**

He had lost points. He had paid a cost.

But he was still standing.

The woman with the iron crown looked at the sky. Then she looked at Gerald. Her smile was gone. Her eyes were wide.

"He lost points," she whispered. "The System punished him."

The heroes looked at Gerald. They saw the red grid. They saw the drop in his aura.

"He is vulnerable," one of them said.

"Yes," the Inspector General said. "He is vulnerable."

The woman raised her sword. "Then why wait?"

She charged.

Not at Gerald.

At the Inspector General.

She lunged upward, her code-sword piercing the veil between the void and the face in the sky.

It was not an attack on Gerald.

It was an attack on the Judge.

Gerald watched the sword hit the face.

The face did not bleed.

It cracked.

A fissure opened in the starry skin. Black liquid poured out.

The Inspector General screamed.

The sound shattered the void.

The grid beneath Gerald's feet dissolved.

He fell.

The woman fell with him.

The crowd fell with him.

Everything fell.

Gerald flailed, grabbing for the clipboard. It was gone.

He hit the ground.

Not the void-grid.

Concrete.

He was back in the library sub-basement.

He was on his knees.

The air smelled of mildew and old paper.

The water leak was dripping.

*Drip.*

*Drip.*

*Drip.*

Gerald looked up.

The woman was gone.

The mob was gone.

The Inspector General was gone.

Only Gerald remained.

And on the wall in front of him, scratched into the concrete with what looked like claws, were words.

**YOU HAVE ONE HOUR TO APPEAL THE DEMOLITION ORDER.**

Gerald looked at his hands.

They were shaking.

He reached for his pocket.

The pen was there.

The clipboard was gone.

But in his hand, a new form appeared.

**DEMOLITION ORDER: ASHFORD MUNICIPAL LIBRARY SUB-BASEMENT.** **AUTHORITY: INSPECTOR GENERAL.** **REASON: UNRELIABLE STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY.**

Gerald stared at the order.

The Inspector General had not just fined him.

He had condemned the dungeon.

And the clock was ticking.

One hour.

To appeal.

To win.

Or to lose everything.

Gerald looked at the rat.

The rat was still there. It was eating a piece of drywall.

Gerald looked at the water leak.

It was worse than before. The puddle was wider. The ceiling was sagging.

He looked at the walls.

They were cracking.

Not from magic.

From stress.

The System was tearing his dungeon apart.

Gerald stood up.

He straightened his tie.

He looked at the clock on the wall. It was a real clock. An analog clock, hanging on a nail.

The hands were moving.

Fast.

Too fast.

Time was not moving at normal speed.

It was moving at *citation* speed.

Gerald realized the loophole.

The Inspector General had said *one hour*.

But he had not said *one real hour*.

He had said *one hour of administrative processing*.

Gerald smiled.

It was a thin, cold smile.

He took out his pen.

He did not write an appeal.

He wrote a counter-citation.

He wrote a citation for the Inspector General.

For *delaying judgment*.

For *procedural misconduct*.

For *conflict of interest*.

He clicked the pen.

The form appeared.

It did not glow amber.

It glowed black.

**CITATION ISSUED.** **TARGET: INSPECTOR GENERAL.** **VIOLATION: ABUSE OF AUTHORITY.**

The clock stopped.

The cracking walls stopped.

The void returned.

But this time, Gerald was not alone.

Standing in the center of the void, waiting for him, was the Architect.

The Architect was not gone.

The Architect was smiling.

"You played the game," the Architect said. "But you forgot who made the rules."

Gerald raised his pen.

"I know," Gerald said. "That is why I am winning."

The Architect's smile vanished.

The void exploded again.

But this time, it was not people.

It was paperwork.
