Chapter 195: False Counsel

# Chapter 195

The crack in Gerald's left hand widened. A hairline fracture, white and dry, running from the wrist bone to the base of the thumb. It did not bleed. It leaked light.

Gold dust drifted from the fissure, settling on the marble floor of the hearing room. The Inspector General watched.

His many eyes blinked in asynchronous sequence, a slow, wet clicking sound that echoed against the vaulted ceiling.

He did not move his finger. The execution light had faded, but the threat remained suspended in the air like smoke.

Gerald stared at his hand. He tried to flex his fingers. The tendons resisted. The Core Integrity warning pulsed in his peripheral vision, a rhythmic red strobe.

*40%. Cause: Existential Contradiction.*

The System did not lie, which did not understand metaphor. It understood structure.

And Gerald had just claimed to be represented by counsel.

He was not. He had no lawyer. He had no team.

He had only a clipboard and a pen that had run out of ink three chapters ago.

The contradiction was a structural load bearing a lie. The load failed. The core cracked.

Gerald looked up.

The Inspector General waited, and he was not attacking. He was auditing.

He was waiting for Gerald to admit the fraud so the System could process the revocation cleanly.

But Gerald did not admit it.

He looked at the notebook in his other hand. The pages were blank. The gold leaf on the walls was still laughing, a low, resonant hum that vibrated in his teeth.

"Your Honor," Gerald said. His voice was steady. Dry. "I object to the premise of the audit."

The Inspector General tilted his head.

Gerald stepped forward. The crack in his hand widened further. A chunk of gold dust fell to the floor.

"The execution order cited unauthorized jurisdictional expansion," Gerald said. "It did not cite perjury. It did not cite lack of representation. It cited location."

He pointed to the floor.

"This room is designated as a Municipal Annex by the Ashford City Charter, Section 4, Paragraph B."

The Inspector General's eyes narrowed.

"I am a municipal employee," Gerald continued. "I am acting within my scope.

The dungeon beneath the library is a municipal sub-basement.

Therefore, this hearing is a municipal proceeding. I am not claiming to be a lawyer. I am claiming to be a civil servant.

And civil servants do not require legal counsel to interpret local zoning laws.

"

The laughter in the walls stopped.

The hum died.

The Inspector General's many eyes focused. They shifted. They aligned. For the first time, he looked uncertain.

Gerald felt the Core Integrity warning flicker.

*42%. Cause: Re-evaluation of Jurisdiction.*

It was a bluff. A massive, precarious bluff resting on a technicality so obscure that only a man who had read every municipal code for thirty-one years would think to use it.

But the System accepted rules, which did not accept intent. If Gerald could prove the room was municipal, the contradiction dissolved. The lie became a legal argument.

Gerald opened his notebook. He did not write. He held it up.

"I am invoking Clause 12," Gerald said. "Right to Local Interpretation. I am requesting a pause in proceedings to verify the municipal status of this floor."

The Inspector General said nothing, and he raised a hand. The air shimmered. A text box appeared in the center of the room.

**REQUEST RECEIVED.** **VERIFICATION PENDING.** **TIME LIMIT: 60 SECONDS.**

Sixty seconds.

Gerald turned. He walked toward the wall.

The marble was cold.

Smooth, and he placed his palm against it. The crack in his hand burned.

He ignored it.

He closed his eyes. He reached out with his senses, not with magic, but with memory.

He remembered the library's foundation, and he remembered the pour dates. He remembered the blueprints filed in the basement of City Hall.

He pulled a mental blueprint.

*Ashford Municipal Library.

Sub-basement. Built 1974.

Municipal zoning.

Public easement.*

He opened his eyes. He looked at the floor tiles.

They were not dungeon stone, and they were linoleum.

Old, yellowed, commercial-grade linoleum.

Gerald pulled a pen from his pocket. It was dry. He used the tip to scratch into the marble. The sound was harsh, scratching like chalk on a board.

"Floor material: Composite Linoleum, Grade 3," he muttered. "Ceiling height: 8 feet, 6 inches. Ventilation: Central HVAC, municipal feed."

He looked up at the vent. It was rusted. Stained with mold.

"Ventilation status: Non-compliant," Gerald said.

The Inspector General's finger twitched.

Gerald turned back.

"The ventilation is non-compliant. Which means this room is not a dungeon. It is a failing municipal asset."

He pointed at the Inspector General.

"You cannot audit a dungeon that does not exist. You are auditing a library sub-basement. And you are doing it without a building permit."

The silence was absolute.

The Inspector General stared.

Gerald held his breath. His heart hammered against his ribs. This was not heroism.

This was paperwork. This was the only language he spoke fluently.

The text box hovered.

**VERIFICATION COMPLETE.** **STATUS: MUNICIPAL ZONE.** **ERROR: TARGET IS NOT A DUNGEON CORE IN THIS LOCATION.** **EXECUTION ORDER: VOIDED.**

The red light in Gerald's peripheral vision vanished.

The crack in his hand stopped spreading. It did not heal. But it stopped bleeding light.

Gerald exhaled. His knees felt weak. He gripped the notebook tighter.

The Inspector General lowered his hand. He looked at Gerald. His expression was unreadable. But the many eyes blinked in unison.

"Procedurally valid," the Inspector General said. His voice was not a voice. It was the sound of a stamp hitting paper. "But inefficient."

"I am an inspector," Gerald said. "Inefficiency is my job."

"Your core is still damaged," the Inspector General said. "The contradiction was real. You lied about your representation. The System recorded it."

"I amended the record," Gerald said.

"Amendment does not erase the stain."

The Inspector General turned. He walked toward the heavy oak doors of the hearing room. He did not look back.

Gerald stood alone, and he looked at his hand.

The crack was still there. A jagged line of gold and white. He could feel the cold air seeping into his chest.

He could feel the dungeon's walls groaning, and he was alive. But he was damaged.

He walked out of the room. The doors opened automatically.

The hallway stretched before him.

It was not the Obsidian Hall. It was the library's service corridor. Fluorescent lights flickered overhead.

Gerald walked. He counted his steps.

One.

Two.

Three, and he needed to fix the ventilation. He needed to cite the mold. He needed to get back to his spawn list.

But when he reached the end of the corridor, he stopped.

The door to the sub-basement was open.

Standing in the doorway was a goblin.

It was not the scout from earlier. This one was larger. It wore armor made of scrap metal and book covers. It held a club made from a rolled-up copy of *Encyclopedia Britannica*.

The goblin grinned. It had no teeth.

"Boss says thanks," the goblin said. "But we're not done."

Gerald looked at the goblin. He looked at the crack in his hand. He looked at the goblin's armor.

*Scrap metal. Book covers. No ventilation holes.*

"Form 11-B," Gerald said. "Unlicensed Biological Entity. Also, your armor lacks adequate ventilation for a Class C hazardous environment. That is a safety violation."

The goblin tilted its head.

"What's a violation?"

Gerald pulled out his pen. He clicked it. No ink. He used the tip to point at the goblin.

"It means you're going to die," Gerald said. "Slowly. And illegally."

The goblin laughed. It swung the book-club.

Gerald did not dodge. He did not have the speed. He had the code.

He raised his hand. The crack flared.

**SYSTEM NOTICE: CITATION ISSUED.** **TARGET: GOBLIN UNIT 7.** **VIOLATION: IMPROPER PPE (PERSONAL PROTECTIVE EQUIPMENT).** **PENALTY: 15% REDUCTION IN PHYSICAL DAMAGE RESISTANCE.**

The club hit Gerald's shoulder.

It hurt. It broke the bone. Gerald heard the snap.

He felt the shockwave travel down his arm. He fell to his knees.

But the goblin flinched. The impact had been heavier than it should have been. The armor had absorbed the shock, but the System had stripped its protective properties. The goblin's own bones cracked under the recoil.

Gerald looked up. Blood dripped from his mouth.

He smiled. A dry, paper-thin smile.

"Appeal that," he whispered.

The goblin roared. It raised the club again.

Gerald closed his eyes. He did not try to stand. He tried to listen.

He listened to the air, and the ventilation was bad. The mold was spreading. The dungeon was sick.

But Gerald was not sick. Gerald was a municipal employee.

And municipal employees did not quit.

He opened his eyes, and he looked at the goblin. He looked at the door behind the goblin. The door to his actual dungeon.

The 4,000-square-foot sub-basement.

The door was locked.

Gerald had locked it.

He had installed a deadbolt. A municipal-grade deadbolt.

The goblin kicked the door. It shook. The frame held.

Gerald pulled himself up. He used the wall for support. The crack in his hand pulsed.

*38%.*

He walked to the door. He placed his palm against it.

"Code 44-A," Gerald said. "Emergency Egress Blockade. In effect during active hostilities."

The door shuddered. The wood splintered.

But it did not open.

The goblin screamed. It battered the door with its club.

Gerald turned away. He walked down the corridor. His shoulder hung limp. His breath came in shallow gasps.

He needed a lawyer, and he needed a partner.

He needed a new core.

He reached the elevator. He pressed the button for the ground floor.

The elevator dinged. The doors opened.

Inside was a woman, and she wore a suit. She held a briefcase. She looked tired. Her eyes were red-rimmed.

She looked at Gerald's bleeding shoulder. She looked at the gold dust on his jacket.

"You're Gerald Hutchins," she said.

Gerald nodded.

"I'm Sarah Vane," she said. "I'm a public defender. I work for the city."

She stepped into the elevator.

"The System sent me a notice," she said. "It said you were being audited. It said you were representing yourself."

"I was," Gerald said.

"You're terrible at it."

"I know."

Sarah Vane looked at his hand. The crack was glowing faintly.

"I can't save your core," she said. "But I can file the paperwork. I can delay the Inspector General. I can buy you time."

Gerald looked at her.

"Why?" he asked.

"Because you cited the goblin," she said. "And the goblin's Core submitted an appeal. The hearing is scheduled for tomorrow morning."

Gerald felt a chill.

"Tomorrow?"

"Yes," Sarah said. "And if you lose, the Inspector General doesn't just void the order. He revokes your core."

The elevator doors closed.

The numbers climbed.

Lobby. First. Second.

Gerald looked at his hand, and he looked at Sarah.

"Prepare the defense," Gerald said.

"I don't have a defense," Sarah said. "You have no team. No defenses. No resources."

"I have code," Gerald said.

Sarah opened her briefcase. She pulled out a stack of papers.

"Code doesn't fight dragons, Gerald," she said.

"No," Gerald said. "But it fights audits."

The elevator dinged. Ground floor.

The doors opened.

Outside, the library was dark. But the dungeon beneath was awake. And somewhere, deep in the roots of the city, the Inspector General was watching.

He was waiting.

And he was not alone.

Gerald stepped out. He limped toward the exit.

He did not look back.

But he knew the next citation was already being written.

Not by him.

By the enemy.

And this time, the violation would be fatal.
