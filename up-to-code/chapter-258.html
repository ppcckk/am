Chapter 258: Hearing Required

# Chapter 258

The drone hovered at eye level, its rotors cutting a steady hum through the thin air of the crater. It had no eyes, only sensors, but the tilt of its chassis suggested curiosity.

The device scanned the rubble, the exposed rebar, the shattered foundation of what had once been the Ashford Municipal Library.

It recorded the debris field. It logged the radiation spikes from the dissipated dungeon core. It waited.

Gerald Hutchins did not wait, and he lay on his side in the wet dirt, clutching the rat against his chest like a newborn.

His suit jacket was shredded. His tie was gone.

One shoe was missing.

The other was caked in mud. The blue grid of the System hovered above him, translucent and pulsing with a rhythm that matched his failing heartbeat.

*Hearing Opening Statement Required.* *Time Remaining: 04:12.*

The drone lowered itself. A small mechanical arm extended from its underside, holding a stylus that glowed with pale light.

This was an Inspector General unit.

Not a player.

Not a monster. A regulator.

Gerald squinted at it. He recognized the model. He had seen similar units in the municipal parking enforcement fleet, though those were smaller and less expensive.

"Identify," the drone's speaker crackled. The voice was synthetic, flat, and devoid of judgment, which made it infinitely more terrifying than a shouting rival Core.

Gerald shifted his weight. Pain shot through his ribs. He ignored it.

He adjusted the rat in his arms. The creature trembled, its tiny claws digging into the fabric of his shirt, but it did not bite.

It had learned trust, or perhaps resignation.

"Gerald Hutchins," Gerald said. His voice was gravel. "Inspector.

Ashford Municipal.

License number 44-A-9. Status: Active.

"

The drone paused. The sensors dilated.

"Core Status: Invalid," the drone stated. "Dungeon footprint: Null. Entity count: One (biological, unregistered). Compliance Rating: N/A."

"Objection," Gerald wheezed. He forced himself to sit up. The movement made the world tilt, but he locked his spine, a habit from thirty years of holding posture during long inspections. "You are evaluating a structure that is in the process of being re-zoned."

The drone hovered closer.

The wind from its rotors blew dust into Gerald's face. He did not flinch. He had worked in asbestos abatement sites.

Dust was a variable, not an enemy.

"Explain zoning," the drone said.

"Section 14, Paragraph C of the System Code regarding Adaptive Infrastructure," Gerald recited.

He knew the text.

He had memorized the entire municipal code, and by extension, the System's mirrored regulations, because the System was just another bureaucracy with a better interface.

"A site undergoing catastrophic structural failure may be re-designated as a Public Utility Zone if it serves a civic function.

The library served a civic function. The rubble serves a civic function. It is a memorial.

"

The drone tilted. Its processing speed was visible in the flicker of its status light.

"Memorials require permits," the drone said. "Permits require a Core."

"The Core is under reconstruction," Gerald said. He looked at the rat. He looked at the drone. "I am filing an emergency variance request based on immediate public safety concerns."

"Variance requests require a physical signature," the drone noted.

Gerald reached into his pocket. He pulled out his pen.

It was dry, inkless, and he clicked it. Nothing. He clicked it again.

Still nothing.

The rat squeaked.

"Insufficient resources," Gerald muttered.

He looked around the crater. Debris. Mud.

Broken glass.

A shadow fell over him.

Not the drone.

Someone else.

Gerald looked up.

Standing on the edge of the crater, silhouetted against the reforming blue grid, was a figure in a high-visibility vest.

The vest was orange, stained with grime. The figure wore a hard hat that was too large for their head.

It was a woman, small and wiry, holding a clipboard, and she was not a player. She was not a Core. She was a human.

Gerald froze. The drone froze.

The woman looked down at Gerald. She looked at the drone. She looked at the rat.

"Hey," she said. Her voice was loud, carrying over the wind. "Is this the site?"

The drone swiveled.

"Unauthorized personnel detected. Please vacate the area."

The woman didn't move. She tapped her clipboard.

"I'm with the County Surveyor's office.

We got a ping about a sinkhole opening up near the library. Thought it was a gas leak. Didn't think it was... whatever this is.

"

Gerald stared at her. A surveyor. Human. Unaffected by the System's magical aura, perhaps because she didn't believe in it, or perhaps because her belief in property lines was stronger than her fear of the supernatural.

"Ma'am," Gerald said, his voice dropping to a whisper so the drone wouldn't register it as a threat. "You need to leave."

"Can't," she said. "I have to mark the boundaries. If this thing expands, I'll get sued."

She stepped off the edge.

The drone shrieked. A laser sight, red and thin, snapped onto her forehead.

"Warning. Lethal force authorized."

Gerald acted before he thought. He threw the rat.

Not away. Up.

The rat sailed through the air, a small, terrified ball of fur. It landed on the surveyor's shoulder. She yelped, dropping her clipboard.

"Shit!" she shouted, flailing.

The laser sight wavered. The drone recalibrated, tracking the biological movement.

"Target ambiguity," the drone said.

Gerald scrambled forward on his hands and knees. He grabbed the clipboard from the mud. He held it up, shielding the woman's face, though he knew it wouldn't stop a laser.

"Citation!" Gerald shouted.

He pointed at the drone.

"Violation of Protocol 77-B!

Assault on a municipal employee!

I am issuing a temporary restraining order on your weapons systems!

"

The drone stopped. The red dot vanished.

Silence returned to the crater, broken only by the woman's heavy breathing and the rat chittering in her collar.

"Who are you?" the woman asked, her eyes wide. She looked at Gerald. She looked at the pen he still held in his other hand. She looked at the blue grid.

"I'm Gerald," he said. "I'm sorry about the rat."

"That rat is huge," she said, trembling.

"It's a juvenile," Gerald corrected. "We'll get it a harness later."

The drone hovered, confused. The System's logic was struggling to parse the input.

A human surveyor.

A Core with no dungeon.

A biological entity acting as a witness.

*Hearing Status: Suspended.* *Reason: Jurisdictional Conflict.*

The words floated in the air, large and golden.

Gerald exhaled. He slumped back against the rubble. His ribs screamed.

His core felt like a dying ember. But the clock had stopped.

The surveyor picked up her clipboard. She looked at the drone. She looked at Gerald.

"You're the building inspector," she said. It wasn't a question.

"Retired," Gerald lied.

"You're not retired. I saw your name on the permit for the library renovation. You're the one who made them change the door hinges three times."

Gerald closed his eyes. He remembered the hinges. They were non-compliant with ADA width regulations.

"They were 32 inches," he said. "The code requires 34."

"Nobody else cares," the surveyor said.

She walked closer. She stopped ten feet away. She looked at the crater, then at the drone.

"So, what happens now?"

"Now," Gerald said, "I appeal the zoning classification."

The drone drifted back.

"The Inspector General is en route. ETA: Four minutes."

Gerald looked at the surveyor.

"Do you have a pen?"

She pulled a cheap ballpoint from her vest.

"Yeah."

Gerald took it. He opened the System interface with his mind, projecting a blank form into the air. The surveyor gasped, stepping back.

"Is that... is that a hologram?"

"It's a projection," Gerald said. "Hold still."

He wrote. Not with his hand.

With his intent.

The System accepted mental input, but it preferred physical signatures for legal weight. Gerald used the pen to trace the air, and the System rendered his strokes in light.

*Form 11-C: Emergency Boundary Redefinition.* *Applicant: Gerald Hutchins.* *Witness: Sarah Jenkins, County Surveyor.*

The drone buzzed.

"Witness must be registered System user."

Sarah looked at Gerald.

"I don't know what that means."

"Just sign it," Gerald said. "For the county."

Sarah hesitated. Then, with a shrug that said *this is probably a prank, but I'm bored*, she stepped forward. She signed the air. The light flashed white.

*Witness Accepted.*

The drone retreated further.

"This is highly irregular."

"It's municipal," Gerald said. He stood up.

His legs shook, but he locked his knees, and he pointed at the crater.

"This site is now a public access corridor. You cannot cite it for lack of egress, because it has no egress.

It is an entry point.

"

The drone scanned the area. It looked at the sky. It looked at the surrounding city, where buildings were now visible through the gap in the grid.

"Logic loop detected," the drone said. "Recalculating."

Gerald looked at Sarah.

"Why are you here, really?"

"Curiosity," she said. "And the gas leak thing. Did you cause this?"

"No," Gerald said. "But I'm going to fix it."

He walked to the center of the crater. He placed his foot on the exposed foundation.

He felt the System's pulse, which was weak. The Core was dormant. The rat was the only battery he had.

He needed a second.

"Sarah," he said. "Do you have a hard hat?"

"Yeah."

"Take it off."

Sarah removed her helmet. She handed it to him.

Gerald placed the helmet on the ground. He placed the rat inside it. The rat curled up, confused but safe.

"System," Gerald said. "Register entity: Rat. Classification: Municipal Worker. Role: Safety Consultant."

*Processing...*

The drone froze.

*Entity Registered.* *Compliance Bonus: +10 (Safety Oversight).*

The blue grid flared. For a second, it was solid. Then it faded.

Gerald felt strength return to his limbs. Not much. But enough.

The drone's lights turned red.

*Violation Detected: Unauthorized Modification of System Parameters.* *Action: Terminate Core.*

A beam of energy gathered in the drone's chest.

Gerald looked at Sarah.

"Run."

"I'm not leaving you!"

"I'm not asking you to leave. I'm asking you to *observe*."

The beam fired.

Gerald didn't dodge. He raised the clipboard.

The energy hit the clipboard.

The plastic melted. The paper caught fire.

But the energy dispersed, and the System had registered the paper as a legal document. Destroying a legal document during a hearing was a Class A felony in the System's penal code.

*Warning: Evidence Tampering.* *Penalty: Drone Disabled.*

The drone sparked. It listed to the side, rotors failing. It crashed into the mud, smoking.

Silence.

Sarah stared at the smoking drone. Then she looked at Gerald.

"You just broke a robot," she said.

"I filed a complaint," Gerald corrected. He picked up the charred remains of the clipboard. "This is evidence. Don't touch it."

He turned to the edge of the crater. The blue grid was fading rapidly.

The sky was just sky, and the library was just rubble.

But the rating...

Gerald checked his internal status.

*Compliance Rating: 52/100.* *Status: Probationary.* *New Mechanic Unlocked: Citizen Witness Corroboration.*

He could now use human witnesses to validate citations. He could bring the bureaucracy of the mortal world into the magical one.

But there was a cost.

A voice boomed from the sky. Not from the drone. From everywhere.

*Inspector General Audit in Progress.* *Target: Gerald Hutchins.* *Reason: Unauthorized Alliance with Non-System Entities.*

Gerald looked up. The clouds were parting. A massive, golden eye opened in the sky.

It was not a face. It was a lens.

Sarah screamed.

Gerald looked at the rat in the hard hat. The rat looked up at the eye and squeaked.

Gerald looked at Sarah.

"Sarah," he said. "Do you know the code for structural load in a non-reinforced concrete foundation?"

Sarah blinked. She looked at the giant eye in the sky. She looked at Gerald. She swallowed hard.

"Type 4500 psi," she whispered.

Gerald smiled. It was a small, dry smile.

"Good," he said. "Because we're about to file a motion for dismissal based on insufficient foundational evidence."

The eye blinked.

The ground beneath them shifted. Not away. Upward.

From the deep, dark earth beneath the rubble, a new structure rose. It was not stone. It was not wood. It was made of compacted dirt and crushed glass, shaped into a perfect, rectangular archway.

It had no dungeon doors. No traps. No monsters.

It had a sign.

*Public Restroom. Under Construction.*

Gerald stepped toward it.

"Sarah," he said. "Hold my pen."

He walked into the dirt archway. The System accepted him. The Compliance Rating ticked up.

*53.*

*54.*

But as he crossed the threshold, the air inside changed. It smelled of wet earth and old paper. And waiting in the shadows of the new archway, sitting on a throne of crushed bricks, was a figure.

It was not a monster. It was a man in a suit. His face was obscured by static.

"Gerald," the figure said. His voice sounded like grinding gears. "You cited the Inspector General?"

Gerald stopped. He looked at the rat. He looked at the man.

"I cited a drone," Gerald said.

"You cited the Authority," the man said. He stood up. He was ten feet tall. "And you won."

Gerald felt a chill that had nothing to do with the temperature.

"Did I?"

"No," the man said.

He pointed to the sky, where the giant eye was closing.

"You just started a war with every Core in the system. They don't like inspectors, Gerald.

And now that you've brought humans into the loop... they're going to try to erase them.

"

The man stepped aside. Behind him, a portal opened.

Through the portal, Gerald saw not a dungeon, but a city street.

Ashford's main street. And it was burning.

"Fix it," the man said.

Gerald looked at the portal. He looked at Sarah, who was screaming at the giant eye.

He looked at the rat.

Gerald stepped through the portal.
