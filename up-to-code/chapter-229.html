Chapter 229: No Longer in Charge

# Chapter 229

The transcript was already three pages long.

Gerald stared at the glowing blue text hovering in the air, his eyes scanning the columns of data with the practiced speed of a man who had spent three decades reading zoning variances. The air in the sub-basement smelled of ozone and wet concrete. It was not a pleasant scent, but it was familiar.

*Case No. 8940-B: Petition for Right of Transfer.* *Appellant: Core Entity "Vine-Root" (ID: 7734).* *Respondent: Core Entity "Ashford Municipal Sub-Basement" (ID: 001).* *Status: Hearing in Progress.*

The text pulsed with a faint, irritated red light. A violation notice.

Gerald adjusted his tie. It was crooked.

He straightened it, feeling the fabric bite into his collar.

This was not a fight. Fights were messy. Fights lacked documentation.

This was a hearing.

A hearing required procedure. And Gerald Hutchins did not deviate from procedure.

He could not.

Mira stood to his left, her sword lowered but not sheathed. Her knuckles were white. She was watching the air, not the text.

"You're wasting time," Mira said. Her voice was tight. "The IG doesn't care about forms."

"The IG cares about the record," Gerald replied, not looking up. "If the record is clean, the decision is binding. If the record is dirty, the decision is appealable. I prefer my decisions binding."

A new line of text scrolled into view.

*Appellant Statement: "Respondent Core violated Privacy Statute 44.1 by installing surveillance runes in public corridors without posted notice. The Citation issued via Form 12-C was therefore procedurally void. Deletion request denied."*

Gerald's eye twitched. It was a small movement, barely visible, but it happened. He pulled a pen from his pocket.

It was a standard-issue ballpoint, black ink, cap attached. He uncapped it.

"Surveillance runes," Gerald muttered. "Section 44, subsection C. Notice must be posted at all entry points greater than four feet in height."

He looked at the dungeon entrance. The archway was three feet, nine inches high.

"Mira," he said. "Measure the arch."

Mira blinked. "Gerald, we are being audited by the Inspector General."

"Measure the arch."

She hesitated, then walked to the stone frame. She pulled a tape measure from her belt pouch, a habit she had picked up from watching him. She extended the tape. The metal clinked against the stone.

"Three feet, nine inches," she said.

Gerald nodded.

He wrote on his clipboard.

Not the System interface. A physical clipboard.

He wrote *ARCH HEIGHT: < 48 INCHES. NOTICE EXEMPTION APPLIES.*

He held the clipboard up to the floating text.

The System paused. The red light flickered.

*Argument Acknowledged. Appellant rebuttal required.*

The air grew colder. Frost crept up the walls. This was the System's version of a gavel strike.

Gerald felt a familiar nausea. It was the same feeling he got when a contractor tried to hand-wave away a load-bearing error. It was a physical rejection of logical inconsistency. He swallowed it down.

"Rebuttal," Gerald said aloud. His voice was flat.

"Appellant claims violation of Privacy Statute.

However, Appellant's primary spawn, the 'Thorn-Strangler' unit, was found engaging in unlicensed combat within residential zoning districts. Residential zoning requires a 'Peaceful Use' certification. The Thorn-Strangler lacks this certification."

He flipped a page. He had prepared this. He had prepared for everything.

"Furthermore," Gerald continued, "the Appellant's attempt to invoke the Right of Transfer was executed via a 'Holding Cell' protocol. The Holding Cell is a municipal detention facility. It does not exist within the System's dungeon network. Therefore, the transfer destination was non-existent."

He tapped the paper with his pen.

"An appeal to a non-existent authority is automatically denied. The original Citation stands. The Deletion Order is valid."

The text froze.

Then, it changed.

*System Notice: Inspector General Reviewing. Calculating Precedent Weight...*

Mira let out a breath she had been holding. "You're banking on the System not understanding municipal zoning laws?"

"The System understands code," Gerald said. "It is built on code. It just has a very narrow definition of what constitutes 'public.'"

A shadow detached itself from the ceiling.

It was not a monster. It was a figure. Tall, slender, made of shifting geometric shapes that resembled a suit made of light. It hovered above them, ignoring gravity.

This was not an adventurer. This was not a rival Core.

This was the Inspector General. Or rather, a projection of one.

The figure did not speak. It simply projected a large, golden stamp onto the air between Gerald and the text.

*DENIED.*

Mira stepped back. "He overturned it?"

Gerald looked at the stamp. His face remained blank. He looked at the fine print beneath the stamp.

*Reason: Jurisdictional Conflict. The Right of Transfer is a System-level privilege. Municipal detention facilities are not recognized as valid transfer destinations unless pre-approved by the Core Registry.*

Gerald's mouth tightened.

He had missed a clause. A tiny clause in subsection D, paragraph 7.

He hated subsection D.

It was always the tricky ones.

The vine-core was not deleted.

Instead, the air in front of Gerald ripped open. A vortex of green light and thorns spun into existence.

The vine-core was here.

Not the small, confused thing he had cited. This was the adult form. It was vast. It pressed against the edges of the dungeon's reality, trying to force its way into the sub-basement physically, bypassing the digital transfer.

Vines erupted from the floor tiles. They smashed through the handrails Gerald had installed. They shattered the egress signage.

Gerald stepped back. He did not draw a weapon. He drew a form.

*Form 22-A: Emergency Structural Condemnation.*

He didn't hesitate. He slammed the form onto the nearest pillar.

The pillar cracked.

The entire dungeon groaned. The ceiling, which was the foundation of the Library above, sagged.

"Gerald!" Mira shouted. "You're bringing the roof down!"

"Condemnation is the only way to seal a breach," Gerald said. "The vine is entering through a structural fault. The fault is the entrance. I am closing the entrance."

The vines whipped toward him. They moved with terrifying speed, sensing the weakness in the air.

Gerald did not move. He watched the vines approach. He watched the ceiling buckle.

He had calculated the load-bearing capacity of the Library's main floor. He had checked the support beams. He had filed a report on the vibration tolerance of the surrounding soil.

He knew exactly how much weight he could add before the structure failed.

He knew that the System's Compliance Rating for Structural Integrity was currently 88%.

If he condemned this section, the rating would drop to 0%.

If the rating dropped to 0%, the dungeon would collapse.

The vine would be crushed.

Gerald would be erased.

Mira saw the fear in his eyes. It was not fear of death. It was fear of failure. Fear of an incomplete file.

"Gerald, run!"

Gerald looked at her. He looked at the vines. They were ten feet away. Five feet.

He pulled a second form from his pocket.

*Form 44-X: Retroactive Permit Application.*

He didn't slam it down. He held it up.

The System recognized the form. The vines paused.

They were confused.

They had never seen a permit application.

They were creatures of instinct and growth. They did not understand bureaucracy.

The pause lasted one second.

Gerald spoke.

"Permit denied," he said.

The System agreed. The vines recoiled, sensing the rejection of authority. They were not attacking a person.

They were attacking a structure. And the structure had just declared itself illegal.

An illegal structure could not be entered.

The vines slammed into the invisible barrier of the condemned zone. They writhed, trying to find a gap. There was no gap. The condemnation was absolute.

The ceiling above them cracked further. Dust rained down.

*Warning: Structural Integrity Critical. Collapse Imminent. Evacuate.*

The System's voice was loud. It shook the walls.

Gerald looked at the vines. They were trapped outside the barrier, but the barrier was failing because the building was failing.

He had one more move.

He looked at Mira. "Take the badge."

Mira froze. "What?"

"Badge #4402. Take it."

She grabbed the plastic card from his belt.

Gerald turned to the System interface.

He began typing. Not on the air. On his clipboard.

He wrote rapidly, his handwriting cramped and precise.

*Motion to Vacate Judgment based on Force Majeure.*

He attached the physical clipboard to the System interface. The System accepted it.

*Motion Received. Processing...*

The ceiling gave way.

A massive slab of concrete, weighing several tons, dropped from the library above. It crashed into the floor, inches from Gerald's feet. The impact shook the entire sub-basement.

The vines shrieked. The sound was like tearing metal.

The slab blocked the entrance. It sealed the breach.

But it also crushed the support column.

The floor tilted.

Gerald stood on the slanted concrete. He did not slide. He had adjusted his stance earlier, angling his boots against the slope.

He looked at the System text.

*Motion Denied. Judgment Stands. Entity "Vine-Root" is now subject to Immediate Exclusion from Ashford District.*

The vines were gone. Not deleted. Excluded. Banished from the area.

They would be back. Soon. But not now.

Gerald looked at Mira. She was holding the badge. She looked pale.

"You just condemned your own dungeon," she said.

"I condemned the entrance," Gerald corrected. "The rest is still standing. Barely."

He looked around. The handrails were broken. The lights were flickering. The ventilation system was whining, struggling against the dust.

His Compliance Rating dropped.

*Current Rating: 31/100.*

A new notification popped up.

*Inspector General Notice: Audit Complete. Findings: Non-Standard Procedural Abuse. Penalty: Suspension of Citation Privileges for 48 Hours.*

Gerald read the text. He felt a cold knot in his stomach.

No Citations.

No Forms.

No power.

For two days, he was just a man in a basement. And the vine-core was waiting outside.

Mira looked at the hole in the ceiling. Above them, the library floor was buckling.

"We have to move," she said. "The library is going to come down."

Gerald looked at his clipboard. The paper was torn. The ink was smudged.

He picked it up. He smoothed out the crease.

"We cannot move," Gerald said. "The exit is blocked by the slab. The secondary egress is under a pile of debris. The ventilation is compromised."

He looked at the System text.

*Compliance Check: Egress Route A: Blocked. Egress Route B: Blocked. Egress Route C: Non-Existent.*

"We are in violation of Life Safety Code Chapter 7," Gerald said. His voice was quiet.

Mira grabbed his arm. "Gerald, this is not a code violation. This is a collapse. We need to dig our way out."

"You cannot dig without a permit," Gerald said.

"I don't care about the permit!"

"Then you will be cited for unlicensed excavation."

The floor lurched. Another piece of the ceiling fell. It landed on the far wall, exploding into dust.

Gerald looked at the dust. He looked at the cracks spreading through the remaining walls.

He realized something.

The Inspector General had not just suspended his Citations.

He had revoked his Core Status.

Gerald looked at his hands. They were not dissolving.

They were not turning into dungeon stone.

They were just hands. Human hands.

He was no longer a Dungeon Core.

He was a squatter.

And the building was unsafe.

Mira stared at him. "Gerald? What did you do?"

Gerald looked at the floating text. It was fading. The System was leaving.

*Registration Cancelled. User: Gerald Hutchins. Status: Civilian.*

He felt the loss of the power. It was like losing a limb. The awareness of the walls, the knowledge of the air currents, the sense of the dungeon's pulse—all of it was gone.

He was blind.

He was deaf.

He was just a 54-year-old man standing in a collapsing basement.

Mira pulled him toward the rubble. "Move! Now!"

Gerald did not move. He looked at the spot where the vine-core had been.

He knew it was still there. Watching. Waiting for him to try to escape.

If he tried to escape, he would trigger a trap. A simple tripwire. A falling beam. A gas leak.

He needed a plan.

He needed a form.

He had no forms.

He had no authority.

He had no dungeon.

He looked at Mira. She was pulling him, her face streaked with dirt.

"Gerald, please!"

Gerald closed his eyes. He thought of Form 11-B. Unlicensed Biological Entity.

He thought of Form 22-A. Condemnation.

He thought of the Inspector General.

He opened his eyes. He grabbed a piece of rebar from the rubble. It was bent, jagged.

He held it out.

"Mira," he said. "Take this."

"What are you doing?"

"I am filing a complaint," Gerald said. "Against the Inspector General."

Mira stared at him. "You can't. You're a civilian."

"Anyone can file a complaint," Gerald said. "It doesn't require a core status. It requires a witness."

He looked at the dark corner of the room. The vines were still there, hidden in the shadows, waiting for the building to finish falling so they could pick over the wreckage.

"I am the witness," Gerald said. "And you are the corroborating evidence."

Mira looked at the rebar. She looked at Gerald. She looked at the darkness.

She took the rebar.

"Okay," she said. "What do I say?"

Gerald straightened his tie. It was shredded. He didn't care.

"Say nothing," he said. "Just stand here. And wait for them to make a mistake."

The floor tilted again. The library above groaned.

And from the darkness, a voice spoke. It was not the vine-core. It was something older. Something deeper.

*You are out of order, Hutchins.*

Gerald looked into the dark.

"I am in order," he said. "I am just no longer in charge."
