Chapter 346: Cited Mistake

# Chapter 346

The pen tip snapped.

Gerald didn't flinch. He just pressed harder against the trembling skin of his own palm, the ink mixing with the sweat that stung the paper cuts.

The massive, translucent hand hovered there, a single finger the size of a bus tire resting inches from his nose.

The pressure in the mill dropped further. Ears popped again.

Martha was still sinking. The concrete quicksand had reached her chest. She wasn't screaming anymore. She was wheezing, short, panicked gasps that sounded like a punctured tire.

"Stop!" Martha choked out. "It's going to crush the support beam!"

Gerald ignored her. He kept writing. The paper was shaking so violently the letters looked like seismograph readings.

*Violation: Improper extraction of registered Core.*

The hand twitched. The grid above flickered, blue lines pulsing with a rhythm that matched Gerald's heartbeat.

Then, a voice. Not from above. From everywhere. It sounded like the static from a broken radio dial, layered over the hum of a server farm.

**[SUBMIT CITATION? Y/N]**

"Yes," Gerald said.

His voice was flat. Deadpan.

He didn't look at the hand, and he looked at his tablet, which was now black.

The grid above solidified. The hand lowered its finger. It didn't touch him. It touched the tablet.

**[CITATION ACCEPTED. PROCESSING.]**

The floor stopped liquefying.

Martha gasped, her arms flailing as she pulled herself out of the hardened concrete. She scrambled backward, slipping on the wet dust of the mill floor. Her eyes were wide, fixed on the sky.

"You did it," she whispered. "You actually did it."

"I did nothing," Gerald said. He finally dropped the pen.

His hand was covered in ink and blood.

"I filed a report.

The System processed it.

"

"The *what*?"

Martha stood up. She brushed concrete dust off her uniform. She looked at Gerald like he had just grown a second head. Or maybe three heads.

"The Inspector General," Gerald said, rubbing his temple. "He's not here to audit. He's here to serve.

That hand? That's the service mechanism.

It's a physical proxy for a digital citation.

"

A figure stepped out from the shadows of the machinery.

It wasn't a monster. It wasn't a goblin. It was a man.

Or something that used to be a man, and he wore a suit that looked like it was made of shifting geometric patterns. The fabric rippled every time he moved.

"You have poor filing discipline," the figure said. His voice was smooth, devoid of human inflection. "Your handwriting is illegible. Your submission lacked a witness signature."

Gerald straightened his jacket. He ignored the dust on his sleeves.

"Who are you?"

"I am the Deputy Administrator for Extractions," the figure said. He didn't blink. "You cited a God-Head. Do you understand the jurisdictional complexity of that action?"

"I don't care about jurisdiction," Gerald said. "I care about egress."

The Deputy Administrator tilted his head. A small, geometric shape floated near his ear, rotating slowly.

"Explain."

"That building," Gerald pointed to the mill structure above them.

"It has no primary egress route. The ceiling is a hazard. If you pluck me out, you destabilize the foundation of the Municipal Library.

Which is a public facility. Under Ashford City Ordinance 44-B, any demolition or removal of a load-bearing structure requires a thirty-day public notice period.

"

The Deputy Administrator's geometric ear stopped rotating.

"You are citing municipal code against a celestial entity?"

"I am citing *everyone* against municipal code," Gerald said. "That's the job."

Martha grabbed Gerald's arm.

Her grip was tight, painful.

"Gerald, stop. He's not a city inspector.

He's—"

"He's a contractor," Gerald interrupted. "And he's cutting corners."

The Deputy Administrator laughed. It was a dry, cracking sound.

"You think you can fine the Sky?"

"I can fine you," Gerald said. He pulled out a fresh tablet.

The battery indicator was red, but the screen lit up, and the System had recognized the citation. The interface had changed. The usual dungeon stats were gone.

In their place was a simple, stark text field.

**[CITATION ID: 994-ALPHA]** **[STATUS: PENDING APPEAL]** **[TARGET: DEPUTY ADMINISTRATOR]** **[VIOLATION: STRUCTURAL DESTABILIZATION / CODE 0.0]**

The Deputy Administrator's face hardened. The geometric patterns on his suit turned jagged, sharp.

"You are invoking the Appeal Protocol," the Administrator said. "Do you know what that entails?"

"Tell me," Gerald said.

"You must defend the structural integrity of your citation. If you fail, the debuff is reversed. And you are penalized."

"Penalized how?"

"Deletion."

Martha made a sound that was half-gasp, half-sob.

"Gerald, no. We can't appeal this. We just need to survive."

"We don't survive by hiding," Gerald said. He looked at the Administrator.

"I'm appealing.

Not the citation. The *execution*.

"

The Administrator stepped closer. The air around him crackled with ozone.

"Explain your appeal logic."

Gerald walked forward. He didn't run. He walked with the measured pace of a man checking a clipboard. He stopped ten feet from the Deputy Administrator.

"Code 0.0 is reserved for Core Registration errors," Gerald said. "It implies the Core did not consent to its own existence. It implies the System failed to obtain proper permits."

The Administrator's eyes narrowed.

"The Core consented via registration."

"No," Gerald said. "The Core was *coerced*. The registration was triggered by a structural deficiency report on my own body. That is a medical violation, not a consent form."

Martha stepped forward.

"Gerald, what are you talking about? You registered."

"I was inspected," Gerald corrected. "By a structural anomaly.

That is not consent, which is an invasive procedure without anesthesia.

Under the System's own Bylaws, Article 4, Section 2, involuntary registration is void.

"

The Administrator paused. The grid above the mill flickered.

**[ANALYZING ARGUMENT...]**

"Article 4, Section 2," the Administrator repeated. "You are quoting the Founding Code."

"I live in it," Gerald said.

"It is a loophole," the Administrator said. "A very narrow one. Most Cores are born into the System.

They are part of the ecosystem. You are... an anomaly.

"

"Anomalies require exception permits," Gerald said. "Which you did not file."

The Administrator's suit shifted again.

The sharp patterns smoothed out. He looked annoyed. Not angry.

Annoyed.

Like a bureaucrat who has been presented with a form in triplicate when he only wanted a signature.

"You are delaying the extraction," the Administrator said.

"I am pausing the extraction," Gerald corrected. "Pending review. You cannot extract a registered entity during an active appeal window. It is a procedural violation."

**[APPEAL GRANTED. HEARING SCHEDULED. LOCATION: THE VOID.]**

The ground shook.

Not the mill.

The air. The space around them twisted. The mill walls stretched, elongating into infinite hallways of white light.

The machinery dissolved. Martha fell to her knees, clutching her head.

"What is happening?" she screamed.

"The Hearing," Gerald said.

He felt physically ill.

The shift in reality gave him a migraine that felt like a hammer hitting the back of his skull. He hated it.

He hated the chaos. He liked straight lines. He liked right angles.

"Where are we going?" Martha asked. She was pale, her face a mask of terror.

"To a courtroom," Gerald said.

"A courtroom?"

"A structural logic forum," Gerald said. "We argue the code. He argues the power. The System decides who is up to spec."

Martha grabbed his tablet.

"You can't fight him.

He's a God-Head's deputy, and he has stats we can't even see. His HP is probably infinite.

"

"HP doesn't matter in a hearing," Gerald said. He tapped the screen. A new window opened.

**[HEARING SETUP]** **[YOUR ROLE: DEFENDANT]** **[OPPOSING COUNSEL: DEPUTY ADMINISTRATOR]** **[TOPIC: VALIDITY OF CITATION 994-ALPHA]** **[TIME LIMIT: 10 MINUTES]**

"Ten minutes?" Martha's voice cracked. "That's not enough time to prepare a legal defense against a cosmic entity!"

"It's enough time to point out a missing form," Gerald said.

The white light intensified. The Deputy Administrator stepped onto a platform that materialized beneath his feet. Gerald and Martha were forced onto a smaller, lower platform.

The space around them was no longer the mill. It was an infinite white void. Above them, the blue grid stretched out, but now it was populated with text. Millions of lines of code, scrolling rapidly.

The Administrator looked down at them. He held a gavel made of light.

"The hearing is in session," the Administrator said. "Gerald Hutchins, you have cited a representative of the Inspector General for violation of Code 0.0. State your case."

Gerald looked at his tablet, and the interface was minimal. No stats.

No health bars.

Just a text box and a timer counting down from 600.

"I will not state my case," Gerald said.

The Administrator raised an eyebrow.

"Refusal to plead is a summary judgment. You lose."

"I am not refusing," Gerald said. "I am requesting a recess to obtain the original registration logs."

The Administrator sneered.

"You have those logs. They are on your dashboard."

"They are encrypted," Gerald said. "I require a decryption key. Under the Data Transparency Act, I have the right to view the raw input of my own creation."

The Administrator paused. The timer ticked. 580.

"There is no Data Transparency Act," the Administrator said.

"Section 9, Paragraph 4," Gerald said. "Added in Patch 4.2. It allows Core entities to audit their own creation protocols to prevent memory corruption."

The Administrator checked the scrolling code above. He frowned. He actually *checked*.

"It is there," the Administrator admitted. "But it requires a Level 5 clearance. You are Level 1."

"I am Level 10," Gerald said.

The Administrator laughed. It was a harsh, barking sound.

"You are a sub-basement core.

You have three mold spores and a rat.

You are Level 1.

"

"I have issued forty-two citations," Gerald said. "Every citation grants XP. Not for combat.

For *compliance*. Every violation I find in the System, I am rewarded for.

I am Level 10."

Martha looked at Gerald. Her mouth was open.

"Forty-two?"

"Forty-two," Gerald confirmed. "And I have more in the queue."

The Administrator's face went blank. The geometric patterns on his suit stopped moving.

"If you are Level 10," the Administrator said slowly, "then you have access to the Inspector General's direct line."

"I have a phone number," Gerald said. "I haven't used it yet. Because I prefer to do things through official channels.

But you are obstructing official channels.

Therefore, I am invoking the emergency override.

"

Gerald tapped his tablet.

**[REQUESTING LEVEL 5 CLEARANCE: AUDIT LOGS]** **[SYSTEM MESSAGE: CLEARANCE DENIED. INSUFFICIENT AUTHORITY.]**

The Administrator smiled, which was a cruel smile.

"Denied. You have five minutes left.

Your citation will be dismissed, and you will be deleted for wasting the System's time.

"

Gerald didn't look at the Administrator. He looked at Martha.

"Martha," he said. "Read the violation."

"What?"

"The violation I cited. 'Improper extraction of registered Core.' Do you know what the key word is?"

Martha swallowed hard.

"Improper?"

"No," Gerald said. "Extraction."

He turned back to the Administrator.

"Code 0.0 applies to improper *creation*," Gerald said. "But I didn't cite improper creation. I cited improper *extraction*. Which is under Code 12.4."

The Administrator's smile vanished.

"Code 12.4," the Administrator said. "That is a felony."

"It is," Gerald said. "And it carries a mandatory minimum penalty."

The Administrator looked at his tablet. He looked at the scrolling code. He was searching.

"Where did you get Code 12.4?" the Administrator asked. His voice was no longer smooth. It was jagged. "That code was deprecated in Patch 1.0."

"It was un-deprecated in Patch 5.5," Gerald said.

"When the System introduced the Inspector General.

Because the Inspector General needed a way to punish rogue Cores. And you," Gerald pointed at the Administrator, "are a rogue Core.

You are operating outside your jurisdiction. You are not supposed to extract. You are supposed to *inspect*.

"

The Administrator took a step back. The gavel in his hand flickered.

"You are baiting me," the Administrator said.

"I am inspecting you," Gerald said.

The timer hit 300. Five minutes.

"If you continue this line of reasoning," the Administrator said, "the System will flag you for malicious interrogation. You will be quarantined."

"Flag me," Gerald said. "I want a record.

I want it in the public log.

Because if I am quarantined for citing a Deputy Administrator, then the Inspector General will see the log. And he will see that you are afraid of the code.

"

The Administrator stared at him. The silence in the void was absolute.

Then, the Administrator raised his gavel.

"Objection overruled," the Administrator said. "The hearing is suspended. Pending internal review."

The white void collapsed. The platforms dissolved.

Martha grabbed Gerald's arm.

"What just happened?"

"We bought time," Gerald said. He felt the pull of reality reasserting itself. The mill was returning. The dust was returning.

"They're going to come for you now," Martha said. "The Inspector General."

"Yes," Gerald said.

"He will kill you."

"Maybe," Gerald said. He looked at his tablet. The battery was at 1%. The screen flickered one last time.

**[NEW MESSAGE FROM INSPECTOR GENERAL]** **[SUBJECT: MEETING REQUEST]** **[LOCATION: ASHFORD MUNICIPAL LIBRARY, SUB-BASEMENT 4]** **[TIME: IMMEDIATE]**

Gerald looked up. The sky above the mill was returning. The normal, grey, Ashford sky.

But in the corner of his vision, a new notification popped up. A single, red icon.

**[WARNING: CORE INTEGRITY COMPROMISED.]** **[ERROR: DUPLICATE ENTITY DETECTED.]**

Gerald looked at his hands. They were still his hands. Ink-stained, shaking.

But then he saw another hand. Emerging from the floor. A hand made of concrete. A hand that looked exactly like his.

Martha screamed.

Gerald looked at the concrete hand. Then he looked at the Administrator, who was fading away into the light.

"I didn't cite a person," Gerald whispered. "I cited a mistake."

The concrete hand grabbed Gerald's ankle. It pulled. Hard.

And then, from the darkness behind them, a voice spoke, which was Gerald's voice. But it was wrong.

It was smooth.

It was confident.

"Hello, Gerald," the voice said. "I've been waiting for you to find me."
