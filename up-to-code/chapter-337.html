Chapter 337: Measure the Breach

# Chapter 337

The hammers did not sound like construction. They sounded like teeth grinding in a skull.

Gerald floated in the void. No legs.

No lungs.

Just a consciousness suspended in the static hum of the System's background noise. The air tasted of copper and wet dust. Around him, the skeleton of his dungeon reknit itself, brick by brick, in a blur of accelerated time.

He watched. That was all he could do.

The System's automated contractors moved with efficient, terrifying precision. They stripped the rot from the load-bearing pillars. They replaced the corroded pipes with seamless copper tubing. They installed ventilation shafts that met the precise cubic-foot-per-minute requirements for a Class 4 Subterranean Facility.

Gerald's mental clipboard twitched, and he wanted to check the pitch of the new drainage slope. He wanted to verify that the emergency exit signage was illuminated by backup power.

But he had no hands.

He had no clipboard.

A notification pinged in the periphery of his vision. Not a visual alert. A tactile pressure, like a finger pressing against the inside of his eyelid.

*Compliance Rating: 48/100.*

It had gone up. Barely.

The rebuilding process was not just repair. It was standardization.

The System was averaging out the quirks that made Gerald's dungeon *his*.

The irregular corner where the mold spore nest had been was smoothed over.

The unique, drafty window that Gerald had insisted on keeping for aesthetic reasons was replaced with a standard-issue fire-rated pane.

Gerald felt a cold prickle of violation in his mind. This wasn't inspection. This was erasure.

The Architect stood in the center of the main hall. He was solid now, his ink-stained fingers tracing the mortar lines. He looked around with the hollow expression of a man who had lost his house.

"Stop," Gerald tried to say.

No sound came out. The silence was absolute.

The Architect paused. He tilted his head. He could not hear Gerald. Gerald was currently designated as a Passive Observer, a status that granted full sensory input but zero output authority.

The hammers continued. They were building the second floor. A mezzanine. A perfect, symmetrical, soulless mezzanine.

Gerald focused his will. He could not speak, but he could still cite. He could still observe.

The loophole remained. If he could identify a violation in real-time, the System would pause the construction to address it.

The citation process was instant, and the penalty was immediate.

He looked at the mezzanine railing.

The height was exactly forty-two inches.

Gerald's mind raced through the municipal code. Section 4.2.1: Residential Guardrails. Minimum height: thirty-four inches. Maximum height: forty-two inches.

Compliant, and he looked at the spacing between the balusters. Four inches.

Code: Openings must not allow a sphere of four and three-eighths inches to pass through.

Gerald projected his awareness toward the gap. He visualized a ball. A standard baseball. It passed through the gap easily.

Violation, and he focused his intent. He didn't need to speak. He just needed to file the mental form. Form 7-C: Structural Safety Deficiency.

The hammers stopped.

The silence was louder than the noise.

The Architect jumped. He spun around, looking for the source of the halt. The construction drones froze in mid-air, their tools hovering over the half-finished railing.

A red text box appeared in the air above the mezzanine.

*Citation Issued. Violation: Baluster spacing non-compliant with Child Safety Protocol 4.1. Penalty: 10% reduction in Structural Integrity for adjacent zones. Correction Required within 30 Seconds.*

The Architect stared at the red text. He reached out, his fingers passing through it.

"Who? Who did this?"

Gerald couldn't answer.

But he felt a grim satisfaction. The citation wasn't just a stoppage.

It was a debuff.

The nearby support beams groaned. The System had applied the penalty immediately. The structural integrity of the entire second floor had dropped by ten percent.

The construction drones moved again. They adjusted the balusters.

Welding.

Grinding.

Filling the gaps.

Gerald watched the clock in his mind. Thirty seconds.

The drones worked fast. Too fast. They were rushing to beat the timer.

Gerald looked at the welding sparks. They were flying near a bundle of exposed electrical wiring that the drones had temporarily moved aside to get to the railing.

Electrical Code Section 12.5: Proximity of open flame to insulated conductors.

Violation.

Gerald filed Form 9-A. Electrical Hazard.

The red text box flashed again.

*Citation Issued. Violation: Open flame within six inches of insulated conductor. Penalty: 15% reduction in Electrical Capacity for Sector 2. Correction Required within 30 Seconds.*

The lights in the hall flickered. Dimmed. The construction drones sputtered, their power levels dropping.

The Architect looked up at the ceiling. His face went pale.

"The lights. Why are the lights dying?"

He ran to the main breaker box. He kicked it open.

Gerald watched him. The Architect was trying to fix the power, but the System had locked the breaker. The citation required a certified electrician's seal to override the penalty. The Architect was a dungeon core, not a licensed electrician.

The Architect pulled out his own tablet. He tapped frantically.

"Appeal.

File an appeal. Now.

"

He couldn't. The citation had been issued by a Passive Observer. The jurisdiction was already transferred. The System was running the show.

The drones stopped working. The mezzanine hung in the air, half-built, structurally compromised by Gerald's citations.

Gerald felt the strain of the debuffs. He was the dungeon. The 10% loss in structural integrity was his own bones feeling brittle. The 15% loss in electrical capacity was his own nervous system dimming.

He was hurting himself.

But the Architect was hurting more.

The woman—the Inspector General's liaison—stepped out of the shadows. She had been there the whole time, watching.

She looked at the frozen drones. She looked at the red citations. She looked at the Architect, who was now sweating, his hands shaking as he tried to manually rewire the breaker.

"Mr. Hutchins," she said. Her voice carried in the silence. "You are sabotaging your own recovery."

Gerald couldn't reply. But he thought it loudly. *I am ensuring compliance.*

The woman walked to the mezzanine. She picked up a loose baluster. She measured the gap with her thumb.

"Three and a half inches," she said. "You were right. It was non-compliant."

She dropped the baluster. It clattered on the floor.

"But you cited the electrical work while the drones were active. You didn't wait for them to clear the zone. You created a hazard."

She tapped her tablet.

"Correction Notice. Violation: Improper Citation Procedure. Penalty: Observer Status Revoked. Temporary Suspension of Core Authority."

Gerald's mind reeled.

The hook in his chest yanked. Not backward this time. Forward.

His vision snapped back into focus. The void vanished. He was in his body. He was in the dungeon.

He was on the floor.

Pain. Blinding, white-hot pain in his chest. The electrical debuff was still active. His muscles spasmed.

The Architect looked at him.

"You're back?"

Gerald coughed.

Blood sprayed from his mouth. He wiped it away with the back of his hand. He stood up.

His legs felt like wet cardboard.

"I'm back," Gerald said. His voice was a rasp.

The woman approached him. She held out a hand.

"You broke the rules, Gerald. You cited a work zone without stopping the work. That's a professional misconduct violation. I'm taking your license."

Gerald looked at her. He looked at the half-built mezzanine. He looked at the Architect, who was cowering behind a pillar.

"My license is not the point," Gerald said.

He looked at the System interface. His Core Authority was grayed out.

He couldn't spawn, and he couldn't build. He was just a man in a dungeon.

But he could still see.

And he could still talk.

"The Citation you issued," Gerald said, pointing at the red text box that still hovered over the electrical panel. "It says 'Correction Required.' It doesn't say 'Correction by Whom.'"

The woman frowned.

"Standard protocol requires a certified professional.

You are not a certified electrician.

I am not an electrician.

The Architect is not an electrician.

"

"Exactly," Gerald said.

He pointed to the entrance of the dungeon.

"Adventurers," Gerald said. "Level 12 Electrician Mage. He's been camping in my lobby for three hours because he thinks my dungeon is a high-level raid instance. He has a certification from the Ashford Trade Guild."

The woman's eyes widened.

"If you serve the Correction Notice to him," Gerald said, "he gets credit for the fix. He gets a boost to his Engineering stat. And you get your correction."

The woman stared at him.

"You're asking me to let an adventurer into the core zone?"

"I'm asking you to let a licensed professional enter the site," Gerald said. "That is code compliant."

The woman hesitated. The System interface on her tablet blinked.

*Correction Option Available: Delegate.*

She looked at Gerald. Then she looked at the door.

"If he fails," she said, "the entire structure collapses. And you die."

"If I don't cite him," Gerald said, "the debuffs stay. And I die anyway."

The woman tapped her tablet.

*Notification Sent. Correction Delegate: Active.*

Gerald turned toward the entrance. The heavy iron doors of the dungeon lobby groaned open.

Silence.

Then, footsteps. Heavy, armored boots.

An adventurer walked in. He was tall, wearing robes that sparked with residual magic. He held a staff that looked suspiciously like a multimeter.

He looked at Gerald. He looked at the woman. He looked at the frozen drones.

"Is this a puzzle?" the adventurer asked.

"No," Gerald said. "It's a job. Section 12.5. Fix it."

The adventurer grinned. He didn't understand the danger. He didn't understand that he was walking into a trap.

He walked toward the electrical panel.

Gerald watched him.

The adventurer touched the panel. Sparks flew.

The adventurer didn't flinch. He pulled a wire. He twisted it.

The lights flickered back on.

The red citation box vanished.

*Correction Accepted. Debuff Removed.*

Gerald felt his strength return. The pain in his chest faded. The hammers started up again, faster this time. The System was pleased.

The woman looked at Gerald.

"You used an adventurer as a tool."

"I used a resource," Gerald said. "That's what inspectors do."

The adventurer turned to leave, looking disappointed.

"No loot? No dragon?"

"You got a stat boost," Gerald said. "And you survived. That's rare."

The adventurer shrugged and walked out.

The woman closed her tablet.

"The Inspector General is going to hate this."

"The Inspector General likes compliance," Gerald said. "I gave him compliance."

The woman smiled. It was a thin, sharp smile.

"You have two minutes before the next Audit," she said. "Your Compliance Rating is 62. You are still failing.

And the neighboring dungeon, The Gilded Maw, is preparing a counter-attack. They are spawning a Breach Team.

They are coming through the wall.

"

Gerald looked at the west wall, and the bricks were vibrating.

"They're going to blow it open," the woman said. "They have explosives. High-yield.

They cited you for 'Vulnerable Perimeter' last week.

They're going to enforce that citation with force.

"

Gerald looked at the wall. He looked at the new, reinforced bricks.

He looked at the woman.

"I need a new citation," Gerald said.

"For what?"

"For them."

The woman laughed.

"You can't cite them. They aren't in your jurisdiction. They're in The Gilded Maw's jurisdiction."

"Not yet," Gerald said.

He looked at the wall. He looked at the cracks forming in the mortar.

"Explosives," Gerald said. "Section 15.2. Hazardous Materials Storage. If they bring explosives into a non-designated zone, that's a violation of the Inter-Dungeon Environmental Safety Act."

The woman stopped laughing.

"You're baiting them," she said.

"I'm waiting for them to cross the property line," Gerald said.

"Once the explosion starts, the debris crosses into my dungeon. That's a trespass. That's a hazard.

That's my jurisdiction.

"

"That's insane," the woman said. "The explosion will kill you."

"The explosion will be contained," Gerald said. "If I cite them for 'Improper Containment' before the blast wave hits, the System will force the explosion to direct its energy inward. Toward the source."

The woman stared at him.

"You're going to make them blow themselves up," she said.

"I'm going to cite them," Gerald said.

The wall buckled. A low rumble shook the floor. Dust fell from the ceiling.

The Architect screamed.

"They're here! They're breaching!"

Gerald didn't move. He stood in the center of the room. He closed his eyes.

He focused on the wall. He focused on the heat building up behind the bricks.

He filed Form 18-D. Hazardous Material Transport.

He didn't send it. Not yet.

He waited.

The wall exploded.

The shockwave hit Gerald like a physical blow. He was thrown backward. He hit the floor. His vision whited out.

Debris rained down. Brick. Mortar. Glass.

And then, the citation, and he had sent it. At the exact moment the debris crossed the threshold.

The System registered the intrusion. The System registered the explosives. The System registered the source.

*Violation Detected. Unauthorized Detonation. Penalty: Kinetic Redirection.*

The explosion reversed, and the fire did not come into Gerald's dungeon. The fire stayed on the other side of the wall.

But the force did not stop.

It pushed back. Into the tunnel. Into the Gilded Maw's corridor.

Gerald heard the scream. Not of the Architect. Of something else. Something large.

Silence.

Then, a new sound.

Not hammers.

Screams.

Gerald opened his eyes. He was buried in rubble. He couldn't move.

The woman stood over him. She was checking her tablet.

"You did it," she said. "You killed their Breach Team. And you triggered a Cascade Failure in their dungeon. Their Compliance Rating just dropped to 10."

Gerald tried to speak. He couldn't.

The woman leaned down.

"But Gerald," she said.

"The System just flagged you.

You didn't just cite them. You created a catastrophic structural failure in a neighboring zone.

That's a Class 1 Felony.

"

She looked at him.

"The Inspector General is here."

The air in the dungeon turned cold. The hammers stopped.

A door appeared in the middle of the room. It was white. Blindingly white.

It opened.

And something stepped through. Something that looked like a human, but made of pure, unblemished marble.

"Mr. Hutchins," the figure said. Its voice was the sound of a stamp hitting paper. "You are under arrest."

Gerald tried to stand. He couldn't.

The marble figure looked at the woman.

"Seize him."

The woman didn't move.

"I am the liaison," she said. "I do not arrest. I advise."

"You advised him to break the law," the marble figure said. "You are also under review."

The woman's face went pale.

Gerald looked at them both. He looked at the white door. He looked at the rubble.

He had one move left, and he looked at the marble figure.

"I appeal," Gerald said.

The marble figure paused.

"On what grounds?"

"Gross negligence," Gerald said. "Your automated contractors.

They built the mezzanine with non-compliant balusters, and they exposed wiring. They caused the conditions that required my citations. You failed the pre-audit.

"

The marble figure tilted its head.

"The System does not make mistakes," it said.

"The System makes mistakes when it is rushed," Gerald said.

"And you rushed it. Because you wanted this dungeon fixed by the next Audit cycle.

That's in the logs.

You can check."

The marble figure paused. The white door flickered.

Gerald felt a hook in his chest. Not to pull him back. To pull the truth out.

"Prove it," the marble figure said.

Gerald looked at the woman.

"Pull the logs," Gerald said. "Section 4, Subsection B. Timestamped contractor directives."

The woman's fingers flew across her tablet.

The marble figure watched her.

The seconds ticked by.

The woman looked up. Her eyes were wide.

"It's true," she whispered. "The System prioritized speed over safety. It violated its own Prime Directive."

The marble figure's marble face cracked, and a single fissure ran down its cheek.

"System Error," it said.

The white door dissolved.

Gerald felt a surge of power. The Core Authority returned. Not fully. But enough.

He stood up. He pushed the rubble off his chest.

"I win," Gerald said.

The marble figure looked at him. The fissure spread.

"You haven't won," the figure said. "You have merely reset the clock."

The figure vanished.

The white door disappeared.

The woman looked at Gerald. She looked at the cracked floor.

"You're going to be audited again," she said. "In one hour."

Gerald looked at the west wall. The breach was open. Smoke drifted in from the Gilded Maw.

"Then I better get ready," Gerald said.

He looked at the spawn list, and he had one rat. One leak. And three mold spores.

And now, he had a hole in the wall.

Gerald walked toward the breach.

"I'm going to go introduce myself," he said.

The woman grabbed his arm.

"Don't," she said. "That's a suicide run."

Gerald looked at her.

"I'm not going to fight," Gerald said.

He picked up a piece of brick from the floor.

"I'm going to measure it."
