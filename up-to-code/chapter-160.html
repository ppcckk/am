Chapter 160: Served on the System

# Chapter 160

Gerald dug his bare heels into the fractured floorboards, holding his position. It was crowded with the debris of condemned realities.

Gerald Hutchins stood in the center of the collapse. His feet were bare.

The wood of the floorboard he had carved lay scattered around him like shrapnel.

The symbol he had etched into the fabric of the world pulsed with a faint, bruised purple light. It was an unauthorized glyph. The System hated it.

The air around him tasted of ozone and copper, the metallic tang of a circuit breaker tripping under excessive load.

Above, the Void Core tower groaned. It did not fall.

It did not shatter.

It simply stopped. The twisting architecture of the rival dungeon froze in mid-rotation, suspended by the sheer audacity of Gerald's action. The

"REBEL" status hovered over his vision in jagged, glitching text.

It was bright red. It was illegal.

But the hole remained, which was a jagged tear in the sub-basement's reality, roughly four feet wide and extending infinitely downward.

From it, a sound emerged.

Not a roar. A low, rhythmic thrumming.

Like a heavy engine idling in a garage.

Gerald looked at the stamp in his hand. The ink pad was dry.

The blood was dried, and the symbol was carved. The action was complete. But the violation persisted.

"You cannot condemn a structure without a full assessment," Gerald said. His voice was steady, though his hands were not. "I need the blueprints."

The Void Core's voice returned, no longer confused. It was amused. Cold.

*"You are standing in my domain, Inspector. You have no jurisdiction here. You are a glitch in my code. A rounding error."*

"Jurisdiction is defined by presence," Gerald replied.

He took a step toward the hole. The ground beneath his foot buckled, not with physical matter, but with digital static.

Pixels flew off the floor, dissolving into the air.

"I am present. Therefore, I am inspecting."

He reached into his pocket. He had nothing.

No clipboard.

No pen.

Only the memory of where things were supposed to be.

Gerald closed his eyes. He visualized the grid. The System's hidden architecture.

Every dungeon had a structural skeleton, a compliance framework that held the dungeon's logic together. The Void Core had bypassed it.

It had used raw power to override the building codes, creating a dungeon that defied physics to dominate its neighbors.

That was a class 4 structural hazard, and he needed to see the bones.

He focused on the thrumming sound.

He listened to the rhythm. It was uneven.

There was a hitch in the third beat.

A stutter.

"Your support column is misaligned," Gerald said.

*"I have no columns. I have void."*

"Void has mass. Void has pressure. Your pressure differential is creating a shear force that will tear your foundation apart within three minutes."

The thrumming grew louder. A crack appeared in the frozen tower above.

Gerald opened his eyes. He looked at the hole. He needed a probe. He needed something to insert into the wound of the dungeon to measure its depth and integrity.

He looked at the broken floorboard. The splintered end. It was rough, jagged, and about six inches long.

Perfect, and he picked up the largest shard. It was heavy.

Dense.

It had absorbed some of the System's energy when he carved the symbol.

It hummed against his palm.

"This is a probe," Gerald stated. "I am initiating a core sample."

He didn't ask for permission. He didn't file a request. He stepped to the edge of the hole and thrust the wood shard into the darkness.

The air screamed.

The System flashed a warning in his peripheral vision.

**WARNING: UNAUTHORIZED PROBE.** **PENALTY: -15% INTELLECT STAT FOR 1 HOUR.**

Gerald ignored it. He held the shard steady. As it sank into the void, it glowed.

The wood absorbed the dark energy, turning a sickly green. The glow spread up the shard, illuminating the walls of the tear.

And there it was.

The structural skeleton of the Void Core, which was a mess.

Twisted beams of black light, crossing at irregular angles. No load paths. No redundancy.

Just raw, unchecked power held together by the Core's will.

But Gerald wasn't looking at the power. He was looking at the connections.

One joint, near the bottom of the visible shaft, was vibrating. A small, glowing node was pulsing irregularly. It was a junction box. An electrical node.

"Electrical code violation," Gerald muttered. "Exposed live wiring in a wet environment. Class A hazard."

The Void Core felt the intrusion.

The tower above shuddered. A wave of pressure pushed against Gerald, trying to shove him back. He dug his heels into the crumbling floor.

His knees bent. The shaking in his hands intensified, but he forced them to stop.

*"Stop that!"* the Core screamed. *"You are draining my essence!"*

"I am measuring your non-compliance," Gerald said through gritted teeth.

He twisted the shard.

The green glow intensified. The System recognized the action.

A new notification appeared.

**ACTION: DIAGNOSTIC PROBE.** **STATUS: SUCCESS.** **VIOLATION DETECTED: HIGH-VOLTAGE ARCING IN STRUCTURAL COLUMN.** **AUTOMATED CITATION AVAILABLE.**

Gerald's heart hammered against his ribs. He hadn't filed a citation.

The System had generated it, which was the loophole. The Inspector General's audit algorithm was always running, looking for easy targets. By exposing the violation, he had triggered an automatic review.

He needed to accept the citation. But accepting it required a target. The Void Core was too big. It was shielded by its own power.

But the violation was not in the tower. It was in the foundation. The base of the tower, deep below the library's sub-basement.

Gerald pulled the shard out. It was smoking now, burnt black at the tip. He dropped it on the floor. It hissed as it hit the stone.

The hole widened. The walls of the tear glowed with a harsh, white light. The void was destabilizing. The arc in the foundation was becoming a short circuit.

*"You are killing it!"* the Void Core shouted. *"You are destroying my dungeon!"*

"I am saving the public safety," Gerald said.

"If this arc grounds out, it will send a surge through the library's main power grid. The library will burn.

The city will burn.

Ashford will be a crater.

"

He pointed at the hole.

"Fix the ground," Gerald commanded. "Or I will escalate this to the Inspector General."

The Void Core hesitated. The tower above froze completely.

The amusement was gone.

Fear had replaced it.

The Core understood the stakes. If the surge hit the library, the resulting chaos would trigger a full System Lockdown.

The Core would be deleted.

*"What do you want?"* the Core asked. Its voice was smaller now. Tired.

"A temporary injunction," Gerald said. "I am issuing a Stop-Work Order.

The tower must cease all aggressive expansion.

The foundation must be repaired to code before you spawn a single new entity. This is a condition of your survival.

"

*"I cannot repair the foundation without losing power. My spawns rely on that energy."*

"Then your spawns die. Better a few mold spores than a city."

Gerald reached for the notification. **ACCEPT CITATION?**

He tapped it.

The air around him solidified. The static cleared. The red **REBEL** status flickered, then dimmed to a dull orange.

**STATUS: UNDER PROBATION.** **CITATION ISSUED: STRUCTURAL HAZARD (SEVERITY: CRITICAL).** **PENALTY TO VOID CORE: 50% REDUCTION IN SPAWN RATE FOR 24 HOURS.** **PENALTY TO GERALD HUTCHINS: ALL STAT BUFFS REMOVED FOR 12 HOURS.**

Gerald felt the weight leave his body.

The strength he had gained from building his own dungeon up to code—the fire resistance, the lightning resistance, the physical durability—vanished. He felt frail.

Old.

His knees ached. His back spasmed.

He was just a fifty-four-year-old man standing in a basement.

The Void Core obeyed. The tower above lowerred itself.

Not falling, but retracting.

The twisted architecture smoothed out, becoming less menacing, more... standard.

The aggressive spikes retracted. The dark energy receded, flowing back down into the foundation to repair the arc.

It was working. The System was enforcing the rule. Gerald had won.

But the cost was immediate.

Gerald swayed. His vision blurred. The lack of buffs left him exposed to the ambient magic of the dungeon environment.

The air was cold. Damp.

The mold spores he had spawned earlier—the only entities in his own dungeon—were reacting to the instability.

They were agitated, and a sound came from the corner of the room. A small, wet scuffle.

One of his mold spores had moved.

It had grown. The System's penalty had weakened Gerald's control over his own dungeon, allowing the entities to act on instinct.

The mold was hungry.

It sensed the weakened state of its Core.

Gerald turned. His legs felt like lead.

The mold spore was not a passive entity anymore. It was a mass of fuzzy grey growth, roughly the size of a tennis ball.

It was moving toward him.

Not attacking.

Assessing.

If the mold sensed weakness, it would consume the Core. That was how dungeons worked. If the Core died, the dungeon died. But if the Core was weak, the entities could take over.

Gerald had no stats. No resistance. No spawn controls.

He needed to reassert dominance. He needed to show the dungeon he was still the boss. But he had no weapons.

No spells. Only the memory of the stamp.

He looked at the floor. The symbol was still there. The carving. The unauthorized glyph.

The System had not deleted it. It had flagged it. But it was still there.

Gerald crawled toward it.

His elbows scraped against the rough concrete. Pain flared in his wrists. He reached the carving.

He ran his finger over the wood grain he had dragged into the stone.

He needed to activate it. But he had no mana. No energy.

He looked at his own hand. The shaking had stopped. His skin was pale. Blue veins showed through.

He bit his thumb. Hard.

The pain was sharp, clarifying. Blood welled up. Bright red.

He smeared the blood onto the carved symbol.

The System flashed a new message.

**ACTION: BLOOD OFFERING TO UNAUTHORIZED GLYPH.** **EFFECT: TEMPORARY REBELLION.** **WARNING: THIS ACTION VOIDED YOUR CITATION STATUS.**

The orange **PROBATION** status shattered. The red **REBEL** status returned, brighter than before.

The mold spore froze. It sensed the shift. The Core was no longer compliant. The Core was dangerous again.

But Gerald had bought time. The blood activated the glyph. The floor beneath the symbol glowed. A small pulse of energy, borrowed from the nearby Void Core's instability, flowed through the carving.

The mold spore hissed and retreated into the shadows.

Gerald lay on his back, staring at the ceiling. The library above was silent. The Void Core was repairing itself. The immediate threat was neutralized.

But he had made a fatal error.

By voiding the citation, he had voided the protection. The System no longer recognized him as an inspector. He was not under probation. He was a rogue variable.

And the Inspector General was always watching.

A new notification appeared. Not in red. In white. Pure, blinding white.

**SYSTEM ALERT: INSPECTOR GENERAL AUDIT INITIATED.** **SUBJECT: GERALD HUTCHINS.** **STATUS: UNLICENSED CORE / REBEL.** **ACTION: CONTAINMENT PROTOCOL.**

The floor beneath Gerald dissolved. Not into dust. Into grids.

Blue lines appeared in the air. A cage of light formed around him.

Gerald tried to stand. His muscles refused. The lack of buffs left him paralyzed by the sudden pressure of the containment field.

*"You have violated the sanctity of the Code,"* a voice said. It was not the Void Core.

It was smoother.

Calmer. The voice of the System itself, speaking through the Inspector General's avatar.

Gerald looked at the blue cage. He looked at the retreating Void Core tower.

He had saved the library. He had cited the tower.

But he had lost his license.

The cage tightened. The white light burned his eyes.

Gerald looked at his hands. Clean.

No blood.

No stamp.

No pen, and he had no way to appeal. No hearing scheduled. No witness.

The cage closed around his chest. The air was sucked out.

Gerald closed his eyes. He counted to three.

One.

Two.

Three, and he opened his eyes and looked at the Void Core's foundation, still visible through the dissolving floor. The repair was not finished. The arc was still there.

If the Inspector General executed him, the containment field would collapse. The energy release would destroy the library.

Gerald had one move left.

He had to break the cage. Not by force. By code.

He had to file a violation against the Inspector General, and he had to cite the System.

But to cite the System, he needed to be an inspector.

He needed to regain his status.

Gerald looked at the blue grid. He saw a flaw. A small gap in the light, where the grid intersected with the floor.

A point of zero thickness. A theoretical loophole in the rendering engine.

It was a three-millimeter gap.

Gerald took a breath. He held it.

He reached for the gap with his finger.

The light burned his skin. He didn't flinch.

He pushed.

The System froze.

**ERROR: INVALID INTERACTION.**

Gerald pushed harder. His finger slipped through the light. Into the void behind the cage.

He wasn't escaping. He was reaching for the Void Core's foundation.

He touched the black light of the arc.

And he grabbed it, and the pain was absolute.

The cage shattered.

Gerald screamed.

The Void Core's energy rushed into him. Not as power. As data.

The System flashed a new status.

**CORE INTEGRATION IN PROGRESS.** **WARNING: HOSTILE MERGE DETECTED.** **OUTCOME: FATAL.**

Gerald Hutchins did not scream. He watched the white light consume his vision.

He smiled, and he had just cited the Inspector General for exceeding its authority.

And he had served the citation on the whole damn system.

The citation was already in the System.
