Chapter 402: Self-Destruct Evacuation

# Chapter 402

The photo had uploaded before Mrs. Gable's thumb could find the trash icon.

Gerald watched the notification bar in his peripheral vision. A small, jagged icon pulsed in the upper right corner of his consciousness. *External Media Alert: Viral Exposure Detected.*

He didn't look at the librarian. He looked at the hole in the wall.

The rat's body had fused with the drywall, sealing the draft, but the masonry was still cooling.

If he touched it now, his hand would burn. If he touched it later, the structural integrity would be compromised by the thermal shock.

He needed to reinforce it.

But he needed to survive the next thirty seconds first.

The air in the sub-basement changed. The static hum of the fluorescent lights dropped in pitch, vibrating against Gerald's teeth. Dust motes stopped falling and hung suspended in the stagnant air.

*Hunter Protocol Initiated.*

The words flashed in red, bold text across his vision. They overlapped the damp patches on the concrete, obscuring the mold counts.

Gerald straightened his vest, and he adjusted his tie. He pulled his clipboard from the air pocket where he usually stored his pen, though today it was empty.

He had no paper.

He had no pen. He had only the System, and the System was currently treating him like a condemned structure.

A shadow detached itself from the far corner of the room. It didn't walk. It flowed.

The figure was clad in armor made of blackened bone and polished iron. No faceplate.

Just a hollow void where eyes should be, glowing with a faint, sickly green light.

It carried a spear that dripped with a viscous, black liquid.

Acid.

Gerald checked his HP. It hadn't changed. He was still at full health.

He was still 47% compliant. He was still a 4,000-square-foot sub-basement with a leaking roof.

"Identify," Gerald said. His voice didn't shake. It projected. It carried the flat, nasal tone of a man who had denied building permits to developers with more money than God.

The Hunter tilted its head. The sound was like grinding stones.

"Core Signature: Irregular," the Hunter rasped.

Its voice was layered, a chorus of dead things speaking in unison.

"Target: Non-Standard. Threat Level: Unknown.

Action: Purge."

Gerald raised one hand. Palm out. Stop sign.

"Objection," Gerald said.

The Hunter lunged.

It crossed twenty feet in a blink. The spear tip aimed for Gerald's chest. Not his physical chest, which was currently a metaphysical abstraction located near the north wall, but his core.

Gerald didn't dodge. He couldn't. He didn't have legs. He had a foundation.

He activated the only defense he had installed yesterday: Section 4.2.1, Load-Bearing Reinforcement.

A sheet of translucent blue energy snapped into existence between him and the spear. It was thin. Fragile. It looked like a pane of glass.

The spear hit it.

The glass shattered. But it didn't break instantly.

It cracked.

The force of the impact was distributed across the entire surface area of the wall behind Gerald. The concrete groaned. A spiderweb fracture appeared in the masonry, spreading out from the point of impact.

Gerald winced. That hurt. That hurt more than the spear.

He checked the damage report.

**[Damage Taken: 15 points. Structural Integrity: 98%.]**

The Hunter pulled the spear back, ready to strike again. It was faster than Gerald.

Stronger than Gerald.

But it was loud.

And it was making a mess.

Gerald looked at the cracks in the wall. He looked at the dust settling on the floor. He looked at the Hunter, who was now preparing a second strike, gathering dark energy in its chest.

"Violation," Gerald said.

The Hunter paused. Just a fraction of a second. Confusion?

No. Calculation.

"Define," the Hunter said.

"Uncontrolled debris accumulation," Gerald said.

He pointed at the dust.

"And unauthorized structural modification. You have compromised the load-bearing capacity of Wall C-4.

This is a Class A Safety Hazard.

"

The Hunter ignored him. It thrust the spear again.

This time, Gerald didn't use a shield. He used a citation.

He reached into the System menu with his mind. He didn't have a physical form to write with, so he thought the words.

**[Citation Generated: Code Violation 104. Improper Disposal of Hazardous Materials.]** **[Subject: The Hunter.]** **[Fine: 500 Compliance Points.]** **[Penalty: Temporary Stun (Duration: 5 seconds).]**

The System hesitated.

**[Error: Target is not a registered Dungeon Core. Cannot issue Citation.]**

Gerald blinked. He hadn't known that. He had assumed all entities in the System were subject to municipal code. He had been wrong.

The spear was two inches from his core.

Gerald panicked. Not with fear, but with the frantic, desperate energy of an inspector who realizes he has forgotten his ruler and must improvise with a yardstick.

He looked around the room. The library's sub-basement was cluttered.

Old archives.

Broken chairs. A water heater.

The water heater, which was a sixty-gallon tank, installed in 1998. It was rusted.

It was leaking.

It was, technically, the only thing in the room that was not part of Gerald's dungeon footprint.

It was municipal property.

It was also under pressure.

Gerald didn't attack the Hunter. He attacked the water heater.

He focused his will on the pressure release valve. He didn't pull it. He *inspected* it. He mentally filed a Form 11-B for Improper Ventilation on the tank.

**[Action: Vent Valve Release.]**

The valve popped. A jet of scalding, pressurized water shot out from the top of the tank. It wasn't aimed at the Hunter. It was aimed at the ceiling.

But Gerald had calculated the trajectory. Or rather, he had remembered the building plans he had memorized in 1998. The sprinkler head was directly above the tank.

The water hit the heat sensor of the sprinkler system.

The system triggered.

Water rained down from the ceiling. Not clean water. Black, stagnant water that had been sitting in the pipes for three years. It splashed over the Hunter.

The Hunter shrieked. The liquid hissed. The acid on its spear neutralized, turning into a foamy sludge that ran down the shaft. The bone armor steamed.

The Hunter slipped. The wet floor had zero traction. Its iron-shod boots skidded on the slick concrete.

It fell backward.

Gerald stood there, soaked. He didn't care. He was checking the damage.

**[System Alert: Environmental Hazard Detected. High Humidity.]** **[Compliance Rating Adjustment: -5 points for Water Damage.]** **[Compliance Rating Adjustment: +10 points for Automatic Fire Suppression Activation (Malfunctioning but Functional).]**

Net gain: +5.

The Hunter scrambled to its feet. It was angry now. The green light in its eyes flared brighter.

"Insolent," it growled. "You play with plumbing. You are no threat."

"I am an inspector," Gerald said. "And you are in violation of the Noise Ordinance."

The Hunter raised its spear. This time, it didn't thrust. It swung. A wide, horizontal arc designed to cleave stone.

Gerald couldn't dodge. He couldn't block.

He had one option left. The only thing he had built himself. The handrails.

He had installed handrails along the main corridor. Steel. Powder-coated. Compliant with ADA regulations for slope and height.

He willed them to move, and they didn't move physically. But he could *activate* them.

**[System Mechanic: Trap Activation. Type: Obstacle. Material: Steel.]**

The handrails rose from the walls, extending into the hallway like spears of metal. They weren't sharp. They were blunt. They were heavy.

The Hunter's swing hit the extended handrail.

*Clang.*

The sound was deafening. The vibration traveled through the floor, up through Gerald's foundation.

The Hunter's spear stopped dead. The momentum was arrested. The Hunter's arm jerked back. It lost its balance.

Gerald saw the opening, and he didn't have an attack spell. He didn't have a monster. He had a clipboard.

He focused on the Hunter's feet. The mud on the boots. The dirt tracked in from the surface.

**[Citation Generated: Code Violation 201. Failure to Maintain Cleanliness Standards in Public Right-of-Way.]** **[Subject: The Hunter.]** **[Note: Target is not a Core. Citation Rejected.]**

Damn it.

Gerald tried again. He needed a different angle.

He looked at the Hunter's armor. It was made of bone and iron. No gaps.

No joints visible. Sealed tight.

Airtight.

Gerald checked the air quality in the room. He had been fined for inadequate ventilation. The CO2 levels were rising. The water had stopped flowing, but the dampness remained.

If the Hunter was sealed tight, it couldn't breathe.

Not because Gerald could suffocate it. But because the System enforced environmental standards. If the environment was toxic, the entity suffered.

But the air wasn't toxic yet. It was just damp.

Unless.

Unless Gerald made it toxic, and he looked at the mold spores.

Three of them. Tiny, pathetic things. He had been ignoring them because they were non-compliant.

They were unlicensed biological entities, and he had never cited them. He had just ignored them.

Now, he needed them.

Gerald focused on the mold. He didn't attack. He *licensed* them.

**[Action: Grant Temporary Operating Permit to Biological Entity #1.]** **[Condition: Must spread within 10 seconds.]**

The System flagged the action.

**[Warning: This action creates a Class B Biohazard. Compliance Penalty will apply after event.]**

Gerald ignored the warning.

The mold spores woke up. They expanded. They multiplied. They drifted toward the Hunter, drawn to the moisture and the warmth of the bone armor.

The Hunter swiped at the air.

"Filth!"

But the spores were too small. They settled into the joints of the armor. Into the seams of the helmet.

The Hunter coughed. It was a dry, rattling sound.

"Poison?" the Hunter gasped.

"Spores," Gerald corrected. "Unlicensed. But currently permitted under emergency sanitation override."

The Hunter staggered. It dropped the spear. It clawed at its faceplate.

It was trying to get air.

Gerald watched. He didn't smile. He checked his watch.

The audit was in forty-five minutes. The knight was coming. The photo was viral.

The Hunter was falling.

It hit the floor with a crash of metal and bone. It twitched. The green light in its eyes flickered and dimmed.

Gerald exhaled. He hadn't breathed in five minutes.

He looked at the body.

**[Target Neutralized.]** **[Loot: Iron Boneplate (Damaged). Acid Spear (Rusted). Hunter's Emblem (Shattered).]**

He didn't want the loot. He wanted the body gone.

He activated his cleanup protocol.

The water from the sprinklers had pooled. He directed the flow. He used the slope of the floor, the 1% grade he had installed to prevent flooding, to guide the water and the sludge toward the drain.

The Hunter's body slid. It went down the drain.

The drain clogged.

Of course it did.

Gerald stared at the clogged drain. He felt a migraine building behind his eyes.

He had saved the dungeon. He had killed the Hunter. He had gained a few Compliance points from the sprinkler activation.

But he had created a new violation.

**[System Alert: Drainage Obstruction Detected.]** **[Compliance Rating: 42/100.]** **[Status: Critical.]**

The rating dropped. The debuffs hit him.

**[Debuff: Moisture Buildup. Regen Rate -20%.]**

He felt weak. He felt heavy. The walls of his dungeon groaned under the weight of the water that wasn't draining.

And then, the phone rang.

Not in the room. In his mind.

A notification.

**[Incoming Call: System Inspector General.]**

Gerald froze, and he hadn't expected this. He hadn't expected a call.

He expected an audit.

He expected a letter. He didn't expect a phone call.

He answered.

"Mr. Hutchins," a voice said.

It was smooth.

Calm.

Terrifyingly polite.

"This is Inspector Vane.

I believe you have made a mess.

"

Gerald looked at the hole in the wall. He looked at the water pooling around his base.

"I am currently addressing the obstruction," Gerald said. His voice was steady.

"Are you?" Vane asked. "Because my sensors indicate that you have just destroyed a Level 4 Elite Spawn belonging to the Gilded Cage. And you have triggered a viral event that has alerted every adventurer within a ten-mile radius to your location."

Gerald looked at the System map.

Red dots. Dozens of them. Moving toward him. Fast.

Adventurers. Parties. Heroes.

They weren't coming to fight him. They were coming to loot him. They had seen the photo. They had seen the

"High Difficulty" tag that the viral post had auto-generated.

They thought he was a boss.

"He is a boss," one of the nearby parties was saying. Gerald could hear them through the walls.

"Look at the damage.

That water heater? That's a trap. That Hunter?

That's a summon.

This guy is legit.

"

"He's got a high-tier lair," another voice said. "Look at the handrails. That's custom work. No low-level core has custom architecture."

Gerald looked at his handrails, and he had built them because the code required them.

Now, they were his reputation.

**[New Quest: Reputation Defense.]** **[Objective: Survive the first wave of Adventurer Parties.]** **[Reward: Permanent Title: "Code-Master."]** **[Failure Penalty: Demolition.]**

The door at the top of the stairs creaked open.

Footsteps. Heavy boots. Clanging armor.

Gerald looked at the water. It was still clogged. He couldn't fix it. He didn't have time.

He looked at the mold. It was still growing.

He looked at the Hunter's emblem, lying in the corner, and he picked it up. It was heavy. It was shattered.

He had one tool left.

He opened the System menu. He scrolled past the Combat tab. He scrolled past the Construction tab.

He went to the Appeals tab.

**[Submit Appeal: Dispute of Viral Status.]** **[Grounds: False Advertising. The Dungeon is a Sub-Basement. It is not a Boss Lair.]** **[Risk: If Appeal Fails, Compliance Rating Reduced by 10 Points.]**

Gerald looked at the stairs, and the first adventurer was coming down. A paladin. Shining armor.

Bright sword.

Gerald submitted the appeal.

The System processed it.

**[Appeal Denied.]** **[Reason: The Dungeon contains Custom Architecture and Elite Spawns. Classification: Boss Lair.]** **[Penalty Applied: Compliance Rating -10.]** **[Debuff: Structural Weakness (All walls take +50% damage).]**

The walls groaned. Cracks appeared in the foundation.

Gerald looked at the paladin, who had just reached the bottom of the stairs.

The paladin raised his sword.

"Prepare to die, foul beast!"

Gerald looked at the cracked wall. He looked at the clogged drain. He looked at the rising water.

He smiled.

"Section 8.1," Gerald said. "Emergency Evacuation."

He didn't mean the adventurers, and he meant himself.

He activated the self-destruct sequence for the water heater.

The explosion was small. But it was enough to blow the floor out.

Gerald fell.
