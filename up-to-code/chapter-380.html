Chapter 380: Subsection D

# Chapter 380

The clipboard was gone.

Gerald patted his chest, then his waistband, then the empty air where the clipboard had been a moment ago. His fingers found only the damp fabric of his inspection vest. The rat lay on the concrete floor, twitching its nose at a droplet of blood that had fallen from Gerald's shredded hand.

"Subsection D," Gerald said, his voice flat and dry. "We do not have a lawyer. We have a rat."

The rat stood up. It shook itself with the vigor of a small engine, scattering dust and dried blood. One eye remained swollen shut; the other stared at Gerald with profound bureaucratic disdain.

"The rat is not a licensed legal representative," Gerald continued. "The System requires a Core or a registered party of interest to file a Form 12-C, Notice of Intent to Appeal. You are neither."

The rat chittered. A tiny, translucent text box hovered above its head.

**STATUS: COMPLIANT CITIZEN OF SUB-BASEMENT 4.** **ROLE: UNLICENSED OBSERVER.** **LEGAL STANDING: ZERO.**

"Exactly," Gerald said. He looked at the cracked door.

The splintered wood still hung in the frame, a jagged mouth that revealed the darkness beyond.

The air from that darkness smelled of ozone and rot. It was the smell of a dungeon that had cut corners on its ventilation shafts.

Gerald walked toward the door. His knee clicked, a sharp sound in the silent room. He stopped. He looked back at the rat.

"If the Inspector General arrives before we can file the appeal, I am subject to Summary Revocation."

"Revocation means you stop being a Core," the rat said. Its voice was a gravelly squeak that the System translated into standard subtitles at the bottom of Gerald's vision. "It means you become a pile of dirt. A pile of dirt cannot cite."

"I am not a pile of dirt," Gerald said. "I am a municipal inspector."

"You are a fraud," the rat said. "You cited a ghost. That is not code."

"It was a spectral entity occupying a load-bearing void," Gerald corrected. "The void was unstable. The citation was valid."

"The IG doesn't care about your logic. The IG cares about precedent. And you just set a precedent that ghosts are people."

Gerald stared at the rat. For the first time in thirty-one years, he felt a physical sensation that was not indigestion or fatigue. It was panic. It tasted like copper in the back of his throat.

He looked at his wrist. The skin was raw, the tendons visible beneath the torn flesh. The pain was distant, muted by the adrenaline of survival. But the clock was ticking.

**SYSTEM ALERT: INSPECTOR GENERAL AUDIT SCHEDULED.** **TIME REMAINING: 14 HOURS 12 MINUTES.**

Fourteen hours, and he could not build a legal defense in fourteen hours. He could not hire a lawyer.

He could not bribe the IG, because the IG did not accept currency.

The IG accepted order.

And Gerald had broken order.

He looked at the broken door again. The man—the ghost—had vanished.

But the damage remained, and the door was non-compliant.

Egress width was insufficient. The latch mechanism was fused.

Gerald walked to the door. He pulled a pen from his vest pocket. It was a cheap ballpoint, the kind issued to employees who were expected to lose them.

He clicked it. The tip was dried out.

"Rat," Gerald said. "Fetch the tape measure."

The rat did not move.

"I am tired."

"Fetch the tape measure."

The rat sighed, a sound of pure bureaucratic exhaustion. It scurried into a pile of debris in the corner. It emerged dragging a twenty-foot steel tape measure. The metal was bent, but the hook at the end was intact.

Gerald took the tape. He walked to the broken door. He measured the gap between the jamb and the floor.

Two inches, and he measured the height of the opening.

Six feet, four inches.

He measured the width of the remaining sliver of door.

Fourteen inches.

"Non-compliant," Gerald muttered. "Egress capacity reduced by sixty percent. Fire load calculation invalid."

He wrote the numbers on the only surface available: the inside of his own wrist guard, scratched into the plastic with the tip of the ballpoint pen.

**DOOR VIOLATION 1: WIDTH.** **DOOR VIOLATION 2: HEIGHT.** **DOOR VIOLATION 3: MATERIAL INTEGRITY.**

Three violations, and he looked at the rat.

"Do you know the appeal process for Summary Revocation?"

The rat blinked.

"I know the appeal process for a noise complaint. I know the appeal process for a parking ticket. I do not know the appeal process for existential erasure."

"Then we improvise," Gerald said.

The rat froze.

"No. You do not improvise. You said you had improvised.

That was the mistake.

"

"We need a new violation," Gerald said. "To overwrite the old one."

The rat tilted its head.

"What?"

"The Inspector General is auditing my unconventional citation methods. To cancel the audit, I must demonstrate that my methods are not unconventional.

They are standard, and they are boring. They are the most boring thing in the System.

"

"How?"

"By citing myself."

The rat's single good eye went wide.

"You want to cite your own dungeon?"

"I want to cite the door," Gerald said. "The door is mine.

The damage is mine, and the violation is mine.

If I issue a self-citation, it is not a fraud. It is self-regulation.

It is the highest form of compliance.

"

The rat stared at him. Then it looked at the broken door. Then back at Gerald.

"That is technically legal," the rat said. "Subsection 9, Paragraph 4: Self-Reporting Violations. It is... rare."

"It is elegant," Gerald said.

"It is stupid," the rat said. "If you cite yourself, you take the debuff.

You get the stat penalty.

And the IG will still come. But they will come as an auditor, not an executioner.

"

"Will they?"

"The IG hates two things: chaos and fraud.

Self-citation is neither. It is bureaucracy.

The IG loves bureaucracy.

They will respect you for it.

"

Gerald felt the panic in his throat subside, replaced by a cold, familiar clarity. This was work. This was paper. This was procedure.

He turned to the door. He raised his pen.

**FORM 11-A: SELF-CITATION NOTICE.** **ENTITY: DUNGEON CORE 8492 (G. HUTCHINS).** **LOCATION: SUB-BASEMENT 4, DOOR 7.** **VIOLATION: NON-COMPLIANT EGRESS STRUCTURE.** **SEVERITY: MINOR.** **PENALTY: TEMPORARY PHYSICAL DAMAGE RESISTANCE REDUCTION (10%).**

Gerald wrote the citation. His hand shook. The pen scratched against the air, leaving glowing red text that hovered in front of the broken door.

**CITATION 4098-S ISSUED.** **STATUS: PENDING APPROVAL.**

The System hesitated. It had never seen a Core cite itself. The algorithm did not know what to do.

"Approve it," Gerald said to the air. "It is within your authority."

The text flickered.

**REASON FOR PENDING: UNUSUAL PATTERN DETECTED.** **QUERY: WHY CITE SELF?**

Gerald looked at the rat. The rat shrugged.

"Answer: To maintain order," Gerald said.

The text pulsed.

**APPROVED.** **STATUS: ACCEPTED.** **COMPLIANCE RATING ADJUSTMENT: -2.** **NEW RATING: 50/100.** **DEBUFF APPLIED: PHYSICAL DAMAGE RESISTANCE REDUCED BY 10% FOR 24 HOURS.**

Gerald felt a sudden lightness in his bones. He looked at his wrist. The bleeding had slowed. The pain was sharper now, unmasked by the resistance.

**SYSTEM ALERT: INSPECTOR GENERAL AUDIT STATUS UPDATED.** **CHANGE FROM: FRAUD INVESTIGATION.** **TO: ROUTINE COMPLIANCE REVIEW.** **TIME REMAINING: 14 HOURS 10 MINUTES.**

The rat let out a long breath.

"It worked."

"It worked," Gerald said. He picked up the tape measure. "Now we fix the door."

"We don't have wood," the rat said. "We don't have nails. We are in a sub-basement. We have rats and mold."

"We have rubble," Gerald said. He pointed to the pile of debris where the rat had been hiding.

"Concrete.

Rebar.

Dust."

"You want to build a door out of rubble?"

"I want to reduce the violation severity from Minor to Negligible," Gerald said. "If I patch the gap, the egress width improves. If the egress width improves, the penalty decreases. If the penalty decreases, the IG has less reason to scrutinize my methods."

The rat looked at the pile of rubble. It looked at Gerald's shaking hands.

"You can't lift concrete," the rat said. "You have no strength stats."

"I don't need to lift it," Gerald said. "I need to arrange it."

He walked to the pile. He did not pick up a chunk of concrete. Instead, he used the tape measure as a lever.

He wedged it under a slab of broken floor tile. He pulled.

The tile shifted, and he tried again. He used the momentum, not his strength. He used the physics of the environment. He treated the dungeon as a construction site, not a combat zone.

For the next three hours, Gerald worked.

He did not fight. He did not cast spells. He moved debris.

He measured gaps, and he filled spaces with dust and rat droppings, packing them tight with his bare hands. The pain in his wrist was a constant companion.

His back ached.

His vision blurred.

The rat watched. It did not help, but it did not mock. It offered small criticisms.

"That corner is uneven," the rat said.

"It is compliant," Gerald replied, wiping sweat from his eyes with a bloody sleeve.

"It is ugly."

"Code does not require beauty. It requires function."

At the fourth hour, the door was no longer a gaping hole. It was a lumpy, uneven barrier of packed earth and concrete shards.

It was not pretty, which was not strong.

But it was solid.

Gerald measured it.

**EGRESS WIDTH: 32 INCHES.** **STATUS: MINIMUM COMPLIANCE.**

He raised his pen again.

**FORM 11-B: REMEDIATION REPORT.** **ACTION: STRUCTURAL REINFORCEMENT.** **STATUS: IN PROGRESS.**

The System pinged.

**VIOLATION 4098-S UPDATED.** **SEVERITY: NEGLIGIBLE.** **DEBUFF REMOVED.**

The lightness in his bones returned. The pain faded to a dull throb.

Gerald sat down on the floor. He leaned against the patched door. It held.

The rat climbed onto his shoulder. It looked tired.

"You are insane," the rat said. "But you are compliant."

"I am thorough," Gerald said.

He looked at the notification panel.

**SYSTEM ALERT: INSPECTOR GENERAL AUDIT SCHEDULED.** **TIME REMAINING: 10 HOURS 04 MINUTES.** **SUBJECT: ROUTINE COMPLIANCE REVIEW.** **PREPARATION: REQUIRED.**

Gerald closed his eyes. He had bought time. He had shifted the category of the threat from Criminal to Administrative.

That was a victory. It was not the victory he wanted, but it was the victory he could manage.

But there was something else.

When he had patched the door, he had felt something. A connection. The dungeon had responded to the repair. It had not just accepted the change; it had *rewarded* it.

**SYSTEM NOTIFICATION: STRUCTURAL CODE COMPLIANCE INCREASED.** **PASSIVE BONUS: PHYSICAL DAMAGE RESISTANCE +5%.**

He had fixed the door. He had cited the door. He had remediated the door. And now the dungeon was stronger because of it.

Gerald opened his eyes. He looked at the rat.

"The System rewards correct procedure," Gerald said.

"It punishes incorrect procedure," the rat said.

"No," Gerald said.

"It rewards it. I have been treating compliance as a shield. As a way to survive.

But it is not a shield.

It is a sword."

The rat frowned.

"What?"

"If I make the dungeon *perfectly* compliant," Gerald said, "I do not just avoid debuffs. I gain buffs. I gain resistances. I gain power."

The rat looked at the patched door. It looked at the dark corners of the room.

"Where do you get the materials?" the rat asked. "You have no resources. No mana. No crafting stations."

Gerald looked at the shadows in the room. The shadows were not empty. There were things in them. Things that had been hiding from the light.

"We don't buy materials," Gerald said. "We harvest them."

"Harvest what?"

"Violations."

Gerald stood up. His legs were shaky, but he was steady. He picked up the clipboard. It was gone, but he held it anyway, his fingers gripping the air.

"The neighboring dungeon," Gerald said. "Core 8493. The one with the fire exits."

"The one you cited yesterday," the rat said. "It is dead."

"No," Gerald said. "It is cited. Cited is not dead. Cited is vulnerable."

Gerald walked toward the broken door. Not the one he had patched, but the one leading out into the main corridor.

"I need to go to work," Gerald said.

"It is your dungeon," the rat said. "You own it."

"I own the space," Gerald said. "I do not own the rules. The rules are the resource. And I am going to mine them."

He stepped through the patched door. The concrete dust crunched under his feet.

The corridor beyond was dark. But the darkness was not empty. It was waiting.

And Gerald had a clipboard in his mind.

**SYSTEM ALERT: NEW OPPORTUNITY DETECTED.** **TARGET: NEIGHBORING DUNGEON CORE 8493.** **STATUS: CITATION PENDING HEARING.** **OPPORTUNITY: INTERVENTION AUDIT.** **WARNING: ENTRY REQUIRES PHYSICAL PRESENCE.**

Gerald smiled. It was not a happy smile. It was the smile of a man who has found a loophole in a tax code.

"Rat," he said. "Bring the tape measure. We have a hearing to attend."

The rat did not move. It stared at Gerald.

"You cannot enter another Core's dungeon without an invitation," the rat said. "That is trespassing. That is a Class A Violation."

"Not if I am invited," Gerald said.

He pointed to the floor. A trail of scorched earth led from the broken door into the darkness.

"That fire mark," Gerald said. "It is not from a spell. It is from a structural failure. The heat vent in the ceiling above us is leaking into their hallway."

The rat blinked.

"So?"

"It means their hallway is touching our ceiling," Gerald said. "Shared infrastructure."

"That is... obscure."

"It is code," Gerald said. "Section 4, Paragraph 2: Maintenance of Shared Infrastructure. Inspectors have the right to enter shared spaces for emergency repair."

The rat's eye widened.

"You are going to walk into a rival dungeon because you fixed your own ceiling?"

"I am going to walk into a rival dungeon to inspect their ventilation," Gerald said. "And I am going to cite them for the heat leak."

"And then what?"

"And then I serve the citation," Gerald said. "In person."

He stepped into the dark.

The air changed. It grew hot. Smoky.

From the shadows, a voice emerged. It was smooth, oily, and laced with anger.

"Who dares enter my domain?"

Gerald did not answer. He clicked his pen. He raised his hand. He pointed at the ceiling, where a crack in the plaster glowed orange.

**CITATION 4099-A DRAFTED.** **VIOLATION: UNAUTHORIZED THERMAL TRANSFER VIA SHARED STRUCTURE.**

The shadow coalesced into a figure. A woman, made of flame and smoke. Her eyes were burning coals.

"You are a bug," she said.

Gerald looked at her. He looked at the crack in the ceiling. He looked at the tape measure in his hand.

"I am an inspector," Gerald said. "And you are out of code."

The woman laughed. It sounded like cracking wood.

"Come then, little man," she said. "Try to serve me."

Gerald took a step forward.

The floor beneath him gave way.
