Chapter 315: Terminated Status

# Chapter 315

The floor did not collapse. It dissolved.

One moment, Gerald stood on the polished obsidian tiles of the Archive's central atrium. The next, the solid ground beneath his feet turned into a churning gray mist. Gravity inverted for a split second, pulling him toward the ceiling, before snapping back down with the force of a hydraulic press slamming into asphalt.

He landed on his hands and knees. The impact jarred his shoulders, a familiar sensation, like dropping a clipboard on a concrete floor.

Dust motes danced in the air.

Not normal dust. These particles glowed with a faint, sickly amber light.

Gerald pushed himself up.

His knees ached. He checked his inventory.

The clipboard was gone.

His pen was gone. His badge, the heavy brass one he'd worn for thirty-one years, was also missing.

Only his body remained. And the smell.

It smelled of ozone and wet rot.

"The Curator," Gerald said. His voice sounded thin, swallowed by the vastness of the space. "Explain the jurisdictional transfer."

Silence answered him.

Gerald spun in a circle. The Archive was gone.

The high vaulted ceilings, the floating manuscripts, the porcelain-faced Curator—all vanished.

In their place stood a corridor, which was narrow, barely four feet wide, with walls made of jagged, unpolished stone. The ceiling was low, sloping down aggressively.

A red light pulsed above him. Not a lamp. A warning beacon.

Gerald looked up. The ceiling height was under seven feet.

"Violation," he muttered. "Non-compliant vertical clearance in a primary egress route."

He tried to stand fully upright, but his head brushed the stone. He flinched. The contact sent a jolt of static electricity through his skull.

**[WARNING: CEILING HEIGHT BELOW MINIMUM CODE STANDARD (7'0

"). STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY COMPROMISED. ELECTROSTATIC DISCHARGE RISK: HIGH.]**

The text appeared in his vision, glowing in a harsh, bureaucratic orange.

Gerald stared at the message. He didn't panic. He didn't scream.

He squinted, trying to read the fine print beneath the warning. His eyes narrowed.

The font was tiny, cramped, and intentionally difficult to read.

Classic municipal obstructionism.

"I need a ladder," he said to the empty air. "And a tape measure."

No ladder appeared.

No tape measure. He had no tools.

He had no status menu, and he had no dungeon core interface. He was just a man in a hallway that violated three separate fire safety ordinances.

Footsteps echoed behind him. Slow, heavy, and wet.

Gerald turned.

A creature emerged from the shadows at the far end of the corridor. It was humanoid, but wrong.

Its skin was the color of bruised plums, slick with mucus.

It held a rusted cleaver in one hand.

In the other, it held a half-eaten human arm.

The creature paused. It sniffed the air. It did not look at Gerald.

It looked at the ceiling. It looked at the floor.

It looked at the cracks in the walls.

Then it looked at Gerald.

"Fresh meat," the creature gurgled. Its voice sounded like gravel grinding in a blender.

Gerald ignored the threat. He walked toward the creature, keeping his posture rigid. He walked into the center of the corridor.

He stopped, and he looked at the floor beneath his feet.

"Where is the slip-resistant coating?" Gerald asked.

The creature tilted its head.

"What?"

"The flooring," Gerald said, pointing at the wet stone.

"It's slick. There's no drainage channel. You're tracking biological waste into a non-sanitized zone.

This is a health code violation. It's also a liability nightmare.

"

The creature laughed. A wet, hacking sound. It stepped forward, raising the cleaver.

Gerald didn't move, and he didn't dodge.

He didn't raise his hands. He pulled out a small, crumpled piece of paper from his pocket.

It was the only thing he had saved from the Archive. A receipt for the permit.

He held it up.

"By accepting the liability of the sector, I am assuming the role of Site Manager," Gerald said. His voice was flat, monotone. "As Site Manager, I have the authority to cite hazards. And you, sir, are a hazard."

The creature lunged.

Gerald didn't flinch. He simply pointed at the wall to the creature's left.

"Exit sign missing," he said.

Nothing happened. The cleaver swung toward his neck.

Gerald closed his eyes. He calculated the distance. He calculated the swing arc. He calculated the angle of the ceiling.

The cleaver missed his neck by an inch.

The creature stumbled. Its foot slipped on the wet stone.

Gerald opened his eyes.

"You're running," Gerald said. "In a corridor with no handrails.

You're carrying a sharp object.

You're in a wet environment. You are violating multiple OSHA regulations.

"

He tapped the receipt against his palm.

"I am filing a formal complaint."

The air around him shimmered. The orange warning text in his vision changed. It turned red.

**[COMPLIANCE RATING: 12/100]** **[STATUS: NON-COMPLIANT STRUCTURE]** **[ACTION: INITIATE AUDIT]**

The walls of the corridor groaned. The stone cracked. Dust fell from the ceiling.

The creature froze. It looked at the walls, confused. It looked at Gerald, angry. It raised the cleaver again.

Gerald looked at the ceiling. He saw a small fissure. A crack running from the north wall to the south. He saw a drip of water falling from it.

He walked to the wall. He placed his hand on the stone.

He didn't push. He didn't pull. He just touched it.

"Load-bearing failure imminent," he said. "Support columns are missing. The tensile strength of the surrounding rock is insufficient to support the upper level. This structure is condemned."

The creature screamed. It charged.

Gerald didn't move. He waited.

The creature's foot hit the wet spot. It slipped. It fell forward, crashing into the wall Gerald had touched.

The impact was minor. A soft thud.

But Gerald had measured it, and he had calculated the structural integrity of the Archive's sub-basement before he was transported. He had memorized the blueprints of the sector he had signed for.

He knew where the weak points were.

He knew where the water had pooled for decades. He knew that the stone on the north wall was laced with ancient, brittle limestone.

He knew that a precise, calculated impact at exactly this angle would trigger a cascade failure.

The stone cracked. Then it shattered.

A chunk of the wall broke away, revealing a dark void behind it. A void filled with rushing water.

The hole widened. The water poured out, a torrential flood that swept the creature off its feet.

The cleaver flew from its hand.

The creature scrambled for purchase, but the floor was too slick, the water too fast, which was dragged into the void, screaming.

The hole grew larger. The corridor shook.

Gerald stepped back. He watched the water rush out. He watched the stone crumble.

He checked his mental map. The water was draining away from his position.

The corridor was flooding, but only partially, and he had created a drainage channel where none existed.

He had violated the structure to save the structure.

Or rather, he had used the violation to enforce the code.

**[VIOLATION REMEDIED: DRAINAGE IMPROVEMENT DETECTED.]** **[COMPLIANCE RATING: 15/100]** **[NOTE: TEMPORARY INCREASE. RE-EVALUATION IN 24 HOURS.]**

Gerald stared at the message. It wasn't a win. It was a band-aid. A temporary patch on a dying patient.

He looked at the remaining corridor. It was still narrow. Still low. Still dark.

"Curator," he said. "I need to know the zoning laws for this sector."

The air remained silent.

Gerald walked forward, and the water was ankle-deep now. It was cold.

It bit into his skin.

He felt the dampness soaking into his shoes. He felt the chill creeping up his legs.

He kept walking.

At the end of the corridor, he found a door. It was heavy iron, rusted shut. There was a small window in the center.

Gerald looked through the window.

Inside the room was not darkness. It was light. Bright, blinding white light.

And in the center of the room stood a desk. A simple wooden desk. And behind the desk sat a figure.

The figure was tall. It wore a suit.

A perfect, charcoal-gray suit, which had no face.

Instead of a face, there was a blank white oval, smooth and featureless.

On the desk sat a stamp. A large, red stamp.

The figure looked up. It saw Gerald.

Gerald looked at the door. He tried the handle. It was locked.

"Open," Gerald said. "I have a permit."

The figure did not move. It did not speak. It simply raised the stamp.

Gerald felt a pressure in his chest. A crushing weight. His lungs compressed. He gasped.

**[AUDIT INITIATED]** **[INSPECTOR GENERAL PRESENT]** **[SUBJECT: GERALD HUTCHINS]** **[CHARGE: UNAUTHORIZED STRUCTURAL MODIFICATION]** **[PENALTY: TERMINATION OF CORE]**

Gerald slammed his shoulder against the door. It didn't budge. He kicked it. The wood splintered, but the iron frame held.

He looked at the window. The figure raised the stamp higher.

Gerald looked at the door frame. He looked at the hinges. They were old. Rusted.

He looked at the floor. The water was pooling near the base of the door.

He looked at the ceiling above the door. There was a light fixture. A single bulb. It was flickering.

Gerald closed his eyes. He thought about the Archive. He thought about the permit. He thought about the signature.

*Applicant acknowledges that by accepting this permit, they agree to assume liability for all previous violations in this sector, retroactive to the founding of the Archive.*

Liability, and he was liable for everything. Every crack. Every leak. Every violation committed by every core that had ever existed in this sector.

He couldn't fight the Inspector General. The Inspector General was the law.

But Gerald was a building inspector. And he knew that no law was absolute. There were always exceptions.

Always loopholes. Always appeals.

He opened his eyes. He looked at the flickering bulb.

He looked at the water, and he looked at the rust.

He smiled. It was a grim, tight smile.

He raised his fist and punched the glass of the window.

The glass didn't shatter. It didn't break.

The water from the corridor rushed up the door, fueled by the pressure from the previous hole he had made. It hit the rusted hinges.

Rust dissolves. It expands. It weakens.

The hinges groaned. They twisted.

The door warped.

The Inspector General raised the stamp.

Gerald didn't back away. He leaned into the door. He put his weight against it.

"Objection," Gerald said. "The structure is unstable. The audit environment is unsafe for the auditor."

The Inspector General paused. The blank white oval tilted.

Gerald pointed at the water seeping under the door. He pointed at the warped frame.

"If you strike me with that stamp," Gerald said, "the kinetic force will be transferred through the unstable door frame into the ceiling joists. The ceiling joists are already compromised by the drainage failure. The ceiling will collapse. The Inspector General will be crushed under four tons of stone."

The Inspector General lowered the stamp.

Gerald held his breath. He didn't move. He didn't blink. He stared at the blank face.

The Inspector General raised the stamp again.

It hovered over the desk, which did not strike.

It waited.

Gerald's heart hammered against his ribs. He could feel the sweat trickling down his back. He could feel the cold water soaking his socks.

The Inspector General picked up a pen. It wrote something on a piece of paper. It slid the paper under the door.

It landed in the puddle at Gerald's feet.

Gerald didn't pick it up. He looked at the paper. The text was clear, even through the water.

**[CITATION ISSUED]** **[CHARGE: UNAUTHORIZED STRUCTURAL MODIFICATION]** **[FINE: 50% OF CORE INTEGRITY]** **[APPEAL WINDOW: 10 SECONDS]** **[COST OF APPEAL: ONE MEMORY]**

Gerald looked at the stamp. It was still raised.

He looked at the paper, and he looked at the memory.

He thought about his first day as a building inspector. He thought about the smell of fresh paint. He thought about the pride he had felt when he passed his first difficult inspection.

He thought about the sword in the vision. The young man with rage in his eyes.

If he appealed, he lost a memory. If he didn't, he died.

The countdown in his vision ticked down.

Nine.

Eight.

Seven.

Gerald reached for the paper. His hand shook. He gripped the wet, soggy sheet.

He looked at the Inspector General.

"Which memory?" Gerald asked. "And which version of me pays the price?"

The Inspector General did not answer. It just watched.

Five.

Four.

Gerald squeezed his eyes shut. He thought about his wife. He thought about the day she left. He thought about the day he realized he had no friends.

Three.

Two.

Gerald opened his mouth. He screamed a single word.

It wasn't a plea. It wasn't a command.

It was a citation.

"Objection overruled!" he shouted. "I appeal on the grounds of inconsistent enforcement!"

The Inspector General's hand moved. The stamp came down.

But it didn't hit Gerald.

It hit the paper.

The paper burst into flames.

The fire spread instantly. It jumped from the paper to the water. The water evaporated in a cloud of steam.

The Inspector General vanished.

The room dissolved.

Gerald was falling again, and he hit the ground. Hard.

He gasped, choking on dust. He rolled over, coughing. He looked up.

He was back in the sub-basement of the Ashford Municipal Library.

The fluorescent lights buzzed overhead. The air smelled of old books and mildew.

His clipboard was in his hand.

His pen was in his hand.

His badge was on his lapel.

Gerald sat up. He checked his body. He was whole. He was alive.

He looked at his clipboard, and a new form was attached to it. A form he had never seen before.

**[FORM 11-C: MEMORIAL APPEAL]** **[STATUS: PENDING]** **[LOSS: ONE MEMORY]**

Gerald stared at the form. He felt a hole in his head. A small, sharp pain.

He couldn't remember his wife's name, and he knew he had a wife.

He knew he loved her. He knew she had left him. But the name was gone.

Erased, and he looked around the sub-basement. Three mold spores floated in the corner. A water leak dripped from a pipe. A confused rat scurried behind a stack of boxes.

Gerald stood up. His legs were weak, but he stood.

He looked at the rat.

"Get out," Gerald said. His voice was hoarse. "This is a non-occupied zone. You are trespassing."

The rat froze. It looked at him. Then it ran.

Gerald walked to the pipe. He took out his pen. He wrote a citation.

**[VIOLATION: LEAKING PIPE. REPORT TO MAINTENANCE.]**

He tore the copy off. He tucked it into his pocket.

He walked toward the stairs. He needed to file a report. He needed to document the incident. He needed to find out what he had lost.

And he needed to find out who the Inspector General was.

Because the stamp hadn't killed him, which had signed something.

Gerald stopped at the door. He looked at the small plaque next to the exit.

**[ASHFORD MUNICIPAL LIBRARY]** **[SUB-BASEMENT B]**

Below the plaque, in tiny letters, was a new line.

**[SECTOR 7: UNDER REVIEW BY OFFICE OF INTERNAL AFFAIRS]**

Gerald pushed the door open.

The light from the library flooded in. It was bright. It was safe.

Gerald stepped out. He walked to the front desk. The librarian looked up, smiling.

"Did you find what you were looking for, Mr. Hutchins?"

Gerald looked at the librarian. He looked at her nametag.

He didn't know her name. He didn't know if he had known her name before.

He looked at the exit sign. It was green. It was compliant.

He looked at the ceiling. It was high.

He looked at his hands. They were shaking.

"I found a violation," Gerald said. "And I paid for it."

The librarian blinked.

"A violation?"

"In my head," Gerald said.

He turned and walked out of the library. He didn't go to his car. He didn't go home.

He went to the records office, and he needed to pull the file on the predecessor. The Inspector.

He needed to see if the name was still there.

He needed to see if he had lost that too.

The door to the records office was locked.

Gerald pulled out his keys. He unlocked it. He stepped inside.

The room was dark.

On the main desk, a single file lay open.

The title on the file was not the Inspector, and the title was **GERALD HUTCHINS**.

And the status was not **ACTIVE**.

It was **TERMINATED**.

Gerald stared at the file. He reached out to touch it.

The door behind him clicked shut.

The lock engaged, and the lights went out.
