Chapter 219: Mandatory Shutdown

# Chapter 219

Gerald Hutchins landed on concrete.

His left arm hung uselessly at his side, the radius snapped clean through. The impact knocked the wind out of him, but the pain was distant, muffled by the shock of the fall and the lingering static of the lightning audit.

He tasted copper, and he tasted dust. He tasted the distinct, metallic tang of old lead paint.

The room was small.

Maybe eight feet by eight. No windows.

No exits.

Just shelves.

Row upon row of them.

They stretched into the darkness, vanishing into a shadow that didn't seem to obey the laws of physics Gerald had spent thirty-one years enforcing. These weren't modern steel racks. These were oak. Heavy, dark oak, warped by centuries of humidity and neglect.

Arthur Penhaligon sat on a crate in the center of the room. He looked older than Gerald remembered.

His suit, once a sharp charcoal grey, was stained with mud and ash.

His tie was gone.

His shirt sleeves were rolled up, revealing forearms thick with muscle and scar tissue.

He held a clipboard.

Not a System interface. A real one. Wood and metal. Paper clips.

"Sit down, Gerald," Arthur said. His voice was flat. "Don't touch the books."

Gerald didn't sit. He looked at the shelves. He looked at the spine of a book three feet away.

The leather was cracked. The gold leaf was flaking.

"Fire hazard," Gerald said. His voice sounded like gravel in a blender.

Arthur sighed.

"It's not a fire hazard, Hutchins. It's the Archive."

"It lacks egress signage," Gerald continued, the compulsion overriding the pain. "Combustible materials stored within three feet of electrical wiring. No suppression system. I am citing this space for immediate condemnation."

Arthur picked up his clipboard. He held it out.

"Read it."

Gerald stared at the paper.

It wasn't a form. It was a transcript. Handwritten.

The ink was fading.

"Transcript of what?"

"The Hearing," Arthur said. "Chapter 40.

You won it.

You cited the Gloom-Weaver's dungeon for blocked fire exits. Three of its elite spawns died of suffocation when the System enforced the citation. You killed them with code.

"

Gerald blinked. He remembered.

The smell of ozone.

The glowing red text.

The satisfaction of a perfectly formatted violation notice.

"I followed protocol."

"You followed protocol so hard you broke the protocol," Arthur said. He tapped the paper. "Look at the bottom."

Gerald looked.

Below the signature line, in handwriting that matched Arthur's, was a note.

*Violation of Statute 12.4: Unlicensed Interference with Sovereign Dungeon Ecology. Penalty: Administrative Detention.*

"I didn't authorize that," Gerald said. "I didn't sign it."

"The System did," Arthur said. "The Inspector General doesn't care if you're right. He cares if you're dangerous.

You're a Core now, Hutchins. You don't just enforce rules.

You *are* the rule.

And you just proved you can rewrite reality with a red pen.

"

A notification pinged in Gerald's mind. Sharp. Loud.

**SYSTEM ALERT: ADMINISTRATIVE HOLD INITIATED.** **LOCATION: THE ARCHIVE (UNLICENSED SUB-BASEMENT).** **STATUS: PENDING REVIEW.**

Gerald's mana bar, already at 12%, dipped to 8%.

"Arthur," Gerald said. "What is this place?"

"It's where the System puts the mistakes," Arthur said.

He stood up. He was taller than Gerald.

"It's where the Cores go when they get too good at finding the loopholes.

I've been here for six years, Gerald. I used to be a lawyer. Then I became a Core.

Then I started winning appeals.

"

Gerald felt a cold knot in his stomach.

Not fear.

Recognition. He knew this feeling.

It was the feeling of a building that looked fine from the street but was rotting from the inside.

"Why me?" Gerald asked.

"Because you're the only one who files paperwork," Arthur said. He walked to a shelf and pulled out a thick, bound volume.

"The Inspector General is coming, Hutchins.

Not an audit. An execution. He's going to erase you.

But he can't do it if there's a pending appeal.

"

Arthur threw the book at Gerald. Gerald caught it with his good hand. The weight nearly broke his wrist.

*The Municipal Code of Ashford: 1994 Edition.*

"I wrote this," Gerald whispered.

"You filed it," Arthur said. "Twelve years ago.

Before the System.

Before the dungeons. You filed it with the city. The city ignored it.

The System... remembered it.

"

Gerald opened the book, and the pages were thick.

Cream-colored. The font was Times New Roman, twelve point.

Just like his.

"This isn't just code," Arthur said. "It's a key.

The System derives its authority from human law.

If you can prove your code predates the System's integration, you have standing. You become a Pre-System Entity. You can't be deleted.

You can only be fined.

"

"I'm bankrupt," Gerald said. "I have 8% mana. I have a broken arm. And I'm in a room with no doors."

"There's a door," Arthur said. He pointed to a bookshelf on the far wall. "Push the third volume from the left. The one about zoning variances."

Gerald crawled over. His numb arm dragged behind him. He reached for the book. His fingers brushed the spine. *Zoning Variance Request #404-B*.

He pushed.

The shelf didn't move.

"It's locked," Gerald said.

"It's not locked," Arthur said. "It's out of code."

Gerald frowned.

"Explain."

"The shelf is three inches from the required clearance from the adjacent unit," Arthur said. "The System recognizes the violation. It's holding the door shut to prevent further non-compliance. Fix the violation, and the door opens."

Gerald stared at the gap between the shelves. It was tight. Maybe four inches. The code required six.

"How do I fix it?" Gerald asked. "I can't move the shelves. They're load-bearing."

"You don't move the shelves," Arthur said. "You change the classification of the room."

Gerald looked at the book in his hands. He looked at his clipboard. He still had it. The paper was crumpled, stained with blood and dust, but the pen was clipped to the side.

He pulled the pen out. His hand shook. He steadied it against his knee.

*RECLASSIFICATION REQUEST.* *SUBJECT: Storage Room 4B.* *CURRENT CLASS: General Storage (Class C).* *REQUESTED CLASS: Hazardous Material Containment (Class H).*

He wrote quickly. His handwriting was cramped, but legible. He cited Section 7.2.1: *Requirements for rooms housing volatile or unstable entities.*

He underlined *unstable entities*, and he signed it. *G. Hutchins, Core ID 404.*

The air in the room shifted. A low hum began in the walls.

**SYSTEM NOTICE: CLASSIFICATION CHANGE PENDING VERIFICATION.**

"It's not working," Gerald said. "The System doesn't recognize me as a municipal official."

"Look at the date," Arthur said.

Gerald looked. Under his signature, the date was printed. Not today's date.

*October 14, 1994.*

"The System uses the earliest valid registration," Arthur said. "You registered this room in 1994. You're still the inspector of record."

The shelf clicked.

It slid backward.

Behind it was a narrow passage. Stone steps. Downward.

Gerald stared at the dark hole. The air coming from it smelled of sulfur and burning wood.

"That's the exit?" Gerald asked.

"That's the Appeals Court," Arthur said. "It's where the Inspector General sits.

If you go down there, you go in front of him.

You argue your case. If you lose, you're deleted.

"

"And if I win?"

"If you win, you get a permanent immunity from minor fines. You get a seat on the Advisory Board. You get to write the new codes."

Gerald looked at his broken arm. He looked at his 8% mana. He looked at the book.

"I don't have the mana to fight him," Gerald said.

"You don't fight him," Arthur said. "You cite him."

Gerald looked up.

"Cite the Inspector General?"

"He has a body, Hutchins. He has a dungeon.

It's vast.

It's complex.

And it's been running for ten thousand years. Do you know what happens when you run a structure that long without updates?

"

Gerald felt a chill that had nothing to do with the temperature.

"Accumulated debt."

"Accumulated violations," Arthur said. "He has millions of them. But they're all grandfathered in.

He's immune to modern codes. But he's not immune to his own.

He can't change the rules he established.

If you find a violation in the foundational code, he has to fix it. It takes him time. It drains his mana.

It slows him down.

"

Gerald opened the book. He turned to the front. *Preamble.*

There, in small print, was the clause the System had forgotten. Or hoped no one would read.

*All systems shall operate within the bounds of the Initial Charter. Any deviation from the Charter shall be considered a Critical Failure.*

"The Initial Charter is the building," Gerald said. "The System is the renovation."

"Exactly," Arthur said. "Find the flaw in the foundation. Cite the Inspector General for structural instability. Force a shutdown."

Gerald closed the book. He stood up. His knees cracked. His arm throbbed.

He walked to the opening. The steps disappeared into blackness.

"I need a form," Gerald said.

Arthur handed him a blank sheet of paper. It was heavy. Parchment.

"Form 0," Arthur said. "The Nullification Request."

Gerald took it. He didn't write anything yet.

He didn't know what the violation was, and he needed to see the Inspector General first. He needed to measure him.

He stepped onto the first stone.

"Wait," Arthur said.

Gerald turned.

"Don't let him talk," Arthur said. "He will try to reason with you.

He will offer you upgrades, and he will offer you power.

Do not accept. Do not negotiate.

Only cite."

Gerald nodded. He stepped down.

The darkness swallowed him, and he descended for ten minutes. The air grew hotter.

The smell of sulfur became overpowering.

The walls were no longer stone. They were crystal. Pulsing.

Alive.

The stairs ended.

Gerald stepped onto a platform, and the room was infinite.

There was no ceiling. There was no floor. There was only light. And in the center of the light sat a figure.

It was not a monster. It was not a dragon.

It was a man, and he wore a suit. A pristine, white suit.

His skin was smooth.

His face was ageless. He sat at a desk. A massive desk, carved from a single diamond.

On the desk was a stack of papers. Thousands of them.

The man looked up. His eyes were black.

No whites.

No irises.

Just void.

"Gerald Hutchins," the man said. His voice was the sound of a closing door. "You are late."

Gerald checked his watch. It was broken.

"I was delayed by a non-compliant egress route," Gerald said.

The man smiled. It was a terrible smile.

"You have no mana, Gerald.

You have no weapons. You have no allies.

Why are you here?

"

Gerald placed his clipboard on the diamond desk. The crystal groaned under the weight of the wood.

"I am here to conduct an inspection," Gerald said.

The man laughed. It was a dry, rasping sound.

"I am the Inspector General. I do not submit to inspections."

"Section 1 of the Initial Charter," Gerald said. He pointed to the book on the desk. "Subsection A.

'The General shall be subject to the same structural requirements as the Core.' It's in the fine print.

Page 12. Footnote 4.

"

The man's smile faltered. Just a fraction.

"You cannot cite me," the man said. "I am the law."

"You are a building," Gerald said. He pulled out his tape measure. He clicked it out. The metal blade slid out with a sharp *snap*.

He walked toward the man.

The man didn't move. He couldn't. He was frozen by the gravity of the room.

Gerald measured the desk.

"Load bearing," Gerald muttered. "Insufficient."

He moved to the walls. He measured the distance between the glowing crystals.

"Spacing violation," he said. "Exceeds maximum tolerance for magical resonance."

The man's face twisted.

"Stop."

Gerald ignored him. He walked to the man. He measured the man's chair.

"Ergonomic non-compliance," Gerald said. "Chair height incorrect for occupant. Risk of musculoskeletal injury. Risk of structural failure."

The man stood up.

The room shook.

"You are making a mistake," the man said. "I can delete you. I can erase your name. I can erase your rat."

Gerald looked at the man. He looked at the man's feet.

One foot was planted firmly. The other was hovering an inch off the ground.

"Uneven footing," Gerald said. "Trip hazard. Immediate citation."

He wrote it down.

*VIOLATION: UNEVEN FLOORING.* *SEVERITY: CRITICAL.* *ACTION: IMMEDIATE STABILIZATION.*

The System rang out.

**CITATION SERVED.** **TARGET: INSPECTOR GENERAL.** **DEBUFF: STABILITY REDUCED BY 50%.**

The man stumbled.

The room tilted.

The crystal walls cracked.

Gerald smiled, which was a small, dry smile.

He turned the page.

He looked at the man.

"Next violation," Gerald said. "Fire safety. You have no extinguishers. And this room... it's very hot."

The man looked at Gerald. For the first time, there was fear in his void eyes.

"You can't," the man whispered.

Gerald raised his pen.

"I can," Gerald said.

He wrote.

**VIOLATION: ABSENCE OF FIRE SUPPRESSION.** **PENALTY: MANDATORY SHUTDOWN.**

The room went dark.

Then Gerald raised his pen again.
