Chapter 320: Tick Tock

# Chapter 320

The pen tip snapped.

Gerald stared at the broken nib, the black ink pooling on the cheap library paper like a dark wound. Six minutes.

The cursor in his peripheral vision pulsed red, a metronome counting down the destruction of his life's work.

He didn't have a replacement nib. He didn't have a printer. He had a shaking hand and a headache that felt like a hammer striking the base of his skull.

*Focus.*

The code was clear.

Section 4.12, Subsection B: Maintenance records must be contemporaneous with the repair. Contemporaneous meant *at the time of*.

Not *after the fact*.

Not *under duress*. Not *to save your skin*.

But Gerald wasn't filing a record of maintenance. He was filing a record of *observation*.

He pulled a fresh sheet from his pocket, the one he used for unofficial notes, and wrote in a cramped, desperate script. He wasn't describing the junction box. He was describing the *inspector*.

*11:43 PM. Subject: Unlicensed Electrical Intervention.*

*Observation: Individual entered restricted sub-basement area without posting permit or signage. Individual performed unauthorized diagnostic testing on live circuits (see: 480V mains). Individual failed to wear Arc-Flash Rated PPE. Individual failed to establish Lockout/Tagout protocol.*

*Violation: OSHA 1910.303(b)(1). Failure to de-energize circuits prior to examination.*

Gerald's breath hitched. This was a gamble.

A massive, terrifying gamble.

If the System accepted this as a valid inspection report, he wasn't admitting to an unpermitted repair.

He was accusing the man who threatened him of an illegal entry and an unpermitted *diagnostic*. And if the man was a contractor, not a Core, he was subject to municipal safety laws within Gerald's dungeon boundaries.

The cursor blinked. Five minutes.

Gerald signed the form. *Gerald Hutchins, Building Inspector, City of Ashford.*

He pressed his thumb to the paper. The System interface flared gold, then red, then settled into a neutral gray.

**REPORT ACCEPTED.** **INITIATING CROSS-JURISDICTIONAL INQUIRY.**

The mist in the room cleared. The oppressive weight of the penalty accrual vanished from Gerald's chest. He slumped against the support pillar, his knees weak.

He had bought time. He hadn't won.

The audit was still coming, but now it was a joint investigation.

He had dragged the Inspector General's representative into a bureaucratic limbo where his own actions were under scrutiny, which was dirty. It was against the spirit of the rules. But it was *within* the rules.

A chime sounded, distant and echoing. Not from the east wing. From the north corridor.

Gerald wiped the ink from his thumb. He checked his status.

**Compliance Rating: 58/100.** **Status: Under Investigation.**

The rating had dropped two points for the messy paperwork, but the *Penalty Accrual* tag was gone. He had survived the night.

He stood up, straightening his tie. The tie was crooked. He fixed it. It was the first thing he had controlled in an hour.

The chime sounded again. Louder.

Something was in the north corridor. And it wasn't a rat.

Gerald walked toward the sound, his boots clicking on the concrete. The north corridor was his

"showroom," the section of the dungeon he kept polished and well-lit to attract high-level adventurer parties who appreciated good lighting and clear signage. He had spent three weeks installing recessed LED strips that met the exact lux requirements for public walkways.

As he rounded the corner, the air temperature dropped. Not a gradual chill, but a sudden, biting freeze that cracked the moisture in the air into visible mist.

Standing in the center of the corridor was a figure, which was not a monster. It was a man, dressed in the pristine, white robes of a High Priest, though the fabric was stained with mud and blood. He held a staff topped with a crystalline skull that pulsed with a faint, sickly green light.

"Inspector Hutchins," the man said. His voice was smooth, cultured, and devoid of warmth. "I am Kaelen, Core of the Sunken Crypt. I wish to file a grievance."

Gerald stopped. He placed his hands behind his back, assuming the posture of a man who had heard every excuse in the municipal zoning board.

"You are trespassing," Gerald said. "This is a private commercial zone. Signage is posted at every ingress point. You have failed to post a permit."

Kaelen smiled. It was a thin, cruel expression.

"I am not trespassing. I am appealing."

"Appeals are filed in writing," Gerald said. "Not in person. Not with a staff of necrotic energy."

"The System allows for oral arguments in cases of *Imminent Structural Failure*," Kaelen said. He tapped the staff against the floor. The concrete cracked. The fissure spread rapidly, spiderwebbing toward the support pillars Gerald had reinforced just last month.

Gerald looked at the crack.

His heart hammered against his ribs. If the pillar failed, the dungeon's Structural Integrity rating would plummet.

It would trigger a cascade failure.

The ceiling would collapse. The dungeon would die.

"Stop," Gerald said. His voice was flat, but his eyes were fixed on the fracture line.

"You are causing damage to a load-bearing structure.

That is a Class A violation, which is not an appeal. It is assault.

"

"It is leverage," Kaelen corrected. "Your dungeon is small. Weak.

Your Compliance Rating is barely above the survival threshold. I have offered you a partnership.

You refused.

Now, I am showing you what happens when a neighbor is unhappy.

"

The crack widened. Dust drifted down from the ceiling.

Gerald's mind raced, and he could cite Kaelen. He could try to shut down his dungeon.

But Kaelen was outside the dungeon's immediate perimeter, in a neutral zone.

Gerald's citation range was limited to his own footprint. He couldn't hit him.

But he could hit the *damage*.

*Structural Code: Section 9.4. Emergency Stabilization.*

Gerald didn't have time to build supports. He didn't have time to call for help. He had to use the environment.

He looked at the recessed LED lighting above the crack. He looked at the fire suppression system sprinkler heads mounted nearby. He looked at the water leak he had been meaning to fix for three weeks but kept putting off because he couldn't find the right form for plumbing repairs.

*Imperfection.*

*Use it.*

Gerald pulled a small wrench from his belt. It was a habit, a security blanket. He walked toward the fissure, not away from it.

"You are violating the principle of *Static Equilibrium*," Gerald said, his voice dropping to a dangerous whisper. "A structure must remain stationary to be considered compliant. You have introduced dynamic force."

Kaelen laughed.

"And you are going to fix it with a wrench?"

"I am going to change the variable," Gerald said.

He reached up and twisted the emergency valve on the fire suppression main line, located ten feet away.

Nothing happened. The valve was stuck. Rusted.

Gerald gritted his teeth. He twisted harder.

The rust flaked off, biting into his palm, and he didn't feel it.

He twisted until his muscles screamed.

*Click.*

The system engaged.

High-pressure water blasted from the sprinkler heads directly above the crack.

The water hit the concrete, seeping instantly into the fissure Kaelen had created. But Gerald wasn't trying to put out a fire. He was trying to freeze it.

The temperature in the room was already below freezing due to Kaelen's aura. The water entered the crack, and in the sub-zero air, it crystallized.

*Ice expands.*

Gerald watched the crack. The water filled the void. Then, the ice formed. And as the ice formed, it expanded.

The pressure pushed back against the force Kaelen was applying to keep the crack open. The ice acted as a wedge, forcing the concrete back toward its original alignment. It was a brute-force application of thermodynamics to fix a magical breach.

Kaelen's smile faltered. He looked at the ice growing in the crack. He looked at Gerald.

"You are freezing your own infrastructure," Kaelen said.

"I am *stabilizing* it," Gerald said. "Ice is a solid. Solids provide support. Under Section 12, *Temporary Shoring*, natural materials are permissible in emergency scenarios."

The ice groaned. The crack stopped widening. The dust stopped falling.

Kaelen stepped back. The green light on his staff flickered.

"This is a workaround," Kaelen hissed. "It is not code. It is improvisation."

"It is effective," Gerald said. He pointed the wrench at the priest. "And you are standing in a puddle of freezing water.

Your boots are wet. Your staff is damp.

You are conducting electricity poorly.

"

Gerald looked up at the electrical panel he had just fought to save. The junction boxes were labeled, neat, and dry. But the floor was now wet.

*Grounding.*

Kaelen realized it at the same time. He saw the water pooling around his feet. He saw the live wires in the conduit running along the ceiling, just inches above the puddle.

"You wouldn't," Kaelen said.

"I don't need to," Gerald said. "The water is conductive. The ice is holding the structure, but it is also bridging the gap to the grounded rebar in the pillars.

If you channel necrotic energy through the staff into the ground, you will short out your own magical circuitry.

You will disrupt your own dungeon's connection to the leylines.

"

It was a bluff. Mostly. Gerald knew that necrotic energy interacted poorly with saltwater, and the municipal water supply had a high mineral content. He didn't know if it would short out a dungeon core, but he knew it would make Kaelen hesitate.

And hesitation was what Gerald needed.

Kaelen raised his staff, aiming it at Gerald.

Then he stopped. He looked at the puddle. He looked at the ice.

He looked at Gerald's dead, unblinking eyes.

"You are a coward," Kaelen said. "You hide behind bureaucracy."

"I am a public servant," Gerald said. "And you are in violation of the *Safety of Occupants* code. I am issuing you a citation for *Reckless Endangerment*. Leave, or I will call the System Police."

Kaelen snarled. He slammed his staff into the ground, but not into the crack. He hit the solid concrete, creating a shockwave that pushed Gerald back, but didn't break the structure. The ice held.

"This isn't over," Kaelen said.

"The Inspector General will come.

And when he does, he will see a Core who destroys his own property to save face. He will see a Core who uses water to fight ice.

He will see a fraud.

"

"I have filed the report," Gerald said. "The audit is a joint inquiry. You are a suspect now, Kaelen. Not a neighbor."

Kaelen's face twisted. He turned and walked away, his robes swirling in the cold mist. He did not run. He walked with the dignity of someone who knew they had been outmaneuvered, but not defeated.

Gerald watched him go. He didn't move until the figure disappeared around the corner.

Then, Gerald walked to the ice-locked crack. He touched the concrete. It was stable. The ice was holding.

But it was cold. So cold.

His hand went numb. He pulled it back, shaking it to restore circulation.

*Cost paid.*

He had saved the dungeon. But he had used a temporary fix.

The ice would melt.

The crack would reopen. He needed a real repair. He needed steel.

He needed concrete, and he needed money he didn't have.

And he had made an enemy who could freeze his entire dungeon in seconds.

A new notification pinged in his vision.

**ALERT: ENVIRONMENTAL HAZARD DETECTED.** **SUBSTANCE: UNCONTROLLED ICE FORMATION.** **RISK: SLIP HAZARD.** **ACTION REQUIRED: INSTALL NON-SLIP MATING OR CORDON OFF AREA.**

Gerald looked at the wet floor. He didn't have mats. He didn't have cones.

He pulled a piece of paper from his pocket. It was the back of the Form 11-E he had almost filed.

He wrote: **CAUTION: WET FLOOR.**

He taped it to the wall. It was a start.

The clock on his internal display read 12:15 AM. Sunrise was in four hours.

The Inspector General was still coming. Kaelen was still out there. And Gerald had just proven that he was willing to break the spirit of the law to save the letter of it.

He looked at the ice. It was beautiful, in a terrifying, jagged way.

He needed to know why Kaelen was so desperate. Why he was willing to destroy his own dungeon to hurt Gerald.

Gerald walked back to his office, a small closet he had converted into a command center. He sat at his desk. He opened his logbook.

He didn't write about the victory. He wrote about the question.

*Who is funding Kaelen?*

*Why does he fear the audit?*

*What is he hiding?*

And then, a new notification appeared. Not from the System. From the door.

A knock. Three sharp raps.

Gerald froze. He didn't have visitors. The dungeon was closed. The signage was up.

He stood. He walked to the door. He didn't open it. He looked through the peephole he had installed last week, a small glass lens that allowed him to see outside without breaching the thermal envelope.

On the other side of the door stood a woman. She was young, perhaps twenty-five, dressed in leather armor that was too clean to be battle-worn. She held a clipboard.

"Inspector Hutchins," she said.

Her voice was muffled by the door.

"I'm from the Adventurer's Guild. We received a report of a *structural instability* in your sector.

We're here to assess the risk to our patrons.

"

Gerald stared at her.

*Report.*

Who had reported it?

He looked back at the ice in the corridor. The ice was holding. The dungeon was safe.

But the report said it was unstable.

Gerald opened the door.

The woman smiled. It was a professional smile, empty and polite.

"Mr.

Hutchins.

We have complaints about freezing temperatures. We have complaints about slip hazards.

We have complaints that you are using *unapproved materials* for structural support.

"

She held up the clipboard.

"We're not here to inspect you, Inspector. We're here to evict you."

Gerald looked at her. He looked at the clipboard. He looked at the ice.

He realized then that Kaelen hadn't just been threatening him. Kaelen had already filed the complaint.

And the Guild, the largest consumer of dungeon services in the city, had acted on it.

"You can't evict me," Gerald said. "I have a lease."

"Leases are void if the property is deemed *uninhabitable*," the woman said. "Under the new System Guidelines, a dungeon with active structural fractures and uncontrolled environmental hazards is classified as a *Hazard Zone*. Hazard Zones are seized by the City for public safety."

She stepped forward.

"Hand over the Core Key, Mr. Hutchins. Or we will call the police."

Gerald felt the blood drain from his face. Not from fear. From rage. A cold, hard rage that burned in his chest.

He looked at the ice. He looked at the woman.

"If you seize my dungeon," Gerald said, his voice quiet, "you are seizing a *live* electrical system. You are seizing a system with *unfiled maintenance logs*. You are assuming liability for every inch of wire and every drop of water in this facility."

The woman's smile slipped.

"That's... procedural," she said.

"It's a trap," Gerald said. "Accept the liability. Sign the form. And if anyone dies when the ceiling collapses next week because of *your* negligence, it's on you."

He pulled a pen from his pocket.

"Fill it out, officer. Section 8. Liability Waiver."

The woman hesitated. She looked at the clipboard. She looked at Gerald.

For the first time, Gerald saw uncertainty in her eyes, and she didn't have a form for this.

Gerald watched her. He held the pen out.

"Tick tock," he said.
