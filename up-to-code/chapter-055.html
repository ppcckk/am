Chapter 55: Self Inspection

# Chapter 55

Gerald measured the encroachment at forty-three centimeters.

He pressed the tape against the boundary line, the System's property demarcation rendered as a faint amber glow in the floor, and held the end of the tape against the far edge of the crack Vexithar's modification had carved into his column. Forty-three centimeters of unauthorized structural work extending past the property line.

He noted the measurement in his panel, and he noted the depth. He noted the angle of the tool marks, which were consistent with a directed resonance pulse than a mechanical bore, which meant Vexithar had used a sonic drill, which meant the violation was willful and premeditated, which meant the aggravated severity checkbox was correct.

Gerald filled it in.

The Citation form sat in his panel, half-complete.

Target: Vexithar, Core ID 4409. Violation: Unauthorized Structural Modification, Load-Bearing Element, Intentional.

Severity: Aggravated.

Service location: the exact point of encroachment, and he hesitated on the last field. The System's language was precise. *The target must be physically present at the service location at the time of service.* Gerald had read this clause forty times.

He had read it in the context of serving Citations to spawn, to minions, to creatures that were extensions of a Core's will but not the Core itself.

The boundary-adjacent method worked because spawn were present at the boundary.

The Core was not.

But Gerald looked at the crack. He looked at the amber line. He looked at the forty-three centimeters of Vexithar's dungeon that had been carved into his.

A dungeon was not a building. The System made that distinction clear in the Core Registration protocol. A dungeon was a Core.

The Core's body was its dungeon. The walls were flesh.

The corridors were arteries, and the columns were bones.

If Vexithar's body extended forty-three centimeters past the property line, then Vexithar was present at the boundary. Vexithar had always been present at the boundary. Every day since the modification was completed, Vexithar had been physically present at the service location, because Vexithar's body was the thing crossing it.

Gerald had been overcomplicating it.

He had been trying to serve the Core at the Core's center, the way you would serve a person at their home address. But the Core's home address was everywhere its dungeon existed.

Including here.

Including forty-three centimeters past the line, in Gerald's column, in the crack that had nearly brought the ceiling down.

He set the service location to the encroachment point, and he pressed Serve.

The Citation materialized as a sheet of amber light, the same as every Citation he had ever issued. It hovered at the crack, at the exact point where Vexithar's structure crossed into his.

The System confirmed: *Served.

Target physically present at service location.

Citation active. Debuff applied: Structural Integrity -18% across all load-bearing elements within 200 meters of the Citation point.*

Gerald allowed himself a nod. Eighteen percent. Vexithar had built a sonic drill into his column.

That drill was now a structural liability in Vexithar's own body, debuffed by eighteen percent. Every load-bearing wall within two hundred meters of the encroachment point was now eighteen percent weaker.

He started walking back to his dungeon center.

The appeal notice arrived before he reached the handrails.

*Target has filed an Appeal.

Hearing scheduled. Both parties must attend. Hearing format: Structural Logic Argument.

Duration: one cycle.

Arbiter: System-Appointed.*

Gerald stopped, and he read the notice twice, and then he opened his panel and checked the Hearing protocol for the first time. He had won the previous Hearing against Dungeon 3312 by proving fire exits were non-compliant, but that had been a standard Citation.

This was an aggravated Citation. The Hearing protocol for aggravated Citations was different.

He read it.

The difference was in the burden of proof. In a standard Hearing, the appellant had to prove the Citation was incorrect. In an aggravated Hearing, the appellant had to prove the Citation was incorrect *and* the original condition was compliant. The appellant had to demonstrate that the structural modification in question was, in fact, up to code.

Vexithar would argue that the sonic drill was a compliant modification, and that it was a legitimate structural element, properly integrated, not a violation.

Gerald could counter. He could show the tool marks, the angle, the lack of a permit. He could show that the modification had been performed without filing a Form 7-C (Structural Alteration Notification) with the System, which was required for any modification to a load-bearing element within thirty centimeters of a property boundary.

He could win this. He had done the measurements. He had the documentation. He had thirty-one years of experience arguing structural logic in rooms where people screamed at him and he did not scream back.

He accepted the Hearing.

The space materialized around him, which was not a room.

It was a cross-section. The System rendered both dungeons in transparent amber, side by side, cut open like a textbook diagram.

Gerald's dungeon on the left. Vexithar's on the right. The boundary line between them glowed white.

Vexithar was not a person. The Core's avatar in the Hearing space was a geometric shape, a rotating polyhedron with too many faces, each face showing a different cross-section of its dungeon. It pulsed with a low, grinding hum that Gerald felt in his teeth.

The Arbiter was a point of white light at the top of the space; it spoke in the flat, affectless tone of a system notification.

*Hearing convened. Citation 4409-7, aggravated severity.

Appellant: Core Vexithar.

Respondent: Core Hutchins. Violation: Unauthorized Structural Modification, Load-Bearing Element, Intentional. Appellant, present your case.*

Vexithar's polyhedron rotated. A face expanded, showing the sonic drill embedded in the column. The System rendered it in cross-section. Gerald observed the drill's geometry and resonance chamber, noting its threading through the column's load-bearing matrix.

*The modification is a compliant load-transfer element,* Vexithar said. Its voice was layered, multiple frequencies speaking in sequence, like a chord played on an instrument Gerald could not name. *It redistributes structural load from the upper matrix to the lower foundation. It is not a violation. It is an improvement.*

The Arbiter's light pulsed. *Respondent, your rebuttal.*

Gerald stepped forward. He pointed at the cross-section.

"The load-transfer angle is incorrect," he said.

"The element is threaded at eleven degrees from vertical. A compliant load-transfer element in a column of this diameter must be threaded at no less than fifteen degrees.

At eleven degrees, the element creates a shear point rather than a load path, which is not a load-transfer element. This is a stress concentrator."

Vexithar's polyhedron rotated faster. *The element is functional. The column remains standing.*

"Functional is not compliant," Gerald said. "The element is functional in the same way a crack is functional.

It does what it does, which does not meet the angle specification.

I have the measurement. Forty-three centimeters of encroachment at an eleven-degree thread angle.

That is two violations in a single element. Unauthorized crossing of the property line, and non-compliant thread geometry."

The Arbiter pulsed. *Appellant, do you have a counter-argument?*

Vexithar was silent for a moment. Then its polyhedron shifted, and a new face appeared.

It showed Gerald's column.

The crack. The seep. The water damage.

*The respondent's column contains an unpermitted structural alteration,* Vexithar said. *The element I installed is now embedded in the respondent's load-bearing matrix.

Under the Joint Liability for Shared Structural Elements protocol, the respondent is liable for the compliance status of any structural element within its dungeon footprint. The element is in the respondent's dungeon.

The element is non-compliant, and the respondent is in violation.*

Gerald felt something cold move through him. He opened his panel. He searched for the protocol Vexithar had named.

It was there. Buried in the compliance appendix, section 14, subsection 9. *Joint Liability for Shared Structural Elements: Where a structural element spans the boundary between two dungeon footprints, both Cores are jointly liable for the compliance status of that element. Neither Core may claim exemption on the basis of origin.*

He had never read this section. He had read every other section. He had read the fire code, the electrical code, the structural code, the ventilation code, the egress code, the occupancy code. He had not read section 14, subsection 9, because he had assumed it applied to shared infrastructure, to bridges and roads and public easements, not to weaponized sonic drills embedded in load-bearing columns.

*The respondent's Compliance Rating has been adjusted,* the Arbiter said. *The element is non-compliant. Both Cores are liable. Respondent's rating reduced by 15 points.*

Gerald's panel flashed. His Compliance Rating, which had been climbing for thirty chapters, which he had spent weeks bringing up from twelve, dropped from 71 to 56.

Fifty-six, and he knew what fifty-six meant. He had read the thresholds.

Below 60, spawned entities received a 10% reduction to all attributes.

Below 50, the reduction doubled.

Below 40, the System issued a Conditional Shutdown notice.

He was at 56. The debuff was already active. His mold spores, his water leak, his rat, all ten percent weaker. His handrails, his ventilation system, his installed fire suppression, all slightly less effective.

Vexithar's polyhedron rotated. It was satisfied. Gerald could tell because the hum dropped a semitone.

*Respondent, do you have a rebuttal to the joint liability finding?* the Arbiter asked.

Gerald looked at the cross-section. The sonic drill. Embedded in his column.

Part of his structure now. He could not remove it without removing the column.

He could not remove the column without collapsing the ceiling, and the element was in his dungeon. It was non-compliant. He was liable.

Unless.

He looked at the thread angle.

Eleven degrees. Non-compliant for a load-transfer element.

But what if it was not a load-transfer element?

What if it was something else?

He pulled up the System's structural element classification table. Every element in a dungeon had a classification. Load-bearing, non-load-bearing, decorative, mechanical, biological. The classification determined which code applied.

The sonic drill was threaded at eleven degrees. That angle was non-compliant for a load-transfer element.

But it was compliant for a mechanical element.

A mechanical element at eleven degrees was fine, and a mechanical element was not required to meet the fifteen-degree thread specification.

If Gerald reclassified the element as mechanical than structural, the joint liability finding would still apply, but the compliance status would change. A compliant element could not be the basis of a violation.

He filed the reclassification request in the middle of the Hearing.

*Requesting reclassification of element 4409-7 from Load-Bearing Structural to Mechanical. Basis: thread angle of eleven degrees is consistent with mechanical element specifications under Section 9, Paragraph 3. The element is not functioning as a load-transfer device.

It is functioning as a resonance device. Resonance devices are classified as mechanical.*

The Arbiter pulsed. It considered. Vexithar's polyhedron went still.

*Reclassification request under review. Appellant, do you object?*

Vexithar's hum rose a semitone. Then dropped. Then rose again.

*The element was installed as a structural modification. Its original classification is structural.*

"Original classification is not current classification," Gerald said.

"Classification is determined by function, not intent. The element is currently functioning as a resonance device. I have the moisture meter readings.

The resonance is generating vibration in the column at a frequency consistent with mechanical operation, not load transfer.

I filed the moisture meter data as evidence in my original Citation. The System has it."

The Arbiter pulsed three times.

Then: *Reclassification approved. Element 4409-7 is now classified as Mechanical. Joint liability finding stands.

Compliance status of element: compliant. Respondent's rating restored.*

Gerald's panel flashed. 56 to 71.

The Arbiter continued. *Citation 4409-7 upheld. Aggravated severity maintained. Debuff active on appellant. Hearing concluded.*

The space dissolved.

Gerald was standing at the boundary line.

His column was still cracked. The seep was still active.

But Vexithar's dungeon was eighteen percent weaker across two hundred meters, and Gerald's rating was intact.

He turned to walk back.

His panel flashed. Not a Citation. Not an audit. A new notification, one he had never seen before.

*Inspector General has flagged Core 4409 for enhanced review. Enhanced review protocol: all Citations issued by the flagged Core will be subject to pre-approval by the Inspector General. Flagged Core may not issue Citations without prior authorization.*

Gerald read it twice, and he could not issue Citations anymore. Not without permission.

The Inspector General had seen what he did.

Had seen the aggravated Citation, the joint liability maneuver, the reclassification play. Had decided that Gerald Hutchins was a problem.

He stood at the boundary line, holding his moisture meter, and felt the specific physical nausea that came from being told he could not do his job.

Then his panel flashed again. A second notification.

*Inspector General has flagged Core 4409 for enhanced review. Enhanced review protocol: flagged Core may not issue Citations without prior authorization. Inspector General will conduct on-site audit within three cycles.*

Three cycles.

Gerald had been a building inspector for thirty-one years. He had never once been audited by his own superior.

He had been the auditor.

He had been the one who showed up with a tape measure and a clipboard and a look that made contractors cry.

Now someone was coming to measure him, and he looked at the crack in his column. He looked at the seep. He looked at the forty-three centimeters of Vexithar's body still embedded in his structure, now reclassified as mechanical, now compliant, now part of his dungeon whether he liked it or not.

Three cycles. He had three cycles to prepare for an audit by an entity that could shut him down, that had just taken away his primary tool, that had flagged him because he had been too good at using it.

Gerald sat down on the floor next to the crack. He opened his panel.

He started drafting a self-inspection report, a type of document he had never written about himself.

He did not know the format.

He wrote the first line anyway.
