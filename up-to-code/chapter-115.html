Chapter 115: Rot After Victory

# Chapter 115 Gerald’s pen hovered over the signature line. The ink was black.

The paper was cream-colored, heavy stock, the kind used for municipal fines and eviction notices.

He did not sign. Instead, he rotated the form one hundred and eighty degrees.

The man across the desk, who had introduced himself as Adjudicator Vane, blinked. His eyes were the color of wet asphalt, and his skin possessed the waxy sheen of a man who had not slept in three centuries. He waited for Gerald to speak. He waited for the plea for mercy, the desperate bargaining, the usual chaotic noise of a dying Core.

Gerald did not speak. He pointed a finger at the bottom margin of the form.

"Subsection 4, Paragraph C," Gerald said. His voice was steady. It was the voice he used when telling a plumber that his copper piping was not, in fact, Type L. "Appeals require the appellant to be physically present in the jurisdiction of the infraction."

Vane’s left eye twitched.

"You are not in Ashford, Mr. Hutchins.

You are in the Void.

The Void is not a jurisdiction."

"The Void is a spatial anomaly," Gerald corrected. "According to the System’s foundational logic, which predates the current iteration of the Core Wars, the Void is not a place.

It is a state of non-existence.

Therefore, I am not physically present anywhere.

I am nowhere. Which means this hearing cannot take place in 'the Void.' It must take place where the violation occurred.

Or, in this case, where the suspension was issued."

Vane leaned forward. The chair creaked. "The suspension was issued by the Inspector General. The IG is everywhere."

"The IG is an entity," Gerald said. "An entity has coordinates. Where were the coordinates of the IG when they stamped this suspension?"

Vane opened his mouth. He closed it.

He looked at the blank signature line, and he looked at Gerald. For the first time, the Adjudicator looked uncertain.

Gerald tapped the form. "If you want me to sign this, you need to specify the venue. If you don't specify the venue, the document is procedurally void. As you already know."

"I know," Vane said. His voice was low, vibrating with a frequency that made Gerald’s teeth ache. "You exploited the lack of a signature field earlier. You do not exploit it again."

"I am not exploiting," Gerald said. "I am citing. There is a difference."

Vane stood up. The small room seemed to expand, the walls receding from the center, and the stale coffee smell intensified, mixed with the scent of ozone and burning hair.

"You are stalling," Vane said.

"The appeal window closes in ten minutes. If you do not sign, the suspension becomes permanent.

Your dungeon remains frozen.

Your spawns remain dead. You remain here."

"And if I sign without a venue?"

"Then you accept the jurisdiction of the Void. You accept that you are property of the System. You lose your rights to a physical hearing."

Gerald looked at the pen. He looked at the clock on the wall. The hands were moving backward.

He picked up the pen. He did not sign. He wrote in the margin: *Requesting venue clarification per Code 114-B: Jurisdictional Precedence.*

Vane stared at the writing. "You are adding text to a legal document before it is validated. That is a violation of the Hearing Protocol."

"Is it?" Gerald asked. "Show me the rule."

Vane snapped his fingers. A translucent blue panel appeared in the air between them. It displayed the System’s rules for Hearings. It was dense, small, and written in a font so old it looked like it had been carved with a chisel.

Gerald squinted. He read it.

His eyes moved fast, scanning the lines, looking for the gaps, the loopholes, the ambiguous definitions.

He had spent thirty-one years reading municipal codes, and he knew that the devil was not in the details. The devil was in the definitions of 'shall' versus 'may', and the lack of a defined 'reasonable time' for response.

No rule existed for adding margin notes.

"Procedurally," Gerald said, "I have submitted a request for clarification. The clock stops when a clarification is pending. That is standard administrative law.

It is also standard System logic. You cannot enforce a deadline while a legal ambiguity is unresolved."

Vane’s face hardened. The waxy skin appeared to fracture. "You are testing my patience, Mr. Hutchins."

"I am testing your competence," Gerald said.

"You are the Adjudicator. You are supposed to know the code. If you don't know the code, you are unfit for the role.

And if you are unfit, this hearing is invalid."

This was a gamble. A massive, terrifying gamble.

If Vane had the authority to override local interpretations, Gerald was dead.

But Vane was a bureaucrat. Bureaucrats feared being wrong more than they feared being evil. If Gerald could make Vane doubt his own understanding of the System, the Adjudicator would hesitate.

And hesitation was the only thing Gerald had.

Vane looked at the blue panel. He looked at Gerald. He looked at the form.

"Submit your argument," Vane said. His voice was flat. "Convince me that the venue is not the Void."

Gerald took a breath, and he thought about his dungeon.

The 4,000-square-foot sub-basement. The handrails he had installed.

The ventilation shafts he had widened. The fire exits he had painted bright orange.

"My dungeon is a municipal structure," Gerald said. "It is registered under the Ashford City Code.

When the System registered me, it did not replace the city code.

It overlaid it. This means my dungeon exists in two jurisdictions simultaneously: the System and the City of Ashford."

Vane raised an eyebrow. "Interesting theory."

"It is not a theory.

It is a fact. The System’s Core Registration form, subsection 1, requires proof of physical location.

My location is Ashford.

Therefore, any legal action taken against my Core must respect the jurisdiction of Ashford, and the Void is a neutral zone. It has no jurisdiction.

You cannot hold a hearing in a place that has no laws."

"But the Inspector General suspended you," Vane said.

"The Inspector General suspended my *access*," Gerald corrected.

"They did not dissolve my *jurisdiction*.

As long as the physical footprint of the dungeon exists, the jurisdiction exists. And the footprint exists.

I can prove it."

Gerald closed his eyes. He reached out with his mind, not to the System, but to the memory of the stone.

He remembered the cold dampness of the library’s foundation, and he remembered the smell of mildew and old paper. He remembered the exact dimensions of the main corridor: twelve feet wide, eight feet high.

He projected this. He pushed the memory into the air between them.

The blue panel flickered.

A second panel appeared, which was green. It displayed a map.

A small, blue dot pulsed in the center of the map.

The map was labeled: *Ashford, Municipal Library, Sub-Level 2.*

"The dot is still there," Gerald said. "The dungeon is not destroyed.

It is suspended.

But it is still *there*.

Therefore, the venue is Ashford. I request the hearing be moved to a physical location within Ashford’s jurisdiction, or at least a virtual construct that accurately represents Ashford’s code."

Vane stared at the green map. The blue dot was pulsing faster.

"You are lying," Vane whispered. "The dungeon is gone. I felt its death."

"You felt its silence," Gerald said. "There is a difference. A building can be silent and still stand.

A body can be comatose and still be alive. You are an auditor, not an executioner.

You do not kill.

You cite. And you cannot cite what is not there."

Vane sat back down, and he rubbed his temples. The tension in the room was palpable.

If Gerald was right, the hearing had to move.

If Gerald was wrong, the suspension was permanent.

"If I move the hearing," Vane said slowly, "it will delay the process. The Inspector General will notice the delay. They may interpret this as obstruction."

"Then let them interpret," Gerald said. "I am not obstructing. I am complying. I am filling out the forms."

Vane looked at the clock. The hands were still moving backward.

"Ten minutes," Vane said. "You have ten minutes to provide physical proof of the dungeon’s existence. If you cannot, I will sign the suspension. And I will destroy your Core."

Gerald opened his eyes. He looked at Vane.

"Physical proof," Gerald said. "I don't have a body. I am here. How do I provide physical proof?"

"Summon something," Vane said. "Something from the dungeon.

A spawn.

A trap.

A piece of the architecture. If you can bring a piece of your dungeon into this room, it proves the dungeon still exists.

If you cannot, you are nothing."

This was the trap. Gerald had no access to his dungeon.

His Core was frozen, and he could not spawn. He could not build. He was cut off.

But he had one thing.

He had the paper.

Gerald looked at the form in front of him. He looked at Vane.

"The paper," Gerald said.

"What about it?"

"The form is printed on paper. Where did you get this paper?"

Vane frowned. "It is standard issue."

"Is it?" Gerald picked up the form.

He rubbed the fibers between his thumb and forefinger. It was rough.

Unbleached.

It smelled of... Pine. And damp earth.

"This is not standard System paper," Gerald said. "This is Ashford municipal stationery. Specifically, the watermarked bond used for building permits."

Vane’s eyes narrowed. "So?"

"So," Gerald said, "you brought this paper from Ashford. You brought it into the Void.

Which means the Void and Ashford are connected.

Which means my dungeon is not just a concept, which is a physical reality that can interact with the Void. Which means I can bridge the gap."

Gerald tore the corner of the form. A small strip of paper.

He held it up.

"I am invoking the Right of Material Evidence," Gerald said. "I am submitting this paper as physical proof of my dungeon’s jurisdiction. The paper exists in the Void.

It was sourced from Ashford. Therefore, the link exists."

Vane stood up. He walked around the desk. He walked up to Gerald. He looked at the strip of paper.

"That is not a spawn," Vane said. "That is a piece of paper."

"It is a fragment of the jurisdiction," Gerald said. "And I will offer more."

Gerald closed his eyes, and he focused on the paper in his hand. He focused on the memory of the library. The smell of the books.

The sound of the HVAC system.

The vibration of the traffic above.

He pushed.

Not with magic. With intent. With the sheer, stubborn will of a man who refuses to be filed incorrectly.

The air in the room changed. The smell of stale coffee vanished. It was replaced by the smell of wet concrete and rusted iron.

A sound echoed in the small room. A dripping sound.

*Drip. Drip. Drip.*

Vane froze. He looked at the floor.

A puddle was forming. Dark, murky water was seeping up through the floorboards of the void-room. It smelled of mold.

Gerald opened his eyes. He looked at the puddle. He looked at Vane.

"Water leak," Gerald said. "Source: Sub-Level 2, Sector 4. The ventilation shaft is compromised. I have a citation pending for that."

Vane looked at the water. He looked at Gerald. His face was unreadable.

"You are bringing the dungeon here," Vane said.

"I am bringing the evidence here," Gerald said. "Is the evidence admissible?"

Vane looked at the blue panel. The rules for Hearings scrolled by. He scanned them. He looked for a rule about admissibility of environmental anomalies.

No rule applied.

The silence stretched.

The water continued to drip. It was spreading. It was touching Gerald’s shoes.

It was warm.

Vane looked at Gerald. A slow, terrifying smile spread across his waxy face.

"You are clever, Mr. Hutchins," Vane said. "You have found a loophole. You have forced a connection."

"Is the evidence admitted?" Gerald asked.

"Yes," Vane said. "The venue is now ambiguous. The hearing is paused. We will reconvene in Ashford."

"Good," Gerald said. "I will see you there."

Vane reached out. He grabbed Gerald’s wrist. His grip was iron.

"Do not celebrate," Vane whispered.

"You have won the pause.

But you have not won the game. The Inspector General is coming.

And they do not like loose ends."

Vane released him.

The room dissolved.

Gerald was back in the void. But he was not alone. The water remained. The smell remained.

And then, a new message appeared in his vision. It was not blue. It was red.

*Notification: System Alert. Unauthorized Cross-Reality Contamination Detected.

Source: Core ID 8940-G (Gerald Hutchins).

Penalty: Immediate Structural Decay. Duration: 10 Minutes.*

Gerald stared at the message.

*Structural Decay.*

His dungeon. The only thing he had left. The thing he had fought to save.

It was beginning to rot, and he looked at the water at his feet. It was turning black.

He had won the hearing. But he had lost the dungeon.
