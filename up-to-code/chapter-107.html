Chapter 107: Improvising the Void

# Chapter 107 The darkness tasted like copper and old pennies. It pressed against Gerald’s face, cold and absolute, trying to fill his lungs with nothingness. The Void King’s shadow-hand wrapped around Gerald’s throat, squeezing until the inspector’s vision spotted with white stars.

Gerald didn’t panic. Panic was inefficient.

Panic led to poor paperwork.

Instead, he did the only thing his muscle memory allowed. He stopped fighting the grip and started measuring it.

The pressure was constant, unyielding, and entirely unregulated.

*Section 402.1: Prohibited Obstruction of Egress.*

*Section 402.2: Use of unpermitted materials in life-safety zones.*

The Void King wasn’t just attacking. He was blocking an exit.

Gerald’s hands, still clutching the golden seal, were numb.

The resin burned, but the pain was distant, muffled by the shock of being choked by a deity. He looked at Kael.

The young dungeon core was frozen, wrench raised but useless.

Kael was looking at the Void King with the expression of a man watching a building collapse in slow motion. He didn’t know what to do because he hadn’t read the code.

Gerald did, and he couldn’t breathe, but he could think. The System was screaming in his peripheral vision.

Red alerts cascaded over his consciousness, overlapping with the Void King’s laughter.

The hearing had technically ended.

The Void King had ruled in his own favor. The seal was open.

The entity was inside.

But an entity entering a registered dungeon without a permit was not a victory. It was a violation.

Gerald slammed his forehead into the Void King’s chin.

It wasn’t a heroic strike. It was a structural assessment. He needed to create space. He needed to break the seal’s physical connection to the floor to interrupt the flow of void energy.

The Void King staggered back, surprised more by the audacity than the impact, and the golden seal, still clutched in Gerald’s bleeding hands, tore free from the stone. A shockwave of purple energy rippled out, shattering the nearby mold spores.

Gerald gasped, sucking in air that smelled of ozone and damp concrete. He coughed, wiping blood from his nose.

His Compliance Rating flashed in his mind: **11/100**.

Critical. Imminent shutdown.

The Void King loomed, towering over him. He was twelve feet of shifting shadow and jagged edges, a hole in the world shaped like a man.

"You resist,"* the Void King whispered. The voice didn’t come from a mouth; it vibrated in Gerald’s teeth. * "But you are merely flesh. I am the End of All Things."*

Gerald straightened his tie. It was ruined. He ignored it.

"You are," Gerald said, his voice raspy, "an unlicensed structure."

The Void King paused. The darkness around him flickered.

Gerald held up the golden seal. It was broken now, cracked down the middle. But it was still the physical anchor of the breach, and gerald had one second left before the Inspector General’s audit finalized.

He didn’t attack the Void King. He attacked the floor.

He slammed the broken seal into the crack in the dungeon foundation where the breach had occurred. He twisted it, jamming it sideways. Not to close the door. To widen it.

**SYSTEM ALERT: STRUCTURAL FAILURE DETECTED.** **ACTION: INITIATING EMERGENCY VENTILATION PROTOCOL.**

A massive fan, hidden within the library’s HVAC system above, roared to life. Air stirred in the sub-basement. Fast. Violent.

The Void King screeched.

Not in pain, but in confusion. Void energy was heavy.

It settled.

It stagnated. It relied on stillness to manifest. But Gerald had just turned his dungeon into a wind tunnel.

The gales hit the Void King, tearing at his edges. He wasn’t being hit by force. He was being dispersed.

*"What are you doing?"* the King hissed.

"Improving air quality," Gerald shouted over the wind. He grabbed Kael’s arm.

"Move!

The ventilation is pulling everything toward the intake!

We’re being suctioned out!"

Kael blinked. "Into the library?"

"Into the municipal HVAC system. Which is, technically, part of the dungeon’s infrastructure. If we stay here, we get sucked into the ducts. If we move to the center, we stay grounded."

Gerald dragged Kael away from the breach point; the Void King roared, trying to reform, but the constant airflow kept shredding his form. He was losing cohesion.

But he wasn’t gone. He was just angry.

The Void King lashed out, and a tendril of shadow whipped across the room, smashing into a stack of shelving units. Books flew everywhere. The ceiling groaned.

**WARNING: STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY 45%.** **WARNING: CEILING COLLAPSE IMMINENT.**

Gerald looked at the ceiling. He looked at the Void King. He looked at Kael.

"We can’t fight him," Gerald said. "We can’t outrun him. And we can’t close the breach fast enough."

"Then what?" Kael yelled.

"We change the zoning."

Gerald’s mind raced. He had spent thirty-one years knowing every loop in the municipal code. He knew that a Dungeon Core could alter its internal layout at will, provided it had enough mana and sufficient structural support.

But altering the layout took time. They didn’t have time.

Unless they used a loophole.

**Loophole 7-B: Emergency Evacuation Route Redefinition.**

A Core could designate any corridor as a primary egress route during a disaster. This granted the route temporary invulnerability and forced all hostile entities to acknowledge it as a safe passage, preventing attacks within that specific space for ten seconds.

It wasn’t combat immunity. It was zoning immunity.

Gerald pointed to the narrow service hallway leading to the boiler room.

"Kael, designate that hallway as the Primary Egress Route. Now."

"I can't!" Kael shouted. "I don't have the authority! You're the Core!"

Gerald was the Core. But his hands were empty, his mana drained from holding the seal. He couldn’t issue a command.

Unless he used the citation, and he had been auditing the Void King.

He had found violations. He had a citation ready. It was sitting in his mental queue, waiting to be served.

But you couldn’t serve a citation to something that was currently attacking you.

You had to be physically present.

Gerald was present, and he ran toward the Void King.

"Gerald, stop!" Kael screamed.

Gerald didn’t stop.

He ran directly into the path of the swirling void storm. The air pressure dropped. His ears popped.

He felt his ribs creaking under the strain, and he pulled the citation from his mind. It wasn’t paper. It was a packet of light, glowing with the dull orange of a violation notice.

He threw it at the Void King.

It passed through the shadow. Nothing happened. The Void King laughed, a sound like grinding stones.

*"You think paper stops the end of the world?"*

Gerald didn’t throw it. He attached it.

He slammed his hand against the Void King’s leg, where the shadow touched the floor, and pressed his palm against the concrete, then against the darkness.

"Service of Process," Gerald whispered.

The System pinged.

**NOTICE SERVED.** **VIOLATION: FAILURE TO MAINTAIN SAFE DISTANCE FROM PUBLIC ACCESS POINT.** **PENALTY: IMMEDIATE KNOCKBACK EFFECT.**

The Void King stumbled.

It wasn’t magic, which was the System enforcing its own rules. By being served with a citation, the Void King had become a registered entity within Gerald’s jurisdiction for exactly three seconds.

And registered entities had to adhere to minimum clearance distances.

Gerald shoved. It was a pathetic shove, the kind a building inspector gives when pointing at a blocked fire exit.

But for those three seconds, the Void King was subject to Gerald’s dungeon physics.

Gerald shoved him toward the designated egress route.

The Void King, confused by the sudden change in physical laws, staggered backward. He looked at the hallway. He looked at Gerald.

*"What is this?"*

"A safe passage," Gerald said. "Move through it, and you won’t be penalized for obstruction."

The Void King hesitated. Gods are arrogant. They believe they can ignore rules because they are above them. But the Void King was currently being held accountable by a System that didn’t care about his divinity, only his compliance.

He stepped into the hallway.

The moment he crossed the threshold, the System recognized him as using the egress route; the penalty for obstruction was paused.

Gerald grabbed Kael and pulled him into the boiler room, sealing the door.

"We bought time," Gerald said, his heart hammering against his ribs. "But we didn’t win. He’s still in there."

Kael slumped against the wall. "He’s going to break the door down. He’s a god, Gerald. You can’t zone a god out of existence."

"I’m not zoning him out," Gerald said. He was breathing hard, wiping the blood from his hands. "I’m trapping him in a transit corridor."

He looked at the boiler room. It was a mess.

Pipes leaked.

Rust coated the walls. The ventilation was terrible. It was a violation nightmare.

But it was also the only place in the dungeon that was structurally reinforced.

"He’s in the hallway," Gerald said. "The hallway is narrow. It’s an egress route. He can’t turn around without violating the code for 'Impaired Turning Radius'."

Kael stared at him. "You’re using the fire code to box him in?"

"I’m using the accessibility code to box him in," Gerald corrected. "Section 302.1: Prohibited Obstruction. If he tries to exit the hallway back into the main room, he’ll be cited for re-entry into a hazardous zone without a permit. The System will stop him."

"That’s insane," Kael said. "That’s bureaucratic suicide."

"It’s municipal strategy," Gerald said.

A loud *crack* echoed from the hallway. The wooden door to the boiler room splintered.

Gerald looked at the crack. Then he looked at the boiler.

The boiler was old, which was non-compliant. It had no pressure relief valve.

It had no shut-off switch.

It was a ticking time bomb.

And it was directly behind the door.

"Gerald," Kael said, his eyes widening. "What are you doing?"

Gerald walked toward the boiler. He didn’t touch it. He looked at the pressure gauge. The needle was shaking.

"He’s trying to break through," Gerald said. "But he doesn’t understand the infrastructure. He thinks this is a dungeon. He thinks this is a place for monsters."

Gerald reached into his pocket and pulled out a item. It wasn’t a weapon. It wasn’t a spell.

It was a wrench.

Kael’s wrench. The one he had dropped earlier.

Gerald picked it up. He looked at Kael.

"Open the door," Gerald said.

"Gerald, no—"

"Open the door, Kael. He’s going to come through. And when he does, I’m going to file a violation against his mass."

Kael hesitated. Then, with a grimace, he pulled the door open.

The Void King was there. He had shrunk, compressed himself to fit through the narrow hallway. He looked smaller now, but denser. He looked like a black hole wrapped in smoke.

"Open,"* the Void King growled. * "Let me pass."*

Gerald stood in front of the boiler. He raised the wrench.

"You are entering a confined space," Gerald said, his voice steady. "You have not filed a Permit for Confined Space Entry. You have not tested for atmospheric hazards. You have not provided a rescue plan."

The Void King lunged.

Gerald didn’t swing the wrench at the King. He swung it at the boiler’s pipe.

He hit the pressure release valve. He jammed it open.

The boiler screamed.

Steam blasted out, hot and white. It filled the narrow hallway instantly. The temperature spiked.

The Void King shrieked. Steam didn’t hurt him. But the sudden change in pressure and temperature caused the void energy to destabilize, and it reacted to the chaotic environment.

The System lit up.

**VIOLATION: THERMAL SHOCK IN UNPROTECTED ZONE.** **PENALTY: TEMPORARY DISINTEGRATION.**

The Void King began to dissolve. Not die. Dissolve. His form lost cohesion in the scalding steam.

But he didn’t disappear. He hardened. He became solid, dark stone.

The steam cleared. The Void King stood there, now a statue of obsidian, and he was trapped in the hallway, unable to move, unable to attack. He was frozen in a pose of rage.

Gerald lowered the wrench. His hands were shaking.

"Did we win?" Kael asked, coughing from the steam.

Gerald looked at the statue. Then he looked at the notification blinking in his mind.

**AUDIT COMPLETE.** **STATUS: COMPLIANT.** **RATING: 85/100.**

Gerald stared at the number. Eighty-five.

That wasn’t a victory. That was a pass. That was a passing grade.

And then, a new message appeared. Not in red. In black.

**INSPECTOR GENERAL NOTE:** *Well done, Inspector. You have successfully detained a Class-1 Threat via improper thermal regulation.* *But your method of enforcement has flagged a new anomaly.* *The statue of the Void King is now considered a permanent fixture of your dungeon.* *As a permanent fixture, it must be inspected.* *As an inspected fixture, it must be maintained.* *If it crumbles, you will be held liable for the release of the entity within.*

Gerald looked at the statue. It was cracking. Small fissures were appearing in the obsidian.

"He’s still in there," Gerald said.

"He’s trapped," Kael said.

"He’s not trapped," Gerald said, walking toward the statue. He ran his hand along the stone surface.

It was cold, which was vibrating.

"He’s waiting."

The statue’s eyes opened. They were white.

"You have inspected me,"* the statue said. The voice was muffled, coming from inside the stone. * "And you have found me wanting."*

Gerald froze.

"I am now part of your structure,"* the Void King continued. * "Which means I am part of your foundation. If I break, your dungeon falls. If I wake, you fall with me."*

Gerald looked at the cracks. They were spreading.

He looked at Kael.

"We didn’t trap him," Gerald said, his voice hollow. "We hired him."

The dungeon floor shook. Not from an earthquake. From the statue’s heartbeat.

Gerald realized he had made a mistake. A huge, catastrophic, code-violating mistake.

He had cited the god. But by citing him, he had registered him.

And now, the god was on the payroll.

**NEW QUEST: MAINTENANCE OF CLASS-1 HAZARD.** **TIME LIMIT: 24 HOURS.** **FAILURE RESULT: TOTAL DUNGEON COLLAPSE.**

Gerald looked at the clock on the wall, and he had twenty-four hours to keep a god from waking up.

And he had no idea how to do that.

Kael looked at him. "Gerald? What do we do?"

Gerald picked up his wrench. He looked at the statue. He looked at the leaking boiler. He looked at the three mold spores trembling in the corner.

"We start a renovation," Gerald said.

He didn’t know if it would work. He didn’t know if it was possible. But for the first time in thirty-one years, Gerald Hutchins didn’t have a form for this.

He had to improvise.

And that made him physically ill.
