Chapter 106: Zoning Laws of Entropy

# Chapter 106 The vibration traveled up through the soles of Gerald’s boots, a low-frequency hum that rattled his molars. It wasn’t just a tremor. It was a frequency. A resonant pitch that matched the exact harmonic of the Void King’s signature, which Gerald had only ever seen listed in the System’s restricted appendix of Forbidden Structural Entities.

"Time is not an option," Gerald said, his voice flat. He didn’t look at the dwarf.

He looked at the gold seal, and the resin was still curing, glowing with a faint, sickly violet hue. "You said the Void King noted my use of Mana Excretion. He’s here to test it.

He doesn’t want to fight me.

He wants to see if my dungeon is structurally sound enough to withstand his presence. If he enters, he brings with him the Void’s own zoning laws.

Entropy."

The dwarf, a stocky man named Kael who had been trying to unionize Gerald’s mold spores for three chapters now, shifted his weight. "So we seal it again? With what?

We’re out of biological assets. You killed the rat, Gerald.

The cat is outside the perimeter and legally distinct from your dungeon footprint.

I can’t just throw a tabby into the core."

"I didn't say use a biological asset," Gerald said. He pulled a clipboard from his spatial inventory.

It was a battered thing, held together with duct tape and spite. "I said get me a catalyst. In municipal construction, when a load-bearing wall is compromised, you don’t just patch it.

You introduce a counter-force. A temporary brace."

"Where do we get a brace for the Void King?" Kael asked.

Gerald tapped the clipboard. "We don’t need a brace. We need a permit."

Kael blinked. "A what?"

"A Variance Permit," Gerald said.

"Section 4, Paragraph 9 of the Ashford Municipal Code, which the System has unfortunately adopted as its metaphysical bylaw, states that any entity attempting to alter the fundamental nature of a space must file for a Variance if the alteration conflicts with the existing zoning. The Void King is trying to convert my Sub-Basement into a Void Zone. That’s a rezoning violation.

He hasn’t filed the paperwork."

The air in the room grew colder. The violet glow of the seal pulsed once, violently. A crack appeared in the center of the gold, thin as a hair but glowing with absolute darkness.

"He’s not going to file paperwork," Kael whispered. "He’s a god of nothingness. He doesn’t care about codes."

"Everyone cares about codes," Gerald said. "Even gods. Because if they don’t, they get shut down. And I have the authority to shut him down."

Gerald stepped toward the seal. The floor beneath him groaned.

The tiles, laid out in perfect two-foot squares, shifted.

The grout lines, applied with high-grade silica, started to dissolve. This was the Void’s weapon. It wasn’t attacking Gerald’s health.

It was attacking his geometry. It was trying to prove that Gerald’s dungeon was non-Euclidean, chaotic, and therefore illegal.

If the dungeon became chaotic, it lost its Compliance Rating. If it lost its rating, it ceased to exist.

"Gerald, back up!" Kael shouted.

Gerald ignored him. He reached into his inventory and pulled out a ruler. Not a magical ruler. A steel ruler, thirty centimeters long, slightly bent at the end from being dropped in 1998.

He placed the ruler against the crack in the seal.

**SYSTEM NOTIFICATION: MEASUREMENT IN PROGRESS** **Anomaly Detected: Dimensional Tearing** **Severity: Critical**

"It’s expanding," Gerald murmured.

"0.4 millimeters per second.

At this rate, the structural integrity of the seal fails in forty seconds. Which means the Void King enters.

Which means my dungeon is condemned."

He closed his eyes. He didn’t think about the Void King. He didn’t think about the death of the rat, though the guilt sat in his stomach like a stone. He thought about Form 11-C: Application for Temporary Structural Variance.

He had never used it. He had spent thirty-one years denying them. But the System had changed the rules. Or rather, Gerald had finally read them closely enough to find the loophole.

"Kael," Gerald said. "Hold this."

He handed the ruler to the dwarf. Kael took it, bewildered.

Gerald raised his hand. He didn’t cast a spell. He didn’t summon a monster. He invoked a System command.

**ACTION: FILE VARIANCE PERMIT** **SUBJECT: Sub-Basement Dungeon** **REQUESTED CHANGE: Temporary Acceptance of Non-Euclidean Geometry** **JUSTIFICATION: Force Majeure / Regulatory Override**

A prompt appeared in Gerald’s vision. A giant, golden stamp.

**ERROR: INSUFFICIENT JUSTIFICATION.** **Variance Permits require a Demonstration of Necessity and a Plan for Mitigation.**

"The Void King is here," Gerald said aloud. "He is the necessity. My mitigation plan is..."

He paused. The crack widened.

The darkness spilled out, not as a liquid, but as a negation of light.

The room's edges blurred. Kael screamed as his left arm flickered out of existence.

"Gerald!"

"I need a mitigation plan!" Gerald shouted.

He looked at the dead rat. The small, curled body of his only friend.

The gold seal had been made from its essence, and the seal was a trap. A bait.

And the Void King had taken the bait.

Gerald realized the error. He had used the rat to seal the hole.

But the hole was not a structural defect, which was a door.

By sealing it, he had trapped the pressure inside. The Void King wasn’t trying to break in.

He was trying to equalize the pressure. Gerald had created a vacuum, and the Void was rushing in to fill it.

To stop the Void, he couldn’t seal the door. He had to widen it.

"Kael, drop the ruler," Gerald said.

"What?"

"Drop it. Now."

Kael dropped the steel ruler. It clattered on the floor.

Gerald looked at the System prompt.

**ACTION: REVOKE VARIANCE REQUEST** **ACTION: ISSUE CITATION FOR UNAUTHORIZED DIMENSIONAL BREACH**

The System hesitated. It had never issued a Citation to a Void Entity before.

Void Entities were considered natural disasters, like earthquakes or floods.

You didn’t cite a flood. You built a levee.

But Gerald wasn’t building a levee. He was citing the flood.

**TARGET: VOID KING (AVATAR)** **VIOLATION: Failure to Obtain Permit for Interdimensional Travel** **PENALTY: Immediate Structural Debuff**

The System processed the request. It took 0.2 seconds.

**CITATION ISSUED.** **SERVING...**

The darkness pouring from the crack froze. It didn’t stop moving, but it stopped *expanding*. The blur around Kael’s arm solidified. The arm returned, whole and trembling.

A message appeared in the air, written in letters of burning black fire.

**HEARING SCHEDULED: IMMEDIATE** **LOCATION: THIS COORDINATE** **PARTIES: GERALD HUTCHINS (CORE) VS. THE VOID KING (ENTITY)**

The floor shook. From the crack, a voice emerged. It sounded like the tearing of parchment.

*"You cite... nothingness?"*

Gerald straightened his tie, and he adjusted his glasses. He looked at the shadow coalescing into a humanoid shape.

It had no face.

Just a void where features should be.

"I cite you for failure to adhere to Ashford Municipal Code Section 12," Gerald said. His voice was steady.

Dry.

Deadpan.

"Section 12 requires that any entity entering a residential or commercial zone must declare their mass and intent. You have neither.

You are an unlicensed alteration to the fabric of reality. I am issuing you a fine."

The Void King laughed. The sound shattered three lightbulbs.

"I am the End,"* the voice said. * "I do not pay fines."*

"You do if you want to stay in this dungeon," Gerald said. "If you leave, you can go anywhere.

If you stay, you are subject to my jurisdiction.

And right now, my jurisdiction says you are violating the building code. Specifically, you are blocking an egress route."

The Void King paused. The shadow tilted its head.

*"Egress?"*

"The door," Gerald said, pointing to the main entrance of the sub-basement.

"You are standing in front of it. You are blocking it.

If an adventurer tries to enter, they cannot.

If an adventurer tries to leave, they cannot. This is a fire hazard. It is a life safety violation.

And I take those seriously."

The Void King seemed to consider this. The System interface flickered.

**HEARING MINI-GAME STARTING.** **OBJECTIVE: PROVE THAT YOUR STRUCTURAL LOGIC IS SUPERIOR TO THE TARGET’S.** **TIME LIMIT: 60 SECONDS.**

Gerald felt the familiar sickness rise in his throat.

The paralysis.

The fear of improvisation. He hated arguments.

He hated debates. He liked forms. He liked stamps.

He liked black and white.

But the Void King was black. It was white. It was everything and nothing.

"I need evidence," Gerald said.

The Void King swirled. *"I am the truth of decay.

All things fall. All things end. Your code is a lie told by the living to comfort themselves.

I am the reality."*

"Reality is irrelevant to the Code," Gerald said. He pulled out a new form. **Form 8-F: Notice of Life Safety Hazard.**

He held it up.

"Look at your mass," Gerald said. "You are dense.

Extremely dense.

If you remain here, you will increase the load on this floor, and the floor is rated for 50 pounds per square foot. You are exerting approximately 200 pounds per square foot.

You are overloading the slab."

The Void King looked down at its feet. The tiles were cracking.

*"I am weightless,"* the King said.

"System, measure the weight," Gerald said.

**MEASUREMENT: 4,500 KILOGRAMS.**

The Void King looked at the floating text. It looked confused.

"You are heavy," Gerald said.

"Therefore, you are a structural liability.

If you do not leave, or if you do not file for a Load Bearing Variance, you must be removed. I am removing you.

Under Section 12, Paragraph 4: 'Any unauthorized structure posing an imminent threat to life safety may be demolished without prior notice.'"

Gerald raised his hand.

**ACTION: DEMOLITION ORDER** **TARGET: VOID KING AVATAR** **METHOD: SYSTEM-ENFORCED EVICTION**

The Void King roared. The sound was a physical blow.

It hit Gerald in the chest, knocking him back, and he slid across the floor, scraping his knees. Kael caught him, grunting under the strain.

"You can't evict a god!" Kael yelled.

"I'm not evicting a god," Gerald gasped. "I'm evicting a violation."

He slammed his hand on the floor.

**EXECUTE DEMOLITION.**

Nothing happened.

The Void King laughed again. * "Your code has no power here, Inspector. I am outside the code." *

Gerald lay on the floor, breathing hard. He looked at the System window.

**ERROR: TARGET LACKS PHYSICAL SUBSTANCE FOR EVICTION.**

Gerald’s heart sank, and he had failed. He had used the right form, but the wrong target.

The Void King wasn’t a building.

It wasn’t a person. It was an idea. And you couldn’t cite an idea.

Unless...

Gerald looked at the gold seal. The seal was a physical object.

It was in the room, which was part of the dungeon.

It was compliant.

But it was also the anchor.

The Void King was anchored to the seal. The seal was his door. If the door was removed, the Void King couldn’t stay.

But removing the seal would break the dungeon’s stability. It would drop the Compliance Rating to zero. It would kill Gerald.

"Gerald," Kael said, shaking him. "We have to run. The hearing is lost.

The Void King is winning. He’s rewriting the room.

The walls are gone."

Gerald looked around. The walls were indeed fading. The sub-basement was becoming a void. Just the floor, the seal, and them.

He had one move left. One loophole he hadn’t tried because it was so stupid, so bureaucratic, so absurd that it might actually work.

He reached into his inventory. He pulled out the **Certificate of Occupancy** for the Ashford Municipal Library, and it was a laminated piece of paper, dated 1984. It had a coffee stain on the corner.

He hadn’t brought it to the dungeon. He had brought it to the *site*.

"Gerald, what are you doing?" Kael asked.

Gerald stood up. He walked to the Void King. The entity towered over him, a swirling vortex of nothingness.

"I am filing an appeal," Gerald said.

The Void King paused. * "An appeal?" *

"Yes. The demolition order was issued against an unauthorized structure. I am arguing that the structure is not unauthorized. It is grandfathered in."

The Void King stopped swirling.

"Grandfathered?"

"The Void," Gerald said. "It existed before the System. Before the Code.

Before Ashford.

According to the System’s own preamble, entities that predate the System are exempt from initial registration laws. They are grandfathered.

Therefore, the Void King is not an unauthorized structure.

He is a historical feature."

The System prompt flashed.

**ANALYSIS: TRUE.** **VOID ENTITIES PREDATE THE SYSTEM CORE REGISTRATION.** **EXEMPTION APPLIES.**

Gerald smiled. It was a small, tight smile.

"However," Gerald said, "the exemption only applies to the *entity*. It does not apply to the *access point*.

The seal is not the Void King, and the seal is a modern addition.

A 2024 addition. It is not grandfathered."

The Void King looked at the gold seal.

*"You would destroy the seal?"*

"I am ordering the removal of the non-compliant accessory structure," Gerald said. "The seal is blocking the egress. It is an unauthorized addition. It must be removed to bring the dungeon back to code."

He looked at Kael.

"Break the seal."

Kael stared at him. "That’s the dungeon’s heart, Gerald! That’s the source of your Mana Regeneration!

If you break it, you lose the passive. You lose the connection.

You’ll be vulnerable."

"I don't care about the passive," Gerald said. "I care about the Code."

The Void King hesitated. It seemed intrigued. This inspector was insane. He was willing to destroy his own dungeon to cite a god.

"Break it," Gerald said. "Or the Void King stays, and the dungeon collapses anyway."

Kael looked at the seal. He looked at Gerald. He picked up a heavy wrench from his belt.

Gerald closed his eyes. He listened for the sound of the snap.

The wrench swung.

There was a sound. But it wasn’t the sound of breaking resin.

It was the sound of a stamp hitting paper.

**SYSTEM NOTIFICATION: APPEAL WON.** **REWARD: VOID RESISTANCE +5%** **PENALTY TO TARGET: VOID KING IS CITED FOR UNAUTHORIZED ACCESS POINT.**

The Void King didn’t vanish. It didn’t laugh. It simply... Froze.

A new message appeared.

**TARGET STATUS: UNDER INVESTIGATION.** **RESTRICTION: TARGET CANNOT USE ACCESS POINT.**

The Void King looked at Gerald. The swirling darkness slowed. It looked almost... Annoyed.

"You filed a lien,"* the Void King said. * "On my existence."*

"I filed a lien on your entry," Gerald corrected. "You are currently under audit, Your Majesty. You cannot leave.

You cannot enter.

You are stuck in the hearing."

The floor shook again. But this time, it wasn’t the Void King. It was the System.

A new notification appeared. Red. Flashing. Urgent.

**SYSTEM ALERT: INSPECTOR GENERAL HAS NOTED THE PRECEDENT.** **ACTION: IMMEDIATE AUDIT OF ALL VOID-ADJACENT DUNGEONS.** **WARNING: IF YOU DO NOT CLOSE THE HEARING IN 30 SECONDS, THE VOID KING WILL BEGRANT THE RIGHT TO CONDEMN YOUR DUNGEON AS A PUBLIC NUISANCE.**

Gerald looked at the timer. 29 seconds, and he looked at Kael.

"How do I close a hearing with a god?" Gerald asked.

Kael looked at the frozen Void King.

"You can't. You have to make him leave. But he can't leave.

Because you cited him."

Gerald realized the trap. He had won the argument, but he had lost the war. He had trapped them both.

He looked at the dead rat, and he looked at the gold seal.

It was unbroken. Kael hadn't swung the wrench.

He had hesitated.

The Void King smiled. A horrible, jagged tear in the darkness.

"The hearing is over, Inspector,"* the Void King said. * "And I rule in my own favor. The seal is compliant. The door is open. And I am coming in."*

Gerald lunged for the seal. He grabbed it with his bare hands. The resin burned his skin. He didn't let go.

"Kael!" Gerald screamed. "The cat! Get the cat! Now!"

But the Void King had already reached him. The darkness touched Gerald’s face. And for the first time in thirty-one years, Gerald Hutchins didn't file a form. He screamed.
