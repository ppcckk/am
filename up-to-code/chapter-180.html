Chapter 180: Non Human Counsel

# Chapter 180

The mop bucket arrived with a wet slap against the concrete floor. Kael skidded to a halt, his boots leaving streaks of mud and blood on the newly polished linoleum. He held the handle with white-knuckled desperation, the yellow plastic pail swaying dangerously.

"Janitor's closet," Kael gasped, bending over to catch his breath. "He wasn't there. I just took it."

Gerald ignored the theft. He focused on the Inspector General, who stood perfectly still in the center of the corridor. The tall, faceless entity tilted its head, the protractor in its hand clicking rhythmically against its thigh.

**VIOLATION: LACK OF WARD STAFF.** **STATUS: NON-COMPLIANT.**

"Gerald," Jinx whispered, her voice tight. "You can't hire staff in four minutes. You don't even have a payroll system."

Gerald didn't answer. He looked at the rat.

The confused rodent sat atop a pile of discarded floor tiles, grooming its whiskers with an air of profound indifference.

Then he looked at Kael. Then he looked at the mop bucket.

The System rule for hospitals was specific.

It required personnel. But the definition of 'personnel' in the municipal code was broader than the dungeon core's usual interpretation.

A hospital required a custodian.

A custodian was a staff member.

"Kael," Gerald said, his voice flat. "Pick up the mop."

"What?"

"The mop. Pick it up. Hold it like you're mopping the floor. Look serious."

Kael blinked. He looked at the Inspector General.

He looked at Gerald.

Then, with the resigned patience of an adventurer who had seen too many weird rituals, he grabbed the wooden handle.

He dipped the mop into the grey water. He wrung it out with aggressive, practiced motions.

**SYSTEM ALERT: WARD STAFF DETECTED.** **CLASSIFICATION: CUSTODIAL.** **COMPLIANCE: PARTIAL.**

The red light over the corridor flickered. It didn't turn green, but the harsh throbbing slowed to a steady pulse.

Jinx stared at the mop. "You're joking."

"I am not joking," Gerald said. He walked over to the water leak. A steady drip was falling from a cracked pipe near the ceiling, pooling on the floor.

According to hospital codes, standing water was a slip hazard. According to general building codes, it was a moisture issue.

Gerald pulled a tape measure from his pocket. He extended it toward the puddle. The metal blade slid out with a metallic *shing*.

"Distance from wall to edge of puddle: twelve inches," he muttered. "Depth: two millimeters. This is not a puddle. It is a spill."

He turned to Kael. "Mop it. Now."

Kael mopped.

The water splashed, but he moved with efficient strokes, clearing the floor. Gerald watched the Inspector General. The entity's protractor had stopped clicking.

It was waiting.

**VIOLATION: INADEQUATE SANITATION PROTOCOLS.** **REMEDIATION: ACTIVE.**

The sterile white light grew brighter. Gerald felt his lungs expand. The air circulation fans, which had been wheezing, kicked into a higher gear. The oxygen returned, sweet and cold.

But the Inspector General did not smile. It stepped forward, its long limbs unfolding.

**VIOLATION: MISSING EMERGENCY EXITS.** **CURRENT CONFIGURATION: SINGLE EGRESS.** **CAPACITY EXCEEDED.**

Gerald froze. His dungeon was a basement.

It had one main staircase and one service door.

The service door led to a utility closet. The utility closet did not lead outside. It led to the sewers.

"Jinx," Gerald said. "Can you break the wall?"

Jinx raised her hands. Green energy crackled around her fingers.

"Which wall?

The load-bearing one?

If I break that, the library above collapses."

"Do not break it," Gerald said sharply. "Measure it."

Jinx lowered her hands. She pulled a small magical compass from her belt and held it against the brick wall behind the Inspector General. "It's solid.

No hidden doors. No vents."

Gerald looked at the wall.

He looked at the blueprint in his mind. He had drawn these walls himself. He had ensured they were up to code.

He had ensured they were safe.

And now safety was killing him.

The Inspector General raised its protractor. The tip glowed with a dull, dangerous red.

"Time remaining: ninety seconds," the entity said. Its voice sounded like grinding stones.

"Mr.

Hutchins. You have demonstrated a custodian. You have demonstrated sanitation.

But a hospital requires emergency egress for patients and staff. You have none.

The violation is fatal."

Gerald's heart hammered against his ribs.

He hated this. He hated improvising.

He hated that the rules were being used against him. He had spent thirty-one years enforcing rules. He had never thought the rules would try to eat him.

*Think,* he told himself. *Think like an inspector.*

What was an emergency exit? It was a path to safety. A path that led out.

The library above was not safe. The sewers were not safe.

But what about the rats?

Gerald looked at the rat. The rat was small. It could fit through spaces humans could not.

"Gerald, what are you doing?" Jinx asked.

Gerald walked to the corner of the room. There was a gap behind a cabinet.

A small, dark space where the foundation of the library had settled.

It was barely eight inches wide. It was a defect. It was a violation.

He crouched down. He pulled a piece of chalk from his pocket. He drew a line on the floor.

"Kael, stop mopping."

Kael froze.

Gerald pointed to the gap. "That is not a wall. That is a ventilation shaft. According to Section 4.2 of the Municipal Code, ventilation shafts are permissible emergency egress routes if they are unobstructed and clearly marked."

Jinx stared at the gap.

"It's a crack in the foundation, Gerald. There's no light.

No ladder.

No signage."

"Signage," Gerald said. "We can fix signage."

He pulled a small sheet of reflective tape from his inventory. It was meant for marking hazardous materials. He slapped it onto the floor, leading from the main corridor to the crack in the wall.

**SYSTEM QUERY: DEFINE EGRESS TYPE.** **OPTIONS: DOOR / WINDOW / VENTILATION SHAFT.**

Gerald selected **VENTILATION SHAFT**.

The System processed the input. The Inspector General paused.

**VIOLATION: MISSING EMERGENCY EXITS.** **RE-EVALUATING...**

The protractor clicked once. Twice.

**ERROR: VENTILATION SHAFTS REQUIRE MINIMUM CLEARANCE OF 18 INCHES FOR HUMAN EVACUATION.** **CURRENT CLEARANCE: 8 INCHES.** **VIOLATION: NON-COMPLIANT.**

Gerald closed his eyes. Of course.

The code always had a catch.

Human clearance.

He wasn't human. He was a core.

He didn't need to evacuate. But his staff did. Kael did.

Jinx did.

Time remaining: forty seconds.

The Inspector General leaned down. Its faceless head was inches from Gerald's.

"Insufficient clearance," the entity said. "The exit is unusable. The violation stands. I am shutting down the ventilation."

The fans sputtered. The sterile white light dimmed. The air grew thick, heavy with the smell of old concrete and fear.

Gerald looked at Kael. Kael was large. Jinx was average. The rat was tiny.

He couldn't change the wall. He couldn't change the gap.

But he could change the definition of the gap.

Gerald opened his mouth. "System. File a variance."

The Inspector General tilted its head. "Variance requests require prior approval and are subject to review."

"File it anyway," Gerald said. "Cite the precedent of *Ashford v. Municipal Library*, case number 44-B. In that case, a narrow utility corridor was deemed an acceptable egress route for maintenance personnel under emergency conditions, provided a secondary escape route was available for the general public."

**SYSTEM SEARCHING...** **PRECEDENT FOUND.** **RELEVANCE: LOW.** **CONDITION: SECONDARY EGRESS REQUIRED FOR GENERAL PUBLIC.**

"Kael," Gerald said, his voice surprisingly calm. "Are you part of the general public?"

Kael looked at the Inspector General. He looked at the glowing protractor. He swallowed hard.

"I am an adventurer," Kael said. "I am a licensed professional. I am maintenance personnel."

The System paused.

**CLASSIFICATION: MAINTENANCE PERSONNEL.** **ACCEPTED.**

"Jinx," Gerald said.

Jinx looked at him. She looked at the crack in the wall. She looked at the reflective tape.

She walked over to the gap. She didn't try to squeeze through. Instead, she raised her staff. Green light gathered at the tip.

"I am not maintenance," she said. "I am the patient."

She slammed the staff against the wall next to the gap.

The bricks shattered. Dust exploded outward. The hole widened.

It wasn't eighteen inches.

But it was enough for a body to twist through.

**VIOLATION: MISSING EMERGENCY EXITS.** **STATUS: REMEDIATED (CONDITIONAL).**

The Inspector General straightened. The protractor lowered.

**TIME EXPIRED.** **AUDIT RESULT: CONDITIONAL PASS.**

The red light vanished. The white light returned, bright and blinding. The fans roared to full speed. The air rushed into Gerald's lungs, cold and clean.

Gerald slumped against the wall. His hands were shaking. He hid them in his pockets before Jinx could see.

The Inspector General stepped back. It pulled a small notebook from its robe. It wrote something down.

"Mr. Hutchins," the entity said. "You have passed the inspection. However, you have incurred a new penalty."

Gerald pushed himself upright. "What penalty?"

**PENALTY: ADMINISTRATIVE FINE.** **AMOUNT: 500 COMPLIANCE POINTS.** **REASON: UNAUTHORIZED ALTERATION OF STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY.**

Gerald's stomach dropped. He didn't have 500 points. He barely had 50. He had spent them all on the handrails.

"Can I pay in installments?" Gerald asked.

The Inspector General smiled. It was the same not-nice smile from before.

"No," it said. "You do not have the currency. Instead, the System will garnish your dungeon's future earnings. Every copper coin earned by your dungeon for the next thirty days will be directed to the System Treasury."

Thirty days.

With no income, Gerald couldn't buy upgrades. He couldn't fix the pipes. He couldn't hire real staff.

He would be stuck with a mop bucket and a rat.

"Is that all?" Gerald asked.

"No," the Inspector General said. "There is one final matter."

It turned to the hole Jinx had made. It pointed to the darkness beyond the bricks.

"You cited a precedent for maintenance egress. Very clever. But you failed to note the destination of that egress route."

Gerald looked at the hole. Beyond the bricks, there was a dark tunnel. It led away from the library. It led toward the city sewers.

"Where does it lead?" Gerald asked.

"To the Ashford Underworks," the Inspector General said. "Which is currently under the jurisdiction of Core Unit 7-Alpha. A rival dungeon."

Gerald felt a chill that had nothing to do with the ventilation.

"The Underworks is classified as a Hazardous Waste Disposal Site," the entity continued. "By citing it as an emergency exit, you have officially linked your dungeon to a hazardous zone. This triggers a Cross-Contamination Protocol."

**SYSTEM ALERT: CROSS-CONTAMINATION DETECTED.** **SOURCE: CORE 7-ALPHA (THE UNDERWORKS).** **STATUS: HOSTILE.** **ACTION REQUIRED: QUARANTINE.**

Jinx looked at Gerald. "Who is Core 7-Alpha?"

Gerald closed his eyes.

He remembered the reports.

The Underworks was a sewer dungeon. It was nasty.

It was aggressive. It was run by a core that specialized in poison and disease.

"It's a rival," Gerald said.

"He's going to come through the exit you made," Jinx said.

"No," Gerald said. He looked at the hole. He looked at the reflective tape. He looked at the mop bucket.

He reached into his inventory. He pulled out a roll of industrial-strength duct tape.

"Kael," Gerald said. "Get the bucket. Jinx, hold your staff ready."

"Gerald, what are you doing?" Jinx asked.

Gerald walked to the hole. He taped the edges of the broken bricks. He taped them shut. He taped them tight.

"I am not closing the exit," Gerald said. "I am sealing the contamination vector."

He finished the seal. He stepped back.

**VIOLATION: BLOCKED EGRESS.** **STATUS: CRITICAL.**

The Inspector General stepped forward. "You have blocked the only exit. This is a critical violation. I must shut down the dungeon."

Gerald raised his hand. "Wait. Check the code."

The Inspector General paused.

"Section 8.1, Subsection C," Gerald said. "In the event of a hazardous containment breach, emergency exits may be sealed to prevent the spread of contamination, provided a secondary, unaffected egress route remains active."

Gerald pointed to the main staircase.

"The main staircase is clear. It is not contaminated.

The sealed exit is the contaminated one.

Therefore, the seal is not a violation. It is a containment measure."

The System processed this. The protractor hovered over Gerald's head.

The Inspector General stared at him. The air was silent.

Then, the entity nodded.

**VIOLATION DISMISSED.** **CONTAINMENT ACCEPTED.**

Gerald exhaled.

But the Inspector General was not done. It pulled a second scroll from its robe.

"Mr.

Hutchins. You have successfully contained the immediate threat.

However, the Core of the Underworks has detected your seal.

It has identified you as a hostile entity attempting to restrict its flow of waste."

A low rumble vibrated through the floor. It wasn't the System. It was real.

From the other side of the taped-up wall, a sound emerged. It was a wet, squelching noise. Like something large moving through thick mud.

**SYSTEM ALERT: BREACH ATTEMPT DETECTED.** **SOURCE: CORE 7-ALPHA.** **METHOD: ACID CORROSION.**

The tape on the wall bubbled. Green smoke rose from the seams.

Gerald looked at Jinx. "You broke the wall. Now you need to fix it."

"How?" Jinx asked. "I can't heal stone."

"You can't heal it," Gerald said. "But you can reinforce it."

He pointed to the mop bucket. "Water is a conductor.

Acid is a chemical.

If you freeze the water in the pipe above the leak, the pressure will increase.

The burst pipe will flood the area. Water dilutes acid."

Jinx looked at the pipe. "You want me to freeze the leak?"

"Yes."

"That will break the pipe completely. The basement will flood."

"Better than being eaten by acid sludge," Gerald said. "Do it."

Jinx raised her staff. She aimed at the cracked pipe.

**SYSTEM QUERY: WATER DAMAGE VIOLATION IMMINENT.** **ACCEPT?**

"Accept," Gerald said.

Jinx fired a bolt of ice magic. It hit the pipe. The metal groaned. It shattered.

Water gushed out. It poured onto the floor, mixing with the acid that was eating through the tape. Steam rose, hissing and popping.

The green smoke dissipated. The bubbling tape slowed.

Gerald looked at the water spreading across the floor. It was knee-high. It was ruining his inventory. It was ruining his paperwork.

**VIOLATION: FLOODING.** **STATUS: ACTIVE.**

The Inspector General looked at Gerald. It wrote in its notebook again.

"You have saved your dungeon, Mr. Hutchins," the entity said. "But you have created a new problem.

Your Compliance Rating has dropped due to flood damage. You are now at 32/100."

Gerald looked at the water. He looked at the sealed wall. He could hear scratching on the other side. Something was trying to get out.

"What happens now?" Gerald asked.

"Now," the Inspector General said, "the Underworks Core will file a complaint for obstruction of public infrastructure. They will demand a Hearing."

Gerald felt a wave of nausea.

A Hearing. He had won one before. But this wasn't a simple fire code dispute.

This was a war against a sewer monster.

"Who is the opposing counsel?" Gerald asked.

The Inspector General smiled.

"They are not human," it said. "And they do not respect your code."

The water rose another inch. The scratching stopped.

And then, the tape on the wall tore open.
