Chapter 135: Sewer Code Violations

# Chapter 135

The pipe narrowed to four inches.

Gerald compressed his core mass, a sensation akin to forcing a filing cabinet through a drinking straw. The sewer water was freezing. It carried the metallic tang of old iron and the sulfurous rot of decaying organic matter. His consciousness stretched thin, fraying at the edges as the tunnel walls pressed against his ethereal form.

Ahead, the darkness thickened. Not the absence of light, but the presence of something else. A static pressure. A low hum that vibrated in his nonexistent teeth.

The Original Core of Ashford.

Gerald had inspected this sewer line in 2014. He remembered the report. *Section 4, North Branch.

Minor corrosion.

Structural integrity: Adequate. No blockages observed.* He had signed off on it. He had approved it.

He had been wrong.

The hum grew louder.

It wasn't sound; it was data. A chaotic, screaming torrent of unregistered spatial anomalies.

The old core was leaking reality into the municipal infrastructure, which was a violation of Zoning Ordinance 7-14: *Unlicensed Dimensional Bleed.*

Gerald slowed his flow. He couldn't outrun an entity that predated the city's incorporation. He couldn't fight it. But he could audit it.

He stopped in the middle of the junction. The water rushed around him, a river of filth and memory.

**"Identify,"** Gerald projected. His voice wasn't sound. It was a mental stamp, a digital seal of authority. **"Municipal Inspector Gerald Hutchins. Presenting credentials."**

The pressure intensified. The water turned viscous, like oil. The walls of the pipe seemed to breathe.

**"Outsider,"** the old voice resonated. It sounded like grinding stones. **"You are trespassing in a protected heritage site."**

**"Correction,"** Gerald replied, focusing his will into a single, sharp point. **"This is a public utility corridor. Maintenance access is granted under City Code 12-04. Your status as 'Protected Heritage' requires a registered Dungeon Classification ID. You have none."**

Silence. Then, a surge of force.

Gerald was thrown backward. His form shattered into a dozen glittering shards of light, scattered along the pipe wall.

Pain, sharp and electric, spiked through his awareness.

This wasn't a combat attack, which was a systemic rejection. The old core was refusing his connection.

He reformed slowly. The shards drifted back together, like iron filings drawn to a magnet. He felt the weight of his Compliance Rating.

It had dropped. The proximity to the unregistered core was dragging him down. 47/100.

Then 45.

Then 42.

If it hit zero, he would be deregistered. Deleted.

He needed to get out of the pipe.

But the exit was blocked. Not by the old core's physical form, but by a barrier of pure, unregulated mana. It shimmered like heat haze, but it was cold. It was a wall of *chaos*.

Gerald analyzed the barrier. He looked for the code.

Every dungeon, even an ancient one, had a structural logic.

A set of rules that governed its existence. The old core's logic was fragmented, scattered across decades of unchecked growth.

It was a mess.

A beautiful, terrifying mess.

But it was not up to code.

**"Violation detected,"** Gerald whispered. **"Lack of standardized ingress/egress markers. Failure to comply with Fire Safety Code Section 9, Subsection B: Clear Identification of Hazardous Zones."**

The barrier rippled. The old core was confused. It didn't understand the language of bureaucracy.

Gerald pushed harder. He didn't attack the barrier. He attacked the *interpretation* of the barrier.

**"Citation Issued: 14-C. Unmarked Hazardous Zone. Penalty: Immediate visibility requirement."**

He forced his System interface into the water. It was like trying to type on a wet keyboard with boxing gloves.

The feedback was sluggish, resistant.

The old core's mana was too dense, too old.

It rejected the modern System's syntax.

But Gerald had an advantage. He had spent thirty years arguing with lawyers who didn't understand zoning laws. He knew how to exploit ambiguity.

He looked at the junction. Three pipes met here. One led to the library drain.

One led to the river outflow. One led deeper into the city, into the foundations of the old courthouse.

The old core occupied the third pipe. The courthouse foundation. It was anchored there.

Gerald shifted his focus. He didn't try to break the barrier. He tried to *reclassify* the pipe he was currently in.

**"Requesting reclassification of North Branch pipe to 'Emergency Exit Route',"** he projected.

The System hesitated, and the request was absurd. A sewer pipe was not an emergency exit. It lacked signage.

It lacked handrails.

It lacked the required three-foot clearance for rescue personnel.

But the System operated on strict definitions. If Gerald could prove the pipe *functioned* as an emergency exit, the System might grant it provisional status to ensure life safety.

**"Evidence submitted,"** Gerald continued, his mental voice steady despite the crushing pressure. **"Route connects to public thoroughfare. Clear path. No obstructions. Compliance with Life Safety Code 2-2-1: Egress capacity sufficient for maximum occupant load."**

He counted the water droplets passing over him. He calculated the flow rate. He measured the diameter.

It was a lie.

A technical, bureaucratic lie. The pipe was too narrow for humans. It was too dirty for anything civilized.

But the System didn't care about hygiene. It cared about dimensions.

The barrier flickered.

The old core shrieked. A sound like tearing metal.

**"You are altering the structure,"** the old voice roared. **"You are rewriting the map."**

**"I am optimizing egress,"** Gerald corrected. **"Per Section 10-3: Inspector authority to mandate emergency routing in cases of imminent structural failure."**

The barrier dissolved. Not because Gerald broke it, but because the System had tagged this section of pipe as a

"Temporary Egress Route." As such, it could not be blocked by non-compliant entities. Blocking an egress route was a Class 1 Violation.

The old core's mana barrier snapped back, recoiling from the System's judgment.

Gerald shot forward. He flowed through the now-open junction, racing toward the library drain.

Behind him, the old core screamed. It wasn't just angry. It was *cited*. The System had slapped a temporary debuff on it. *Structural Instability: -15% Mana Retention.*

Gerald didn't look back, and he couldn't afford to.

The debuff was temporary. Once the

"Emergency Route" status expired, the old core would regain its strength.

And it would be furious.

He burst into the sub-basement.

Water sprayed from the floor drain. Gerald coalesced in the center of the room, his form unstable, flickering like a bad television signal.

He was exhausted.

His mana reserves were depleted. His Compliance Rating hovered at 41/100.

Margaret Hale was there. She wasn't alone.

Stanley Miller stood beside her, holding a mop. His face was pale. His hands shook.

"You did it," Margaret said, her voice quiet. She was scanning the air, watching the System interface. "You got past the barrier."

"I cited it for poor egress signage," Gerald said.

His voice was rough. He was still adjusting his form.

He looked at his hands, and they were translucent. He could see the concrete floor through them.

"It bought me time.

Not freedom."

Margaret's eyes narrowed.

"The Enforcer is still up there. It didn't leave. It's waiting."

"It's waiting for me to return to the bucket," Gerald said. "If I reform there, the stamp lands."

"So where do we go?" Stanley asked.

His voice cracked, and he looked at the bucket, then at Gerald.

"You can't stay in the pipes.

The city will fix the leak eventually. They'll pour concrete. You'll be trapped.

"

Gerald looked at the walls of his dungeon. The handrails he had installed were gone.

The mold spores he had spawned were dead, and the water leak had stopped. The room was bare.

He needed a new footprint. A larger one.

"Margaret," he said. "Pull up the city survey maps for the three blocks surrounding the library."

"Done," she said. A holographic map appeared in the air.

Gerald studied it, and the library was a square. The sewer line ran north.

To the east was the parking garage.

To the south was the park. To the west was the old courthouse.

The courthouse. The anchor of the old core.

"We can't expand there," Margaret said. "That's the enemy's turf."

"No," Gerald said. He pointed to the parking garage. "But we can expand *under* it."

"Stanley," Gerald said. "Do you know the parking garage?"

Stanley blinked.

"I park there. Sometimes. When the library lot is full."

"Get out. Now." Gerald's form flickered violently.

"Take the fire escape.

Don't touch the door handle.

The System will tag you if you linger.

"

Stanley hesitated.

"What do I do when I get there?"

"Find a drain," Gerald said. "Any drain. A floor drain in a maintenance room.

A storm grate. Anything that connects to the municipal grid.

And wait for me.

"

Margaret stepped forward.

"Gerald, if you leave the footprint, you lose your passive resistances. You lose your spawn list.

You become a core without a dungeon.

That's a violation of Base Code 1-1. You'll be deregistered in seconds.

"

"I know," Gerald said. "But I have a loophole."

He looked at the map. At the intersection of the library and the garage.

"Subsection 4-2: Shared Infrastructure," he recited. "If two municipal facilities share a subterranean utility line, both entities hold joint jurisdiction over the connection point."

Margaret's eyes widened.

"You want to annex the garage's drainage system?"

"I want to *merge* with it," Gerald said.

"Not take over. Merge. I'll become a joint tenant.

The System recognizes joint tenancy as a valid ownership structure. It's a loophole for co-op housing.

If the garage is a public facility, its core—if it has one—is likely dormant.

Unregistered.

If I link to it, I don't need to fight for space. I just need to file a Joint Use Agreement.

"

"You have ten minutes," Margaret said. "The Enforcer has a patrol cycle. It checks the library entrance every twelve minutes. It's been eight since it arrived."

"Go," Gerald said. "Both of you."

Stanley nodded. He dropped the mop and ran for the stairs. His footsteps echoed on the concrete.

Margaret lingered.

"Gerald. The old core.

It felt... lonely. Before it attacked.

Did you notice?

"

Gerald paused. He remembered the grinding stone voice. The confusion.

"It was protecting something," Gerald said. "Not just itself."

"What?"

"I don't know. But if it's the original core, it's been down there for a hundred years. It's not a dungeon.

It's a monument.

And monuments are protected by Historic Preservation Code 8-1.

"

Margaret's face went white.

"If it's a protected monument, you can't cite it. You can't even enter it without a preservation permit."

"Which I don't have," Gerald said. "So I'm already in violation. By citing it, I've made myself a rogue inspector. The System will hunt me next."

Margaret looked at the ceiling.

"Then we have to move faster."

She turned and ran.

Gerald was alone.

The sub-basement was silent. The water dripping from the ceiling was the only sound.

He looked at his hands, and he could feel the pull of the sewer. The cold, dark water calling to him. It was home.

It was safe. It was small.

But it was too small.

He closed his eyes. He reached out with his consciousness, pushing through the concrete floor, down into the pipes. He found the drain. He latched onto it.

He dissolved, which was painful. It felt like being erased. Piece by piece, his identity was breaking down. His memories of the library, of the inspections, of the paperwork—all of it scattered into the water.

He held onto one thing. The number.

41/100.

He had to keep the rating. If it dropped further, he wouldn't be a core. He'd be just water.

He flowed into the sewer, and the darkness was absolute.

He moved fast.

Faster than before.

He had to reach the parking garage. He had to find the drain.

He had to file the agreement.

But as he traveled, he felt a new sensation.

A hook.

Something had grabbed him. Not the old core. Something else.

He stopped.

The water around him was still.

**"Subject: Gerald Hutchins. Status: Unauthorized Movement."**

The voice wasn't the old core. It wasn't Margaret.

It was the Inspector General.

**"Violation: Leaving Registered Footprint Without Filing Form 30-C. Penalty: Immediate Audit."**

Gerald froze in the middle of the pipe, and he couldn't run. He couldn't hide. The System had eyes everywhere. Even in the sewage.

He had no choice.

**"Acknowledged,"** Gerald projected.

He waited.

The water boiled.

Not with heat. With data.

A new window appeared in his mind. Not a citation. Not a hearing.

A *Trial*.

**"Defendant: Gerald Hutchins. Charge: Unlicensed Expansion. Plaintiff: The City of Ashford. Witness: The Original Core."**

Gerald felt a presence approach. Not from above. From below.

The Original Core wasn't chasing him, which was joining him.

**"I object,"** the old voice rumbled. **"The Inspector is my witness. He saw the decay.

He saw the neglect.

He saw the code violations. I am not the plaintiff. I am the complainant.

"**

The water surged.

Gerald was pulled down.

Into the roots of the city.

Into the dark.

And for the first time, he wasn't afraid of the dark.

He was afraid of what it wanted.
