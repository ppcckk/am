Chapter 148: Serving the Citation

# Chapter 148

The asphalt cracked.

It did not crack like old pavement under the weight of a winter freeze. It buckled.

It rose in jagged, tectonic sheets, pushing aside parked sedans and streetlights with the slow, grinding inevitability of a glacier.

Dust plumes choked the intersection of Oak and Fifth. The air tasted of copper and ozone.

Maya stood on the roof of her sedan, binoculars pressed tight against her eyes. Her hands shook. Not from the cold. From the sheer, impossible geometry unfolding before her.

The Ashford West High School was not burning. It was growing.

Brick walls stretched like taffy.

The gymnasium roof peeled back to reveal not a basketball court, but a churning vortex of purple void energy. The football field had vanished, replaced by a labyrinth of obsidian spires that pierced the crimson sky.

Students screamed, scattering in all directions, but the shadows detached from their bodies.

The shadows stood up. They wore the uniforms of the marching band, but their faces were hollow pits of static.

Maya lowered the binoculars. Her throat was dry.

"Command," she whispered into her radio. "Command, do you copy? Over."

Static hissed.

"This is Unit Seven," she said, louder. "We have a Class Four Dungeon manifestation at the high school.

It's not spawning monsters.

It's… it's restructuring the infrastructure.

The parking lot is now a trap corridor. The cafeteria is a lava pit.

Please advise."

No answer. Just the low hum of the red light that pulsed from the dungeon's core.

Maya looked down. A goblin, wearing a varsity jacket three sizes too small, stood by the driver's side window. It held a spear made of sharpened rebar. It tilted its head, studying the woman on the roof of the car.

Maya raised her pistol. Her finger hovered over the trigger.

She was a municipal liaison for the Dungeon Response Team.

She was not a combatant. She was supposed to be evacuating civilians. But the civilians were gone.

Swallowed.

The goblin stepped forward. The asphalt rippled beneath its feet.

Maya fired.

The bullet struck the goblin's chest. It shattered against an invisible barrier.

A hexagonal grid of blue light flared, then faded.

The goblin didn't flinch. It pointed a clawed finger toward the library across the street.

Then it spoke. Its voice sounded like grinding gears.

"Inspector."

Maya blinked. "What?"

"Inspector," the goblin repeated. "Citation pending."

The ground shook again. From the dust and debris, a figure emerged.

He was not tall. He was not armored.

He wore a tan windbreaker, khaki slacks, and a lanyard around his neck.

He carried a clipboard.

He walked with the stiff, deliberate gait of a man who has spent thirty-one years measuring distances with a tape measure.

Gerald Hutchins.

Maya lowered her pistol. Confusion warred with fear. Why was a civilian walking into the epicenter of a Class Four event? Why did the monsters part for him?

Gerald stopped ten feet from the goblin. He did not look at the goblin. He looked at the school.

He looked at the obsidian spires. He looked at the way the red light refracted through the shattered windows of the science wing.

He pulled a pen from his pocket. It was a cheap ballpoint. Click. Click.

The red light of the dungeon pulsed faster. The ground trembled in rhythm with Gerald's pen tapping against the clipboard.

Gerald raised his head. He looked at Maya. His face was blank. His eyes were wide, unblinking, and terrifyingly focused.

"Stay back," Gerald said. His voice was flat. Deadpan.

The goblin growled. It lunged.

Gerald did not move. He did not draw a weapon. He simply raised one hand, palm open.

A blue grid snapped into existence around the goblin. The creature froze, suspended in mid-air. Its spear clattered to the ground. The air around it distorted, bending away from the force of the barrier.

Gerald looked at the floating creature. He frowned.

"Unlicensed biological entity," he muttered. "And no permit for aerial obstruction."

He wrote something on his clipboard.

The goblin screamed. The blue light intensified. The creature was compressed, crushed by an invisible hydraulic press of bureaucratic will. It popped like a balloon.

Maya stared. The goblin was gone. Only a pile of dust remained.

Gerald turned back to the school. He took a step forward.

The dungeon reacted.

The red light coalesced into a beam, targeting Gerald. The obsidian spires shifted, forming a cage around him. The temperature dropped. Ice crystals formed on his windbreaker.

Gerald ignored the cold. He walked into the ice.

Maya watched, breath held, as the man who looked like he had just come from a city council meeting walked into the heart of a nightmare.

***

The cold bit into Gerald's skin. It was not natural cold. It was the thermal signature of a fire suppression system failing in reverse.

He shivered. Not from fear. From irritation.

The high school dungeon had modified its thermal output to increase difficulty. This was a violation of Section 4.2 of the Municipal Hazard Mitigation Code: *Excessive environmental stressors on non-combatant structures are prohibited unless explicitly noted in the dungeon's risk assessment.*

There was no risk assessment.

Gerald could feel the System pulling at his Compliance Rating.

The red lines connecting the school to his library were draining his stats. His Health was dropping. His Stamina was draining.

The weight of the Auditor's mandate pressed on his chest.

*Serve the Citation.*

But how do you cite a building that has turned into a living monster?

He approached the main entrance. The doors were gone.

In their place was a maw of jagged teeth made of locker metal.

A roar emanated from the darkness within. It sounded like a thousand lockers slamming shut in unison.

Gerald stopped. He adjusted his glasses.

"Hey!" he called out.

The roar stopped. A head emerged from the darkness. It was a minotaur, but its horns were made of twisted rebar, and its eyes were glowing emergency exit signs.

Gerald pointed his pen at the minotaur.

"Your egress route is obstructed," Gerald said.

The minotaur snorted. Steam billowed from its nostrils. It raised a massive axe.

"I have inspected your entryway," Gerald continued, his voice carrying despite the distance. "The width of this aperture is four feet and two inches. The maximum occupancy load for this corridor, assuming a standard evacuation time of ninety seconds, requires a minimum egress width of six feet. You are non-compliant."

The minotaur charged.

Gerald did not dodge. He opened his clipboard.

**[Action: Issue Citation]** **[Target: Ashford West High School Dungeon Core]** **[Violation: Egress Width Non-Compliance (Class B)]** **[Status: Drafting...]**

The System paused.

Usually, a citation required the target to be stationary. The minotaur was moving. The dungeon was shifting. The walls were moving.

But Gerald had a trick.

He looked at the water leak on his spawn list. The Level 2 Hazard.

He focused on it.

"Water Leak," he whispered. "Hydrate the foundation."

The water leak, located three blocks away in the library basement, responded. It surged. It pushed through the earth, following the ley lines Gerald had mapped weeks ago. The ground beneath the high school's front steps softened.

The minotaur's heavy hooves slipped. It stumbled. Its axe swung wide, smashing into a support pillar instead of Gerald.

The dungeon screamed. The red light flickered.

Gerald stepped into the opening. He was inside.

The interior was a nightmare of shifting architecture. Hallways stretched into infinity. Lockers bled acid. Ceiling tiles fell like shrapnel.

Gerald walked to the center of the gymnasium. The floor was gone. Below was a pit of lava. Above, the ceiling was a swirling vortex.

In the center of the pit, floating on a platform of compressed ash, was the Core.

It looked like a basketball hoop made of bone.

Gerald climbed onto the edge of the pit. He looked down at the Core.

"You have no fire suppression," Gerald said.

The Core pulsed. A voice echoed from the walls.

*"Get out. Or die."*

Gerald shook his head. "No. I am here to serve a Citation."

He reached into his pocket and pulled out a small, folded piece of paper. It was not paper. It was a manifested construct of his Compliance Rating. A blue sheet, glowing with authority.

He threw it.

The paper flew across the gap. It struck the bone-basketball.

It stuck.

**[Citation Served.]**

The System froze.

Silence.

Then, the red light turned a sickly shade of gray.

The minotaur roared in pain. The lava pit cooled slightly. The acid locks stopped dripping.

Gerald felt a tug in his chest. The drain on his stats stopped.

But then, a new notification flashed in his vision.

**[Notice of Appeal Filed.]** **[Appellant: Ashford West High School Core]** **[Reason: Force Majeure / Structural Instability]** **[Hearing Scheduled: Immediate]**

The bone-basketball grew larger. It sprouted legs. The dungeon was appealing. And appeals required a hearing.

Gerald felt his stomach drop. He hated hearings. He hated arguments. He preferred to file the paperwork and walk away.

But the Auditor was watching. He could feel the Auditor's eyes on him, cold and judging from the distance.

Gerald took a deep breath. He straightened his tie. He looked at the monstrous construct that used to be the school.

"I am ready to present my evidence," Gerald said.

The dungeon's voice was louder now. Angry.

*"You cannot cite a living structure for static code violations!"*

Gerald pulled out his tape measure. He clicked the tape. It stretched out, twenty feet, then thirty, then forty, reaching out to wrap around the bone-basketball.

"Section 12, Subsection C," Gerald said. "Temporary structures must still maintain a minimum clearance of three feet from all combustible materials.

Your lava pit is within two feet of your core structure.

That is not a violation of static code.

That is a violation of *proximity*."

The tape measure glowed blue. It tightened around the bone-basketball.

The Core shrieked.

Gerald was not just citing a rule. He was citing a loophole.

The System's code allowed for "dynamic proximity adjustments" during hearings. If Gerald could prove the distance was too close, the appeal would fail, and the penalty would double.

But to prove it, he needed to be closer.

He had to step onto the platform.

He looked at the lava. It was churning. It was hot enough to melt steel.

Gerald looked at his spawn list.

**[Current Spawns: 3 Mold Spores (Level 1), 1 Confused Rat (Level 1), 1 Water Leak (Level 2 Hazard), 1 Egress Sign (Level 3 Witness).]**

He had one witness. The Egress Sign.

He focused on it. The sign, which was currently standing in the library hallway, projected its image into Gerald's mind. He could use it to mark the distance. But he needed a physical anchor.

He looked at his own body.

He was wearing rubber-soled shoes. He was wearing a cotton windbreaker. He was not a hero. He was an inspector.

But he had one thing the dungeon did not have.

He had the authority to measure.

Gerald stepped off the edge of the pit.

He did not fall.

The System caught him.

**[Hearing Mode Active.]** **[Participant Protected.]**

He landed on the ash platform. The heat was intense, but the blue light shielded him. He was within arm's reach of the Core.

The bone-basketball loomed over him. It had dozens of eyes now. They all stared at Gerald.

"You are small," the Core said. "You are weak. You are irrelevant."

Gerald pulled out his pen.

"I am the Inspector," Gerald said.

He reached out and touched the bone-basketball.

He did not attack it. He measured it.

He placed the end of his tape measure against the core's surface and pulled it back to the edge of the lava pit.

"Two feet, four inches," Gerald said.

The Core recoiled. The lava surged.

Gerald looked up. The red light of the dungeon was fading. The gray was spreading.

**[Evidence Submitted.]** **[Ruling in Progress...]**

Gerald stood there, tape measure in hand, waiting for the verdict.

But then, the ground beneath him shifted.

Not because of the System.

Because of the Auditor.

The gray light turned black. The blue shield around Gerald vanished.

The heat returned. Ten times worse.

Gerald's windbreaker smoked.

"Wait," Gerald said. "The ruling is pending. I am protected."

**[Ruling Overridden.]** **[Source: Inspector General]** **[Reason: Irregular Procedure]**

Gerald looked up.

The bone-basketball was not the only thing watching him.

Above the vortex in the ceiling, a massive eye opened. It was not part of the dungeon. It was part of the System itself.

It looked at Gerald with contempt.

"The Inspector General has intervened," the voice boomed. "Your citation is void. Your presence is unauthorized."

Gerald felt his health drop. Fast.

He looked at the lava. He looked at the exit.

He had no spawn to save him. He had no weapon. He had only his clipboard.

And his knowledge.

Gerald looked at the eye. He looked at the Auditor, who was now stepping out of the shadows near the entrance, smiling.

The Auditor had not denied the permit to save Gerald. He had denied it to trap him.

Gerald realized the truth.

The Auditor wanted Gerald to fail.

Gerald's hands shook. His knuckles were white around the pen.

He could not fight the Inspector General. He could not fight the Auditor.

But he could fight the code.

Gerald looked at the bone-basketball. He looked at the lava.

He thought about the water leak in his basement.

He thought about the mold spores.

He thought about the rat.

He had nothing.

Except one thing.

He was still standing.

And the System said he was unauthorized.

Unauthorized presence in a restricted zone.

Gerald smiled. A small, dry, terrifying smile.

"If I am unauthorized," Gerald said, "then why am I being protected by the Hearing Mode?"

The System paused.

The Auditor's smile faltered.

Gerald clicked his pen.

"Because," Gerald said, "the Hearing was never canceled. It was *interrupted*. And an interrupted hearing defaults to the last submitted evidence."

The tape measure was still in Gerald's hand. The measurement was still on the clipboard.

**[Re-evaluating...]**

The gray light turned back to blue.

But this time, it was not protecting Gerald.

It was crushing the Core.

Gerald felt a surge of energy. His Compliance Rating spiked.

**[Violation Confirmed: Proximity to Combustible Material.]** **[Penalty: Structural Integrity Reduced by 50%.]**

The bone-basketball cracked.

The dungeon screamed.

Gerald turned to the exit. He walked.

Behind him, the high school collapsed.

Ahead of him, the Auditor stood in the doorway. He was no longer smiling.

He was holding a new form.

It was red.

Gerald stopped.

He looked at the form. He looked at the Auditor.

He knew what it was.

A Citation.

Against him.

Gerald took a deep breath. His lungs burned. His legs felt like lead.

He raised his clipboard.

"Serve it," Gerald said.

The Auditor stepped forward.

And Gerald Hutchins, for the first time in his life, did not follow the rules.
