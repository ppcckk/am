Chapter 403: Sub-Basement Liability

# Chapter 403

The darkness below the library floor was not empty. It was wet.

Barnaby Thorne adjusted his visor, the enchanted glass clicking into place over his eyes. The lens magnified the cracked masonry, the weeping mortar joints, and the stagnant puddle spreading across the sub-basement floor. To a paladin, this was a battlefield. To Barnaby, it was a structural liability.

He stepped down onto the debris. His plate armor clanked, a heavy, rhythmic sound that echoed off the low ceilings.

Behind him, two squires hovered near the broken floorboards, looking pale.

They had expected a dragon's hoard. They had found a flooded room with a single, shattered emblem lying in the corner.

"Where is it?" Barnaby whispered. His voice was a practiced rumble, designed to instill fear.

Silence answered. Only the drip of water from a rusted pipe kept time.

Barnaby moved forward, his sword drawn.

The blade hummed with holy light, casting long, sharp shadows. He scanned the corners.

No monster.

No trap. Just mold. A thick, black fuzz creeping up the load-bearing pillars.

He frowned. The air tasted of rot and old paper.

This wasn't a dungeon, which was a basement.

A municipal sub-basement, judging by the standardized electrical conduit running along the ceiling.

He kicked a piece of shattered tile. It skittered across the wet floor.

"Search the perimeter," Barnaby ordered.

The squires scattered. One poked at the mold with a torch. The other checked the electrical panel, which sparked ominously.

Barnaby approached the center of the room. There was nothing here. No chest.

No altar. Just a drain cover, clogged with debris, and a puddle rising around his greaves.

He felt a strange absence.

The System usually pinged him when a Core was near. A notification. A quest marker.

A threat assessment.

Here, there was only dampness.

Barnaby reached out, his gauntlet brushing the wet wall. He felt a vibration. Low. Constant.

A heartbeat? No. A pump.

He looked up. The ceiling was low, barely high enough for him to stand upright without ducking. The lighting fixtures were industrial, encased in dusty cages.

This place wasn't built for combat. It was built for storage.

Barnaby lowered his sword. The holy light flickered.

"Captain," one squire called out, pointing at the electrical panel. "Look at this wiring."

Barnaby walked over. The wires were bundled together, stripped, and taped. The junction box was missing its cover. Sparks danced between the exposed copper and the metal casing.

"Violation," Barnaby muttered. The word slipped out before he could stop it.

He was a paladin. He fought evil. He did not cite building codes.

But the sensation lingered. The air felt wrong. The layout felt wrong. It felt like a space that had been forced into a role it wasn't designed for.

"Captain," the second squire said, his voice trembling. "The water. It's rising."

Barnaby looked down. The puddle had reached his knees. The cold seeped through the leather padding of his armor.

Something was wrong.

The vibration in the wall grew louder. A grinding noise. Metal on metal.

Barnaby turned back to the center of the room. The drain cover was vibrating. The debris around it shifted.

And then, from beneath the grate, a hand emerged.

Not a monster's hand. Not a claw.

A human hand. Pale. Dripping. Clutching a clipboard.

The hand punched through the grate, shattering the remaining metal bars. Water gushed out, black and foul.

Barnaby raised his sword.

"Demon!"

The hand didn't attack. It grabbed the edge of the floor, pulling upward.

Shoulders followed.

Then a torso. Then a head.

Gerald Hutchins surfaced, and he was soaked. His suit was ruined, hanging off his frame like wet rags. His glasses were missing. He spat out a mouthful of black water and coughed, a dry, hacking sound that echoed in the small space.

He looked at Barnaby. He looked at the squires. He looked at the rising water.

Gerald didn't scream. He didn't rage. He adjusted his wet collar, which did nothing because there was no collar left.

"Section 8.1," Gerald said. His voice was flat. Bored.

Barnaby blinked.

"What?"

"Emergency Evacuation protocols," Gerald said. He pointed a shaking finger at the electrical panel.

"You are standing in a Class D hazard zone.

High voltage.

Wet floor. No ground fault circuit interrupters.

Your armor is conductive. You are at risk of electrocution.

"

Barnaby looked at his sword. The holy light sputtered.

"Gerald Hutchins," the System announced in a voice that sounded like grinding stones. **"Core Status: Critical. Structural Integrity: 12%. Water Ingress: Uncontrolled."**

Barnaby stared. The Core. It was him. This man was the Core.

"You are the villain?" Barnaby asked.

"I am the landlord," Gerald corrected. He waded toward the stairs. The water was up to his waist now. "And you are trespassing on private property without a permit."

"I am a paladin of the Order of the Silver Dawn," Barnaby said, recovering his composure. He leveled his sword at Gerald's chest. "I am here to purge the corruption."

Gerald stopped. He looked at the sword. Then he looked at the ceiling.

"Purge away," Gerald said. "But note that the ceiling height is seven feet, three inches.

Your helmet adds four inches.

Your swing arc requires six feet of clearance. You have three. You are violating Section 4.2: Minimum Clearance for Weapon Swings in Occupied Spaces.

"

Barnaby frowned. He tried to swing. His helmet clipped the low-hanging conduit.

Sparks showered down.

Barnaby flinched. The holy light died completely. The sword went dark.

"That," Gerald said, pointing at the sparking wires, "is a fire hazard."

The squires scrambled up the stairs. Barnaby hesitated. He looked at Gerald, who was now chest-deep in the water, holding onto a pipe to stay afloat.

Gerald wasn't fighting. He was inspecting.

He was looking at the cracks Barnaby's party had caused. He was measuring the spread with a ruler he had pulled from... somewhere.

The water was rising faster. The explosion of the water heater had ruptured the main line. The library above was likely flooding too.

Barnaby felt a chill that had nothing to do with the damp.

"You're not trying to kill us," Barnaby realized.

"I'm trying to keep the building standing," Gerald said.

"Which you are actively undermining. Every step you take adds load to a foundation rated for storage, not combat.

Your plate armor weighs two hundred pounds.

This floor is rated for fifty pounds per square foot. You are overloading the joists.

"

Barnaby looked at his feet. The floorboards were bowing.

"If I leave," Barnaby said, "will you attack me?"

"If you leave, I will file a complaint with the City Planning Commission," Gerald said. "Which is currently defunct due to the apocalypse, but the System will still process it."

Barnaby laughed. It was a nervous, jagged sound.

"You're bluffing."

"I am a bureaucrat," Gerald said. "We don't bluff. We document."

The floor groaned. A loud, splintering crack.

Barnaby stepped back. The water surged.

"Get out," Gerald said. "Now. Before the structural failure triggers a collapse. Section 9.4: Mandatory Evacuation upon signs of imminent collapse."

Barnaby looked at the squires. They were gone.

He looked at Gerald. The man looked small.

Wet.

Defeated.

And serious.

Barnaby sheathed his dark sword. He climbed the stairs. He didn't look back.

***

Gerald waited until the heavy boots faded into silence, and he held his breath. His lungs burned. The water was cold, biting at his skin.

He floated there, clutching the pipe. The water was rising past his chin.

He had to move. He had to fix it.

The System menu hovered in his vision, translucent and red.

**[Structural Integrity: 12%]** **[Water Ingress: Critical]** **[Compliance Rating: 37/100]** **[Status: Condemned]**

Condemned. The word hit him harder than the paladin's sword would have.

He couldn't leave. If he left the dungeon footprint, he died. He was the dungeon. The water was inside him.

He needed a pump. He needed a drain. He needed to lower the water level before the foundation gave out.

He looked at the mold. It was spreading rapidly in the damp. The spores were thick.

Gerald closed his eyes. He remembered the code. Not the combat code. The *plumbing* code.

Ashford Municipal Code, Chapter 14: Sanitary Drainage, and he opened his eyes. He looked at the clogged drain.

He couldn't fix the pipes. He didn't have materials. He didn't have time.

But he could cite the mold, and he focused his will on the black fuzz creeping up the pillar.

**[Target: Mold Colony (Biological Entity)]** **[Violation: Unauthorized Biological Growth in Wet Area. Lack of Ventilation.]**

Gerald concentrated. It hurt. Filing a Citation required focus, and his focus was fragmented by the pain in his chest.

**[Citation Issued: Biological Hazard.]** **[Penalty: Mold Spores deprived of regeneration for 10 minutes.]**

The mold didn't die. It just stopped growing.

It wasn't enough, and he needed to divert the water.

He looked at the electrical panel. It was sparking, dangerous.

Section 20.1: Electrical Safety.

If he shut down the power, the pumps might stop. If the pumps stopped, the water stayed.

But if he *re-routed* the power.., and he couldn't. He had no hands. He was the building.

Wait.

He looked at the shattered emblem on the floor. The Hunter's Guild emblem.

It was heavy. Metal.

He looked at the drain, and he looked at the ceiling.

A new thought formed. A loophole.

The System enforced compliance. It punished violations.

But it also rewarded *correction*.

If he could prove the water was not a hazard, but a feature...

No. That was madness.

He looked at the stairs. The paladin was gone. The threat had passed.

But the water kept rising.

Gerald felt the weight of the library above him. Thousands of books. Thousands of tons of paper.

If the floor gave way, the library collapsed.

If the library collapsed, the city block collapsed, and he had to stabilize the floor.

He looked at the cracks.

**[Repair Cost: 500 Mana.]** **[Available Mana: 12.]**

He couldn't pay.

He looked at the Citation he had just filed.

**[Appeal Window: Open.]**

Could he appeal his own violation?

**[Target: Self.]** **[Violation: Structural Failure.]** **[Grounds: Force Majeure.]**

It was worth a try, and he submitted the appeal.

**[Appeal Processing...]**

The water reached his nose.

**[Appeal Denied.]** **[Reason: Force Majeure does not apply to Core negligence.]** **[Penalty: Compliance Rating -5.]** **[New Status: Hazardous Material Zone.]**

The air grew toxic. The mold released spores, unchecked now that his citation had expired.

Gerald choked, and he needed a new angle.

He looked at the electrical panel again. The sparks.

He remembered the paladin's comment. *Violation.*

Barnaby had seen it. The System had seen it.

But what if the violation wasn't the wiring?

What if the violation was the *presence* of the electrical system itself?

In a sub-basement, electrical systems require GFCI protection. Gerald didn't have it.

He had cited the mold. He had cited the water.

He needed to cite the *building*, and he targeted his own foundation.

**[Target: Foundation Slab.]** **[Violation: Substandard Concrete Mix. Air Entrainment Insufficient for Freeze-Thaw Cycle.]**

It was a lie. He didn't know the mix. But the System didn't check for truth. It checked for plausibility.

And he was a core. He *knew* his own structure. He could feel the air bubbles in the concrete. He could feel the weakness.

**[Citation Issued: Material Non-Conformance.]**

The System paused.

**[Correction Required: Replace Slab.]** **[Cost: 10,000 Mana.]**

Too expensive.

But wait.

**[Alternative: Apply Surface Sealant.]** **[Cost: 50 Mana.]**

He had 12, and he looked at the mold. The mold was biological. The mold was wet.

He looked at the electrical panel.

He looked at the water.

He had an idea. It was stupid. It was dangerous. It was against every rule he had ever written.

He targeted the electrical panel.

**[Target: Electrical Junction Box.]** **[Violation: Lack of Waterproof Enclosure.]**

**[Correction: Install GFCI or Seal Enclosure.]**

He couldn't install a GFCI.

But he could seal the enclosure.

With what?

Water, and the water was already there.

He commanded the water to flood the junction box.

The water surged toward the panel. The sparks hissed and died. The circuit shorted out.

The lights went out.

Complete darkness.

Gerald floated in the black water, and he had created a short circuit. That was a violation.

**[Violation Detected: Electrical Fire Hazard.]** **[Penalty: Lightning Resistance Lost.]**

He didn't care about lightning resistance.

He cared about the *grounding*.

When the circuit shorted, the fault current sought the path of least resistance.

It sought the water.

Gerald was in the water.

The current flowed through him.

It didn't kill him. He was the Core. The current flowed into his mana pool.

**[Mana Generated: +15.]**

He had 27 mana, and he looked at the foundation slab.

**[Apply Surface Sealant: 50 Mana.]**

He was short.

He looked at the mold again.

He cited the mold for *interfering with electrical equipment*.

**[Citation Issued: Interference.]**

The mold recoiled. It pulled back from the pillar.

It exposed the bare concrete behind it.

Gerald focused on the exposed concrete, and he used the water. He used the current. He used the mana.

**[Cast: Hardened Mortar.]**

It wasn't a spell he had learned. It was a *code requirement*.

Mortar must be applied to fill voids, and he forced the water to settle into the cracks. He forced the mana to bind it.

It hurt. It felt like burning his own bones.

The water level stopped rising.

For a second.

Then, a new notification popped up.

**[System Audit: Inspector General Detected.]** **[Reason: Anomalous Mana Usage.]** **[Status: Investigation Open.]**

Gerald gasped.

The Inspector General, and he had never seen one. Rumors said they were invisible. Silent.

But he felt a presence. A cold, judging presence hovering above the library floor.

Above Gerald's head.

The presence looked down.

It saw the flooded sub-basement.

It saw the broken floor.

It saw Gerald, floating in the water, holding a citation against himself.

The presence didn't speak. It didn't issue a fine.

It marked the location.

**[Priority Target: Core ID 89402.]** **[Reason: System Integrity Threat.]**

Gerald stared up at the dark ceiling, and he wasn't just a landlord anymore.

He was a suspect.

And the Inspector General was coming down the stairs.
