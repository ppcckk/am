Chapter 207: Audit the Core

# Chapter 207

Marble dust coated his tongue. It tasted like old tax returns and chalkboard eraser.

Gerald did not blink. Blinking slowed down the inspection cycle.

He clicked the ballpoint pen against his thigh, a rhythmic *tap-tap-tap* that echoed in the cavernous marble hall.

The sound was small. Insignificant. But it was the only sound he controlled.

The rest of the room screamed with the chaos of inherited debt.

Three thousand citations. The number hung in the air, visible as a translucent red counter above the throne of rejected permits.

The Inspector General had vanished.

Mara Vane had vanished. Only Gerald, the Architect, and the dungeon remained.

The Architect stood in the corner, bound by chains of glowing blue code. He looked less like a dungeon core and more like a man who had been chewed up and spat out by a wood chipper. His form flickered, translucent and jagged.

"Move," Gerald said.

The Architect didn't move. He glared.

"Move," Gerald repeated, louder. "You are obstructing the inspection path. That is a Class C violation."

The Architect's eyes narrowed.

"You cannot inspect this place. It is a war zone."

"It is a structure," Gerald corrected. "Structures have rules. Even if they are angry."

Gerald stepped forward. The floor beneath his feet was polished obsidian, slick and treacherous.

He adjusted his stance, widening his base of support.

Balance was a safety requirement.

If he fell, he would violate his own posture guidelines, and the System might dock him for improper form.

To his left, a trapdoor snapped open. A jet of steam erupted, hissing violently.

Gerald didn't dodge. He pulled a small, laminated card from his pocket. Form 44-A: *Hazardous Ventilation Report*. He held it up.

"Violation," he stated. "Unregulated steam release within ten feet of a pedestrian thoroughfare. No pressure relief valve. No signage."

The steam jet stuttered. The dungeon shuddered. The red counter above the throne ticked down by one.

*2,999.*

Gerald felt a surge of dry satisfaction. It wasn't happiness. It was the feeling of a checkbox being ticked.

He walked toward the throne. The guards were not attacking him. They were frozen.

The System had paused their aggression protocols while an official inspection was in progress. This was the loophole Gerald had found in the fine print of Chapter 12, Section 4: *An active inspection renders hostile entities passive until the inspection report is filed or rejected.*

Gerald needed to file three thousand reports before midnight. He had forty-five minutes.

He reached the first guard. It was a statue of a lion, but its eyes were mechanical gears, spinning with lethal intent. Gerald ignored the eyes. He looked at the paws.

"Cracked pavement," Gerald muttered. "Trip hazard."

He wrote the citation in the air. The pen tip glowed. The text manifested in floating gold letters: *CITATION #1: Uneven flooring constitutes a slip-and-fall risk. Penalty: Structural integrity reduction.*

The lion statue crumbled. Not destroyed. Just... weakened. Its gears slowed.

*2,998.*

Gerald moved to the next hazard. A pool of acid bubbled in a decorative moat surrounding the throne.

"Improper chemical storage," Gerald said. "No containment barrier. No ventilation system. No Material Safety Data Sheets posted."

The acid evaporated. The bubbling slowed.

*2,997.*

This was working.

But it was too slow. He was writing individual citations for minor hazards. The debt was massive.

The System expected him to address the root causes, not just the symptoms.

The Architect watched him with a mixture of horror and confusion.

"You are killing them," the Architect said. "You are killing my dungeon by citing it."

"I am correcting it," Gerald said. "There is a difference."

"There is no difference! You are erasing my existence!"

"Your existence is non-compliant," Gerald said, not looking up. He was measuring the height of the throne's steps with a tape measure he had manifested from his inventory.

"These steps are seven inches high.

Code requires seven and one-eighth. A discrepancy of one-eighth of an inch across ten steps is a cumulative fall risk.

"

He wrote the citation.

The throne wobbled. A crack appeared in the marble base.

The Architect screamed. It was a sound of digital static and tearing metal.

"Stop!

I will pay you!

I will give you mana! I will give you experience points!

"

"I do not want experience points," Gerald said. "I want compliance."

He turned to the largest hazard in the room. The ceiling.

It was a dome of glass, painted with frescoes of angels. But the glass was cracked. A spiderweb fracture ran from the center to the edge.

"Gerald," the Architect warned. "If you cite the ceiling, the roof collapses. The entire dungeon is destabilized."

Gerald looked at the crack. He looked at the timer in his peripheral vision.

*00:42:15 remaining.*

He had cleared twelve citations. He needed to clear two thousand and nine hundred and eighty-eight more.

He could not measure every tile. He could not weigh every guard. He could not test every trap individually.

The volume of work was impossible. It was a bureaucratic impossibility.

Gerald felt a familiar nausea.

The itch of unfinished paperwork. The paralysis of a backlog that never ends. He breathed in.

He breathed out, and he needed a group citation.

A Class A Multi-Unit Violation.

Gerald walked to the center of the room. He stood directly under the cracked ceiling.

He looked at the guards, and he looked at the traps. He looked at the acid pools.

"All entities in this room," Gerald announced. His voice was flat, carrying the weight of a municipal decree. "Are subject to a Joint Liability Audit."

The System paused. The red counter flickered.

*What is this?* the System seemed to ask.

Gerald pulled out a new form. Not a laminated card.

A full-sized document, and he had been saving it.

A blank master citation sheet, reserved for catastrophic failures.

"Violation: Structural Instability," Gerald read aloud. "The primary load-bearing structure—the ceiling—shows critical failure. Due to the ceiling's instability, all secondary structures, including decorative elements, security units, and fluid retention systems, are deemed compromised under the Doctrine of Shared Risk."

He wrote it. Fast. His handwriting was cramped, but legible.

*Pursuant to System Code 902.1, when a primary structural element is cited for critical failure, all dependent systems are subject to immediate suspension pending remediation.*

He stamped it.

The air in the dungeon went silent. The dust settled.

The guards stopped moving. The traps disarmed. The acid pools froze.

The cracks in the ceiling widened, but the ceiling did not fall. It held.

Because the System had recognized the citation as valid.

*2,500.*

Two thousand and five hundred citations cleared in a single stroke.

Gerald stared at the number. It was a miracle. A loophole. A bureaucratic hack so dirty it made his teeth ache.

The Architect slumped against the wall.

"You... you grouped them."

"I applied a systemic correction," Gerald said. "Do not confuse efficiency with magic."

He walked toward the exit. The door behind the throne had opened. A hallway stretched beyond it, lined with doors. Thousands of doors.

"The debt is not just here," Gerald said.

"No," the Architect whispered. "It is everywhere. I owed citations to the Goblin King.

To the Lich of the Eastern Reach.

To the Fire Giant Clan.

"

Gerald stopped. He looked at the hallway.

"If I do not clear them," Gerald said.

"Then you die," the Architect said.

Gerald nodded. He understood the stakes. He also understood the method. He had found the lever.

He stepped into the hallway. The first door was labeled *Goblin Warrens - Sector 7*.

Gerald pushed it open.

The smell hit him first. Wet fur, rotting meat, and stale beer.

The Goblin Warrens were a nightmare of compliance.

Corridors were narrow. Lighting was nonexistent. The floor was littered with sharp objects.

It was a health code violation of epic proportions.

A goblin screamed and charged, wielding a rusty cleaver.

Gerald did not raise a hand. He did not cast a spell. He held up his pen.

"Form 104: Obstruction of Egress," he said.

The goblin froze. The System recognized the citation. The goblin's attack animation glitched. It stood there, cleaver raised, waiting for the report to be processed.

*2,499.*

Gerald looked at the next door. *Lich's Crypt - Sector 12*, and he could feel the cold radiating from it. Necrotic energy. Poor ventilation. No proper disposal of hazardous biological waste.

He clicked his pen.

He had forty minutes. He had two thousand and four hundred and ninety-nine doors.

But he had the Group Citation, and he didn't need to enter every room.

He could stand in the hallway and cite the hallway.

The hallway connected to the rooms. The rooms shared a ventilation system.

The ventilation system was non-compliant. the rooms were non-compliant, which was a cascade failure of logic.

Gerald turned to the Architect, who was dragging his chained feet into the hallway.

"Get the list of all connected ventilation shafts," Gerald said.

"The... what?"

"The HVAC system. The ducts.

If the main duct is dirty, every room connected to it is dirty.

Cite the duct. The rooms fall with it.

"

The Architect's eyes widened.

"That is... that is insane. The System will reject it. It is too broad."

"The System loves broad strokes when the evidence is overwhelming," Gerald said. "Show me the blueprints."

The Architect hesitated. Then, with a sigh that sounded like a hard drive failing, he pulled a scroll from his chest cavity.

He unrolled it. The blueprint was a mess of tangled lines, overlapping zones, and chaotic wiring.

Gerald studied it, and he saw the pattern. The dungeon was not a series of rooms.

It was a network.

A single, flawed network.

He picked up his pen.

He walked to the center of the hallway. He placed his foot on a specific tile.

"Here," Gerald said. "This is the junction box. The main electrical feed for this sector."

He looked at the Architect.

"Is this junction box grounded?"

The Architect checked the blueprint.

"No. It is ungrounded. It is leaking power into the floor tiles."

"Excellent," Gerald said.

He wrote.

*Violation: Ungrounded Electrical Source.*

*Pursuant to System Code 404, all electronic devices and magical conduits connected to an ungrounded source are subject to immediate shutdown to prevent fire hazard.*

He stamped it.

The lights in the hallway died. The hum of the dungeon ceased.

The guards, powered by magic conduits linked to that junction box, went limp.

They didn't die, and they just stopped. Their power source was cut.

*1,000.*

A thousand citations cleared.

Gerald felt a wave of dizziness. The effort of writing so much, so fast, was draining his Core Energy. His vision blurred. His hands shook.

He had to keep going, and he moved to the next junction. A steam pipe.

"Improper insulation," he muttered.

He moved to the next. A sewage drain.

"Backflow risk."

He moved to the next. A support column made of wood.

"Termite infestation."

He was flying now. He was not inspecting. He was diagnosing. He was finding the one flaw that broke everything.

The Architect watched him, terrified.

"You are dismantling the dungeon's logic."

"I am enforcing the law," Gerald said.

But then he stopped, and he stood in front of a heavy iron door. It was labeled *The Vault*.

Inside the Vault was the heart of the debt. The Architect's Core. The source of the three thousand citations.

If Gerald cited the Core, it would be destroyed. The debt would be cleared. But the Architect would die.

Gerald looked at the Architect.

The Architect looked back. He looked tired. He looked like a man who had been waiting for this moment for a long time.

"Do it," the Architect said.

Gerald hesitated, and he was a building inspector. He was not an executioner. He did not destroy. He corrected.

But the code was clear. A Core that cannot comply must be deregistered.

Gerald raised his pen.

He looked at the door. He looked at the countdown.

*00:12:00.*

He looked at the pen, and he clicked it.

He did not write a citation for the Vault.

He wrote a citation for the *Hallway*.

*Violation: Proximity Hazard.*

*The Vault is located within ten feet of a public thoroughfare without a blast wall. This is a zoning violation.*

*Penalty: Forced Relocation.*

The System processed it, and the Vault did not explode. It did not vanish.

It *moved*.

The iron door slid sideways. The entire room, the walls, the floor, the Core inside, shifted. It was dragged through the stone like a piece of furniture being moved by a mover.

The Architect screamed as the walls closed in around him.

Gerald watched the room disappear into the depths of the dungeon, relocated to a compliant distance from the hallway.

The debt counter dropped. Not to zero. But to a manageable number.

*45 citations.*

Gerald lowered his pen. His hand was cramping.

He had not cleared the debt. He had pushed it away.

The door behind the relocated Vault was gone. In its place was a wall of solid stone.

And from the other side of that stone, a voice came through. Not the Architect's voice.

A deeper voice. A voice that vibrated in Gerald's bones.

"Inspector," the voice said.

Gerald froze.

"I am the Inspector General," the voice said. "And you have just violated Section 9, Paragraph 3: *Interference with a Sealed Evidence Room.*"

The red counter above the hallway spiked. Not down. *Up.*

*50,000.*

Gerald looked at the counter. He looked at the voice.

"You have been cited," the voice said. "By me. Personally."

The dungeon shook. The floor beneath Gerald cracked.

And then, from the darkness of the hallway, something stepped out, which was not a guard. It was not a goblin.

It was a figure made of red tape. Literal, sticky, crimson tape. It had arms.

It had legs. And it was holding a clipboard.

Gerald clicked his pen, and he had made a mistake. A big one.

And now he had to inspect himself.
