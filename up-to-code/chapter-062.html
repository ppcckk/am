Chapter 62: Core Zero Citation

# Chapter 62

His hand found the clipboard before he told it to.

He did not reach for it with the urgency of a man grabbing a weapon, but reached for it with the rote familiarity of a man completing his eleven-thousandth form.

The motion was muscle memory.

The hand knew where the pen was. The pen knew where the checkbox was. The checkbox knew what it was for.

"You're not an inspector," Gerald said.

The woman in the gray suit tilted her head. A degree. Maybe two.

"I am the first Core," she said. "I predate the framework. I predate the distinction between Core and Inspector. I am both."

"That's not what the framework says."

"The framework is mine."

Gerald looked down at the citation, and he read it again. Unpermitted alteration of System infrastructure.

Penalty: permanent removal from Core status.

He read the serving authority line. It said CORE 0001. It did not say INSPECTOR.

He looked up.

"You're citing me as a Core," he said. "Not as an inspector."

"Does it matter?"

"It matters because of Section 4.2 of the Compliance Framework. Subsection C." Gerald's voice went flat.

The voice he used in hearings at City Hall when a contractor tried to argue that his interpretation of the egress requirement was "close enough." "Citations may only be served by entities holding Inspector-level clearance.

A Core serving a citation is not a citation.

It's a citizen's complaint."

The woman in the gray suit said nothing. Her clipboard did not move. Her weight stayed on her left leg.

"A citizen's complaint," Gerald continued, "must be filed with the Inspector General's office for review before it can be served on a party. You skipped that step. You served it directly.

That's not a citation. That's a citizen's complaint that hasn't been docketed."

"I wrote the framework."

"And the framework says you can't do this." Gerald held up his clipboard. "I'm filing a motion to dismiss on procedural grounds. Service was improper. The citation is void."

The woman in the gray suit looked at him for a long moment.

The System's ambient hum filled the silence. Gerald could feel the dungeon around him. The handrails he'd installed in Chapter 8.

The ventilation ducts he'd carved in Chapter 14.

The fire suppression system he'd spent nine chapters building from scratch because the System's default configuration had been non-compliant and he'd refused to sleep until it was fixed.

All of it. Gone. If this citation stood.

"I'll grant your motion," she said.

Gerald blinked.

"I'll grant your motion to dismiss," she repeated. "The citation is void. You're correct on the procedural point. I served it improperly."

Gerald's hand tightened on his clipboard, which was wrong. This was too fast. In thirty-one years of municipal work, he had never once seen a citation dismissed on the first motion.

Not even when the procedural error was obvious. Not even when the inspector had misspelled the property owner's name.

"Then we're done," Gerald said.

"No."

The woman in the gray suit uncapped a pen. She wrote something on her clipboard. Gerald could not see what.

"I'm re-filing," she said.

"Properly.

Through the Inspector General's office. Which means you now have a docketed complaint, and you have a Hearing window of forty-eight hours to respond.

If you don't respond, the citation defaults to upheld."

"You just said the framework prevents you from serving it directly."

"I'm not serving it. The Inspector General is.

I'm the complainant." She capped the pen.

"That's allowed. That's always been allowed. Any Core can file a complaint with the Inspector General.

The Inspector General can then serve the citation.

I'm just the party who filed."

Gerald felt something cold move through his dungeon footprint. Not a temperature change. Something structural. The floor of his main chamber shifted imperceptibly, settling like a building under redistributed load.

"You're not just a Core," he said. "You're a complainant. And the Inspector General will enforce your complaint because the Inspector General has no choice. It's automatic."

"That's how the system works."

"The system you wrote."

"Yes."

Gerald looked at the rat, and the rat sat in the corner of the main chamber, grooming its paw. The rat had been here since the beginning. Since the moment Gerald's body had dissolved into the dungeon footprint and the System had handed him a spawn list with three mold spores, a water leak, and a confused rat.

The rat had never been cited. The rat had never been anything.

The rat had simply been.

"Forty-eight hours," Gerald said.

"Forty-eight hours," the woman confirmed.

"The Hearing will be conducted by the Inspector General. You'll need to argue the substantive merits of your alteration.

Not the procedure. The substance. Why your modification to the System infrastructure was justified."

She turned. Walked toward the corridor. Her heels clicked on the floor Gerald had poured himself, mixing the aggregate by hand because the System's default flooring had failed the ADA-equivalent accessibility standard.

At the corridor's entrance, she stopped.

"One more thing," she said. "You should know that you're not the first Core I've cited."

Gerald said nothing.

"You're the first one who challenged the service," she said. "The others accepted.

All of them.

Forty-three other Cores have been cited for unpermitted alterations over the last six hundred years. None of them filed a motion to dismiss. None of them argued procedure.

They all just... accepted it."

"Why?"

"Because they didn't know they could."

The woman in the gray suit walked into the corridor. Gerald watched her go. He watched until the System's rendering of her form dropped below the visibility threshold and she was simply gone.

He looked at his clipboard. The citation was still there.

Voided, but still there.

A record of the attempt. He flipped to a blank page and started writing.

Motion to Dismiss. Filed. Granted.

He wrote it down anyway. Because if it wasn't written down, it hadn't happened. That was the rule. That had always been the rule.

Forty-eight hours. A Hearing.

Substantive argument, and he would need to prove that his modification to System infrastructure was justified.

Which meant he would need to prove that the modification was necessary. Which meant he would need to prove that the System's default configuration was, in fact, non-compliant.

Which meant he would need to prove that Core 0001's framework was, itself, a violation.

Gerald sat down on the floor of his main chamber. The concrete was cold. He'd installed radiant heating in Chapter 31, but it was currently offline because he was running a diagnostic on the electrical code compliance of the wiring he'd pulled through the conduit himself.

He opened a new form. Hearing Preparation Worksheet.

Form 7-A.

He'd created it himself in Chapter 19 because the System didn't have one. The System assumed Cores would never need to prepare for Hearings because the System assumed Cores would never cite each other.

Gerald began filling it out.

The first field: Nature of Alteration. He wrote: Modification of System infrastructure to include compliance enforcement protocols not present in original framework.

The second field: Justification, and he paused. He thought about the forty-three Cores who had been cited.

The forty-three Cores who had just accepted it.

Who had let Core 0001 remove their Core status without argument.

He wrote: The original framework lacked compliance enforcement mechanisms. This created an unenforceable standard.

An unenforceable standard is, by definition, not a standard, which is a suggestion.

A suggestion cannot protect entities from harm. The modification was necessary to convert a suggestion into a standard.

He stared at the words. They were true. They were also, he realized, an admission.

He had modified the System. He had changed the rules.

He had done it without a permit.

And now he was arguing that the change was necessary because the original rules were inadequate.

Which was, essentially, what every contractor said when they were caught doing unpermitted work.

The inspector finds you've moved a load-bearing wall without a permit. You say, "Well, the original configuration was unsafe." The inspector says, "Then you should have filed for a permit." And the answer is always the same.

You didn't file because filing would have stopped you.

Because filing would have meant someone else looked at your plans and said no.

Gerald set down his pen.

The rat crossed the room. It climbed onto his clipboard. It looked at the words. It sniffed the ink.

"You knew," Gerald said to the rat.

The rat blinked.

"You've been here since the beginning. You were the first spawn.

The System generated you.

Core 0001's framework generated you.

And you've been here this whole time, watching me build."

The rat groomed its paw.

Gerald looked at the citation again. Unpermitted alteration of System infrastructure. He looked at the serving authority.

Core 0001. He looked at the date.

Something clicked.

Not a metaphorical click. An actual mechanical click, like a latch engaging. The date on the citation was wrong.

Not wrong by a day.

Wrong by a century. The date read as though it had been generated by a timestamp system that predated the current framework by approximately one hundred and twelve years.

Gerald picked up his pen. He wrote a new line on the Hearing Preparation Worksheet.

Substantive defense: The citation's timestamp indicates it was generated under the pre-framework legal regime. Under that regime, the concept of "unpermitted alteration" did not exist because the concept of "permit" did not exist. A permit is a framework construct. Core 0001 is citing me under a framework that did not exist when the alleged violation occurred.

He paused, and the alleged violation had not occurred when the alleged violation occurred.

Because the alleged violation had not occurred at all. The framework Gerald had modified was itself a framework construct, and Gerald had modified it after the framework existed, which meant the framework's own rules applied. Which meant Gerald was subject to the framework.

Which meant the citation was valid on substance.

Unless.

Unless the framework Gerald had modified was not the same framework Core 0001 was citing under.

Unless two frameworks existed.

The original one, which Core 0001 wrote. And the current one, which Gerald had modified.

And if Gerald had modified the current one, and Core 0001 was citing under the original one, then the citation was referencing a legal regime that Gerald had not violated because Gerald had never operated under it.

Gerald wrote: The citation references the original framework. I operate under the modified framework.

These are distinct legal regimes.

I have not violated the modified framework. I have not operated under the original framework. The citation is inapplicable.

He underlined it. Twice.

The rat sat on his clipboard, still. Its eyes reflected the System's ambient glow. Gerald stared at those eyes and felt something he had not felt in thirty-one years of municipal work.

He felt like he was wrong.

Not about the argument, and the argument was sound. He'd checked it three ways.

But he sensed the argument’s structural unsoundness, much as he assessed load-bearing walls.

That the load path was not what it appeared to be, and a hidden condition, subsidence, or water table issue that surface inspection would miss.

The rat stood up. It walked to the edge of the clipboard.

It looked down at the floor of the main chamber.

It looked at Gerald.

It opened its mouth.

No sound came out.

But the System registered something. A ping. A notification.

Gerald registered it as he would a new citation. A small vibration in the core of his being.

He opened the notification.

ENTITY INSPECTION: Biological entity (species: Rattus norvegicus) has been present in dungeon footprint for duration exceeding System-defined grandfathering threshold. Entity qualifies for Pre-Framework Protected Status.

Gerald read it again.

Pre-Framework Protected Status, and a status he had never seen. A status that, by the System's own logic, should not exist for an entity spawned within a dungeon. Unless the entity had been spawned by the pre-framework system.

Unless the rat had not been spawned by Gerald's Core.

Unless the rat had been there before Gerald's Core existed.

The rat looked at him.

Gerald looked at the rat, and the rat had been in his dungeon since Chapter 1. The rat had never filed a permit.

The rat had not needed to.

Gerald looked at the Hearing Preparation Worksheet. He looked at the forty-eight-hour countdown, now ticking at forty-seven hours and fifty-one minutes. He looked at the rat.

"What are you?" he asked.

The rat did not answer. It groomed its paw. It climbed back onto the clipboard. It sat on the words Gerald had written, covering them with its small gray body, and it waited.

Gerald picked up his pen. He did not write. He held it. He looked at the rat, the citation, and the corridor where the woman in the gray suit had walked, realizing a fundamental violation that required tearing down the entire structure.

Core 0001 had not come to cite him.

Core 0001 had come to warn him, and the citation was not an attack.

The citation was a shield. Core 0001 had served a void citation, on purpose, knowing Gerald would dismiss it, knowing it would buy him forty-eight hours of docketed complaint status, which meant Gerald was now under the Inspector General's jurisdiction. Which meant Gerald was now protected from something else.

Something that was coming. Something that Core 0001 could not fight directly because it was not a Core.

Gerald looked at the rat. The rat looked at him.

And then the dungeon shook.

Not with the shake caused by a spawn.

Not with the shake caused by an adventurer party breaching the entrance; it shook the way a building shakes when the ground beneath it is removed.

The way it shakes when the foundation is taken out and the structure is left hanging in the air with nothing to hold it.

The rat stood up on the clipboard. It looked toward the entrance corridor. Its small body went rigid.

Gerald looked toward the entrance corridor.

Something was coming. Something that Core 0001 had needed forty-eight hours to prepare Gerald for. Something that required the Inspector General's jurisdiction as a shield.

The rat opened its mouth again. This time, sound came out.

Not a squeak.

A word. One word. Flat and small and precise.

"Run."
