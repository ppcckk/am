Chapter 325: Dungeon Raid Sirens

# Chapter 325

The dust had not yet settled when the floor eated.

Gerald Hutchins lay on his back in the rubble of the Ashford Municipal Library's sub-basement entrance. Debris pressed against his ribs, heavy and cold. Above him, a jagged hole in the ceiling revealed the sky, now bruised purple by twilight. Through the gap, he could see the silhouette of the creature, pinned beneath tons of broken masonry, twitching with dying spasms.

It was not dead. Gerald knew it was not dead because the System interface flickered into existence in his peripheral vision, red and pulsing with urgent data streams he had no time to read.

*Structural Integrity: Critical Failure.* *Foundation Exposed.* *Unregistered Entity Detected: Deep-Earth Maw.*

The stone beneath his boots did not just hum. It vibrated with a low-frequency thrum that rattled his teeth.

This was not the ambient magic of a dungeon core, which was geological hunger. The library sat on a fault line, a detail Gerald had flagged in a 1998 report that had been buried in a municipal archive, ignored, and forgotten.

He rolled to his left.

Pain shot through his side, a sharp, distinct line of agony that mapped perfectly to the fracture of his fourth rib. He ignored it.

Pain was a variable, and he needed constants.

Gerald reached into the pocket of his vest. His hand shook.

Not from fear, but from the sheer physical effort of lifting his arm while pinned by a slab of drywall.

His fingers brushed against the cold metal of his backup pen.

He retrieved it. He did not have his clipboard.

He did not have his primary notebook. He had a pen, a fragment of a shattered window pane, and the knowledge that the floor was currently trying to digest him.

A tendril of black, viscous sludge oozed from the cracks in the foundation, inches from his left boot. It moved with unnatural speed, seeking warmth. Seeking Gerald.

He scrambled backward, dragging his body across the jagged shards of glass and brick. The sludge snapped at his heels, missing by a fraction of an inch. It recoiled, confused by the sudden movement, then surged forward again.

*Violation: Unauthorized Subterranean Excavation.* *Penalty: Immediate Structural Collapse.*

The text floated in his mind, unbidden. Gerald did not read it.

He had seen the warning three chapters ago when he first noticed the seismic anomalies.

He had filed the report. He had been overruled by the city council, who wanted to open the library for the weekend festival. He had told them the load-bearing capacity of the sub-basement was compromised by soil erosion.

They had laughed.

Now the soil was laughing last.

Gerald dragged himself toward the collapsed support beam he had severed. The creature was still pinned, but its legs were free, kicking uselessly against the weight above.

Its eyes, large and milky, fixed on Gerald, which did not care about the code. It did not care about the citation. It only cared about biomass.

The sludge was closer now. It pooled around the broken foundation, rising like a tide of oil.

Gerald looked at the pen. He looked at the sludge. He looked at the creature.

He could not fight the sludge. He could not fight the creature. He could not fight the collapsing building.

But he could fight the paperwork.

The System had a specific rule regarding emergency structures. *Section 404: Temporary Shoring in Life-Threatening Scenarios.* It allowed for the immediate designation of any material as a temporary support structure if it prevented imminent total collapse. The penalty for misuse was high. The penalty for ignoring it was death.

Gerald grabbed a chunk of wet plaster from the floor. It was heavy, jagged, and covered in dust. He held it up.

"Designate," Gerald whispered. His voice was a rasp, dry from the dust.

The System interface froze. The red warnings paused.

*Command Unrecognized.* *Material Type: Debris.* *Status: Non-Structural.*

Gerald closed his eyes. He visualized the load path.

He remembered the blueprints, and he remembered the exact angle of the original support beam, now snapped in two.

The ceiling above the creature was not falling randomly; it was pivoting on the remaining stub of the beam. If he placed the plaster chunk under that pivot point, it would not support the whole room.

It would only support the creature's weight, preventing it from crushing the sludge source, which in turn would prevent the sludge from gaining enough leverage to destabilize the entire foundation.

It was a physics problem. It was a code violation.

He jammed the plaster chunk under the jagged end of the broken beam.

*Command: Designate Temporary Support.* *Material: Unspecified.* *Risk: High.* *Approval: Pending.*

The sludge surged. It wrapped around Gerald's ankle, cold and slick.

He kicked it away, screaming silently.

The weight of the ceiling groaned. The beam shifted. The plaster crumbled.

"Insufficient density," the System flashed.

Gerald needed something stronger. He needed steel.

He looked at the Demolitionist's hammer. It lay ten feet away, half-buried in dust. He could not reach it.

He looked at the creature. Its leg was pinned, but its other leg was free. The free leg was resting on a fragment of rebar that had twisted out of the concrete during the collapse.

Rebar.

Gerald dragged himself closer to the creature. The milky eyes widened. The jaw unhinged, revealing rows of needle-like teeth. The smell was sulfurous and rotting, a stench of ancient earth and decay.

Gerald did not recoil. He reached out, not for the creature, but for the rebar protruding from its leg.

"Objection," he said.

The creature lunged. Its teeth snapped inches from Gerald's nose. A drop of saliva hit Gerald's cheek, burning like acid.

Gerald grabbed the rebar with both hands, which was sharp. It sliced into his palms.

He ignored the blood.

He pulled. The rebar was bent, trapped under the creature's weight. He pulled harder.

His muscles screamed. His vision blurred.

*System Alert: Entity Aggression Detected.* *Countermeasure: Repulsion.*

The creature slammed its head against Gerald's forehead. Gerald saw stars. He tasted copper. But he kept holding the rebar.

"Structure," Gerald gasped. "This is... structure."

He jammed the end of the rebar into the crack between the plaster chunk and the beam.

It fit, which was tight. It was ugly.

It was temporary.

*Designation Accepted.* *Temporary Support Established.* *Warning: Load Exceeds Material Capacity by 400%.*

The ceiling stopped falling.

The creature shrieked, a sound like grinding metal, as its weight was distributed differently. It could not move. It could not attack. It was trapped by its own mass, held in place by Gerald's makeshift shoring.

The sludge receded. It slithered back into the cracks, denied its path to the surface. The floor was stable. For now.

Gerald lay on the ground, breathing hard. His hands were shredded. His ribs were on fire. But he was alive.

And the System had logged the action.

*Violation Cured: Imminent Total Collapse.* *Bonus: Structural Insight.* *New Passive: Improvised Load-Bearing (Tier 1).*

Gerald stared at the text. It was the first time the System had given him a passive bonus that wasn't tied to a completed inspection report. It was tied to a desperate, illegal act.

He looked up. The sky was darker now. The sun had set. The Sanctuary had expired.

From the shadows of the collapsed library, footsteps approached. They were heavy, rhythmic, and accompanied by the clink of metal on stone.

A figure emerged from the smoke, which was tall, wearing armor that looked like it had been forged in a furnace and cooled in blood. The helmet had no eye slits. Instead, the visor was a smooth, opaque black surface.

"Inspector," the figure said. The voice was distorted, mechanical. "You have caused significant collateral damage to public infrastructure."

Gerald tried to sit up. He failed. He slumped back against the rubble.

"I prevented a total collapse," Gerald said. His voice was steady. "I followed emergency protocols."

"Protocols are for buildings," the figure said. It stepped closer. "Not for combat zones. You are out of jurisdiction, Hutchins."

The figure raised a gauntleted hand. A spear of black ice formed in its grasp.

"Who are you?" Gerald asked.

"I am the Auditor," the figure said. "Specifically, I am the Auditor of Deviations."

Gerald's blood ran cold. The Auditor was not a dungeon core. The Auditor was a System enforcer. A janitor for reality.

"You are citing me for... improvisation?" Gerald asked.

"I am citing you for unauthorized reality manipulation," the Auditor said. "The support you created is not code-compliant. It is an anomaly. It must be removed."

"You can't remove it," Gerald said. "The ceiling will fall. The creature will escape. The fault line will rupture."

"The fault line will rupture regardless," the Auditor said. "That is the natural order. You interfered. That is a violation of the Natural Code."

The spear of ice leveled at Gerald's chest.

"Submit to corrective action," the Auditor said. "Or be erased."

Gerald looked at the ice. He looked at the Auditor. He looked at the creature, trapped by his illegal rebar.

He had one option, and he could appeal.

*System Mechanic: Hearing.* *Trigger: Dispute of Structural Validity.* *Requirement: Physical Presence of Both Parties.*

Gerald closed his eyes. He focused on the rebar. He focused on the plaster. He focused on the exact angle of the load.

"I appeal," Gerald said.

The Auditor paused. The spear lowered slightly.

"Appeals are suspended during active combat," the Auditor said.

"This is not combat," Gerald said. "This is an inspection. And you are obstructing it."

Gerald pulled the pen from his pocket. He held it up.

"Show me your credentials," Gerald said. "Show me your code."

The Auditor tilted its head.

"My code is the System. I am the System."

"Then you are not up to code," Gerald said. "Because the System requires a counter-party for a Hearing.

You cannot be both the prosecutor and the judge, which is a conflict of interest. It is a procedural violation.

"

The interface flashed blue.

*Procedural Anomaly Detected.* *Hearing Initiated.* *Time Limit: 60 Seconds.*

The Auditor stepped back. The spear vanished.

A translucent box appeared in the air between them.

**HEARING: DISPUTE OF STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY** **Plaintiff: Gerald Hutchins (Dungeon Core)** **Defendant: The Auditor (System Entity)** **Issue: Validity of Improvised Support Structure**

**Argument Phase: 10 Seconds Remaining.**

Gerald had ten seconds to prove that his illegal, dirty, desperate act of jamming rebar into a crack was a valid structural solution.

He had to prove it using only the language of code.

He could not use magic. He could not use strength. He had to use logic.

"The support is valid," Gerald said, his voice ringing out in the silent, dusty air.

"It follows the Principle of Least Material. It utilizes existing debris.

It prevents catastrophic failure, which is not permanent. It is temporary. Therefore, it falls under Section 404, Subsection B, which permits non-standard materials in emergency egress scenarios.

"

The Auditor spoke. Its voice was flat.

"Section 404 requires a licensed engineer.

You are not a licensed engineer.

You are a dungeon core. You are an anomaly.

"

"I am a municipal inspector," Gerald said. "I am licensed by the City of Ashford. My license number is 44-B-99.

It is valid until 2030. The System recognizes municipal licenses as a subset of Authority Credentials.

"

The interface flickered.

*Verification: Municipal License 44-B-99.* *Status: Active.* *Jurisdiction: Ashford Municipal Limits.*

The Auditor stiffened.

"You are within city limits," Gerald continued.

"The library is city property. The collapse occurred on city property. Therefore, my municipal authority supersedes the System's generic enforcement protocols, provided I am acting within the scope of public safety.

Which I am."

He pointed at the creature.

"That entity is a public hazard. I am neutralizing it.

By removing my support, you would release a Class 4 Hazard into a residential zone.

That would be a greater violation than my improvised repair. You would be liable for damages.

"

The Auditor did not move.

The timer counted down.

10... 9... 8...

Gerald's heart hammered against his ribs. He had gambled everything on a technicality. He had gambled that the System, in its infinite bureaucratic cruelty, valued legal precedence over raw power.

3... 2... 1...

**Hearing Result:** *Argument Validated.* *Violation: Procedural Conflict (Auditor).* *Penalty: Auditor Restricted to Observation Mode.* *Reward: Citation Validated.*

The blue box shattered, and the Auditor stood still. Its armor seemed to dim. The spear did not return.

"You have won this round," the Auditor said. Its voice was no longer distorted.

It was cold.

"But the System does not forget debt.

You have created an anomaly. It will be corrected.

Eventually."

The figure turned. It walked away, fading into the shadows of the library ruins.

Gerald exhaled. He slumped back into the rubble.

He was alive. He had beaten an Auditor.

But as he lay there, waiting for the dust to settle, the ground beneath him shifted.

A new window opened.

**System Alert: Inspector General Audit Scheduled.** **Time: Dawn.** **Subject: Core ID 7734 (Gerald Hutchins).** **Reason: Accumulation of Unlicensed Citations and Procedural Manipulation.**

Gerald stared at the text, and he had just won. And now he was on a watchlist.

He looked at his hands. They were bloody. The rebar was still holding. The creature was still pinned.

But outside the library, sirens wailed. Not police sirens. Not fire sirens.

They were the sirens of the Dungeon Raid.

Adventurers were coming. And they were coming for him.
