Chapter 333: Subpoena Siege Engine

# Chapter 333

**Chapter 333**

The siege engine did not fire a projectile. It fired a subpoena.

It was a massive, trebuchet-like construct built from black iron and crystallized mana, designed by the Ashford Adventurers' Guild to breach dungeon cores that refused to yield. But this machine had been modified by legal counsel. Instead of a boulder, the basket held a glowing sphere of binding law, wrapped in scrolls so dense they radiated heat.

The sphere slammed into the ceiling of the library's sub-basement.

Gerald Hutchins did not look up. He was on his hands and knees, measuring the gap between the water leak and the electrical panel.

The impact shook the dust from the rafters, coating his clipboard in gray powder.

He wiped the dust with his thumb, checking the measurement again. Three inches. Non-compliant.

"Sir?" A young man in a pristine blue tunic approached the breach.

This was Kaelen, the Guild's junior liaison, assigned to observe Gerald's 'self-correcting behavior.' Kaelen looked terrified. His hands shook as he held a tablet displaying the dungeon's current status.

"The Guild Master sent a team.

They're going to cite you for obstruction of public commerce.

"

Gerald straightened his back. A pop sounded in his spine, loud enough to be heard over the rumbling of the collapsing ceiling. He adjusted his tie, which was still perfectly knotted despite the apocalypse.

"Obstruction of commerce requires a valid business license," Gerald said, not looking at Kaelen. He pointed a wrench at a crack in the foundation.

"I am a municipal entity.

I do not engage in commerce.

I engage in compliance. If they attempt to charge me, they must first prove I have a taxable income.

"

Kaelen blinked.

"The Guild says you're hoarding loot."

"I am hoarding *assets*," Gerald corrected. "And I am holding them in escrow pending an environmental impact study. Show them the Form 88-A."

Kaelen frowned, tapping his tablet. He pulled up a file Gerald had uploaded ten minutes ago. It was a forty-page document detailing the ecological risks of harvesting slime cores without proper disposal protocols.

"The Guild Master will hate this," Kaelen said, a flicker of respect crossing his face.

"Hate is not a legal defense," Gerald replied. He turned back to the wall. "The ceiling is failing.

The siege engine compromised the load-bearing support beams in Sector 4. That is a critical structural failure.

If the ceiling collapses, we lose square footage.

If we lose square footage, my density ratio drops. If the density ratio drops, my spawn rate decreases. If my spawn rate decreases, I cannot generate enough biomass to repair the breach.

"

He paused. The logic was circular, but the root cause was physical. The building code required a minimum ceiling height of eight feet for public access areas. The breach had lowered the effective ceiling in Sector 4 to four feet.

"System," Gerald said. "Open Structural Analysis."

A blue grid overlaid his vision.

Red lines pulsed where the integrity was compromised. He identified three support columns that had sheared.

They needed reinforcement.

Fast.

But he had no materials. He had no money. He had seventy-two hours, and he was currently being attacked by a bureaucracy that viewed him as a glitch.

"Can you... can you fix it?" Kaelen asked. He stepped closer, lowering his voice.

"The Guild is bringing more engines.

The next one is a sonic weapon.

It's designed to shatter weak points.

"

Gerald stared at the shattered column. A sonic weapon targeted resonance frequencies. Weak points vibrated at specific pitches. If he changed the structure, he changed the frequency.

"I need wood," Gerald said.

"We don't bring wood to dungeon raids. We bring swords."

"Then find a tree," Gerald said. "Or a table. A chair.

Anything organic and rigid. Bring it to the breach.

"

Kaelen looked confused, then nodded, and he tapped his earpiece.

"Sir, we need furniture. Yes, furniture.

Bring the breakroom table from the upper floor.

And the oak cabinet from the archives.

"

Gerald watched the boy run away.

Good. The boy was thinking laterally. That was dangerous.

It was also necessary.

Gerald returned to the wrench, and he needed to shore up the column before the sonic pulse hit.

He could not weld; he lacked the skill and the heat source. He could not use concrete; he lacked the mix.

But he could use the dungeon's own resources.

The mold spores, and they were currently in the ventilation shaft, a violation of hygiene code, but they were biomass. They grew on organic matter.

If he fed them enough nutrients, they could form a structural lattice.

It was disgusting. It was unsanitary. It was potentially fatal to anyone breathing near it.

But it was free. And it was fast.

Gerald crawled into the ventilation shaft. The air was thick and stale. He found the cluster of mold spores. They were pulsing gently, feeding on the condensation.

"System," he said. "Designate Mold Cluster 4 as a load-bearing element. Assign it a structural rating of 'Temporary Support.' Grant it a nutrient injection."

A prompt appeared.

**[WARNING: Designating biological hazards as structural elements violates Health Code 4.2. Penalty: -10 Compliance Rating.]**

Gerald hovered his finger over the accept button.

Minus ten. He was currently at 47.

If he dropped below 40, his spawn regeneration would halt entirely, and he would be a hollow shell.

But if he didn't do it, the ceiling would collapse, and he would be erased by the Inspector General for failing to maintain minimum habitability standards.

He pressed accept.

A green light pulsed through the dungeon. The mold spores surged, expanding rapidly. They thickened, hardening into a fibrous, brown mass that wedged itself between the broken column and the ceiling joists. The structure groaned, then stabilized.

Gerald coughed. The air tasted like old bread and decay. He gagged, turning away.

**[COMPLIANCE RATING: 37.]** **[STATUS: CRITICAL. REGEN HALTED.]**

He felt a cold emptiness in his chest. The usual hum of his dungeon's energy faded. The rat stopped squeaking. The water leak slowed to a drip.

He was dying. Slowly. But he was holding the line.

Kaelen returned, dragging a heavy oak cabinet behind him. Two other adventurers helped, one carrying a broken chair, the other a crate of books.

"Here," Kaelen panted. "This should work."

Gerald looked at the pile.

"Books are flammable. Code violation. Do not place them near the electrical panel."

"Where do we put them?"

"On the floor. Away from the walls. Create a buffer zone."

The adventurers hesitated. They were used to fighting monsters, not staging interior design disasters. But Gerald's tone brooked no argument. It was the tone of a man who had denied a permit for an illegal patio extension in 1998 and slept well that night.

They moved the furniture.

"System," Gerald said. "Deploy the cabinet as a brace for the secondary column. Use the chair as a wedge."

The dungeon responded. The furniture slid into place, guided by invisible forces.

It was clumsy, ugly, and absurd.

A dungeon core defended by office furniture.

But it held.

The sonic weapon fired.

A high-pitched whine filled the basement. The air rippled. Glass bottles on the shelves shattered. The adventurers clamped their hands over their ears.

The vibration hit the reinforced columns. The oak cabinet groaned. The mold lattice flexed. The books scattered, fluttering in the air.

But the ceiling did not fall.

The resonance frequency shifted. The addition of the furniture, the books, and the damp mold changed the mass distribution of the sector. The sonic pulse passed through without finding its target. It faded into a dull thud.

Silence returned.

Gerald exhaled. His heart rate was elevated, a fact he noted with mild irritation. Stress was inefficient.

Kaelen lowered his hands.

"That... worked?"

"Structural damping," Gerald said. He stood up, brushing dust from his trousers. "The Guild's weapon relies on harmonic resonance.

By altering the mass and stiffness of the structure, we detuned the system. Physics, Mr.

Kaelen.

Not magic."

Kaelen stared at him. Then, a slow smile spread across his face.

"You just used a filing cabinet to defeat a siege weapon."

"I used a non-compliant object to achieve a compliant outcome," Gerald corrected. "It is a loophole. The Guild did not cite the cabinet for being heavy. Therefore, I could use it."

The entrance to the basement darkened. A figure stepped through the breach.

It was not an adventurer. It was not a monster.

It was a man in a gray suit, carrying a briefcase. His skin was pale, almost translucent, and his eyes were the color of wet slate. He moved with a fluidity that suggested he had no bones.

Behind him, two armored guards followed. They carried no weapons. They carried ledgers.

Gerald recognized the aura. It was not hostile. It was bureaucratic.

"Inspector," Gerald said.

The man stopped, and he adjusted his glasses.

"Core Gerald Hutchins.

I am Adjudicator Vane.

I am here to conduct an interim audit of your emergency repairs.

"

Gerald straightened his spine. An interim audit was dangerous. It allowed the Inspector General to bypass the standard appeal process and directly assess whether the core's actions were

"reasonable."

"I have not filed the final report yet," Gerald said. "The 72-hour window is still open."

"The window is for total compliance," Vane said. His voice was smooth, devoid of empathy.

"But you have violated Health Code 4.2.

You introduced a biohazard into a public access area.

This is a Class A violation.

"

"It was a temporary measure," Gerald argued. "To prevent total structural collapse. The penalty for collapse is existence erasure. The penalty for mold is a fine."

"Which you cannot pay," Vane said. "You have no currency. You have no revenue. You are insolvent, Core Hutchins."

Vane opened his briefcase. Inside, there were no papers. There was a small, glowing crystal.

"The Guild has filed a petition for Forced Liquidation. They claim you are a danger to public safety. I am here to determine if the danger is real."

Vane walked toward the mold-laced column. He did not wear a mask. He ran a finger along the fibrous growth.

"It is spreading," Vane noted. "The nutrient injection was too aggressive. The mold has begun to digest the wooden cabinet.

If it digests the wood, the brace fails. The ceiling falls.

The library above collapses.

People die."

Gerald felt a cold sweat prickle his neck. He had miscalculated the growth rate. The mold was eating its own support.

"I can remove it," Gerald said. "I can purge the sector."

"Purging a sector violates Environmental Code 11.3. Disposal of hazardous waste requires a licensed contractor. You are not a contractor. You are a dungeon."

Vane smiled. It was a thin, sharp smile.

"You are trapped, Core Hutchins. Remove the hazard, and you break the law. Keep the hazard, and you kill people. The Guild will have their excuse to destroy you."

Gerald looked at the mold. It was pulsing, alive and hungry. He looked at the rat, who was hiding behind a stack of books, terrified.

He looked at Kaelen, who was watching with wide, hopeful eyes.

Gerald's mind raced, and he had thirty seconds before Vane issued the liquidation order. He could not remove the mold.

He could not add more weight.

He had no money.

But he had a violation.

He had created a biohazard. The penalty was a fine.

The fine was in compliance points, and he had lost 10 points.

He needed 63 more.

Vane waited. His hand hovered over the crystal.

Gerald looked at the breach in the ceiling. The hole was jagged, exposed to the outside air. It was unsealed. It was a draft.

A draft caused a loss of thermal efficiency.

**[VIOLATION: THERMAL INEFFICIENCY. PENALTY: -5 COMPLIANCE.]**

If he sealed it, he gained compliance points? No, fixing a violation restored points. But he couldn't seal it without materials.

Unless.., and he could cite the breach itself.

The breach was a structural anomaly. It was a defect. Defects were the responsibility of the property owner.

But the breach was caused by the siege engine. The siege engine was a Guild asset.

If Gerald cited the breach as a violation of *Guild* property standards, he could force the Guild to fix it.

But he couldn't cite the Guild. He could only cite dungeons.

Vane stepped closer.

"Time is short, Core. I must make a determination."

Gerald looked at the crystal in Vane's hand. It was a piece of pure order. It was a tool of the Inspector General.

Was the Inspector General a dungeon?

No. The Inspector General was a system entity. It had no body. It had no walls.

But it had a *presence*. It occupied space. It exerted force.

Gerald raised his clipboard. He did not write on it. He wrote on the air.

"System," Gerald said, his voice steady. "Issue Citation to Entity: Adjudicator Vane."

Vane froze. His slate-gray eyes widened.

"Excuse me?" Vane said.

"Citation Reason: Failure to maintain adequate clearance for public access. By standing in the center of the walkway, you are blocking the egress route. Violation of Fire Code 7.1."

Vane laughed. It was a dry, rasping sound.

"I am not a dungeon. I cannot be cited."

"The System defines a 'Dungeon' as any entity with a spatial footprint and a compliance rating," Gerald recited.

"Adjudicators have a Compliance Rating. It is their primary metric. If you fail your audit, you are demoted.

If you are demoted, you lose your authority.

"

Vane's smile vanished.

"You are bluffing. The System does not cite its own enforcers."

"Read the fine print," Gerald said. "Chapter 4, Section 2, Paragraph 12: 'All entities subject to inspection are subject to citation if they obstruct the inspection process.'"

Vane looked down at his feet. He was standing in the middle of the aisle.

The mold was spreading toward the exit.

If he moved, he would step in the mold. If he stepped in the mold, he would contaminate the audit.

He was trapped. Not by physics, but by procedure.

"If I cite you," Gerald said, "and you refuse to move, you are obstructing justice. That is a Class S violation. It triggers an immediate suspension of your authority."

Vane stared at Gerald. The air between them crackled.

"You are betting your existence on a technicality," Vane said.

"I am betting my existence on the code," Gerald said. "It is the only thing that is real."

Vane looked at the guards. They looked at each other. They looked at Vane.

Vane took a step sideways.

The crystal in his hand flickered.

**[SYSTEM ALERT: CITATION ISSUED.]** **[TARGET: ADJUDICATOR VANE.]** **[VIOLATION: OBSTRUCTION OF EGRESS.]** **[PENALTY: AUTHORITY SUSPENDED FOR 10 MINUTES.]**

Vane gasped. The light in his eyes dimmed. He stumbled back, clutching his chest. His suit seemed to fade, becoming less solid.

"You..." Vane wheezed. "You cannot..."

"I just did," Gerald said. He turned to Kaelen.

"Fix the ceiling.

Use the books.

Stack them. Now.

"

Kaelen blinked, then grabbed the crate of books. The other adventurers scrambled to help.

Gerald looked at Vane, who was now fading into a mist of gray light. The interim audit was cancelled. The Guild's liquidation petition was stalled.

But the clock was still ticking.

Gerald looked at his dashboard.

**[COMPLIANCE RATING: 37.]** **[REMAINING TIME: 71 HOURS, 12 MINUTES.]**

He had bought time. He had not fixed the mold. He had not fixed the water leak. He had not fixed the ventilation.

And he had just made a powerful enemy.

Vane's mist coalesced, drifting toward the ceiling. His voice echoed, distorted and angry.

"You have delayed the inevitable, Inspector. The General will hear of this. And when he does, he will not send an Adjudicator."

"Who will he send?" Gerald asked.

"The Auditor," Vane whispered. "He does not cite. He deletes."

Vane vanished.

Gerald stood alone in the center of his broken, moldy, furniture-strewn dungeon. The rat peeked out from behind the oak cabinet.

Gerald opened his clipboard. He had ten minutes of authority suspension for the enemy. He had to use them.

He had to make the mold safe. Or he had to make the rat a load-bearing element.

He looked at the rat. The rat looked at him.

Gerald sighed. He picked up his wrench.

He had a lot of work to do.
