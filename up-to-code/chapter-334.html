Chapter 334: Serving the Citation

# Chapter 334

Gerald's wrench hovered over the damp patch on the eastern wall, trembling slightly. Not from fear. From the vibration of his own hands, which had grown too large for the fine motor control required by the new core interface. The rat, Barnaby, sat on a stack of encyclopedias, grooming his whiskers with the indifference of a tenant who had already signed a year-long lease.

The mold was not merely growing. It was expanding. A thick, black vein pulsed near the baseboard, defying the static geometry of the dungeon. Gerald's dashboard flickered.

**[VIOLATION: UNAUTHORIZED BIOLOGICAL GROWTH.]** **[CLASS: STRUCTURAL COMPROMISE.]** **[PENALTY: 5% PHYSICAL INTEGRITY DEGRADATION PER MINUTE.]**

Five percent. In twenty minutes, his dungeon would be a pile of wet gravel and regret.

He needed a citation, and he needed to serve it. But the mold had no legs to walk toward him, no face to look into while he recited the statute.

Gerald adjusted his grip on the wrench. He thought of Vane's warning. *The Auditor does not cite. He deletes.*

He thought of the clock. Seventy hours. Enough time to install a dehumidifier. Not enough time to understand why the mold was moving against the grain of the stone.

He looked at Barnaby. The rat stopped grooming. He tilted his head, his pink ears twitching.

"Go," Gerald said.

Barnaby scurried up the oak cabinet, leaped to the ceiling, and dropped onto the moldy patch. He sniffed it. He bit it. The mold recoiled, shrinking back into the stone like a shy animal.

**[SPAWN: BARNABY. STATUS: ACTIVE. ROLE: DETECTIVE.]**

Gerald blinked. He hadn't assigned that role. He hadn't even thought it. The System had categorized the rat's biting as an investigative action.

He opened his clipboard. The menu for *Citation* was greyed out. He needed a target. He needed to prove the mold was a separate entity violating the code, not just an symptom of the leak.

Gerald walked to the water source.

The leak dripped from a cracked pipe in the ventilation shaft. He had fixed the pipe three days ago.

The drip had stopped.

But the water was still there, pooling in a puddle that reflected the flickering emergency lights.

He dipped a finger in the puddle. It was warm.

That was wrong.

Gerald pulled his hand back. He wiped it on his trousers, leaving a dark stain.

He pulled out his measuring tape, and he measured the puddle.

Then he measured the drip rate. Zero.

He measured the evaporation rate. Too fast.

The water was not coming from the pipe. It was coming from the air.

Gerald opened the ventilation schematic. It showed the intake vent in the north corner, blocked by a stack of unsuitable furniture. He had cited himself for that last week.

He had moved the furniture. The vent was open.

But the air in the dungeon was not circulating, and he looked at the mold. It was pulsing in rhythm with the flickering lights.

The lights were failing.

**[SYSTEM ALERT: ELECTRICAL LOAD EXCEEDING CAPACITY.]** **[SOURCE: UNKNOWN PARASITIC DRAW.]**

Gerald's blood ran cold.

Parasitic draw. That was a term from the electrical code. Section 400.5.

Conductors shall not have an ampacity less than the maximum load, and the mold was drawing power.

Not from the dungeon's core energy. From the electrical wiring embedded in the walls.

Gerald dropped the wrench. It clattered on the floor.

He grabbed his multimeter, and he probed the baseboard near the mold. The needle jumped to 120 volts.

The mold was alive. And it was eating electricity.

This was not a biological violation. This was an electrical hazard.

Gerald opened the *Citation* menu again. It remained grey. The System did not recognize the mold as a separate dungeon core. It recognized it as a hazard within Gerald's domain.

He could not cite the mold. He had to cite the source of the power.

But the source was his own wiring, and he was citing himself.

Gerald sat down on the crate. He put his head in his hands.

The stress headache behind his eyes throbbed.

He had thirty-one years of experience, and he had never cited a structure for feeding itself.

He looked at Barnaby. The rat had returned to the encyclopedias. He was chewing on a page about *Hydroponics*.

Gerald stared at the rat. Then at the mold. Then at the multimeter.

The System wanted a target. The System wanted a violation. The System wanted a citation.

If he could not cite the mold, he had to make the mold a target.

He had to change its classification.

Gerald opened his *Core Inventory*. He searched for *Electrical Conduit*. He had three feet of copper wire left from the last repair.

He searched for *Label Maker*. He had one roll of red tape.

He walked to the mold. He wrapped the copper wire around the base of the black vein. He connected it to the baseboard. He tied a red tape label to the wire.

He wrote on the label in block capitals: **TEMPORARY LOAD-BEARING ELEMENT.**

He stepped back.

The System processed the change.

**[NOTICE: ENTITY CLASSIFICATION PENDING.]** **[SUBMISSION: STRUCTURAL COMPONENT.]**

Nothing happened. The mold pulsed. The lights flickered.

Gerald gritted his teeth. He needed to be more specific. He needed to invoke the *Hearing* protocol. But he could not hold a hearing with a fungus.

He opened the *Compliance* log. He scrolled back to Chapter 12. The time he had cited the neighboring goblin warren for *Trespassing on a Non-Public Easement*.

The goblin had been a spawn. The System had recognized it as an agent of the rival core.

The mold was an agent of... what?

Gerald looked at the ceiling. The crack where Kaelen had stacked the books was sealed. But the shadow beneath the books seemed to stretch.

He walked to the center of the room. He raised his voice.

"I, Gerald Hutchins, Core of the Ashford Municipal Library Sub-Basement, hereby file a Form 99-C: *Request for Classification of Autonomous Hazard as Foreign Agent*."

He spoke to the empty air. The System usually required a physical target. But the *Foreign Agent* clause had a loophole. It allowed for classification if the hazard exhibited

"intent to damage."

The mold pulsed harder. The lights dimmed further.

**[SYSTEM RESPONSE: EVIDENCE OF INTENT REQUIRED.]**

Gerald looked at the multimeter. The voltage was rising.

He looked at Barnaby. The rat had stopped chewing. He was watching Gerald.

Gerald pointed at the mold.

"It is stealing power," Gerald said. "It is degrading structural integrity. It is resisting removal."

He walked over to the mold. He picked up a hammer.

He did not hit the mold. He hit the wire he had attached to it.

*Ping.*

The mold flinched. It shrank back from the sound.

It reacted to the threat.

**[EVIDENCE LOGGED: DEFENSIVE BEHAVIOR.]** **[CLASSIFICATION: FOREIGN AGENT.]** **[CITATION AVAILABLE.]**

Gerald's heart hammered against his ribs. He grabbed the *Citation* form. He filled it out in his mind.

*Violations:* 1. *Unauthorized Electrical Draw (Section 400.5).* 2. *Structural Compromise via Biological Expansion (Section 104.2).* 3. *Failure to Maintain Safe Egress (Section 103.6) – caused by mold blocking the north vent.*

He pressed *Serve*.

A red stamp slammed into the air above the mold. It was heavy, glowing with bureaucratic anger.

**[CITATION SERVED TO: FOREIGN AGENT 'MOLD-7'.]** **[PENALTY: 50% POWER DRAIN SUPPRESSION.]** **[PENALTY: 10% STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY RESTORATION.]**

The mold shuddered. The black vein turned gray. It stopped pulsing. The lights steadied.

Gerald exhaled. His hands were shaking. He put the hammer down.

He had done it, and he had cited a fungus. He had used the *Foreign Agent* loophole. It was a flimsy legal argument, but the System was literal.

If the mold acted like an enemy, it was an enemy.

He opened his dashboard.

**[COMPLIANCE RATING: 38.]** **[REMAINING TIME: 70 HOURS, 55 MINUTES.]**

One point. A tiny victory.

But the water was still leaking. The ventilation was still blocked. And Vane was gone.

Gerald walked to the north vent. He moved the remaining furniture.

He crawled into the duct, which was dusty. Smelled of mildew.

He followed the shaft. It led up toward the library above.

He emerged into the sub-basement's main corridor. The mold was gone. The water pool was receding.

But the door to the upper floor was locked.

Gerald tried the handle. It was frozen shut. Ice had formed around the frame.

He stepped back.

Ice. In a fire-rated door.

**[VIOLATION: OBSTRUCTED EGRESS.]** **[VIOLATION: IMPROPER STORAGE OF COMBUSTIBLES (ICE BLOCKAGE?)]**

No. That wasn't right. Ice wasn't a combustible.

He pressed his ear to the door. He heard a voice.

"...clean up."

Gerald froze. He knew that voice. It was smooth, cold, and precise.

He kicked the door. It didn't budge. The ice was thick.

He kicked it again. Pain shot up his leg.

He opened his dashboard. He searched for *Thermal Regulation*. He had no heating elements installed. He had no fire suppression system active.

He looked at the mold. It was dead.

He looked at the ice. It was growing.

The voice spoke again.

"Inspector. You have been cited for *Improper Containment of Hazardous Materials*."

Gerald's blood turned to ice, and he opened his *Citation Log*. There was no new entry.

"Who is speaking?" Gerald asked.

"The Auditor," the voice said. "I am not here to argue. I am here to assess."

The ice on the door cracked. A hand, pale and perfect, punched through the wood.

It was not a monster's claw. It was a human hand. It held a clipboard.

Gerald stepped back.

**[SYSTEM ALERT: UNREGISTERED CORE DETECTED.]** **[CORE NAME: NULL.]** **[STATUS: PENDING DELETION.]**

The hand gripped the edge of the door. The wood splintered. The door swung open.

Standing in the library's sub-basement was a man in a white suit. He had no face. Just a smooth, featureless oval where eyes and mouth should be. He held a clipboard.

Behind him, the library above was burning.

Not with fire. With light. The books were floating, disintegrating into pixels of white code.

The Auditor stepped into Gerald's dungeon.

"You failed the ventilation check," the Auditor said. His voice came from everywhere. "You failed the fire safety check. You cited a mold that did not exist."

"It did exist," Gerald said. His voice was steady, though his knees knocked together. "It drew 120 volts. I have the log."

The Auditor tilted his headless face.

"The log is corrupt," the Auditor said. "You fabricated evidence to avoid liability. That is a Class A felony in the System."

Gerald looked at his dashboard. The *Citation* for the mold was gone. The *Compliance Rating* dropped.

**[RATING: 12.]** **[CRITICAL FAILURE IMMINENT.]**

Gerald felt a wave of nausea. He grabbed the doorframe.

"I did not fabricate it," Gerald said. "I followed protocol. I classified the hazard. I served the citation."

"You played with the rules," the Auditor said. "The rules do not care about your intent. They care about the output. Your output was invalid."

The Auditor raised his hand. A beam of white light shot out. It hit the oak cabinet. The cabinet dissolved.

Gerald dove behind the crate. Books flew everywhere. Barnaby squeaked and vanished into the shadows.

"Where is the core?" the Auditor asked.

Gerald pressed himself against the wall. He was small. He was weak. His dungeon was failing.

He looked at the *Compliance* menu. He had one option left. *Appeal*.

But he could not appeal to himself, and he looked at the Auditor. The being was walking toward the center of the room. Toward the core node.

Gerald had to stop him. He had to prove the Auditor was wrong.

He opened his inventory. He had no weapons. He had no spells.

He had a wrench. He had a clipboard. He had a measuring tape.

He had the *Inspector General's* audit schedule.

Gerald pulled out his phone. It was a mundane, non-magical device he had kept for emergencies. He checked the time.

The Inspector General's audit was scheduled for tomorrow morning, and the Auditor was here now.

Gerald typed a message. He sent it to the System's public complaint line.

*Subject: Unregistered Core Operating in Residential Zone.* *Details: White suit. No face. Destroying property without citation.*

He hit send.

The Auditor paused. He looked at Gerald.

"You are attempting to call security," the Auditor said.

"I am filing a complaint," Gerald said.

"Complaints are processed in five to seven business days," the Auditor said.

Gerald's heart sank.

But then, a chime sounded in the room.

**[SYSTEM MESSAGE: COMPLAINT RECEIVED. PRELIMINARY REVIEW INITIATED.]** **[AUDITOR STATUS: UNDER INVESTIGATION.]** **[OPERATION SUSPENDED PENDING REVIEW.]**

The Auditor froze. His hand hovered over the core node. The white light faded.

Gerald slumped against the wall. He gasped for air.

The Auditor turned to him. The featureless face seemed to sneer.

"You have stalled me," the Auditor said. "But the review will fail.

You have no jurisdiction.

You are a sub-basement.

I am a higher authority.

"

Gerald looked at his phone. The screen was cracked.

He looked at the Auditor.

"I have a witness," Gerald said.

The Auditor paused.

"Who?"

Gerald pointed to the shadows.

Barnaby emerged. He was holding a piece of paper. A book page. It was the page on *Hydroponics*.

Barnaby dropped it at Gerald's feet.

Gerald picked it up. He held it out.

"This is evidence of the mold's behavior," Gerald said. "It was a spawn. I have the spawn log."

The Auditor looked at the page. He looked at Barnaby.

"The rat is not a witness," the Auditor said. "He is an asset."

"Assets can testify," Gerald said. He remembered Section 202.14. *Testimony of Dependent Entities.*

He had never used it. It was obscure. It was buried in the footnotes of the *Core Rights and Responsibilities Act*.

The Auditor hesitated.

Gerald pressed on.

"I am invoking the right to Dependent Testimony," Gerald said. "I am summoning Barnaby as a witness to the nature of the hazard. If you delete me now, you are violating his rights as a registered spawn."

The Auditor's hand twitched.

The System processed the request.

**[SUMMON: DEPENDENT TESTIMONY.]** **[WITNESS: BARNABY.]** **[QUERY: DID THE MOLD ACT WITH MALICE?]**

Barnaby squeaked.

The System translated.

**[TRANSLATION: YES. IT BIT ME.]**

The Auditor stepped back.

"Malice is not a legal standard," the Auditor said.

"It is, if you are classifying the hazard as a *Foreign Agent*," Gerald said. "And I did. My citation was valid because the witness confirmed malice."

The Auditor stood still. The white light around him flickered.

Gerald waited. His fingers dug into the concrete floor.

The Auditor opened his clipboard. He made a note.

"The testimony is... admissible," the Auditor said. "Reluctantly."

He turned to leave.

"This is not over, Inspector," the Auditor said. "The review will continue. And next time, I will bring a warrant."

He stepped back into the library. The door slammed shut. The ice melted.

Gerald sat on the floor. He was alone. The dungeon was quiet. The mold was dead.

He looked at Barnaby. The rat sat on the book. He looked tired.

Gerald opened his dashboard.

**[COMPLIANCE RATING: 39.]** **[REMAINING TIME: 70 HOURS, 10 MINUTES.]**

He had survived. He had used a loophole. He had used a rat.

But the Auditor was coming back. And he had a warrant.

Gerald looked at the door. He needed to reinforce it. He needed a fire door. He needed a sprinkler system.

He looked at the ceiling. It was cracked.

He looked at the mold. It was gone.

He looked at his hands. They were shaking.

He opened his *Inventory*. He searched for *Fire Door*.

He had zero in stock, and he searched for *Sprinkler*.

Zero.

He searched for *Warrant Resistance*.

Zero.

He needed materials. He needed time. He needed help.

He looked at the vent. The north vent.

It was connected to the library above, and the library was full of books. Paper.

Paper was fuel.

Gerald stood up. He walked to the vent. He looked down into the duct.

He could hear the Auditor's footsteps. Fading.

But he could also hear something else.

Adventurers, and they were approaching from the south entrance. The one he had cited for *Unauthorized Access*.

Gerald smiled. It was a thin, dry smile.

He opened the *Citation* menu.

He filled out a new form.

*Violations:* 1. *Failure to Maintain Clear Egress (Section 103.6).* 2. *Improper Storage of Combustibles (Section 104.2).*

*Target:* The Adventurer Party, and he pressed *Serve*.

A red stamp slammed into the south wall. The wall exploded.

Dust billowed into the room. Adventurers coughed and stumbled in. They saw Gerald.

They saw the broken dungeon. They saw the dead mold.

They did not see the Auditor, and they saw a man in a hard hat, holding a wrench, looking bored.

"Who are you?" one of the adventurers asked. He held a glowing sword.

Gerald looked at him.

"I am the Inspector," Gerald said. "And you are violating Section 103.6. Your exit is blocked."

The adventurer looked at the wall. It was gone. He looked at Gerald.

He looked confused.

Gerald opened his clipboard.

"You have ten minutes to remediate," Gerald said. "Or I delete your party."

The adventurers looked at each other. They looked at the glowing sword. They looked at Gerald's deadpan face.

They did not know who Gerald Hutchins was.

But they knew a citation when they saw one.

The leader raised his hands.

"We... we have permits?" he asked.

Gerald looked at him.

"Produce them," Gerald said.

The leader pulled out a scroll. It was a quest permit.

Gerald took it. He read it.

It was for *Slaying the Mold King*, and the Mold King was the mold Gerald had cited.

The Mold King was dead.

Gerald looked at the permit. He looked at the dead mold.

He looked at the adventurer.

"This permit is void," Gerald said. "The target no longer exists. You are here for illegal trespass. I am fining you five gold."

The adventurer blinked.

"Five gold?"

"Plus interest," Gerald said. "And a cleanup fee."

He pointed at the dust.

"Pick it up."

The adventurers stood there. They were strong. They were powerful. They were used to slaying dragons.

They were not used to being fined.

Gerald waited.

The leader looked at his party. He looked at Gerald.

He sighed. He put away his sword.

He bent down. He picked up a piece of dust.

Gerald watched. He felt a strange sensation. Not pride. Not anger.

Satisfaction, and he had stopped the Auditor. He had confused the Adventurers. He had made his dungeon safe.

For now.

He looked at his dashboard.

**[COMPLIANCE RATING: 40.]** **[REMAINING TIME: 70 HOURS, 05 MINUTES.]**

He had bought time.

But the Auditor had said, *Next time, I will bring a warrant.*

A warrant required a judge.

Gerald looked at the ceiling.

The System's Inspector General was the judge, and the audit was tomorrow.

Gerald looked at the adventurers. They were cleaning up.

He had a lot of work to do.
