Chapter 240: Loophole or Survival

# Chapter 240

The wrench felt heavier with every block of asphalt Gerald crossed.

It wasn't just the weight of the steel. It was the weight of the Citation. The System had generated the document in real-time, a shimmering overlay on his retina that pulsed with a red, warning light. *Violation 14-C: Impersonation of Public Official.*

Gerald kept his eyes on the pavement. He counted his steps.

One, two, three, four.

The rhythm was a metronome against the chaos of the city. Police sirens wailed in the distance, a dissonant chord that grated against his desire for order. He didn't run.

Running led to sloppiness.

Sloppiness led to missed measurements. Missed measurements led to structural failure.

He checked his wrist. Forty-seven minutes remained.

The Mayor's office was in the Civic Plaza, a brutalist concrete slab that Gerald had inspected twice in the last decade. He knew every crack in the foundation. He knew the load-bearing capacity of the lobby tiles. He knew that the third floor elevator shaft was two inches out of plumb, a fact he had flagged in 2019 and which had been ignored by the previous administration.

That ignored fact was now his weapon.

A police cruiser screeched to a halt ten feet away. Two officers stepped out, hands resting on their holsters. They looked confused. They looked at a man in a rumpled windbreaker, clutching a pipe wrench, walking with the stiff, purposeful gait of a man who has never in his life broken a rule.

"Sir, step away from the street," the officer said.

Gerald didn't stop. He didn't look up. "I am responding to an active Code Violation in progress. You are obstructing an inspector."

The officer blinked. "You don't look like an inspector."

"I am the inspector," Gerald said.

He turned his head, just enough to show the badge he'd forged—not with magic, but with a printer and laminator in the library basement three years ago, when he'd thought the job required it. The System had accepted the intent. The badge glowed with a faint, System-sanctioned blue aura.

"And this street is a public right-of-way. You are blocking my path to a citation."

The officer hesitated.

The blue glow was unsettling.

It wasn't police blue. It was deeper, colder.

The kind of blue that meant *The System Agrees With This Man.*

"Move aside," Gerald said. It wasn't a command. It was a statement of fact, delivered with the flat tone of a man reading a lease agreement.

The officer stepped back. Not because he believed Gerald, but because the air around Gerald felt pressurized, like the moment before a storm breaks.

Gerald kept walking.

Thirty minutes.

The crowd thickened as he approached the plaza. News drones buzzed overhead, their rotors cutting into the heavy humidity. A reporter shoved a microphone in his face.

"Is it true you're trying to shut down the Mayor?"

Gerald didn't break stride. "I am enforcing municipal code."

"Who are you working for?"

"The City," Gerald said. "Which is currently operating under a nullity clause."

He ducked under the velvet rope of the Civic Plaza entrance. The security guards, large men in ill-fitting suits, stepped forward.

They didn't have badges.

They didn't have authority. They had muscle, which Gerald had learned to respect, but not to fear.

"Stop," the lead guard said. He reached for Gerald's shoulder.

Gerald sidestepped.

Not a dodge. A pivot.

He used the guard's momentum, letting the man's arm slide off the slick fabric of his windbreaker.

The guard stumbled.

Gerald didn't hit him. He didn't attack. He simply walked past the stumbling man, his eyes fixed on the revolving doors.

*Violation 14-C requires physical service.*

To serve the Citation, Gerald had to touch the target. The target was the Core, which was currently inhabiting the Mayor's body. To get to the Mayor, he had to get past the security. To get past the security, he had to use the environment.

The revolving doors.

They were non-compliant.

Gerald had flagged them in 2015. *Excessive rotational speed. Lack of emergency stop mechanism. Non-conforming to ADA accessibility standards regarding manual force requirements.*

He pushed.

The doors spun faster than they should have. A mechanical groan echoed from the housing. The guard who had stumbled lunged for Gerald, grabbing the back of his coat.

Gerald yanked free. He didn't fight. He let the door carry him.

The guard was pulled with the rotation, off-balance, his feet tangling. He fell into the lobby, sliding on the polished marble floor.

Gerald stumbled out the other side, catching himself against the reception desk. His heart hammered against his ribs.

He hated this.

He hated the lack of control.

He hated that he was improvising. His stomach churned, a physical nausea rising in his throat.

*Do not vomit. Not yet. Deliver the Citation first.*

He looked up. The Mayor was there.

Councilman Thorne stood behind the security desk, smiling. His smile was too wide. His eyes were too shiny.

The Mimic inside him was wearing the skin, but it hadn't mastered the micro-expressions. The left eyelid twitched.

The jaw hung open, revealing teeth that were a fraction too uniform.

"Mr. Hutchins," Thorne said. His voice was smooth, devoid of the gravelly rasp Gerald remembered from their previous encounters. "You're late."

"I am on time," Gerald said. He pulled the wrench from his coat. The metal was cold. "I am here to serve a Citation."

Thorne laughed. It was a dry, rattling sound. "You have no authority.

I am the Mayor.

I am the law."

"You are a structure," Gerald said. He checked his watch. Twelve minutes. "And you are in violation of Zoning Ordinance 88-B, which prohibits sentient, non-municipal entities from occupying public office during active inspection periods."

Thorne's smile vanished. "You think you can stop me with paperwork?"

"I think I can stop you with physics."

Gerald took a step forward.

Thorne signaled to the remaining guards. "Seize him."

Two guards rushed forward. Gerald didn't run. He turned his back to them and slammed his wrench into the side of the revolving door mechanism.

Metal shrieked against metal. Sparks flew.

The door jammed.

The guards crashed into the glass. They bounced off, disoriented. The mechanism, already flagged for poor maintenance, shattered. Glass rained down like hail.

Gerald ducked. He crawled through the broken frame, shards cutting his windbreaker. He came out on the other side, shaking off glass dust.

He was in the lobby. The Mayor's office was at the end of the hall.

But the path was blocked.

A wall of water erupted from the floor.

The Mimic had triggered the building's emergency suppression system, but it had wired it wrong. The pipes had burst, flooding the lobby with an inch of murky water.

Gerald froze.

Water.

Electricity.

He looked at the ceiling. The main lighting grid was exposed. A panel had fallen loose, dangling wires swaying like pendulums.

The System flashed a warning in his vision.

**DANGER: ELECTRICAL SHORT-CIRCUIT IMMINENT.** **VIOLATION: UNINSULATED WIRING ABOVE WATER SOURCE.** **COMPLIANCE PENALTY: LIGHTNING RESISTANCE REMOVED.**

Gerald's skin prickled. The passive resistance he had built up over the last ten chapters—earned by meticulously inspecting and repairing his own dungeon's electrical panels—vanished.

He was vulnerable.

Thorne walked forward.

The water didn't slow him down. He moved through it like it wasn't there. "You're trapped, Gerald.

The building is mine. The water is mine.

The fire is mine."

Gerald looked at the water. He looked at the dangling wires. He looked at the wrench in his hand.

He couldn't fight Thorne. Thorne was stronger, faster, and likely magical.

But Gerald was an inspector.

He looked at the floor. The tiles were cracked. The grout was deteriorating. He had noted this in 2018. *Grout degradation leading to potential slip hazard.*

He looked at the ceiling. The wires were swinging.

He looked at the wrench.

Gerald took a deep breath. He steadied his hand. He didn't throw the wrench. He aimed at the water.

He struck the floor. Hard.

The impact traveled through the water, a shockwave that rippled outward. It wasn't a magical attack. It was kinetic energy, transferred through a medium.

The water moved.

The waves hit the dangling wires.

The sparks connected.

A bolt of blue-white lightning arced from the ceiling, striking the water.

The lobby flashed white.

Gerald had braced himself. He had planted his feet wide, lowering his center of gravity. He had insulated himself by standing on the only dry patch of floor near the entrance, a raised platform he had mentally mapped moments before.

Thorne didn't brace.

The electricity surged through the water, seeking the path of least resistance. Thorne was the largest conductor in the room. The Mimic's flesh, though tough, was wet.

Thorne screamed. It wasn't a human scream. It was the sound of tearing metal and burning flesh.

His skin flickered. For a second, the human mask dissolved, revealing the chitinous, shifting mass beneath. The Mimic convulsed, its form destabilizing by the electrical surge.

Gerald moved.

He slid across the wet tile, his boots finding purchase on the raised platform. He closed the distance. Thorne was stunned, smoke rising from his shoulders.

Gerald raised the wrench.

He didn't swing it. He placed it on Thorne's chest.

"Citation," Gerald said.

The wrench touched the Mimic's form.

The System registered the contact.

**SERVICE COMPLETE.** **VIOLATION: IMPERSONATION OF MUNICIPAL OFFICIAL.** **PENALTY: STAT DEBUFF APPLIED.** **TARGET: CORE DESIGNATION 'MIMIC-PRIME'.** **DEBUFF: -50% PHYSICAL REGENERATION. -50% STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY.**

Thorne gasped. The human skin reformed, but it was gray, slack. The Mimic inside was weakened. The electrical damage had bypassed its magic, striking at the biological components it relied on to maintain the illusion.

Gerald pulled the wrench back. He stepped away.

Thorne slumped against the desk. He tried to stand, but his legs buckled. The debuff was taking hold. The System was punishing the Core for the violation, weakening its ability to maintain its form.

Gerald pulled out his phone. He dialed 911.

"This is Gerald Hutchins," he said.

"I am reporting a medical emergency at the Civic Plaza. The Mayor is suffering from a severe electrical injury.

Also, please send a Code Enforcement team. The revolving doors are non-compliant."

He hung up.

He looked at Thorne. The Mimic's eyes were wild, confused.

It hadn't expected this.

It had expected a fight. It had expected magic. It had expected a hero.

It hadn't expected a building inspector.

Gerald waited for the ambulance. He didn't move. He didn't celebrate. He stood still, watching the water recede, watching the smoke clear.

Then, his vision blurred.

The System flashed a new alert.

**AUDIT IN PROGRESS.** **INSPECTOR GENERAL NOTIFIED.** **SUBJECT: GERALD HUTCHINS.** **STATUS: FLAGGED.**

A cold voice echoed in his mind. It wasn't the System's usual robotic tone. It was sharper. Colder.

*You have violated the spirit of the Code, Core 402. You used infrastructure as a weapon. This is not permitted.*

Gerald froze.

The spirit of the Code?

He had followed the letter of the Code. He had identified a hazard. He had mitigated the hazard by neutralizing the source of the hazard. Where was the violation?

*The Act of Inspection cannot be used as an Act of War,* the voice continued. *You are suspended from issuing Citations for a period of 72 hours.*

Gerald's blood ran cold.

Without Citations, he had no defense.

Thorne, or what was left of him, laughed. A low, gurgling sound.

"You're suspended," the Mimic whispered. "Who will cite you now?"

From the shadows of the lobby, other figures emerged.

They weren't human. They were Cores.

Small, localized dungeons that had been hiding in the city's infrastructure.

The sewer rats. The pigeons. The mold in the walls.

They had all felt the electrical surge. They had all seen the Mayor fall.

And they had all heard the suspension.

Gerald gripped his wrench. It was just a wrench now. No glow. No System sanction.

He was alone.

And the clock was still ticking.

The news vans were outside. The police were coming. But the real threat was inside.

Gerald looked at the suspended status bar in his vision. It was shrinking. 71:59.

He had one hour to find a loophole.

Or he had to survive the next hour without one.

He took a step back.

The mold spores from the ventilation shaft drifted toward him.
