Chapter 159: Carving with Spite

# Chapter 159

Gerald counted to three, the floor’s edge crumbling under his left shoe. That was the problem.

Gerald stared at the blank white rectangle in his hands, the pen hovering a millimeter above the paper. The hole in the floor pulsed with a rhythmic, bruised light, expanding inch by inch. Ashford's skyline warped through it, the buildings twisting like wet clay, the sky a sickly purple bruise. The black glass tower from the previous timeline—or perhaps the correct one—loomed in the distance, jagged and obscene against the clouds.

His status read **ARCHITECT**. The objective read **REBUILD THE WORLD**.

Rebuilding required materials. Rebuilding required permits. Rebuilding required a site survey.

Gerald took a breath. It rattled in his chest, tasting of copper and old dust.

He looked down at the hole, and the edges were fraying, reality peeling back to reveal the raw code of the universe beneath. It wasn't chaos. It was structural failure.

A load-bearing wall had been removed without support beams.

He uncapped his pen. The ink was black, viscous, and cold.

He didn't write *Demolition* again. That had been a reflex.

A mistake.

You don't tear down a condemned building until you have filed the proper notice to vacate and secured the perimeter. You document. You cite.

You condemn, and he wrote: **NOTICE OF CONDEMNATION: REALITY STRUCTURE.**

The pen moved with a certainty that felt foreign in his hand. The words didn't stay on the paper. They sank into it, glowing with a faint amber hue before fading.

A notification chimed. Not the soft ping of a quest update. This was the harsh, grinding buzz of a municipal alarm.

**SYSTEM ALERT: UNAUTHORIZED MODIFICATION DETECTED.** **SUBJECT: LOCAL REALITY GRID.** **ACTION: STOP WORK ORDER ISSUED.**

Gerald ignored it, and he turned his attention to the hole.

The fraying edges were spreading faster now, eating into the library's foundation. He could feel the vibration of the earth shaking, the roots of the city groaning.

If the foundation gave way, the tower wouldn't just stand; it would anchor itself to the void, pulling the rest of Ashford down with it.

He needed to shore up the perimeter. But he had no concrete. No steel. No timber.

He had the dungeon.

His dungeon, the sub-basement, was still connected to this point. He could feel its layout. Four thousand square feet of damp concrete, three mold spores (currently dormant), one water leak (now a torrent), and a confused rat (presumed deceased). The Compliance Rating had fluctuated wildly since the void incident, currently hovering at a catastrophic 14/100 due to

"Existential Instability."

But the code was still there. The System's building code was universal.

It applied to rooms.

It applied to dungeons. And if Gerald had learned anything in thirty-one years of public service, it was that the code applied to *everything*, provided one could define the boundaries.

He looked at the twisted city through the hole. He needed to define a boundary around it.

He wrote: **PROVISIONAL FENCE LINE: SECTOR 4, GRID B.**

The air shimmered.

A line of translucent, amber light snapped into existence around the edge of the hole, stretching out into the library's broken floor. It hummed with energy.

It was a barrier.

A temporary fence.

Gerald stepped toward it. The light resisted him, buzzing against his skin.

**VIOLATION: UNLICENSED CONSTRUCTION ON PRIVATE PROPERTY.**

The message flashed red across his vision.

Gerald frowned. He checked the property deed in his mind.

The library was municipal, and he was the municipal inspector. he was the state.

The state could not be cited by the state. Unless the property had been seized.

The black tower had seized the library. The Core had become the landlord. And Gerald was trespassing.

He smiled. It was a thin, joyless expression.

He walked back to the center of the room, where the throne of shadows had been. He sat down. The floor was hard.

His knees popped. He opened his mental file cabinet, the one that had expanded since he became a Core.

It wasn't full of combat logs or loot inventories, which was full of forms.

**FORM 14-C: EXEMPTION FOR EMERGENCY STRUCTURAL STABILIZATION.**

He drafted it.

*Reason for Exemption: Imminent collapse of local reality substrate due to unauthorized void expansion.* *Authority Cited: Municipal Code Section 88.12, "Right to Prevent Total Catastrophic Failure."* *Applicant: Gerald Hutchins, Inspector of Public Works.*

He signed it with a flourish. The pen scratched against the paper.

The red notification vanished.

The amber fence stabilized. It turned from translucent to opaque, a solid wall of light. The fraying edges of the hole stopped spreading. The twisted buildings outside seemed to pause, their melting forms freezing in place.

But the tower was still there. It was growing. A new spire of black glass pushed up through the clouds, sharp and aggressive. It was trying to pierce the barrier Gerald had erected.

He needed to attack it. But how?

He couldn't spawn monsters.

His spawn list was empty. The rat was gone.

The mold was dead, and he had no soldiers. No traps. No fireballs.

He had citations.

He looked at the tower. It was a structure.

It had dimensions, which had materials.

It had a purpose.

He focused his intent on the tower. He needed to see it. To measure it. To find the flaws.

The System interface flickered. A new window opened.

**INSPECTION MODE: ACTIVE.** **TARGET: THE SPIRE OF THE SOURCE.** **COMPLIANCE RATING: UNKNOWN.** **CALCULATING...**

Numbers scrolled. Height. Width.

Material density. Foundation depth.

The numbers were wrong, and they were nonsensical. Infinite loops. Division by zero errors.

The tower wasn't built according to any code Gerald knew.

It was built on magic, on will, on the sheer arrogance of the Core that had been the

"Source."

But the System didn't care about magic. It cared about code.

If the tower didn't follow the code, it was illegal.

Gerald stood up. He walked to the edge of the fence. He pointed his pen at the tower.

**CITATION: VIOLATION OF ZONING ORDINANCE 12.4.** **SUBSECTION: PROHIBITION OF STRUCTURES EXCEEDING THREE HUNDRED FEET WITHOUT A VARIANCE PERMIT.**

The tower was three thousand feet tall.

The citation struck the barrier. The amber light flared. The tower shuddered. A crack appeared in its black glass surface, high up near the peak.

It was working.

But the barrier was cracking too.

The energy cost of holding the fence while issuing citations was draining Gerald. His vision blurred. His hands shook.

He felt a physical nausea, the same sickness that came when he found a violation he couldn't fix because the owner refused to pay. Impotence.

Paralysis, and he couldn't fight it with force. He had to fight it with bureaucracy.

He needed to appeal.

If the tower appealed, there would be a Hearing. In a Hearing, Gerald could argue structural logic. He could prove the tower was unsafe. He could get it condemned.

But the Core running the tower was powerful. It wouldn't just accept a citation. It would counter-cite.

A new notification flashed.

**COUNTER-CITATION RECEIVED.** **SENDER: UNKNOWN CORE [SIGNATURE: VOID].** **CLAIM: ILLEGAL USE OF MUNICIPAL AUTHORITY IN NON-MUNICIPAL ZONE.** **PENALTY: REVOCATION OF INSPECTOR STATUS.**

Gerald's status bar flickered. **ARCHITECT** turned gray. Then it cracked.

He was losing his license.

If he lost the license, he couldn't write. He couldn't cite. He would be just a man in a basement, watching the world end.

He needed a loophole. A way to keep the license active even while being cited.

He remembered **Form 11-B: Unlicensed Biological Entity**. He couldn't use it on himself, because he was the entity. But what about the hole?

The hole was an entity. It was a breach. It was unauthorized.

He looked at the hole. He looked at the fence. He looked at the tower.

The tower was the cause. The hole was the symptom. The fence was the symptom's bandage.

If he cited the hole, he would be citing himself.

If he cited the tower, the tower cited him, and he needed a third party.

The city.

Ashford was still there, under the bruised sky.

The people were still there, twisted but alive. The buildings were melting, but they were still standing.

They were still structures. They still had owners.

Gerald closed his eyes. He reached out with his Core sense, extending it beyond the basement, beyond the library, into the city.

He felt them. Thousands of them.

The shop owners.

The homeowners. The tenants. The municipal employees.

They were all connected to the buildings, and the buildings were connected to the ground. The ground was connected to the dungeon.

They were all linked.

He wrote: **SUMMONING OF JOINT DEFENDANTS.**

It wasn't a combat spell. It was a subpoena.

The air around Gerald's dungeon grew heavy. The smell of rot vanished, replaced by the scent of coffee, exhaust fumes, and wet pavement.

Images flickered in the corners of his vision. Not monsters. People.

Mrs.

Gable from the bakery on Fourth Street. Mr. Henderson from the hardware store.

The librarian, old and confused. They weren't physically there.

But their *intent* was.

Their attachment to their homes, their businesses, their lives, which was a structural load.

The System recognized it.

**JOINT LIABILITY ASSUMED.** **APPEAL WINDOW OPEN.** **HEARING INITIATED.**

The floor of the closet dissolved.

Gerald was no longer in the basement. He was in a room.

It was a large room. Wood-paneled. High ceilings.

Long tables. A judge's bench at the front, empty.

Two podiums on either side.

On one side stood the Void Core. It had no face, just a swirling mass of gray static inside a suit that looked like it was made of smoke. It held a briefcase made of black glass.

On the other side stood Gerald. He still held his clipboard. He still held his pen.

The judge entered. It was the Inspector General. The same entity that had audited him in Chapter 1, but larger now.

Taller.

Its skin was made of parchment, and its eyes were red wax seals.

"Court is in session," the Inspector General said. The voice was dry, like leaves skittering on concrete. "Case number 99-442. *The State of Reality vs. The Void Core.*"

The Void Core stepped forward. Its voice was the sound of tearing paper.

"The defendant is a rogue element," the Void Core said.

"It violates the fundamental laws of entropy. It builds. It organizes.

It imposes order on chaos. This is illegal.

The universe is meant to decay.

This Core prevents decay.

It is a cancer.

"

Gerald didn't speak. He looked at the Void Core's briefcase. He looked at the static.

He opened his clipboard.

"The plaintiff," the Inspector General said. "State your case."

Gerald stepped forward. He didn't argue about entropy. He didn't argue about good and evil.

He opened his folder, and he pulled out a stack of papers.

"Exhibit A," Gerald said. His voice was calm. Flat. "The Void Core's foundation."

He threw the papers onto the table. They slid across the wood, stopping inches from the Void Core's podium.

"We have reviewed the structural integrity of the black tower," Gerald continued. "It is built on a substrate of pure void. There is no bedrock.

No footings. No anchor points.

"

The Void Core shifted. The static rippled.

"It is floating," Gerald said. "A structure that does not touch the ground is not a building. It is a balloon."

He turned to the Inspector General.

"Under Municipal Code 44.2, any structure exceeding ten stories must have a certified foundation. The Void Core has none. It is relying on atmospheric pressure and magical buoyancy to stay up."

He pointed his pen at the Void Core.

"That is a violation of the Weight Load Ordinance. If the wind shifts, or the atmospheric pressure changes, the structure will collapse. And when it falls, it will crush the adjacent residential zones."

The Inspector General leaned forward. Its wax-seal eyes narrowed.

"Show the calculation," it said.

Gerald held up a chart. It was complex. It was boring. It was perfect.

"The load-bearing capacity of the void substrate is zero," Gerald said.

"The tower weighs approximately four million tons. The buoyant force is insufficient to counteract gravity for more than six hours. After that, failure is inevitable.

It is not a threat to the world, Inspector General.

It is a liability.

"

The Void Core hissed.

"You are manipulating the code!"

"I am following the code," Gerald said.

The Inspector General tapped its gavel. The sound was loud. Final.

"The plaintiff's citation is upheld," the Inspector General said. "The defendant is found in violation of Code 44.2. The structure is hereby ordered to be condemned."

The Void Core screamed. The static exploded outward.

But nothing happened.

The tower outside didn't fall.

Gerald looked at the Inspector General.

"Why hasn't the penalty been applied?"

"Because the citation was served," the Inspector General said. "But the appeal is not over."

The Void Core was laughing now. It pulled a new document from its briefcase.

"I appeal on the grounds of *Precedent*," the Void Core said.

It slammed the document onto the table.

**APPEAL: STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY VIA INTENTION.**

"The defendant claims that its structure is sustained by its own will," the Inspector General read. "Under System Rule 12, 'Will of the Builder' supersedes physical laws if the Core's Compliance Rating exceeds 90."

Gerald felt a chill, and he looked at his own status.

**ARCHITECT.** **COMPLIANCE RATING: 47.**

He didn't have 90.

He never had 90. He was a municipal inspector. He followed rules.

He didn't break them. He didn't exceed them.

The Void Core had 90. It was a perfect dungeon. A perfect lie.

"The appeal is granted," the Inspector General said. "The citation is voided. The tower remains."

Gerald's clipboard cracked. The paper turned to ash.

He had lost.

The room dissolved.

Gerald was back in the closet, and the fence was gone. The hole was widening.

The tower was growing.

He was on his knees. The pen was broken.

He had no license. No paper. No ink.

He looked at his hands. They were shaking.

Then, he saw it.

On the floor, amidst the dust and blood, was a single object. It wasn't his pen. It wasn't his clipboard.

It was a stamp.

The official ink stamp of the City of Ashford. He had lost it years ago. Or maybe he had never had it.

He picked it up. It was cold. Heavy.

There was no ink pad.

But there was the blood from the floor.

Gerald dipped the stamp into the red puddle, and he looked at the hole. He looked at the tower.

He pressed the stamp onto the air.

**DENIED.**

The word hung in the void. Red. Angry. Final.

The System didn't respond.

Gerald didn't care. He stamped it again.

**DENIED.**

**DENIED.**

**DENIED.**

The hole didn't close. The tower didn't fall.

But the air stopped moving. The twisting buildings outside froze.

The Void Core's voice echoed from the tower. It sounded confused.

"What are you doing?"

Gerald looked up. He was smiling. It was a scary smile. The kind you give a contractor who thinks he can get away with using cheap drywall.

"I'm not citing you," Gerald said. "I'm condemning you."

He pulled out his pen. It was gone.

He pulled out a piece of wood from the broken floorboard.

He carved.

Not a word. A symbol.

The symbol of the Building Inspector.

A circle. A triangle. A line through the middle.

He carved it into the floor. Into the world.

The System flashed white.

**ERROR: UNDEFINED ACTION.**

**NEW STATUS: REBEL.**

Gerald kept carving.

The hole shrinked.

Not because of code.

Because of spite.
