Chapter 21

Gerald was on line seven of Form 9.3 when the freeze notification hit.

He had been filling it out in triplicate, which the System had insisted on, and line seven required him to describe the nature of unauthorized credential use in language that would satisfy a legal review. He had written "impersonation" and crossed it out. He had written "misrepresentation" and crossed it out. He had settled on "unauthorized exercise of regulatory authority under a registration number not lawfully assigned to the acting party" because that was what the municipal code called it when a contractor pulled a permit under a licensed electrician's number without the electrician's knowledge.

The notification appeared in the corner of his vision, the same pale blue as every other System alert.

*Investigation opened. Citation authority suspended pending review. Duration: indeterminate. Appeal window: closed.*

Gerald read it twice. He set down the pen he had been using, which was a mental construct the System had provided because Gerald refused to fill out forms without a pen, and he stared at the word "indeterminate."

Indeterminate meant no timeline. Indeterminate meant he could not cite anything until the System decided the investigation was closed. Indeterminate meant that the one tool he had spent twenty chapters perfecting was now locked in a drawer with no key.

He was still staring when the first alarm went off.

Not the System alarm. His own. The pressure sensors he had installed along the east corridor at the beginning of the month, the ones he had calibrated to trigger at anything heavier than a house cat. The alarm meant something large had crossed the threshold from the neighboring dungeon core's territory into his sub-basement.

The second alarm meant something large had crossed the threshold into his spawn chamber.

The third alarm meant it was heading toward the central junction, where Gerald kept his spawn list and his compliance documentation and the three handrails he had installed that morning.

Gerald did not panic. He had not panicked in thirty-one years of inspections, not when the contractor showed up with a forged permit, not when the developer threatened to sue, not when the water main broke beneath the parking lot and he had to decide whether to close the road or let traffic continue. He had a process. He followed the process.

The process was: identify the hazard, determine the applicable code section, issue the appropriate enforcement action.

He reached for a Citation. The System rejected it. Citation authority suspended.

He tried again. Same result.

The third alarm was still going. Whatever had come through was moving fast. Gerald could feel it through the dungeon's sensory network, the way he felt a water leak in the walls, a vibration in the substrate, a wrongness in the air pressure. It was heavy. It was warm-blooded. It was angry.

He pulled up the dungeon map. The intruder was in the north corridor, moving toward the junction at roughly two meters per second. Based on the vibration pattern, Gerald estimated it at four hundred kilograms, possibly more. A bear, maybe. Or a construct. Something with dense bone and heavy footfall.

He had no Citations. He had no spawn capable of fighting it. His strongest entity was the rat, and the rat had a name now because Gerald had started keeping a log, and the rat was named Unit 7, and Unit 7 was currently hiding behind a handrail and was not going to fight a four-hundred-kilogram bear.

Gerald opened the enforcement menu. Citations: grayed out. Fines: grayed out. Compliance Orders: grayed out.

Everything punitive was grayed out.

He scrolled down.

*Advisory Notices: available.*

Advisory Notices were non-binding. They went on the record but they did nothing to the target's stats. They were the equivalent of a letter that said please consider upgrading your smoke detectors. Gerald had never issued one. They were useless.

He scrolled further.

*Stop Work Orders: available.*

Gerald stopped scrolling. He stared at the option.

A Stop Work Order was not punitive. It was administrative. It was the tool he used when a contractor was building something unsafe and he needed them to stop immediately, before the structure collapsed. It did not fine them. It did not debuff them. It simply said: you cannot continue building until the order is lifted.

The System's version of a Stop Work Order, Gerald realized, would freeze a dungeon core's ability to spawn new entities. Not debuff existing ones. Not damage them. Just stop the pipeline. No new spawns until the order was lifted.

He could not fight the bear. But he could stop the bear's creator from making more bears.

The intruder was in the junction now. Gerald could feel it sniffing around his spawn chamber, could feel it bumping against the handrails he had installed. Unit 7 had gone silent. The rat was probably in the walls.

Gerald opened a Stop Work Order form. It was Form 14-A, which he had never seen before, which meant the System had generated it specifically for this situation. The form required three pieces of information: the target's registration number, the nature of the unsafe condition, and the scope of the stop.

He did not know the target's registration number. He could see the intruder but not its owner.

The form rejected the submission. Target identification required.

Gerald thought. He thought the way he thought when a contractor tried to argue that a permit was substantially complete and he had to find the one line in the code that said substantially was not a legal term.

The intruder had come from the east. The east corridor connected to the territory of a dungeon core Gerald had cited in Chapter 14. That core's registration number was 00892-2020. Gerald had cited it for inadequate egress width in its main chamber. The Citation had been upheld. The core's Compliance Rating had dropped to 31.

If the intruder had come from that territory, it belonged to that core.

He filled in the registration number. He filled in the unsafe condition: uncontrolled biological entity operating in a non-registered structure without proper containment. He filled in the scope: all spawn operations within registration 00892-2020, pending inspection and clearance.

He submitted.

The System processed. The form turned green.

*Stop Work Order issued. Registration 00892-2020. Spawn operations frozen. Duration: until inspection and clearance. Note: Stop Work Orders are not subject to Citation authority suspension. This order is valid.*

Gerald felt the effect immediately. It was not dramatic. There was no explosion, no flash of light, no notification that the rival core had been punished. There was only a sensation, like a faucet being turned off. Somewhere in the east, a pipeline that had been running for hours or days or weeks had simply stopped. No new spawns. No new entities. The core was frozen.

The bear in his junction, however, was still very much present.

Gerald had not frozen the bear. He had frozen the core's ability to make more bears. The existing bear was still there, still heavy, still four hundred kilograms, still looking for something to eat or something to destroy.

He needed to get it out.

He looked at his options. He could not cite it. He could not fight it. He could not spawn anything to fight it.

He looked at the dungeon map. The bear was in the junction. The junction had three exits. East corridor, west corridor, and the south access shaft. The south access shaft was the one Gerald had built to municipal code, which meant it was exactly the right width for a person to walk through and exactly the wrong width for a four-hundred-kilogram bear to squeeze through.

The west corridor was where the handrails were. The east corridor was where the bear had come in.

Gerald checked the east corridor dimensions. Two meters and ten centimeters wide. The bear was, based on the vibration pattern, approximately one meter and eighty centimeters at the shoulder. It could fit through the east corridor. It had come through the east corridor.

The south access shaft was one meter and forty centimeters wide. Municipal code minimum for a service corridor. Gerald had built it to code.

He could not push the bear out. But he could make it want to leave.

He opened the environmental controls. He had installed a ventilation system in the east corridor three weeks ago, after the System's first audit cited him for inadequate airflow. The ventilation system was rated for a certain cubic feet per minute, and it worked, and it kept his dungeon at a comfortable temperature.

He set the east corridor ventilation to maximum. Then he set the south access shaft ventilation to maximum. Then he closed the west corridor vents.

The air moved. It always moved from high pressure to low pressure, and Gerald had just created a pressure differential that pushed air from the junction toward the east corridor. The bear would feel it. Four hundred kilograms of animal instinct would feel the change in airflow and respond the way animals always respond to a draft: by moving away from it.

The bear moved. Gerald felt it through the floor sensors. It turned. It walked east. It went through the east corridor, which was wide enough for it, and it exited Gerald's dungeon and returned to the territory of registration 00892-2020, which was now frozen and could not spawn anything to replace it.

Gerald exhaled. He had not fought the bear. He had made the bear leave. This was not heroic. This was what he did. This was what building inspectors did. They did not fight the contractor. They made the contractor leave the site.

He went back to Form 9.3. He was on line eight now. Line eight required him to list all parties who had accessed his registration number. He had one name: the Acting Inspector General. The System had provided the registration number. 00417-2019-A.

He was about to fill in line nine, which asked for the date of first unauthorized use, when a new notification appeared.

It was not blue. It was amber. Amber meant action required. Amber meant something had happened that the System wanted him to address immediately.

*Stop Work Order 00892-2020 has been counter-signed. Counter-signature authority: Acting Inspector General, registration 00417-2019-A. Counter-signature effect: order modified. New scope: inspection authority granted to Acting Inspector General within the boundaries of registration 00417-2019.*

Gerald read it three times.

The Acting Inspector General had counter-signed his Stop Work Order. That was legal. The System allowed higher-authority override of lower-authority orders. The Acting IG outranked Gerald. The counter-signature was valid.

But the counter-signature had modified the scope. It had granted the Acting Inspector General inspection authority inside Gerald's dungeon.

Gerald checked his spawn list. He checked his entity registry. He checked every biological

...entities. The mold spores were in their enclosure. The water leak was in its containment basin. The rat was in its designated habitat, which was a 400-square-foot area with proper ventilation and a minimum of two egress points.

He checked his Compliance Rating.

It was 67.

He had been at 67 for three days. He had been trying to get it to 70. The last three points required a fire suppression system, and Gerald did not yet have the materials to build one. He had filed a requisition with the System's resource allocation module and was waiting on approval.

Sixty-seven. The Inspector General's audit threshold for a Stop Work Order was 50. Gerald was above it. Gerald was safe.

He checked the notification again. The counter-signature had granted inspection authority. It had not granted a Stop Work Order. It had not granted a Citation. It had granted the right to look.

Gerald understood what that meant. An inspection was not a punishment. An inspection was a discovery process. The Inspector General would look at his dungeon, find the violations he had not yet corrected, and note them in the audit record. The audit record would be visible to every other Core in the System.

His Compliance Rating would become public.

Gerald sat very still. He thought about what that meant for the Cores he had Cited. The ones who had appealed. The ones who had lost appeals. The ones who were currently frozen. They would see his rating. They would see that he was not compliant. They would understand that he was not above the rules he enforced. They would understand that he was a man with a 67 who had been shutting down people with 40s and 50s.

A new notification appeared. White this time. White meant informational.

*Inspector General arrival window: 48 hours. Inspection scope: full compliance audit. Inspector General will enter via primary entrance. Core is advised to ensure all spawn entities are in designated habitats and all signage is legible from a distance of 10 meters.*

Gerald looked at his east corridor. The signage was legible from eight meters.

He looked at his fire suppression requisition. Still pending.

He looked at the 48-hour window.

He did not have materials. He did not have time. He did not have a fire suppression system, and he could not build one from nothing, and the Inspector General was coming to look at him in forty-eight hours, and every Core in the System would know what he was.

He opened Form 9.3 again. He filled in line nine. He wrote the date. He signed it. He filed it.

Then he opened a new form. Form 14-C. Request for Temporary Compliance Extension. Reason: pending resource allocation.

The System accepted it.

Gerald stared at the acceptance notification. He had not lied. The resource allocation was pending. It was true.

He did not know if it would be enough.
