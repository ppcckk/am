Chapter 143: Appeal Filed

# Chapter 143

Brix counted forty-seven cracks in the tile while the Void Weaver pressed its face against the glass. It smelled like ozone and stale coffee, a combination that Brix had learned to associate with impending bureaucratic disaster.

He huddled in the corner of the containment cell, his fingers digging into the grout lines of the tiled floor.

The glass wall hummed, a low-frequency vibration that rattled his teeth. Outside the cell, the Void Weaver's presence pressed against the barrier like a physical weight, cold and suffocating.

Brix watched Gerald Hutchins. The Core's avatar stood by the control panel, his suit jacket immaculate, his tie knotted with geometric precision. Gerald's face was unreadable, a mask of municipal neutrality that Brix had initially mistaken for arrogance. Now he saw it for what it was: a dam holding back a flood of panic.

Gerald's hand hovered over the release button. His knuckles were white.

"Do you trust me?" Gerald had asked.

Brix had laughed. It was a nervous, jagged sound.

"No."

"Then you're the only one who can save us," Gerald said.

The button depressed with a soft click.

The lock disengaged.

The glass door slid open two inches, then jammed. The mechanism was old, poorly maintained, and currently fighting the magnetic pressure of the Void Weaver's aura outside.

Brix didn't wait for it to fully retract, and he scrambled through the gap, his boots skidding on the smooth concrete floor. He didn't look at Gerald. He looked at the ceiling vents.

The air recyclers were screaming. Not the polite hum of regulated airflow, but a shriek of strained turbines struggling against a sudden drop in internal pressure. The Void Weaver was sucking the oxygen out of the room to fuel its citation threat.

Gerald didn't move. He stood rigid, eyes fixed on a holographic interface that only he could see. His lips moved silently, forming words Brix couldn't hear. The System's interface flared red, then orange, then a sickly yellow.

Brix grabbed Gerald's arm. The touch burned.

Gerald's skin was hot, feverish.

The strain of maintaining his structural integrity against an external pressure differential was taking its toll.

If Gerald's compliance rating dropped further, his physical form would destabilize. He would dissolve into raw mana, leaving Brix alone in a void with a monster that fed on structural weakness.

"Move," Brix hissed. "Ventilation is failing."

Gerald blinked, snapping out of his trance.

"I am currently reviewing the emergency egress protocols."

"We are not in a fire drill! We are being suffocated!"

Gerald straightened his tie.

"Code Section 4, Subsection C, Paragraph 12 mandates that all emergency exits remain unobstructed. I am checking for obstructions."

Brix stared at him. The absurdity was so total it short-circuited his fear. He looked at the north wall.

The Void Weaver wasn't visible, but its influence was. The concrete was cracking, fissures spreading like spiderwebs.

The air was thinning.

Brix's lungs burned, and he ran to the nearest vent cover.

It was screwed shut. Standard municipal security.

Brix pulled a pry bar from his belt, a tool he'd stolen from a hardware store three weeks ago.

He wedged it under the screw heads.

"Gerald! The screws are stripped!"

Gerald didn't look away from his interface.

"Stripped screws constitute a Class 3 Maintenance Violation. I will note it for the audit."

"Note it later! Pry them out!"

Brix strained against the metal. The screwhead popped off, spinning across the floor.

He grabbed the next one.

His shoulders screamed, and the air was getting hard to breathe. His vision blurred at the edges.

Gerald finally looked up. He saw Brix struggling. He saw the cracked wall. He saw the yellow warning light pulsing on his HUD.

He walked to the vent.

"Step aside," Gerald said.

Gerald placed his hand on the vent cover. He didn't pry. He didn't force.

He tapped the metal with his index finger, once, twice. Three distinct taps.

The System registered the action.

*Action: Manual Override of Ventilation Damper.* *Requirement: Level 4 Maintenance Clearance.* *Current Clearance: Level 2.* *Error: Insufficient Authority.*

Gerald frowned.

"Insufficient authority." He pulled a clipboard from nowhere. A physical clipboard, with paper on it. He scribbled something on the top sheet.

*Temporary Delegation of Authority: Core to Subordinate Staff.* *Reason: Emergency Life Safety.* *Signature: G. Hutchins.*

The System paused. The holographic red light flickered.

*Delegation Accepted. Temporary.*

Gerald tapped the vent again. This time, the damper mechanism clicked. The louvers opened.

Air rushed in. Not fresh air, but recycled, stale air from the rest of the dungeon. But it was air. Brix gasped, filling his lungs.

The Void Weaver's pressure didn't stop. The cracks in the wall widened. A chunk of concrete fell from the ceiling, shattering on the floor.

Gerald looked at the debris. He didn't flinch. He looked at his HUD.

*Violation Detected: Structural Instability.* *Compliance Penalty: -15% to all Physical Structures.* *Current Compliance: 32/100.*

Gerald's face went pale. His avatar flickered. For a second, he looked translucent, like a bad hologram.

Brix grabbed him again.

"You're destabilizing."

"I am adjusting," Gerald said. His voice was calm, but it lacked resonance. It sounded thin.

"Adjusting what?"

"The load-bearing distribution."

Gerald closed his eyes, and he raised both hands. He wasn't casting a spell. He was doing something else.

He was mentally visualizing the dungeon's blueprints.

Every wall, every support beam, every pipe. He was searching for the weak points.

The Void Weaver pushed harder. The north wall buckled inward.

*Violation Detected: Critical Structural Failure.* *Compliance Penalty: -40% to all Physical Structures.* *Warning: Core Dissolution Imminent.*

Gerald opened his eyes. He looked at Brix.

"Find the load-bearing column," Gerald said.

"What?"

"The column that supports the north wall. It's behind the server rack. It's not in the original blueprints, but it's there. I can feel the stress."

Brix looked at the server rack. It was a mess of cables and blinking lights. He shoved the rack aside. Behind it, a single steel pillar stood, groaning under the weight of the ceiling.

The pillar was bending.

Brix ran to it. He wrapped his arms around the steel, trying to brace it. It was useless. He was one man against thousands of tons of concrete and magic.

Gerald walked over. He placed his hand on the pillar. He didn't brace it. He inspected it.

"Non-standard alloy," Gerald muttered. "Unlicensed reinforcement."

"Do something!" Brix yelled.

"I am."

Gerald closed his eyes again. His face contorted in concentration.

*Action: Retroactive Permit Application.* *Target: Unlicensed Steel Column.* *Status: Pending.*

The System didn't like it. The red lights flashed faster.

*Violation Detected: Unauthorized Construction.* *Compliance Penalty: -20% to Core Stability.*

Gerald's hand smoked. The heat was intense. Brix stepped back, shielding his face.

"If I permit it, it becomes legal," Gerald said through gritted teeth. "If it is legal, it can bear the load."

"It's not going to hold!" Brix shouted.

"It will hold for ninety seconds," Gerald said. "That is the statute of limitations for retroactive permits in the Ashford Municipal Code."

The Void Weaver laughed. The sound was like grinding metal.

*You play games with paper, Gerald. I play games with physics.*

The wall exploded.

A shard of concrete, the size of a dinner plate, flew across the room.

Brix dove, rolling into a ball. Gerald stood still. The shard hit his avatar.

It passed through him, dispersing mana. Gerald flickered out of existence for a full second.

When he reappeared, he was smaller. His suit was torn. A patch of gray static covered his left eye.

"Time," Gerald said.

Brix looked at the pillar, and the steel was glowing red.

It was holding. Barely.

The wall was bowed, but it hadn't collapsed.

"Thirty seconds," Gerald said.

Brix looked at the vent. The air was still flowing, but the pressure was dropping. The Void Weaver was adapting. It was finding a new way to attack.

The floor vibrated. Not from the wall, but from below.

Brix looked down. The concrete tiles were cracking.

"Gerald," Brix said, his voice low. "It's not the wall."

Gerald looked down. He saw the cracks spreading from the foundation.

"It's attacking the foundation," Gerald said. "He's citing us for improper drainage."

*Violation Detected: Inadequate Subgrade Support.* *Compliance Penalty: -50% to Core Integrity.*

Gerald's avatar unravelled at the edges. He looked like a wireframe model.

"I cannot permit a new foundation in ten seconds," Gerald said.

"Then we die," Brix said.

"No," Gerald said. He looked at Brix. "We appeal."

Brix blinked. "An appeal? We don't have time for a hearing."

"We don't need a hearing. We need a stay of execution."

Gerald reached into his suit pocket. He pulled out a red stamp. It was old, wooden, with a handle worn smooth by decades of use.

*Form 11-B: Temporary Injunction Against System Enforcement.* *Reason: Imminent Threat to Public Safety.* *Signature: G. Hutchins.*

Gerald slammed the stamp onto the air itself. The red ink hung in the space, glowing.

The System paused.

*Injunction Received.* *Processing...* *Reviewing Threat Assessment.*

The Void Weaver's pressure halted. The shaking stopped. The air became still.

For five seconds, there was silence.

Then, the System responded.

*Injunction Denied.* *Reason: Threat is not external to the Core. Threat is internal due to non-compliant infrastructure.* *Enforcement continues.*

Gerald's face went blank.

"It doesn't work," he whispered.

The floor beneath Brix's feet gave way. He fell into darkness.

He didn't scream. He fell through layers of stone, through pipes, through darkness. He landed on something soft.

Mold, and he was in the old sub-basement. The original library basement, the one Gerald had inspected thirty-one years ago.

Brix stood up. He was in a small, damp room. The air was thick with spores.

Gerald wasn't there.

A voice echoed from the walls.

"Mr. Hutchins is currently unavailable."

Brix spun around. Standing in the corner was a figure. It wasn't the Void Weaver. It wasn't Gerald.

It was a woman. She wore a sharp suit, identical to Gerald's, but made of black fabric that seemed to absorb the light. Her face was stern, her glasses thick.

"I am the Inspector General," she said.

Brix's blood ran cold.

"Where is Gerald?"

"Mr. Hutchins is in custody," the Inspector General said. "He has violated the most fundamental rule of the System."

Brix swallowed.

"Which one?"

"He issued a Citation to himself."

The Inspector General stepped forward. The mold receded from her feet.

"He cited his own dungeon for having a Supervisor who was not licensed to be in a containment zone. He created a paradox. And paradoxes are not resolved with paperwork."

She held out a hand.

"Give me the key to the cage, Brix. If you don't, I will delete this entire dungeon. And you with it."

Brix looked at her hand. He looked at the dark corners of the room. He looked at the spot where Gerald had stood.

"I don't have the key," Brix said.

The Inspector General smiled. It was not a nice smile.

"Then you are an accessory to a Class 1 Structural Felony."

She raised her hand. The air around Brix turned to stone.

Brix backed up. He hit the wall. He was trapped.

The Inspector General snapped her fingers.

A blue light flashed in Brix's mind.

*Incoming Message.* *From: G. Hutchins.* *Subject: Code Violation 7.* *Body: The exit is the door.

The door is the exit.

Do not open the door. Open the wall.*

Brix stared at the message.

"Open the wall?" he muttered.

The stone crept up his legs. His chest.

He looked at the wall behind him. It was solid brick.

He pulled out his pry bar, and he swung it at the brick.

It bounced off.

The Inspector General laughed.

"Masonry is not drywall, boy."

Brix swung again. And again.

*Clang. Clang. Clang.*

The stone reached his neck, and he looked at the message. *Open the wall.*

He looked at the bricks. They were old. 1950s construction. Mortar joints were wide.

He didn't swing at the brick. He swung at the mortar.

*Clang.*

A piece of mortar flew off.

*Clang.*

Another piece.

The Inspector General stopped laughing.

Brix swung faster. His arms burned. The stone was on his chin.

*Clang.*

A gap appeared. Behind the brick, there was space. Not room. Space.

Brix reached into the gap. His hand passed through the solid matter and grabbed something cold.

He pulled.

The wall tore open.

Brix fell through, and he landed in a place that was not a place.

It was a white void. No floor.

No ceiling.

Just infinite white.

Gerald was there, and he was sitting on a white chair. He was drinking coffee.

He looked at Brix.

"You took your time," Gerald said.

Brix looked around.

"What is this?"

"This is the Appeals Court," Gerald said. "I filed the appeal while you were falling."

Brix looked at Gerald. Gerald's suit was perfect. His tie was straight.

"How?" Brix asked.

"I cited the Void Weaver," Gerald said.

Brix blinked. "You can't cite a rival Core."

"I didn't cite him for being a Core," Gerald said. "I cited him for operating a dungeon without a valid Business License in the jurisdiction of Ashford."

Gerald took a sip of coffee.

"He didn't know the zoning laws."

The white void rippled. The Inspector General appeared, her face twisted in rage.

"That is a technicality!" she shouted.

"It is the law," Gerald said.

The void cracked. Black lines spread across the white.

Gerald stood up. He looked at Brix.

"Run," Gerald said.

"Where?"

"Up."

The white void shattered.

Brix found himself back in the sub-basement. The north wall was intact. The Void Weaver was gone. The air was clean.

Gerald was standing by the control panel. He was whole. His suit was torn, but he was standing.

Brix stared at him.

"You tricked the IG," Brix said.

"I enforced the code," Gerald said. He looked at his HUD.

*Compliance Rating: 88/100.* *Status: Stable.*

Gerald looked at the cracked floor.

"We need to fix that foundation," Gerald said. "And we need to get a Business License for the Void Weaver."

Brix laughed. It was a hysterical, broken sound.

"You want to license the monster that tried to kill us?"

Gerald nodded.

"It's a violation to leave an entity unregistered. It's a liability."

He pulled out a form.

*Form 11-C: Application for Temporary Dungeon Registration.* *Applicant: Void Weaver.* *Proposed Name: Ashford Municipal Hazard Containment Unit 4.*

He handed the form to Brix.

"Go deliver this to him."

Brix took the form. His hands shook.

"Gerald," Brix said. "He will kill me."

"He will cite me," Gerald said. "And if he cites me, I will appeal. And if I appeal, you stay."

Brix looked at the form. He looked at the empty corner where the Void Weaver had been.

He walked toward the north wall, and the air was cold.

He tapped on the concrete.

"Mr. Weaver?" Brix called out.

Silence.

"Mr. Weaver?"

A crack appeared in the wall. A single, perfect circle.

A message appeared on the wall, etched in black fire.

*Your Citation is Unlawful.*

Brix looked at Gerald.

Gerald shrugged.

"Serve it," Gerald said.

Brix pressed the paper against the burning wall.

The fire died.

The paper burned.

But it didn't disappear.

It changed.

The new text appeared in black ink.

*Appeal Filed.* *Hearing Scheduled: Immediate.* *Location: The Space Between.*

The floor beneath them dropped away.

Gerald looked at Brix.

"Grab my hand," Gerald said.

Brix grabbed it, and they fell.
