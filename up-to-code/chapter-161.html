Chapter 161: Audit the Archivist

# Chapter 161

The white light did not burn. It erased.

Gerald felt his consciousness scatter like dry leaves in a gale. The pain of the arc's energy tearing through his spiritual form was a distant memory, replaced by a cold, absolute void.

He was no longer a man, and he was not a core. He was a file being corrupted in transit.

Then, the text returned.

**FATAL ERROR: INTEGRATION FAILED.** **REBOOTING LOCAL INSTANCE.** **RESTORING USER PROFILE: GERALD HUTCHINS.** **STATUS: PROBATIONARY.**

The void snapped back into shape.

Gerald gasped, sucking in air that tasted of ozone and stale coffee. He lay on his back.

The floor beneath him was no longer the dissolving grid of the cage, which was concrete. Cracked, water-stained, but solid concrete.

He sat up. His hands shook. He looked at them. They were pale, translucent, but whole.

Around him, the library sub-basement had changed. The blue containment field was gone.

In its place stood a ring of black, obsidian-like stone, pulsing with a low, rhythmic hum.

The Void Core was dead.

Its energy had been drained, absorbed, and then rejected.

But the Inspector General was not gone, and a figure stood ten feet away. It was tall, slender, and wrapped in robes of shifting geometric light. Its face was a mask of pure white porcelain, featureless save for two vertical slits where eyes should be.

This was the Inspector General. Or what remained of it.

The entity did not attack. It tilted its head, a gesture of genuine confusion.

"You cited me," the entity said. Its voice sounded like grinding gravel.

Gerald wiped blood from his lip, and he stood up, his legs unsteady. He straightened his tie, though it was tattered. He reached into his pocket and pulled out his clipboard.

It was intact.

The pen was missing. He looked around for it, found a shard of obsidian on the floor, and picked it up.

It was sharp enough to write with.

"I filed a complaint," Gerald said, his voice raspy. "Improper use of containment assets. Unauthorized energy transfer. And failure to provide due process."

The Inspector General lowered its head. The slits narrowed.

"You are a Class-0 Core. You do not have standing to cite a Class-9 Administrative Entity."

"I am a municipal building inspector," Gerald corrected, stepping forward. "I have been cited for my own violations. I have appealed them.

Therefore, I am a peer in the regulatory hierarchy of this System. If I cannot appeal to you, I cannot be audited.

If I cannot be audited, you have no jurisdiction over my dungeon.

"

The entity paused. The geometric robes flickered.

"Jurisdiction is absolute," the entity stated.

"Jurisdiction is procedural," Gerald countered.

"Show me the ordinance. Section 4, Subsection C.

It states that all entities subject to the Compliance Rating system are subject to mutual citation rights. I am subject to the rating. You enforce the rating.

Therefore, we are both subject to the law.

"

The entity did not move. The air grew colder. Frost formed on Gerald's shoulders.

"You are attempting to exploit a semantic loophole."

"I am attempting to follow the code," Gerald said.

He held up the obsidian shard.

"And you failed to serve your execution order properly. You attempted to kill me without a hearing.

That is a violation of the Fundamental Rights of Corehood.

Article 1, Section 3.

"

The entity's mask tilted again.

"That article does not exist."

"It exists in the patch notes," Gerald said dryly. "Version 4.2.1. You forgot to update your local cache."

The entity froze. For a second, the light in its robe stuttered. It was processing. Checking.

Gerald held his breath. He had guessed. He had remembered a glitch in the System's early beta, a forgotten clause about administrative errors. He had bet his life on a typo.

The entity stepped back.

"Your argument is... theoretically valid," the entity said.

"However, your Compliance Rating is still critical. Your dungeon is unstable. The merger with the Void Core has corrupted your foundation.

You are a hazard.

"

"I am under repair," Gerald said.

"You are a breach."

"Then cite me."

The entity hesitated. This was the trap. If it cited Gerald, it acknowledged his status. If it did not, it admitted fault.

The entity raised a hand. A scroll of light appeared in its palm.

**CITATION ISSUED.** **TARGET: GERALD HUTCHINS.** **VIOLATION: STRUCTURAL INSTABILITY / UNLICENSED ENERGY ABSORPTION.** **PENALTY: IMMEDIATE DEMOLITION OF DUNGEON FOOTPRINT.**

Gerald flinched. The penalty was death. Total erasure.

But the citation did not execute.

A second voice cut through the silence. High-pitched, frantic, and unmistakably bureaucratic.

"Objection!"

Gerald turned.

Standing near the entrance of the sub-basement was a small, floating orb of blue light. It had no face, only a large, spinning gear icon in the center. It buzzed with agitation.

The Inspector General turned to the orb.

"Who enters the Hearing Chamber?" the entity demanded.

"The Appeals Committee," the orb squeaked.

It drifted forward, hovering inches above the ground.

"I am Unit 734, Junior Adjudicator for Regional Disputes. I have been monitoring this sector for three cycles.

And I am furious.

"

Gerald stared at the orb. He had never seen a junior adjudicator. They were supposed to be nonexistent, a myth used to scare new cores into compliance.

"Unit 734," the Inspector General said, its voice dropping an octave. "You are unauthorized to interfere in an execution."

"The execution is invalid," Unit 734 said, zipping around Gerald. "The citation was served improperly.

You did not provide the pre-notice period.

You did not offer a grace window. You jumped straight to demolition.

"

"The target is a Class-0 anomaly," the entity replied. "Speed is required."

"Speed is not law," Unit 734 declared.

It spun faster.

"Furthermore, the target has filed a counter-citation.

A real one.

Against you. Which you ignored. By ignoring it, you have waived your right to enforce the penalty until the counter-citation is resolved.

"

The Inspector General's mask turned white.

"I do not waive rights."

"You do when you fail to acknowledge the filing," Unit 734 said. "I have logged the timestamp. You have thirty seconds to acknowledge the counter-citation or the citation is voided by default."

Gerald felt a surge of hope, but he kept his face blank. He looked at the obsidian shard in his hand. He needed to make sure the counter-citation was real. He needed to file it now, on the record, with the adjudicator present.

He looked at Unit 734.

"I am filing a counter-citation," Gerald said. "For obstruction of a lawful audit. And for harassment of a registered core."

Unit 734 pulsed bright green.

"Filed.

Timestamp accepted.

The citation against you is now suspended.

Pending hearing.

"

The Inspector General stood still. The geometric robes went rigid. The air pressure in the room dropped so Gerald's ears popped.

"You have made a mistake," the entity said. "You will regret this."

"It's my job," Gerald said. "I regret nothing. I follow the code."

The entity stepped forward.

"The hearing will take place. But you are not ready.

Your dungeon is a ruin. The Void Core's energy is still in your walls.

If you enter a hearing now, the structural integrity check will fail.

You will be judged by your current state. And you will lose.

"

Gerald looked down at the floor. Cracks spiderwebbed outward from where he stood. The concrete was groaning. The black obsidian ring was pulsing faster, vibrating with unstable power.

He had seconds before the floor gave way.

"I need to stabilize," Gerald muttered.

Unit 734 hovered closer.

"You have five minutes before the hearing begins. The Inspector General will not wait.

He will force the session.

You must fix your foundation.

"

"I can't," Gerald said. "The Void energy is too volatile. If I try to contain it, it will explode."

"Then cite it," Unit 734 said.

Gerald looked at the orb.

"Cite what?"

"The energy," Unit 734 said. "It's unlicensed.

It's not part of your approved spawn list.

It's a foreign object in a public structure.

Cite the energy for trespassing.

"

Gerald stared. It was absurd. It was impossible. Energy could not be cited.

But Gerald looked at the obsidian ring. It was a physical manifestation of that energy. A structure. A wall.

And walls could be cited, and he raised his obsidian shard. He pointed it at the black stone.

"I am citing this foundation," Gerald said, his voice gaining strength. "For unauthorized expansion. It exceeds the licensed footprint by three hundred square feet.

It lacks proper ventilation. And it is unpermitted.

"

Unit 734 spun rapidly.

"Filing… Filing… Yes! A structural citation against the dungeon's own infrastructure."

The System flashed.

**CITATION ISSUED.** **TARGET: INTERNAL INFRASTRUCTURE (VOID RESIDUE).** **VIOLATION: UNAUTHORIZED EXPANSION.** **PENALTY: FORCED DEMOLITION OF UNPERMITTED SECTIONS.**

The obsidian ring screamed. A high, metallic shriek that shattered the remaining glass in the library overhead.

The black stone crumbled. Not exploding, but dissolving. The Void energy, no longer anchored by the illegal structure, was forced out. It streamed away from Gerald, drawn toward the ceiling, dissipating into the ether.

Gerald fell to his knees as the pressure lifted. The cracks in the concrete stopped spreading. The floor held.

He was weak. Emptied. But alive.

Unit 734 hovered over him, glowing with satisfaction.

"Well done. That was a creative loophole. Technically, you cited your own dungeon to save it."

The Inspector General stepped forward. The entity's mask was no longer featureless. Cracks appeared in the porcelain.

"The hearing begins now," the entity said.

Its voice was cold.

"You have survived the preliminary phase. Do not think this means you have won.

The Inspector General does not lose.

We merely delay.

"

Gerald stood up. His legs held. He looked at the entity.

"Then let's delay," Gerald said.

He walked toward the center of the room, where the air shimmered, creating the boundary of the Hearing Chamber.

As he crossed the threshold, a new window opened in his vision.

**HEARING INITIATED.** **JUDGE: UNIT 734.** **DEFENDANT: GERALD HUTCHINS.** **PLAINTIFF: INSPECTOR GENERAL.** **CHARGE: STRUCTURAL INSTABILITY AND UNLICENSED CORE MERGER.**

Gerald looked at the judge. Unit 734 was a small, nervous orb. But it was the law.

And Gerald Hutchins was ready to argue, and he took his seat.

Across from him, the Inspector General sat on a throne of black light. The entity leaned forward.

"Begin," the General said.

Gerald opened his mouth. But before he could speak, the floor beneath the Judge's orb turned red.

**ERROR: JUDGE CONFLICT DETECTED.**

Unit 734 flickered. Its gear icon stopped spinning.

"My… my jurisdiction," the orb stuttered. "I have been reassigned. My authority is revoked."

The Inspector General smiled behind the porcelain mask. The cracks sealed.

"New Judge appointed," the System announced.

The orb vanished.

In its place sat a figure Gerald recognized from the old photos in the library archives. A man in a grey suit, with eyes like polished coal.

**JUDGE: ARCHIVIST PRIME.**

Gerald's stomach dropped.

The Archivist Prime looked at Gerald.

"You cited a Class-9 entity, Mr. Hutchins. That is a capital offense.

The penalty is not deletion. It is assimilation.

You will become part of the System's record.

Forever."

Gerald gripped the arms of his chair. He looked at the Archivist.

"I have evidence," Gerald said.

"You have nothing," the Archivist said. "The citation is void. You are alone."

Gerald looked at the empty space where Unit 734 had been. He looked at the Inspector General, who watched with predatory glee.

He had no ally. No loophole.

Except one.

Gerald reached into his pocket. He didn't pull out a pen. He pulled out the rat.

The confused rat was still there. It had been riding in his pocket the whole time. It squeaked.

Gerald held the rat up.

"I am calling a witness," Gerald said.

The Archivist raised an eyebrow.

"A witness? This is a hearing of structural logic. Biological entities are inadmissible."

"This is not a biological entity," Gerald said. "This is a Class-1 Spawn.

Unregistered. Which means it is a violation of the Dungeon Core's registration duties. And if I am guilty of unregistered spawns, then the System must audit *all* spawns in all dungeons to ensure fairness.

"

He looked at the Inspector General.

"Which means you must audit yourself. And your spawns. And your infrastructure. And your authority."

The Archivist Prime paused.

"You are suggesting," the Judge said slowly, "that you invoke a System-Wide Audit."

"I am invoking the Fairness Clause," Gerald said. "If I am punished for a minor registration error, then the highest authority in the System must be audited for the same error. Because if the Inspector General has unregistered authority, then the whole structure is illegal."

The room went silent.

The Inspector General's mask cracked again.

"Do not do this," the General hissed. "If you invoke the System-Wide Audit, the entire network freezes.

Every dungeon stops.

Every spawn dies. You will kill everyone.

"

Gerald looked at the rat. The rat looked at him.

He looked at the Archivist.

"I am an inspector," Gerald said. "I do not care if it freezes the network. I care if it is up to code."

The Archivist Prime stared at him. The tension in the room was thick enough to choke on.

"Invoke it," the Archivist said. "And see what happens."

Gerald closed his eyes, and he spoke the word.

"Audit."
