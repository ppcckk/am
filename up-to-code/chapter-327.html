Chapter 327: Let It Breathe

# Chapter 327

The Magistrate's fist connected with the air where Gerald's head had been a fraction of a second prior.

Gerald didn't dodge. He sidestepped. It was a clumsy, shuffling motion, the kind a man makes when he's trying to avoid spilling coffee on a client's lap, but it worked. The black coat snapped back from the force of the punch, the silver badges flickering like dying lightbulbs.

The System window flashed red.

**Violation: Physical Assault on a Registered Inspector.** **Status: Pending Review.**

Gerald held up his empty ink pad. He didn't say a word. He just pointed.

The Magistrate froze. The figure's head tilted, the void behind the eyes swirling with confused code.

It looked at the ink pad.

It looked at Gerald. Then it looked at the rogue, who was currently holding a dagger to his own throat in a dramatic display of emotional distress.

"Is he armed?" Gerald asked. His voice was flat. Administrative.

The rogue blinked.

"What?"

"Your dagger. Is it a concealed weapon?"

The rogue looked down at the blade.

"I mean, technically, it's a tool. For opening locks. And throats."

"Does it have a safety latch?"

"It has a point."

"Then it is unsecured hazardous material," Gerald said.

He scribbled on a phantom clipboard.

"Citation 327-B.

Failure to secure lethal implements in a public gathering space.

Penalty: Immediate disarmament.

"

The System hummed. A low, grinding sound, like a garbage disposal chewing on bone. The rogue's dagger vanished from his hand. It reappeared in a containment box that materialized out of thin air and dropped onto the floor with a heavy *clang*.

The rogue stared at his empty hands.

"Hey!"

"Appeal later," Gerald said. "Or don't. I'm busy."

The Magistrate roared. It wasn't a human sound. It was the sound of a filing cabinet slamming shut in a room full of screaming people. The figure lunged again, this time with both hands, fingers elongated into shadowy claws.

Gerald didn't move. He stood his ground.

He had to.

If he moved, he admitted fault.

If he admitted fault, the Authority shifted.

Authority was a load-bearing wall. If you removed the support, the whole building collapsed.

"Cite him for obstruction," Gerald shouted to the mage.

The mage, eyes wide, pointed his staff.

"On what grounds?"

"Blocking an egress path!"

The mage blinked. Then he grinned. He traced a sigil in the air.

Not a spell. A form.

A form for *Illegal Occupancy of a Fire Escape*.

The air around the Magistrate turned into a lattice of glowing blue lines. The figure slammed into them and bounced back. The System had categorized the space the Magistrate was standing in as a non-conforming exit route. The figure could not move through it without filing a variance request.

The timer hit ten seconds.

Gerald felt the sweat trickle down his back. He didn't wipe it. He couldn't. To wipe sweat was to acknowledge discomfort, and discomfort was a subjective experience, and the System did not respect subjectivity.

Five seconds.

The Magistrate vibrated. The black coat frayed at the edges, unraveling into lines of binary code. The silver badges went dark.

"It's trying to crash," the mage whispered. "It can't decide if he's an enemy or a building."

"He's both," Gerald said. "That's the problem."

Three seconds.

Gerald closed his eyes. He listened to the hum of the System. He listened to the static of the glitch. And in that static, he heard the frequency of a permit denial.

He opened his eyes. He reached into his pocket. He didn't pull out a weapon. He pulled out a stamp.

It was a simple rubber stamp. *DENIED*. It had belonged to the City of Ashford's Planning Commission, circa 1998. Gerald had kept it.

It was a relic.

A talisman, and he slammed it onto the floor.

*Thump.*

The sound echoed. It wasn't loud, but it resonated with the floorboards, with the foundations, with the concept of bureaucracy.

**System Alert: Permit Denied.** **Reason: Zoning Conflict.**

The Magistrate stopped.

The clawing hands retracted. The figure straightened, its posture rigid, perfect, and still. It looked at Gerald.

It looked at the stamp.

Then, it dissolved.

It didn't die. It didn't explode. It simply ceased to be present in the room. One moment, a terrifying entity of shadow and authority; the next, a patch of empty air.

The timer hit zero.

Silence.

The rogue slumped against the wall, breathing hard. The mage lowered his staff, the light fading.

"Did we win?" the rogue asked.

Gerald picked up the stamp. He brushed dust off the rubber face.

"We didn't win," Gerald said. "We filed a motion. The case is closed, but the judge is still in the building."

The mage frowned.

"What does that mean?"

"It means the Inspector General knows we're here."

A new window popped up. It wasn't red. It was a sterile, clinical white.

**Notification: Audit Schedule Confirmed.** **Auditor: Unit 734 (The Auditor).** **Estimated Arrival: 48 Hours.** **Penalty for Non-Compliance: Total Demolition.**

Gerald stared at the window.

Demolition.

Not death. Demolition.

They would level the dungeon. They would erase the library. They would erase him.

"We have to leave," the mage said. "Now. While the glitch is still fresh."

"No," Gerald said.

The rogue looked up.

"What?

Why?

You just cited a ghost into oblivion. You're a genius. Let's go.

"

"Gerald, listen to me," the mage said, stepping closer.

"That was a high-level Auditor. You can't fight that with paperwork.

That thing eats cores for breakfast.

We need to run. We need to find another dungeon to hide in.

"

"I don't run," Gerald said. He walked to the center of the room. He looked at the cracks in the floor. The cracks that had appeared when the Magistrate dissolved.

"Where do you go when a building is condemned?" Gerald asked.

The mage hesitated.

"You... you leave. You wait for it to be torn down."

"Wrong," Gerald said. "You appeal.

You argue that the condemnation was hasty.

That the inspection was flawed.

That the structure is, in fact, sound.

"

"You're crazy," the rogue said.

"I'm an inspector," Gerald corrected. "And Unit 734 is coming to inspect my work. If I run, I admit I have nothing to show. If I stay, I can show it everything."

He looked at the mage.

"Can you create a hard light barrier? Something that looks like a load-bearing wall?"

"Yes. But why?"

"Because I need to hide the evidence," Gerald said.

The mage stared.

"What evidence?"

"The ventilation system," Gerald said. He pointed to the ceiling. "See that ductwork?

It's rusted. It's non-compliant.

It's going to be cited.

I need to replace it.

"

"You're fixing a duct while the apocalypse walks through the door?"

"I'm fixing a violation," Gerald said. "And while I fix it, I'm going to install a new feature."

"A feature?"

"An emergency egress route."

The rogue groaned.

"We don't have time for construction!"

"We have forty-eight hours," Gerald said. "I've done renovations in less. I've done them on a budget of zero. I've done them while being yelled at by aldermen."

He walked to the wall, and he placed his hand on the stone. He closed his eyes.

He didn't use mana.

He didn't use magic. He used *intent*. He used the knowledge of where a wall should be, how it should feel, how it should bear weight.

The stone shifted. It groaned. It cracked.

The System window flashed.

**Modification Detected.** **Category: Structural Alteration.** **Permit: Required.** **Warning: Unauthorized Construction.**

Gerald ignored the warning. He focused.

He visualized the layout.

A corridor.

Straight. Wide.

Well-lit. With handrails at the correct height.

The stone moved. It formed a arch. A perfect, semicircular arch.

The rogue walked over. He touched the stone.

"It's... solid."

"It's compliant," Gerald said.

He turned to the mage.

"I need you to cast a spell. A specific one."

"Which one?"

"Light. Permanent, cold light.

No flicker.

No warmth. Just light.

"

"I can do that. But it drains mana quickly."

"I don't care about your mana.

I care about the lumens. I need five hundred foot-candles at floor level.

Uniform distribution.

No shadows."

The mage looked at the white notification window. *48 Hours.*

"If I do this," the mage said, "the Auditor will see us. It will see the work."

"Exactly," Gerald said. "I want it to see the work.

I want it to see that I fixed the ventilation.

That I fixed the egress.

That I fixed the lighting.

"

"And if it still decides to demolish us?"

"Then I cite it for arbitrary enforcement," Gerald said. "And I demand a hearing."

The rogue laughed. It was a nervous, high-pitched sound.

"You're going to sue the apocalypse."

"I'm going to audit it," Gerald said.

He turned back to the wall. He worked.

The next six hours were a blur of dust and sweat and frustration. Gerald didn't sleep. He didn't eat.

He measured. He cut.

He fit, and he used the rogue's lockpicking tools to pry out rusted pipes. He used the mage's light spells to illuminate dark corners. He used the System's own physics to snap stones into place.

Every time the System tried to stop him, he cited it.

*Violation: Unnecessary Resistance to Maintenance.*

The System paused. Confused. It allowed him to proceed.

*Violation: Lack of Adequate Lighting During Work Hours.*

The System brightened the room.

Gerald worked. He replaced the rusted duct with smooth, sealed metal. He installed handrails on both sides of the new corridor. He painted the walls a neutral, non-distracting beige.

By the time he finished, he was exhausted. His back ached. His hands were blistered.

But the room looked different.

It looked... clean.

It looked like a place where things were done right.

The white notification window pulsed.

**47 Hours.**

Gerald sat on the floor, and he watched the new corridor. It was perfect. It was boring.

It was safe.

The mage walked over.

"It's done."

"It's not done," Gerald said. "I haven't filed the completion report."

He pulled out a piece of paper. Not System-generated. Real paper. He had found it in the library ruins.

He wrote: *Project: Egress Improvement and Ventilation Upgrade. Contractor: Gerald Hutchins. Date: Today.*

He signed it.

Then he pinned it to the wall.

**System Alert: Document Detected.** **Class: Official Record.** **Action: Archive.**

The paper vanished. Absorbed into the system.

Gerald smiled. It was a small, tired smile.

"Now," he said. "We wait."

The rogue paced.

"What if it doesn't come? What if it just blows us up from orbit?"

"It won't," Gerald said.

"Bureaucracy hates inefficiency.

Blowing us up is inefficient. Auditing us is efficient.

It's in the code.

"

The timer ticked down.

Thirty hours.

Twenty hours.

Ten hours.

The silence in the dungeon was heavy. The air, for the first time in months, felt clean.

No mold.

No damp. Just dry, still air.

The mage sat by the new ductwork, checking the readings.

"Ventilation is up to code," he said. "Humidity is optimal."

"Good," Gerald said. He was sitting by the door. He had his stamp. He had his pen.

The rogue was hiding behind the handrail.

"I hate this," he whispered. "I hate waiting."

"You're not waiting," Gerald said. "You're pre-inspecting."

The rogue glared at him.

**System Alert: Arrival Imminent.** **Auditor Unit 734 has entered the premises.** **Location: Main Entrance.**

Gerald stood up. He straightened his tie. It was frayed, but it was straight.

The door didn't open. It dissolved.

A figure stood in the doorway, which was tall. Thin.

Made of white porcelain and black ink.

It had no face. Just a smooth, white oval where a head should be.

It held a clipboard.

The figure stepped forward. It didn't walk. It glided. The floor didn't creak under its weight.

It looked at Gerald.

It looked at the new corridor.

It looked at the ventilation ducts.

It looked at the pinned paper.

The figure raised a hand. It pointed at the handrail.

"Is this compliant?" the Auditor asked. Its voice was not a sound. It was a vibration in the bones.

Gerald didn't hesitate. He didn't stutter. He didn't feel ill.

"It is," Gerald said. "Section 4, Paragraph 2. Handrails must extend continuously along the full length of the run, with no interruption at the top or bottom return."

The Auditor tilted its head. The porcelain surface rippled.

"And the height?"

"Thirty-four inches," Gerald said. "Measured from the leading edge of the tread."

The Auditor stepped closer. It reached out a long, white finger. It touched the handrail.

It didn't pull. It didn't push. It just measured.

The System window appeared.

**Inspection: In Progress.** **Item: Handrail.** **Status: Pending.**

The Auditor turned to the ductwork.

"Ventilation?"

"Eighty cubic feet per minute," Gerald said. "Per occupant. Exceeding minimum requirement of fifteen."

The Auditor glided to the duct. It ran its finger along the seam.

"Seal integrity?"

"Perfect," Gerald said. "No leaks. No drafts."

The Auditor paused. The white oval where its face should be seemed to narrow.

"This is... unusual."

"Compliance is unusual," Gerald said. "In this world."

The Auditor turned back to Gerald.

"You have filed a complaint against the previous Auditor."

"Yes."

"Who was that?"

"An error," Gerald said. "A glitch in the system. I corrected it."

The Auditor stared at him.

The timer in Gerald's head stopped.

The Auditor raised its clipboard. It wrote something.

Gerald watched. He couldn't see what it wrote.

The Auditor tore off a page. It handed it to Gerald.

Gerald took it, and he looked down.

It wasn't a fine.

It wasn't a citation.

It was a **Promotion**.

**Rank: Senior Auditor.** **Authority: Local Jurisdiction.** **Permissions: Override, Cite, Demolish.**

Gerald's blood ran cold, and he looked up. The Auditor was already leaving. It dissolved into the doorway, leaving no trace.

The rogue stood up.

"Did you get fired?"

Gerald looked at the paper. His hands were shaking. Not from fear. From realization.

"No," Gerald said. "They gave me their job."

The mage looked at the paper.

"What does that mean?"

Gerald looked at the new corridor. He looked at the dungeon that was now, legally, his territory.

He looked at the window.

**System Update: New Permissions Granted.** **Ability: Cite Anything.**

Gerald smiled. It was a cold, sharp smile.

"It means," Gerald said, "that the rules just changed. And I'm the one who writes them."

The floor beneath them shuddered.

**System Alert: Threat Detected.** **Entity: The Dragon.** **Distance: 2 Miles.** **Status: Enraged.**

Gerald looked at the window, and he looked at the paper.

He picked up his stamp.

"Let's see," Gerald said, "if it has a permit to breathe."

The dragon breached the wall.
