Chapter 144: Under Hearing

# Chapter 144

The drop lasted exactly four seconds. Gerald counted them.

One. The concrete of Ashford's sub-basement vanished into gray mist.

Two. The air pressure dropped enough to pop his eardrums, a sensation he cataloged as 'barometric irregularity' than fear.

Three. Brix's grip on his hand was white-knuckled, trembling, and warm.

Four. They landed.

Not on stone. Not on dirt. On paper.

The floor beneath Gerald's boots was a vast, unbroken sheet of parchment, stretching infinitely in every direction. The sky above was not sky, but a ceiling of stacked filing cabinets, thousands of feet high, humming with the low-frequency vibration of infinite bureaucracy.

Gerald straightened his tie. He checked his watch. It was broken. He checked his internal HUD.

*Location: The Space Between (Administrative Zone).* *Compliance Rating: 88/100.* *Current Status: Under Hearing.*

Brix scrambled up, brushing off dust that didn't exist. He looked around, eyes wide, mouth agape.

"Gerald," Brix whispered. "Where are we?"

"An appeals court," Gerald said. His voice echoed strangely, flattened by the paper floor. "Or a hearing room. The distinction is academic if the judge is fair."

He looked up. A massive figure sat behind a desk made of compressed ledgers. The figure had no face, only a series of shifting stamps and seals where features should be. It held a gavel made of solidified red tape.

*Case No. 904-B:* *Void Weaver vs. Ashford Municipal Hazard Containment Unit 4.*

*Presiding Official: The Auditor.*

*Charge: Citation for Unlicensed Entity Operation and Failure to Maintain Egress Standards.*

Gerald stepped forward. He did not bow. He did not tremble. He reached into his pocket and pulled out a clipboard.

"I request the record be entered into evidence," Gerald said.

The Auditor's head—a floating cluster of wax seals—tilted. A voice boomed, sounding like paper tearing.

*The defendant is present. The appellant is present. Proceed.*

The Void Weaver materialized from the shadows of a filing cabinet. He was taller than before, his form shifting between humanoid and geometric abstraction. Black fire licked at his edges, but here, in this sterile white void, the fire looked like ink stains.

*Your Citation is invalid,* the Weaver projected. His voice was cold, sharp, cutting through the air. *You lack jurisdiction over the Void. The Void exists outside municipal boundaries. Your City of Ashford does not extend into the Ethereal Plane.*

Gerald didn't look at the Weaver. He looked at the Auditor.

"Objection," Gerald said. "Relevant only if the entity in question was operating within a licensed dungeon.

The Void Weaver entered Ashford Dungeon Core Sector 4, Sub-Basement Level.

He interacted with registered biological entities (see Form 11-B, filed three years ago). He caused structural damage to load-bearing walls (see Inspection Report 44-C).

"

Gerald pulled a sheaf of papers from his clipboard. He handed them to the Auditor.

"Furthermore," Gerald continued, pointing a finger at the Weaver's shifting form, "the defendant failed to adhere to Local Ordinance 7.2: *Identification and Tagging of Hazardous Entities.*"

The Weaver flared, black fire rising. *I am not a hazard. I am a force of nature.*

"Nature is unregulated," Gerald said dryly.

"You are not nature. You are a spawn.

You have a spawn rate.

You have a mana cost. Therefore, you are regulated. You have no ID badge.

You have no liability insurance. You are a walking OSHA violation.

"

The Auditor's stamps shifted. A new document appeared on the desk.

*Evidence Reviewed.* *Violation Confirmed: Failure to Tag.*

The Weaver laughed. It was a sound like cracking ice.

*You think a tag matters? I will unmake this court.*

The Weaver raised a hand. Black energy coalesced, forming a spear of void energy aimed directly at Gerald's chest.

Gerald didn't move. He held up a single sheet of paper.

"Stay of Execution," Gerald said. "Pending review of Procedural Error."

The spear hit the paper. It stopped.

The Weaver paused. *What?*

Gerald tapped the paper.

"You attempted to attack the presiding officer during an active hearing. This is Contempt of Court. Under System Rule 12.4, *Hostile Actions During Adjudication*, the attacking entity is stripped of all active abilities for the duration of the proceeding."

The Weaver's form flickered. The black fire died out. He looked down at his hands, confused.

*This is... arbitrary.*

"It is Code," Gerald said. "You violated the timeline. You interrupted the process. Now, sit down."

Brix stared at Gerald.

"You... you just stopped a Void Weaver with a piece of paper?"

"It wasn't paper," Gerald said, not looking at Brix. "It was a subpoena. And he was late to court."

The Weaver sat. He looked smaller now. Diminished.

*The appeal is over,* the Weaver said, his voice hollow. *I concede the jurisdiction.

But I will not pay the fine.

I cannot. I have no gold.

I have no mana reserves accessible via the banking system.*

Gerald nodded.

"Common issue.

Unregistered entities often lack fiscal integration.

However, the penalty for non-payment is not death. It is Repossession.

"

Gerald looked at the Auditor.

"Requesting immediate enforcement of Asset Seizure Protocol."

The Auditor's stamps aligned.

*Protocol Initiated.*

A hole opened in the paper floor beneath the Weaver. Chains of red light shot out, wrapping around his wrists and ankles.

*No!* the Weaver screamed. *You can't take me! I am eternal!*

"You are a dungeon spawn," Gerald corrected. "You are a line item. And you are delinquent."

The chains pulled. The Weaver dragged, screaming, into the hole. The hole closed.

Silence returned to the Space Between.

Brix slumped against the filing cabinet.

"He's gone."

"He's being audited," Gerald said. He made a note on his clipboard. *Void Weaver: Detained pending financial restitution. Estimated timeline: 30-60 business days.*

Gerald turned to Brix.

"We're done here. Let's go home."

"Home?" Brix laughed, a shaky, hysterical sound. "Gerald, you just bannedished a monster from existence. That's not a building inspection. That's war."

"It's compliance," Gerald said.

He tapped his clipboard.

"And we have more work to do.

My Compliance Rating dropped three points during the hearing.

I need to file the appeal results. Form 14-A: *Outcome of Administrative Hearing.*

"

He looked around the infinite white space.

"How do we get back?"

Gerald pulled out another form.

*Form 99-Z: Request for Teleportation via Administrative Exit.*

He held it up.

"Sign here," Gerald said, handing it to Brix.

Brix took the pen. His hand shook so hard the pen leaked ink onto the paper.

"Gerald," Brix said. "What if they don't let us leave?"

"They will," Gerald said. "I have the right to a swift resolution. It's in the Constitution of the System. Section 4, Paragraph 2."

Brix signed.

The white floor rippled. A door appeared. A normal, wooden office door.

Gerald walked through it, and they fell again. This time, only two seconds.

They landed on the cracked concrete of the sub-basement. The air was cold, but familiar. The smell of mold was still there, though less potent. Gerald noted this improvement.

*Compliance Rating: 88/100.* *Penalty Applied: -3 for Contemptuous Behavior by Opposing Party (credited to Plaintiff).*

Gerald looked at the empty corner where the Weaver had been.

"Clean up crew," Gerald said. "We need to repair the wall. And we need to install signage indicating 'Hazardous Entity Removed'."

Brix sat on the floor, head in hands.

"You're insane."

"I'm efficient," Gerald said. He pulled out a tape measure. He walked to the cracked foundation.

He measured.

*Crack Width: 1.5 centimeters.* *Depth: Unmeasurable.* *Material: Concrete, reinforced with unknown alchemical binding.*

"Structural Code 8.1," Gerald muttered. "Cracks exceeding one centimeter require immediate engineering review. We don't have an engineer. We have a rat."

He looked at the rat. The rat was hiding behind a stack of moldy books.

"Mr. Rat," Gerald said. "Do you have a degree in civil engineering?"

The rat squeaked. It was a negative squeak. Gerald understood it perfectly.

"Then we improvise," Gerald said. "Shoring. Temporary bracing."

He looked around. He saw a broken support beam from the library above, protruding from the ceiling. It was splintered, jagged, and dangerous.

*Violation: Exposed Structural Element.*

Gerald pulled out a wrench. He didn't have one.

He was a core, and he didn't have hands that could hold tools in the traditional sense.

But he had will. And he had Code.

He focused on the beam. He visualized the code. *Support. Stability. Load Distribution.*

He pushed.

The beam didn't move. It was stuck.

Gerald tried again. He poured mana into the beam, trying to reinforce it. The mana flowed, but it leaked out of the cracks.

*Error: Insufficient Mana for Structural Reinforcement.*

Gerald sighed. This was frustrating. He hated when the numbers didn't add up.

"Gerald," Brix said, standing up. He looked weak, pale. "You can't fix that with mana.

You need materials. You need concrete.

"

"We have concrete," Gerald said. He pointed to the floor.

"That's the floor. That's our dungeon."

"It's a resource," Gerald said. "If we grind it down, mix it with water from the leak, and re-pour it, we can patch the crack."

Brix stared at him.

"You want to destroy your own dungeon to fix your dungeon?"

"It's recycling," Gerald said. "Sustainable practice. Grants a +2 bonus to Environmental Compliance."

Brix shook his head.

"You're a monster."

"I'm an inspector," Gerald corrected. "And we have a problem."

He pointed to the wall where the Weaver had been.

The black fire mark was gone. But something else had appeared.

A symbol, which was etched into the concrete. Not burned. Carved.

It looked like a gear, overlaid with a skull.

Gerald walked over to it. He touched it. It was warm.

*Scanning...*

The System text appeared.

*Symbol Detected: The Iron Gear.* *Affiliation: Unregistered Consortium.* *Warning: Illegal Trade Network.*

Gerald frowned. He had never seen this symbol before.

"Who drew that?" Gerald asked.

Brix walked over, looking pale.

"The Weaver didn't do that. It was there before he left. It appeared when the floor dropped."

Gerald traced the symbol with his finger. It hummed.

*Interaction Required.* *Prompt: Join the Consortium?*

Gerald pulled out his pen. He drew a large, red **X** over the symbol.

*Rejection Recorded.* *Penalty: None.*

He looked at Brix.

"We have a new competitor," Gerald said. "And they're not playing by the rules. They're operating without permits."

Brix rubbed his temples.

"Gerald, please. We just survived a Void Weaver. Can't we rest?

Can't we just... fix the leak?

"

"The leak is a violation," Gerald said. "But this..." He tapped the X he had drawn. "This is a conspiracy."

He pulled out a new form.

*Form 22-C: Report of Illegal Syndicate Activity.*

"I need to file this," Gerald said. "Immediately. The Statute of Limitations on Conspiracy is 24 hours."

Brix looked at the form. He looked at Gerald. He looked at the X on the wall.

"You're going to report the Mafia?" Brix asked.

"I'm going to report the zoning violation," Gerald said. "They're operating in a residential zone without a Business License. And they're using unapproved signage."

He walked toward the stairs.

"Come along, Brix. We have paperwork to do."

Brix didn't move. He stood there, staring at the wall.

"Gerald," Brix said softly. "Look at the symbol again."

Gerald turned back, and the X he had drawn was fading. The symbol was re-forming. And beneath it, new text was appearing.

*Notice of Audit.* *From: The Inspector General.* *Reason: Unregistered Interactions Detected.* *Time Remaining: 00:59:00.*

Gerald froze.

The Inspector General? He had met the IG once, in Chapter 40.

The IG was not a bureaucrat, and the IG was a predator. The IG was the System's immune system.

And the IG had found them.

Not because of the Weaver.

Because of the Consortium.

Gerald looked at Brix.

"We have to run," Gerald said. His voice was flat, but his hands were clenched at his sides.

"Run where?" Brix asked.

"The only place the IG can't go," Gerald said.

He pointed to the ceiling. To the library above.

"The surface," Gerald said. "I have a building permit there. I have an office. I have a nameplate."

Brix laughed, a broken, terrified sound.

"You think the IG cares about your nameplate?"

"The IG follows the Code," Gerald said. "And I am about to file a complaint."

He looked at the timer.

*00:58:30.*

Gerald pulled out his clipboard. He opened it to a fresh page.

*Form 01-A: Emergency Request for Immunity.*

"Write your statement," Gerald said. "Fast.

You were a witness.

You saw the violation.

You didn't participate.

"

Brix stared at him.

"I helped you. I served the citation."

"You delivered a form," Gerald said. "You did not execute the citation. There is a legal distinction."

Brix stared at the timer.

*00:58:00.*

Brix grabbed the pen. His hand shook so violently he could barely hold it.

"Gerald," Brix whispered. "If this doesn't work..."

"It will work," Gerald said. He looked at the ceiling, at the stone that separated them from the world of sunlight and taxes and normal people. "I have thirty-one years of experience filing appeals. I have never lost."

*00:57:30.*

Gerald climbed the stairs. One step. Then two.

He did not look back, and he did not wait for Brix.

He climbed.

Because if he waited, they would die.

And Gerald Hutchins did not die. He retired.

He just hadn't gotten the paperwork right yet.
