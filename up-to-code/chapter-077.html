Chapter 77: Audit at Forty-Four

# Chapter 77

The patch was wrong. Gerald knew it was wrong the moment he pressed the sealant compound into the joint and felt it resist at the wrong angle.

The moisture had been migrating along the mortar line for three weeks, maybe four.

The channel was deeper than the surface crack suggested. A surface patch would pass visual inspection but fail a moisture meter reading within six hours.

He scraped it out, and the compound came off in a wet strip that clung to his fingers like a dead leaf. He looked at the exposed channel.

The water main behind the eastern wall had a hairline fracture, maybe half a millimeter wide, running diagonally along a weld seam.

Not a leak, technically. A seep. Below the threshold for Category 7 if measured by volume per hour.

Above the threshold if measured by cumulative exposure over a twelve-hour window.

The audit would measure by cumulative exposure. Gerald had read the protocol twice. He had read it three times. The Inspector General's measurement methodology was specified in Appendix C, subsection 4, paragraph 2, and it was unambiguous.

He needed to seal the fracture itself, not the surface channel. That meant accessing the weld seam. That meant cutting into the wall. That meant structural work, which meant Form 3-A, which meant he needed a structural engineer's assessment.

He did not have a structural engineer. He had a clipboard and a sealant compound that cost him four dungeon resource points he could not spare.

Gerald set down the sealant, and he pulled up his System interface and navigated to the audit protocol again.

He had been through it before. He had been through it forty minutes ago.

But he was looking for something specific now, something he had half-noticed during the first read and had not yet let himself consider.

There. Subsection 9. Provisional Pass.

The text read: Where a documented remediation is actively in progress at the time of inspection, the Auditor shall issue a Provisional Pass in lieu of a shutdown order, contingent upon completion within the standard remediation window of thirty days.

Gerald read it again. He read it a third time.

Provisional Pass. Not a citation.

Not a debuff.

A pass. Conditional, but real.

The condition was the problem, and the remediation had to be actively in progress at the time of inspection. Not completed.

Not planned.

In progress. The audit was in eleven hours. If Gerald finished the repair before the Inspector General arrived, the repair would be complete, and a completed repair would not qualify for a Provisional Pass.

It would simply be a fixed violation, which the audit would note as "resolved" and move on.

That was fine. That was better than fine. A resolved violation meant no citation at all.

But Gerald could not finish the repair. He could not access the weld seam without cutting into the wall, and cutting into the wall without a structural assessment would create a new violation, Category 2, Unauthorized Structural Modification. The cure would be worse than the disease.

So he needed the repair to be in progress at the moment of inspection.

He needed to start the wall cut, expose the fracture, begin the weld repair, and then stop.

Leave it open. Leave it wet.

Leave it in progress, and he could do that. He had done that his entire career. He had written thousands of inspection reports that said "remediation in progress, re-inspection scheduled." He knew exactly what an open repair looked like. He knew exactly what it looked like when it was adequate.

He needed it to look adequate. Not complete. Not sloppy. Adequate.

Gerald pulled up his resource tracker. He had thirty-one points.

The wall cut would cost six.

The weld sealant would cost nine. The temporary shoring to keep the cut section from collapsing during the audit would cost four. Total: nineteen.

He would have twelve points left, and he could do it.

He picked up his pen. He wrote Form 3-A.

He listed himself as the structural assessor.

The System accepted it. He was a licensed inspector, which meant he was technically qualified to assess structural modifications to his own dungeon. The System did not care about conflict of interest.

The System cared about credentials, and he approved his own Form 3-A.

He filed it.

He filed Form 7-C, the remediation plan. He filed Form 3-B, the modification permit.

Three forms, three stamps, three seconds each. The System accepted all three.

Then he started the wall, and the cut was clean. Gerald made it with a chisel he had spawned for the purpose, four resource points, because he could not afford the proper diamond-tipped tool and the chisel was sufficient for the depth he needed.

He cut a rectangle, eighteen inches by twelve, into the eastern wall.

The mortar came away in strips. Behind it, the water main. The weld seam.

The fracture, which was worse than he had estimated. The crack was not half a millimeter.

It was closer to two.

And it was branching, following the heat-affected zone of the original weld. Gerald looked at it and felt the specific cold recognition of a man who has seen this exact failure mode before, in a different context, in a building where someone had welded a pipe without preheating the steel.

He had cited that building. Fourteen years ago.

He had cited it for improper welding procedure, Category 5, Mechanical Systems, and the owner had appealed.

The appeal had failed. The owner had fixed the weld.

The building was still standing.

This was the same failure mode. The System had built his water main with a rushed weld. Gerald was going to fix it with a proper one.

He could not do a proper weld. He did not have a welding kit.

He had sealant compound, which was not a weld, and he had a chisel, which was not a torch.

What he could do was clean the fracture, apply a mechanical clamp, and seal the gap with compound. That was not a repair. That was a patch.

A patch was not a weld.

But a patch was remediation in progress. A patch was what you did before the proper repair. A patch was adequate.

Gerald cleaned the fracture with a wire brush. He applied the clamp.

He sealed the gap, and he installed the shoring around the cut section. He cleaned the debris. He left the wall open, the clamp visible, the sealant still curing.

He stepped back. He looked at it.

It looked like a repair in progress. It looked exactly like a repair in progress. It looked like a repair that would be completed in three to five days, pending proper materials.

He filed Form 7-D: Remediation Progress Report. He noted the current status.

He noted the planned completion, and he noted the required materials.

The System logged it. The audit protocol updated.

His compliance rating ticked up by two points. Not enough. But enough to show activity.

He had seven hours left, and the message came through the dungeon's ambient communication channel. Gerald had not enabled that channel. He had not disabled it either. The System defaulted to it being on, as it did with most things, and he had meant to fix it for weeks.

The message was from Core ID 7741. Gerald did not recognize the ID.

He pulled up the registry.

Core 7741 was a mid-tier dungeon, approximately two kilometers east, registered as a fungal biome with a focus on poison damage. Its compliance rating was listed as 61. Not great.

Not terrible.

The message read: "I see you're doing structural work. I have a welding kit.

I also have a proper sealant rated for water mains.

I'll send them through the boundary corridor. In exchange, I want you to drop your citation against Core 3302. You know the one.

The one with the bad egress."

Gerald read it twice.

Core 3302, and the one he had cited four chapters ago.

The one whose three elite spawns had died when the fire safety debuff hit. He remembered.

He remembered the hearing. He remembered the target Core arguing that its egress routes were "functionally adequate given the biome topology" and Gerald responding that the System's definition of adequate did not include "functionally adequate given the biome topology."

The target had lost. The spawns had died. The citation stood.

Core 7741 was offering to help him with his audit in exchange for a reversal of that citation.

Gerald typed back: "The citation against Core 3302 was issued under the fire safety protocol. It is not within my authority to reverse.

It is within my authority to decline to re-issue upon expiration.

The citation expires in forty-one days. I will not re-issue if compliance is demonstrated."

Core 7741 responded within seconds: "Forty-one days is too long. My spawns are being affected by the adjacency debuff. Their poison damage is down twelve percent. I need it gone now."

Gerald typed: "Adjacency debuffs are not within my authority to modify.

I did not create them. I cited Core 3302 for fire safety violations.

The adjacency debuff is a systemic consequence of the citation, not a discretionary penalty.

You may file a grievance with the Inspector General."

Core 7741: "The Inspector General is coming to your dungeon in seven hours. They are not coming to mine."

Gerald stopped typing, and he pulled up the audit schedule again. He had the Inspector General's arrival time listed as approximately fourteen hours from the initial notice. The notice had been issued six hours ago. That put the arrival at eight hours from now.

Core 7741 said seven hours.

Core 7741 was closer to the Inspector General's current position than Gerald was. Core 7741 could track the IG's movement. Gerald could not. Gerald had no way to know where the Inspector General was or how fast it was moving.

Core 7741 was giving him information. Information that Core 7741 could only have if it was monitoring the IG's approach. Which meant Core 7741 had access to the audit tracking system. Which meant Core 7741 had a higher-level interface than Gerald did.

Which meant Core 7741 was more powerful than Gerald.

Gerald typed: "How do you know the IG's position?"

Core 7741: "I've been audited twice. I have the tracking privilege.

You don't.

You're a first-audit Core.

You have no way to know if the IG is coming early or on schedule. I do.

And I'm telling you it's coming early."

Gerald looked at his wall. The sealant was curing.

The clamp was holding, and the shoring was in place. It looked adequate. It looked like a repair in progress.

If the IG arrived in seven hours instead of eight, Gerald had one hour less of margin. That was not catastrophic. The repair would still be in progress. The Provisional Pass would still apply.

Unless the IG arrived before the repair was in progress. Unless the IG arrived before Gerald had started the wall cut.

Gerald checked the timestamp on his Form 3-B.

The modification permit. He had filed it forty minutes ago.

The wall cut had begun thirty minutes ago, and the repair was in progress; it had been in progress for thirty minutes.

If the IG arrived in seven hours, the repair would have been in progress for seven and a half hours. That was within the acceptable window.

Gerald typed: "Why are you telling me this?"

Core 7741: "Because if the IG arrives early and you're not ready, you get shut down. And if you get shut down, your dungeon footprint becomes unclaimed territory.

And I want that territory.

It borders my biome.

I need the space for expansion. But I'd rather you stay alive and owe me a favor than be dead and useless.

So. The welding kit. You drop the citation.

I give you the kit. You finish the weld before the IG arrives.

No Provisional Pass needed.

Clean pass."

Gerald looked at the message. He looked at his wall. He looked at the sealant, still curing, still wet, still a patch and not a repair.

A clean pass was better than a Provisional Pass, and a clean pass meant no thirty-day window. No re-inspection.

No risk of the sealant failing before the follow-up.

A clean pass meant the violation was resolved, permanently, with a proper weld.

But a clean pass required a welding kit. And the welding kit required a price. And the price was dropping a citation that had killed three spawns and that Gerald had won in a hearing.

He could not drop the citation. He had not cited Core 3302 out of malice.

He had cited it because the egress routes were non-compliant.

He had cited it because the System's fire safety protocol required a minimum of two exit paths per floor, and Core 3302 had one.

He had cited it because that was the rule, and he could decline to re-issue in forty-one days. He could not reverse it now.

But he could do something else.

Gerald pulled up the citation against Core 3302. He read it. He read the hearing transcript. He read the appeal denial.

Then he pulled up a new form. Form 14-F: Citation Amendment.

The System had an amendment protocol, and a citation could be amended to change the scope of the violation without reversing it. Gerald had never used it. He had not known it existed until now.

Form 14-F allowed the issuing Core to modify the citation's penalty parameters. Not the violation itself. Not the finding.

The penalty. The adjacency debuff.

Gerald typed into the amendment: "Original citation finding: unchanged.

Penalty modification: adjacency debuff to Core 7741 reduced from twelve percent to four percent. Rationale: Core 7741's poison damage is not the primary mechanism of harm to its dungeon ecosystem. The twelve percent adjacency debuff was calibrated for combat effectiveness.

Core 7741's biome does not rely on combat effectiveness for structural stability.

The debuff is disproportionate to the violation's scope."

The System processed it. The amendment was logged. The adjacency debuff against Core 7741 dropped from twelve percent to four.

Gerald typed: "I have amended the penalty scope.

The citation stands. The finding stands. The debuff is reduced.

I will not reverse the citation. I will not drop it.

This is the maximum concession available under the amendment protocol."

Core 7741 did not respond for a long time.

Then: "The welding kit is coming through the boundary corridor. You have six hours and forty minutes. The IG is moving faster than I expected."

Gerald looked at the boundary corridor, and he could see it on his interface, a narrow passage connecting his dungeon footprint to the unclaimed space beyond. He could see something moving through it.

A crate. Small, metal, marked with Core 7741's ID.

He had the kit. He had the time. He had the fracture, still wet, still open, still waiting for a proper weld.

He picked up his pen. He filed Form 3-B Amendment: Modification Permit Update, adding welding equipment to the authorized tools list. The System accepted it.

He walked to the eastern wall. He set down the crate. He opened it. Inside: a compact welding torch, a set of filler rods, a temperature gauge, and a small vial of flux.

Gerald checked the temperature gauge. He checked the flux.

He checked the filler rods, and they were adequate. They were not premium. They were adequate.

He lit the torch.

He preheated the steel. He checked the temperature.

He applied the flux, and he fed the first rod into the fracture.

The weld started.

The IG arrived four hours and twelve minutes early.

Gerald heard it before he saw it. A pressure change in the dungeon's ambient field. A shift in the air. The System's presence, concentrated, focused, moving through his corridors like a man walking through a house he owns and does not like.

He was mid-weld. The second pass.

The fracture was three-quarters closed, and the weld was real.

It was proper. It was in progress.

The Inspector General was in his eastern corridor. Gerald could feel it. The audit scan was sweeping his dungeon, reading his walls, his floors, his systems; it would reach the eastern wall in ninety seconds.

Gerald fed the third rod. The fracture was almost closed. He needed one more pass.

Maybe two. He had forty seconds, maybe less.

The scan touched the eastern wall.

Gerald felt it like a hand pressing flat against his chest. He felt it read the fracture. He felt it read the weld.

He felt it read the weld and pause.

The System's interface flashed a message he had never seen before.

AUDIT NOTICE: PARTIAL WELD DETECTED. CATEGORY 7 VIOLATION STATUS: IN PROGRESS. PROVISIONAL PASS ISSUED. RE-INSPECTION SCHEDULED: 30 DAYS.

Gerald exhaled.

Then a second message appeared.

ADDITIONAL FINDING: UNAUTHORIZED STRUCTURAL MODIFICATION DETECTED AT EASTERN WALL. FORM 3-B VALID.

FORM 3-A VALID.

FORM 14-F VALID. ALL DOCUMENTATION IN ORDER.

NO CITATION ISSUED.

Gerald stared at the message. He had done it.

The audit was over, and the Provisional Pass was his.

The thirty-day window was his.

Then a third message.

INSPECTOR GENERAL NOTE: Core 7741 has filed a joint audit petition. Core 7741 is requesting a simultaneous audit of its own dungeon, conducted at your location, using your infrastructure as a reference baseline. Petition granted.

Core 7741's audit will begin in forty minutes. You are now a witness.

Your compliance rating will be used as the comparative standard.

Gerald looked at his dungeon. His Compliance Rating was 78. Core 7741's was 61.

If the IG used Gerald as the baseline, Core 7741 would be audited against Gerald's standards.

Core 7741 would fail by seventeen points.

Core 7741 had just volunteered to be audited against him.

Core 7741 had just volunteered to be shut down.

Unless Core 7741 knew something Gerald did not.

Gerald pulled up Core 7741's compliance breakdown. He had not looked at it before. He looked at it now.

Ventilation: 84.

Fire safety: 71. Structural: 68. Electrical: 59.

Egress: 44.

Those were Core 7741's scores. Those were bad scores. Those were scores that should have triggered a shutdown order years ago.

But Core 7741 had been audited twice before. And it was still alive.

Gerald looked at the audit history. Core 7741's first audit: Compliance Rating 61, Provisional Pass issued. Core 7741's second audit: Compliance Rating 61, Provisional Pass issued.

Two audits. Same rating. Two Provisional Passes.

Core 7741 was not improving. Core 7741 was not failing. Core 7741 was maintaining a score that was low enough to avoid triggering an escalation protocol but high enough to avoid a shutdown order. Core 7741 was gaming the audit system.

And now Core 7741 had invited Gerald into the game, and the message came through the communication channel one last time.

Core 7741: "Thank you for the welding kit.

You should know something before the joint audit begins.

The Inspector General's reference baseline is not static. It adjusts to the audited Core's weakest category.

If my weakest category is egress at 44, and your weakest category is egress at 67, the IG will audit me against 44, not against 67. I will pass. And you will be audited against my 44.

Do you know what your egress score is, Gerald?"

Gerald checked.

His egress score was 52.

Core 7741's was 44.

The IG would audit Gerald against 44. Gerald was at 52. He would pass.

But Gerald's thirty-day re-inspection window had just been set. And the re-inspection would be conducted against the same baseline, and the baseline that Core 7741 had just lowered to 44.

Gerald's Provisional Pass was contingent on completing the weld within thirty days, and the weld would be done in an hour. That was fine.

But the re-inspection would check everything. Not just the weld. Everything. Against a baseline of 44.

Core 7741 had just set Gerald's next audit threshold. And Gerald had not known it was coming.

The joint audit started in thirty-seven minutes.
