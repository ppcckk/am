Chapter 127: Disaster over Heroes

# Chapter 127

Gerald pressed his thumb against the rusted latch pin. It was slate. Dark, polished, and heavy enough to crack a kneecap.

Gerald didn't look at the figure holding it. He looked at the doorframe.

The jamb was oak.

Standard municipal grade. The latch, however, was rusted. A Class 4 maintenance failure.

If the door opened, the rust flakes would contaminate the ventilation zone he had just spent ten minutes trying to clarify.

He had to stop that latch from engaging.

The figure pushed. The door groaned. A sound like a bone breaking.

Gerald's core pulsed. Not with magic. With irritation.

He focused on the hinge. *Lubricate.*

A trickle of oil, synthesized from the fat reserves of the dead rat, seeped into the pin joint. The groaning stopped. The door swung open on silent bearings.

The Inspector General stepped over the threshold, and he was tall. Impossibly so.

His suit was cut from fabric that absorbed light, leaving only the white of his shirt and the black of his tie visible in the dim dungeon light.

He wore no helmet. No armor. Just a tie and a clipboard that smelled of ozone and old blood.

"Gerald Hutchins," the Inspector said. His voice was the sound of a stamp hitting a form. "Core ID 8940-B. Current Compliance Rating: Forty-Seven."

Gerald stayed in the center of the room. The mold spores had settled on the floor in a grey carpet. They looked like ash.

"Inspector," Gerald said. He kept his voice flat.

Professional.

"You are standing in a designated biological containment zone.

Please move to the yellow line.

"

The Inspector looked down at the yellow paint. He looked at Gerald. He smiled. It was not a nice smile.

"You cited Kael the Iron-Fist for improper stacking of gold," the Inspector said. He flipped a page on his slate. "Kael has filed an appeal. He claims your Citation was invalid because his dungeon's structural integrity rating was actually ninety-two, not forty."

"He was wrong," Gerald said.

"He has hired a Legal Core," the Inspector continued. "A Level 4 Arbitrator. They are currently drafting a motion to dismiss based on jurisdictional ambiguity. They argue that Kael's dungeon is not a 'public accommodation' and therefore not subject to municipal egress codes."

Gerald felt a cold spike in his center. Jurisdictional ambiguity. The one thing Gerald could not fight.

He could fix walls. He could fix pipes.

He could not fix a loophole in the cosmic bylaws unless he had a precedent.

"Then let them argue," Gerald said. "I have a Hearing scheduled. I am ready."

"You are not ready," the Inspector said. "You are unprepared. Your Compliance Rating is still low.

The Arbitrator will cite your own ventilation issues.

Your mold spore release was an unpermitted modification to the air handling system. That is a Code Violation 12-C: Unauthorized Biological Modification.

"

Gerald's hand twitched. He wanted to reach for a pen. He had no pen. He had only the damp, cold weight of his own stone walls.

"I did not modify the system," Gerald said. "I utilized existing infrastructure for emergency remediation."

"Semantics," the Inspector said. "If you lose the Hearing, your dungeon is condemned. Demolition is mandatory. You will be dissolved."

The Inspector stepped closer. The air around him grew colder. The mold on the floor curled inward, shrinking away from his presence.

"Kael knows you are alone," the Inspector said.

"He knows you have no defenders. He has sent a team to your perimeter. Three Level 3 Paladins.

They are breaching your north wall now.

"

Gerald looked at the north wall. It was solid granite. Four feet thick. Rated for seismic activity.

"They cannot breach it," Gerald said.

"They are using *Demolish*," the Inspector said.

"A high-level spell.

It ignores structural integrity ratings if the caster's mana pool exceeds the target's health pool. Your health pool is forty-seven.

Their combined mana is two thousand.

"

Gerald's breath hitched. *Demolish.* A brute force spell. It broke things. It did not care about code.

"Where are they?" Gerald asked.

"North corridor. Forty feet away."

The floor shuddered. Dust fell from the ceiling.

Gerald looked at the mold. Then he looked at the hatch. The one he had sealed. The one leading to the sewer main.

Kael's Paladins were coming to destroy him. Gerald had no weapons. No traps. No mana.

He had a mold colony. A biological asset.

And he had a sewer main.

Gerald walked to the hatch. He did not walk with purpose. He walked with the stiff, measured gait of a man checking for uneven flooring.

"Inspector," Gerald said. "If I prove that the mold is a critical component of the dungeon's waste management system, does that negate the unauthorized modification charge?"

The Inspector tilted his head.

"Prove it."

Gerald opened the hatch, and the smell was worse than before. Sewage. Rot. Ancient, stagnant water.

Gerald did not flinch. He had inspected septic tanks for thirty years. He knew the smell of failure. He also knew the smell of necessity.

He focused on the mold spores on the floor. He did not try to make them grow in the air.

He pushed them down.

Through the cracks in the floor. Into the gaps beneath the tiles. Into the sewer main.

*Action: Integrate Fungal Network with Sanitary Line.*

*Reason: Bio-Remediation of Effluent.*

*Warning: Pathogen Risk. High.*

*Compliance Check: Municipal Code Section 44. Waste Water Treatment.*

The System paused.

For a heartbeat, nothing happened.

Then, a green light flickered in the corner of Gerald's vision.

*Violation 12-C: Downgraded to Warning. Pending Inspection.*

Gerald exhaled. It wasn't a victory. It was a stay of execution.

The north wall exploded.

Stone shards sprayed into the main chamber.

Dust choked the air. Through the hole, three figures stepped.

They were clad in shining plate armor.

Their weapons glowed with white light.

The lead Paladin stepped forward. His voice boomed.

"HUTCHINS. SURRENDER YOUR CORE. KAEAL THE IRON-FIST CLAIMS THIS TERRITORY BY RIGHT OF CONQUEST."

Gerald looked at the Paladin. He looked at the hole in the wall. The edges were jagged. Unreinforced.

"Your entry point lacks a lintel," Gerald said. "You are creating a hazard."

The Paladin raised his sword.

"Silence, bug. Prepare to die."

Gerald did not move. He focused on the sewer main beneath the floor.

The mold had taken root. It was feeding.

Fast, and the sewage was rich in nutrients.

The fungal network was spreading through the pipes, expanding, blooming.

Gerald needed it to hit critical mass.

He needed a trigger.

He looked at the Inspector. The Inspector was watching. He was not interfering. He was recording.

"Inspector," Gerald said. "I am invoking Emergency Protocol 9."

The Inspector raised an eyebrow.

"Explain."

"Mass biological failure in the waste line. Immediate evacuation of all personnel from the affected sector."

The Paladins were already charging. The first one leaped over the rubble.

Gerald pulled the pin on his core's emergency vent.

Not the damper. The *flush*.

Every drop of pressurized water from the library's sprinkler system, which had been dormant for years, rushed into the sewer main.

It hit the mold.

The mold reacted. It did not just grow. It *bloomed*.

A wave of grey, fuzzy spores erupted from the floor tiles. It burst upward, a cloud of choking, sticky rot.

The Paladins were in the middle of the room.

The cloud hit them.

"GAH!" The lead Paladin coughed. His armor was too tight. He couldn't breathe.

The second Paladin tried to cast a shield. *Wind Wall.*

It failed. The spores were not wind. They were solid particles.

Heavy. Wet.

They clogged the air intakes of their armor. They clogged their lungs.

Gerald stood in the center of the cloud. He held his breath. He closed his eyes. He counted to ten.

*One. Two. Three.*

The Paladins fell, and they clawed at their throats. They did not fall to damage. They fell to suffocation.

To biological obstruction.

*System Alert: Enemy Unit Neutralized by Environmental Hazard.*

*No Citation Issued. Cause of Death: Natural Causes (Exposure).*

Gerald opened his eyes. The Paladins were dead. Their armor was covered in a thick layer of grey fuzz.

The Inspector stepped over the body of the first Paladin. He did not step in the mold. He stepped on the clean, yellow line.

"Interesting," the Inspector said. He wrote something on his slate. "You used the building code against the invaders. You turned a violation into a weapon."

"I cited them for trespassing on a hazardous waste site," Gerald said. His voice was raspy. His throat burned. "They entered without protective gear."

"Adventurers do not need protective gear," the Inspector said. "They are heroes."

"Heroes do not violate OSHA regulations," Gerald said.

The Inspector looked at the dead Paladins. Then he looked at Gerald.

"You have bought yourself time," the Inspector said. "But Kael is not done. He knows you are alive. He knows you have a connection to the city infrastructure."

The Inspector turned to leave.

"Kael has appealed to the High Court of Dungeons," the Inspector said. "He is suing for defamation. He claims you fabricated the compliance violations to destroy his economy."

"He lost three elite Paladins," Gerald said. "His economy is already destroyed."

"He has hired a Bard," the Inspector said. "A Level 10 Storyteller. He is rewriting the narrative. By tomorrow, the System will view your actions not as code enforcement, but as malicious sabotage."

The Inspector stopped at the door.

"You have twenty-four hours before the Narrative Shift locks in," he said. "Once it locks, your Citations become invalid. Your defenses become void. You will be hunted by every hero in the region."

Gerald felt the hunger in his core twist. The mold was still growing. It was eating his walls.

"How do I stop a Storyteller?" Gerald asked.

The Inspector smiled. It was a thin, cruel expression.

"You don't," he said. "You change the plot."

The Inspector left. The door clicked shut.

Gerald stood alone in the cloud of spores. The dead Paladins lay at his feet. Their armor was useless. Their swords were useless.

Gerald looked at the hole in the north wall. The rain from outside was pouring in.

He could fix it. He could patch it.

Or he could make it bigger.

If the System viewed him as a villain, then a villain did not fix holes. A villain exploited them.

Gerald walked to the center of the room. He placed his hand on the floor.

He felt the sewer main pulsing beneath him. The city's waste. The city's filth. The city's hidden plumbing.

He had thirty-one years of knowledge about how things broke. How they leaked. How they failed.

He did not need mana. He did not need magic.

He needed a leak.

Gerald focused on the pipe running directly under the library's main reading room. The pipe that supplied water to the restrooms. The pipe that was, according to his last inspection report, corroded.

*Action: Induce Micro-Fracture in Supply Line.*

*Reason: Pressure Testing.*

*Warning: Structural Integrity Compromised.*

The floor trembled.

Above him, miles away, in the pristine, quiet halls of the Ashford Municipal Library, a pipe burst.

Water sprayed across the history section. Books soaked. Pages warped.

The System flashed a new notification.

*Alert: Public Infrastructure Failure Detected.*

*Source: Dungeon Core 8940-B.*

*Classification: Civil Liability.*

Gerald stared at the notification. He had not attacked the library. He had merely inspected it.

But the System did not care about intent. It cared about cause.

And now, the city of Ashford would know that their library was flooded by a dungeon core.

The heroes would not come to fight a villain, and they would come to save the city.

Gerald looked at the yellow line. He erased it with his foot.

He did not need to be a hero. He needed to be a disaster.

And disasters, unlike heroes, were never cited for being too loud.

The heroes arrived before Gerald could move.
