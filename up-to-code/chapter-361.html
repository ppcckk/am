Chapter 361: Excavation Not Violation

# Chapter 361

Gerald's boot slid on the loose scree, sending a cascade of pebbles tumbling into the darkness below. He caught himself against the damp rock wall, fingers scrabbling for purchase on the wet stone. The pickaxe had missed by inches. The wind from the swing had rustled his hair.

The dwarf grinned, showing teeth like jagged flint.

"Little man," the dwarf spat, "you cite the air? You cite the rock?

You have no power.

You have no level. You have no *anything*.

"

Gerald straightened his spine. He brushed dust from his tunic, a reflex ingrained from thirty-one years of wearing khakis. He did not look at the dwarf. He looked at the floor.

"Impact hazard," Gerald said. "Uneven surface. Tripping risk."

The dwarf laughed again. It was a harsh, grating sound.

"You're talking to me like I'm a contractor. Like I'm a *contractor*."

"Section 4," Gerald continued, his voice flat and monotone.

"Flooring must be slip-resistant in wet conditions. This is wet.

This is slippery.

You are creating a hazardous work environment.

"

The dwarf swung again.

Gerald stepped back. The pickaxe bit into the floor where he had stood. A chunk of granite flew loose, embedding itself in the ceiling. Dust choked the air.

"Obstruction of egress," Gerald noted.

He reached into his pocket. Empty.

He reached into his other pocket.

Empty, and he had no forms. He had no clipboard.

He had no rat. He had only his voice, and the cold, hard certainty of the code.

"Stop," Gerald said.

The dwarf paused. The hammer hovered in the air.

"I am not stopping," the dwarf said. "I am killing."

"You are violating the right of way," Gerald said. "You are standing in a corridor that requires a minimum clearance of thirty-six inches. Your pickaxe swing requires a four-foot clearance for safety."

The dwarf looked around. The tunnel was narrow. The walls were close. The ceiling was low.

"It's a tunnel," the dwarf said.

"It's a non-compliant tunnel," Gerald corrected.

The dwarf stepped forward. The ground beneath his feet squelched. Mud bubbled up around his boots. The dwarf frowned, looking down.

"What?"

"Section 12," Gerald said. "Prohibition of unauthorized excavation within fifty feet of a water source."

The mud was spreading. It was not just mud.

It was a violation, and the System was listening. The code was active.

The dwarf tried to step back, but his boot sank deeper. The mud was sticky, viscous, and rapidly expanding.

"Magic?" the dwarf grunted.

"Compliance," Gerald said.

The dwarf pulled his leg free with a wet sucking sound. He lunged.

Gerald did not run. He did not dodge. He stepped to the left, into a shallow alcove in the wall. The dwarf's momentum carried him past Gerald, stumbling toward the deeper dark.

Gerald turned. He watched the dwarf's back.

"You need lighting," Gerald said.

The dwarf stopped. He turned slowly. His eyes were wide, confused.

"Lighting?"

"Illumination requirements," Gerald said. "Minimum of five foot-candles at the working surface. You are operating in near-total darkness. This is a violation of occupational safety standards."

The dwarf snarled. He raised the pickaxe again.

"I will crush you."

"You will be fined," Gerald said.

"I don't care about your fines!"

"You should."

Gerald closed his eyes, and he focused. He did not try to spawn a monster.

He did not try to cast a spell.

He tried to remember the specific wording of Sub-Section 9, Paragraph C.

*Insufficient illumination constitutes a failure to provide a safe workspace.*

The air in the tunnel shimmered.

A faint, ghostly outline of a light fixture appeared on the ceiling. It flickered. It buzzed. It emitted a weak, sickly yellow glow.

The dwarf stared at it.

"What is that?"

"A citation," Gerald said.

The light intensified. It was blinding.

It was harsh, which was not a spell.

It was a regulatory enforcement mechanism. The System demanded compliance.

If the dungeon did not provide light, the System would provide it, and it would provide it aggressively.

The dwarf shielded his eyes. He stumbled back. The pickaxe dropped from his hands, clattering on the wet stone.

"Make it stop!" the dwarf yelled.

"It won't stop until you comply," Gerald said.

"Comply with what?"

"Install a fixture. Or leave."

The dwarf scrambled backward, slipping on the mud. He fell onto his knees. He looked up at Gerald, his face twisted in pain and confusion.

"Who are you?"

"Gerald Hutchins," Gerald said. "Building Inspector. City of Ashford."

The dwarf laughed, a broken, wheezing sound.

"A bureaucrat. In the Deep."

"A bureaucrat," Gerald confirmed. "And you are out of code."

A figure emerged from the shadows further down the tunnel. It was tall, thin, and wrapped in tattered rags. It held a staff made of bone. The figure stopped when it saw the blinding light and the mud.

The figure looked at the dwarf. Then it looked at Gerald.

"Inspector," the figure said. Its voice was like dry leaves scraping together.

Gerald turned. He did not flinch. He adjusted his collar.

"You are trespassing on a private easement," Gerald said.

The figure tilted its head.

"This is the Deep. There are no easements."

"There are," Gerald said. "The code applies everywhere. The System is universal. The code is universal."

The figure stepped closer. The air grew colder. Frost formed on the walls.

"You reset," the figure said. "Level 1.

No spawns.

No defenses. You are naked.

"

"I am exposed," Gerald agreed. "Which is a violation of privacy standards, but that is not my current concern. Your concern is the lighting."

The figure looked at the blinding yellow glow. It looked at the mud. It looked at the dwarf, who was now shivering and trying to crawl away.

"Why did you do that?" the figure asked.

"Because it was wrong," Gerald said.

"The floor was uneven. The lighting was insufficient.

The excavation was unauthorized.

I cited it."

The figure lowered its staff.

"You are a small man. You have no power. How did you force the System to act?"

Gerald paused. He looked at his hands.

They were trembling slightly.

Not from fear.

From exertion. Using the code without a level, without a form, without a rating, took a toll.

It burned in his chest like heartburn.

"I used the loophole," Gerald said.

The figure leaned in.

"What loophole?"

"Section 1," Gerald said. "The Definition of a Dungeon."

The figure frowned.

"A dungeon," Gerald recited, "is defined as a constructed space intended for the confinement of entities or the storage of resources. If a space is not up to code, it is not a dungeon.

It is a hazard, and the System does not protect hazards. The System corrects them.

"

The figure's eyes narrowed.

"You argued with the System."

"I filed a complaint," Gerald said.

"Against yourself?"

"No," Gerald said.

"Against the environment. The environment was non-compliant.

The System corrected the environment.

The correction hurt you.

"

The figure stood up straight. The frost retreated slightly.

"You are dangerous," the figure said.

"I am thorough," Gerald said.

"Thoroughness gets you killed in the Deep."

"Non-compliance gets you killed anywhere," Gerald said.

The figure looked at the dwarf, who was now sitting in the mud, weeping. The figure looked back at Gerald.

"Come with me," the figure said.

Gerald shook his head.

"I am conducting an inspection."

"You have nothing to inspect. Your rating is zero.

Your dungeon is gone.

The library above you is sealed.

You are alone."

"I am not alone," Gerald said. "I have the code."

The figure smiled. It was a thin, cruel smile.

"The code is a tool," the figure said. "Tools require hands. Hands require strength.

You have neither. You are holding a hammer with no arm.

"

"I have a voice," Gerald said.

"Voice is not enough."

"It was enough for the dwarf."

"It was enough for one dwarf.

One mistake. One slip.

" The figure stepped forward. The bone staff glowed with a faint purple light.

"I am a Necromancer Core.

My dungeon is a crypt. My walls are lined with dead.

My air is stale.

My lighting is poor. I know this. I have accepted these violations.

I pay the debuffs. I survive them.

"

Gerald looked at the figure. He looked at the purple light.

"You are in violation of Section 14," Gerald said. "Biohazard containment."

The Necromancer stopped.

"Decomposing organic matter must be contained within sealed vessels to prevent airborne pathogens," Gerald said. "Your crypt is open. The air is unfiltered. You are spreading disease."

The Necromancer's face went pale.

"I... I did not know."

"It is written," Gerald said. "Page 402. Sub-Section D."

The Necromancer looked at his staff. He looked at the shadows in the tunnel. He looked at the blinding light still buzzing above the dwarf.

"If I cite you," Gerald said, "you will take damage. Your minions will rot faster. Your mana regeneration will drop. You will be vulnerable to every adventurer who enters this tunnel."

The Necromancer swallowed.

"What do you want?"

"Compliance," Gerald said.

"I have no builders. I have no resources."

"Then you must cease the operation," Gerald said.

"Shut down the crypt.

Seal the doors. Ventilate the chambers.

Install filtration systems.

"

"That will take weeks," the Necromancer said.

"You have days," Gerald said. "The next audit is in forty-eight hours."

The Necromancer's eyes widened.

"The Inspector General is coming?"

"Periodically," Gerald said. "And he is strict. He dislikes open graves."

The Necromancer looked at the dwarf, who was now silent. He looked at Gerald.

"You are bluffing," the Necromancer said. "You cannot issue a Citation without a Core Level of at least 5. The System requires it."

Gerald held his gaze. He did not blink.

"Section 8," Gerald said. "Emergency Enforcement Powers. In the event of an imminent threat to public safety, a registered inspector may bypass level requirements."

The Necromancer stared.

"Is that true?"

"It is," Gerald said. "You can check. It's in the fine print. The same fine print you ignored."

The Necromancer lowered his staff. The purple light faded.

"You want me to close my dungeon," the Necromancer said.

"I want you to fix it," Gerald said. "Or I will cite you for negligence. And I will cite you for obstruction of the Inspector General. And I will cite you for operating an unlicensed business."

The Necromancer shuddered.

"The Inspector General...

I have heard of him, and he audits the ancient cores. He finds violations in tombs that have existed for a thousand years.

"

"He finds them everywhere," Gerald said.

The Necromancer looked at the tunnel. He looked at the darkness. He looked at Gerald.

"If I fix it," the Necromancer said. "If I spend my mana on ventilation and filtration... my defenses will drop. A party will come. They will kill me."

Gerald nodded.

"It is a risk."

"It is a death sentence."

"It is a choice," Gerald said. "Between a fast death and a slow one."

The Necromancer paced back and forth. The mud squelched under his feet. He stopped in front of Gerald.

"Help me," the Necromancer said.

Gerald raised an eyebrow.

"Help me," the Necromancer said again.

"You know the code. You know what is needed.

Tell me.

How do I fix this? Quickly. Cheaply.

Without builders.

"

Gerald looked at the walls. He looked at the air currents. He looked at the layout.

"Section 3," Gerald said. "Temporary Ventilation Shims. If you excavate a secondary shaft, angled at fifteen degrees, and line it with porous stone, you can create a passive draft. It will not filter the air perfectly, but it will meet the minimum air exchange rate for a Class C dungeon."

The Necromancer listened. He memorized the angle. He memorized the material.

"Second," Gerald continued.

"Fire suppression.

You have no water. Use sand.

Excavate a trench along the entrance. Fill it with sand. Label it.

Post a sign. That satisfies the minimum requirement for a Class C hazard zone.

"

"And the lighting?"

"Open the ceiling. If you cannot install a fixture, you must remove the obstacle. Break the roof. Let the sky in."

The Necromancer looked up. The tunnel was deep. The surface was miles away.

"Impossible," the Necromancer said.

"Not impossible," Gerald said. "Expensive. And dangerous. But legal."

The Necromancer looked at Gerald. He looked at the dwarf, who was now trying to stand up.

"Why do you help me?" the Necromancer asked.

"I do not help," Gerald said. "I inspect. I advise. I enforce."

"You are saving me."

"I am bringing you into compliance."

The Necromancer smiled. It was a different smile this time. Not cruel. Calculating.

"If I do this," the Necromancer said. "If I follow your instructions. My dungeon will be safe. My Compliance Rating will rise."

"Yes," Gerald said.

"And if my rating rises," the Necromancer said. "I will be strong enough to fight the Inspector General. Or to fight *you*."

Gerald paused. He looked at the Necromancer. He looked at the mud. He looked at the empty inventory slot in his mind.

He had no forms. He had no level. He had no rat.

He had a plan.

"If you comply," Gerald said. "You will be safe. And I will be out of compliance."

The Necromancer's eyes gleamed.

"Why?"

"Because I have no dungeon," Gerald said.

"I have no resources. I have no defenses. If you fix your dungeon, you become a threat.

If I do nothing, I remain a Level 1 Core with no spawns.

The System will delete me within the week.

"

The Necromancer laughed. It was a dry, rattling sound.

"So you need me to be a threat," the Necromancer said. "You need me to be strong enough to draw the Inspector's attention. You need me to be a shield."

Gerald did not answer.

The Necromancer looked at the dwarf. Then he looked at Gerald.

"I will fix it," the Necromancer said. "I will open the ceiling. I will dig the shafts. I will fill the trenches."

Gerald nodded.

"When it is done," the Necromancer said. "I will come for you."

"I expect nothing less," Gerald said.

The Necromancer turned. He walked into the darkness. He pulled out a pickaxe from his robes. He dug.

The dwarf watched him go. Then the dwarf looked at Gerald.

"You are a fool," the dwarf wheezed. "He will kill you."

Gerald looked at the ground. He looked at the mud. He looked at the blinding light.

He reached into his chest. Not into his pocket. Into his core.

He pulled out a single, glowing thread, which was not a form. It was not a rat. It was a connection.

A link to the Necromancer's dungeon.

He tied the thread around his finger.

He felt the Necromancer's mana. It was cold. It was dark. It was strong.

He felt the connection snap into place.

**System Notification: Shared Liability Agreement Detected.**

**Warning: You are now liable for the Compliance Rating of Dungeon Core 'Necro-Vault-7'.**

**Penalty for Failure: Immediate Deletion.**

**Reward for Success: Shared Mana Pool.**

Gerald looked at the message. He looked at the Necromancer digging.

He had no level. He had no defense. He had no rat.

But he had a partner. And he had a deadline.

He took a breath. The air was stale. The air was non-compliant.

He walked toward the Necromancer.

"Angle is wrong," Gerald said. "Fifteen degrees. Not ten."

The Necromancer did not look up. But he adjusted.

Gerald smiled. It was a small, tight smile.

He picked up a rock. He dug.

Then he felt a vibration in the ground. A deep, rhythmic thumping. It was coming from below. From the center of the cavern.

The Necromancer froze. His pickaxe stopped in mid-swing.

"That is not a violation," the Necromancer whispered.

"No," Gerald said. "That is an excavation."

The ground cracked.

A hand emerged from the earth. It was made of stone. And it was holding a clipboard.
