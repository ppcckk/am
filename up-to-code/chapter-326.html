Chapter 326: Authority Holds the Stamp

# Chapter 326

The dust from the Auditor's departure still hung in the stagnant air of the sub-basement. Gerald didn't look up.

He looked down at the Citation slip in his hand, the paper already curling at the edges from the dampness.

His knuckles were white. The rebar pinning the creature nearby groaned under the weight of its own decay, but the System's enforcement kept it stationary.

Sirens wailed outside. Not the distant hum of the city, but the sharp, rhythmic clatter of combat boots on the library's marble stairs.

Gerald straightened his tie, which was torn, stained with ichor, but the knot was still symmetrical. He adjusted it.

A reflex.

A habit.

The heavy oak doors to the sub-basement burst open. Splinters flew. Three figures stood in the threshold, silhouetted by the flickering light of the upper library. They wore mismatched armor, enchanted with low-level glow runes that cast a sickly green hue over the debris.

The leader, a woman with a scar running from her left ear to her chin, stepped forward. She held a sword that looked too heavy for her to lift comfortably. Behind her, a mage with robes made of patchwork leather clutched a staff topped with a dull crystal. The third, a rogue, hung back in the shadows, picking his teeth with a dagger.

"Clear," the scarred woman said. Her voice was rough, used to shouting over battle noise.

She scanned the room, her eyes landing on Gerald, and she didn't see a monster.

She saw a man in a rumpled suit holding a clipboard.

"Who are you?" she demanded. "Where's the Core?"

Gerald didn't answer immediately. He pulled a pen from his pocket. The ink was dry.

He clicked it. The sound was loud in the silence.

"You have breached the perimeter," Gerald said.

His voice was flat. Monotone.

"And you have damaged public infrastructure.

That is a Class 3 civil violation.

"

The mage stepped forward, raising his staff.

"Cut the crap, old man.

This is a dungeon.

There are no laws here. Only loot and death.

"

"Actually," Gerald said, pointing the pen at the mage, "there is a law. It's called the Municipal Safety Ordinance of Ashford, Section 4, Paragraph B.

It stipulates that any structure designated as a public library must maintain clear egress paths.

You just broke the load-bearing support for the third floor.

You're standing on unstable footing.

"

The rogue laughed. It was a dry, hacking sound.

"Stable footing.

Right. And I suppose you're going to cite us for bad posture?

"

"I am going to cite you for negligence," Gerald said.

He looked at the ceiling. A crack ran through it, widening as the adventurers' weight shifted.

"You are currently standing under a fractured concrete slab.

According to the blueprints I memorized in 1992, this slab supports the fiction section.

If it collapses, you will be crushed. That is not a combat issue.

That is a liability issue.

"

The scarred woman frowned. She looked up. The crack was indeed visible.

A small pebble fell, hitting her shoulder. She brushed it off, but her stance shifted.

"Is he serious?" she asked the mage.

"The Core is trying to confuse us," the mage said.

"It's a psychic attack.

Or a charm spell. Ignore the logic.

Kill the host."

"Host?" Gerald raised an eyebrow. "I am the landlord. And you are trespassing on private property without a permit for excavation. Which means you are subject to immediate eviction."

"Evict me," the rogue sneered, stepping out of the shadows. He moved fast, a blur of leather and steel. He aimed for Gerald's throat.

Gerald didn't move. He didn't dodge. He simply held up the Citation slip.

**System Alert: Citation Served.** **Target: Adventurer Party (Unregistered).** **Violation: Unlicensed Excavation & Structural Negligence.** **Effect: Physical Restraint (Temporary).**

The rogue froze in mid-air. His feet left the ground. Invisible wires, thick as cables, snapped into existence around his wrists and ankles. He dangled there, thrashing, his dagger falling to the floor with a clang.

The scarred woman stared. The mage lowered his staff slightly.

"What did you do?" the woman asked. Her voice had lost its edge. It was confused.

"I served the papers," Gerald said. He walked toward her.

His steps were measured, and he avoided the cracked floor tiles. He stepped only on the reinforced concrete pads he had installed three weeks ago.

"You are now under arrest by the System's Compliance Enforcement Division.

You will remain stationary until the Inspector General reviews the incident.

"

"That's impossible," the mage said. "Players don't get restrained by non-combat mechanics."

"Players operate within the System," Gerald corrected. "The System is a legal framework. You are violating the terms of service. Specifically, you are creating a hazard in a zone designated for 'Low-Risk Civic Engagement.' Do you know what that means?"

"It means we can farm you," the rogue choked out, hanging upside down. "Let me go, you bureaucrat."

"I cannot," Gerald said. "The fine is pending.

If I release you, I am complicit in your violation.

And I do not allow complicity. It is unprofessional.

"

The scarred woman looked at the hanging rogue, then at Gerald. She sheathed her sword. Slowly. Deliberately.

"You're not a monster," she said.

"I am a Core," Gerald said. "I am also a certified municipal inspector. Those are not mutually exclusive categories."

The mage lowered his staff completely.

"Okay.

Look.

We're not here to fight you.

We're here because the raid beacon pointed here. We thought there was a boss.

A big one. We didn't expect... paperwork.

"

"The beacon was a trap," Gerald said. "I set it up to lure in high-level threats so I could issue mass citations.

But you are not high-level threats.

You are hazards. You are messy. You break things.

"

"We break things because that's what we do," the woman said.

She stepped closer. She didn't attack.

She examined the room, and she looked at the handrails Gerald had installed. She looked at the fire extinguishers mounted at every ten-foot interval. She looked at the emergency exit signs, glowing with a soft, compliant green light.

"This place," she said. "It's... safe."

"It is up to code," Gerald said.

"It's boring," the rogue groaned. "I'm stuck. I want to stab something."

"You can stab the air," Gerald said. "But you will not stab the infrastructure.

If you damage the support beams, the Citation Severity escalates from Civil to Criminal.

Criminal violations result in permanent deletion of your character's inventory.

Do you understand?

"

The mage looked at the woman.

"She's right. The debuff on the rogue isn't just physical.

It's status-based. I can't cast at him.

The System won't allow magic on a 'protected civic asset.'

"

The woman sat down on a pile of rubble. She pulled out a canteen. She took a drink. Then she offered it to Gerald.

He declined. He did not drink on duty.

"Why are you doing this?" she asked. "Most Cores want us to kill them. They want the blood.

They want the fear.

You want... compliance.

"

"Because fear is unpredictable," Gerald said.

"Compliance is measurable. If you follow the rules, you do not die. If you break the rules, you suffer penalties.

It is a fair exchange.

"

"It's insane," the mage said. "But the rogue is actually stuck. And I can't heal him. The System is treating him like a piece of furniture."

Gerald pulled a notebook from his pocket. He opened it. The pages were filled with handwritten notes, dated and timed. He flipped to a page titled *Adventurer Mitigation Protocols*.

"If you wish to remove your companion from restraint," Gerald said, "you must submit a Correction Plan. You must identify the structural damage you caused, propose a repair method, and provide materials for said repair."

The woman blinked.

"We don't have cement."

"You have mana," Gerald said.

"And you have time.

The System allows for a grace period of thirty minutes for minor repairs. After that, the Citation becomes permanent.

The rogue stays stuck. And you are banned from entering this dungeon for six months.

"

The rogue whimpered.

"Six months? I'll lose my rank."

"Then fix it," Gerald said. "Use your magic.

Reinforce the crack in the ceiling.

Use the rogue's dagger to chip away the loose concrete. It's a simple task. It requires no heroism.

Only effort."

The mage hesitated. He looked at the ceiling crack.

He looked at Gerald.

Then, slowly, he raised his staff. He didn't cast a fireball. He cast a hardening spell.

A pale yellow light wrapped around the fractured concrete. It solidified.

The crack stopped widening.

The rogue stopped thrashing. The invisible wires loosened. He dropped to the floor, rubbing his wrists.

"Did it work?" the woman asked.

Gerald looked at his System window.

**System Alert: Minor Violation Resolved.** **Correction Plan Accepted.** **Citation Downgraded to Warning.**

"The penalty is lifted," Gerald said. "You are free to leave. Or you can stay and inspect the ventilation system. I have a spare checklist."

The woman stood up, and she brushed dust off her armor. She looked at Gerald with a new expression.

It wasn't fear. It wasn't anger. It was something closer to respect.

Or perhaps, confusion mixed with respect.

"You're not like the others," she said.

"No," Gerald agreed. "I am not."

"Why?"

"Because the others rely on chaos," Gerald said.

"Chaos is inefficient. It requires constant energy to maintain. Order is self-sustaining.

If the dungeon is up to code, it requires less mana to maintain.

That mana can be used for other things. Like... upgrades.

"

The mage's eyes widened.

"Upgrades? You mean you're not just defending? You're improving?"

"Improvement is the only defense," Gerald said.

"A dungeon that is well-maintained is a dungeon that is durable. A durable dungeon attracts better adventurers. Better adventurers bring better loot.

Better loot funds better code compliance. It is a cycle.

"

The woman laughed. It was a short, sharp sound.

"You're running a business."

"I am running a public service," Gerald corrected. "But the economics are unavoidable."

Before the woman could respond, the air in the room changed. The temperature dropped. The light from the exit signs flickered.

Gerald looked at his watch.

"Dawn," he said.

The sirens outside stopped. Silence rushed in, heavy and suffocating.

The System window in the center of Gerald's vision expanded. It was no longer a small notification. It was a full-screen interface.

**System Alert: Inspector General Audit Initiated.** **Auditor: Magistrate Thorne.** **Status: Active.** **Challenge: Comprehensive Structural Review.**

The floor beneath them shook. Not from an earthquake. From weight. Heavy, iron-shod boots marching in unison.

The doors to the sub-basement, already broken, were forced open again. But this time, there were no adventurers.

A figure stepped through, and he wore a long coat of black leather, adorned with silver badges.

He carried no sword. He carried a clipboard.

His face was hidden behind a mask of polished steel.

"Mr. Hutchins," the figure said.

His voice was amplified, echoing off the concrete walls.

"You have filed three hundred and twelve citations in the last forty-eight hours. You have cited two Auditor-class entities. You have reclassified the Library Sub-Basement as a 'Mixed-Use Commercial Zone' without a variance permit."

The adventurer party backed away. The mage whispered, "Run."

The woman shook her head.

"No. Watch."

Gerald stepped forward. He held up his own clipboard. His hands did not shake.

"Magistrate Thorne," Gerald said.

"I have prepared a counter-argument. I have also prepared a request for a variance permit.

Form 22-C.

It is attached.

"

The Magistrate stopped. He tilted his head. The steel mask caught the light.

"Form 22-C is for temporary event zones," Thorne said. "You are running a permanent dungeon."

"I am running a permanent public attraction with regulated entry points and fee-based access," Gerald said. "It falls under Section 9, Subsection D: 'Novelty Establishments.' I am operating within the legal gray area you created when you failed to update the zoning laws after the apocalypse."

Thorne went still. The air grew colder.

"You are invoking the Pre-Apocalypse Municipal Code," Thorne said.

"I am invoking the only code that has not been overwritten," Gerald said. "The System follows the laws you wrote. If you break them, you lose jurisdiction."

Thorne took a step forward. The floor cracked under his boot.

"Precedent will be established," Thorne said. "And you will be deleted."

"Try it," Gerald said. He clicked his pen.

"But note that deleting a registered civic official requires a two-thirds vote of the remaining Cores.

And I have citations against forty-seven of them, and they are on my side. Not because they like me.

But because I am the only one who can tell them how to keep their fire exits clear.

"

Thorne looked at the adventurer party. The woman stepped forward.

"He's right," she said. "The fire code in the North District is a mess.

Half the Cores are burning down their own spawns because of ventilation errors.

Hutchins fixed it. Or he tried to.

"

Thorne's mask turned toward her.

"You are interfering with a System Audit."

"I am citing you for obstruction," the woman said. She pulled a small slip of paper from her pocket. It was blank. But the System recognized her intent.

A red box appeared over Thorne.

**System Alert: External Citation Attempted.** **Target: Magistrate Thorne.** **Violation: Abuse of Process.** **Status: Pending Review.**

Gerald smiled. It was a small, tight smile. It did not reach his eyes.

"Welcome to the hearing, Magistrate," Gerald said. "I have prepared the evidence. And I have brought witnesses."

The Magistrate did not move.

But the air around him warped. The System was processing the contradiction.

A Core citing an Auditor.

An adventurer citing an Auditor.

The timer started.

60 seconds.

Gerald looked at the woman.

"Keep talking," he said.

"Keep citing.

Keep confusing the system, which has to process the logic. And logic is where I live.

"

The woman nodded. She turned to the mage.

"Cite him for excessive force.

The way he walked in. He broke the door.

That's destruction of city property.

"

The mage raised his staff. He didn't cast magic. He wrote in the air with light. A citation.

The rogue looked at Gerald.

"Can I cite him for being rude?"

"Yes," Gerald said. "If you can prove it caused emotional distress to a protected species."

The rogue grinned.

"I am a protected species. I have a disability. His silence caused me anxiety."

The timer ticked down. 50 seconds. 49.

Gerald watched the Magistrate. The figure's shoulders slumped slightly. The black coat rippled. The silver badges glowed brighter, then dimmed.

The System was glitching. Not breaking. Glitching.

It was trying to reconcile the old world's laws with the new world's magic.

And Gerald Hutchins, a man who had spent thirty-one years finding the tiny, microscopic discrepancies in paperwork, was standing right in the middle of the glitch.

**System Alert: Conflict Detected.** **Resolution Required: Manual Override.** **Who Has Authority?**

The question hung in the air.

Gerald looked at the Magistrate. He looked at the adventurers. He looked at the System window.

"Authority," Gerald said, "belongs to the one who holds the stamp."

He held up his ink pad. It was empty. But the System recognized the symbol.

The Magistrate lunged.
