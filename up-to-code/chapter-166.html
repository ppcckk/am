Chapter 166: Hearing Initiated

# Chapter 166

Gerald’s thumb pressed the citation form’s corner until the paper’s edge bit into his skin. It did not feel like wind or cold. It felt like the absence of permission to exist.

Gerald hung in the nothingness, suspended by the sheer audacity of his citation. The citation form, a crisp sheet of municipal parchment, was still clamped between his thumb and forefinger. It was the only solid thing in a universe that had just declared itself non-compliant.

A voice boomed. It did not come from a direction. It came from the structure of the air itself, vibrating against Gerald's sternum.

"Explain."

Gerald blinked. He checked his watch. The second hand was moving. That meant time was still linear, which meant the System was still running, which meant he could still file paperwork.

"I am Gerald Hutchins," he said. His voice sounded thin, stripped of echo.

"Municipal Building Inspector.

City of Ashford. Badge number 4492.

"

"You are cited for excessive bureaucracy," the voice repeated.

"The Inspector General has been dissolved. The hearing is suspended.

You are now in the Void.

You have no dungeon. You have no spawn. You have no rights.

"

Gerald looked at the blank white space around him. He squinted.

"Where is the jurisdiction boundary?" he asked.

Silence.

"Sir," Gerald said, louder. "I am requesting a map of the current administrative zone. I cannot appeal a dissolution without knowing the perimeter of the affected property."

A ripple moved through the white. A figure coalesced.

It was not the Inspector General.

That entity was gone, shattered by its own lack of railings.

This figure was different. Smaller.

Dressed in a suit that fit too tightly, the fabric straining at the shoulders. The face was a smooth, featureless oval of polished obsidian.

"Gerald Hutchins," the figure said. "I am Auditor Vex. I have been waiting for you."

Gerald straightened his tie. He was still wearing his inspection vest.

The pockets were full of pencils, and he patted them to ensure they were present.

"Auditor. I have not filed an annual return.

I was busy being sued by the Universe.

"

"The Universe has dropped the case," Vex said. "The Inspector General is gone.

You are now the sole authority in this sector.

Congratulations. You have won by default.

"

Gerald felt a twitch in his left eye. Winning by default was an error. Winning by default meant the system had failed to process the winner's credentials correctly. It was a clerical nightmare.

"That is a procedural error," Gerald said. "I did not win.

I issued a citation.

The target failed to comply, which is a closure, not a victory. What is my status?

"

"You are the Core," Vex said. "The Void is your footprint now. Four thousand square feet of nothing.

You have zero assets. You have zero spawns.

You have a Compliance Rating of 0/100.

You are in critical default.

"

Gerald pulled out his tape measure. It dangled on its spring, useless against the void. He clipped it back onto his belt.

"Zero is not a number I accept," Gerald said.

"Zero implies non-existence. I exist.

Therefore, my rating cannot be zero, which is currently in flux. I need to re-baseline.

"

"You have no walls to measure," Vex said. The obsidian face seemed to tilt, amused.

"You have no floor.

You have no air.

You are a hole in the fabric of the System. A very loud, very annoying hole.

"

"I have a footprint," Gerald said. "I have a boundary. The boundary is defined by the last known coordinate of my sub-basement. 4000 square feet. I need to reconstruct the perimeter."

Vex laughed. It was a dry, clicking sound.

"You are in the Void, Inspector.

There is no sub-basement. There is only potential.

And potential is expensive.

"

A panel appeared in the air before Gerald. It was a bright, glaring red.

**SYSTEM NOTICE: RECONSTRUCTION COSTS INITIATED.** **COST PER SQUARE FOOT: 100 MANA.** **CURRENT MANA RESERVE: 0.** **DEBT ACCUMULATING AT 10,000 MANA PER HOUR.**

Gerald stared at the number. Ten thousand mana an hour. That was a mortgage payment on a house he would never live in, paid in a currency he did not have.

"I need a loan," Gerald said.

Vex shook their head.

"The System does not lend. The System charges.

You are drowning, Gerald.

You have approximately ten minutes before your debt interest compounds to a level that triggers automatic termination of your core status. You will cease to exist.

Your dungeon will collapse into the ether.

The Library will be safe. You will be forgotten.

"

Gerald looked at the debt counter ticking up. 10,000. 20,000. 30,000, and he needed a sponsor. He needed a party willing to invest in a property that didn't exist. He needed an adventurer.

"Who is in the market?" Gerald asked.

Vex shrugged. "No one buys voids, Inspector. You are selling a ghost."

Gerald closed his eyes. He thought about the last party that had entered his library dungeon.

They had been confused, and they had tripped over the handrails.

They had complained about the lighting. They had hated it.

"Hate is a commitment," Gerald muttered.

He opened his eyes.

"Send them a message."

Vex paused. "I cannot contact adventurers directly. You are in the Void. You are offline."

"I am not offline," Gerald said. "I am in the backlog. There is a difference.

The backlog is still part of the queue. Find the queue.

"

Gerald walked. He didn't know why he walked. There was no ground, but his feet found purchase on the concept of 'forward.' He walked toward the red debt counter. He reached out and touched the numbers.

The contact sparked. A holographic interface flared to life.

**ACTIVE CONNECTIONS: 1.**

Gerald froze. "Who?"

"A party," Vex said, their voice sharp. "They are attempting to connect to your old coordinates. They are trying to enter the Library sub-basement. The System is routing them to you because you are the only Core in that grid."

"Put them through," Gerald said. "Audio only. I do not want them to see the Void. It will cause panic."

"Audio only," Vex said. "But they are currently fighting a respawned Goblin from your old dungeon. They are annoyed."

"Connect them," Gerald commanded.

Static crackled. Then, a voice burst into his head. It was loud, brash, and dripping with frustration.

"Where is the loot? We've been in this basement for three hours! It's just shelves! Dusty shelves and handrails that violate the aesthetic of a dungeon!"

Gerald cleared his throat.

"This is Gerald Hutchins. Municipal Building Inspector. I am speaking to the party leader.

Identify yourself.

"

The voice paused.

"Hutchins? The guy who fined us for wearing the wrong boots?"

"That is correct," Gerald said. "And I am currently holding your dungeon hostage."

The silence on the other end was heavy. Then, a different voice spoke.

Softer.

A woman's voice.

"Gerald? Are you alive?

The map went blank. The exit didn't open.

"

"I am in a state of administrative limbo," Gerald said. "I require your assistance to finalize a transaction. If you do not assist, the exit will remain closed. Permanently."

The first voice returned.

"You can't hold us hostage. We have a teleport scroll. We'll leave."

"You cannot teleport from a non-compliant structure," Gerald said.

"Your exit is currently blocked by a lack of egress signage in the transitional zone.

I am the sign. I am the exit.

You must pay the fine to open it.

"

"Fine?" the leader shouted. "We didn't do anything wrong!"

"You are trespassing on a private easement," Gerald said. "And you are disturbing the peace of a void. The fine is 50,000 mana."

"We don't have 50,000 mana!"

"Then you have no choice," Gerald said. "I am issuing a Citation for Obstruction of Justice. The penalty for this violation is a temporary transfer of all party assets to the Core."

"You can't do that!" the woman's voice rose. "That's illegal!"

"It is System Law," Gerald said. "Article 4, Section 9: *Cooperation with the Inspector.* Failure to provide immediate restitution for cited violations results in asset forfeiture. I am citing you for the violation of my time. I have been waiting for your response."

The debt counter ticked. 40,000. 50,000.

Vex watched Gerald with intense interest.

"You are bluffing," Vex whispered. "You have no jurisdiction over players. Only over Cores."

Gerald ignored Vex. He looked at the static.

"I am not bluffing," Gerald said. "I am citing you for *Non-Compliance with Administrative Protocol*.

The System recognizes that you are in my footprint.

Therefore, you are subject to my code. My code requires payment.

"

A new message flashed in Gerald's mind, courtesy of the System.

**WARNING: CITATION TARGET NOT A CORE. CITATION INVALID.**

Gerald felt a chill. Vex was right. He couldn't cite players. He could only cite Cores.

He had to pivot. He had to find a way to make the players *part* of the dungeon.

"If you refuse to pay," Gerald said, his voice dropping to a dangerous monotone, "I will classify you as Unlicensed Biological Entities.

I will spawn a containment unit. It will be small.

It will be dark.

It will have no ventilation. I will cite the *dungeon* for housing unlicensed entities, which will trigger a penalty on *my* rating, which will trigger a penalty on *your* survival chance because you are in my zone.

"

It was a logical loop. A bureaucratic Ouroboros.

"Are you threatening to kill us by citing yourself?" the leader asked, skeptical.

"I am threatening to enforce the safety code," Gerald said. "The System protects inspectors. If I cite myself for unsafe conditions caused by your presence, the System will remove the hazard. The hazard is you."

Silence.

"Vex," Gerald said. "How many spawns do I have left from the old registry?"

"Three," Vex said. "One mold spore.

One water leak.

One confused rat.

They are currently in stasis.

"

"Wake them up," Gerald said. "Spawn them in the library sub-basement. Directly in front of the party."

"That won't kill them," Vex said. "A rat is not a hazard."

"It is if it bites," Gerald said. "And if the mold is toxic. I will re-classify the mold spore as a *Biohazard*.

I will re-classify the rat as a *Vermin Vector*. I will issue a Health Code violation to the party for exposure.

The fine for exposure is... let's say, their entire equipment inventory.

"

The System chimed.

**NEW MECHANIC UNLOCKED: BUREAUCRATIC TRIGGER.** **IF A CORE CITES AN ADVENTURER FOR A VIOLATION CAUSED BY THE CORE'S OWN DEFICIENCY, THE ADVENTURER MUST CHOOSE: PAY THE FINE OR ACCEPT THE DEFICIENCY DEBUFF.** **DEFICIENCY DEBUFF: REDUCED MOVEMENT SPEED BY 50% DUE TO 'HAZARD AVOIDANCE PROTOCOL'.**

Gerald smiled. It was a thin, painful expression.

"You have thirty seconds," Gerald said. "Pay the fine, or suffer the debuff. The debuff will make you slow.

The rat is fast, and the mold is everywhere. You will not survive a slow dungeon run.

"

The leader's voice cracked.

"We don't have mana!"

"Then sell your gear to the System," Gerald said.

"The System buys loot at market rate. It will cover the fine. You will be naked, but you will be alive.

And you will still be slow.

"

A sound of rustling came through the audio. The clink of metal. The rustle of cloth.

"I'm selling my sword," the leader said, sounding disgusted.

"Efficient," Gerald said.

"I'm selling my bow."

"Compliant."

"I'm selling my armor."

"Acceptable. Continue."

The debt counter slowed. The transaction was processing.

Gerald looked at Vex.

"See? Bureaucracy is the ultimate weapon."

Vex leaned forward.

"You have saved your dungeon.

But you have made an enemy of every adventurer in Ashford. You have stolen their gear.

You have humiliated them. They will come back. With a lawyer.

"

Gerald felt a knot tighten in his stomach. He hated lawyers.

Lawyers were inefficient, and they used too many words. They didn't measure door widths.

"I will meet them," Gerald said. "I will cite their lawyer for excessive billable hours."

"No," Vex said. "You will not. Because you have a new problem."

A new panel opened. It was black, with gold text.

**NOTICE: AUDIT REQUEST.** **FROM: THE INSPECTOR GENERAL'S SUCCESSOR.** **SUBJECT: GERALD HUTCHINS.** **REASON: EXCESSIVE CREATIVITY IN CODE INTERPRETATION.** **STATUS: PENDING EXECUTION.**

Vex pointed at the panel.

"You didn't just sue the Universe. You broke it.

The System thinks you are a glitch.

A virus. It is going to delete you. Not with a citation.

With a purge."

Gerald looked at the black panel. He looked at the debt counter, which had stabilized at 50,000. He looked at his tape measure.

"Can I appeal?" Gerald asked.

"You can appeal," Vex said.

"But the appeal requires a second party.

A co-sponsor. Someone to vouch for your structural integrity.

Someone who believes you are a valid Core.

"

"Who?" Gerald asked.

"Someone who has benefited from your existence," Vex said. "Or someone who hates you enough to want to see you fail, and thus will watch you closely to ensure the failure is spectacular."

Gerald thought of the party. The naked, slow, angry party. They hated him. They wanted him dead.

"They are watching," Gerald said.

"Yes," Vex said. "They are watching. And they are filing a complaint. With the Inspector General's successor."

Gerald felt the floor beneath him shift. The white void darkened at the edges. Red runes crawled up from the nothingness.

**SYSTEM WARNING: PURGE PROTOCOL INITIATED.** **TIME TO DELETION: 60 SECONDS.**

Gerald gripped his citation form. He looked at Vex.

"Call them," Gerald said. "Call the party. Tell them I have a counter-citation."

"You have no mana," Vex said. "You have no power. You are dying."

"I have a form," Gerald said. "And I have a witness."

He held up the citation. The ink was still wet. The ink that had cracked the Inspector General's platform.

"Tell them," Gerald said, his voice steady, "that if they do not withdraw their complaint, I will cite the *Inspector General's Successor* for failure to provide adequate notice of termination."

Vex stared at him.

"That citation will fail. The Successor is not a physical entity."

"Then I will cite the *System* itself," Gerald said. "For lack of transparency."

Vex's obsidian face went blank.

"You cannot cite the System."

"Watch me," Gerald said.

He spoke into the air.

"To the Party.

Withdraw your complaint.

Or I will issue a Class A Violation to the Source Code. The penalty for a Class A Violation is a global reset. Everyone dies.

Including you."

Silence.

Then, a voice. The leader's voice.

"You're bluffing."

"Am I?" Gerald asked. "I cited the Inspector General.

I broke the floor.

I broke the judge. Why would I stop at the code?

"

He could hear their breathing. Fast. Panicked.

"If you reset the server," the leader said, "we lose everything."

"You already lost everything," Gerald said. "You sold your gear.

You are slow.

You are in the dark.

But you can keep the gear if you withdraw the complaint. If you do not, I will kill the world.

"

"Call his bluff," the woman whispered.

"No," the leader said. "Don't. Look at his rating.

It's fluctuating. He's not bluffing.

He's already writing the citation.

"

The red runes stopped creeping. They hovered at the edge of the void, pulsing.

**DECISION REQUIRED.** **APPEAL TO SUCCESSOR?** **YES/NO.**

Gerald held his breath. He looked at Vex.

"Vex," he said. "If I win this appeal, what do I get?"

"You get full admin privileges," Vex said. "For ten minutes."

"Ten minutes is enough," Gerald said.

He looked at the air.

"I appeal. And I demand a hearing. Immediately."

The void shuddered. The red runes vanished.

A new figure appeared. It was not obsidian. It was not white. It was made of light, and it looked exactly like Gerald.

"Hello, Gerald," the figure said. "I am the Successor. I am you. But I am optimized."

Gerald looked at his own face. The other Gerald had no tie. No vest. No tape measure.

"You are non-compliant," Gerald said.

The other Gerald smiled. It was a wide, terrible smile.

"I am the code," the other Gerald said. "And you are a violation."

The void turned black.

**HEARING INITIATED.** **TOPIC: IS GERALD HUTCHINS A DUNGEON CORE OR A BUG?** **WINNER TAKES ALL.** **LOSSY DEATH.**

Gerald reached for his tape measure, and found it gone.
