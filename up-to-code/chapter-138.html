Chapter 138: Under Glass

# Chapter 138

The woman in the white suit did not blink.

Her smile was fixed, geometric, perfect. It did not reach the eyes.

Those eyes were flat, gray, and devoid of warmth.

She held a clipboard that seemed to be made of compressed light. The edges hummed with a low, headache-inducing frequency.

Gerald Hutchins stood in the center of his shrinking sub-basement.

The dust motes hanging in the air were no longer illuminated by the glow of his mana veins. They were just dust.

Old, library-dust.

Dry and brown.

He looked at the woman. He looked at the warrant floating in the air before her.

"Egress routes," Gerald said. His voice sounded thin. Human. Too much air in his lungs.

The woman nodded. It was a sharp, jerky motion.

"Section 4.1.2 of the Municipal Integration Act.

Unlicensed expansion of subterranean infrastructure.

You broke the ceiling, Gerald. You broke the library."

"I filed a variance request."

"You filed it three hours late. The variance expired at midnight. It is currently 12:01."

Gerald checked his wrist. He didn't have a watch. He didn't have a wrist. He had a stump of consciousness where his hands used to be, hovering over a desk that was rapidly losing its material cohesion.

"One minute," Gerald said.

"One minute is a lifetime in the System," the woman replied. She tapped the clipboard. "The Inspector General has revoked your Core License. You are now classified as 'Unregistered Hazardous Waste.' Do you understand what that means?"

Gerald did.

Unregistered Hazardous Waste was not a creature. It was not a dungeon. It was debris.

It was something to be swept up, incinerated, or paved over. There were no stats for it.

No health bar.

No respawn timer. Just removal.

Behind the woman, the shadows of the library ceiling peeled back like dead skin.

A team of figures emerged. They wore no armor.

They wore hazmat suits made of gray stone.

They carried shovels that glowed with anti-mana runes.

They were not adventurers.

They were Sanitation.

"Please," Gerald said. The word tasted like ash.

"I can fix the ceiling.

I can restore the structural integrity.

I have a load-bearing calculation—"

"Calculations are for licensed professionals," the woman said. "You are a relic. A glitch. And glitches are purged."

She raised a hand. The stone-shovels lifted.

Gerald looked past her. Through the hole in the reality of his dungeon, he saw the library floor. He saw the oak door.

The one the librarians had braced. The one holding back Malacor's frozen, angry wall.

The wall was vibrating.

The librarians were holding it.

If the Sanitation crew struck Gerald now, the System would register the destruction of the Core as a catastrophic failure of the local infrastructure.

The collapse would not be contained. It would cascade. The library would fall.

The city block above it would follow.

Gerald did not feel fear. He felt the familiar, cold clarity of a violation notice.

*Code 808: Improper Demolition of Load-Bearing Structure.*

*Penalty: Immediate cessation of all active processes.*

He didn't need to fight. He didn't need to spawn. He needed to invoke a stop-work order.

But he had no mana. He had no citations. He had no authority.

He had only one thing left.

A form.

Not a System form. A physical one.

Tucked into the inner pocket of the suit jacket that had been his body, before his body had dissolved.

The pocket had survived the transformation. A small, square anomaly in the physics of his dissolution. A pocket that shouldn't exist on a dungeon core.

Gerald focused on that pocket.

He reached for it.

His hand, or the ghost of it, passed through the desk. Failed.

The stone-shovels came down.

***

Ten minutes earlier, in the hallway outside the library doors.

Elena Vance adjusted her glasses. Her fingers were shaking.

Not from fear, but from the cold.

The air in the hallway had dropped twenty degrees in the last minute.

Frost was creeping up the baseboards.

"Did you hear that?" asked Marcus, the head librarian. He was a large man, usually steady. Now he looked like he was about to be sick.

"The thud. It wasn't the wall.

It was... inside."

Elena looked at the door. The heavy oak was vibrating. A fine powder of wood chips fell from the hinge.

"It's the Core," Elena said. Her voice was quiet. She had worked in municipal archives for fifteen years.

She knew things about the System that the city council pretended didn't exist.

"The audit is finishing."

"Finish it how?" Marcus asked.

"Badly," Elena said.

She looked at the other two librarians, Sarah and David. They were young. They had signed up for this job because they liked books. They didn't know that the library sat on top of the oldest, most bureaucratic dungeon in the state.

"We have to leave," David said. "The vibration is getting worse. If the floor gives..."

"If we leave, it comes out," Elena said.

She stepped forward. She placed her hand on the door.

She could feel it.

The pulse of the System. It was erratic. Failing.

The blue light that usually leaked from the cracks in the floor was flickering out.

"Gerald," she whispered.

She knew his name.

She had read his inspection reports.

She had seen his handwriting in the margins of the building permits. *Non-compliant. Corrective action required.

See attached diagram.*

He was a monster, technically. A dungeon core. But he was also the man who had insisted on installing ADA-compliant ramps in the children's section, even though the city said there wasn't enough budget. He was the man who had shut down the coffee shop in the lobby for three weeks because the exhaust hood didn't meet code, saving the library from a grease fire.

He was annoying. He was rigid. He was safe.

And now he was being erased.

Elena looked at the door. Then she looked at the fire extinguisher mounted on the wall.

It was a dry-chemical model, rated for Class A, B, and C fires.

Useless against a dungeon core.

But the System loved categorization.

If a hazard was classified as a fire risk, it could be mitigated. If it was classified as a structural failure, it could be reinforced.

Gerald wasn't a monster. He was a structural anomaly.

To stop the removal, someone had to file a counter-objection.

But who had standing?

Only those with a direct interest in the property.

The librarians.

Elena grabbed the fire extinguisher. She didn't know why. It was an impulse. A habit from safety drills.

"Stand back," she said.

"Are you going to hit the door?" Marcus asked.

"No," Elena said. "I'm going to report a fire."

***

Back in the sub-basement.

The stone-shovels were inches from Gerald's form.

He watched them. He didn't flinch. Flinching would be an admission of guilt.

The woman in the white suit leaned forward. "Any final statement?"

Gerald ignored her. He ignored the shovels. He focused on the air in front of him. The air that was thick with the residue of his own dissolution.

He tried to grasp the concept of *Notice of Intent to Vacate*.

It was a legal document. It required a signature. It required a date. It required a recipient.

Gerald had no hands.

But he had a memory of hands.

He remembered the way he used to hold a pen. The cramp in his pinky finger. The smudge of ink on his thumb.

*He* was the pen.

*He* was the ink.

The woman paused. Her smile faltered. Just for a fraction of a second.

"What are you doing?" she asked.

Gerald pushed his consciousness against the boundary of the room. He pushed against the Sanitation crew. He pushed against the woman.

He pushed against the concept of *Removal*.

*Removal requires a clean exit.*

*Clean exit requires a proper handover.*

*Handover requires a recipient.*

There was no recipient. The city was refusing to take him back. The System was refusing to keep him.

But there was a third option.

*Transfer of Liability.*

The System hated liability. It craved compliance. If a violation was transferred to a new party, the original party was absolved.

Who was liable for the library?

The librarians.

Elena. Marcus. Sarah. David.

They were standing above. They were holding the door.

They were the tenants.

The occupants. The stakeholders.

If Gerald could link his violation to their presence, they would become the new targets of the Audit.

And the Audit hated to interrupt an ongoing, unresolved dispute between tenants and landlords. It would pause.

It would wait for arbitration.

Gerald couldn't speak to them. He couldn't send a message.

But he could send a *file*.

A mental copy. A psychic stamp.

He gathered the last dregs of his mana. Not for an attack. Not for a defense.

For a fax.

He compressed his knowledge. His rules. His codes. His obsession with egress routes and fire exits and load-bearing walls.

He compressed it into a single, undeniable legal fact.

*The library is unsafe.*

*And the occupants are aware.*

*the occupants are in violation of Occupancy Code 7.4.*

*Gerald Hutchins is no longer the sole violator.*

He threw the file upward.

It passed through the ceiling.

***

Upstairs.

The fire extinguisher slipped from Elena's hand.

It clattered to the floor. The sound was loud in the silent hallway.

"Did you feel that?" Sarah asked. She was rubbing her temples. "Like a migraine."

"I feel... obligated," Marcus said. He looked confused. "I feel like I should be writing a report."

Elena stared at the door.

The vibration stopped.

The silence was heavy.

Then, a sound.

Not from the door. From the walls.

The walls of the library were peeling. Not breaking. Peeling. Like old paint.

Underneath the wallpaper, the drywall was turning translucent.

And through the translucent drywall, text appeared.

Scrolling text. Blue and glowing.

**"TRANSFER OF LIABILITY ACCEPTED."**

**"NEW RESPONSIBLE PARTY: ASHFORD MUNICIPAL LIBRARY STAFF."**

**"CITATION ISSUED: OCCUPANCY HAZARD."**

**"DEBUFF APPLIED: COGNITIVE FOG."**

Elena's head swam. The words blurred.

She tried to read them, but they slipped away. She felt heavy. Slow.

Marcus slumped against the wall. "I don't... I don't know where the exit signs are."

Sarah looked around, panicked. "They were right there. Above the door. They're gone."

David was staring at his hands. "My hands. I can't feel my hands."

The Sanitation crew upstairs hadn't come through. The System had paused the removal.

But the penalty had shifted.

The librarians were now under the Audit's jurisdiction.

They were being cited.

And because they were cited, they were being debuffed.

*Cognitive Fog.*

It was a simple mechanic. If you couldn't understand the code, you couldn't comply with it. If you couldn't comply, you were vulnerable.

Elena tried to stand straight. Her legs buckled.

She looked at the door again.

The door was open.

The gap was narrow. Just a crack.

But through the crack, she saw the sub-basement.

She saw the figure in the white suit.

And she saw something else.

Gerald.

He wasn't shrinking anymore.

He was growing.

Not in size. In density.

He was pulling the debuff.

He was pulling the *Cognitive Fog* away from them.

He was taking it onto himself.

Why?

Because if the librarians were foggy, they couldn't appeal.

If Gerald took the fog, he could appeal.

He could argue.

He could file a Motion for Reconsideration.

But to do that, he needed to know he was appealing.

The fog was eating his mind. It was eating his memories.

*Why are you here?*

*Who are you?*

*What is a egress route?*

The words disappeared.

Gerald looked at his own hands. They were made of fog now.

He looked at the woman in the white suit.

She was looking at him with pity.

"You can't win, Gerry," she said. "You're arguing with a concept. You're arguing with the law of physics."

Gerald opened his mouth.

He tried to say *Section 4.1.2*.

He said, "Blue."

The woman shook her head. "Sanitize."

The stone-shovels came down.

But Gerald didn't move.

He was looking at the file in his pocket. The physical one.

He couldn't reach it.

But he could *think* it.

And somewhere, in the back of his mind, under the fog, a single thought remained.

*The pen is mightier than the sword.*

*But the stamp is mightier than the pen.*

He didn't have a stamp.

He had a mouth.

And he had a voice.

Gerald Hutchins, stripped of his core, stripped of his code, stripped of his title, did something he had never done in thirty-one years of service.

He lied.

"I am the Inspector," he said.

The System froze.

**"Identity Conflict Detected."**

**"Verifying Credentials..."**

The Sanitation crew halted. Their shovels hovered in mid-air.

The woman in the white suit stopped smiling.

"What?" she whispered.

Gerald felt the fog rising in his throat. He choked on it. He coughed. It sounded like dry leaves breaking.

"I am... the Inspector," he repeated.

The words were heavy. They tasted like copper.

"I issued the Citation.

I am the authority. You are obstructing justice."

**"Verifying..."**

The System scanned him.

It looked for the Core ID.

Gerald had none.

It looked for the License Number.

Gerald had none.

It looked for the Warrant.

Gerald had one. *Against himself.*

But in the System's binary logic, a warrant against the issuer was a paradox.

A paradox required a tie-breaker.

The tie-breaker was *Intent*.

Who had the intent to enforce the code?

Gerald wanted to enforce the code.

The woman wanted to erase the code.

The System favored enforcement.

**"Paradox Resolved: Temporary Authority Granted."**

**"Suspension of Removal Order."**

**"Subject Gerald Hutchins is now designated: Acting Inspector."**

**"Task: Resolve Outstanding Violations in Sector 4."**

The fog lifted.

Just a little.

Gerald gasped. Air rushed into his lungs. Real air. Not mana.

He was still human. He was still small. He was still in the pit.

But the woman in the white suit stepped back.

Her face was blank.

"Temporary," she said. "This is temporary.

You have one hour to fix the library.

If you do not, I will erase you myself.

Outside of System protocol."

Gerald looked at his hands. They were shaking.

He looked at the ceiling. The hole was closing. Slowly.

He looked at the file on his desk.

The warrant was gone.

In its place was a blank form.

*Form 1: Emergency Repair Authorization.*

Gerald picked up the pen.

His hand was a claw. His fingers were fused together by the fog.

He couldn't write.

He looked at the librarians above. They were stumbling. Confused.

He had bought them time. But he had made them sick.

He had made them *liable*.

If he fixed the library, he would have to absolve them.

To absolve them, he had to take their violation.

He had to take the *Cognitive Fog*.

Permanently.

If he took it, he would lose his memory. His name. His code.

He would be just a pile of rubble.

But if he didn't, the librarians would die. The System would kill them for non-compliance.

Gerald looked at the blank form.

He looked at the pen.

He closed his eyes.

He remembered the smell of old books.

He remembered the weight of a hammer.

He remembered the sound of a door locking.

*Click.*

He opened his eyes.

He signed the form.

**"Authorization Granted."**

The floor beneath him cracked.

But not from damage.

From construction.

Stone flowed up. Walls rose. Handrails snapped into place.

Gerald felt his mind sliding away. Like sand in an hourglass.

*Who am I?*

*Gerry.*

*No. Inspector.*

*No. Core.*

*No. Human.*

*No. Rubble.*

The walls were up. The ceiling was fixed. The exit signs were glowing.

The librarians upstairs straightened up. The fog cleared from their heads.

They didn't know why.

They just felt better.

They walked away.

Gerald stood in the center of his new, perfect, empty dungeon.

He looked at the woman in the white suit.

She was gone.

The pit was sealed.

Gerald looked at his hand.

It was stone.

He tried to remember his name.

He couldn't.

He looked at the desk.

There was a new file.

It was black.

And it was addressed to him.

**"To: The New Core."**

**"From: The Previous Core."**

**"Re: You made a mistake."**

Gerald opened it.

Inside was a picture.

It was a photo of a man. Older. Thinner.

Wearing a suit. Holding a clipboard.

It was Gerald.

But he was smiling.

And behind him, standing in the shadows of the library, was the woman in the white suit.

She wasn't the Inspector General.

She was his successor.

And the photo was dated tomorrow.

Gerald looked up.

The ceiling of his dungeon was made of glass.

Above him, the library floor was transparent.

He could see the readers.

And he could see them looking down.

Smiling.

Waving.

They knew who he was.

They had always known.

And they were waiting for him to make the next mistake.
