Chapter 16

The paladin's warhammer hit the load-bearing wall and Gerald watched his Compliance Rating tick down by two points.

He had forty-seven seconds to fix it before the damage registered as a maintenance failure. The System attributed all structural damage inside a dungeon to the Core's upkeep obligations. Heroes were not exempt. The System did not care that a Level 30 paladin had the right to swing a two-hundred-pound hammer at a dungeon wall. The System cared that the wall was now cracked, and the Core responsible for that wall had not repaired it within the tolerance window.

Gerald redirected his mana channel toward the crack. Not repair. That would take too long. Instead, he carved a shallow channel along the fracture line and filled it with compressed stone dust. A cosmetic patch. The System's damage scanner read surface-level. It saw filled cracks and moved on.

It was a lie. A small one. Gerald felt the familiar nausea of improvisation, the way his stomach turned when he did something that wasn't in the manual. But the alternative was his Compliance Rating dropping below the threshold that would debuff every monster he'd spawned in the last three days. The goblins in the east corridor had already lost twelve percent of their regeneration. The slime puddles in the lower level were losing cohesion.

He could not afford another hit.

The paladin swung again. Gerald redirected the party's path by adjusting a pressure plate in the floor. The plate triggered a sliding door that closed off the corridor they'd been heading toward. The paladin turned, confused, and the party regrouped. Gerald had bought himself a corridor's worth of time. He sealed the damaged section behind them with a quick pour of molten stone. Not a repair. A concealment.

His Compliance Rating held at sixty-one. Barely above the threshold.

He needed to keep the heroes fighting. That was the whole point of letting them in. Active hero combat created a force majeure zone that blocked Skarn's counter-citation. But the zone had a boundary. The System defined it by the actual location of engaged combat, not the dungeon entrance. Gerald had known that going in. What he hadn't known was that the zone moved.

He watched the party's tracker dot crawl through his dungeon's first level. They were heading toward the goblin warren. Good. The goblins were weak, Level 8 at best. The fight would last maybe two minutes. Two minutes of force majeure. Then the zone would collapse and Skarn could walk to his entrance and serve the citation.

Gerald needed more than two minutes.

He spawned three new goblins in the corridor between the warren and the next chamber. Not strong ones. Level 5. They wouldn't kill anyone. They would just exist. The System counted any hostile engagement as active combat. Even a goblin with a rusty dagger counting as a combatant was enough to maintain the zone.

The party hit the new goblins. The tracker dot slowed. Gerald checked the timer. The force majeure zone was holding. Skarn was still locked out.

But Gerald had a new problem. Every goblin he spawned cost mana. Every hero swing that hit his walls cost Compliance Rating. He was spending resources to maintain a shield that would expire the moment the heroes left. And the heroes would leave. They always left. They'd clear the first level in maybe ten minutes, realize his dungeon was shallow, and exit.

He needed them to stay longer.

Gerald opened his dungeon layout and studied it. His corridors were narrow. His rooms were small. Everything was built to code, which meant everything was designed for efficient circulation. Efficient circulation meant heroes moved through fast. They followed the path of least resistance.

He could break the path. Not with traps. Traps would kill them, and dead heroes triggered a different penalty. He could break it with geometry.

He carved a new junction. A loop. A corridor that curved back on itself without being obvious. The heroes would walk it and think they were heading deeper. They'd spend an extra four minutes in a loop before realizing. Gerald felt the nausea again. Building something that served no purpose except to waste time. It was wrong. It violated the spirit of circulation design. But the System didn't care about the spirit. It cared about the letter.

The party hit the loop. The tracker dot slowed. Gerald checked his mana. He was at forty percent. The new goblins were dead. The paladin was now swinging at a different wall. Gerald patched it with compressed dust. His Rating dropped to fifty-eight.

Fifty-eight. The threshold was sixty. He was below it.

The debuff hit immediately. All spawned entities lost fifteen percent to all stats. The goblins he'd just spawned flickered, their health bars dropping. The slime puddles lost another layer of cohesion. Gerald felt the effect on himself too. A Core's stats were tied to its Compliance Rating. He felt sluggish. Slow. The mana channel in his left arm stuttered.

He needed to get back above sixty. Fast.

He scanned his dungeon for the fastest possible compliance gain. Fire code. He'd been meaning to add fire extinguishers. Not real ones. The System recognized "fire suppression apparatus" as any object that met a specific mana-density threshold and was mounted at a specific height. Gerald could carve a bracket, fill it with water mana, and mount it at sixty inches. Four of those would give him a two-point fire code bonus. Permanent.

He carved. The paladin hit another wall. Gerald didn't patch it. He couldn't afford the mana. Instead, he filed a variance. Section 7.3: "Structural damage sustained during active hero combat may be deferred for repair if the Core files a Combat Damage Exemption within thirty seconds of the damage event."

The exemption didn't fix the damage. It just paused the penalty. Gerald filed four exemptions in rapid succession. His Rating climbed back to sixty-one. The debuff lifted. The goblins steadied.

But he'd burned thirty percent of his remaining mana.

The party was still in the loop. Gerald checked the tracker. They'd been walking for six minutes. They hadn't found the exit yet. The loop was working. Gerald felt a small, cold satisfaction. The geometry was correct. The sightlines were misleading. The turning radius was just tight enough to create a subtle directional bias without triggering suspicion. It was good design. Even if its purpose was dishonest.

He checked Skarn's position. Still at the entrance. Still blocked. The counter-citation remained unserved. Gerald had time. Not much. Maybe another eight minutes before the heroes found the loop's exit and cleared the remaining goblins.

Eight minutes.

Gerald opened his dungeon scanner and pointed it at Skarn's territory. He'd been meaning to do this for days. The scanner had a range of two hundred yards. Skarn's dungeon was adjacent to Gerald's, separated by a thin wall of natural stone. The scanner could read structural data through the boundary.

Skarn's dungeon was bigger than Gerald expected. Three levels deep. More rooms. More corridors. And Gerald's eyes went straight to the violations.

Corridor width: thirty-four inches. Too narrow. Not a violation by itself, but combined with the occupancy load Skarn was running, it meant the corridor was below minimum egress capacity. That was a life safety violation.

Junction rooms: no turning radius. Same problem Gerald had solved twenty minutes ago. But Skarn hadn't solved his.

Fire suppression: none detected. Zero apparatus. Zero fire exits.

Electrical: Gerald scanned for mana conduit routing. Skarn's conduits were exposed. No protective sheathing. No grounding. That was a code violation so fundamental it bordered on negligence.

Gerald's hands shook. Not from fear. From recognition. These were the same violations he'd been writing citations for for twenty-two years. He knew the exact penalty values. He knew the exact language to use.

He started drafting.

The citation would be massive. Multiple violations. Life safety, fire code, electrical code, structural egress. The combined debuff would be proportional to the count. Skarn's entities would lose thirty, maybe forty percent of their stats. His Compliance Rating would crater.

But Gerald needed to serve it. Physical presence. He'd have to go to Skarn's entrance.

Which meant leaving his own dungeon. Which meant the heroes would finish clearing and exit. Which meant the force majeure zone would collapse. Which meant Skarn could serve his counter-citation while Gerald was gone.

A timing problem. Gerald ran through it. If he left now, he'd arrive at Skarn's entrance in about ninety seconds. He'd need thirty seconds to serve the citation. Then he'd need to get back before the heroes exited. Total round trip: four minutes. He had eight minutes. It was possible.

But the counter-citation. If Skarn served his counter-citation while Gerald was gone, the Fraudulent Variance Application would go through. Gerald had filed four Combat Damage Exemptions in the last two minutes. Were they fraudulent? They were technically valid. Section 7.3 allowed them. But Skarn was claiming they were filed in bad faith. The Hearing would examine intent.

Gerald paused. He looked at the exemptions he'd filed. Four in rapid succession. All filed within thirty seconds of damage events. All technically valid. But filed in a burst that suggested premeditation.

Skarn's counter-citation was clever. It didn't claim the exemptions were invalid. It claimed they were fraudulent. That was a different standard. Fraud required intent. The Hearing would ask: did Gerald file these exemptions because damage occurred, or did he file them because he knew damage was coming?

The answer was the latter. Gerald had known the paladin would hit the wall. He'd been watching the swing pattern. He'd pre-positioned his mana to file the exemption the instant contact occurred.

That was fraud. Gerald recognized it immediately. He'd seen it in real life. Contractors who pre-filed variance applications before inspections. The same trick. The same intent.

He couldn't win that Hearing.

Unless he could invalidate the counter-citation before it was served. Section 12.4: "A Citation is void if the filing Core is itself subject to a higher-severity Citation served before the original Citation's service deadline."

Higher severity. Skarn's counter-citation was a single violation. Fraudulent filing. Severity: moderate. Gerald's planned citation was four violations. Life safety plus three code categories. Severity: severe.

If Gerald could serve his citation on Skarn before Skarn served his counter-citation on Gerald, the counter-citation would be void.

The timing was razor thin. Gerald had eight minutes of force majeure. He needed to cross to Skarn's entrance, serve the citation, and return. But Skarn was standing at his own entrance, waiting. If Gerald walked into Skarn's territory to serve the citation, he'd be leaving his own dungeon. The force majeure zone would collapse the moment the heroes exited.

Gerald checked the tracker. The party was still in the loop. Six minutes and forty seconds. They'd found the exit. They were heading toward the final goblin cluster.

Three minutes, maybe.

Gerald made his decision. He grabbed the citation scroll. He moved toward his own entrance. He would walk to Skarn's entrance, serve the citation, and run back. The heroes would exit his dungeon during the walk. The force majeure zone would collapse. Skarn would be free to serve his counter-citation. But if Gerald served his citation first, even by one second, the counter-citation would be void.

One second.

Gerald moved fast. His Core's movement was slow. The debuff from the Compliance Rating dip had left residual effects. His mana channel felt heavy. He crossed the distance between his entrance and Skarn's entrance in what felt like an eternity. The stone between them was natural. Neither dungeon had carved into it. It was a neutral boundary.

He saw Skarn. The Core manifested as a cluster of dark crystal hovering above a pit of magma. Skarn's entrance was a wide archway carved into basalt. Skarn's crystal pulsed with something Gerald recognized as anticipation.

Gerald unrolled the citation scroll. He began to speak the service words.

Skarn moved. Not toward Gerald. Away. Toward his own entrance. Toward the neutral boundary. Toward Gerald's territory.

Skarn was trying to serve his counter-citation first. He was running the same play. Both Cores, racing to serve.

Gerald's voice faltered. The service words required continuous speech. If he stopped, the service failed. He kept talking. His eyes tracked Skarn's movement. The dark crystal was moving fast. Faster than Gerald could have predicted.

Skarn crossed the boundary. He was inside Gerald's dungeon now. He was heading for Gerald's entrance point. The counter-citation scroll was already in his crystal's grip.

Gerald kept speaking. The citation words poured out of him. He could feel the System registering each violation. Life safety. Fire code. Electrical. Structural egress. The severity was climbing.

Skarn reached Gerald's entrance. He began to speak.

Both Cores were serving at the same time. The System would compare timestamps. A difference of less than one second would be a tie. And a tie, according to Section 12.4, meant both citations were void.

Gerald heard the tie notification before Skarn did. He saw it in his peripheral vision. A single line of System text, green, small.

**CITATION SERVICE TIMED TIE. BOTH CITATIONS VOID. APPEAL WINDOW: 0. NO REMEDY AVAILABLE.**

Both citations were dead. Neither Core had won. Neither Core had lost. The counter-citation was void. Gerald's citation was void. Everything was reset.

But Gerald noticed something else. The System text had a second line. Smaller. Grey. He almost missed it.

**NOTICE: Core "Skarn" has been flagged for Inspector General audit. Flag triggered by citation service attempt. Audit window: 72 hours.**

The act of attempting to serve a citation, even a void one, triggered an automatic audit flag. Skarn would be audited in seventy-two hours. Gerald would be audited too. Both of them. The Inspector General would look at everything. Every exemption. Every variance. Every carving.

Gerald's four Combat Damage Exemptions. The compressed dust patches. The loop corridor. All of it would be in the audit file.

Skarn saw the notification too. Gerald could tell because the crystal stopped pulsing. It went still. For the first time since Gerald had known him, Skarn looked afraid.

Seventy-two hours. Gerald looked at his dungeon. His patched walls. His filed exemptions. His loop corridor that served no legitimate circulation purpose. The audit would find it all.

He looked at Skarn. Skarn looked at him. Neither spoke.

Then Gerald noticed the third line. The one he really almost missed.

**NOTICE: Core "Hutchins, G." Compliance Rating has dropped below 50. Threshold violation. Demotion review initiated. Demotion review window: 72 hours. Core will be demoted to Dungeon Class 1 if Compliance Rating is not restored above 60 before review.**

Dungeon Class 1. The lowest class. No citations. No appeals. No force majeure exceptions. Just a hole in the ground with some rats in it.

Gerald had seventy-two hours to fix everything. And the audit would make it harder, not easier. Because the auditor would see the patches. The exemptions. The loop.

He started walking back to his dungeon. His legs felt heavy. His mana was at twelve percent. His Compliance Rating was forty-nine.

He needed to rebuild. Fast. And he needed to do it without leaving evidence.
