Chapter 19: Signed by the Inspector

# Chapter 19

The measuring tape clicked against the wall at the forty-third foot. Gerald wrote the number on his clipboard.

He moved to the forty-fourth foot.

The tape clicked. He wrote the number.

The wall was not straight, and he had known this for eleven minutes.

He had been measuring it in half-foot increments, recording each deviation, building a dataset that any code enforcement officer would recognize as a concealed structural irregularity. The wall bowed inward by roughly three-eighths of an inch at the midpoint.

The bow was consistent across its full height.

It was not settling. It was not thermal expansion. It was the signature of a load path redirect, the kind that happened when someone built over an existing structure without properly accounting for what was underneath.

Gerald pulled his pen from his shirt pocket and drew a line on the clipboard. He labeled it. He applied the same labeling he had used for forty-one years of deficiencies: section number, severity rating, correction deadline.

Section 7.3. Concealed Footprint Overlap. Severity: Critical. Correction deadline: Immediate.

The System pinged. A notification appeared in his peripheral vision, the pale blue text hovering in the air like a stain on glass.

*Form accepted. Report ID: 7.3-0041. Status: Under Review.*

He did not look at it. He kept measuring.

The second wall was worse. The third wall was worse than the second.

By the time he reached the fourth, Gerald had filled two pages of the clipboard with numbers, and the pattern was unambiguous.

The dead Core's footprint was still here.

Not as a ghost. Not as a memory.

As a structural layer. A latent foundation that the System had never disclosed to him because the System had never told him adjacent footprints could persist after termination.

He set down the measuring tape. He pulled out the Citation form from his inventory. He unfolded it on the floor.

The Citation was addressed to the Inspector General. The violation was Concealed Footprint Overlap, Section 7.3.

The service requirement was physical presence, and the Inspector General had no body. The Inspector General had no footprint of its own, because the Inspector General was not a Core. It was an auditor.

An auditor did not have a footprint.

Except.

Except the Inspector General had registered over the dead Core's footprint. That registration created a footprint.

A footprint was a physical structure, and a physical structure had a location. The location was here. This room.

These walls. This floor.

The Inspector General had a footprint here. It was latent. It was concealed. But it was physical, and it was present, and it was standing on the floor of Gerald's dungeon.

Gerald picked up the Citation form. He walked to the center of the room. He set the form on the ground.

He could not serve it on a person, and he could serve it on a structure.

A Citation did not require a hand to receive it, and it required a physical presence at the address of service.

The address of service was this room. The physical presence was the latent footprint.

He pressed his palm flat against the Citation form.

The System pulsed. The form went rigid. The air in the room dropped three degrees. Gerald felt it in his molars, the same sensation he got when a citation cleared intake at the municipal office, that cold snap of bureaucratic finality.

*Citation served. Target: Inspector General, Registration #0000-IG.

Violation: Section 7.3, Concealed Footprint Overlap.

Severity: Critical. Status: Served.*

Gerald exhaled.

His mana ticked down from two percent to one-point-eight. The Citation had cost him.

He had expected it to cost more, and he had expected it to fail.

The System did not fail. The System processed. The System logged. The System moved on to the next thing.

And then the System did not move on, and the notification came fast. Faster than any notification he had ever received. It did not appear in his peripheral vision. It appeared directly in front of his face, blocking his view of the far wall, a solid block of text that he had to physically lean around to see the room.

*APPEAL FILED. Hearing initiated.

Both parties present.

Window: Immediate.

No grace period. No preparation interval.

Reason: Emergency Structural Review.*

Gerald stared at the text.

Emergency Structural Review. He knew this term.

He had used it himself, three times in his career, when a building was actively collapsing and the usual fourteen-day appeal window would have meant people died.

An Emergency Structural Review bypassed every procedural safeguard. It started the moment the appeal was filed. Both parties were forced into the Hearing immediately.

No preparation time existed.

The session did not adjourn; the Inspector General filed an appeal immediately after the Citation cleared. The Inspector General had been watching. The Inspector General had known he was measuring. The Inspector General had known what he would do.

The room changed.

The walls did not move.

The floor did not shift. But the quality of the space changed. Gerald recognized the sensation, similar to the pressure shift when a fourth-floor window blew out and the stairwell acted as a chimney.

The air had a direction now. It pulled toward the center of the room, toward the Citation form, which was still pressed flat against the floor.

A shape assembled itself above the form.

Not a body.

A geometry. A flat plane of pale light that rotated slowly, like a ceiling fan made of parchment.

It had no face. It had no arms. It had a registration number floating above it: 0000-IG.

"Inspector General," Gerald said.

The plane of light rotated faster. The System's Hearing protocol activated.

Gerald felt it as a tightening in his chest, the same way the System tightened his Compliance Rating when he failed to install a handrail.

The Hearing was not a conversation. It was a structural argument. Both parties would present their case.

The System would evaluate the logic.

The logic would decide.

"Respondent is present," the System announced.

"Appellant is present. Hearing commences. Argument format: Structural Logic.

Both parties will present load-bearing assertions. The System will evaluate consistency.

The party whose assertions maintain structural integrity wins.

The party whose assertions collapse loses."

Gerald understood. This was not a debate.

This was a proof. He constructed the argument like a citation, ensuring each assertion supported the next. If one assertion failed, the whole structure failed.

The System would test it like a load-bearing wall. Apply pressure.

See if it held.

The Inspector General's argument came first, and it arrived as text, scrolling down the plane of light in tight, precise characters.

*Assertion One: The Inspector General's registration is valid because the prior Core was terminated for structural collapse. A terminated Core's footprint is null. A null footprint cannot be overlapped. No concealed footprint exists.*

Gerald read it twice. It was clean. It was logical.

It was also wrong, and he knew exactly why it was wrong, because he had seen this exact argument before.

A contractor had tried it on a duplex in the Riverside district. "The old house was condemned, so I didn't need to disclose the foundation." The contractor had lost.

Gerald had written the citation himself, and he spoke his first assertion aloud. The System captured it.

"Assertion One: The prior Core was terminated for structural collapse. Termination removes the Core. Termination does not remove the footprint.

The footprint is a structural layer. Structural layers persist until actively removed.

The System did not remove the layer.

Therefore, the layer persists."

The plane of light flickered. The System was testing his assertion. Applying pressure. Looking for the crack.

The Inspector General responded.

*Assertion Two: A latent footprint has no Core. A footprint without a Core is not a structure. It is residue. Residue does not require disclosure.*

Gerald closed his eyes. He thought about the Riverside duplex.

He thought about the foundation. He thought about the word "residue" and how it was a word that meant "leftover" and "insignificant" and "you don't have to worry about it." He had heard that word from contractors eleven times in his career. He had rejected it eleven times.

He opened his eyes.

"Assertion Two: A latent footprint is a structural layer. Structural layers bear load.

If a latent footprint did not bear load, the Inspector General's registration would not be built on it.

It would be built beside it. The registration is built on it. The registration bears load through it.

Therefore, the latent footprint is load-bearing.

Load-bearing structures require disclosure. Disclosure was not made."

The plane of light stopped rotating.

The System pressed against his assertion, testing the logic as he had tested a load path a hundred times. Trace the force from the roof to the foundation. Check each connection.

Find the gap.

No gap remained. The logic held. The force path was clean.

The Inspector General's next assertion came slow. Slower than the others. Gerald could tell, from the speed of the text, that the Inspector General was recalculating.

*Assertion Three: Even if disclosure was required, the disclosure obligation falls on the prior Core, not the Inspector General. The prior Core is terminated. It cannot disclose. The obligation dies with the Core.*

Gerald almost smiled. This was the contractor's second argument. The one that came after the first one failed. "The previous owner was responsible, not me." He had rejected that one too.

"Assertion Three: The disclosure obligation is not a personal obligation.

It is a structural obligation.

It attaches to the footprint, not the Core. When a footprint is reused, the obligation transfers to the new registrant.

The Inspector General reused the footprint. The obligation transferred. The Inspector General did not disclose."

The plane of light flickered again. Harder this time.

The System was testing the transfer clause.

Gerald felt the pressure build. He had never cited the transfer clause before. He had read it.

He had memorized it.

But he had never used it. He was not certain it applied to Core registrations.

He was not certain the System recognized footprints as transferable structures.

The pressure held. The System accepted the assertion.

The Inspector General went silent. The plane of light rotated slowly, and the text stopped scrolling. The System waited.

Then the plane of light changed. It folded inward, collapsing into a smaller shape. The registration number flickered.

The text that appeared was not an assertion. It was a question.

*Query: Does the Inspector General have a Core? If not, can the Inspector General be a party to a Hearing?*

Gerald's stomach dropped, and he had not thought of this.

The Inspector General was not a Core.

The Inspector General was an auditor. An auditor did not have a Core.

A Hearing required two Cores. The Inspector General was not a Core.

The Inspector General was using Gerald's own logic against him. Gerald had argued that the Inspector General had a footprint.

A footprint implied a Core.

But the Inspector General did not have a Core. The Inspector General had registered a footprint without a Core. That was the fraud.

But if the Inspector General was not a Core, then the Inspector General could not be a party to a Hearing.

The Hearing would be void. The Citation would be void.

Gerald looked at the plane of light. He looked at the question. He had thirty seconds before the System ruled on it. Thirty seconds to find the structural flaw in the Inspector General's own argument.

He looked at the Citation form on the floor, and he looked at the registration number. 0000-IG. Four zeros. The lowest possible registration number.

The first registration. The original.

The Inspector General had registered before every other Core.

The Inspector General had the first footprint, and the first footprint was the foundation. Every other footprint was built on it.

The Inspector General was not a Core; he was something else. Something that came before Cores.

Gerald opened his mouth.

"Assertion Four: The Inspector General has a registration number. Registration numbers are issued to Cores. The Inspector General has a registration number. Therefore, the Inspector General is a Core."

The plane of light went still. The System applied pressure.

Gerald felt it test the assertion, and he felt it trace the logic. Registration numbers are issued to Cores. The Inspector General has a registration number.

Therefore.

The System accepted it.

The plane of light shuddered. The registration number above it flickered, and for one instant, Gerald saw something else.

Not 0000-IG.

A different number. A longer number. A number that had been buried under the first one, like a name scraped off a wall and painted over.

Then the number went back to 0000-IG. The plane of light resumed rotating. The System's text appeared.

*Hearing continues. Appellant has two assertions remaining.

Respondent has two assertions remaining.

The party with the most structurally sound assertions wins.

Time limit: ninety seconds.*

Ninety seconds. Gerald had two assertions left. The Inspector General had two left.

The Inspector General had already shown it was not a Core, and Gerald had forced it to be one. But the Inspector General had not lost.

The Hearing was still active, and the Inspector General was still arguing.

And the number Gerald had seen, the one buried under 0000-IG, was still in his head.

He did not know what it meant. He did not know what it was.

But he knew this: the Inspector General had not wanted him to see it.

The Inspector General had folded the plane of light specifically to hide it.

The Inspector General had a secret. And the secret was the real reason the Inspector General had registered over the dead Core's footprint.

Not convenience.

Not bureaucracy.

Something else. Something that the Inspector General was willing to lose a Hearing to protect.

Gerald looked at the plane of light. He looked at the Citation form. He looked at his clipboard, covered in measurements.

He had ninety seconds. He had two assertions. He had one number he could not read.

The Inspector General's next assertion appeared.

*Assertion Four: The Respondent's Compliance Rating is 12; a Core with a Compliance Rating below 15 cannot initiate a Hearing. The Hearing is procedurally void.

The Citation is void. The Respondent will be terminated for structural inadequacy within forty-two days regardless of this Hearing's outcome.*

Gerald read it. He read it again.

It was true.

His Compliance Rating was 12. He had not raised it. He had been too busy measuring walls.

The System's rule was absolute.

A Core below 15 could not initiate a Hearing. He had initiated this Hearing.

He had violated the threshold, and the Inspector General was not arguing. The Inspector General was ending the argument. The Inspector General was telling Gerald that the Citation did not matter, because Gerald would be dead in forty-two days, and the Hearing was already void because Gerald was not allowed to be here.

The System applied pressure to the assertion. It tested the rule. A Core below 15 cannot initiate a Hearing.

Gerald's Rating was 12. Gerald initiated the Hearing.

The Hearing was void.

The System accepted it.

Gerald felt the room begin to dissolve, and the walls went soft. The floor lost its certainty.

The Citation form on the ground turned translucent. The Hearing was collapsing. The Inspector General had won.

Gerald had lost. The Citation was void.

The Inspector General would audit him in forty-two days and terminate him.

Gerald looked at the measuring tape in his hand. He looked at the clipboard. He looked at the Citation form, fading.

He had one assertion left, and he spoke it.

"Assertion Five: The Inspector General's Compliance Rating."

The room stopped dissolving. The plane of light went still. The System paused.

Gerald said it again. "The Inspector General's Compliance Rating. What is it?"

The System held. The Inspector General held. The room held.

No one had ever asked the Inspector General for its Compliance Rating. No one had ever thought to ask. The Inspector General was an auditor.

Auditors did not have Compliance Ratings.

Auditors audited.

But the Inspector General had a registration number. Gerald had forced the Inspector General to be a Core. Cores had Compliance Ratings. The Inspector General had a Compliance Rating.

The System was waiting. The Inspector General was waiting. Gerald was waiting.

The Inspector General did not respond.

The System ruled.

*Failure to provide requested structural data constitutes an assertion collapse.

Appellant's structural integrity is compromised. Hearing result: Respondent wins. Citation upheld.

Appellant debuff applied.*

The plane of light cracked, and a fissure ran through its center, pale and bright.

The registration number shattered. The buried number was visible.

Gerald saw it. He saw the full number. He saw what the Inspector General was.

The plane of light collapsed. The room solidified. The Citation form went dark and rigid on the floor, confirmed, permanent, binding.

Gerald stood in his dungeon. His hands were shaking.

His mana was at one-point-two percent.

His Compliance Rating was still 12. He had forty-two days before the Inspector General's audit became irrelevant, because the Inspector General was now debuffed and he did not know by how much.

He looked at the Citation form. He looked at the number he had seen.

The number was his own registration number, and the Inspector General was him.

He had signed it himself.
