Chapter 382: Violations Cannot Be Fined

# Chapter 382

The extinguisher lunged.

It did not spray. It bit.

The nozzle snapped shut like a trap jaw, closing on the drywall inches from Gerald's clipboard.

Red foam, not chemical, but viscous and dark, erupted from the mimic's mouth. It coated the floor. The smell hit Gerald first.

Sulfur.

Rancid, ancient, and deeply illegal.

Gerald did not run. Running was inefficient. Running triggered panic responses that impaired fine motor control.

He stepped back. One step. Two. He kept the clipboard between himself and the creature.

"Identification," Gerald said. His voice did not shake. It hovered at the pitch of a man reading a list of missing screws.

The mimic hissed. It uncurled from the wall mount, dropping to the floor with a wet thud. It was shaped like a standard ABC extinguisher, but the rubber hose was a tongue, and the pressure gauge was a single, dilated pupil.

Gerald checked his status screen. It floated in his peripheral vision, translucent and mocking.

**STATUS: SUB-CONTRACTOR.** **RANK: UNLICENSED.** **PERMISSION: OBSERVE ONLY.**

No citation button. No fine calculation. Just the ability to look, and the obligation to report.

The mimic circled him.

It moved with a slithering grace, the red body slick against the concrete. It expected fear. It expected a fight.

It did not expect Gerald to pull a tape measure from his back pocket.

Gerald extended the tape. The metal hook caught on a protruding bolt on the wall. He reeled it in.

"Distance from egress route: four feet," Gerald muttered. "Minimum clearance for emergency access equipment: thirty-six inches. You are in violation of Egress Safety Protocol 4.2."

The mimic paused. The pupil-gauge narrowed.

Gerald did not stop. He walked the perimeter, measuring. The mimic followed, confused by the lack of aggression.

"Wall mounting height: twenty-four inches. Statutory requirement: between thirty and fifty-four inches. You are too low. You are a tripping hazard."

The mimic shrieked. It lashed out. The tongue-whip caught Gerald's shoulder.

Pain flared, hot and bright.

Gerald's jacket tore.

Blood welled on his shirt. He did not flinch.

He adjusted his glasses.

"Assault on a municipal official," he noted. He did not write it down.

He couldn't, and he had no authority. But he could see it. He could categorize it.

The mimic lunged again. This time, Gerald sidestepped. He had read the body language of aggressive entities for three decades, mostly in the form of contractors avoiding OSHA fines. The shift in weight, the tension in the coil.

The mimic crashed into the tape measure line Gerald had drawn on the floor with chalk.

It stumbled. Its footing slipped on the red foam.

Gerald looked at the ceiling. The sprinkler system.

His dungeon had no water suppression.

It never had. He had cited himself for it in Chapter 4.

He had been fined, and he had paid the fine. He had not installed the sprinklers because he could not afford the materials.

But he could observe.

And he could use the environment.

Gerald walked to the corner where the previous mimic, the one he had successfully dismantled in the last audit, had been stored. It was a pile of rubber and metal, broken.

He kicked the debris into the center of the room.

"Improper storage of hazardous materials," Gerald said to the air.

The new mimic paused. It looked at the pile. It looked at Gerald.

Gerald pointed to the pile.

"You are obstructing the walkway. Move it."

The mimic, insulted, roared. It charged.

Gerald did not move. He held his ground.

At the last second, he dropped to one knee.

The mimic flew over him. Its momentum carried it forward, directly into the open closet door Gerald had left ajar.

The door slammed. The latch caught.

The mimic was inside. It thrashed against the wood. The wood was not reinforced. It splintered.

Gerald stood up. He dusted off his knee.

He walked to the door. He did not open it. He placed his ear against the wood.

Inside, the mimic was pounding. The sound was rhythmic, desperate.

Gerald closed his eyes. He thought.

He was a Sub-Contractor. He could not issue a Citation. He could not fine. He could not banish.

But he could report.

And he could call someone who could, and he opened his System interface. Not to the citation screen. To the directory.

**LOCAL CORE LIST.**

He scrolled past the friendly rat, the water leak, and the mold spore. He scrolled past the neutral entities.

He stopped at **Emberheart**, and she was three dungeons over. A fire-based entity.

A high-ranking Core.

She had just won a Hearing against him.

She had laughed at his demotion.

Calling her was dangerous. It was unprofessional. It was likely to result in immediate termination of his license.

Gerald's hands trembled. Not from fear. From the sheer, overwhelming stress of breaking protocol.

He typed the query.

**SUB-CORE REPORT: UNLICENSED ENTITY DETECTED.** **LOCATION: SECTOR 7 (MUNICIPAL LIBRARY SUB-BASEMENT).** **TYPE: CLASS 3 MIMIC.** **VIOLATIONS: TRESPASS, ASSAULT, OBSTRUCTION OF EGRESS.** **REQUEST: AUTHORITY INTERVENTION.**

The System blinked.

**ERROR: SUB-CONTRACTORS MAY NOT REQUEST INTERVENTION FROM EQUAL-RANK CORES.**

Gerald stared at the red text, and he couldn't call Emberheart.

He couldn't call the Inspector General.

He was alone.

The closet door burst open. Wood shards flew. The mimic emerged, larger now.

It had absorbed some of the rubber from the old mimic. It was bloated, angry.

It looked at Gerald. It opened its mouth.

Gerald looked at the floor, and the red foam was spreading. It was eating the concrete. Acidic.

Corrosive.

If the floor weakened, the library above would collapse.

Gerald had a duty to the public. Not to the dungeon. To the public.

He looked at the ceiling. The ventilation duct.

It was small. Four inches by four inches. Uninsulated. Unlabeled.

A violation.

Gerald grabbed his clipboard. He ripped the top sheet. He wrote, in large, block letters.

**WARNING: STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY COMPROMISED.** **DO NOT ENTER.** **SEE INSPECTOR G. HUTCHINS.**

He taped the note to the doorframe.

Then he looked at the mimic.

"You are violating the Fire Code by generating an open flame," Gerald said. "And you are violating the Hazardous Materials Act by leaking corrosive fluid onto a non-sealed surface."

The mimic tilted its head. It didn't care about code. It cared about meat.

Gerald sighed. He reached into his pocket.

He pulled out a standard municipal fire extinguisher.

Not a mimic. A real one. Red canister.

White label, and he had carried it for ten years.

He had never used it.

He had inspected it every six months. He knew its weight.

He knew its range, and he pulled the pin.

The mimic lunged.

Gerald squeezed the handle.

White powder blasted the mimic's face.

It shrieked. The powder blinded it.

It thrashed, knocking over a bookshelf.

Books scattered. Paper rained down.

Gerald advanced. He kept the nozzle pointed.

"Extinguish," he commanded.

The mimic stumbled back. It tried to bite. Gerald sidestepped, using the tape measure line as a boundary. He stayed on his side of the chalk.

The mimic crossed the line.

Gerald sprayed again. This time, he aimed for the base. The feet.

The powder clogged its joints. It slowed.

Gerald walked around it, and he did not stop spraying. He circled.

He sprayed the back.

The sides. The top.

It was overkill. It was inefficient. It used up the canister.

But it worked.

The mimic slowed to a crawl. It lay on the floor, twitching. The red foam around it turned gray as the powder mixed with it.

Gerald stepped back. He checked the canister. Empty.

He dropped it, and the mimic was still alive. It was blind, suffocating, but alive.

Gerald looked at his status.

He couldn't kill it. He couldn't banish it.

He had to wait for the System to notice.

But the System was slow. The System was bureaucratic.

And Gerald was out of powder, and a sound came from the hallway. Heavy footsteps. Metal on concrete.

Gerald turned.

Through the doorway, he saw them.

Not adventurers. Not goblins.

Inspectors.

Three of them. They wore gray robes. They held clipboards that glowed with blue light. Their faces were blank, obscured by visors.

The System Inspector General's enforcement division, and they had not come because of Gerald's report. They had come because of the noise. The energy spike. The structural stress.

They looked at the mimic.

One of them stepped forward. He raised a hand. A beam of pure white energy struck the mimic.

It dissolved. Instantly. Not into ash, but into data. Green code that faded into nothing.

The Inspectors turned to Gerald.

The lead Inspector spoke. His voice was synthesized. Cold.

"Core Hutchins. You are operating without a license."

Gerald straightened his tie. He looked at the Inspectors. He looked at the empty spot where the mimic had been.

"I observed a violation," Gerald said. "I mitigated the immediate threat. I filed a report."

"You engaged in combat," the Inspector said. "Sub-Contractors may not engage."

"I defended myself," Gerald said. "Self-defense is not a violation. It is a right."

The Inspectors conferred. They whispered in a language that sounded like static.

Then the lead Inspector looked at Gerald.

"Your defense is noted. However, the mitigation used—chemical suppression—was unauthorized.

You utilized a Class B extinguisher on a Class 3 biological entity, which is a mismatch of tools.

It indicates a lack of proficiency.

"

Gerald's jaw tightened.

"It worked," he said.

"Efficiency is the metric," the Inspector said. "You wasted resources. You damaged the property.

The bookshelf is destroyed. The floor is stained.

The library above has filed a complaint.

"

Gerald looked up. The ceiling. The library.

He had caused damage.

"I will repair it," Gerald said.

"You will pay for it," the Inspector corrected. "And you will be fined for the unauthorized use of municipal property."

Gerald felt the fine hit his account. A negative number flashed in his vision. Then another. Then another.

His balance went from positive to negative.

**BALANCE: -4,200 SYSTEM CREDITS.**

If he went below -10,000, his dungeon would be seized. The library would be condemned. He would be erased.

"You have one week to rectify the structural damage," the Inspector said. "You have one week to restore your balance. Otherwise, your dungeon is shut down. Permanently."

They turned to leave.

"Wait," Gerald said.

The Inspectors paused.

"The mimic was not a random spawn," Gerald said. "It was placed. It was a test."

The lead Inspector turned back.

"By whom?"

Gerald looked at the empty space. He thought of Emberheart. She had vanished after the Hearing.

She had laughed, and she had said *good luck*.

He looked at the chalk line on the floor. The mimic had crossed it. It had been pushed. Or provoked.

But by whom?

Gerald looked at his status again.

**RANK: UNLICENSED.**

He couldn't investigate. He couldn't accuse.

But he could observe, and he walked to the spot where the mimic had died. He picked up a shard of rubber. It was still warm.

He closed his fist around it.

"Inspector," Gerald said. "I request a variance."

The Inspector raised an eyebrow. Or what passed for one.

"A variance from what rule?"

"From the rule that says I can only observe," Gerald said.

"I request the right to conduct a preliminary investigation into the source of this entity. Without a license. Without a citation.

Just observation.

"

The Inspectors looked at each other.

"Denied," the lead Inspector said. "Observation is limited to your own perimeter. You may not investigate external causes."

They walked out. The door swung shut.

Gerald was alone, and the silence was heavy. The air smelled of powder and sulfur.

He looked at the door.

He looked at the chalk line.

He looked at his status.

He was trapped. He was broke. He was powerless.

But he was an inspector.

And inspectors find holes.

Gerald walked to the wall where the mimic had been mounted. The mount was broken. Screws were stripped.

He pulled a screwdriver from his belt, and he unscrewed the remaining bolt.

It wasn't a violation to repair a fixture. It wasn't a violation to check the structural integrity of a mount.

As he pulled the bolt free, a small piece of paper fell out from behind the drywall.

It wasn't a note. It was a tag.

A small, metal tag. Like the kind used for heavy machinery.

It had a serial number.

And a name.

**PROPERTY OF THE ASHFORD HOUSING AUTHORITY.**

Gerald froze.

The Ashford Housing Authority was not a dungeon core, which was a municipal entity. A city department.

A department he used to work for.

He turned the tag over.

On the back, etched into the metal, was a symbol.

Not the System's eye.

Not a Core sigil.

A wrench. Crossed with a hammer.

The union logo of the City Workers Guild.

Gerald's hands went cold.

A mimic, disguised as a fire extinguisher, planted in his dungeon. Tagged as city property. Owned by the union.

The union had no dungeon. They had no System interface.

Unless they had found a way in.

Unless they had found a loophole.

And they had put it in his dungeon. To hurt him. To break him.

Gerald looked at the tag. He looked at the door.

The Inspectors were gone, and the library was safe. For now.

But the threat was inside. Not in the dungeon. In the bureaucracy.

He had to find out who planted it, and he had to find out how they bypassed the System.

And he had to do it without a license.

Gerald picked up the tag. He tucked it into his pocket.

He walked to the center of the room. He sat down on the floor.

He opened his clipboard. He started a new file.

**SUB-CORE INVESTIGATION: FILE 88-B.** **SUBJECT: EXTERNAL INFLUENCE.** **METHOD: OBSERVATION.**

He wrote, and he did not know if it was legal.

He did not know if it was wise.

But he knew the code.

And the code said that if you see a violation, you report it.

Even if you can't fine it.

Even if you can't stop it.

You write it down.

Gerald wrote.

And in the shadows of the sub-basement, a single mold spore drifted down from the ceiling.

It landed on Gerald's shoulder, which did not move.

It waited.
