Chapter 126: Code Violation Feature

# Chapter 126

Gerald counted the seconds between the lock click and the door’s squeal. It pressed against Gerald's senses, a physical weight that tasted of stale copper and recycled dust.

He did not move.

He did not breathe. He simply waited for the door to open.

The heavy steel hatch, which Gerald had reinforced with three additional locking mechanisms and a secondary latch system compliant with Class B security standards, groaned. The hinges screamed. Not from rust, but from the sheer force applied to them by someone who did not respect torque specifications.

The lock clicked. Not unlocked. Clicked. A distinct, metallic snap that indicated the tumbler had been forced, not turned.

Gerald's consciousness focused on the threshold, and he did not have eyes. He had sensors.

He had pressure plates.

He had a network of dampers and airflow monitors that mapped the room with the precision of a laser surveyor.

A boot stepped into the corridor. It was a heavy, steel-toed boot.

Size eleven.

Worn tread.

Heavy impact signature.

"Inspector," a voice said. It was smooth, cultured, and devoid of the fear that usually accompanied entry into a Dungeon Core's territory.

Gerald did not respond. He adjusted the ventilation damper in the east wall. The air flow shifted, carrying the scent of ozone and mold directly toward the intruder.

"You're quiet," the voice noted. "I expected a welcome mat. Or perhaps a trap."

"The welcome mat is non-compliant with fire safety codes," Gerald replied. His voice did not come from a throat. It resonated through the stone, vibrating the atoms of the sub-basement. "The trap is currently undergoing inspection."

A laugh echoed. It was a dry, rasping sound.

"Arrogant.

I like it. Let's see the paperwork.

"

The intruder moved deeper into the dungeon.

Gerald tracked the movement through the vibration sensors in the floor. The footsteps were confident. Too confident.

This entity believed it was walking into a monster's lair, not a municipal compliance office.

*Alert: Unauthorized Entity Detected.* *Profile: High-Level Adventurer.* *Class: Bureaucrat (Hidden).*

Gerald felt a flicker of interest. The System had labeled this person as a Bureaucrat.

Not a fighter.

Not a mage. A Bureaucrat.

"Name," Gerald demanded. The word slammed into the air, amplified by the acoustic properties of the narrow corridor.

The figure stopped. The boot hovered in mid-air, then descended with a heavy thud.

"Agent Kael. Inspector General's Office. Division of Regulatory Oversight."

"Show credentials," Gerald said.

Kael paused. The silence stretched, taut and brittle.

Then, a flash of golden light illuminated the darkness, which was not a spell.

It was a System prompt. A digital badge materialized in the air, hovering over Kael's chest.

**ID: 9021-B** **Title: Senior Auditor** **Clearance: Level 5**

Gerald scanned the code. He did not need to read it. He felt the weight of the authorization.

It was valid. It was terrifyingly valid.

"You have a citation for trespassing on a non-public easement," Gerald said. "And for unauthorized structural modification of the entry hatch."

"I have a warrant for your compliance record," Kael said.

His voice dropped an octave.

"Gerald Hutchins. Former municipal inspector.

Current Dungeon Core.

Your Compliance Rating is fluctuating dangerously. It is currently at 48.

This puts you in the Red Zone.

"

"The fluctuation is due to ongoing construction," Gerald said. "I am installing a secondary egress route. The primary route is currently blocked by a water leak."

"A water leak," Kael repeated. He sounded amused. "In a dungeon."

"In a *compliant* dungeon, a water leak is a hazard," Gerald corrected. "In an *incubator* dungeon, it is a breeding ground for mold. Which, according to Section 14, Paragraph C of the Biological Safety Act, is a Class 3 health violation."

Kael moved forward. The golden light of his badge dimmed, replaced by the flickering torch held in his free hand. The flame illuminated the rough-hewn walls. Gerald could feel Kael's gaze sweeping the room, looking for the mold, looking for the rot, looking for the excuse to shut him down.

"Show me," Kael said.

Gerald did not show. He directed.

"Follow the yellow line," Gerald said.

Kael looked down. Painted on the concrete floor was a thick, bright yellow stripe. It led from the entrance to the center of the room, curving around obstacles with geometric precision.

"Is this a joke?" Kael asked.

"It is a designated pedestrian walkway," Gerald said. "Non-compliant entities who step off the path assume liability for any damage incurred."

Kael stepped onto the yellow line. He took a step. Then another. He moved with the hesitation of a man walking on ice.

"You have no guards," Kael observed.

"I have code," Gerald said.

"Code doesn't kill me, Inspector."

"No," Gerald agreed. "But it might slow you down."

Kael reached the center of the room. The rat was still there, curled in the corner, too weak to move. The mold was sealed behind the ventilation shutters, a dark stain on the ceiling that Gerald could feel throbbing with malicious intent.

Kael looked around. He looked at the handrails, bolted securely to the walls at exactly 34 inches from the floor. He looked at the emergency exit signs, illuminated by battery-powered LEDs, compliant with the 30-minute backup requirement. He looked at the fire extinguisher mounted on the wall, its inspection tag dated for the current month.

"Where is the dungeon?" Kael asked. "Where are the monsters?"

"This is the lobby," Gerald said. "The dungeon begins where the code ends."

Kael frowned. He pulled a scroll from his belt. It unrolled in the air, projecting holographic text.

"I am here to audit your structural integrity. I see no load-bearing columns.

I see no support beams.

This room should have collapsed ten minutes ago.

"

"It is a cantilevered structure," Gerald said. "Supported by the library foundation above. I filed Form 11-C (Structural Dependency Report) upon registration. It was approved by the System."

Kael's eyes narrowed. He tapped the scroll.

"The System approves many things that are physically impossible. I am here to verify the physics."

He raised his hand. A pulse of mana radiated from his palm, testing the walls. The stone vibrated. It held.

"Impressive," Kael muttered. "For a human."

"I am not human," Gerald said. "I am a function of the building."

Kael ignored him. He walked along the yellow line, tapping the walls, checking the corners.

He was thorough. Gerald respected it. Most adventurers just kicked doors open.

Kael checked the hinges.

"You have a ventilation issue," Kael said. He stopped near the sealed duct. "The air quality here is terrible. My lungs are burning."

"The air quality is poor because I sealed the vents to contain a biological hazard," Gerald said. "You are citing me for the containment measure, not the hazard itself."

Kael turned. His face was pale, illuminated by the torch.

"You trapped yourself to save your Compliance Rating?"

"I trapped the hazard," Gerald said. "The air quality penalty is temporary. The mold infection would have been permanent. And permanent infections lead to closure."

Kael smiled, which was a thin, sharp smile.

"You're playing a dangerous game, Hutchins. The Inspector General doesn't care about your logic.

He cares about the numbers.

And right now, your numbers are a mess.

"

"Then fix the numbers," Gerald said. "Or cite me. I have forms prepared for both outcomes."

Kael laughed again.

But this time, there was no humor in it.

"You think this is a negotiation. It is an inspection.

And I have found a new violation.

"

"Which one?" Gerald asked.

He felt a spike of anxiety, but he suppressed it, and he checked the list in his mind. Ventilation: Cited.

Egress: Fixed. Fire Safety: Compliant.

"Psychological Warfare," Kael said.

Gerald paused.

"That is not a code category."

"It is under *Harassment of Patrons*," Kael said. "Section 8, Subsection D. Any Core that creates an environment of excessive stress, fear, or psychological discomfort for authorized auditors is subject to immediate suspension."

Kael pointed at the sealed vent.

"The trapped mold.

The darkness.

The silence. The pressure. You are weaponizing the atmosphere.

You are creating a hostile environment for a state official.

"

Gerald felt the System's attention shift. A new notification blinked in his mind.

**Violation Detected: Environmental Harassment.** **Severity: Moderate.** **Penalty: -15 Intelligence to All Core Functions.**

Gerald's thoughts slowed. The mental fog that had been creeping in during the last few minutes thickened. He felt heavy. Tired.

"That is a loophole," Gerald said. His voice was slower. "You are interpreting 'stress' as a product of my compliance efforts, not my malice."

"I am interpreting it as you perceive it," Kael said. "And I am perceiving it as malicious. Therefore, it is malicious."

This was new. Gerald had never encountered a Core that used the auditor's subjective experience as the basis for a citation. It was unfair. It was brilliant.

"If I open the vents," Gerald said, "the mold spreads. My Compliance Rating drops."

"And if you keep them closed," Kael said, "you are harassing me. Your Rating drops."

Kael was forcing a choice. A lose-lose scenario.

Gerald looked at the scroll. He looked at the golden badge. He looked at the rat, still curled in the corner.

*Action: Appeal.*

"You cannot cite me for the environment I have legally maintained," Gerald said. He focused his will, pushing against the mental fog.

The System resisted.

It liked the ambiguity. Ambiguity meant more paperwork. More hearings.

More power for the Inspector General.

"I am citing you for the *intent*," Kael said. "And your intent is to obstruct."

"My intent is to comply," Gerald said.

"I am compliant with the Biological Safety Act.

I am compliant with the Ventilation Standards. I am compliant with the Egress Requirements.

I am *over*-compliant.

"

Kael tilted his head.

"Over-compliant?"

"I have installed three additional fire extinguishers," Gerald said. "I have reinforced the floor with steel plating. I have installed a backup generator that runs on vegetable oil. I have exceeded every minimum standard by a factor of two."

Kael paused. He looked at the scroll. He looked at Gerald.

"Exceeding standards," Kael said slowly. "Is that a violation?"

"It is a defense," Gerald said. "Under the *Good Faith Effort* clause. If a Core demonstrates excessive diligence in maintaining safety, the System presumes the intent is protective, not malicious. The burden of proof shifts to the auditor."

Kael's eyes widened. He flipped through the holographic scroll. Pages turned rapidly.

"The *Good Faith Effort* clause," Kael muttered. "That is... obscure."

"It is in the appendix," Gerald said. "Page 4,000. I read it. You did not."

Kael stopped flipping. He stared at Gerald. The torch flame flickered.

"You read the appendix?" Kael asked.

"I read everything," Gerald said. "It is my job."

Kael lowered his hand. The mana pulse faded. The pressure in the room seemed to ease, just slightly.

"This is not over," Kael said. His voice was cold.

"You found a technicality.

But technicalities are fragile. And you are playing with fire.

"

"I am playing with code," Gerald corrected. "And code is law."

"Code is a tool," Kael said. "And tools can be broken."

He stepped back. The boot clicked on the yellow line.

"I am withdrawing the citation for Environmental Harassment," Kael said. "For now."

Gerald felt the mental fog lift. His thoughts cleared. The penalty reversed.

**Citation Withdrawn.** **Compliance Rating: 48.**

"But," Kael continued, "I am opening a formal Hearing regarding the 'Over-Compliance' clause. I will argue that your excessive measures constitute *Hoarding* of resources, which is a violation of the Economy Balance Act."

Gerald's heart—or the mechanical equivalent of it—stuttered.

*Hearing Scheduled: Immediate.* **Opponent: Agent Kael.** **Topic: Resource Hoarding vs. Safety Standards.**

"If I have too much safety gear," Gerald said, "is that a crime?"

"If it prevents other dungeons from acquiring resources, yes," Kael said. "You are a monopoly, Hutchins. And monopolies are illegal."

Kael turned. He walked back toward the hatch.

"I will send the summons within the hour," Kael said.

"Prepare your arguments. And prepare your resources.

Because if you win, you keep your dungeon.

If you lose..."

He did not finish the sentence. The hatch slammed shut.

The locking mechanisms engaged. *Click.

Clunk.

Clunk.*

Gerald was alone.

The silence returned. Heavier than before.

He looked at the rat. The rat looked back, its beady eyes reflecting the dim light of the exit signs.

Gerald checked his inventory.

**Water:** 0/100. **Stone:** 20/500. **Wood:** 0/200. **Metal:** 5/500. **Mana:** 10/1000, and he was poor. He was weak. He had no monsters.

He had no magic. He had only forms.

*Action: Begin Hearing Preparation.*

*Requirement: Evidence of Resource Scarcity.*

Gerald looked at the empty shelves, and he looked at the single fire extinguisher he had saved from the library's supply closet. It was a Class A extinguisher. Cheap.

Standard.

He had no rare materials. He had no exotic alloys. He had no dragon scales.

He had municipal steel.

Kael would claim he was hoarding. Gerald had to prove he was *justifying*. He had to prove that every ounce of metal he had used was necessary for code compliance, and that his scarcity was a systemic failure, not a personal greed.

But how do you prove scarcity when you have nothing?

Gerald closed his eyes. He accessed the System's Resource Log. It was empty.

Then he saw it.

**Log Entry: 14:02.** **Entity: Gerald Hutchins.** **Action: Consumed 1 Unit of Drywall.** **Purpose: Nutritional Support for Core Function.**

Gerald froze, and he had eaten the drywall. The rat had eaten it.

But Gerald had absorbed the calcium.

The gypsum. The paper.

He had consumed the infrastructure.

*Violation Detected: Self-Consumption of Assets.* **Status: Pending Investigation.**

If Kael found out he had eaten his own walls to survive, the argument for *Good Faith Effort* would collapse. You cannot claim to be maintaining the structure if you are digesting it.

Gerald looked at the sealed vent. The mold was still there. Waiting.

The clock on the wall ticked.

03:59.

The summons would arrive in one minute.

And Gerald had no defense. He had only the memory of the taste of plaster, and the terrible, gnawing hunger in his core.

He looked at the yellow line. It led nowhere.

He looked at the hatch. It was locked.

He looked at the mold.

*Action: Unseal Ventilation Ducts.*

*Reason: Emergency Ventilation for Hearing Preparation.*

*Warning: Releasing Biological Hazard.*

Gerald opened the damper.

The air rushed out. The smell hit him like a physical blow.

Rot.

Decay.

Death.

The mold spores drifted into the room. They settled on the floor. They settled on the yellow line.

**Compliance Rating: 47.** **Penalty: -5 Health to All Entities.**

The rat died. It simply stopped moving.

Gerald felt his own strength fade. The walls of his dungeon seemed to soften.

But now, there was air.

And in the air, swirling on the wind, was the answer, and the mold was not just a hazard. It was a resource. A biological agent.

If Kael was accusing him of hoarding metal, Gerald could counter-claim that he was managing a *biological* asset. A fungal colony. A renewable resource.

He had to prove the mold was useful, and he had to make it grow.

And he had to do it before the Inspector General arrived.

Gerald watched the spores settle. He focused his will. He did not have mana. He did not have magic.

He had time.

And he had a code violation that could become a feature, if he twisted it just right.

The hatch clicked.

Someone was at the door again.

This time, it was not a boot, which was a clipboard.

The clipboard held his eviction notice.
