Chapter 384: Mold Board Member

# Chapter 384

The phone was dead.

Gerald stared at the cracked screen, the lithium battery leaking a thin, acidic trickle onto the linoleum. The smell of burnt plastic mixed with the ozone tang of his own absorbed voltage. He didn't wipe his hands. His fingers were trembling, not from fear, but from the residual current still buzzing in his bones.

A notification chime pinged inside his skull.

**SYSTEM ALERT: UNAUTHORIZED POWER DRAW DETECTED.** **CITATION ISSUED: VIOLATION OF LOCAL UTILITY ORDINANCE 7.4.** **PENALTY: TEMPORARY STAT DEBUFF (CONDUCTIVITY -15%).** **APPEAL WINDOW: 10 MINUTES.**

Gerald blinked. The number on the screen flickered.

He had expected a fine. Or a curse. Or a dragon.

He had not expected a utility fine.

The mold spore on the briefcase twitched. Its tiny fungal cap rotated, sensing the shift in atmospheric pressure. It was waiting. But for what?

Gerald sat on the floor, his legs splayed beneath him. The library sub-basement was silent, save for the dripping of the ceiling pipes he had finally fixed last Tuesday. He looked at the citation notice hovering in his peripheral vision. It was a standard form. *Form 14-C: Utility Interference.*

He knew this form. He had printed three thousand copies of it during the Great Brownout of '98.

He reached for the air.

Not with his hands. With his will.

The System interface was a mental construct, usually a gray haze of menus and stats. But Gerald didn't see stats.

He saw filing cabinets, and he saw clipboards. He saw the weight of paper.

He grabbed the citation notice and pulled it toward him.

*Appeal.*

The word appeared in bold, red text.

Gerald focused. He needed to prove that the power draw was not a violation. He needed to prove it was a necessary safety measure.

He opened the **Appeal Hearing** interface.

The room dissolved.

In its place stood a sterile, white chamber. No walls. Just infinite white space, and a single wooden desk.

Across the desk sat a figure.

It wasn't a person. It was a projection. A generic avatar of the System's Inspector General. A faceless man in a suit that looked like it was made of graph paper.

**"Gerald Hutchins,"** the avatar said. Its voice was the sound of a printer jamming. **"You are accused of drawing 400 amps from the municipal grid without a permit. How do you plead?"**

Gerald sat down. He straightened his tie. He didn't have a tie. He had a tunic of woven cobweb, but he acted as if it were a tie.

"Not guilty," Gerald said.

The avatar tilted its head. **

"Explain."**

"The nullifier device," Gerald began.

He pointed at the empty air where the black phone had been.

"It was an unlicensed electrical device.

It was emitting electromagnetic interference that violated the library's quiet hours ordinance.

Section 12.4. Noise pollution. The nullifier was creating a hum at 60 hertz.

That is audible. That is a violation.

"

The avatar paused. **

"Irrelevant to the power draw."**

"It is relevant," Gerald said.

He leaned forward.

His eyes narrowed.

"Because the device was malfunctioning.

It was overheating. It was a fire hazard. Code 3.1: *All electronic devices must be properly grounded and cooled.* This device was neither.

It was a hazard.

"

**"So?"**

"So," Gerald said.

"I had a duty to mitigate the hazard. Under the Good Samaritan Act, codified in System Bylaw 89, a Core may take reasonable emergency action to prevent imminent structural failure or fire. My power draw was an emergency shutdown.

I overloaded the circuit to trip the breaker, thereby killing the device.

I saved the library from a potential electrical fire.

"

The avatar's face remained blank. **

"You destroyed the device."**

"I neutralized it," Gerald corrected.

**"You also absorbed the energy."**

Gerald felt a cold sweat prickle at the back of his neck. This was the trap. Absorbing energy was usually flagged as 'Theft' or 'Power Stealing.'

He had to pivot, and he thought of the wiring in the walls. The frayed copper. The old insulation.

"The energy was not stolen," Gerald said.

"It was transferred. The device was acting as a bridge. I used my body as a grounding wire.

I diverted the excess voltage from the hazardous device into the surrounding infrastructure. Specifically, into the library's electrical panel.

"

The avatar frowned. **

"You connected yourself to the city grid."**

"I connected my dungeon's electrical system to the city grid," Gerald said.

"This is standard practice for high-compliance cores.

See Section 4.2: *Interconnected Utility Systems.* By integrating my core's resistance with the municipal grid, I stabilized the load. I did not steal power.

I balanced the load. The power flowed through me, yes. But it exited into the grid.

I acted as a capacitor.

"

Silence.

The white room hummed.

Gerald waited. He hated this part. The uncertainty. He preferred the finality of a signed form.

**"Provide proof,"** the avatar said. **"Show me the flow."**

Gerald closed his eyes, and he reached into the dungeon's structural map. He found the electrical panel he had installed three weeks ago. It was a simple breaker box, mounted on the north wall of the sub-basement. He had labeled it *COMPLIANT: GROUNDING NODE 1.*

He traced the path of the current.

He remembered the surge. The way the lightning had hit him. He hadn't let it dissipate. He had forced it down his own channels, into his core, and then pushed it out into the walls, into the wiring, back into the grid.

He visualized the circuit.

*Input: City Grid -> Nullifier -> Gerald's Core -> Library Wiring -> City Grid.*

It was a loop.

A closed loop, and he showed it to the avatar.

The avatar stared at the visualization. It spun the data. It checked the voltage levels. It checked the timestamp.

**"The flow was unidirectional during the event,"** the avatar noted. **"You absorbed it."**

"Temporarily," Gerald said.

"For storage. See Section 9.3: *Transient Energy Storage.* A core may hold energy for up to three seconds to facilitate a transfer. I held it for 1.4 seconds.

Well within the limit.

"

The avatar paused.

Gerald's heart hammered against his ribs. He was lying.

He didn't know the limit was 1.4 seconds, and he was guessing. But he was guessing based on the refresh rate of the library's old fluorescent lights.

**"Acceptable,"** the avatar said.

The word hung in the air.

**"CITATION WITHDRAWN."** **"DEBUFF REMOVED."** **"BONUS: +10 INTELLIGENCE FOR SUCCESSFUL LEGAL ARGUMENT."**

The white room shattered.

Gerald was back in the sub-basement, and he gasped. The air tasted stale.

The mold spore was still on the briefcase.

Gerald stood up. His knees cracked.

He looked at the broken phone. He looked at the scorch marks on the wall.

He had won.

But the victory felt hollow, and he had used a loophole. He had exploited a technicality. He had twisted the truth until it fit the form.

He hated it.

He hated that it worked.

He walked to the door. He needed to file the paperwork.

Even if the System had withdrawn the citation, he needed a record.

A record of the incident.

A record of the *cause*, and he reached for the door handle.

It was locked.

Gerald frowned. He twisted the knob. It didn't move.

He looked at the lock. It wasn't the old padlock he had installed. It was new.

A smart lock. Sleek.

Black.

No keyhole.

A notification pinged.

**SYSTEM ALERT: DOOR ACCESS DENIED.** **REASON: SECURITY CLEARANCE LEVEL 1 REQUIRED.** **CURRENT CLEARANCE: LEVEL 0.**

Gerald stared at the lock, and he hadn't installed it.

He hadn't authorized it.

Someone else had modified his dungeon.

He stepped back. He looked at the ceiling. The water leak he had fixed... was it still dripping?

He listened.

*Drip.*

*Drip.*

*Drip.*

The rhythm was wrong.

It wasn't random, which was Morse code.

*Short. Short. Long. Short.*

Gerald tilted his head.

*H.*

*Short. Long.*

*L.*

*Long.*

*T.*

*H-L-T.*

Halt.

Someone was inside his dungeon.

Someone who could change the code.

Gerald looked at the mold spore. The tiny fungus had grown. It was larger now. It had formed a small, distinct cluster on the briefcase.

And the cluster was moving, which was crawling toward the smart lock.

Gerald rushed forward. He grabbed the briefcase.

The mold spore leaped.

It didn't attack him. It attacked the lock.

It slithered over the metal surface, secreting a green slime that sizzled against the plastic.

**SYSTEM ALERT: BIOLOGICAL INTERFERENCE DETECTED.** **ELECTRICAL SYSTEM COMPROMISED.**

The lights flickered.

The smart lock clicked.

The door swung open.

Standing in the hallway was not a goblin. Not an adventurer.

It was a man, and he wore a suit. A sharp, modern suit. He held a tablet.

He looked at Gerald with bored, professional eyes.

"Mr. Hutchins," the man said. "I represent the Ashford Utility Cooperative."

Gerald didn't move. He held the briefcase tight.

"You're not a core," Gerald said.

"No," the man said. "I'm a contractor."

He looked at the scorch marks on the wall. He looked at the dead phone.

"You caused a surge," the contractor said. "Our sensors picked it up. We detected an unauthorized interconnection."

"I appealed," Gerald said. "The System cleared me."

"The System clears the *dungeon*," the contractor said. "It doesn't clear the *person*."

He tapped his tablet.

**"I'm here to serve a writ of execution."**

Gerald felt a chill.

**"The City Council has voted,"** the contractor continued. "They've declared your dungeon a 'Public Hazard.' They've revoked your municipal license. You are no longer a registered building inspector. You are no longer a registered core."

Gerald's stomach dropped.

"That's not possible," Gerald said. "The System registered me."

"Registration is tied to civic status," the contractor said.

"You were a city employee. You exploited city resources.

The Council voted 7-2.

Your lease is terminated. Your property is condemned.

"

He pointed at the floor.

"Eviction notice served."

Gerald looked at the floor, and the concrete was turning brown.

The wood was rotting.

The walls were crumbling.

**SYSTEM ALERT: STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY FAILING.** **CONDEMNATION IN PROGRESS.** **TIME TO TOTAL COLLAPSE: 60 SECONDS.**

Gerald looked at the mold spore, which had stopped moving.

It was waiting.

The contractor smiled. It wasn't a nice smile.

"You have sixty seconds to vacate the premises," the contractor said. "If you remain, you will be considered a squatter. And we have strict rules about removing squatters."

Gerald looked at the door. The contractor had stepped aside, blocking the exit.

There was no way out, and the dungeon was collapsing.

The System was withdrawing its support.

His resistances were fading.

He looked at the contractor.

"Who sent you?" Gerald asked.

The contractor checked his watch.

"Fifty seconds," he said. "Ask the union."

He pointed at the ceiling.

"The lights are going out, Mr. Hutchins."

The lights died.

Total darkness.

And in the dark, Gerald heard the sound of something else.

Not the drip.

The *crack* of concrete.

And the whisper of many feet.

Gerald realized the truth.

The Council hadn't just revoked his license, and they had invited the neighbors in.

The rival cores.

They were coming for the ruins.

Gerald gripped the briefcase.

He had one second left to think, and he had no lightning. He had no fire. He had no strength.

He had only the form.

**FORM 11-B: UNLICENSED BIOLOGICAL ENTITY.**

He couldn't file it against a person.

But he could file it against a *structure*.

If the dungeon was condemned, it was no longer a dungeon, which was a ruin.

A ruin was public domain.

If he filed for **Emergency Historical Preservation**, he could pause the demolition.

But he needed a historical significance.

He needed to prove that his sub-basement was a landmark.

He looked at the dark, and he looked at the mold.

He looked at the rat that had been hiding under the desk since Chapter 1.

The rat squeaked.

It was holding a piece of parchment.

A copy of the original building permit. Dated 1924.

Signed by the Mayor.

It mentioned *Ashford Library Sub-Basement.*

It mentioned *Historical Foundation.*

Gerald grabbed the parchment, and he shouted into the dark.

"I FILE FOR HISTORICAL PRESERVATION! FORM 22-D! URGENT!"

The System paused.

The crumbling stopped.

The contractor's eyes widened.

"That's not a valid form!" the contractor shouted.

"It's in the manual," Gerald said. "Page 412. *Emergency Heritage Clause.*"

The System processed.

**HISTORICAL PRESERVATION REQUEST: ACCEPTED.** **DEMOLITION HALTED.** **STATUS: UNDER REVIEW.**

The contractor's face went pale.

Gerald stood in the dark, breathing hard, and he was safe.

For now.

But the contractor was smiling again.

"You bought yourself time," the contractor said. "But the review committee meets tomorrow."

He turned to leave.

"See you then, Inspector."

The contractor left.

The door slammed.

Gerald was alone, and the dungeon was safe from collapse.

But it was under review.

And the review committee was the real enemy.

Gerald looked at the rat.

The rat dropped the parchment.

It looked at Gerald.

It looked at the mold.

The mold spread. Not on the briefcase.

On the walls, which was writing.

Letters formed in the slime.

**WELCOME TO THE BOARD, MR. HUTCHINS.**

Gerald stepped back.

The mold wasn't attacking him, which was *joining* him.

And the first thing it wanted was to change the name of the dungeon.

The mold began spelling a name Gerald did not recognize.
