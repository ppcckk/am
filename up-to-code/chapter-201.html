Chapter 201: Inert and Waiting

# Chapter 201

The darkness was not empty. It was textured, thick, and smelled faintly of ozone and wet concrete.

Gerald felt the absence of his own mana like a missing tooth. The lockout had engaged instantly, severing the connection between his core and the spawn pool.

No rats could scurry.

No mold could spread. He was, in every functional sense, inert.

A boot scraped against the fractured ceiling debris.

"Stay back! It's a trap!"

The voice was young, shrill, and panicked. It echoed off the walls of the sub-basement, amplifying the terror of the speakers.

Gerald lay on his side, his core pulsing with a dull, rhythmic thud that served as his only heartbeat.

He could not move. He could not speak.

But he could see.

The System's vision mode remained active during Mana Lockout, though it rendered the world in grayscale and static.

Through the gap in the ceiling, three figures dangled from ropes. They wore polished steel armor that gleamed under their own torchlight.

One carried a mace.

Another held a glowing staff.

The third, the one who had spoken, clutched a shield emblazoned with a golden sun.

"Identify yourself!" the shield-bearer shouted down into the void. "By order of the High Council of Ashford, you are ordered to surrender your core essence for immediate extraction."

Gerald tried to raise a hand. Nothing happened.

Instead, he focused on the one thing he still controlled: the audit log.

The Citation window hovered in his peripheral vision, frozen in the 'Submitted' state. The penalty notification was stark: *Mana Lockout Active. Duration: 24 Hours. Reason: Self-Cited Critical Structural Failure.*

Above him, the mace-wielder stepped closer to the edge of the hole. "I can feel the corruption. It's weak. One more hit and it'll shatter."

"Wait," the staff-holder said. She lowered her rod, squinting into the gloom. "Look at the floor."

The mace-wielder paused. "What about it?"

"The cracks," she said. "They aren't chaotic. They're geometric. And look at the handrails."

Gerald watched them with detached fascination. His handrails, installed three weeks ago with meticulous attention to ADA-compliant heights, remained untouched by the collapse. They framed the chaos of the rubble with stubborn order.

"It's... organized," the shield-bearer muttered. He hesitated, stepping back from the edge. "That's not normal dungeon behavior.

Usually, the layout degrades into organic chaos. This looks like..."

"Like a construction site," the staff-holder finished.

The mace-wielder spat over the edge. "Construction sites get condemned. This gets purged."

He swung his mace.

The impact shook the entire structure. Dust rained down on Gerald's core. A hairline fracture appeared in his primary housing unit. Pain, simulated but visceral, spiked through his consciousness.

*Warning: Structural Integrity at 88%. Further damage will result in Core Breach.*

Gerald needed them to stop. But he could not fight. He could only inspect.

"Hey!" the shield-bearer shouted. "Who's in charge of this dump? We want to speak to the administrator."

Silence.

"Administrator?" the staff-holder called out. "We have a warrant for the removal of hazardous materials."

Gerald's mind raced. He couldn't speak, but the System allowed for one form of communication during Lockout: *Notice of Violation.*

He couldn't issue a new Citation. The lockout prevented any action that required mana expenditure, including serving legal notices. But he could *display* existing records. He could force the System to overlay the current state of his dungeon onto their vision.

It was a loophole he had discovered in Chapter 145, during a dispute with a fire-elemental core. The System prioritized visibility of active compliance violations over combat stealth. If a violation was active, the System highlighted it for all parties within the zone to ensure fair adjudication.

Gerald mentally navigated to the *Active Violations* tab.

There was only one.

*Violation 401-B: Unpermitted Excavation.*

*Location: Ceiling Aperture.*

*Status: Active.*

*Severity: Critical.*

*Required Action: Immediate Cessation of Activity.*

He triggered the display.

Above him, the three adventurers froze. The air shimmered, and a red holographic banner snapped into existence across the hole in the ceiling. It blocked their view of Gerald.

"What in the hell?" the mace-wielder grunted, pushing his mace through the light. It passed through harmlessly.

"It's a System notice," the shield-bearer said, his voice dropping to a whisper. "Look at the header. *City of Ashford Municipal Code, Section 12.4.*"

"Why is a dungeon citing us?" the staff-holder asked.

She pointed her staff at the red text. "That's not possible. Cores don't enforce municipal law.

They enforce survival law."

"Read it," the shield-bearer said. He sounded annoyed now, less heroic, more irritated. "It says: *Any entity engaging in excavation without a Class-3 Permit is subject to immediate fine and liability for structural damages.*"

"We're not excavating," the mace-wielder said. "We're destroying."

"Same thing, legally," the staff-holder murmured. She pulled a small book from her belt, flipping through pages rapidly. "I have a copy of the Ashford Code. I stole it from the clerk's desk last month."

"You did what?" the shield-bearer asked.

"Focus," she said. "If we continue striking, we are actively committing a violation.

The System logs everything.

If we break this core while under a Red Banner Citation, the liability isn't just to the dungeon. It's to the city."

"Liability to the city?" the mace-wielder scoffed. "Who's going to sue us? The Library is empty."

"Because it's a municipal building," the staff-holder said. "If we cause a collapse in a municipal building while ignoring a posted violation notice, the city's legal department can flag our party for 'Reckless Endangerment of Public Infrastructure.' They can revoke our adventurer licenses."

The mace-wielder lowered his weapon. "Revoke our licenses?"

"Gerald Hutchins is the inspector," the shield-bearer said, his eyes narrowing. "He's been filing reports on the city's infrastructure for thirty years. If we damage public property because we ignored his citation, we're in a lawsuit."

The three adventurers looked at each other. The tension shifted from combat to bureaucratic dread.

"So what do we do?" the mace-wielder asked.

"We stop," the staff-holder said. "And we wait for the fine to process."

"Fine?" Gerald thought. *I have no mana. I can't collect fines.*

But the System didn't require him to collect them. It required him to *file* them. And he had already filed the self-citation.

A new notification popped up in Gerald's mind.

*Violation 401-B Acknowledged by External Parties.*

*Liability Transfer Initiated.*

*Penalty: Asset Seizure.*

The red banner vanished. The ground beneath the adventurers' feet glowed orange.

"Wait," the mace-wielder said. He looked down at his boots. "Why is the floor hot?"

"It's not hot," the staff-holder said, her face pale. "It's *bonded*."

Above them, the ropes they used to descend were anchored to heavy steel stakes driven into the library's floor above. The stakes, currently under tension, vibrated.

"Cut the ropes!" the shield-bearer yelled.

"No!" the staff-holder shouted back. "If you cut the ropes, the stakes are abandoned. Abandoned structural supports in a municipal zone incur a daily penalty per day they remain unsecured. The System calculates it retroactively from the moment of placement."

The mace-wielder stared at the orange glow. "How much?"

"Five hundred gold per hour," the staff-holder said. "Per stake. We used three."

"That's four thousand five hundred gold," the shield-bearer calculated. "We're losing money by standing here."

"Then we move," Gerald thought. *Move, you idiots.*

The shield-bearer looked down at the glowing floor. "We can't move.

The stakes are anchored to the library floor.

If we pull them, we tear up the library floor.

Which is another violation. Section 18: Damage to Municipal Assets."

The mace-wielder threw his mace down in frustration. "This is ridiculous. It's a dungeon! We're supposed to kill it!"

"It's a *code-enforced* dungeon," the staff-holder said. She looked directly down, seemingly making eye contact with Gerald's core. "You know who runs it, right?

Gerald Hutchins. The man who fined the Mayor for a non-compliant ramp."

"He's just a Core now," the mace-wielder said.

"He's still an Inspector," the staff-holder corrected. "The System recognizes the title. And the title carries weight."

Gerald felt a surge of something warm. Not mana. *Vindication.*

He couldn't act. But he could wait. The lockout had a timer. 23 hours and 40 minutes remaining.

But the adventurers wouldn't stay for twenty-three hours. They needed a resolution.

"What's the cure?" the shield-bearer asked. He leaned over the edge, careful not to touch the glowing zone. "How do we clear this citation?"

Gerald focused on his interface. He couldn't speak, but he could *annotate*. The System allowed Core owners to add notes to active citations during disputes.

He typed: *Remediation Required.

Cease all destructive actions. Apply temporary shoring to ceiling aperture. Verify structural stability.

Submit Form 11-C for inspection.*

The text appeared in the air above the hole.

"Shoring?" the mace-wielder asked. "What does that mean?"

"It means hold it up," the staff-holder said. "We have to stabilize the ceiling before we leave. Otherwise, the collapse is deemed 'Negligent' rather than 'Accidental.'"

"We don't have wood," the mace-wielder said.

"We have rope," the shield-bearer said. "And we have our weight."

"That's not shoring," the staff-holder argued. "That's dead weight. The System will reject it."

"Then what?" the shield-bearer asked.

Gerald typed another note. *Acceptable Alternative: Core Stabilization Pulse. Requires 50 Mana. Provided by Compliant Entity.*

The adventurers read the note.

"He wants us to give him mana?" the mace-wielder asked.

"To stabilize the ceiling," the staff-holder said. "He cited himself for instability. He can't fix it himself because of the lockout. If we provide the energy, the System accepts the repair as 'Cooperative Remediation.'"

"We help the dungeon?" the mace-wielder looked disgusted.

"It's cheaper than the fine," the staff-holder said. "Four thousand five hundred gold versus fifty mana. Do the math."

The shield-bearer sighed. He pulled a mana crystal from his belt. It pulsed with blue light.

"I hate this," he muttered.

He tossed the crystal down.

It landed on the floor, near Gerald's core. The blue light spread, wrapping around the fractured ceiling like a bandage. The orange glow faded. The shaking stopped.

*Notification: Violation 401-B Resolved via Cooperative Remediation.*

*Penalty Waived.*

*Liability Transferred to Party: Sun-Guard.*

*New Status: Probationary Compliance.*

The red banner vanished completely. The floor returned to gray concrete.

"Done," the staff-holder said. "We're clear."

"Can we kill him now?" the mace-wielder asked, picking up his mace.

"No," the staff-holder said. "Read the new status. *Probationary Compliance.*"

The text floated above the dungeon entrance.

*Under Probation, the Core may not be destroyed for a period of 72 hours unless a new violation is committed. The Core must be monitored for stability.*

"Seventy-two hours?" the mace-wielder shouted. "We have a schedule! We have a raid in forty minutes!"

"Then you have to wait," the staff-holder said. She looked tired.

"Or you leave.

If you leave, the probation timer keeps running. If you come back before it expires, you're in violation of Probation. That's a felony-level infraction.

License revocation. Permanent ban from Ashford."

The mace-wielder looked at Gerald's core. He looked at the staff-holder. He looked at the shield-bearer.

"I'm not staying here for three days," the mace-wielder said.

"Neither am I," the shield-bearer said.

He grabbed the rope.

"We leave. We report the violation cleared.

We let the System handle the probation."

"Wait," the staff-holder said. She was staring at her interface. "There's another notification."

"What?" the shield-bearer asked.

"Gerald Hutchins," the staff-holder read. "He just filed a *Counter-Citation*."

The mace-wielder froze. "On who?"

"On *us*."

The staff-holder turned her staff toward them. The air crackled.

"Charge: Unauthorized Presence in a Restricted Zone."

"We were invited," the shield-bearer said. "By the System."

"Read the fine print," the staff-holder said. Her voice was shaking. "It says: *Entry into a Core with Active Structural Probation requires a Class-2 Safety Permit. Sun-Guard Party lacks said permit.*"

"We didn't know!" the mace-wielder yelled.

"Ignorance of the code is no defense," the staff-holder recited. "It's in the handbook."

A red banner slammed into existence over the adventurers' heads. It was bright, blinding, and undeniable.

*Citation Served.*

*Penalty: Immediate Detention by System Enforcement.*

The ground beneath the adventurers' feet turned into bars. Metal erupted from the concrete, forming a cage around them. The ropes snapped as the cage rose, pulling them up toward the hole in the ceiling.

"What is this?" the shield-bearer screamed. He clawed at the bars. "I'm a Hero! I have rights!"

Gerald watched the bars rise. He couldn't move. He couldn't spawn. But he could watch.

The bars dragged the three adventurers up through the hole and out of his dungeon. He heard their protests fading as they were hauled away, presumably to a System Holding Cell.

Silence returned to the sub-basement.

The lockout timer ticked down. 23 hours and 15 minutes.

Then, a new message appeared. Not from the adventurers. Not from the System.

It was a direct message. Encrypted. From an unknown sender.

*Subject: Regarding Citation 401-B.*

*Dear Inspector Hutchins,*

*I represent the Consortium of Unlicensed Dungeons. We have observed your recent activity.*

*You are disrupting the balance of power in Ashford.*

*We propose a meeting.*

*Location: The Ashford Municipal Library, Ground Floor, Reading Room 4.*

*Time: 48 Hours.*

*Bring your files. And bring a lawyer.*

*If you do not appear, we will appeal your probation status. We will prove that your self-citation was fraudulent. We will have your license revoked.*

*You have 48 hours.*

Gerald read the message twice.

The Consortium. Rival cores. A coalition of the chaotic, the unregulated, the ones he had been citing for months.

He looked at his mana pool. Still locked.

He looked at the ceiling. Repaired.

He looked at the clock.

48 hours.

He couldn't fight them. He couldn't spawn defenders. He couldn't even open the door.

But he had 48 hours to prepare a legal defense.

And Gerald Hutchins had never, not once in his thirty-one-year career, lost an argument with a building code.

The rat in the corner twitched.

It looked at Gerald's core.

Then it looked at the ceiling.

It chewed on the repaired shoring.

Gerald didn't have mana to stop it.

He watched the rat chew.

He waited for the timer to drop.
