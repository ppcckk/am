Chapter 277: No Precedent

# Chapter 277

The pipe was cracked. Not just cracked. It was non-compliant.

Gerald's hands moved through the black sludge, blind but certain. He felt the fracture line running along the PVC casing like a spiderweb across a windshield. Under normal circumstances, he would have called for a licensed plumber. Under current circumstances, licensed plumbers were three streets over, pinned down by a monster that defied all known biological taxonomies.

He grabbed the exposed edge of the pipe. The material felt cold, brittle, and illegal.

The water rushed past his shoulders, carrying grit and debris that scratched against his translucent core-form. He didn't flinch.

Pain was a variable he had long ago decided was irrelevant to structural integrity.

If the structure held, the pain didn't matter. If the structure failed, the pain would be the least of his problems.

Above the surface, the shouting had died down. The police were waiting. The woman with the tablet was waiting. The Punsing Garden Core was watching.

Gerald twisted his torso. He needed leverage. He needed to break the remaining seal.

He pushed against the opposing side of the trench wall, and the mud gave way. He slipped, sliding deeper into the muck.

His boots were gone.

He didn't care. He kicked downward, anchoring himself against the gravel bed of the street.

*Crack.*

Not the pipe. The gravel.

He reset his stance. He dug his fingers into the sludge, finding purchase on a submerged curbstone. He pulled.

The pipe groaned. A low, resonant vibration traveled up his arms. It was the sound of a system under stress. It was the sound of a violation about to be corrected.

The sludge-monster above the surface roared. The water churned. The police shouted warnings that sounded like they were coming from underwater, which, technically, they were.

Gerald ignored them. He focused on the fracture point.

If he tried to patch it, he would fail. The pressure was too high. The water table was too low. The material was too old.

He had to divert it, and he reached into his inventory. The System menu flickered in his peripheral vision, a ghostly overlay of forms and checkboxes.

*Form 74-C: Emergency Structural Modification.* *Cost: 15 Mana.* *Prerequisite: Valid Permit.*

He had the permit. The woman had typed it. He had the mana. He had spent weeks hoarding it, refusing to spawn even a single slime mold in favor of saving every drop for infrastructure upgrades.

He selected the form.

*Target: Municipal Sewage Main, Sector 4.* *Action: Redirection.* *Destination: Temporary Containment Basin (New).*

The System processed. The delay was minimal.

But there was a catch. A loophole Gerald hadn't considered until this exact second.

The redirection required a destination. A *compliant* destination.

He didn't have a containment basin. He had a dungeon footprint of 4,000 square feet, mostly empty, mostly filled with handrails and warning signs.

He looked at his inventory. He looked at the pipe.

He couldn't just dump the sewage into his dungeon. That would violate sanitation codes. It would tank his Compliance Rating. It would invite an audit from the Inspector General.

But he could create the basin, and he needed earth. He needed space.

He looked up. The street surface was ten feet above him. Above that, the sidewalk. Above that, the Punsing Garden's entrance.

Gerald activated *Excavate*.

Not on the pipe. On the ground above him.

The System hesitated.

*Warning: Surface interference detected. Unauthorized excavation above public right-of-way.*

Gerald stared at the warning. He didn't dismiss it. He amended it.

*Amendment: Emergency Hazard Mitigation. Authority: Municipal Inspector. Status: Active.*

The System accepted.

The earth above him shifted.

It didn't happen instantly. It happened in layers.

The asphalt cracked.

The dirt slumped.

The sidewalk buckled.

Above the surface, the police officer dropped his radio.

"What is happening?"

The woman with the tablet tapped furiously.

"He's... he's moving the ground."

The Punsing Garden Core pulsed. The water in the street swirled, drawn by the sudden change in topography. The sludge-monster shrieked, its form losing cohesion as the water level dropped.

Gerald felt the pressure change. The crack in the pipe widened. Water sprayed out, hitting him in the face. He blinked.

He was close, and he grabbed a chunk of concrete from the trench wall. He jammed it into the crack.

*Structural Integrity: 12%.*

Not enough.

He needed more mass. He needed weight.

He looked at the police officer standing on the crumbling edge of the trench.

"Officer!" Gerald shouted. His voice was muffled by the water.

The officer leaned over.

"Hey! Come out!"

"I need you to move back!" Gerald yelled. "The load-bearing capacity of the edge is compromised!"

The officer hesitated. Then he stepped back.

The trench edge collapsed. A ton of asphalt and dirt slid into the water, burying the pipe's fracture point.

Gerald winced. That was a bad move. It wasn't a fix. It was a burial.

But it was enough.

The water flow slowed. The pressure dropped. The pipe stopped spraying.

Gerald floated in the muddy slurry, breathing heavily. He looked at his inventory.

*Excavation Complete.* *New Structure Detected: Temporary Pit.* *Status: Non-Compliant.*

He ignored the status. He had seconds, not minutes.

He surfaced, and the street was a mess. A crater had formed where the sidewalk used to be. The sludge-monster was shrinking, its mass draining into the new pit along with the sewage. It thrashed, trying to maintain its form, but the water current was pulling it apart.

Gerald climbed out of the trench. His suit was shredded. His skin was grey with grime.

The police raised their weapons again.

"Step away from the crater!" the officer shouted.

Gerald raised his hands. He didn't reach for a weapon. He reached for his clipboard.

He held it up.

"Report filed," Gerald said. His voice was calm. "Hazard mitigated."

The officer stared at him. Then he lowered his weapon.

The woman with the tablet walked over. She looked at the crater. She looked at Gerald.

"You just dug a hole in the middle of Main Street," she said.

"I fixed the leak," Gerald said.

"The monster is dissolving," she said.

"The monster was the leak," Gerald said. "Or rather, the lack of containment."

The Punsing Garden Core pulsed. The entrance to the Garden sealed itself. The light behind it turned red.

A notification appeared in Gerald's vision.

*Target: Punsing Garden Core.* *Status: Defensive Mode Engaged.* *Violation Detected: Failure to Maintain Separation from Public Infrastructure.* *Citation Issued.*

Gerald felt the familiar tug in his chest. The Citation locked onto the Core.

*Penalty: -15% Mana Regeneration for 24 Hours.* *Penalty: -10% Spawn Rate for 24 Hours.*

The Core screamed. It wasn't a sound. It was a frequency that vibrated in Gerald's teeth.

The monster dissolved completely. The last of its mass flowed into the pit, mixing with the mud.

Gerald stood on the edge of the crater. He looked at the police. He looked at the woman. He looked at the sealed Garden.

"You're done here," Gerald said.

"We're not done," the officer said. "You have to come with us. This is a crime scene."

"This is a repair," Gerald said. "And you're blocking the work zone."

The officer stepped forward. He grabbed Gerald's arm.

Gerald didn't pull away. He let the officer take his arm.

He waited.

The officer tried to pull him up. He couldn't.

Gerald was anchored. Not by his feet, but by his dungeon.

The 4,000 square feet beneath the library were now connected to this crater. The System had registered the excavation as an extension of Gerald's dungeon footprint.

Technically, the crater was part of his domain.

The officer pulled harder. His boots slipped on the asphalt.

Gerald looked at him.

"You are trespassing," Gerald said.

The officer froze.

"I... what?"

"You are standing on private dungeon property," Gerald said. "Without a visitor permit. I am citing you for trespassing on a non-public easement."

The officer looked at his partner. The partner looked at the crater.

"Is that... real?" the partner asked.

"The System doesn't lie," Gerald said. "Check your interface."

The officer looked down at his wrist. His smartwatch flashed red.

*Notice: Citation Issued.* *Violation: Trespassing.* *Penalty: -5% Stamina for 1 Hour.*

The officer's legs went weak. He stumbled back, dropping Gerald's arm.

Gerald straightened up. He dusted off his shredded suit.

"Next time," Gerald said, "ask for a permit before you touch a suspect."

He turned to the woman.

"Fill the crater," he said. "Use sand. No gravel. Gravel creates drainage issues."

The woman nodded. She was trembling.

"Gerald," she said. "The Inspector General."

Gerald paused.

"What about them?"

"They're coming," she said. "The audit. They've been watching you since the first Citation.

This... this excavation. It's too big.

They'll see it.

"

Gerald looked at the sealed Garden.

"Let them see it," Gerald said.

He turned and walked toward the library. His steps were heavy. Each step sent a ripple through the dungeon's mana grid.

He felt the strain. The excavation had cost him. Not just mana. Stability.

His Compliance Rating had fluctuated wildly. It was no longer 47. It was 32.

He had saved the street. But he had broken his own house.

The handrails in the sub-basement were shaking. The water leak he had been managing for weeks had reversed. The dungeon was flooding.

Gerald reached the library entrance. The doors were open.

Inside, the lobby was empty.

On the floor, in the center of the lobby, sat a single rat, which was not his rat. It was not confused.

It wore a tiny, formal vest. It held a scroll in its teeth.

Gerald stopped.

The rat dropped the scroll.

Gerald picked it up, which was an Appeal.

*Appellant: Punsing Garden Core.* *Target: Gerald Hutchins.* *Reason: Malicious Interpretation of Code.* *Hearing Scheduled: Immediate.*

Gerald looked at the rat. The rat looked at him.

The rat spoke. Its voice was high, squeaky, and arrogant.

"You're not going to win this one, Inspector."

Gerald looked at the scroll, and he looked at the System menu.

The Hearing had started.

And for the first time in thirty-one years, Gerald Hutchins had no precedent.

Gerald looked up, and the rat smiled.
