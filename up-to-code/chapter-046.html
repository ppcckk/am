Chapter 46: Beating the Rule

# Chapter 46

Gerald flipped the clipboard over. The back was blank. He turned it back. The front still read DENIED.

He pulled the parcel registration form from the air. The System had printed it in that particular shade of grey that meant the message had been generated by a process, not a person.

The header read: PARCEL REGISTRATION APPLICATION, FORM PR-1.

The body referenced Surveyor Certification, Section 4.2.1. The footer read: ALL PARCELS MUST BE REGISTERED PRIOR TO HEARING COMMENCEMENT.

Gerald read the footer again. Then he read the header. Then he read the body. Then he started reading the fine print at the bottom, which was set in a font so small it required him to press his face against the paper.

The fine print read: This form is governed by the Land and Structure Registration Ordinance, hereafter referred to as LSRO.

All provisions of the LSRO apply. Section 4.2.1 requires Surveyor Certification.

Section 4.2.2 requires a metes and bounds description.

Section 4.2.3 provides for self-survey by a licensed professional. Section 4.2.4 provides for surveyor certification by proxy. Section 4.2.5 provides for provisional registration pending survey completion.

Section 4.2.6 provides for emergency registration under conditions of imminent structural threat. Section 4.2.7 provides for exemption of existing structures predating this ordinance.

Gerald stopped, and he read 4.2.7 again.

He looked at the clock. One hour and thirty-one minutes.

He looked at the form.

He looked at the System's message history, and he scrolled back. He scrolled further.

He scrolled to the first message the System had ever sent him. The Core Registration notice. It was dated fourteen months ago.

He scrolled forward. He found the message that introduced the parcel registration requirement.

It had arrived six days ago.

A new mechanic. A new rule. A new form.

Fourteen months versus six days.

His dungeon predated the ordinance.

Gerald pulled a fresh sheet from the air, and he wrote: REQUEST FOR EXEMPTION UNDER LSRO SECTION 4.2.7. He wrote: THE STRUCTURE DESCRIBED HEREIN WAS ESTABLISHED PRIOR TO THE ENACTMENT OF THIS ORDINANCE.

THE STRUCTURE PREDATES THE ORDINANCE BY FOURTEEN MONTHS.

THE STRUCTURE IS AN EXISTING STRUCTURE. EXISTING STRUCTURES ARE EXEMPT FROM SURVEYOR CERTIFICATION REQUIREMENTS UNDER SECTION 4.2.7.

He attached the Core Registration notice as evidence, and attached the parcel registration introduction notice as evidence of the ordinance's effective date. He attached a timeline. He attached a cross-reference.

He signed it. He stamped it. He submitted it.

The System processed it. The grey text appeared.

NOTICE: EXEMPTION REQUEST RECEIVED. VALIDATING.

NOTICE: STRUCTURE ESTABLISHMENT DATE CONFIRMED.

NOTICE: ORDINANCE EFFECTIVE DATE CONFIRMED.

NOTICE: STRUCTURE PREDATES ORDINANCE. EXEMPTION GRANTED.

NOTICE: PARCEL REGISTRATION WAIVED UNDER SECTION 4.2.7.

NOTICE: DEMOLITION PROTOCOL CANCELLED.

NOTICE: HEARING CANCELLED.

Gerald set the clipboard down. His hands were shaking. He put them in his pockets so they would stop.

He had found the form, and he had found the form.

He sat on the floor. The warmth of the structure moved through him.

Forty-one points.

Distributed, and the structure was compliant. The structure was the land.

The land was compliant.

He sat there for a long time.

Then the System sent another message.

NOTICE: EXISTING STRUCTURE EXEMPTION GRANTED. PROCEEDING TO RETROACTIVE COMPLIANCE REVIEW PER LSRO SECTION 4.2.7(b).

Gerald read it twice. He had not seen 4.2.7(b) on the form.

He pulled the form out again, and he read the fine print. Section 4.2.7 provided for exemption of existing structures predating this ordinance.

(b) did not exist.

He looked at the System message.

He looked at the form. The form was wrong.

The form was incomplete, and the ordinance had a subsection that the form did not disclose.

NOTICE: RETROACTIVE COMPLIANCE REVIEW COMMENCING.

NOTICE: ALL STRUCTURES BUILT, MODIFIED, OR EXPANDED AFTER ORDINANCE EFFECTIVE DATE ARE SUBJECT TO PERMIT REQUIREMENTS UNDER LSRO SECTION 7.1.

NOTICE: UNPERMITTED STRUCTURAL MODIFICATIONS DETECTED: 47.

NOTICE: VIOLATION CLASSIFICATION: UNPERMITTED STRUCTURAL MODIFICATION.

NOTICE: SEVERITY: CRITICAL.

NOTICE: PENALTY: STRUCTURAL DECOMMISSION OF ALL NON-PERMITTED ELEMENTS.

NOTICE: REVIEW BOARD ASSIGNED: INSPECTOR GENERAL.

Gerald read the number again. Forty-seven.

Forty-seven unpermitted modifications, and he had built handrails. He had widened corridors.

He had installed a fire suppression system.

He had added ventilation shafts.

He had reinforced a load-bearing wall. He had added egress signage.

He had installed a sprinkler manifold. He had built a second exit. He had added a ramp.

He had reinforced the ceiling. He had added a sub-drainage system.

He had widened the junction corridor by six inches, and he had added a second handrail on the north wall. He had installed a fire door. He had added a smoke detector.

He had widened the spawn alcove.

He had added a ventilation grate. He had reinforced the floor slab.

He had added a second egress route, and he had installed a fire-rated partition. He had added a drainage channel. He had widened the corridor by four inches.

He had added a third handrail. He had installed a sprinkler head.

He had added a fire extinguisher mount.

He had widened the spawn alcove by two inches, and he had added a ventilation shaft extension. He had reinforced the junction floor.

He had added a fourth handrail. He had installed a fire-rated door on the east wall. He had added a smoke barrier.

He had widened the north corridor. He had added a drainage grate.

He had installed a second fire extinguisher mount, and he had reinforced the south wall; had added a fifth handrail. He had installed a sprinkler manifold extension.

He had widened the east corridor.

He had added a ventilation shaft cap. He had reinforced the west wall.

He had added a sixth handrail, and he had installed a smoke detector extension; had widened the south corridor. He had added a fire-rated partition extension.

He had reinforced the junction ceiling. He had added a seventh handrail.

He had installed a second sprinkler head.

Forty-seven.

Every thing he had done to bring his dungeon up to code was now, itself, a violation.

He had built a compliant structure without permits.

The System sent another message.

NOTICE: INSPECTOR GENERAL HAS REVIEWED YOUR FILE.

NOTICE: INSPECTOR GENERAL FINDING: YOUR DUNGEON IS, IN FACT, COMPLIANT WITH ALL STRUCTURAL, FIRE, ELECTRICAL, AND VENTILATION CODES.

NOTICE: INSPECTOR GENERAL FINDING: COMPLIANCE WITHOUT PERMIT IS ITSELF A VIOLATION OF THE PERMITTING ORDINANCE.

NOTICE: INSPECTOR GENERAL DETERMINATION: YOUR DUNGEON WILL BE DECOMMISSIONED IN SEVEN DAYS UNLESS PERMITS ARE OBTAINED RETROACTIVELY.

NOTICE: RETROACTIVE PERMIT APPLICATION WINDOW: 48 HOURS.

NOTICE: RETROACTIVE PERMIT FEES: 47 x STANDARD FEE x SEVERITY MULTIPLIER.

NOTICE: SEVERITY MULTIPLIER FOR UNPERMITTED STRUCTURAL MODIFICATION: 10x.

NOTICE: STANDARD FEE PER PERMIT: 500 SYSTEM CREDITS.

NOTICE: TOTAL DUE: 235,000 SYSTEM CREDITS.

NOTICE: CURRENT BALANCE: 1,200 SYSTEM CREDITS.

NOTICE: INSUFFICIENT FUNDS.

Gerald stared at the number. Two hundred thirty-five thousand.

He had twelve hundred, and he looked at the forty-seven modifications. Each one had taken him hours. Some had taken days. The sprinkler manifold alone had required him to understand fluid dynamics, which he did not understand, which he had learned, which had taken three days and two failed attempts and one flood that nearly killed his rat.

He looked at the clock. The hearing was cancelled. The demolition was cancelled. He had bought himself seven days.

He did not have two hundred thirty-five thousand credits, and he did not have a surveyor.

He did not have a permit.

He had a dungeon.

He had a clipboard.

He had forty-seven violations that were also forty-seven improvements that were also forty-seven things that had kept his Compliance Rating above the threshold that would have killed him.

The System sent one more message.

NOTICE: INSPECTOR GENERAL ADDENDUM.

NOTICE: RETROACTIVE PERMIT APPLICATIONS MAY BE FILED IN BULK UNDER LSRO SECTION 12.3.

NOTICE: SECTION 12.3: BULK RETROACTIVE PERMIT APPLICATIONS REQUIRE A COMPLETED STRUCTURAL INSPECTION REPORT CERTIFYING THAT ALL UNPERMITTED WORK MEETS CURRENT CODE.

NOTICE: STRUCTURAL INSPECTION REPORTS MAY BE FILED BY A LICENSED BUILDING INSPECTOR.

NOTICE: GERALD HUTCHINS HOLDS A LICENSED BUILDING INSPECTOR CREDENTIAL.

NOTICE: GERALD HUTCHINS MAY FILE THE STRUCTURAL INSPECTION REPORT.

NOTICE: HOWEVER, LSRO SECTION 12.3(c) PROHIBITS AN INSPECTOR FROM FILING AN INSPECTION REPORT ON A STRUCTURE THEY HAVE A PERSONAL INTEREST IN.

NOTICE: GERALD HUTCHINS IS THE STRUCTURE.

NOTICE: GERALD HUTCHINS HAS A PERSONAL INTEREST.

NOTICE: SECTION 12.3(c) APPLIES.

NOTICE: BULK RETROACTIVE PERMIT APPLICATION DENIED.

NOTICE: ALTERNATIVE PATHS:

NOTICE: PATH A: HIRE A LICENSED BUILDING INSPECTOR TO INSPECT YOUR DUNGEON.

NOTICE: PATH B: HIRE A LICENSED BUILDING INSPECTOR TO INSPECT YOUR DUNGEON.

NOTICE: PATH C: HIRE A LICENSED BUILDING INSPECTOR TO INSPECT YOUR DUNGEON.

NOTICE: ALL PATHS REQUIRE A LICENSED BUILDING INSPECTOR WHO IS NOT THE STRUCTURE.

NOTICE: CURRENT ADVENTURER PARTIES WITHIN RANGE: 3.

NOTICE: CURRENT LICENSED BUILDING INSPECTORS WITHIN RANGE: 0.

Gerald read the last line. Zero.

There had always been a form. There had never been a night like this one.

He had never once in thirty-one years been unable to find an inspector.

He picked up his clipboard. He turned to a fresh page. He wrote: PATH A.

He wrote: HOW DO I FIND A LICENSED BUILDING INSPECTOR WHO IS NOT THE STRUCTURE.

The System did not answer.

Gerald stood, and he walked to the junction. He pressed his hands against the floor.

Forty-one points. The warmth. The structure.

The land, and he thought about the forty-seven modifications. Each one a violation. Each one a necessity. Each one built by hands that were no longer hands but walls and floors and the spaces between.

He thought about the rat. The rat was alive because of the ventilation. The ventilation was unpermitted. The rat was alive because of an illegal act.

He thought about the handrails. The handrails were unpermitted. The handrails were the reason his Compliance Rating was forty-one and not twelve.

He thought about the word decommissioned, and he walked to the east corridor.

The one he had widened by six inches. He pressed his palm against the wall. The wall was warm.

The wall was his, and the wall was illegal.

He pulled out his clipboard.

He wrote: NOTICE OF INTENT TO APPEAL.

He wrote: I, GERALD HUTCHINS, HEREBY NOTICE INTENT TO APPEAL THE DETERMINATION THAT A STRUCTURE MAY NOT INSPECT ITSELF.

He wrote: THE STRUCTURE IS THE INSPECTOR.

THE INSPECTOR IS THE STRUCTURE. THE STRUCTURE IS COMPLIANT. THE INSPECTOR HAS CERTIFIED COMPLIANCE.

THE CERTIFICATION IS VALID. THE CERTIFICATION IS INVALID.

THE CERTIFICATION IS BOTH.

He stopped writing.

He looked at what he had written. It was not a legal argument. It was a paradox.

He crossed it out, and he started again.

He wrote: NOTICE OF INTENT TO APPEAL.

He wrote: LSRO SECTION 12.3(c) PROHIBITS AN INSPECTOR FROM FILING AN INSPECTION REPORT ON A STRUCTURE THEY HAVE A PERSONAL INTEREST IN.

He wrote: I DO NOT HAVE A PERSONAL INTEREST IN THE STRUCTURE.

He wrote: I AM THE STRUCTURE, and he wrote: A STRUCTURE CANNOT HAVE A PERSONAL INTEREST IN ITSELF.

A STRUCTURE IS NOT A PERSON. THE PROHIBITION APPLIES TO PERSONS.

I AM NOT A PERSON. I AM A STRUCTURE. THEREFORE THE PROHIBITION DOES NOT APPLY TO ME.

He stared at it.

It was either the most brilliant legal argument he had ever constructed or the most insane thing he had ever written.

He signed it. He stamped it. He submitted it.

The System processed it.

NOTICE: APPEAL RECEIVED. VALIDATING.

NOTICE: APPEAL ARGUMENT: THE APPELLANT IS NOT A PERSON AND THEREFORE NOT SUBJECT TO PERSONAL INTEREST PROHIBITION.

NOTICE: SYSTEM REVIEW.

NOTICE: SYSTEM REVIEW COMPLETE.

NOTICE: APPEAL GRANTED.

NOTICE: GERALD HUTCHINS IS CLASSIFIED AS A STRUCTURE.

NOTICE: GERALD HUTCHINS IS CLASSIFIED AS AN INSPECTOR.

NOTICE: A STRUCTURE MAY INSPECT ITSELF.

NOTICE: BULK RETROACTIVE PERMIT APPLICATION UNLOCKED.

NOTICE: STRUCTURAL INSPECTION REPORT MAY BE FILED BY THE STRUCTURE.

NOTICE: PERMIT FEES STILL DUE: 235,000 SYSTEM CREDITS.

NOTICE: CURRENT BALANCE: 1,200 SYSTEM CREDITS.

NOTICE: INSUFFICIENT FUNDS.

NOTICE: TIME REMAINING: 47 HOURS, 58 MINUTES.

Gerald set down the clipboard, and he had forty-seven hours to find two hundred and thirty-three thousand, eight hundred credits.

He looked at the junction. He looked at the rat, which was gnawing on a handrail bracket that was itself unpermitted.

He looked at the message.

He looked at the forty-seven modifications, and he looked at the word decommissioned.

Then a new message arrived. It was not grey. It was red.

NOTICE: INSPECTOR GENERAL ADDENDUM.

NOTICE: WHILE YOUR APPEAL IS VALID, THE INSPECTOR GENERAL NOTES THAT YOUR DUNGEON CONTAINS AN ADDITIONAL VIOLATION NOT PREVIOUSLY IDENTIFIED.

NOTICE: VIOLATION: UNAUTHORIZED DUNGEON CORE OPERATION.

NOTICE: DUNGEON CORES REQUIRE A CORE OPERATOR LICENSE ISSUED BY THE INSPECTOR GENERAL'S OFFICE.

NOTICE: GERALD HUTCHINS DOES NOT HOLD A CORE OPERATOR LICENSE.

NOTICE: CORE OPERATOR LICENSES ARE NOT AVAILABLE TO THE PUBLIC.

NOTICE: CORE OPERATOR LICENSES ARE ISSUED BY THE INSPECTOR GENERAL'S OFFICE AT ITS SOLE DISCRETION.

NOTICE: APPLICATION FOR CORE OPERATOR LICENSE MAY BE FILED.

NOTICE: APPLICATION PROCESSING TIME: 90 DAYS.

NOTICE: TIME REMAINING: 47 HOURS.

Gerald read it. He read it again.

The System wanted him to have a license to be a dungeon core.

He was a dungeon core, and he could not have a license to be what he was.

The red message pulsed once. Then it went grey. Then it was gone.

Gerald picked up his clipboard. His hands were steady now. They had stopped shaking. They stopped shaking the moment he realized the problem was worse than he thought.

He turned to a fresh page, and he wrote: HOW DO I GET THE INSPECTOR GENERAL TO ISSUE ME A LICENSE.

The System did not answer.

He looked at the junction. He looked at the rat. He looked at the handrail bracket that the rat was gnawing.

He looked at the forty-seven walls that were his body and his body that was forty-seven walls and all of it illegal and all of it necessary and all of it about to be torn down.

He looked at the clock.

Forty-seven hours, and he had never once in thirty-one years been unable to find a form.

He had never once in thirty-one years been unable to find a way to make the system work.

He had never once in thirty-one years met a rule he could not beat.

He wrote the rule out by hand, and then he wrote the exception under it.
