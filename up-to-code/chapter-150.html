Chapter 150: Point of Order

# Chapter 150

The seat was not a chair. It was a void.

Gerald sat in it.

The void hummed. It vibrated against his spine, a low-frequency thrum that felt like standing next to a large, poorly insulated HVAC unit.

He adjusted his posture.

His back was straight. His feet were flat on the floor of the void. He placed his hands on his knees, palms down, fingers aligned with his shins.

This was the correct posture for a seated official.

Around him, the other eleven seats floated in a semicircle of grey mist.

Most were empty. Three were occupied by the Ironclad proxies.

They were not human.

They were geometric shapes wrapped in corporate suits, pulsing with a dark, oily light. The first proxy was a cube. The second was a sphere.

The third, who had taken the seat directly opposite Gerald, was a jagged shard of obsidian.

"Point of order," the Cube said. Its voice sounded like gravel grinding in a blender. "The candidate has not submitted Form 14-A: *Declaration of Conflict of Interest*."

Gerald did not look at the Cube. He looked at his own hands.

"My conflict of interest is my dungeon," Gerald said. "It is a 4,000-square-foot sub-basement. It has a Compliance Rating of 68. It is currently compliant with all local egress standards."

The Sphere rolled forward.

"Local standards do not apply to the System Council.

The Ironclad network proposes an amendment to the Core Protection Act. Section 4, Clause 9.

We wish to remove the requirement for 'physical presence' when serving Citations."

Gerald's pen stopped moving.

He had been making a tally mark in the air, a small gesture of habit. It hung there, a faint blue line.

"Physical presence is required," Gerald said. "To serve a Citation, the Inspector must witness the violation. This ensures due process."

"Due process is a luxury," the Obsidian Shard said. It did not have a mouth.

The words just appeared in the air, sharp and jagged.

"Efficiency is the mandate of the System. We propose remote service. A Citation can be issued from a distance of one mile.

This allows for broader enforcement."

This was a trap.

Gerald knew it. He had written the code himself.

Or rather, he had learned to read it.

Section 4, Clause 9 was the only reason he could survive. If he could not be in the room, he could not cite. If he could not cite, he was just a rat in a basement.

The Ironclad network wanted to kill him with paperwork. They wanted to change the definition of the rules so that his specific talent—bureaucratic precision—became obsolete.

Gerald stood up.

His legs shook. The void felt colder now. The cold was not just air; it was the weight of eleven other minds pressing down on him.

"Objection," Gerald said.

The Cube tilted. "Basis?"

"The amendment is structurally unsound," Gerald said. He pulled a form from his pocket.

It was Form 12-C.

He had been carrying it since chapter one.

It was dog-eared, stained with coffee, and crumpled. "Section 4, Clause 9 exists to prevent remote harassment.

If Citations can be served remotely, any Core can be cited for a violation that occurred hours ago. This creates an infinite liability loop. A Core would never be safe.

A Core would never be able to repair a violation because the Citation would always be pending."

The Sphere pulsed. "That is the point. Compliance must be continuous."

"Compliance is a state," Gerald said.

"It is not a punishment. If you allow remote citation, you are not enforcing code. You are enforcing terror.

And terror creates bad data."

Silence.

The Obsidian Shard leaned forward. The jagged edges of its body scraped against the floor of the void, creating sparks that died before they hit the ground.

"Bad data?" the Shard asked.

Gerald looked at the empty seats. Three of them.

One was his.

Two were still open. The Ironclad network had two proxies. They needed one more vote to pass the amendment.

They needed to fill the last seat before the vote began.

If they filled it, Gerald lost.

If Gerald filled it, the vote would be tied.

Or maybe he could bring in an ally.

But he had no allies. He had a rat.

The rat was asleep.

Gerald looked at the third empty seat. It was glowing.

He had accepted the nomination. That meant he was a candidate.

He had not yet been seated.

The system had let him sit in his own void, but the third seat remained vacant because the election was not over. It was a procedural limbo. The Ironclad proxies had tried to fill the other two seats, but the System had blocked them because they were not 'independent entities.' They were proxies.

The System did not recognize proxies as valid voters.

Gerald realized it then.

The System was rigid. It was stupid. It was bureaucratic.

And Gerald was the only one who knew how to use its rigidity against it.

"Point of order," Gerald said. He did not raise his voice.

He kept his tone flat, the voice he used when telling a contractor that their rebar spacing was off by two inches.

"The Ironclad network has submitted two proxies for seats. These are invalid. The System requires independent cores.

The proxies are extensions of a single entity. Therefore, there are three valid candidates for the remaining two seats.

Me.

And two others."

The Cube stopped pulsing. "Who are the others?"

Gerald did not answer. He looked at the air.

He reached into his inventory. He did not have much.

A flashlight. A measuring tape. A half-eaten sandwich.

And a copy of the *Ashford Municipal Building Code, Edition 4*.

He pulled out the book. It was heavy. Real paper. The System usually deleted physical items unless they were 'essential to gameplay.' The Code was essential to Gerald.

He opened the book to Appendix J: *Appeals and Heuristics*.

There was a rule in there. A small, obscure rule. Rule J-14. *Nomination Substitution.*

A candidate could withdraw their nomination in favor of a 'Qualified Entity' if the Entity had a Compliance Rating above 50 and had filed at least ten valid Citations in the last cycle.

Gerald had filed forty-two Citations.

He had a Compliance Rating of 68.

He could nominate someone else.

But who?

He had no friends. He had no army. He had a rat.

The rat had a Compliance Rating of 12.

Wait.

Gerald looked at the rat. The rat was not in his inventory. The rat was in his dungeon. But the rat was a 'spawn entity.' Spawn entities were part of the Core.

Could a Core nominate its own spawn?

Rule J-14 said 'Qualified Entity.' It did not say 'Independent Core.' It said 'Entity.'

Gerald looked at the Obsidian Shard. The Shard was waiting. The Sphere was waiting. The Cube was calculating.

If Gerald stayed, the vote would be 2-1 in favor of the Ironclad network once they filled the last seat. Or maybe they would try to fill it with a proxy, and the vote would be invalid.

If Gerald withdrew, he could nominate himself again. But that wasted time.

He needed a third vote.

He needed an ally.

Gerald thought about the adventurer parties. The ones who kept breaking into his dungeon. The ones who complained about the handrails. The ones who tried to burn his mold.

They were not Cores. They could not vote.

Gerald thought about the Inspector General. The IG who audited him. The IG had never spoken to him. The IG had just sent notices.

Notices were communication.

Gerald closed the book.

"I withdraw my nomination," Gerald said.

The Sphere made a sound like a laugh. "Good. The seat is now open for Ironclad appointment."

"No," Gerald said. "I withdraw my nomination to substitute a Qualified Entity. Per Rule J-14."

The void went silent.

The Cube froze. "J-14 is for emergency substitutions. It requires a Level 5 Hazard."

"This is a Level 5 Hazard," Gerald said.

"Systemic corruption of the Code. The Code is the foundation of the System. If the Code is amended by force, the System fails.

That is a Level 5 Hazard."

The Sphere pulsed brighter. "You are bluffing."

"I am citing you," Gerald said.

He didn't have a pen. He used his finger. He wrote the Citation in the air.

**[CITATION ISSUED: Ironclad Proxy 2.]** **[Violation: Improper Nomination Procedure.]** **[Severity: Critical.]** **[Penalty: Immediate Seizure of Voting Rights.]**

The Sphere screamed. It was not a human scream.

It was the sound of metal tearing.

The Sphere's light flickered. The oily darkness around it peeled away. The Sphere shrank.

It became smaller. Less important.

The Sphere was debuffed. Its voting power was suspended.

"Procedural error," the Cube shouted. "You cannot cite a voter!"

"Voters are subject to the Code," Gerald said.

"Section 1, Clause 1.

All entities within the System are subject to the Code. There are no exemptions for voters.

If you were exempt, you would be above the law. And nothing is above the law."

The Cube turned to the Obsidian Shard. "Do something."

The Shard did not move. It was watching Gerald.

Gerald looked at the empty seat.

It was still glowing.

He needed a substitute.

He looked at the System interface. He looked for the nomination field.

He typed in a name.

*Target: Gerald Hutchins.*

He nominated himself. Again.

It was a trick. A recursive loop. By withdrawing and immediately re-nominating himself, he reset the timer for the seat's availability. The System could not fill the seat with an Ironclad proxy because the nomination was 'active.'

It was a bureaucratic stall.

It bought him time.

The Cube raged. "This is a violation of the one-person-one-vote rule!"

"It is a violation of the one-person-one-*nomination* rule," Gerald said. "I am currently in two states.

I am a candidate.

I am also an inspector. As an inspector, I have the right to enforce the Code. As a candidate, I have the right to be seated.

The System must resolve the conflict.

It cannot seat a proxy while a valid candidate is in procedural limbo."

The void shook.

The floor beneath Gerald's feet cracked. A notification appeared in the air. It was red.

**[SYSTEM ERROR: CONFLICTING STATE DETECTED.]** **[INITIATING ARBITRATION PROTOCOL.]** **[ARBITRATOR: INSPECTOR GENERAL.]**

The IG was coming.

Gerald had forced the IG to intervene. He had used the Code to break the Code.

The Obsidian Shard finally moved. It slid toward Gerald. The jagged edges cut the air.

"You are slowing this down," the Shard said.

Its voice was cold. "But you cannot stop the vote. The Ironclad network controls the market.

We control the resources. If we cannot change the Code through legislation, we will change it through force.

We will destroy your dungeon.

We will starve your rat.

We will bury you."

Gerald felt a chill. Not the cold of the void. The cold of his own blood.

He looked at the rat. The rat was still asleep. It was curled up on a stack of citations. It looked peaceful.

Gerald looked at the Shard.

"You will have to get through the door," Gerald said.

The Shard laughed. It was a horrible, grinding sound. "We have already sent our team. They are in your sub-basement."

Gerald's heart stopped.

He checked his dungeon map.

The map showed four red dots. They were in the ventilation shaft.

The ventilation shaft was not up to code. It was narrow. It was dusty. It had a non-compliant bend.

But it was *his* dungeon.

And he knew every inch of it.

Gerald looked at the Cube. The Cube was trying to override the arbitration. The Cube was trying to vote.

Gerald looked at the Sphere. The Sphere was shrinking. It was losing power.

Gerald looked at the Shard. The Shard was smiling. Or it was trying to smile. It looked like a crack in reality.

"If you destroy my dungeon," Gerald said, "you destroy the evidence. You destroy the proof that the Ironclad network is violating Section 8. The audit trail will be deleted.

The System will reset. You will win.

But you will have to explain it to the Inspector General."

The Shard paused.

The IG was arriving.

A figure appeared in the center of the void. It was not human. It was a floating eye, surrounded by floating scales. The scales weighed the truth.

The IG looked at Gerald.

The IG looked at the Ironclad proxies.

The IG looked at the map of Gerald's dungeon.

The IG saw the four red dots.

The IG saw the narrow shaft.

The IG saw the bend that violated the minimum turning radius for a Class C Firefighter.

The IG did not look angry. The IG looked bored.

**[ARBITRATION COMPLETE.]** **[RULING: VACANCY REMAINS OPEN.]** **[NOTICE: GERALD HUTCHINS IS CITED FOR FAILURE TO MAINTAIN VENTILATION INTEGRITY.]** **[PENALTY: REDUCED AIRFLOW BY 40%.]** **[DEBUFF APPLIED TO ALL SPAWNS: -20% TO REGEN RATE.]**

Gerald felt the weight of the penalty.

His rat would slow down. The mold would grow slower.

But the vote was stalled.

The Ironclad network had to fix their own violations before they could vote.

The Sphere was still shrinking. The Cube was still frozen. The Shard was still staring at Gerald.

"Next time," the Shard said. "We will not use proxies. We will use heroes."

Gerald looked at the notification.

**[CITATION RECEIVED.]**

He had been cited. By the System. For his own dungeon.

He was the victim and the defendant.

He looked at his rat. The rat stirred. It opened one eye. It looked tired.

Gerald looked at the IG.

"Can I appeal?" Gerald asked.

The IG blinked. A single, slow blink.

**[APPEAL WINDOW: OPEN.]** **[TIME REMAINING: 10 MINUTES.]**

Gerald picked up his pen.

He had ten minutes to prove that a narrow ventilation shaft was actually *better* for fire safety.

He had to lie. Or he had to find a loophole so small and so stupid that the System would believe it.

He looked at the Code. Appendix J. Page 44.

*Exception for Historical Structures.*

His dungeon was not historical. It was new.

But the library above it was built in 1920.

Was his dungeon part of the library?

Technically, no. It was a separate core.

But it was *under* the library.

If he argued that the dungeon was 'integral to the structural integrity of the historic library,' then the ventilation shaft might be exempt from modern codes.

It was a stretch. It was a lie.

It was the only chance he had.

Gerald wrote.

The clock ticked down.

Nine minutes.

Eight minutes.

The Obsidian Shard leaned closer. It was waiting for him to fail.

Gerald's hand shook. He steadied it. He thought about the rat.

He thought about the mold.

He thought about the door widths.

He wrote.

The System watched.

And in the sub-basement, four red dots moved toward the ventilation shaft. They were armed. They were fast. And they were not there to inspect.

They were there to kill the rat.
