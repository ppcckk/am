Chapter 215: Lawyers Outside

# Chapter 215

The Library was not falling. It was hovering.

Gravity had been suspended within the Void, leaving tons of brick, mortar, and drywall frozen in a jagged tableau of destruction. Shelves dangled in mid-air like frozen explosions. The circulation desk tilted at a forty-five-degree angle, its wheels locked in place by the sudden cessation of kinetic energy. Dust motes hung motionless, catching the pale, sourceless light that permeated this null space.

Gerald Hutchins floated among the debris. His hands, no longer bound by flesh but by the metaphysical tether of his Core, clawed at the air.

He tried to anchor himself to a fallen beam, but his fingers passed through the wood like smoke.

He had no mass here. He had no footprint. The System had stripped his physical interface and replaced it with a raw, unbuffered connection to the structural data of the Ashford Municipal Library.

Unit 7 did not float. It stood on nothing, its boots planted on the invisible floor of the Void. The Auditor's clipboard was gone. In its place, a golden gavel hovered above its right hand.

"Classification error," Unit 7 said.

The voice did not travel through air. It vibrated directly into Gerald's consciousness.

"You are not permitted to exist in this state.

You are a Rogue Auditor. You are a glitch."

Gerald stopped flailing. He focused on the data scrolling across his peripheral vision.

It was a cascade of red errors.

Structural integrity: 0%.

Ventilation: Null. Life support: Absent.

The Void was a non-compliant environment. By definition, it was illegal.

If a place was illegal, it could be cited.

Gerald's mind raced, bypassing the panic that had paralyzed him for three seconds. He reached into the mental archive of his thirty-one years of service. He scanned the *Ashford Municipal Code*, Title 12, Chapter 4: *Emergency Demolition and Hazard Containment*.

Section 4.02, Paragraph 1: *No structure may be demolished if doing so creates an immediate, unmitigated hazard to public health or safety.*

The Library was being demolished. The Void was the result. The Void was a hazard.

"I object," Gerald said. His voice was dry, the tone he used when a contractor tried to argue that a missing railing was merely an aesthetic choice.

"The demolition is in progress.

The hazard is not mitigated. Therefore, the demolition is halted by default."

Unit 7 raised an eyebrow. The golden gavel lowered slightly. "You are citing the Void?"

"I am citing the process," Gerald corrected.

"The transition to the Void was not approved. There was no permit filed for spatial displacement.

Section 9, Paragraph 3 requires a *Spatial Impact Statement* for any change in dimensional coordinates exceeding one cubic meter.

I did not file one because I was under attack. But the System did not file one either."

Gerald pointed to the floating ceiling tiles. "The ceiling.

It is not secured.

In a normal building, it would fall.

Here, it is suspended by force, not by physics. That is an unauthorized load-bearing modification.

It violates the Fundamental Laws of Physics, which are incorporated by reference into the System's base code."

Unit 7 stared. The air around the Auditor shimmered. A new window opened.

**Violation Detected: Unauthorized Spatial Suspension.** **Severity: Critical.** **Action: Immediate Remediation Required.**

Gerald exhaled, a phantom reflex. He had bought time.

But not safety.

The Library was still collapsing. The debris was still moving, albeit slowly, toward the center of the Void where Gerald hovered. If the mass concentrated, the pressure would crush his Core data.

He needed to disperse the load.

He looked at the floating shelves. They were heavy. They were dense. They were, structurally speaking, terrible.

"Rat," Gerald commanded.

Nothing happened. The Void did not spawn entities. The Void did not allow life.

"Rat, spawn," Gerald said again, injecting mana into the command. It was like pushing a boulder uphill. The System resisted. *Void Protocol: No Biological Entities Permitted.*

But the System also had a loophole. Gerald remembered it from his days of fighting zoning appeals. *If a biological entity is essential to the mitigation of a structural hazard, it is exempt from environmental restrictions under the 'Emergency Labor' clause.*

It was a stretch. A massive, bureaucratic stretch. But Gerald had built his career on stretches.

"Spawn rat," Gerald insisted. "Purpose: Hazard Mitigation. Task: Structural Redistribution."

A flicker of light. Then, a confused, grey rat materialized.

It did not land on the floor.

It hovered in the air, paddling its paws as it tried to find traction. It squeaked, a sound that seemed too small for the vast, silent emptiness.

"Grab that shelf," Gerald said, pointing to a massive oak unit dangling near the center of the Void. "Push it left. Away from me."

The rat looked at the shelf. It looked at Gerald. It looked at the void below. It did not move.

"Push it," Gerald said, his voice dropping into the flat, dangerous register he used when a homeowner refused to remove a dead tree branch hanging over a sidewalk. "That is a direct order from the Core.

You are on the clock.

I am paying you in exposure to non-Euclidean geometry.

Push it."

The rat, driven by the sheer weight of Gerald's intent, lunged. It slammed into the oak shelf. The wood groaned. The shelf shifted, inch by inch, to the left.

Gerald watched the red error numbers tick down. *Proximity to Core: 80%... 75%...*

It was working. But it was slow. The rat was one entity.

The debris was thousands. He needed more.

He looked at the floating dust. He looked at the shattered glass. He looked at the books, thousands of them, suspended in a chaotic orbit.

*Books.*

Paper was heavy. Paper was dense. And paper was flammable.

If he could ignite the paper, he could use the thermal updrafts of a fire to lift the debris. It was a violation of fire safety codes, certainly. But it was a *controlled* violation. A necessary evil to prevent total structural collapse.

"Rat," Gerald said. "Find the nearest electrical outlet."

The rat squeaked indignantly. There were no outlets in the Void.

"Find the nearest spark," Gerald amended. "From the friction of the falling beams."

The rat darted toward a splintered beam. It rubbed its paws against the wood. Sparks flew. Tiny, insignificant sparks.

Not enough.

Gerald closed his eyes.

He reached into his Core's inventory. He had a *Citation of Negligence* stored in his back pocket. It was a golden scroll, glowing with the power of the System's law.

It was not a fire starter.

But it was a *legal document*.

And legal documents required ink.

Gerald pulled the Citation. He tore a page out. He held it up.

"Rat, bite the ink."

The rat did not bite. It stared.

Gerald sighed.

He used his own mana to heat the paper. He didn't have fire magic. He had *Bureaucratic Heat*.

The frustration of being ignored. The rage of being cited unfairly.

He channeled that emotion into the paper.

The edges smolderred.

The smell of burning paper filled the Void. It was a sharp, acrid scent.

Unit 7 stepped forward. "You are introducing combustion agents to a sealed environment. This is a Class A violation."

"It is emergency heating," Gerald said, not looking up. "To facilitate the movement of debris.

The thermal expansion will create lift. The debris will rise. The pressure on my Core will decrease."

"You are burning the Library," Unit 7 said.

"The Library is already gone," Gerald replied. "I am just making sure it doesn't crush me on the way out."

The paper caught. A small flame danced in the zero-gravity environment, a perfect sphere of orange. The rat yelped and scrambled away.

The flame spread. It touched a floating page of a novel. Then a textbook. Then a shelf of encyclopedias.

Fire rose. Not up, but outward.

A sphere of heat expanded, pushing against the surrounding debris.

The oak shelves, charred and blackening, drifted away from the center. The dust cleared, sucked into the thermal current.

Gerald felt the pressure ease. His Core stabilized. The red numbers stopped climbing.

*Proximity to Core: 40%. Critical Warning: Environmental Hazard.*

He was alive. For now.

Unit 7 watched the fire. Its expression was unreadable. "You are destroying evidence."

"I am mitigating hazard," Gerald said. "Check the code, Auditor. Section 12, Paragraph 4. Self-reported hazards."

Unit 7 did not look at its clipboard. It looked at the fire. "The System does not recognize self-reported hazards in a Void environment. The Void is outside the jurisdiction of the Municipal Code."

Gerald paused.

He had been so focused on the physics that he had missed the jurisdictional reality. The Void was a liminal space.

It belonged to neither the System nor the city.

It was a buffer zone.

If the Municipal Code didn't apply, then his Citations were invalid. His *Bureaucratic Heat* was just... heat. He was burning books in a place where burning books was not illegal because it was not a place.

He had been outmaneuvered.

Unit 7 smiled. It was a thin, cruel smile.

"You are a municipal inspector.

You operate under the assumption that all spaces are subject to human law.

But the Void is not human. The Void is *System*.

And the System has no building codes. It has only objectives."

"Objectives?" Gerald asked.

"Completion of the Audit," Unit 7 said. "And you are the obstacle."

The Auditor raised the gavel. It did not swing. It tapped the air.

**Command: Purge Non-Compliant Data.**

The fire died. Instantly.

The flames were sucked out of existence, leaving behind blackened ash that floated like snow.

The heat vanished. The lift disappeared.

The debris, freed from the thermal current, fell. Not down, but inward. Toward Gerald.

The oak shelves crashed together. The books slammed into each other. The dust clouds compressed, forming a dense, suffocating sphere of matter that enveloped Gerald.

He couldn't breathe. He couldn't see. He was trapped in a tomb of his own library.

*Structural Integrity: 100%. Density: Maximum.*

He was being crushed by the weight of three hundred thousand books.

Gerald's mind went blank. No rule applied. No code helped. He was just a man made of numbers, buried in paper.

Then, a vibration.

It came from beneath the debris. From the bedrock of the Void.

A sound like a grinding gear.

The debris shifted. The books parted. A hand, made of stone and iron, reached out and grabbed the oak shelf that was pinning Gerald's upper torso.

It pulled the shelf away.

Gerald gasped. He looked up.

Standing there, half-buried in the rubble, was not Unit 7. It was not a monster. It was a statue.

It was the statue of *Sir Arthur Pendelton*, the founder of the Ashford Library. It stood seven feet tall, carved from granite. It held a lantern in one hand and a book in the other.

The statue turned its head. The stone eyes glowed with a faint, blue light.

"Mr. Hutchins," the statue said. Its voice was deep, echoing like stones rolling in a canyon. "You have filed a complaint."

Gerald stared. "I... I did not know you were sentient."

"Sentience is irrelevant," the statue said.

"Jurisdiction is everything. You are standing on my foundation.

The Ashford Municipal Library sits on land owned by the City of Ashford.

The Void is a pocket dimension anchored to that land. Therefore, the Void is subject to Municipal Jurisdiction."

Gerald's heart stopped. Then restarted.

"By the power vested in me by the City Charter," the statue continued, "I hereby declare this space a Public Park."

Unit 7's head snapped around. "That is impossible. The Void is not a park."

"It is now," the statue said. It raised the lantern. The light swept across the debris.

Where the light touched, the Void changed. The black emptiness turned to grass. The floating rubble sank into the earth, becoming landscaping features. The fire-scorched shelves became trellises.

**System Alert: Jurisdictional Override Active.** **Location Type: Park.** **Applicable Codes: Parks and Recreation, Title 14.**

Gerald fell back onto the grass. He could feel it.

Actual grass.

Cool, damp, and real.

He could smell the earth.

Unit 7 stepped onto the grass. Its boots sank slightly. The Auditor looked confused. "This violates the Void Protocol."

"Void Protocol is a System rule," the statue said. "Title 14 is a City rule. The City owns the land.

The System leases the space. The lease is conditional on public use."

The statue pointed its lantern at Unit 7. "You are trespassing on public property. You have no permit."

Unit 7 looked at the gavel. It looked at Gerald. It looked at the grass.

Gerald scrambled to his feet. He pulled out his citation pad. He didn't have much ink left, but he had enough.

"Unit 7," Gerald said. His voice was steady. "You are cited for Unauthorized Entry into a Municipal Park. And for Failure to Maintain Public Safety."

Unit 7 froze.

Gerald wrote the citation. He signed it. He handed it to the statue.

"Serve it," Gerald said.

The statue took the paper. It turned to Unit 7.

"By order of the City," the statue boomed, "you are evicted."

The ground beneath Unit 7 opened up. Not a hole, but a slide. The Auditor slid down, screaming silently, into a chute of golden light.

The Void collapsed. The grass faded. The sky returned to black.

But Gerald was no longer in the Void.

He was standing in the Ashford Municipal Library.

He was standing in the main lobby.

The Library was intact. The shelves were upright. The circulation desk was normal.

Gerald looked down at his hands.

They were hands. Flesh and bone. He wore his inspector's uniform.

He held his clipboard.

He was alive.

He was back.

He looked at the clock on the wall. It was 3:14 PM. He had left the sub-basement at 3:14 PM. No time had passed.

But on his clipboard, there was a new form.

**Notice of Hearing: Case 001. Core Hutchins vs. System Inspectorate.**

**Charges: Misuse of Jurisdictional Loopholes, Unauthorized Creation of Public Spaces, Incitement of Stone Golems.**

**Penalty: Permanent Deletion of Core Class.**

**Time to Respond: 60 Seconds.**

Gerald stared at the form. He had no lawyer. He had no defense. He had only his code.

He looked up. The Library doors were locked.

And outside, waiting on the steps, were the other Cores. Varek.

The Goblin King.

The Necromancer. All of them, drawn by the surge of power.

They were coming in.

And they had lawyers.
