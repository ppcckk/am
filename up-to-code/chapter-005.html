Chapter 5

Core-4471's avatar was a dodecahedron. It rotated slowly at the far end of the hearing room, which was otherwise identical to Room 7B of the Ashford Municipal Building. Same beige walls. Same water-stained ceiling tile in the upper left corner. Same motivational poster that read TEAMWORK: BECAUSE NOBODY IS AS GOOD AS EVERYBODY.

Gerald had spent eleven years in that room. He knew every crack in the linoleum.

The System had reproduced it faithfully. Gerald found this either comforting or deeply unsettling, and he hadn't decided which.

The adjudicator was a featureless grey figure seated at a raised desk. It held no gavel. It held a clipboard. Gerald appreciated this. A gavel would have been performative. A clipboard was honest.

"Citation 4471-A-0019 is under appeal," the adjudicator said. Its voice had no gender, no age, no warmth. "Appellant: Core-0000. Respondent: Core-4471. Appellant has filed on grounds of Zone Non-Compliance. Appellant, state your argument."

Gerald stood. He didn't know why he stood. He was a dungeon core. He didn't have legs. But the hearing interface gave him a body, and his body stood, and that was apparently how this worked.

"The citation designates the target area as Class 2 residential," Gerald said. "Per Section 9.3 of the Compliance Framework, Class 2 residential requires a minimum lot area of 400 square meters. The zone boundary specified in the citation encompasses 380 square meters. The citation is therefore built on a non-compliant designation and is void under Section 14(b)."

The dodecahedron stopped rotating.

"That's a rounding error," Core-4471 said. Its voice came from everywhere and nowhere, like a PA system in a parking garage. "The zone boundary was approximated. The actual lot includes a 20-square-meter buffer zone that wasn't included in the citation text."

"Section 9.3, subsection 4," Gerald said. "Zone designations must be exact to within one percent tolerance. A 20-square-meter shortfall on a 400-square-meter minimum is a five percent deviation. That's five times the tolerance."

The dodecahedron rotated again. Faster this time. "The buffer zone was omitted for brevity. It's standard practice to abbreviate boundary descriptions in citation text."

"Section 14(b) does not recognize abbreviation as a valid defense for zone non-compliance," Gerald said. "A citation is a legal instrument. It must contain the full and accurate zone designation. Omitting boundary data is not brevity. It's a defect."

The adjudicator made a note on its clipboard. Gerald couldn't see what it wrote.

"Respondent, do you have a rebuttal?"

Core-4471's avatar pulsed once, a color Gerald couldn't name. "The appellant is exploiting a technicality. The intent of the citation was valid. The appellant's structure violates the spirit of the zoning code regardless of the boundary description."

"The Compliance Framework does not recognize intent," Gerald said. "It recognizes compliance. That's the whole point."

The adjudicator set its clipboard down. "The appellant's argument is sustained. The citation is void. Respondent, you are found to have issued a Faux Citation under Section 14(b). Penalty: 48-hour compliance debuff. All spawned entities within your dungeon will suffer a fifty percent reduction in all primary attributes for the duration."

The dodecahedron went very still.

"That's not fair," Core-4471 said.

"It's in the Framework," Gerald said. "Section 14(b), paragraph 3. Faux Citations carry a mandatory penalty. You can appeal that, if you want."

"I'm not going to appeal."

"No. You probably won't."

The hearing room dissolved. Gerald was back in his corridor. The citation interface was gone. In its place, a new notification blinked in the upper left of his vision.

PUBLIC NOTICE ISSUED. APPEAL FILED BY CORE-0000. ALL CORES WITHIN 500-METER RADIUS NOTIFIED.

Gerald stared at it.

He'd known about Public Notices. Every appeal triggered one. It was in the Framework. He'd read the section. He'd read all the sections. He'd read them twice.

But reading a section and experiencing its consequences were different things. The notice was a ping. A System-wide broadcast. For one second, every Core within 500 meters of Gerald's location now knew that Core-0000 existed, that it was here, and that it had just won a legal argument.

That was the cost. Gerald had known it was a cost. He'd accepted it because the alternative was a cottage.

He pulled up the notice details. Three Cores had received it. Core-4471, obviously. Core-3318, which was probably the small fungal network three corridors over. And Core-0001.

Gerald pulled up Core-0001's profile. The System gave him the barest minimum: compliance rating unknown, age unknown, threat level classified. The only thing he could see was that Core-0001 had been operating for at least six months longer than Gerald had been a Core. In a System that was barely eight months old, that meant Core-0001 had been one of the first.

He closed the profile. He didn't want to think about it yet. He had bigger problems.

The zone designation. He'd used the zone designation to win the appeal. But the zone designation was a thing you were supposed to file before you built anything. Gerald had never filed one. He'd been a Core for four days and he'd never filed a zone permit because he hadn't known he needed to.

He opened the Compliance Framework and searched for "zone permit."

Section 3.1: All Cores must file a Zone Permit with the System prior to constructing any structure. Failure to file constitutes Operating Without a Valid Zone Permit. Violation severity: Class 3. Penalty: Immediate dissolution of all structures built during the non-compliant period.

Gerald read it twice.

He had built a corridor. He had built a small chamber. He had spawned three rats and a slime. All of it, built without a Zone Permit.

He opened the filing interface. It was there, buried in a submenu he'd never explored. The form was simple. You specified your zone designation, your lot boundaries, and your intended use. He filled it out. Class 2 residential. 380 square meters. Intended use: low-density dungeon operations.

He submitted it.

The System rejected it.

REJECTED. Zone designation "Class 2 residential" requires minimum lot area of 400 square meters. Submitted area: 380 square meters. Please revise.

Gerald stared at the rejection. His corridor was 380 square meters. That was the space he had. That was the space the System had given him when it converted him. He couldn't expand it. The walls were solid rock and he didn't have the mana to carve more.

He tried Class 3 residential. That required 250 square meters minimum. His 380 qualified. He submitted again.

REJECTED. Class 3 residential permits are restricted to Cores with a Compliance Rating of 40 or above. Your current Compliance Rating: 12.

Twelve. Of course it was twelve. He'd built a corridor with no ventilation, no fire exits, no electrical grounding. Of course his rating was twelve.

He sat with it. He ran through every zone classification in the Framework. Commercial required a minimum of 600 square meters. Industrial required 1000. Mixed-use required 800. Agricultural required 2000. He didn't qualify for any of them.

There was one more. Unzoned. It appeared at the bottom of the list, in small grey text, the way all the worst options always appeared at the bottom. Unzoned meant you had no permit at all. You were operating outside the system. It wasn't illegal. But it meant you had no protections. You couldn't issue Citations. You couldn't file appeals. You couldn't claim the passive resistances that came with code compliance. You were, in Gerald's professional vocabulary, a structure without a certificate of occupancy.

He could file as Unzoned. It would stop the dissolution penalty. But it would lock him out of every tool he'd just used to win his first real fight.

The alternative was to raise his Compliance Rating to 40 before he could get a Class 3 permit. He didn't know how long that would take. He didn't know what the thresholds were for individual code categories. He'd need to install ventilation, fire suppression, electrical grounding, structural reinforcement. Each one cost mana he didn't have. Each one took time he might not have.

Because Core-0001 had received the Public Notice.

Gerald checked the timestamp. The notice had gone out four minutes ago. Core-0001's response was immediate. A new notification appeared.

CITATION SERVED. Citation 0001-A-0447. Filed by Core-0001. Violation: Operating Without a Valid Zone Permit. Zone designation: Unfiled. Penalty upon execution: Immediate dissolution of all structures.

Gerald looked at the citation. He looked at the time. He looked at the appeal window.

He had six hours to file an appeal.

He had no defense. He genuinely, actually, did not have a Zone Permit. He couldn't argue the citation was invalid. He couldn't argue the zone designation was wrong. He couldn't argue intent or abbreviation or tolerance. He had no permit. The citation was correct.

Unless.

Gerald pulled up the citation's details. He looked at the zone designation Core-0001 had specified. Unfiled. That was accurate. Gerald had never filed.

But Core-0001 had specified a zone designation in the citation. It had labeled Gerald's location as "Unfiled." Was "Unfiled" a valid zone designation?

Gerald opened the Compliance Framework. He searched for "Unfiled" as a zone category.

It wasn't there.

The zone categories were Class 1 residential, Class 2 residential, Class 3 residential, commercial, industrial, mixed-use, agricultural, and Unzoned. There was no category called "Unfiled."

Core-0001 had used a zone designation that didn't exist.

Gerald felt something cold move through him. Not excitement. Gerald didn't get excited. It was more like the feeling of finding a load-bearing wall that had been cut without a permit. The kind of feeling that meant someone else had made a mistake and was going to have to pay for it.

But he'd just argued that a citation with a non-compliant zone designation was void. That was the exact same argument. Section 14(b). A citation must contain a valid zone designation. "Unfiled" was not a valid zone designation. The citation was void.

Except.

If the citation was void, then Gerald had no permit, no defense, and Core-0001 would simply refile with a correct designation. Gerald would win the appeal and lose the war.

Unless he filed his Zone Permit first.

He could file as Unzoned. It would be valid. It would mean he had a permit, even if it was the worst permit. And if he filed before Core-0001's citation executed, then the citation's claim of "Unfiled" would be factually incorrect at the time of execution.

But Unzoned meant no protections. No Citations. No appeals. No resistances.

Gerald opened the filing interface again. Unzoned. He filled out the form. He submitted it.

ACCEPTED. Core-0000 is now designated Unzoned. Zone Permit filed. Compliance Rating unchanged. Passive resistance eligibility: None. Citation authority: None. Appeal authority: None.

He had a permit. A terrible permit. But a permit.

The citation interface updated. Citation 0001-A-0447 now showed a status change. The violation had been "Operating Without a Valid Zone Permit." Gerald now had a valid Zone Permit. The violation was cured.

But the citation had already been served. The question was whether a cured violation still executed.

Gerald opened the appeal interface. He filed.

The hearing room materialized again. The same beige walls. The same water-stained tile. The adjudicator with its clipboard.

Core-0001's avatar was not a geometric shape. It was a hand. A single hand, palm down, fingers spread, hovering above the table like it was pressing something flat. It didn't rotate. It didn't pulse. It just sat there.

"Citation 0001-A-0447 is under appeal," the adjudicator said. "Appellant: Core-0000. Respondent: Core-0001. Appellant, state your argument."

"The citation alleges Operating Without a Valid Zone Permit," Gerald said. "At the time of citation, the appellant had no permit. However, the appellant has since filed a Zone Permit designation of Unzoned. The violation is cured."

The hand did not move.

"Cured violations do not void citations," the adjudicator said. "The violation existed at the time of service. The citation is valid. The penalty executes unless the appeal succeeds on other grounds."

Gerald looked at the hand. "I'd like to challenge the zone designation specified in the citation. The citation lists my zone as 'Unfiled.' 'Unfiled' is not a recognized zone designation under the Compliance Framework. Section 14(b) requires a valid zone designation. The citation is void."

The adjudicator paused. Its clipboard hovered in mid-air.

"Respondent, rebuttal?"

The hand moved. One finger extended, pointing at Gerald. "The appellant filed an Unzoned permit after the citation was served. The citation's zone designation was accurate at the time of service. The appellant was Unfiled. The designation reflects the state of the appellant's compliance at the moment of citation."

"Section 14(b) does not specify temporal validity," Gerald said. "It requires that the citation contain a valid zone designation. 'Unfiled' is not a zone designation. It is a description of a state. There is a difference."

The adjudicator's clipboard dropped an inch. Gerald watched it. He'd seen this before. It was the moment before a ruling that would go badly.

"Section 14(b), paragraph 1," the adjudicator said. "A citation must specify the zone in which the violation occurred. 'Unfiled' is not a zone. The citation is void."

The hand went still.

"Respondent, you are found to have issued a Faux Citation under Section 14(b). Penalty: 48-hour compliance debuff. All spawned entities within your dungeon will suffer a fifty percent reduction in all primary attributes for the duration."

The hand curled. Slowly. Like a fist closing.

The hearing room dissolved.

Gerald was back in his corridor. The three rats were in the chamber. The slime was in the corner, pulsing softly. Everything was intact. The citation had been voided. Core-0001 was debuffed for 48 hours.

Gerald had won.

He opened his Compliance Rating. It was still 12. He was Unzoned. He had no passive resistances. He couldn't issue Citations. He couldn't file appeals. He was a structure without a certificate of occupancy, operating in a System that rewarded compliance.

A new notification appeared.

AUDIT SCHEDULED. Inspector General audit of Core-0000. Timeframe: 72 hours. Scope: Full compliance review.

Gerald read it three times.

An audit wasn't a citation. An audit was the System checking your work. And Gerald was Unzoned. He had no permit that carried any protections. He had a Compliance Rating of 12. He had no ventilation, no fire suppression, no grounding, no structural reinforcement.

An audit would find all of it.

The penalty for a failed audit was not dissolution. It was worse. It was a Compliance Lock. Your dungeon froze. No new spawns. No new structures. No mana generation. Your existing entities decayed until they died.

Seventy-two hours.

Gerald looked at his corridor. 380 square meters. No permit that qualified for any zone category above Unzoned. No way to raise his Compliance Rating without mana. No way to get mana without raising his Compliance Rating.

He sat with it. The rats squeaked. The slime pulsed.

Then a new notification appeared, and Gerald felt the cold feeling again.

CORE-0001 HAS FILED A JOINT CITATION WITH CORES 0002, 0003, 0004, 0005, 0006, 0007, 0008, 0009, AND 0010. CITATION TYPE: MULTILATERAL COMPLIANCE REVIEW. TARGET: CORE-0000.

Gerald counted the Cores. Ten. Core-0001 had brought nine others.

A Multilateral Compliance Review wasn't a citation. It was a vote. If a majority of Cores within range voted to review your compliance, the System executed the review immediately. No appeal. No hearing. No window.

The vote was already open.
