Chapter 211: Inspected by a Pen

# Chapter 211

Gerald pressed his thumb against the cracked glass until a sharp pain confirmed he was still awake. Gerald Hutchins wiped his glasses on his shirt, leaving a smear of soot across the lenses.

He did not look at the hole he had just punched through the load-bearing wall.

He looked at the airflow monitor hovering in his peripheral vision. The ventilation rating had dropped to Critical. The structural rating had stabilized at thirty, hovering dangerously close to the shutdown threshold of twenty-five.

A heavy thud echoed from the library above.

Then another. The floorboards groaned under the weight of shifting timber.

Gerald straightened his tie, a reflex that felt absurd given that he currently had no torso, only a disembodied consciousness anchored to the dungeon core.

Still, the posture helped.

"Inspector?" A voice crackled through the stone walls. It was not the System. It was a voice. Human.

Gerald focused his awareness toward the source. A man in a charred tunic was standing at the base of the collapsed pillar.

He held a glowing staff that flickered with unstable energy, which was Kaelen, a mid-tier adventurer who had been trying to breach Gerald's perimeter for three weeks.

Kaelen was bleeding from a gash on his forehead, but he was smiling.

"You did it," Kaelen said, his voice echoing in the confined space. "The wall is gone. We can pass."

"The wall is not gone," Gerald corrected, his mental voice projecting into the minds of everyone in the room. "The wall has been repurposed. There is a difference."

Kaelen stepped forward, his boots crunching on debris. He pointed his staff at the new opening.

"Don't play games with me, old man.

My party is stuck on the other side. The ceiling supports are failing.

If that hole doesn't stabilize, the whole library collapses.

We all die."

Gerald ignored the threat, and he pulled up the inspection overlay. The new breach was jagged, rough-hewn.

It violated Section 4.2: *Provision of Openings in Load-Bearing Structures*.

Specifically, it lacked a lintel.

"State your name and party affiliation," Gerald said.

"We don't have time for this," Kaelen snapped. "Look at the cracks. They're spreading."

"Name," Gerald repeated. Flat. Unyielding.

Kaelen hesitated. The adventurer's eyes narrowed.

He looked at the other members of his party who were huddled behind him, waiting for permission to enter the new zone.

A healer, a rogue, and a heavily armored fighter, and they looked terrified. Not of Gerald.

Of the ceiling.

"Kaelen of the Iron Vanguard," the man said through gritted teeth. "We are licensed adventurers, Class B. We have paid our guild dues. We have a right to dungeon access under the System Charter."

"Access is granted upon compliance," Gerald said. "Your party is currently in a zone with zero egress signage.

You are violating Fire Code 19-B.

I am issuing a verbal warning. This will be logged.

"

Kaelen threw his hands up.

"You're insane! The ceiling is cracking! Do you hear that?"

Gerald heard it, and the stone was screaming. A high-pitched whine of stress fractures propagating through the bedrock.

But Gerald did not panic.

Panic was inefficient. Panic did not fill out forms.

"I hear it," Gerald said. "And I note that the sound is consistent with improper bracing.

You are standing in a hazardous zone.

Move to the left side of the room.

Away from the pillar.

"

"Left is where the monsters spawned!" the rogue yelled from the back.

"Then you spawned them in violation of Spatial Density regulations," Gerald said. "Which means they are not yours to control. Which means they are a nuisance. Which means I have the authority to remove them."

The air in the room shifted. The mana crystals pulsed with a rhythmic, angry red light.

Kaelen stepped forward, his staff glowing brighter.

"You're not going to remove us. You're going to fix the hole.

That's your job. You're a Core.

You build things.

"

"I am an Inspector," Gerald said. "I do not build. I verify."

"Then verify it fast!" Kaelen shouted. "Because I am going to hit that wall with my staff, and if the lintel isn't there, I will bring this whole place down!"

Gerald felt the threat. It was crude. It was loud.

But it was accurate.

Kaelen was right about one thing: the structural integrity was failing. If the ceiling collapsed, the library above would fall.

The Ashford Public Library was a heritage site.

Gerald had inspected the foundation ten years ago. He remembered the footings. They were not deep enough for a total collapse.

He had to act. But he could not fix the wall.

He had used his improvisation quota, and he had no repair tools.

He had no mana left for heavy construction.

He had only one tool left. The Citation.

But he needed a target that was physically present. Kaelen was present. But you could not cite an adventurer. Only Cores.

Unless.

Gerald looked at the debris. He looked at the dust. He looked at the mana crystal.

*Infrastructure: Any system designed to support the operation of the dungeon.*

The debris was not infrastructure. It was waste.

But what if the waste was part of the support system?

"Gerald," a new voice said. It was smooth, oily, and came from the shadows near the rogue. "Put down the clipboard, or whatever you use, and let us work."

This was Marn, the rogue. He had been quiet until now.

"Identify yourself," Gerald said.

"I'm Marn. And I'm suggesting you listen to Kaelen before he breaks you."

"You are in a restricted zone," Gerald said. "You have not filed Form 7-C for unauthorized entry into a structural hazard zone."

"Who cares about forms?" Marn laughed. It was a nervous, jagged sound. "We're here to kill you.

Or at least, that's what the guild wants. But if you die, the library dies.

So we're negotiating.

"

Gerald froze.

Negotiating.

The word hung in the air, heavy and dangerous.

"Negotiation implies a dispute," Gerald said. "A dispute implies a legal framework."

"Yes," Kaelen said, lowering his staff slightly. He seemed to realize the trap he had walked into. "We can negotiate."

"On what terms?" Gerald asked.

"Open the dungeon," Kaelen said. "Let us pass. Let us clear the monsters. And we will help you reinforce the ceiling."

"Help me?" Gerald repeated.

"We have earth magic," the fighter grunted. "We can pack the hole. We can stabilize it."

Gerald looked at the fighter. He looked at the staff. He looked at the cracks in the ceiling.

"You are offering labor," Gerald said. "In exchange for access."

"Yes," Kaelen said.

"That is a labor contract," Gerald said. "Do you have a work permit?"

Kaelen's face turned purple.

"This is not a construction site! This is a dungeon!"

"It is a sub-basement beneath a public library," Gerald said. "It is subject to municipal labor laws. If you intend to perform structural reinforcement, you must be registered with the Bureau of Underground Works.

You must wear hard hats.

You must have insurance.

"

"We are adventurers!" the healer shouted. "We have insurance!"

"Show me the policy number," Gerald said.

Silence.

The dust motes danced in the beam of light from the mana crystal. The ceiling groaned again, louder this time. A chunk of plaster fell from the library above, landing with a soft *thud* on the floor.

Kaelen looked at the fighter. The fighter shrugged.

"We don't have a policy number," Kaelen admitted.

"Then you cannot work," Gerald said.

"If you attempt to reinforce the structure without a license, I will cite you for Unlicensed Construction Activity. The penalty is immediate expulsion from the dungeon. You will be teleported to the surface.

Your party will be separated.

"

"You can't do that," Marn said. "We're right next to you!"

"I am the Core," Gerald said.

"I am the system.

I am the walls. I am the air.

And right now, I am the only one who cares if this ceiling holds.

"

Kaelen stepped closer. He was close enough that Gerald could feel the heat of the fire spell on his staff.

"Listen to me, Hutchins," Kaelen said, using his name for the first time. "I know you.

I read your file.

You were fired from the city council. They said you were obsessed. They said you couldn't let anything go.

Look at you.

You're a rock. You're a hole in the ground.

You're alone."

Gerald did not respond. He was analyzing the mana flow. The dungeon was feeding off the fear in the room. Fear was a potent fuel source, but it was unstable.

"I am not a rock," Gerald said. "I am a facility."

"You're a monster," Kaelen spat.

"I am a code," Gerald said. "And you are in violation."

The ceiling cracked again. This time, a beam of sunlight from the library above pierced through the dust, illuminating Kaelen's face. He flinched.

"If you don't let us fix this," Kaelen said, "we will leave. And you will collapse. And you will die."

"I will be fined," Gerald said. "And then I will be audited. And then I will appeal."

"Who will you appeal to?" Marn asked, sneering. "No one cares about you."

"The Inspector General," Gerald said.

The room went silent.

Kaelen's eyes widened.

"You're joking."

"I do not joke," Gerald said. "I am currently under audit. My rating is thirty.

I am one violation away from shutdown. But if I cite you for Unlicensed Construction, and you resist, you trigger a System Dispute.

A Dispute requires a third party adjudicator.

The Inspector General will arrive.

"

Kaelen looked at the ceiling. He looked at Gerald. He looked at his party.

"He won't come," Kaelen said. "He's busy."

"He is always auditing," Gerald said. "He is the System's eye.

He does not sleep. He does not blink. He only measures.

"

Kaelen swallowed. He lowered his staff.

"Fine," he said. "We'll wait. But if the ceiling falls, I'm killing you first."

"You can try," Gerald said. "But you will be trespassing on a protected heritage site. The penalties are severe."

Gerald closed his mental interface. He focused on the vibration of the floor. He needed to buy time. He needed the Inspector General to arrive before the ceiling collapsed.

But how did he call the General?

He couldn't. The General came when audits were due.

Unless.

*Improvisational Maintenance.*

He had used it. He was out of charges.

But what if he didn't repair the wall? What if he repaired the *audit*?

Gerald looked at the mana crystal. It was pulsing in time with the dungeon's heartbeat. The heartbeat was erratic.

He reached out with his mind, not to the structure, but to the compliance log.

He found the entry for his previous citation against the neighboring dungeon, *The Gloomroot*. He had cited it for Poor Ventilation. The citation had been served, but the appeal was pending.

The appeal was still active.

Gerald pulled the appeal file into the present moment. He linked it to the current structural failure.

*Connection established.*

He was not repairing the wall. He was citing the wall.

He was citing the ceiling for failing to support its own weight.

**Citation Generated.**

**Target: Internal Infrastructure (Unrecognized).**

**Violation: Structural Inadequacy.**

**Penalty: Immediate Structural Reinforcement by External Party.**

The dungeon shook violently.

Kaelen stumbled.

"What did you do?"

"I have filed a complaint," Gerald said.

A new window opened in the System interface. It was red. It pulsed with a blinding light.

**System Alert: Dispute Detected.**

**Adjudicator En Route.**

**ETA: 10 Seconds.**

Kaelen looked up, his face pale.

"No," he whispered. "No, he's not coming here. He doesn't care about small dungeons."

"He cares about code," Gerald said.

The air in the room changed. The dust stopped dancing. The light from the mana crystal turned a cold, sterile white. The temperature dropped ten degrees.

A voice boomed, not from the walls, but from the air itself. It was vast, impersonal, and terrifying.

**"Gerald Hutchins. You have requested a hearing."**

Kaelen fell to his knees. The rogue and the fighter backed away, hands over their ears.

"I did not request a hearing," Gerald said. "I issued a citation."

**"Citations against infrastructure are invalid,"** the voice said. **"Infrastructure is not a sentient entity. It cannot be cited."**

Gerald felt a surge of triumph, followed immediately by dread. He had made a mistake. He had cited the ceiling, but the System did not recognize ceilings as legal entities.

**"Your citation is dismissed,"** the voice said. **"Your compliance rating drops by five."**

**Current Rating: 25.**

**Shutdown Imminent.**

The ceiling groaned. A massive crack split the stone above them.

Kaelen looked up.

"You idiot! You just killed us!"

Gerald's consciousness flickered. He was fading. The darkness was creeping in from the edges of his perception. He had seconds left.

He needed to change the target, and he needed to cite something that *was* sentient.

Something that was present.

Something that had the authority to fix the ceiling.

Gerald looked at Kaelen.

But Kaelen was not a Core, and he looked at the mana crystal.

The crystal was part of the dungeon.

He looked at the cracks in the library above.

And then he saw it.

A shadow. Moving in the upper library. Through the hole he had just made.

Someone was up there. Someone who owned the building.

Gerald pushed his awareness upward. Through the rubble. Through the floorboards.

He saw feet. Heavy, leather boots. Walking toward the hole.

And he saw a badge.

A City Inspector badge.

Gerald's heart stopped, and he had not called the Inspector General.

He had called the City Council.

The ceiling collapsed.

Not the whole thing. Just a section. A shower of plaster and lath rained down on Kaelen, who threw his arms up to protect his face.

Through the hole in the floor of the library above, a face appeared. It was a young woman with glasses and a clipboard. She looked down into the sub-basement, her eyes wide with shock.

"Who's down there?" she called out.

Gerald realized, with a cold clarity, that he had just been inspected by a human inspector.

And she was holding a pen.
