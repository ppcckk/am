Chapter 20: Identity Theft Audit

# Chapter 20

The mold spores were dying.

Gerald watched the first one collapse in on itself, its cellular structure unraveling into grey powder that settled on the dungeon floor like ash. The second followed three seconds later. The third was still holding, barely, its membrane translucent and stretched thin over a void that hadn't been there an hour ago.

His Compliance Rating had dropped to 9, and the System was auditing him against himself. Every deficiency the Inspector General catalogued was a deficiency Gerald had already documented in his own inspection logs.

The feedback loop was compounding.

The IG flagged the water leak as a Category 4 moisture intrusion, and Gerald's own log from Chapter 6 had flagged it as a Category 4 moisture intrusion, and the System treated the overlap as a finding of willful non-compliance than a simple finding. Willful non-compliance carried triple the penalty.

His dungeon was being fined to death by his own paperwork.

The water leak pulsed in the east corridor, which was the only spawn that hadn't degraded, mostly because it wasn't alive. It was a leak.

It didn't have a health bar.

It had a flow rate, and that flow rate was currently three gallons per minute, which Gerald knew because he'd measured it with a graduated cylinder and a stopwatch during his third week as a Core.

The rat was gone. It had left the dungeon two chapters ago, slipping through a gap in the foundation wall that Gerald had flagged as a structural deficiency but never repaired. He'd been meaning to. He'd been meaning to do a lot of things.

He sat on the floor of his central chamber, which was technically a corridor because he hadn't designated it as a room, and stared at the System interface floating above his left hand. The audit notice was still active. The Inspector General's findings scrolled in real time, each one a line item from Gerald's own inspection reports, reformatted into penalty language.

Category 4 moisture intrusion. Willful. Penalty: 3 points.

Missing egress signage at junction 7. Willful. Penalty: 2 points.

Inadequate ventilation in corridor 3. Willful. Penalty: 4 points.

He'd written all of these. He'd written them himself, in his own handwriting, on his own forms, and filed them in his own dungeon.

The System had found them, and the System had cross-referenced them.

The System had concluded that Gerald knew about every violation and had chosen not to fix them, which was, technically, true.

He'd chosen not to fix them because fixing them required mana he didn't have and materials he couldn't spawn. He'd chosen not to fix them because he'd been too busy fighting for survival against three dungeon cores who wanted his territory. He'd chosen not to fix them because he was fifty-four years old and had never once been asked to fight a dragon.

The System did not care about his reasons.

The audit notice updated.

Category 1 structural deficiency, foundation gap at grid reference 14-C. Willful. Penalty: 6 points.

That was the gap the rat had escaped through. Gerald had documented it on day two. He'd written the deficiency, noted the severity, and moved on.

He'd never filled the gap. He'd never filled it because filling it would have required him to allocate mana to structural repair, and he'd needed that mana for the handrails.

The handrails were up to code. The foundation was not.

His Compliance Rating ticked down to 8.

The mold spore's membrane ruptured. It dissolved. Gerald had no biological spawns left.

His dungeon was now a water leak and a set of handrails.

He pulled up the audit timeline. The Inspector General had filed the self-audit fourteen minutes after the Hearing concluded.

The penalties were applied retroactively to the moment of registration.

Every violation Gerald had ever documented was now a willful violation. Every finding was a confession.

He could not appeal. An appeal would require arguing that he hadn't documented the violations, which was false.

He'd documented them, and the System had his forms.

The System had his handwriting. The System had his registration number, which was the Inspector General's registration number, which was the same number, which was the problem.

Gerald closed the audit notice. He opened his inspection log. He scrolled through forty-seven entries. Every one of them was now a weapon pointed at his own dungeon.

He needed to recuse himself.

The thought arrived not as inspiration but as procedure. In thirty-one years of municipal inspection, Gerald had never once audited a building he'd inspected, and it was a conflict of interest.

It was grounds for recusal. The municipal code addressed it in Section 14.7: no inspector may conduct a compliance review of a structure for which they have filed a prior inspection report.

The System had a municipal code, and the System had sections. The System had conflict of interest provisions. Gerald had read them.

He'd read them during his first week, when he'd been trying to understand what the hell was happening to his body.

He read them cover to cover, in order, as he did with all codes.

He searched the System interface for the term "recusal."

The result appeared in a format he'd never seen.

Not a notice. Not a citation. A form.

Form 14.7-A: Motion for Conflict of Interest Recusal. It required three fields.

Field one: Registration number of the auditor.

Field two: Registration number of the audited party.

Field three: Third-party attestation.

Gerald filled in field one.

His registration number, and he filled in field two. His registration number.

The form accepted both. A new line appeared.

Third-party attestation requires a Core of registered standing who is not party to the audit. Attestor must physically serve co-signature within the audited party's dungeon footprint.

Gerald read it twice. Then he read it a third time, because the third time he noticed the second sentence.

The attesting Core had to be physically present in Gerald's dungeon.

He had no allies. Every Core within range had been either Cited by him or had sent spawns to kill him.

The nearest neutral Core was a mid-tier fire dungeon three blocks east, run by something called the Cinderfold.

Gerald had never interacted with the Cinderfold. The Cinderfold had never interacted with him. But the Cinderfold was a registered Core, and Gerald needed a registered Core, and the Cinderfold was three blocks east.

He could send a summons.

A dungeon core could issue a formal summons to another Core within range, compelling a physical presence. It was the equivalent of a subpoena.

The System enforced it.

A Core that refused a summons forfeited its right to appeal future Citations.

Gerald had never issued a summons. He'd issued Citations. He'd issued a Hearing notice. He'd never summoned anyone to his dungeon, because summoning someone to your dungeon was an act of trust, and Gerald did not trust the System, and he did not trust the other Cores, and he did not trust himself.

But the form was in front of him. The audit was still running. His Compliance Rating was 8 and falling. The water leak was his only spawn and it was not a spawn, it was a leak, and the System might classify it as a Category 3 nuisance next, and then he'd have nothing.

He opened the summons form.

The Cinderfold's registration number was public. All Cores could see each other's registration numbers.

Gerald had looked it up once, early on, out of professional curiosity, and the Cinderfold was registered as a Class 3 dungeon with a Compliance Rating of 61.

Gerald had noted, in his own log, that the Cinderfold's fire suppression system appeared to be decorative than functional. He logged it as a finding, without acting, as he did with everything.

He filled in the summons. He served it.

The response came in eleven minutes.

*Attestor accepted. Physical presence within four minutes.*

Gerald did not know what to expect. He prepared the dungeon. He checked the handrails.

He checked the egress signage he'd installed in week three. He checked the water leak, which was still dripping at a steady three gallons per minute.

He checked the spot where the mold spores had been and found grey powder, which he swept into a corner because it was not a spawn and therefore not his responsibility.

The Cinderfold arrived as a column of heat. It did not have a body. It had a presence, a thermal signature that occupied the central chamber and raised the ambient temperature by eleven degrees.

Gerald felt his mana drop another point.

The water leak evaporated slightly.

"Inspector." The Cinderfold's voice was not a voice. It was a vibration in the air, shaped into words by pressure differentials. "You cited my fire suppression array in Chapter 12."

"I did."

"Two of my elite spawns died."

"I'm aware."

"I want to know why you're summoning me."

Gerald pulled up Form 14.7-A. He displayed it in the interface space between them. The Cinderfold's thermal signature shifted, which Gerald interpreted as reading.

"You want me to attest that you're auditing yourself," the Cinderfold said.

"Yes."

"That's true."

"It is."

"Why would I help you?"

Gerald considered this. The honest answer was that he didn't know. The procedural answer was better.

"If you refuse, you forfeit your right to appeal my Chapter 12 Citation. If you accept, you gain immunity from the Inspector General's authority for the duration of the audit cycle. Forty-two days."

The Cinderfold's temperature dropped. Gerald understood the math. The Cinderfold had been operating under the assumption that the Inspector General's audit was a future threat, not a current one. If Gerald was the Inspector General, then the Cinderfold was also subject to Gerald's authority, and Gerald could cite the Cinderfold again, and the Cinderfold had no defense against a self-auditing IG.

"Forty-two days of immunity," the Cinderfold said.

"According to the form."

"And if I co-sign, you'll be able to keep issuing Citations?"

"I'll be able to keep existing."

The Cinderfold was quiet for a long time. The water leak dripped. Gerald counted the drops. Forty-three.

"There's a second condition," the Cinderfold said.

Gerald's stomach tightened. "State it."

"You co-sign my appeal against your Chapter 12 Citation. You reverse the debuff on my fire suppression array. You restore my spawns."

Gerald looked at the form. He looked at the Cinderfold. He looked at his Compliance Rating, which was now 41.

"If I reverse the Citation, I lose the structural evidence that justified it. I lose the Hearing win. The System treats reversed Citations as withdrawn findings. I can't re-file the same violation."

"That's correct."

"And my Compliance Rating continues to drop."

"That's also correct."

Gerald sat down, and the floor was cold. His hands were steady, which surprised him. He'd expected them to shake.

They didn't shake. They were the hands of a man who had filled out Form 14.7-A and was now deciding whether to co-sign a reversal that would cost him the only defensive position he had.

The System interface pulsed. A new notification.

*Conflict of Interest Recusal pending. Attestor present. Awaiting counter-signature. Time remaining: 2:14.*

The Cinderfold waited. The water leak waited. Gerald's dungeon waited, and his Compliance Rating ticked down to 40, and the System's audit continued its slow, methodical work of killing him with his own paperwork.

He thought about the municipal code. Section 14.7, subsection C: a recused inspector may be reappointed to a case if the recusal is found to be procedurally defective. Gerald had written that subsection. He'd written it because he'd been recused from a case once, in 1997, a residential renovation on Elm Street, and he'd been furious, and he'd looked for the loophole, and he'd found it, and he'd used it.

The loophole was that a recusal could be challenged if the conflict was "apparent rather than actual." Gerald and the Inspector General shared a registration number.

That was apparent, which was not actual. They were not the same entity.

They were the same number. The System had conflated them. The conflation was the error.

If Gerald could prove the conflation was procedural than substantive, the recusal would be defective, and the Inspector General's authority over him would be restored, and the audit would resume under normal parameters than willful-violation parameters.

But proving the conflation was procedural required the Inspector General to acknowledge the distinction. The Inspector General was Gerald. Gerald could not acknowledge the distinction to himself, because Gerald was the Inspector General, and the Inspector General was Gerald, and the System treated them as one entity.

Unless the Inspector General was not Gerald.

Unless the shared registration number was a coincidence.

Unless Gerald had made an error, and he looked at the number he'd seen when the plane of light collapsed. He looked at his own registration number, which was displayed in the upper-left corner of his interface, as it always had been.

They matched.

They matched exactly. Every digit.

But Gerald had made an error before.

In 2003, he'd misread a load-bearing designation and cited a wall as non-structural when it was, in fact, load-bearing. The error had been small.

A digit.

A 7 that should have been a 1, and he compared the numbers again. Character by character.

They matched.

They matched exactly.

The timer hit 0:47.

The Cinderfold's thermal signature pulsed. "Decision."

Gerald looked at the form. He looked at his Compliance Rating. He watched the leak drip, indifferent and persistent, as leaks are.

He thought about the Inspector General. He thought about the number. He thought about the possibility that he was wrong, that he'd misread the number in the moment of collapse, that the digits had blurred in the light.

He thought about the fact that if he was wrong, the audit was legitimate, and his dungeon would die within the week.

He thought about the fact that if he was right, and the Inspector General was not him, then the Inspector General was someone else with his registration number, and that person had been auditing him for twenty chapters, and that person was still out there, and that person had never been cited.

Gerald signed the co-signature field. He signed the reversal field. He signed both.

The Cinderfold's fire suppression array restored. Two elite spawns reformed from ambient heat. The Cinderfold's temperature spiked, then stabilized.

Gerald's Compliance Rating dropped to 38, and the recusal form went through. The audit paused. The Inspector General's authority over Gerald's dungeon was suspended for forty-two days.

The Cinderfold withdrew. The temperature normalized. The water leak resumed its steady three gallons per minute.

Gerald sat alone in his dungeon. His mana was at 0.4 percent.

His Compliance Rating was 38.

His spawn list was empty.

His dungeon was four thousand square feet of handrails and a leak.

He opened the inspection log. He scrolled to the entry where he'd recorded the Inspector General's registration number. He stared at it. He compared it to his own.

They matched.

But now he noticed something he hadn't noticed before. The Inspector General's number had a suffix. A character at the end, separated by a hyphen. Gerald's number had no suffix.

Gerald's number: 00417-2019.

The Inspector General's number: 00417-2019-A, and the suffix was not a coincidence. The suffix was a designation. Gerald had seen designations like this in the municipal code. It stood for "Acting."

The Inspector General was not Gerald. The Inspector General was an acting Inspector General. Acting in Gerald's name.

Acting under Gerald's registration number. Acting on Gerald's behalf.

Which meant someone was pretending to be Gerald Hutchins.

Which meant Gerald Hutchins had been auditing himself for twenty chapters, and the audit had been real, and the findings had been real, and the willful non-compliance was real, because Gerald had filed those reports, and someone else had used them.

Someone had Gerald's registration number. Someone had Gerald's authority. Someone had been citing Gerald's dungeon using Gerald's own findings.

The notification appeared.

*Unauthorized registration use detected.

Filing Form 9.3: Identity Fraud. Investigation opened. Suspect: unknown.

Range: System-wide.*

Gerald stared at the notification. He had never filed a fraud complaint. He had never filed anything except deficiency reports and Citations, and had never once suspected that someone was using his credentials.

He had been a building inspector for thirty-one years. He had never once been asked to fight a dragon. He had never once been asked to investigate his own identity theft.

He opened Form 9.3. He filled it out.
