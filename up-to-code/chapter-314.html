Chapter 314: Signed Death Warrant

# Chapter 314

The darkness did not have a floor.

Gerald Hutchins fell through it, the air rushing past his ears with a sound like tearing parchment. His stomach lurched, a familiar sensation he associated with the drop in pressure before a storm, but here the pressure was magical and wrong. He twisted his body, instinctively reaching for a handhold that did not exist. His fingers scrabbled against nothing but cold, empty void.

The fall lasted an eternity. Or perhaps three seconds. Time behaved poorly in the absence of light.

Then, a thud.

Not a soft landing. A hard, marble-slapping impact that knocked the wind out of him. Gerald hit the ground face-first, his glasses skittering away into the gloom. He lay there for a moment, listening to the ringing in his ears and the distant, wet drip of water somewhere above.

He pushed himself up on trembling arms. The cardigan was ruined. The knees of his slacks were damp. He blinked, clearing the dust from his eyes, and looked around.

He was not in a dungeon corridor. He was in a vast, circular chamber, the walls lined with shelves that stretched upward into infinite shadow. The floor was polished obsidian, reflecting the faint, bioluminescent glow of the moss that clung to the ceiling. It smelled of old paper and ozone.

A notice appeared in his peripheral vision, crisp and white against the dark.

**[NEW LOCATION DETECTED: THE ARCHIVE OF VOIDS.]** **[STATUS: UNLISTED.]** **[NOTE: THIS LOCATION IS NOT SUBJECT TO THE STANDARD DUNGEON CODE. IT EXISTS OUTSIDE THE GRID.]**

Gerald reached into his pocket. The receipt was gone.

The sandpaper was gone.

But his clipboard remained, tethered to his wrist by a System-imposed strap he hadn't noticed before. He unclipped it, the paper snapping taut.

He pulled a pen from his breast pocket. It was a standard-issue municipal ballpoint, blue ink, chewed at the cap. He clicked it. It worked.

In a place without code, he would write one.

A figure emerged from the shadows. It was tall, draped in robes that shifted color like oil on water. Its face was a mask of porcelain, featureless except for two dark, hollow eyes. It held a staff topped with a crystal that pulsed with a sickly green rhythm.

"You are not registered," the figure said. Its voice sounded like gravel grinding in a mixer.

Gerald stood up, brushing the obsidian dust from his knees. He adjusted his glasses. They were cracked on one side.

"I am a Core," Gerald said. He pointed to the air where his status window usually hovered. "And you are obstructing a public thoroughfare. I didn't see a permit for this room."

The figure tilted its head. The crystal on its staff flared brighter.

"There are no permits in the Archive. There are only rules of containment."

"Code is code," Gerald said flatly. "If there is no permit, there is no occupancy. I am citing this structure for unauthorized use of unregistered dimensional space."

The figure laughed, which was a dry, rasping sound.

"You speak of bureaucracy.

I speak of hunger.

The Inspector General woke because someone broke the seal on the Primordial Vault. You are here because you are collateral damage.

"

Gerald ignored the insult. He looked at the walls.

The shelves were not holding books, and they held jars.

Thousands of them. Inside each jar was a swirling, chaotic mist, a different color for each.

Some were red. Some were blue. Some were a sickly yellow.

He walked toward the nearest shelf. The figure did not stop him. It seemed amused.

Gerald picked up a jar. The label was handwritten in a script that hurt to look at.

He squinted, and he couldn't read it, but he could see the seal. A wax stamp, cracked down the middle.

"Improper storage," Gerald muttered.

He set the jar back down carefully.

"Contagion risk.

Cross-contamination between elemental types.

This violates Section 14 of the Municipal Hazardous Materials Ordinance, which I am assuming the System has adopted as a baseline standard, given the lack of any other governing body here.

"

The figure stepped closer.

"You are trying to apply human laws to cosmic horrors."

"I am trying to keep the room clean," Gerald corrected. He pulled out his pen. On the first page of his clipboard, he had already written the header: *Violation Notice 314-A.*

He looked at the figure.

"Who are you?"

"I am the Curator," the figure said. "And you are wasting my time. The Inspector General is coming. He will unmake this place, and you with it, unless you prove you have a purpose here."

Gerald paused. He tapped his pen against the clipboard.

"A purpose," Gerald repeated. "I am an inspector.

I find violations.

I fix them.

That is my purpose.

"

The Curator's porcelain face remained blank, but the crystal on the staff dimmed.

"The Inspector General does not care for inspectors. He cares for order.

Pure order. And you are a mess, Gerald Hutchins.

Your dungeon is a chaotic amalgamation of half-finished projects and half-baked rules.

"

"My dungeon is up to code," Gerald said. "Compliance Rating 47. It's low, but it's accurate."

"It is a sham," the Curator said. "You cite others for fire safety while your own egress routes are blocked by your own indecision. You cite others for electrical faults while your mana lines are frayed and exposed.

You are a hypocrite, Gerald.

And the System knows it.

"

The accusation hung in the air. Gerald felt a cold sweat prickle at the back of his neck. He hated being called a hypocrite. It implied a lack of integrity, and integrity was the only thing he had left.

He looked at the jars again. The crack in the seal on the one he had touched was widening. A wisp of red mist curled out, hissing like a snake.

Gerald stepped forward. He reached into his other pocket and pulled out a roll of silver duct tape. It was a staple of municipal repair kits. He peeled off a strip.

"Stop," the Curator said. "That material is not magical. It will not hold."

"It holds because it is applied with intent," Gerald said. He wrapped the tape around the crack in the wax seal. He pressed it down firmly, smoothing out any bubbles.

The hissing stopped. The red mist retreated.

The Curator stared at the jar. Then at Gerald.

"You sealed it," the Curator said. "With mundane adhesive."

"The code doesn't specify the material," Gerald said. "It specifies the seal. A seal is a seal."

A new message flashed in Gerald's vision, purple and jagged.

**[NEW MECHANIC UNLOCKED: MUNDANE REMEDIATION.]** **[DESCRIPTION: By applying non-magical solutions to magical problems, the Core demonstrates a level of practicality that bypasses certain System protections. Violations fixed via mundane means are treated as 'Permanent Corrections' than 'Temporary Repairs.']**

Gerald smiled. It was a thin, tight smile. He turned to the Curator.

"I am going to cite this entire room," Gerald said.

The Curator recoiled.

"You cannot. This is a sacred space."

"Sacred spaces have fire exits," Gerald said.

"I don't see any. I don't see any sprinkler systems. I don't see any signage indicating the presence of hazardous materials.

This is a death trap.

"

He wrote. The pen scratched against the paper.

*Violation 1: Lack of emergency egress.* *Violation 2: Improper storage of volatile magical compounds.* *Violation 3: Failure to display required hazard warning placards.*

The Curator raised its staff. Green lightning crackled around its hands.

"I will incinerate you."

"If you incinerate me, you destroy the evidence," Gerald said without looking up.

"The Inspector General is coming.

He will ask for a report. If you kill me, he will find no report.

He will assume you are hiding something. He will audit you.

"

The lightning died. The Curator lowered its staff slowly.

"You are threatening me with a bureaucracy I cannot understand," the Curator said.

"I am offering you a choice," Gerald said. "You can let me fix the problems.

I will make this room compliant.

I will install the signage. I will create the egress path. And when the Inspector General arrives, he will see a well-ordered archive, not a den of chaos.

He will pass you.

"

"And if I refuse?"

"Then I cite you. And I have the sandpaper.

I have the receipt.

I have the precedent. I will appeal your refusal to the Inspector General directly. And I will win.

"

The Curator looked at the jars. It looked at the cracked seals. It looked at the lack of handrails on the upper shelves.

For the first time, the Curator looked uncertain.

"What do you require?" the Curator asked.

Gerald ripped a page off his clipboard. He handed it to the Curator.

"Permit application for minor structural alterations," Gerald said. "Sign here.

And here.

Initial here.

Do not use red ink. The System rejects red ink for administrative documents.

"

The Curator took the paper. Its long, spindly fingers trembled as it held the pen Gerald had provided.

"You are insane," the Curator whispered.

"I am thorough," Gerald corrected.

The Curator signed.

As soon as the ink dried, the air in the room changed. The oppressive, heavy feeling of the Archive lifted slightly. A faint, golden light emanated from the walls, forming the outline of a door that hadn't been there before.

**[PERMIT APPROVED.]** **[WORK COMMENCING.]**

Gerald walked to the new doorway. He inspected it. He checked the swing radius. He checked the latch.

"Acceptable," he said.

He turned back to the Curator.

"Now," Gerald said. "Show me the other rooms. There are more jars. And I suspect the ventilation in the lower levels is nonexistent."

The Curator looked at the new door. It looked at Gerald. It looked at the clipboard.

It sighed, a sound like wind through a hollow tree.

"Follow me," the Curator said.

Gerald followed. But as they walked deeper into the Archive, he noticed something. The shelves were not just holding jars.

They were holding memories. And as he passed them, he saw flashes of his own life.

The day he was born.

The day he got his first job. The day he found the anomaly in the library.

But there was one memory that wasn't his, which was a young man, standing in front of a burning building. A man with Gerald's face, but younger. And in his hand, he held not a clipboard, but a sword.

Gerald stopped.

"Curator," he said. "What is that?"

The Curator did not answer. It just kept walking.

Gerald stared at the memory. The young man looked at Gerald with eyes full of rage and purpose.

And then, a message flashed in Gerald's vision, not purple or red, but black.

**[RECORD FOUND: PREDECESSOR CORE -

"THE INSPECTOR"]** **[STATUS: TERMINATED.]** **[CAUSE OF DEATH: CITATION ISSUED BY SYSTEM.]** **[NOTE: THE INSPECTOR GENERAL WAS ALSO THE PROSECUTOR.]**

Gerald's blood ran cold, and he had thought he was fighting other dungeons. He had thought the System was a neutral arbiter.

But the System had killed his predecessor.

And now, the Inspector General was awake.

Gerald looked at the clipboard. The permit for the minor structural alterations was still in the Curator's hand.

He realized, with a sinking horror, that the permit was not for the room.

The signature line was not for the Curator, which was for him.

And below it, in small, fine print he had not read, were the words:

*Applicant acknowledges that by accepting this permit, they agree to assume liability for all previous violations in this sector, retroactive to the founding of the Archive.*

Gerald had just signed his own death warrant. Or worse, he had signed the Curator's.

The Curator looked at him. And for the first time, the porcelain mask cracked.

"Welcome to the job," the Curator said.

The floor beneath them opened up.
