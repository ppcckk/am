Chapter 91: Audit of the Void

# Chapter 91

Gerald read the audit notice for the fourth time.

He had been reading it for two hours. The text was short. The text was also wrong.

AUDIT NOTICE: INSPECTOR GENERAL. SUBJECT: ACTING INSPECTOR, DISTRICT 7.

SCOPE: COMPLIANCE ENFORCEMENT ACTIONS, PRECEDING 90 SYSTEM DAYS.

NOTIFICATION PERIOD: 6 HOURS. PARTY STATUS: SINGLE SUBJECT.

Gerald set the notice down on his inspection desk. He picked it up again. He read the scope line. He read it a third time.

Compliance enforcement actions, and the Acting Inspector had not issued Gerald a compliance enforcement action.

The Acting Inspector had issued a demolition order. Gerald checked his own records.

Form 22-D.

Administrative Demolition Authorization. Filed by the Acting Inspector. Served on Gerald's dungeon.

Paused by the pending citation, and a demolition order was not a compliance enforcement action.

It was an administrative termination.

Different category. Different procedural pathway.

Different form number.

Gerald knew this because he had spent thirty-one years knowing this. In municipal government, the distinction between an enforcement action and an administrative termination was the difference between a fine and a wrecking ball.

The paperwork was different, and the appeal rights were different. The scope of review was different.

The audit would review the Acting Inspector's compliance enforcement actions.

It would not review the demolition order. The Acting Inspector would pass the audit clean.

The citation pause would expire.

The demolition would resume.

Gerald looked at his clock. Three hours and fourteen minutes remaining.

He pulled up the System's procedural index. He had never needed it before.

He had never needed to challenge an audit, and he had never even thought about challenging an audit.

Audits were things that happened to other people. Audits were things that happened to Cores who had been active long enough to attract attention.

Gerald had been active for sixty-nine chapters. The Inspector General had never once looked at him.

Now the Inspector General was looking at someone else, and that someone else was the one entity that could destroy Gerald's dungeon.

Gerald opened a new form. He hesitated.

His hands were steady.

His stomach was not. He had never filed a complaint against a regulatory authority, and had spent his entire career on the other side of that transaction.

He was the one who found violations, and he was not the one who reported them.

He started typing anyway.

The form was called SUPPLEMENTAL COMPLAINT.

It required three things: the subject of the complaint, the nature of the complaint, and the requested remedy. Gerald typed the subject. ACTING INSPECTOR, DISTRICT 7.

He typed the nature. The Acting Inspector's demolition order constituted a compliance enforcement action conducted without proper procedural notice, without a prior citation, and without a documented finding of imminent hazard.

He typed the requested remedy.

Expand audit scope to include all compliance enforcement actions, including improperly categorized administrative terminations.

He read it back. It was accurate. It was also, he realized, a confession.

By reclassifying the demolition order as a compliance enforcement action, Gerald was confirming that the Acting Inspector had jurisdiction over his dungeon. He was confirming that the Acting Inspector had authority to act against him. He was confirming that the demolition order had been issued under the same authority that the Inspector General was now auditing.

If the audit found the Acting Inspector guilty of improper enforcement, the System would void the enforcement action. The demolition order would be voided permanently. Gerald's dungeon would be safe.

If the audit found the Acting Inspector innocent, the demolition order would stand. The pause would expire. Gerald would be demolished.

A third possibility occurred to Gerald when he was three-quarters of the way through the form. The audit's party status line said SINGLE SUBJECT.

If Gerald filed a supplemental complaint, he became a party to the audit. He would be listed. He would be present.

The Inspector General would see him.

Gerald stopped typing, and he looked at the half-finished form. He looked at his Compliance Rating. 86. He looked at his violation history.

He had been cited for ventilation, for egress, for electrical, for structural, for biological contamination.

He had cured every one of them. But the history was there.

The Inspector General would see it.

A Core with 86 and a clean current record was not a problem. A Core with 86 and a history of violations drawing attention from an Acting Inspector, a rival Core, and the Inspector General himself was different. It was a pattern. Patterns attracted scrutiny.

Gerald deleted the form. He started over.

This time he did not request a remedy. He requested a scope clarification. He wrote: the audit notice defines scope as "compliance enforcement actions" but does not define the term.

Gerald requested that the Inspector General issue a definition prior to the audit proceeding. This was a procedural challenge.

It did not make Gerald a party, which did not confirm jurisdiction.

It simply asked the Inspector General to clarify its own language.

He checked the procedural rules. Scope clarifications could be requested by any Core within the audit's jurisdictional range. Gerald was within range.

The request was valid. The Inspector General would have to respond before the audit could proceed.

It would delay the audit.

It would not stop it.

Gerald submitted the request.

The System accepted it.

Gerald exhaled, and he checked the clock. Two hours and fifty-one minutes remaining.

The audit would be delayed.

The Acting Inspector would have more time. Gerald would have more time.

Then a notification appeared.

SCOPE CLARIFICATION REQUEST: DENIED. DEFINITION OF "COMPLIANCE ENFORCEMENT ACTIONS" IS PROVIDED IN SYSTEM PROCEDURAL MANUAL, SECTION 14.3.1. SUBJECT MAY CONSULT MANUAL. AUDIT PROCEEDS AS SCHEDULED.

Gerald stared at the denial. He had not read Section 14.3.1.

He had not read the procedural manual, and he had assumed the term was ambiguous.

It was not ambiguous. It was defined.

He had not checked because he had assumed that a term in an audit notice would be self-explanatory. It was not. It was defined elsewhere.

He opened the procedural manual. Section 14.3.1.

"Compliance enforcement actions include: citations, debuffs, forced corrections, and administrative terminations issued pursuant to a documented finding of non-compliance."

Administrative terminations. The demolition order was an administrative termination.

It was issued pursuant to a documented finding of non-compliance, and the Acting Inspector had documented Gerald's violations. The finding existed. The termination was within scope.

The audit would cover the demolition order.

The Acting Inspector would be audited on it. Gerald had been wrong.

His first instinct had been right.

The audit would help him. He had second-guessed himself and wasted forty minutes on a scope clarification that was denied because the scope was already correct.

Gerald felt something hot move through his chest. It was not anger.

It was embarrassment, and he had made an error.

A procedural error. The kind of error he had never made in thirty-one years.

He had assumed a term was ambiguous when it was not; had failed to check the definition before challenging the scope.

He checked the clock. Two hours and forty-three minutes.

The audit was proceeding on schedule.

The Acting Inspector would be audited on the demolition order. If the audit found the Acting Inspector's enforcement improper, the order would be voided. If it found the enforcement proper, the order would stand.

Gerald needed to know what the audit would find, and he needed to know whether the Acting Inspector's demolition order had been properly conducted. He had the records.

He had the form.

He had the timeline. He had served his citation before the order was issued. The citation pause should have blocked the order.

The Acting Inspector had issued the order despite the pause, which was a violation.

The Acting Inspector had acted during a citation pause.

The audit would find that. The Acting Inspector would fail.

The order would be voided.

Gerald sat back. He allowed himself one breath. Then he opened the procedural manual again. He searched for what happened when an Acting Inspector failed an audit.

The answer was not what he expected.

When an Acting Inspector failed an audit, the System did not remove the Acting Inspector, and it did not appoint a replacement.

It did not void the pending enforcement actions.

It applied a debuff. The Acting Inspector's enforcement authority was reduced by fifty percent for a period of thirty days. Existing enforcement actions remained in effect.

The Acting Inspector simply lost the ability to issue new ones.

Gerald read this three times.

The audit would not save him. The audit would not void the demolition order.

It would not even pause it further.

It would debuff the Acting Inspector's authority, but the existing order would stand, and the pause from Gerald's citation would expire in forty-seven hours. The order would execute.

Gerald needed a different solution. He needed to void the demolition order itself. Not through the audit. Through a direct challenge.

He searched for appeal procedures. Administrative terminations could be appealed.

The appeal window was seven days from issuance.

Gerald checked the issuance date, and the demolition order had been issued eleven days ago; the appeal window was closed.

He searched for other options. Reconsideration requests. He filed one.

The System required a basis. Gerald typed: the order was issued during an active citation pause.

The order was procedurally invalid at the moment of issuance.

The System accepted the reconsideration request and set a hearing in four hours.

Gerald checked the clock.

Four hours. He had the records.

He had the timeline, and he had the citation and the order and the pause; had everything he needed to prove the order was issued during a pause.

He started preparing his case file. He pulled the citation.

He pulled the order.

He pulled the pause notification, and he arranged them in chronological order. He wrote a one-page summary.

He checked it for errors. He checked it again.

Then he noticed something. The reconsideration hearing was scheduled for four hours from now.

The audit was scheduled for two hours and forty-three minutes from now.

The audit would happen first. The audit would debuff the Acting Inspector. The Acting Inspector would be debuffed when the reconsideration hearing occurred.

Gerald pulled up the hearing rules.

A debuffed party could not represent itself. A debuffed party required a proxy.

The Acting Inspector, debuffed by the audit, could not argue its own case at the reconsideration hearing.

It would need to appoint a proxy.

Gerald checked the proxy rules. A proxy had to be another Core with enforcement authority.

The Acting Inspector's district had no other Cores with enforcement authority.

The Acting Inspector would have no proxy.

The hearing would proceed with no representation for the Acting Inspector.

Gerald sat still.

The audit would debuff the Acting Inspector. The debuff would strip the Acting Inspector's right to self-representation. The reconsideration hearing would occur with no opposition.

Gerald would argue alone. The System would hear only his side.

He had two hours and forty-three minutes until the audit.

He had four hours until the hearing. He had a case file. He had records.

He had a timeline.

Gerald stood up. He walked to his inspection desk. He opened a new form. He filled it out.

NOTICE OF INTENT TO ARGUE: I, Gerald Hutchins, Core Registration Number 0047-ASH, intend to argue in full at the reconsideration hearing scheduled for [timestamp]. I will present the following evidence: Citation Form 19-C, served [timestamp].

Administrative Demolition Authorization Form 22-D, issued [timestamp].

Citation Pause Notification, effective [timestamp]. The order was issued during an active pause. The order is procedurally invalid.

He submitted it.

The System accepted.

Gerald sat back down. He looked at his hands. They were steady. His stomach was not.

Then a new notification appeared.

AUDIT PROCEEDING: INSPECTOR GENERAL. SUBJECT: ACTING INSPECTOR, DISTRICT 7.

The audit had started early.

Gerald's clock said two hours and forty-three minutes remaining. The audit notice said it was scheduled for that time.

But the audit was starting now.

The System's interface pulsed.

A new window opened.

AUDIT OPENING STATEMENT: INSPECTOR GENERAL.

Gerald watched the text appear. It was not addressed to him. It was addressed to the Acting Inspector.

But Gerald could see it. He was within the audit's jurisdictional range.

The audit was public.

INSPECTOR GENERAL: The subject has been served notice of this audit. The subject has been given a notification period. The subject has not requested an extension.

The subject has not appointed counsel.

The audit will proceed.

Gerald read this. The Acting Inspector had not requested an extension. The Acting Inspector had not appointed counsel. The Acting Inspector was going into the audit alone.

Then a second line appeared.

INSPECTOR GENERAL: The subject has submitted a motion to consolidate. The subject requests that this audit be consolidated with a pending reconsideration hearing involving the same subject and the same enforcement action. The Inspector General has granted the motion.

Gerald's hands went cold, and the Acting Inspector had filed a consolidation motion.

The audit and the reconsideration hearing would be merged. The Acting Inspector would not face the audit alone, and it would face the audit and the hearing at the same time.

It would be debuffed by the audit findings and then immediately required to argue against Gerald in the same proceeding.

No, which was not what consolidation meant.

Consolidation meant they would be heard together. The Acting Inspector would argue both cases simultaneously.

It would use the audit as a defense against the reconsideration. It would argue that its enforcement was proper, that the audit would confirm this, and that the reconsideration should be denied.

Gerald had four hours until the merged proceeding. The Acting Inspector had four hours to prepare a combined defense. Gerald had a one-page summary. The Acting Inspector had thirty-one years of enforcement experience.

Gerald opened his case file. He looked at his one-page summary. He looked at the consolidation notice. He looked at the clock.

He needed a better argument.
