Chapter 192: Unlicensed Key Defense

# Chapter 192

The pen snapped in Gerald's hand.

Ink bled onto the concrete floor, black and viscous. It pooled around the shattered fragments of the pressure gauge.

Gerald stared at the Void Construct. The creature stood seven feet tall, composed of jagged obsidian shards held together by a humming, violet void.

It did not breathe.

It did not blink. It simply waited, its head tilted at an angle that suggested predatory curiosity.

"Master License," Gerald said. His voice was steady, though his left eye twitched.

The Construct nodded. Stone grated against stone.

"The Inspector General's office has flagged your Core ID," the Construct said. "Your Compliance Rating is volatile. Your citations are... aggressive. The audit begins in twelve minutes."

Gerald looked at the broken pipe. He looked at the Guardian, who was cowering behind a support pillar, clutching its head in a manner that suggested a migraine than existential dread.

"Twelve minutes," Gerald repeated.

He didn't panic.

Panic was inefficient. Panic led to missed deadlines.

Gerald walked past the Construct, stepping over the spilled ink.

He approached the wall where the explosion had occurred.

Debris littered the floor. Exposed rebar twisted like broken ribs.

Gerald pulled a tape measure from his utility belt. The metal click echoed in the sudden silence.

He extended the tape. It hit the opposite wall.

"Seven feet, four inches," Gerald muttered. "Load-bearing capacity compromised by forty percent."

He turned to the Construct.

"Are you here to assist with the inspection?"

The Construct's head rotated. A low hum emanated from its chest.

"I am here to serve the summons."

"Then move that rebar," Gerald said, pointing to a twisted steel rod protruding from the rubble. "It violates Section 402.1: *Hazardous Protrusions in Egress Pathways*. If an adventurer trips on that during the audit, you will be liable for damages."

The Construct looked at the rebar. Then at Gerald.

"I am a weapon of the deep," the Construct said.

"You are a hazard," Gerald corrected. "Move it."

The Construct hesitated. Then, with a grunt of displaced earth, it kicked the rebar out of the path.

Gerald nodded. He pulled a laminated card from his pocket. *Form 11-B: Temporary Hazard Mitigation.*

He signed it. He tucked it into his belt.

The Guardian peeked out from behind the pillar.

"Sir, the water pumps are still failing," the Guardian said. Its voice cracked. "If the audit sees a flood, we lose."

"The audit sees code," Gerald said. "It does not see water unless the water is pooling in a non-permitted area."

Gerald walked to the intake valve. He picked up the shattered pipe. He looked at the leak.

He didn't try to fix it. He couldn't. He wasn't a plumber. He was an inspector.

Instead, he looked at the floor. He looked at the drain grate.

He kicked the grate loose.

Water surged into the sub-floor cavity. The level dropped.

"Drainage is secondary," Gerald said. "Primary drainage is blocked. Therefore, secondary drainage is now the primary drainage, provided the depth of the secondary cavity meets municipal standards."

He pointed to the hole in the floor.

"Guardian. Measure the depth."

The Guardian stared at him.

"Sir?"

"Measure. The depth.

If it is under two feet, it is a hazard.

If it is over two feet, it is a retention basin.

We need it to be a retention basin."

The Guardian scrambled forward, pulling out a measuring stick. It dropped to its knees, plunging the stick into the muddy water.

"It's... it's twenty-four inches," the Guardian said.

Gerald pulled out his notebook. He wrote: *Secondary cavity depth: 24 inches. Meets minimum requirement for temporary containment.*

He tore the page out. He slapped it onto the wet floor.

"Compliant," Gerald said.

The Construct watched this display with growing confusion. Its stone fingers twitched.

"You are altering the environment to fit the rules," the Construct said.

"I am ensuring the environment meets the rules," Gerald said. "There is a difference."

A tremor shook the room. Not a small one. A deep, tectonic shudder.

Dust rained from the ceiling.

**System Notification: Inspector General Arrival Sequence Initiated.** **Time Remaining: 9 Minutes.**

Gerald looked at the Construct.

"Nine minutes," he said. "That is not enough time to rebuild the wall."

He looked at the hole in the wall. The black void of the tunnel behind it.

"We need a barrier," Gerald said. "Something temporary. Something that can be classified as a 'Provisional Safety Wall' under Exception Clause 19-C."

The Construct stepped forward.

"I can build a wall," it said. "I am made of void."

"No," Gerald said. "Void is not a recognized building material in Ashford. It lacks a fire rating.

It lacks structural integrity certification. If you build a wall, the Inspector will ask for the permit.

You do not have a permit."

The Construct growled. "I am the permit."

"You are a liability," Gerald said. He looked around the room. His eyes landed on the shattered glass of the pressure gauge.

Shards of reinforced glass. Thick. Industrial.

Gerald walked over. He picked up a shard. It was jagged, heavy.

"Guardian," Gerald said. "How many shards?"

The Guardian looked at the floor. "Maybe... forty?"

"Enough," Gerald said. "Stack them."

"Stack them?" the Guardian asked, bewildered.

"Make a wall," Gerald said. "Use the mud as mortar. Stack the glass.

Interlock it.

It will hold for ten minutes."

The Construct lowered its head. "That is insane."

"It is compliant," Gerald said. "Glass has a high crush strength. If we angle it, we can create a buttress effect. It will technically be a 'non-structural decorative screen' while also functioning as a barrier."

He grabbed a handful of shards. He began placing them against the exposed rebar.

"Help me," Gerald said.

The Construct did not move.

Gerald looked at it. "If you do not help, the Inspector sees a gaping hole in the dungeon. That is a Class A violation. The penalty is immediate Core Dissolution."

The Construct froze.

The threat of dissolution outweighed its pride.

It stepped forward. It lifted a massive slab of stone from the rubble. It placed it behind the glass shards.

It was crude. It was ugly. But it was solid.

Gerald worked faster. His hands moved with the practiced efficiency of thirty years of clipboard work. He placed shards, pressed mud into gaps, checked alignment with his eye.

**Time Remaining: 6 Minutes.**

The air in the room changed.

It grew cold. Static electricity crackled along the walls.

The shadows lengthened.

A door appeared in the far wall. It was not there before. It was white, pristine, and out of place.

A handle turned.

Gerald stopped working. He wiped his muddy hands on his pants. He straightened his tie. He tucked his notebook into his breast pocket.

"Stand at attention," Gerald said.

The Guardian stood up, trembling.

The Construct remained silent, its stone form rigid.

The door opened.

A figure stepped through.

It was not a monster. It was not a hero.

It was a man. He wore a suit that seemed tailored from light itself. His face was a blank mask of porcelain. He carried a briefcase made of iron.

**Inspector General.**

He looked at the room. He looked at the water. He looked at the glass-and-stone wall.

Gerald stepped forward.

"Inspector General," Gerald said. "Welcome to Ashford Municipal Library Sub-Basement Level 3."

The Inspector General did not speak. He opened his briefcase.

Inside were scrolls. Thousands of them.

He unrolled one. It floated in the air.

**Compliance Audit: Gerald Hutchins.** **Core ID: 8842-G.** **Current Rating: 47/100.**

The Inspector General pointed a finger at the water.

"Flooded," the voice boomed. It sounded like grinding tectonic plates.

Gerald pointed to the hole in the floor.

"Secondary retention basin," Gerald said. "Depth: twenty-four inches. Meets municipal code for temporary containment during structural remediation."

The Inspector General floated closer. He looked at the glass wall.

"Improvised barrier," the voice said.

"Provisional safety screen," Gerald corrected. "Exception Clause 19-C. Permitted for emergency hazard mitigation."

The Inspector General turned to the Construct.

"Unregistered Entity," the voice said.

Gerald stepped in front of the Construct.

"Not unregistered," Gerald said. "Voluntary Assistant Inspector. Hired under contract for hazardous material removal."

The Construct shifted. It looked at Gerald.

"Contract?" the Construct hissed.

"I wrote one," Gerald said. He pulled a crumpled piece of paper from his pocket. It was a napkin. On it, in shaky handwriting, was a signature: *Void Construct.* And below it: *Gerald Hutchins.*

The Inspector General took the napkin. He read it.

The porcelain face remained blank.

He unrolled another scroll.

**Violation: Operating without Master License.**

Gerald's heart hammered against his ribs. He kept his face neutral.

"I am not operating," Gerald said.

"I am inspecting. The Master License is required for dungeon construction. I am not constructing.

I am maintaining."

The Inspector General tilted his head.

"The System defines 'construction' as any change to the dungeon layout."

Gerald looked at the glass wall.

"The wall is a hazard," Gerald said. "Removing a hazard is not construction. It is remediation."

The Inspector General paused.

This was the loophole.

Gerald had seen it in the fine print of the *Dungeon Core Liability Act*, Section 4, Subsection B. *Remediation of existing defects does not count toward construction quota limits unless new materials are introduced.*

Glass was not a new material. It was existing debris.

Stone was existing debris.

The wall was not new. It was a reorganization of old trash.

The Inspector General floated closer. He circled the room. He checked the handrails. He checked the egress signs.

He stopped at the leak.

He looked at Gerald.

"Your rating is still 47," the voice said.

"Correct," Gerald said.

"You have three minutes to raise it to 50, or the Core is suspended."

Gerald looked at the room.

He looked at the Construct.

The Construct looked back.

Gerald realized something.

The Construct was not just a monster. It was a void entity. Void entities had high resistance to magical damage. They also had a unique property: they absorbed light.

Absorbed light meant lower lumens.

Darkness was a code violation in public spaces.

But...

Gerald looked at the dark corners of the room.

He pulled out his pen. The broken one.

He wrote on his notebook.

**Observation: Ambient lighting insufficient in Sector 4.**

He looked at the Inspector.

"I have one more citation to issue," Gerald said.

The Inspector General raised an eyebrow.

"To whom?"

Gerald pointed to the Construct.

"To you," Gerald said.

He pointed to the Construct.

"No," Gerald said. "To the System."

He pointed to the ceiling.

"The System requires minimum lumens for adventurer safety. The Construct is absorbing the light. It is a violation of the Illumination Code."

The Construct's eyes widened.

"You cannot cite the Inspector General," the Construct said.

"I am citing the *environment*," Gerald said. "And the environment is being altered by an external agent."

He pointed to the Construct.

"Agent: Void Construct. Action: Light Absorption. Result: Code Violation."

The Inspector General looked at the Construct.

He looked at the dark corners.

He looked at Gerald.

"Explain," the voice said.

Gerald took a breath.

"If the Construct remains, the dungeon fails the Illumination Code. If the Construct leaves, the dungeon fails the Security Code, because the breach in the wall remains open."

Gerald paused.

"There is no compliant outcome unless the Construct is removed OR the lighting is increased."

The Inspector General floated still.

"Increase the lighting," the voice said.

"I have no bulbs," Gerald said.

"Then remove the Construct."

The Construct looked at Gerald.

"You want me to leave?" it asked.

"I want you to fix it," Gerald said.

"You are made of void.

You create darkness. But you can also create structure.

Build a light fixture. Make it compliant."

The Construct looked at the ceiling.

It raised its hand.

Violet energy coalesced.

It formed a sphere.

It floated upward.

It attached to the ceiling.

It glowed with a harsh, purple light.

The room brightened.

The Inspector General looked up.

He checked the lumen count.

He looked at the glass wall.

He looked at the water.

He looked at Gerald.

**Compliance Rating: 51/100.**

**Status: Pass.**

The Inspector General closed his briefcase.

"Temporary pass," the voice said. "Re-audit in one hour."

The door slammed shut.

The white door vanished.

Silence returned.

Gerald slumped against the wall. He slid down to the floor.

The Construct stared at him.

"You cited me," the Construct said.

"I cited the violation," Gerald said. He pulled out his notebook. He wrote.

"You saved my core," the Construct said.

"I saved the compliance," Gerald said.

The Construct looked at the glowing purple sphere on the ceiling.

"That light is ugly," the Construct said.

"It is code-compliant," Gerald said.

He stood up. He dusted off his pants.

He looked at the Guardian.

"Get the mop," Gerald said. "We have an hour before the Inspector comes back. We need to get this rating up to 60."

The Guardian looked at the massive hole in the wall.

"We can't fix the hole in an hour," the Guardian said.

Gerald looked at the Construct.

"Can you make more light?" Gerald asked.

The Construct hesitated.

"I can make more," it said.

"Good," Gerald said. "Make it brighter. Make it blinding. Make it so bright it compensates for the lack of ventilation."

The Construct looked confused.

"How does brightness compensate for ventilation?"

"Because," Gerald said, "if it's bright enough, no one notices the air smells like mildew."

He pulled out a fresh pen.

He wrote: *Form 12-A: Temporary Lighting Augmentation for Atmospheric Masking.*

He signed it.

"Get to work," Gerald said.

The Construct sighed. It raised its hands.

More purple spheres formed.

Gerald watched them. He checked his watch.

Fifty-nine minutes left.

He looked at the door where the Inspector had exited.

He knew something the Inspector didn't.

The Inspector had seen the glass wall. He had seen the light.

But he hadn't seen what was behind the glass.

Gerald looked at the rubble pile.

He walked over. He kicked aside a few stones.

Underneath the rubble, buried in the mud, was a small, metallic box.

It was not part of the dungeon.

It was not part of the library.

It was marked with the logo of the Ashford City Council.

Gerald picked it up.

He opened it.

Inside was a single, golden key.

And a note.

*For the next audit, bring this to the door. It will open a different one.*

Gerald stared at the key.

The Inspector General had not just audited him.

He had left him a way out.

Or a way in.

Gerald looked at the Construct.

The Construct was building a chandelier.

Gerald pocketed the key.

He looked at the clock.

Fifty-eight minutes.

He had a new violation.

And a new opportunity.

He pulled out his notebook.

He wrote: *Addendum: Unauthorized Artifact Found.*

He underlined it twice.

He looked at the Construct.

"Stop," Gerald said.

The Construct froze.

"Put the light down," Gerald said.

"Why?"

"Because," Gerald said, "I think the Inspector General is testing me."

He looked at the white door that was no longer there.

"He left a key," Gerald said. "He wants to see what I do with it."

The Construct lowered its hands. The purple spheres dimmed.

Gerald gripped the key.

The air in the room grew heavy.

Somewhere, deep in the dungeon, something else woke up.

Gerald could hear it.

A heartbeat.

Slow.

Loud.

Coming from beneath the floor.

He looked at the Guardian.

"Did you hear that?" Gerald asked.

The Guardian shook its head.

Gerald looked at the floor.

The mud was bubbling.

The key in Gerald's pocket grew hot.

It burned his thigh.

He didn't drop it.

He stood still.

He waited.

The bubbling stopped.

The silence was absolute.

Then, a voice spoke. Not from the air. From inside Gerald's skull.

*"Citation Accepted,"* the voice said.

Gerald blinked.

He hadn't issued a citation.

He had just found an artifact.

The System notification appeared in his vision, red and flashing.

**New Violation Detected: Possession of Unlicensed Key.** **Penalty: Immediate Core Lockdown.** **Time to Appeal: 30 Seconds.**

Gerald looked at the clock.

He looked at the Construct.

He looked at the bubbling floor.

He pulled out his pen.

He had thirty seconds to write a legal defense for a key he hadn't asked for.

He started writing.
