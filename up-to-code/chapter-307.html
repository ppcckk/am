Chapter 307: Existence Without Permit

# Chapter 307

The web snapped.

Not broke. Snapped.

Gerald felt the vibration travel down the silk thread, a high-frequency tremor that rattled his teeth. He was three feet from the ceiling slab, hanging in the dark, his claws dug into the sticky strands. Above him, the concrete crack pulsed with sickly green light. Below, the void entity's corpse gaped open, a wound in the fabric of reality that leaked raw mana into his basement.

He tried to climb. His grip failed. The silk was too smooth, too fragile against his chitinous paws.

Gravity pulled. He dropped a foot.

"Stabilize," he muttered. The word tasted like copper.

The spider in the corner chittered, a sound like dry leaves skittering on pavement. It spun another line, thick as a shoelace, angling toward a rusted pipe protruding from the wall.

Gerald lunged.

His claws caught the pipe. The silk anchored him. He hung there, swaying, the drop below a black abyss of leaking energy.

He looked up. The crack was widening.

Small stones fell from the ceiling. *Clink. Clink.*

**[Warning: Structural Integrity of Surface Foundation Compromised.]** **[Current Load-Bearing Capacity: 12%]**

Gerald stared at the message hovering in his peripheral vision. The text was red. Not the usual amber of a notice, but deep, pulsing crimson.

He hadn't received a red warning since the initial registration. Since the day he'd dissolved into a rat and watched the library crumble around him.

"Twelve percent," he whispered.

That wasn't just a leak, which was an imminent collapse. If the library ceiling came down, it wouldn't just crush his dungeon.

It would crush the books.

The people. The entire municipal block.

And if the library was destroyed, the City would condemn the land. The System would deregister his Core. He would cease to exist.

The woman's voice echoed in his memory. *You have to go up.*

But he couldn't go up. The hole was too small for a rat, let alone a Core. He was anchored here, tethered to the leak.

He looked at the pipe he was holding. It was old galvanized steel. Rust flaked under his touch.

A new thought formed. A dangerous thought.

The Spider wasn't just a filter. It was a Weaver. Registered.

**[Weaver Status: Active]** **[Skill Unlocked: Thread Tension]**

Gerald didn't know what that meant. He didn't have time to read the tooltip.

He looked at the crack. He looked at the spider.

"Pull," he commanded.

The spider hesitated.

"Pull the thread!" Gerald shouted. His voice echoed in the wet stone chamber. "Tighten the web!"

The spider moved. It didn't spin new silk. It grabbed the existing line leading to the pipe and pulled.

The pipe groaned. Metal shrieked against metal.

Gerald felt the tension in the line. It wasn't just holding his weight. It was pulling the ceiling slab.

He watched the crack. The green light flared. A chunk of concrete the size of a dinner plate broke free.

It fell.

Gerald dodged. The debris smashed into the mold-spore patch, scattering spores like smoke.

He couldn't patch the hole. He couldn't climb out.

But he could brace it, and he looked at the pipe again. It was rusted, weak. It wouldn't hold the weight of the library floor. Not for long.

He needed support. He needed a column.

He looked at the void entity's corpse. It was massive, pulsating, oozing mana.

It was a violation of every sanitation code Gerald had ever written, which was a biohazard.

It was illegal.

It was also the only thing big enough to fill the space under the crack.

"I'm going to cite it," Gerald said.

The spider chittered. A confused sound.

"Not for you," Gerald said. He turned his mind toward the System interface. It was blurry, obscured by the green glow of the leak.

He reached for the Citation function. His mental hand shook. He hadn't used it in weeks.

Not since the Hearing with the Mid-Tier Core. Not since he'd learned that winning an argument didn't mean you survived the aftermath.

He focused. He pushed his will into the System.

**[Target: Unlicensed Biological Hazard (Void Entity Remains)]** **[Violation: Failure to Contain Hazardous Materials]** **[Violation: Obstruction of Egress Route]** **[Violation: Improper Disposal of Toxic Waste]**

The System hesitated.

**[Target is Non-Registered.]** **[Citation Cannot Be Served.]**

Gerald gritted his teeth. He couldn't cite a dead thing. The System didn't recognize corpses as legal entities.

But the leak wasn't the corpse. The leak was the *hole*.

The hole wasn't a thing. It was a lack of a thing.

He changed his tactic. He didn't cite the corpse. He cited the *wall*.

**[Target: Sub-Basement Wall (North Sector)]** **[Violation: Structural Failure Due to External Load]** **[Violation: Lack of Fire Suppression in Proximity to Open Void]**

The System paused. The red warning flickered.

**[Violation Accepted.]** **[Penalty: Immediate Structural Reinforcement Required.]** **[Cost: 500 Mana.]**

Gerald didn't have 500 Mana. He had twelve. The leak was draining him dry.

But the System didn't ask if he had it. It asked if he *accepted* the cost.

He accepted.

The green light surged, and the System took what it wanted. It took the mana from the leak. It took the mana from the spider.

It took the mana from Gerald's own reserves.

For a second, Gerald felt nothing. No sight. No sound. No self.

Then, the wall moved.

Not physically. The concrete didn't shift. But the *concept* of the wall changed.

The System enforced the code. If the wall was failing due to external load, it needed reinforcement. If it lacked fire suppression, it needed material resistant to heat.

The void energy leaking through the crack was heat. It was chaos.

The System pulled the leaking mana and forced it into the concrete. It hardened. It bonded. The green sludge didn't fall away; it fused into the stone.

The crack sealed.

Not fully. A hairline fracture remained. But the pressure dropped. The green light dimmed.

Gerald hung on the pipe, gasping. His claws were numb. His vision swam.

He had bought himself time. He had patched the hole with poison.

But the debt was paid.

**[Mana Balance: -120]** **[Debt Collector Notified.]**

A new notification popped up. Small. White. Bureaucratic.

**[Inspection Scheduled: 48 Hours.]** **[Inspector: Inspector General Unit 7]** **[Subject: Gerald Hutchins, Core ID 44-902]**

Gerald stared at the text.

The Inspector General.

The one who audited everyone. The one who had watched him from the shadows since Chapter 40.

He hadn't just fixed a leak. He had flagged himself.

By using the void energy, he had created a hybrid structure. The System's code didn't have a category for

"mana-infused concrete." It had called it a violation, then fixed it, but it had left a trace. A fingerprint.

The Inspector General was coming.

And the City was still above him, and he looked up. The seal was holding, but the ceiling slab was still cracked. The library above was still unstable.

He heard a sound from above. Footsteps. Heavy boots.

Voices.

"...heard the explosion..." "...structural damage assessment..." "...check the sub-basement..."

They were already here. The City's inspectors. Not the System's. Human inspectors.

Gerald pulled himself up the pipe. He had to hide. He had to become part of the dungeon's background. He had to make himself invisible.

But he was a Core. He couldn't hide from the System. And he couldn't hide from the humans who were now walking down the stairs.

He looked at the spider.

"Clean it up," he whispered. "Make it look like a water leak. A normal, boring water leak."

The spider didn't understand. It looked at the sealed crack. It looked at Gerald.

"Clean it up!" Gerald snapped.

The spider weaved. Not silk.

Dust.

Cobwebs. It covered the fresh seal with layers of gray grime. It made the wall look old.

Neglected. Normal.

The footsteps grew louder.

A beam of light cut through the darkness.

"Hello?" a voice called. "Is anyone down here?"

Gerald froze. He pressed himself into the shadows of the pipe joint. He made himself small. He made himself rat.

A man in a hard hat descended the stairs. He held a flashlight in one hand and a clipboard in the other.

The man swept the beam across the room. It hit the mold spores. It hit the water leak. It hit the spider.

The spider froze, pretending to be a dead thing.

The man frowned. He walked closer.

"Smells weird," the man said. "Like ozone and rot."

He stepped onto the wet floor. His boot splashed.

Gerald watched.

One wrong move.

One slip. The man would fall into the spider's web.

The man would see the Core. The Core would be exposed. The City would condemn the library.

The man looked at the wall. He tapped the sealed crack with his pen.

*Tap. Tap.*

Solid.

"Huh," the man said. "Must have settled. Cracked the plaster."

He scribbled something on his clipboard.

Gerald exhaled.

The man turned to leave.

Then he stopped, and he looked at the spider.

The spider looked back.

"Hey," the man said. "That spider looks big."

He leaned in.

Gerald felt a spike of panic. Not fear. Calculation.

The man was not a hero. He was a bureaucrat. He cared about forms. He cared about liability.

If the man reported a giant spider, he would have to file a pest control report. He would have to wait for a specialist. He would have to delay his inspection.

If he didn't report it, he might be liable if it bit someone.

The man hesitated. He looked at his clipboard. He looked at the spider.

"Is that... regulated?" the man muttered.

He pulled out his phone. He took a picture.

**[Flash Detected.]**

Gerald felt the System stir. A photo wasn't a citation. But it was evidence.

The man typed into his phone.

*Potential Code Violation: Unlicensed Arachnid in Commercial Space.*

Gerald's mind raced. The System had a category for this. Form 11-B. Unlicensed Biological Entity.

The man was trying to serve a citation.

But he wasn't a Core. He had no authority.

The System would ignore him.

Unless.

Unless Gerald accepted it.

If Gerald accepted the citation, he could control the outcome. He could appeal it. He could argue that the spider was a registered Weaver, not an unlicensed pest.

But if he appealed, he had to be present. He had to show himself.

The man was waiting. The photo was uploaded.

**[Pending Citation: Unlicensed Biological Entity.]** **[Respond within 60 seconds or forfeit right to appeal.]**

Gerald stared at the prompt.

If he appealed, the City would know a Core was here. They would bring the SWAT team. They would bring the exorcists.

If he didn't appeal, the System would fine him. A minor fine. Manageable.

But the fine would be on record. It would be a mark of non-compliance.

The Inspector General was coming in forty-eight hours. A mark of non-compliance would make the audit harder. It would justify a full demolition.

Gerald looked at the man. The man was just a man. He was bored. He wanted to go home.

Gerald had a choice.

Hide and pay the fine. Or fight and reveal himself.

He looked at the ceiling. The seal was holding. The library was safe.

For now.

But the debt was real. The Inspector General was coming. And the City was already poking at the edges.

Gerald made his decision, and he didn't appeal. He didn't hide.

He did something else.

He reached out to the spider. He grabbed the thread of its consciousness. He pushed his own intent into it.

*Move,* he thought. *Look aggressive.*

The spider chittered. It raised its front legs. It hissed.

The man jumped back.

"Whoa!"

The spider lunged. Not at the man.

At the wall.

It spun a web across the cracked section. A thick, sticky barrier.

The man stared.

"That... that's a hazard," the man said. He started backing away. "I'm calling it in."

He ran up the stairs.

Gerald felt the connection snap. The spider was alone. Exposed.

But the man was gone. The photo was taken.

And the System had seen the spider's action.

**[Compliance Update: Self-Defense Authorized.]** **[Status: Threat Contained.]**

Gerald slumped against the pipe. He was safe. For tonight.

But he had used a loophole. He had used a registered entity to commit an act of aggression, thereby triggering a self-defense exemption that masked the Core's presence.

It was brilliant. It was illegal.

And it had worked.

Until the phone rang.

The man's phone. Left behind. Dropped on the wet floor.

It buzzed.

**Incoming Call: Ashford City Planning Department.**

Gerald stared at the phone, and he couldn't move. He was a rat in the shadows.

The call connected. A voice came from the phone, tinny and distant.

"Did you find the source?"

"Yeah," the man's voice said, from upstairs. "Big spider. Hissing. Threw web."

"Okay. Log it as a Class 3 Aggressive Fauna. Quarantine the area. No entry."

"Already done."

"Good. Also, check the structural integrity again. The scanner picked up a mana spike."

Gerald's heart stopped.

"Mana spike?"

"Yeah. Weird reading. Like a Core signature."

Gerald looked at the ceiling. The seal held. The spider sat in the web, waiting for orders.

The man's voice drifted down.

"Probably interference. Old wiring."

"Check the sub-basement," the voice said. "The Inspector General flagged this location this morning. There's a pending audit."

Gerald closed his eyes, and the Inspector General was already here.

Not in forty-eight hours.

Now.

He looked at the stairs.

He couldn't go up, and he couldn't go down.

The walls were closing in. The system was tightening.

And somewhere, in the dark above, a new door was opening.

Gerald Hutchins, Municipal Building Inspector, Dungeon Core, Criminal, stood in the dark and listened to the footsteps returning.

He didn't run, and he picked up the phone.

He dialed 911.

"I'd like to report a noise complaint," Gerald whispered.

The line clicked.

A new voice answered. Not the City. Not the System.

"Mr. Hutchins," the voice said. "We need to talk about your ventilation."

Gerald dropped the phone.

**[Hearing Initiated.]** **[Adversary: Inspector General Unit 7.]** **[Charge: Existence Without Permit.]**

The charge wasn’t a crime; it was a death sentence.
