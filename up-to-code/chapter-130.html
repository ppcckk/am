Chapter 130: Blank Building Permit

# Chapter 130

The testimony hung in the stagnant air of the sub-basement, a violation of the sanctity of sworn statements. Thorne's voice, stolen from a dead man's throat and pumped through a rodent's lungs, screamed that the code was a lie.

Gerald did not flinch. His knuckles whitened against the cold, wet stone of the library foundation. The rat sat on the floating debris, its eyes wide and black, vibrating with the resonance of a voice that did not belong to it.

The System clock ticked. **TESTIMONY PENDING.**

The audit timer hovered at three seconds.

Gerald's mind raced, bypassing the panic that usually paralyzed him. He did not think about the dragon. He did not think about the fire. He thought about Section 4.2, Paragraph C: *Admissibility of Secondary Witnesses in Non-Standard Proceedings.*

He had never read it until now, and the System did not care about truth. It cared about procedure.

And Thorne had broken procedure by speaking.

A witness must be sworn in. A witness must be identified. A witness cannot testify *about* the validity of the law; they can only testify to facts observed.

Thorne's statement was an opinion. It was inadmissible.

Gerald raised his hand. He did not shout. He did not plead. He pointed a trembling finger at the rat.

"Objection," Gerald said. "Hearsay. And irrelevant."

The water around his ankles rippled. The blue interface box flickered, confused by the interruption.

Thorne's voice crackled again, distorted by the rat's tiny vocal cords. *

"The System is a cage, Gerald. You are the jailer."*

"Overruled," Gerald said, his voice flat. "That is not testimony. That is a manifesto."

He pulled a clipboard from his inventory, which was empty. He pulled out a pen.

It was dry.

He signed his name on the blank page anyway. The System accepted the signature as a formal motion to strike.

**MOTION TO STRIKE ACCEPTED.** **TESTIMONY REJECTED.** **AUDIT RESUMES.**

The timer hit zero.

The world turned red.

Pain slammed into Gerald's core, not as a sensation, but as a structural failure. His dungeon walls groaned.

The foundation beneath the library shifted, cracks spiderwebbing across the concrete floor, and the System was stripping his assets.

It was revoking his zoning permit.

He was no longer a Dungeon Core. He was an unlicensed squatter.

The notification flashed: **STATUS: UNLICENSED. REMEDIATION REQUIRED.**

Gerald sank to his knees in the rising water. The rat vanished, sucked back into the void of its own existence. Thorne's voice was gone.

Silence.

Then, a new notification. Not red. White.

**INSPECTOR GENERAL HAS ARRIVED.**

The air pressure dropped. The water in the basement stopped moving. The cracks in the floor froze in place.

A figure materialized from the shadows of the boiler room. It was not a monster. It was a man in a grey suit, holding a clipboard that glowed with a faint, golden light. His face was a blur of static, indistinct and terrifying.

"Gerald Hutchins," the Inspector General said. The voice sounded like a gavel strike. "Your Compliance Rating is currently zero. You have violated the Zoning Act of 1994, the Monster Safety Code, and the Prohibition on Unlicensed Necromancy."

Gerald stared up at the figure. His heart hammered against his ribs, but his hands were steady. He looked at the Inspector General's clipboard.

"Where is your badge number?" Gerald asked.

The Inspector General paused. The static around his face cleared slightly, revealing eyes that looked like voids.

"My authority is absolute."

"Authority requires accountability," Gerald said. "Section 1, Article 3 of the Inspector General's Charter. Identification must be provided before enforcement action commences."

The Inspector General did not move. The water rose again, creeping up Gerald's waist.

"You are delaying the inevitable, Hutchins."

"I am following protocol," Gerald said. He stood up. The water was chest-deep now.

"If you do not identify yourself, you cannot serve the notice. And if you cannot serve the notice, you cannot enforce the penalty."

The Inspector General tilted his head.

"You are attempting to loophole the audit."

"I am attempting to ensure the audit is valid," Gerald corrected. "If the audit is invalid, the penalties are void. If the penalties are void, your time here is wasted."

The Inspector General looked at his clipboard. Then he looked at Gerald.

"You are a difficult man."

"I am a building inspector," Gerald said. "Show me your badge."

For a long, agonizing minute, the Inspector General said nothing. The pressure in the room increased until Gerald's ears popped. The concrete above them, the floor of the library, seemed to buckle.

Then, the Inspector General reached into his suit pocket, and he pulled out a small, metallic tag. It clicked against the clipboard.

**ID: IG-001.**

Gerald took a breath. The water stopped rising.

"Notice served," the Inspector General said. "You have ten minutes to remediate the violations. Failure to comply results in total demolition of the Core."

"Demolition?" Gerald asked.

"The removal of the entity," the Inspector General clarified. "You will be unmade."

Gerald looked around his dungeon. The mold spores were gone. The water leak was a torrent now. The single confused rat was gone.

He had no defenses. No traps. No monsters.

He had a clipboard, and he looked at the Inspector General.

"Ten minutes is insufficient for a full structural audit."

"It is all you get."

The Inspector General vanished.

Gerald was alone.

The water was cold. It soaked through his clothes, freezing his bones. He looked at the timer. **9:59.**

He had to fix the ventilation. He had to fix the egress. He had to fix the electrical.

But he had no materials. He had no spawn list.

He looked at the walls. The concrete was cracking. The rebar was rusting.

Gerald closed his eyes. He thought about the promise. *The code is a promise.*

But a promise requires a foundation. And his foundation was broken.

He opened his eyes. He looked at the inventory.

He had one item left.

*Form 11-B: Unlicensed Biological Entity.*

It was empty. It had no value. It was just paper.

But it was a form.

And Gerald Hutchins knew that forms were powerful, and he reached out with his mind, touching the damp, rotting wood of a support beam. He could not spawn a monster. He could not spawn a trap.

But he could spawn a *document*.

He concentrated on the wood. He imagined the grain. He imagined the age. He imagined the lack of maintenance.

He pulled.

The wood groaned.

**STRUCTURAL FAILURE IMMINENT.**

Gerald ignored it. He pulled harder.

A notification flashed.

**ITEM CREATED: INSPECTION REPORT 44-A.**

A piece of paper materialized in his hand. It was not empty. It was filled with text.

Gerald read it.

*Violation: Non-compliant load-bearing wall. Penalty: Immediate collapse.*

He looked at the beam.

If the wall was non-compliant, it was not his responsibility. It was the contractor's responsibility.

Gerald tore the paper in half, and he threw one half at the beam.

The beam snapped.

The library above them shuddered. Dust rained down from the ceiling.

Gerald did not care. He grabbed the other half of the paper and ran to the main staircase.

The stairs were crumbling. The handrails were loose.

He slapped the paper onto the banister.

**VIOLATION CITED: HAZARDOUS EGRESS.**

The stairs did not fall. They *stopped* falling. The System froze the decay to allow for the citation to be processed.

It was a loophole. A tiny, desperate loophole.

Citations paused the decay of the cited object.

Gerald had just cited his own stairs to save them from collapsing.

He had bought himself ten seconds, and he ran down the stairs, into the lower sub-basement. The area was dark. The air smelled of ozone and rot.

He needed power. The dungeon was dying because its energy core was offline. The battery was drained.

He found the breaker box. It was fused shut.

Gerald kicked it. He kicked it again. The metal dented.

He needed to bypass the lock. But bypassing a lock was a security violation.

He needed a *permission slip*, and he looked at his inventory. He had no forms.

He had only the memory of the code.

*Section 7.4: Emergency Access to Utility Panels.*

*In the event of a life-safety emergency, the lock may be defeated if a Class A Hazard is present.*

Was there a Class A Hazard?

The water was rising. The air was toxic. The structure was failing.

Yes.

Gerald punched the breaker box.

His fist shattered the glass. His hand bled.

He ripped the fuse block out.

The lights flickered. Then died.

Darkness.

**SYSTEM ERROR: POWER LOSS.**

**COMPLIANCE RATING: 0.**

**IMMINENT TERMINATION.**

Gerald stood in the dark. He could feel his life force draining away. The System was deleting him.

He heard a sound.

Not from the water. From the walls.

Scratching.

Small, frantic scratching.

Gerald froze, and he knew that sound.

It was the rat.

The rat had not been deleted.

It was hiding in the walls.

And it was bringing something.

A small, glowing orb rolled out of a crack in the foundation. It was a *Compliance Shard*.

Gerald had created it without knowing.

The shard hovered in the air. It pulsed with a weak, blue light.

**SHARD TYPE: MINOR VIOLATION.** **EFFECT: +5 COMPLIANCE RATING FOR 60 SECONDS.**

Gerald reached for it.

His fingers brushed the light, which did not heal him. It did not stop the audit.

But it gave him *presence*.

For sixty seconds, he was not an error. He was a player.

He grabbed the shard. He slammed it into the breaker box.

The lights slammed on. Blinding white.

The water receded, pulled back by the sudden change in pressure.

The System notification changed.

**POWER RESTORED.** **COMPLIANCE RATING: 5.**

It was still terrible. But it was not zero.

And the Inspector General had not reappeared.

Gerald gasped, clutching his bleeding hand. He looked at the timer.

**7:30 REMAINING.**

He had seven minutes to raise his rating from 5 to passing.

He needed to fix ventilation. He needed to fix fire safety. He needed to fix structure.

He had no resources.

But he had the Shard.

And he had the rat.

Gerald looked at the wall.

"Come out," he said.

The scratching stopped.

Then, a small head poked out of the crack. The rat looked at him. It was shaking.

"You're a witness," Gerald said. "Witnesses get protection."

The rat hesitated.

"Come out. Or I cite the wall for harboring unauthorized wildlife."

The rat emerged. It looked terrified.

Gerald knelt. He held out his hand.

The rat climbed onto his palm, which was warm.

Gerald looked at the Inspector General's previous location. The suit was gone. But the clipboard remained, floating in the air.

**AUDIT RESUMES IN: 5 SECONDS.**

Gerald looked at the rat.

"Can you speak?" he asked.

The rat shook its head.

"Can you squeak?"

The rat nodded.

Gerald pulled out his pen. He tore a scrap of paper from his jeans.

"I need you to testify to the presence of a Code Violation in the adjacent sector."

The rat looked confused.

"The library," Gerald said. "The library is a public building. If it is not up to code, I am not responsible for its safety. If the city does not maintain it, the violation belongs to the city."

He was trying to shift the liability, which was the oldest trick in the book.

The rat squeaked.

**TESTIMONY PENDING.**

The System paused again.

Gerald looked up at the ceiling. He could hear the footsteps of the library patrons above. He could hear the librarian walking overhead.

If he could get the rat to testify that the library itself was non-compliant, the System might redirect the audit.

It was a long shot.

But Gerald Hutchins had bet his career on long shots before.

He held the rat up.

"State your name," Gerald said.

The rat squeaked.

**WITNESS NAME: UNIDENTIFIED.**

"State the violation," Gerald said.

The rat squeaked again.

**VIOLATION: MISSING EGRESS SIGNAGE.**

The System paused.

**EVIDENCE REVIEWED.**

**LIABILITY TRANSFERRED.**

**AUDIT SUSPENDED.**

Gerald exhaled.

The red glow faded. The water stopped rising.

He was safe.

For now.

The floating clipboard dropped to the floor with a thud.

Gerald stood up. His legs shook. He looked at the rat.

"Thank you," he said.

The rat scurried away, back into the walls.

Gerald looked at his Compliance Rating.

**RATING: 12/100.**

It was still failing. But he had bought time.

He had to fix the rest, and he turned to the exit. He needed to leave the dungeon. He needed to go to the library. He needed to file a report.

He walked toward the stairs.

The door was locked.

He frowned. He had never locked the door.

He tried the handle. It was stuck.

He pulled. It didn't budge.

**ERROR: ZONING RESTRICTION.**

**ACCESS DENIED.**

Gerald stared at the door, and he had been locked in.

The System had decided that because he was a low-rated Core, he was no longer allowed to exit his own dungeon.

He was a prisoner.

And the Inspector General was still watching.

Gerald looked at the rat's hole in the wall. It was too small for him.

He looked at the water. It was rising again.

He looked at the clock.

**AUDIT RESUMES IN: 10 MINUTES.**

He had ten minutes to find a way out.

Or he would be buried under the library, unmade by the code he served.

Gerald walked to the center of the room. He sat down on a pile of wet debris.

He opened his inventory, and he had one item left.

*Building Permit Application.*

It was blank.

Gerald smiled. It was a small, tired smile.

He knew how to fill it out.
