Chapter 267: Code Compliant Tomb

# Chapter 267

The pen snapped.

Gerald stared at the plastic casing in his hand. The ink had pooled into a black puddle on the ledger page, obscuring the signature line. He dropped the ruined pen. It clattered against the stone floor, echoing in the long, unlit corridor.

Six months, and the number sat heavy in his stomach. Not because of the time itself, but because of what it implied. A delay in administrative processing was not a pause. It was a permission slip for chaos.

He turned away from the desk. The woman, the Administrative Liaison, did not follow. She remained behind the iron door, watching through the grate. Her eyes tracked him with the cold patience of a predator who knows the prey is already caged.

Gerald walked.

The corridor stretched into darkness. The floor tiles were square, gray, and uniform.

He counted his steps.

One. Two. Three.

The walls were bare. No signage. No handrails.

This was a violation, and he stopped. Turned. Walked back.

The Liaison blinked.

"Where are you going?"

"Egress inspection," Gerald said.

"This is the Administrative Layer. There is no public egress."

"There is always egress. That is the law."

Gerald pulled a measuring tape from his pocket. It was not a standard tape. It was a Dungeon Core interface tool, manifested from his own mana reserves, disguised as a yellow retractable tape measure. He extended it.

The red blade shot forward. It hit the opposite wall.

Twelve feet, and he retracted it. Measured the other side.

Twelve feet.

He looked up. The ceiling height.

Eight feet.

"Gross floor area: two hundred and eighty-eight square feet per segment," Gerald muttered. "Ceiling height insufficient for assembly occupancy under current code."

The Liaison frowned.

"You are a Core. You do not have occupancy."

"I have a body," Gerald said. "It is currently dissolved into this space. Therefore, this space is my body. And my body must comply with structural integrity standards."

He walked forward again. Faster this time.

The corridor bent left. Then right. A maze.

Gerald did not panic. He mapped it.

*Left turn, forty feet. Right turn, thirty feet. T-junction.*

At the junction, three doors stood closed. They had no numbers. No handles.

He approached the center door. He placed his palm against the wood.

*Structural check.*

The System responded with a prompt in his peripheral vision.

**[SUBSTRATE: WOODEN. DENSITY: LOW. FIRE RATING: UNRATED.]**

Gerald's lip curled. Unrated. A Class 3 Fire Hazard.

He did not open the door. He kicked it.

The wood splintered.

The Liaison rushed to the grate, her face pressed against the bars.

"Stop that! You are damaging the infrastructure!"

"The infrastructure is non-compliant," Gerald said. He kicked again. The door flew off its hinges.

Behind the door was not a room. It was a void.

A swirling mass of gray mist churned in the darkness. It looked like fog, but it moved with intent. Tendrils of gray reached toward the hallway, testing the air.

Gerald stepped back, and he pulled out a small notebook. He wrote.

*Violation 402: Improper containment of hazardous atmospheric material.*

*Penalty: Immediate abatement required.*

The tendrils lashed out. One wrapped around his ankle.

Gerald did not pull away. He looked at the limb holding him.

It was thin. Weak.

He snapped his fingers.

**[CITATION ISSUED: SEVERE HAZARD.]**

A red stamp materialized in the air. It slammed into the tendril.

The gray mist shrieked. It a sound like tearing metal.

The tendril withered. It dropped away from his ankle, recoiling into the void.

Gerald rubbed his ankle.

"That is a citation. Pay your fine."

The Liaison stared.

"You... you cited the Void?"

"It is part of the building envelope," Gerald said. "If it leaks, it is a code violation."

He walked past the void. He chose the right-hand corridor.

The path narrowed. The ceiling dropped.

He had to stoop, which was bad.

He felt a pressure in his chest. Not fear. Indigestion. The physical manifestation of his professional discomfort.

The air tasted stale. Metallic.

He stopped. Closed his eyes.

He listened.

Far off, in the direction of the left corridor, he heard a sound.

*Click. Click. Click.*

Hard shoes on stone.

Multiple pairs.

Gerald opened his eyes. He looked at the Liaison, who was still watching from the iron door.

"Who is coming?" he asked.

"Nobody," she said. But her eyes shifted away.

Gerald looked down the narrow hallway.

At the far end, a light flickered, which was not electric. It was green. Sickly green.

Gerald walked toward it.

The hallway opened into a larger chamber.

This was not an administrative office.

It was a courtroom.

But the architecture was wrong. The pillars were twisted, spiraling like DNA helices. The floor was glass, revealing a churning abyss beneath.

And sitting at the high bench were three figures, and they were not human.

The center figure was a mass of floating geometry. Cubes of light orbiting a sphere of dark iron.

To its left, a woman with skin like bark and leaves growing from her hair.

To its right, a creature made entirely of rusted gears and steam.

The Central Figure spoke. Its voice was a harmonic resonance that vibrated in Gerald's teeth.

**"INSPECTOR GERALD HUTCHINS. YOU HAVE ENTERED THE APPEAL VENUE WITHOUT A SCHEDULED HEARING."**

Gerald straightened his tie. He adjusted his glasses.

"I did not enter without a hearing," Gerald said. "I was detained. I am exercising my right to immediate review."

**"THE INSPECTOR GENERAL IS UNAVAILABLE."**

"The Inspector General is a bureaucrat," Gerald said. "Bureaucracies have hierarchies. You are the hierarchy."

The Bark-Woman leaned forward.

"He has no authority here, Gerald. This is the Court of Structural Logic. We decide if your citations hold water."

Gerald looked at them. He looked at the geometry. The gears. The roots.

Three different elemental affinities.

And none of them were compliant, and he looked at the floor. The glass was cracked. Three main fractures.

He looked at the ceiling. No sprinklers.

He looked at the exits.

There were none.

"You have no egress," Gerald said.

The Gear-Creature hissed. Steam vented from its shoulders.

"We do not need exits. We are the exit."

"That is a misconception," Gerald said.

He pulled the yellow tape measure from his pocket, and he walked to the nearest pillar.

The three judges watched him. They did not stop him. They believed he was powerless. They believed the Administrative Layer was a place of stasis, not action.

Gerald reached the base of the twisted pillar, and he measured the circumference.

He measured the distance to the adjacent pillar.

He calculated the load-bearing capacity in his head.

The pillar was decorative. It supported nothing. It was a facade.

Gerald tapped the pillar with his tape measure.

*Ding.*

Hollow, and he looked up at the Geometry.

**"WHAT ARE YOU DOING?"**

"Inspecting," Gerald said.

He kicked the base of the pillar.

Hard.

The stone cracked.

The Geometry pulsed. **

"STOP."**

Gerald kicked again.

A chunk of stone fell. It shattered on the glass floor.

The cracks on the floor spread.

The Bark-Woman stood up.

"You are destabilizing the chamber."

"The chamber is already unstable," Gerald said. "You have violated the Structural Integrity Act, Section 4, Paragraph 2. Load-bearing elements cannot be ornamental."

He kicked a third time.

The pillar groaned.

The Gear-Creature lunged forward. Metal limbs clanking.

"I will dismantle you."

Gerald did not move, and he held up his hand.

**[CITATION ISSUED: ILLEGAL MODIFICATION.]**

The red stamp hit the Gear-Creature.

It froze.

Its gears seized. Smoke poured from its joints.

The Geometry flared. **

"THIS IS NOT A HEARING. THIS IS DESTRUCTION."**

"This is enforcement," Gerald said.

He turned to the Geometry.

"You are the presiding judge," Gerald said. "You are responsible for the safety of this chamber."

**"I AM THE LAW."**

"You are the landlord," Gerald said. "And you are negligent."

He walked toward the center of the room, and the floor was cracking faster now. The abyss below was rising.

The Bark-Woman screamed. Roots erupted from the floor, trying to anchor the cracking glass.

Gerald watched the roots grow.

He noted their density.

Too thick, and they were creating leverage points.

He pulled a small item from his pocket.

It was a piece of chalk.

He drew a circle on the glass floor, directly beneath the Geometry.

He wrote inside the circle: **UNLICENSED STRUCTURAL ELEMENT.**

The Geometry hovered above the circle.

**"YOU ARE MAKING A MISTAKE."**

"No," Gerald said. "I am making a correction."

He snapped his fingers.

**[CITATION ISSUED: OBSTRUCTION OF EGRESS.]**

The red stamp fell on the Geometry.

The Geometry shrieked.

Its orbiting cubes scattered. The dark iron sphere flickered.

The connection between the judges broke, and they were no longer a unified entity. They were three separate defendants.

And they were all cited.

The Bark-Woman fell to her knees. Her roots withered.

The Gear-Creature sputtered and died, collapsing into a heap of scrap.

The Geometry faded, and the room went dark.

The only light came from the cracks in the floor.

Gerald stood in the darkness.

He was alone.

Except for the sound of the abyss, which was rising.

He looked down.

The glass floor was gone in a ten-foot radius around him.

He was standing on a narrow strip of stone.

Ahead of him, across the gap, was a door, which was wooden.

It had a handle.

Gerald walked to the edge of the pit.

He looked across.

The door was closed, and he could not cross.

He looked back at where the judges had been.

Nothing remained.

He looked at his tape measure.

The tape was broken. The blade was bent.

He folded the tape, and he walked back to the starting point of the chamber.

The Liaison was gone.

The iron door was open.

Gerald walked through it.

He returned to the corridor, and he walked back to the desk.

The Liaison was sitting there.

She was not looking at him.

She was looking at a new form.

"You caused damage," she said. Her voice was flat.

"I corrected a hazard," Gerald said.

"The Inspector General will hear about this."

"Let him," Gerald said. "He can cite me for excessive force. I will appeal. I have evidence."

He pointed to the empty chair where the Gear-Creature had been.

"Is the hearing over?"

"No," the Liaison said. She slid a paper across the desk.

It was a black card.

Gerald picked it up.

**[SUMMONS: HIGH COURT OF STRUCTURAL LOGIC.]**

**[DATE: IMMEDIATE.]**

**[LOCATION: THE CORE MIND.]**

Gerald stared at the card.

"The Core Mind?" he asked.

"That is where the System processes reality," the Liaison said. "If you want to appeal the citation against the Void, you must go there. You must argue your case before the Architects."

"The Architects?"

"The ones who built the System."

Gerald looked at the card.

Then he looked at the pen on the desk, which was fixed. The ink was dry.

He signed the summons.

He stood up.

He walked to the center of the room.

He closed his eyes, and he pulled on his mana.

He did not try to move his body.

He tried to move his *understanding*.

He thought about code.

He thought about the rules, and he thought about the space between the atoms.

*Shift.*

The world dissolved.

He was no longer in the room.

He was in a white space.

Infinite.

And in the center of the white space stood a single figure, which was a child.

It was wearing a hard hat.

The child looked at Gerald.

The child held a blueprint.

The blueprint was blank.

"You are late," the child said.

Gerald checked his watch.

"I am on time," Gerald said.

"Time is not linear here," the child said. "But you are still late."

"Late for what?"

The child pointed to the blueprint.

Then the blueprint changed.

Lines appeared.

Walls.

Doors.

Windows.

But the structure was impossible.

It looped back on itself, which had no outside.

"This is your dungeon," the child said.

Gerald looked at it.

"It is up to code," Gerald said.

"No," the child said. "It is *over* code."

The child erased a line.

A wall disappeared.

In the distance, Gerald heard a scream.

One of his mold spores.

"The System is removing the excess," the child said. "You added too many rules. Now the structure cannot support the weight of your compliance."

Gerald looked at the blueprint, and the floor was tilting.

The ceiling was lowering.

"The fine print," Gerald said.

"Yes," the child said. "If you enforce every rule, you remove all possibility. You create a prison."

Gerald looked at the blueprint, and he saw the exits.

There were none.

Because he had cited them for being unmarked.

He had cited them for being too narrow.

He had cited them for being too far away, and he had cited them until there were no exits left.

He had built a perfect, code-compliant tomb.

The child smiled.

"Case closed," the child said.

The white space turned black.

Gerald felt the floor tilt again.

But this time, there was no ceiling to hit.

There was only the fall.
