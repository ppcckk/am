Chapter 288: Uninspected Code

# Chapter 288

**Chapter 288: The Audit of Ashford**

Martha stood in the doorway, her cardigan still buttoned wrong. She blinked at the sub-basement, or what remained of it.

To her eyes, the concrete floor was solid.

The fluorescent lights hummed with their usual, irritating buzz. But she felt the vibration in her teeth, a low-frequency thrum that made her fillings ache. She had been a library administrator for fifteen years.

She knew the sound of a building settling, which was not settling. This was breathing.

Behind her, the woman in the sharp suit stepped into the light.

Her heels clicked against the linoleum with a precision that felt violent. She did not look at Martha. She looked at the air where Gerald stood, invisible to the layperson but blazing with data to anyone with the right clearance.

The new Deputy Inspector General raised a pen. It was not a pen.

It was a stylus of pure, condensed judgment.

"Subject: Gerald Hutchins," the woman said.

Her voice was smooth, devoid of the friction that usually accompanied human speech.

"Status: Active Core.

Compliance Rating: Suspended pending review. Allegation: Unauthorized modification of municipal infrastructure.

"

Martha rubbed her arms.

"Gerald? Are you... are you here?"

The woman ignored the question. She tapped the stylus against the air.

A holographic window snapped into existence, blue and translucent.

It listed names. Hundreds of them. Gerald Hutchins.

The Goblin King of Sector 4.

The Slime Queen of the Storm Drain. A dozen other cores, each with a red warning icon hovering over their heads.

"Move, Martha," the woman said. It was not a request. It was a directive.

Martha stepped back, trembling, and she had seen Gerald fired. She had seen him weep in the parking lot. But she had never seen him looked at like this.

Like a glitch in reality.

The Deputy Inspector General walked through the wall.

***

Gerald watched the woman approach from the corner of his eye.

His body was not a body anymore, which was a grid of light and intent, anchored to the sub-basement's foundation. The black door that had held him was gone, shattered by the arrival of the IG's deputy.

Now, there was only the open space of his dungeon, and the woman walking through it.

She wore a badge that pulsed with a rhythm Gerald recognized from his own heartbeat. *Inspection Pending.*

He tried to speak. His voice manifested as a ripple in the air, a distortion of sound waves.

"Inspector General's Office, please present credentials."

The woman stopped three feet from his core. She smiled again. It was colder than the first time.

"I am Deputy Inspector General Vane," she said. "I have reviewed your file, Mr.

Hutchins.

You are a liability. Not because you are weak. But because you are inefficient.

You spend forty percent of your mana reserves on aesthetic compliance.

Handrails. Signage.

Paint colors.

It is a waste of resources.

"

Gerald's core flickered. The color of his light shifted from a calm blue to a defensive orange.

"Compliance is not a waste," Gerald said. The words came out distorted, like a radio signal through static.

"It is the foundation of stability.

Without structure, chaos reigns.

Chaos is a violation.

"

Vane laughed. It was a short, sharp sound.

"Chaos is the norm, Gerald.

Dungeons are monsters. Monsters do not care about building codes.

They care about damage output.

You have turned a combat environment into a public park. The System does not reward parks. It rewards survival.

"

She raised her stylus. A beam of white light shot from the tip, striking the nearest wall.

*VIOLATION DETECTED: NON-COMBATIVE OBSTRUCTION.*

The wall groaned. The handrails Gerald had installed dissolved, turning into gray mist. The egress signs fell from the ceiling, shattering on the floor.

Gerald felt the loss. It was physical. A phantom pain in limbs he no longer had. His dungeon's structural integrity dropped. 98%. 95%. 90%.

"Stop," Gerald said. "That is unauthorized removal of safety equipment."

"It is optimization," Vane corrected.

She tapped her stylus again. Another beam.

This time, she targeted the water leak in the corner.

The moisture condensed, then froze, cracking the concrete.

*VIOLATION DETECTED: UNLICENSED HYDROLOGIC FEATURE.*

The leak died. The humidity dropped. The mold spores that had been his first allies screamed as they desiccated into dust.

Gerald's spawn list vanished. The confused rat, who had been hiding behind a support pillar, evaporated into a cloud of particles.

Vane looked at her holographic screen.

"Your population is zero. Your threat level is negligible. I am initiating a Demolition Order."

A countdown appeared in the air. Red numbers. Ten seconds.

Gerald stared at the numbers. He could not run. He could not fight. He was a core in a basement, and the woman above him held the hammer.

But he was not without resources. He was a bureaucrat. And bureaucrats know that every order has a form.

He focused his will on the one thing Vane had ignored. The clipboard she carried. It was not just a prop. It was a link to the System's archive.

*QUERY: FORM 77-DELTA. JURISDICTIONAL OVERLAP.*

The System processed the request. A window opened in Gerald's mind, small and cramped.

*REQUEST DENIED: INSUFFICIENT PROOF OF OVERLAP.*

Gerald pushed harder. He remembered the day he was fired.

The zoning map of Ashford, and the library was public land.

The dungeon was underground. But the IG's office was located in City Hall.

City Hall was also public land.

*QUERY: FORM 12-B. CONFLICT OF INTEREST.*

The System paused. The countdown froze at four seconds.

Vane frowned. She tapped her screen.

"Glitch," she muttered. She tried to resume the demolition.

"Conflict," Gerald said. His voice was stronger now, fueled by the System's hesitation.

"You are auditing a dungeon located on municipal property.

Your office is located on municipal property. You are a government employee. I am a government employee.

This is not an external audit.

This is an internal review.

"

Vane's eyes narrowed. She looked at the hologram. A small yellow icon had appeared next to Gerald's name. *INTERNAL DISPUTE.*

"That is irrelevant," Vane said. "The System supersedes municipal law."

"Does it?" Gerald asked.

He pulled up the file. The original registration protocol. The one that had triggered when he filed the structural deficiency report on his own body. It cited Municipal Code Section 4.2, Paragraph B. *All inspections conducted by city employees are subject to internal peer review.*

The System accepted the citation.

*DEMO ORDER SUSPENDED. PENDING PEER REVIEW.*

The red countdown faded. The white light from Vane's stylus died.

Vane's expression changed. The smile vanished. Her face went rigid. She looked at Gerald's core with genuine surprise.

"You used a technicality," she said.

"I used the code," Gerald replied.

"Section 4.2. Peer review required for internal disputes. You cannot demolish me until you have filed a formal grievance with the Inspector General's internal affairs division.

That requires a forty-eight-hour waiting period.

"

Vane stared at him. For a second, he saw fear. Not of him. Of the paperwork.

"This is not over," she said.

Her voice was low.

"You have bought time. But time is a resource you do not have.

The Goblin King is approaching. He does not care about your forms.

"

She turned and walked away. Her heels clicked. Martha followed, looking back with tears in her eyes.

The black door reformed. The light in the sub-basement dimmed.

Gerald exhaled a stream of mana. His dungeon was empty.

No rats.

No mold. No water. Only the cold, hard concrete.

He checked his status.

*POPULATION: 0* *STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY: 82%* *COMPLIANCE RATING: 47/100*

He had survived the interview. But he had lost his defenses. And Vane was right about one thing. The Goblin King was coming.

Gerald looked at the shattered handrails.

The broken signs. He could rebuild.

He had mana, and he had time. But he needed a way to make the Goblin King care about the rules.

He opened his inventory. He did not have weapons. He did not have spells.

He had a pen. A real pen, left behind in the library office before he died. It was mundane. It was useless against a monster.

Unless he used it to sign a contract, and he thought of the Hearing Mini-Game. The appeal process.

To win an appeal, you had to argue structural logic.

But what if you didn't appeal a citation?

What if you invited a challenge?

*ACTION: POST PUBLIC NOTICE OF COMPLIANCE AUDIT.*

He focused on the entrance. The stairs leading up to the library.

He wrote in the air with his will.

*WARNING: THIS DUNGEON IS UNDER AUDIT. ALL VISITORS MUST SUBMIT TO STRUCTURAL INSPECTION PRIOR TO ENTRY. FAILURE TO COMPLY WITH EGRESS PROTOCOLS WILL RESULT IN IMMEDIATE CITATION.*

It was a bluff. He had no power to stop the Goblin King. But the System enforced the notice. If the King walked in, he would be violating a posted regulation.

Gerald waited. He felt the floor tremble. Heavy footsteps. The smell of sulfur and wet fur.

The stairs creaked.

A goblin head appeared at the top of the landing. It was large. Its ears were torn. Its eyes were glowing green.

It saw the notice. It read it.

Then it laughed. A guttural, ugly sound.

"Paperwork?" the goblin grunted. It stepped onto the first stair.

Gerald held his breath, and the goblin took a second step. Then a third.

It did not stop.

*VIOLATION DETECTED: FAILURE TO PRESENT COMPLIANCE FORM.*

The System acted.

The floor beneath the goblin's left foot tilted. Not much. Just an inch. But it was enough.

The goblin stumbled. It roared, slashing at the air.

*VIOLATION DETECTED: DESTRUCTIVE BEHAVIOR IN PUBLIC ZONE.*

The walls closed in. Not enough to crush. Just enough to confine.

The goblin was pinned between two sliding concrete slabs. It struggled, its claws scraping against the stone.

Gerald watched. It was working. The code was a weapon.

But then, a voice came from the darkness above.

"Leave him," a deep voice rumbled.

A shadow stepped onto the landing, which was not a goblin. It was a troll. Massive.

Gray skin.

Wearing armor made of car tires and rebar.

The troll looked at the pinned goblin. Then it looked at Gerald's core.

"You insult the System," the troll said. "You hide behind rules."

Gerald's light flared.

"I enforce them."

The troll grinned. It pulled a giant hammer from its back. The handle was a steel I-beam.

"Let's see if your rules can hold this," the troll said.

It swung the hammer, and the impact was silent to Gerald, but he felt the shockwave. The concrete slab holding the goblin cracked.

*VIOLATION DETECTED: UNAUTHORIZED USE OF HEAVY CONSTRUCTION EQUIPMENT.*

The System flagged the hammer.

*STATUS: CONTRABAND. SEIZURE IMMINENT.*

The hammer dissolved. The troll blinked, looking at its empty hands.

Gerald felt a surge of hope. The System was on his side. He had cited the weapon.

But the troll did not panic. It reached into its pocket and pulled out a single, small object.

A key.

*ITEM: MASTER KEY TO MUNICIPAL VAULTS.*

Gerald froze.

The troll held up the key. It glowed with a dark, purple light.

"Your dungeon is connected to the city's sewer system," the troll said. "The sewers are under the jurisdiction of the Water Department. Not the Building Department."

Gerald checked his jurisdiction map. It was true. The sewer lines were outside his code authority.

The troll threw the key into the sewer grate at the edge of the room.

*ACCESS GRANTED: SEWER NETWORK.*

The floor beneath Gerald's core sank. The connection to the library was severed. The anchor point was moving.

Gerald's dungeon was no longer static. It was being dragged into the sewers.

He had used the code to stop a weapon. But he had not accounted for the infrastructure.

The troll laughed.

"Run your codes, Inspector. But you can't inspect what you can't reach."

The floor dropped. Gerald fell.

He plunged into darkness, away from the library, away from Martha, away from the light.

And as he fell, he saw another window open. Not from the System. From his own memory.

The old blueprint of Ashford. The secret passages. The ones that were never inspected. The ones that were never cited.

Gerald reached for them, and he had one choice. Break the code, or drown in the violation.
