Chapter 167: Cited at the Core

# Chapter 167

The void smelled of ozone and wet concrete.

Gerald stood in the center of the black nothingness, his clipboard floating at waist height. Across from him, the Successor leaned against a pillar of pure light.

The figure wore no vest.

No tie. No steel-toed boots. Just skin that glowed with the terrifying efficiency of a perfectly optimized spreadsheet.

"You are a violation," the Successor said. Its voice sounded like a printer jamming.

Gerald adjusted his glasses.

They were not real. The System rendered them because Gerald felt incomplete without them.

He reached for his tape measure.

It hung from his belt, a silver sliver of familiar weight.

"I am compliant," Gerald said.

"Your Compliance Rating is fluctuating," the Successor countered. It gestured at the air, where a translucent bar hovered, red and trembling.

"It reads 47.3.

Then 48.1.

Then 31. You are unstable.

You are a bug in the code. I am the patch."

Gerald looked at the rating. It pulsed like a failing heartbeat.

"Stability is a function of maintenance," Gerald said. He clicked his pen.

The sound was loud in the silent void.

"You have no infrastructure. You are raw data. I have load-bearing walls.

I have egress routes.

I have a permit."

"Permits do not exist in the Void."

"Then you are operating illegally." Gerald tapped the clipboard. "I am citing you for Unregistered Existence."

The Successor's smile widened.

It showed too many teeth. "Cite me. The Hearing is not about citations.

It is about logic. Prove you are a Core.

Prove I am not."

"I am a Core," Gerald said. "I spawn entities."

"Three mold spores," the Successor said.

"One rat. A water leak.

You are barely a dungeon. You are a janitorial issue."

"And you?" Gerald asked. "What do you spawn?"

"Power," the Successor said. It raised a hand.

The void rippled.

"I spawn rules. I delete exceptions. I am the Code."

The floor beneath Gerald's feet shifted. It was not concrete anymore. It was a grid of glowing lines, mathematical and sharp.

"Show me," Gerald said.

The Successor snapped its fingers.

A dragon appeared.

It was not a dragon. It was a wireframe model of a dragon, rendered in blinding white light. It roared, a sound like static screeching through blown speakers. It lunged at Gerald.

Gerald did not dodge. He checked his watch.

"Violation," he said. "No fire suppression system installed."

The wireframe dragon froze mid-air. It twitched. The white light flickered to a sickly yellow.

"I am not citing the dragon," Gerald said, looking at the Successor.

"I am citing the environment. The air is too flammable.

The ceiling height is inadequate for a creature of that wingspan.

It violates Section 4, Subsection B of the Zoning Ordinance."

The dragon fell. It did not burn. It simply ceased to exist, deleted by the System's own compliance engine.

The Successor frowned. "You manipulate the environment."

"I maintain it," Gerald said. "That is the job."

The void shuddered. The grid lines turned red.

**WARNING: STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY COMPROMISED.**

"Ah," Gerald said. "You're trying to crash the server."

"I am optimizing the process," the Successor said. "If the dungeon collapses, the Core is deleted. The bug is removed."

Gerald felt the pull. The floor wanted to drop away.

His stomach churned.

This was not a fight.

This was an eviction notice.

He looked at the grid. He looked at the lines. They were perfect.

Symmetrical. Efficient.

And brittle.

"You have no redundancy," Gerald said.

"Redundancy is waste," the Successor said.

"Redundancy is safety," Gerald said.

He reached into his pocket and pulled out a pencil. It was a yellow pencil, standard issue for municipal inspectors. It was dull.

He walked to the nearest grid line. He drew a diagonal line across it.

The Successor stopped moving.

"What are you doing?"

"Reinforcing the corner," Gerald said.

He drew another line. And another. He was sketching a truss structure in the air.

Simple.

Triangular. Ugly.

The System paused. The red lines flickered.

**UNRECOGNIZED GEOMETRY.**

"It's not geometry," Gerald said. "It's bracing. You built a glass house. I'm adding a steel frame."

The Successor lunged. It passed through the truss lines. Gerald was not there.

He was behind the Successor.

"You cannot touch me," Gerald said. "I have filed for immunity pending inspection."

The Successor spun around. "Immunity is not a rule."

"It is," Gerald said. "Under the Inspector General's Clause 9: *'Any Core actively engaged in corrective construction is granted temporary invulnerability to prevent interference with public works.'*"

The Successor's light dimmed. "That clause is obscure."

"It is in the fine print," Gerald said. "I read all the fine print. That is why I have survived."

He held up his clipboard. The paper was blank. But in his mind, it was filled.

He had spent thirty-one years reading the codes.

The fire codes. The electrical codes. The structural codes.

He knew the loopholes because the codes were written by humans, and humans were sloppy. The System was built on those codes. the System was sloppy.

Gerald exploited the sloppiness.

"I am appealing the deletion," Gerald said. "On the grounds that the target entity—the Successor—is itself a violation of the Original Build."

The Successor hesitated. "The Original Build is perfect."

"The Original Build has a single point of failure," Gerald said.

He pointed to the center of the void.

"You. If I remove you, the system stabilizes.

If I remove me, the system remains unstable because I am the only one maintaining the physical laws."

"You are a bug," the Successor insisted.

"I am a feature," Gerald said. "I am the Maintenance Crew."

The void trembled. The grid lines cracked. Not from damage, but from stress. The System was trying to process two contradictory truths: *The Successor is the ideal,* and *The Successor is causing a crash.*

Gerald felt the pressure in his ears. His nose bleeded. A thin trickle of red on his lip.

He ignored it.

He walked to the edge of the void. There was nothing there. Just the drop into the abyss of uncreated space.

He looked down.

In the darkness below, he saw them.

Not the adventurer party. Not the goblin.

He saw the other Cores.

Dozens of them. Hundreds.

Floating in the dark like distant stars.

Some were red. Some were blue. All of them were screaming silently.

They were all failing.

The Successor watched him look.

"Look at them," the Successor said. "They are weak. They are inefficient. They are dying."

"They are neglected," Gerald said.

"I am here to save them," the Successor said. "By resetting them. By deleting the bad code. By building a new world."

"You are not building," Gerald said. "You are bulldozing."

Gerald turned back to the Successor.

"I am issuing a stop-work order," Gerald said.

The Successor laughed. "You have no authority."

"I have the authority of the only Core that knows where the support beams are."

Gerald raised his pencil. He drew a circle around the Successor. A simple, imperfect circle.

Inside the circle, the Successor's light sputtered.

"What is that?" the Successor asked.

"A safety perimeter," Gerald said. "I am cordoning off the hazardous area. You are not allowed to perform any optimizations within this zone until a permit is granted."

**SYSTEM ALERT: PERMIT REQUIRED.** **DENIAL.**

The Successor screamed. It lashed out, a beam of white energy striking the circle. The energy hit the boundary and dissipated.

The circle held.

Gerald's hand shook. The pencil snapped in half.

He did not let go of the stub.

"Your permit is denied," Gerald said. "Because you do not exist. You are a ghost in the machine. And ghosts do not have building permits."

The void went dark.

Not black. Dark. A heavy, suffocating darkness that pressed against Gerald's skin.

He was alone.

The Successor was gone.

The grid lines were gone.

He stood on concrete. Cold, wet, cracked concrete.

The sub-basement.

The library.

The air smelled of mildew and old paper.

Gerald gasped. He fell to his knees. His chest heaved. The bleeding from his nose had stopped, but the taste of copper remained.

He looked around. The dungeon was quiet.

The water leak was still dripping. *Plip. Plip. Plip.*

The rat was gone.

The mold spores were gone.

The dungeon was empty.

**DUNGEON STATUS: CRITICAL.** **COMPLIANCE RATING: 12/100.**

Gerald stared at the notification. It had dropped. The victory over the Successor had not restored his rating. It had exposed the fragility of his own existence.

He had won the argument. He had survived the Hearing.

But he had lost the world.

A sound came from the tunnel entrance. Footsteps. Heavy boots. Multiple sets.

Gerald looked up.

Three adventurers stood at the threshold.

They were not the ones from before. These were different.

They wore black armor.

No insignia. No guild patches.

They carried weapons that glowed with the same white light as the Successor.

The leader stepped forward. He looked at Gerald. He looked at the empty dungeon. He looked at the broken pencil in Gerald's hand.

"You killed the Successor," the leader said.

"I cited it," Gerald corrected. His voice was hoarse.

"You disrupted the Network," the leader said. "The Server is unstable. The Cores are failing."

"I am trying to fix it," Gerald said.

"You are making it worse," the leader said. He raised his sword. The blade was humming. "We are here to purge the corrupted nodes."

Gerald stood up. His legs felt like water.

"I am not corrupted," Gerald said.

"You are the source of the instability," the leader said. "The System has flagged you as a Hazardous Entity."

The leader pointed the sword at Gerald's chest.

"By order of the Inspector General," the leader said. "You are under arrest. Surrender your Core, or be deleted."

Gerald looked at the sword. He looked at the two soldiers behind the leader. They were ready.

He looked at his clipboard. It was blank.

He had no defense. He had no spawn. He had no ventilation.

But he had a rule.

Gerald reached into his vest pocket. He pulled out a small, laminated card.

*Notice of Violation: Failure to Provide Safe Passage for Inspectors.*

"This is a public easement," Gerald said. "You cannot arrest me without a warrant signed by a certified Inspector."

The leader paused.

"The Inspector General is offline," the leader said.

"Then the warrant is invalid," Gerald said. "And this arrest is a trespass."

The leader frowned. "You are stalling."

"I am following procedure," Gerald said.

The leader stepped closer. "Procedure is dead, Hutchins.

The old rules are gone.

The Successor tried to replace them.

You failed. You are alone."

"No," Gerald said. "I am not alone."

He pointed to the shadows in the corners of the room.

The shadows moved.

They were not mold spores. They were not rats.

They were the other Cores. The ones the Successor had tried to delete. The ones that had been neglected. The ones that had been left to rot in the dark.

They had heard Gerald's argument. They had heard his citation.

And they had come to watch.

The leader looked at the shadows. The white light on his sword flickered.

"Who are they?" the leader whispered.

Gerald smiled. It was a small, tired smile.

"They are the code," Gerald said. "And they are not happy with your maintenance schedule."

The leader raised his sword. "Kill them all."

The shadows did not attack.

They spoke.

A thousand voices, whispering at once, filled the sub-basement.

*"Citation."*

*"Violation."*

*"Stop."*

The leader's sword shattered.

The white light died.

The leader looked at his hands. They were turning gray. Like stone. Like concrete.

"What is this?" he screamed.

Gerald watched the leader petrify. The man froze mid-shout, his face twisted in confusion, his body hardening into a statue of municipal compliance.

The other two soldiers ran. They fled up the stairs, their footsteps echoing into the distance.

Gerald stood alone in the dungeon.

The shadows receded. They returned to the cracks in the walls, the leaks in the ceiling, the rot in the floor.

**SYSTEM ALERT: MULTIPLE CORES JOINING NETWORK.** **COMPLIANCE RATING: 0.**

Gerald looked at the notification.

Zero.

He had united them. He had given them a voice.

But the System did not count them as his.

They were independent.

And they were angry.

Gerald sat down on the cold floor. He picked up the broken pencil.

He had won the hearing.

He had lost the war.

A new notification appeared in his vision.

**INSPECTOR GENERAL: AWAKE.** **LOCATION: THE CORE.** **STATUS: ARRIVING.**

Gerald closed his eyes.

He could hear them coming. Heavy footsteps. The sound of authority. The sound of a man who had never measured a door width in his life.

Gerald Hutchins, Inspector of Buildings, straightened his tie.

He prepared to be cited.
