Chapter 283: Form 12-C

# Chapter 283

The cardigan smelled of lavender and old paper. It was a comforting scent, the kind that usually preceded a lecture on proper spine support or a shushing gesture loud enough to rattle teeth.

Margaret Halloway adjusted her glasses. She looked at the tiny blue shard resting in her palm. It pulsed with a faint, rhythmic light. A soft hum emanated from it, vibrating against her skin.

"Mr. Hutchins?" she asked again.

Her voice didn't waver. In thirty years of working at the Ashford Municipal Library, Margaret had seen everything from patrons bringing in live cats to students trying to photocopy their souls. A sentient shard of dungeon core material was merely unusual. Not unprecedented.

She glanced at the open drawer. Form 12-C sat there, blank and waiting.

The pen floated above it, driven by an invisible force.

The nib dipped into the inkwell. Scratches appeared on the paper.

*TEMPORARY RESIDENCY.* *SUBJECT: GERALD HUTCHINS.* *STATUS: UNLICENSED.* *PERMIT ISSUED: PENDING REVIEW.*

Margaret watched the words appear.

Her lips pressed into a thin line. She hated unlicensed entities.

They cluttered the stacks, and they disturbed the quiet.

"Review requires a signature," she said. "And a fee."

The pen stopped. The shard's pulse slowed.

Margaret leaned forward. She pulled a small stamp from her desk. It was brass, heavy, and engraved with the words *OVERDUE*.

"I can waive the fee," she said, "if you can prove you have no history of noise violations."

The shard flashed red.

Margaret nodded.

"I didn't think so.

You dropped that grate on your way in.

That's a Section 402 disturbance.

I'm writing you up.

"

She stamped the form. A loud *thud* echoed through the archives. The sound was final.

The shard vibrated violently. It wasn't angry. It was confused.

Gerald Hutchins, in his current state, did not understand social cues. He understood codes.

He understood egress, and he understood that a stamp was a legal document, and legal documents required process.

Margaret placed the shard back on the desk. She turned to her computer. The screen flickered. Text appeared, not from her keyboard, but from the shard's network connection.

*SYSTEM ALERT: COMPLIANCE RATING DROPPED. REASON: PUBLIC NUISANCE.*

Margaret sighed. She typed a response.

*FORM 12-C ACCEPTED. SUBJECT REMAINS UNDER OBSERVATION. NEXT INSPECTION: IMMEDIATE.*

She looked at the shard.

"Don't move," she said. "I'm calling Facilities."

The lights in the aisle flickered.

A shadow lengthened at the end of the row of books. It wasn't Margaret's shadow.

It was taller.

It wore armor.

A Paladin walked in, and he was seven feet tall. His armor was polished to a mirror shine.

His sword was glowing.

He looked at Margaret.

He looked at the shard.

"Is this the source of the anomaly?" the Paladin asked.

Margaret didn't look up from her screen.

"Who is asking?"

"Sir Kaelen of the Order of the White Flame."

"You're in the restricted section," Margaret said. "You need a permit to be here."

The Paladin blinked. He lowered his sword slightly.

"I am here to purge the corruption."

"Purge?" Margaret raised an eyebrow. "This is a public library. You can't 'purge' anything.

You can only request removal of hazardous materials. And you must submit a Form 88-H.

"

The Paladin frowned, and he looked at Gerald. The shard was shrinking. It was losing mass.

The dust motes in the air were being sucked into it, but not fast enough.

Gerald was fading.

*INTEGRITY: 0.05%.*

He couldn't move. He couldn't speak. He could only observe.

Margaret turned to Gerald.

"You're running out of time, aren't you?"

The shard pulsed weakly.

"Facilities is on the way," she said. "But if you want to survive the night, you need to file an appeal. The System doesn't like silence. It likes paperwork."

Gerald's code flared. He couldn't write. He had no hands. But he could push.

He pushed his consciousness toward the floating pen. He pushed harder. He focused on the ink.

The pen jerked. It moved to the side of the desk. It hovered over a blank sheet of paper.

Margaret watched. She didn't stop it. She knew the rules. If the core filed paperwork, the System recognized it as a legal entity, even if it was currently non-functional.

The pen wrote.

*APPEAL: CITATION 402.* *DEFENSE: EMERGENCY EGRESS.* *ARGUMENT: OBSTRUCTION REMOVED FOR LIFE SAFETY.*

Margaret picked up the paper, and she read it. She snorted.

"Emergency egress?

You fell through a ceiling tile because you were starving. That's not emergency egress.

That's structural failure.

"

But she stamped it. *PENDING.*

The System paused.

*APPEAL RECEIVED. HEARING SCHEDULED: 10 MINUTES.*

Gerald's code stabilized. The fading stopped.

He was safe. For now.

***

Gerald woke up.

Not with a gasp.

Not with a start. He woke up with a headache.

A specific, bureaucratic headache. The kind that came from reading too many footnotes.

He was in a box, which was a small, white box. Inside were pencils. Erasers. A stapler.

He was on Margaret's desk.

The Archives were dark. The lights had been cut. Only the emergency exit signs glowed green.

Gerald scanned his surroundings.

*LOCATION: ASHFORD MUNICIPAL LIBRARY, ARCHIVES.* *TIME: 02:14 AM.* *INTEGRITY: 0.08%.*

He was still tiny. Still weak. But he was registered.

A door creaked open.

A man walked in. He was large.

He wore a maintenance uniform.

His name tag read *DAVE*. He held a flashlight in one hand and a wrench in the other.

Dave looked at Margaret. She was asleep at her desk, head on her arms.

"Hey," Dave whispered. "Did you get that weird thing?"

Margaret didn't stir.

Dave walked to the desk. He shined the flashlight on the box. He saw the blue shard.

He didn't scream. He didn't run. He looked at the shard. He looked at the pen.

"Right," Dave said. "I'm here for the ventilation."

He opened the box. He took out the shard. He held it up to the light.

"System," Dave muttered. "Scan object."

A green beam hit the shard.

*OBJECT: DUNGEON CORE (FRAGMENT).* *STATUS: INACTIVE.* *COMPLIANCE RATING: 12/100.*

Dave frowned. He put the shard back in the box. He closed the lid.

"Can't fix what's broken," Dave said. He walked toward the door.

Gerald's code panicked, and he was going to be thrown out. He was going to be discarded.

*ACTION: ATTEMPT COMMUNICATION.*

Gerald couldn't speak. He couldn't move. He could only emit a low-frequency hum. It was the sound of a fluorescent light dying.

Dave stopped. He turned around.

"What was that?"

Gerald hummed again. Louder.

Dave walked back. He opened the box. He looked at the shard.

"Are you... alive?" Dave asked.

Gerald pulsed. Once. Yes.

Dave scratched his head. He looked at Margaret. She was still asleep. He looked at the shard.

"Look," Dave said.

"I'm a plumber. I'm not a wizard.

But I know what a leak looks like.

And you're leaking.

"

He pointed at the shard. A thin trail of blue light was trailing from it, dripping onto the desk.

"You're losing integrity," Dave said. "If you don't patch it, you'll evaporate before the morning."

Gerald's code flashed red. *CRITICAL FAILURE IMMINENT.*

Dave sighed. He put the shard in his pocket.

"I'm taking you to the basement," Dave said. "Municipal building. It's got better infrastructure. Maybe you can find some energy there."

Gerald felt the world shift. The box disappeared.

He was in a dark pocket.

Fabric pressed against his code.

The warmth of a body.

Dave walked out of the Archives. He walked down the hall. He walked to the elevator.

The elevator descended.

*BASEMENT LEVEL 3.*

The doors opened.

Dave walked out. He walked down a corridor. It was concrete.

It was cold. It smelled of mildew.

He stopped in front of a door. It was marked *MAINTENANCE ACCESS ONLY.*

He opened it, and he stepped into a small room. It was filled with pipes. Valves. Filters.

Dave put the shard on a workbench.

"Alright," he said. "Let's see what we're dealing with."

He picked up a wrench. He turned on a small lamp.

Gerald scanned the room.

*STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY: 98%.* *VENTILATION: EXCELLENT.* *POWER SOURCE: MUNICIPAL GRID.*

This was not a dungeon. This was a utility room. But it was stable. It was safe.

Dave looked at the shard.

"You need power. I can give you power. But you have to pay."

Gerald waited.

"Payment," Dave said.

"You fix the leak in Sector 4. The one that's flooding the sub-basement. I've been trying to patch it for weeks.

The pressure is too high.

The valve is stuck.

"

Gerald's code processed the request.

*TASK: REPAIR VALVE.* *REWARD: ENERGY TRANSFER.*

Dave pointed to a large pipe in the corner. A rusted valve wheel was stuck. Water hissed from the seam.

"I can't turn it," Dave said.

"It's corroded. The threads are fused. If I use a hammer, I'll break the pipe.

If I use a torch, I'll melt the gasket.

"

Gerald looked at the valve, and he scanned it.

*OBJECT: GATE VALVE.* *MATERIAL: CAST IRON.* *CONDITION: SEIZED.* *CAUSE: OXIDATION AND MINERAL BUILDUP.*

Gerald didn't have hands. He didn't have tools.

But he had the System.

*COMMAND: INITIATE MINERAL DISSOLUTION.* *TARGET: VALVE THREADS.* *METHOD: ELECTROCHEMICAL DEPOSITION.*

This was a new mechanic. One Gerald had discovered only yesterday. A loophole in the building code.

*SECTION 9.4: ALLOWANCE FOR AUTOMATED MAINTENANCE VIA SYSTEM INTERVENTION IF NO LICENSED TECHNICIAN IS AVAILABLE WITHIN 30 MINUTES.*

Dave was a licensed technician. He was available. But he couldn't fix it.

Gerald interpreted this as

"unavailable for effective service."

*ACTION: APPROVED.*

A blue spark jumped from Gerald's shard to the valve.

The spark crawled along the pipe. It reached the valve wheel.

The rust cracked. It didn't crumble. It dissolved. It turned into a fine blue powder that floated into the air.

Dave watched. His mouth hung open.

"What the..."

The valve wheel turned. It moved smoothly.

The water stopped hissing. The leak sealed itself as the metal contracted.

Dave stared at the valve. He looked at Gerald.

"You... you fixed it."

Gerald pulsed. *TASK COMPLETE.*

Dave nodded slowly. He walked to a breaker box. He flipped a switch.

A cable ran from the breaker box to the workbench. It ended in a plug.

Dave plugged the cable into the shard.

Energy flooded Gerald's code.

*INTEGRITY: 0.08% -> 15.00%.*

Gerald felt strength return. He felt mass. He felt presence.

He expanded. He wasn't a shard anymore. He was a small, blue cube. Three inches wide.

Dave looked at the cube.

"Okay," Dave said.

"That's enough for tonight.

I'm going home. You stay here.

Don't touch anything.

"

Dave walked to the door. He opened it.

He stopped, and he looked back at Gerald.

"Hey," Dave said. "You know the Inspector General is coming for an audit tomorrow?"

Gerald pulsed. *YES.*

"He's coming for *you*," Dave said. "He traced the citation you filed on the goblin. He's coming for you, and he's bringing a demolition crew."

Dave closed the door.

Gerald sat on the workbench. He was strong. He was stable.

And he had twelve hours before a man with a sledgehammer and a clipboard arrived to tear his dungeon apart.

Gerald looked at the room. He looked at the pipes. He looked at the concrete walls.

He scanned.

*PROJECT: INFRASTRUCTURE UPGRADE.* *OBJECTIVE: MAXIMIZE COMPLIANCE RATING.* *METHOD: CONVERSION OF UTILITY ROOM TO CLASS-4 DUNGEON CORE.*

He didn't have time to build walls. He didn't have time to spawn monsters.

But he had time to file a form, and he focused on the door Dave had just closed.

*OBJECT: DOOR.* *STATUS: UNAUTHORIZED ACCESS POINT.* *CODE VIOLATION: SECTION 102.4: UNLOCKED PERIMETER.*

Gerald pushed his code into the door's lock.

*ACTION: LOCKDOWN INITIATED.*

The deadbolt slid into place. A chain slid into place. A secondary latch engaged.

The room was sealed.

*COMPLIANCE RATING: 12/100 -> 15/100.*

It wasn't much. But it was a start.

Gerald waited for the sun to rise.
