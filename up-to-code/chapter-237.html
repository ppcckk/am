Chapter 237: Illegal Dumping

# Chapter 237

The pen tip snapped.

Gerald stared at the broken nib. A thin line of ink bled into the parchment, ruining the header for Form 19-Omega.

He did not curse.

He did not sigh. He simply set the pen down on the clipboard with a precision that suggested he was placing it on a scale in a chemistry lab.

"Damn it," Pendergast muttered, snatching a backup pen from his pocket.

"Write it down. The Inspector General is already scanning the perimeter.

If you don't file a Self-Assessment in thirty seconds, the audit defaults to maximum severity.

That means total structural condemnation."

Gerald's hands were still shaking. He grabbed the new pen. The gold light pulsing through the library floorboards above them had shifted from a warm amber to a harsh, sterile white. The air tasted of ozone and old paper.

The sealed wall behind them groaned. The Maw was testing the lock.

"Ventilation," Gerald said, his voice flat. "We have zero airflow. The previous chapter's damage to the east corridor blocked the intake vents. The Compliance Rating will plummet."

"Fix it," Pendergast urged. "Improvise."

Gerald's stomach turned. Improvising was a physical illness.

It felt like swallowing a stone.

But the Inspector General was not a neighbor sending a goblin.

The Inspector General was a force of nature that viewed his sub-basement as a non-compliant hazard.

He looked at the rubble pile near the broken support column. He looked at the cracked ceiling where water was leaking. He looked at Whiskers, who was currently chewing on a piece of drywall.

"I need the schematic," Gerald said.

Pendergast froze. "The what?"

"The original blueprints for the library foundation. They're in my office. Upstairs."

"We don't have time to go upstairs!" Pendergast shouted over the rising hum of the audit.

Gerald didn't argue. He closed his eyes. He visualized the layout.

Thirty-one years. He had inspected every inch of this building.

He knew where the joists were.

He knew where the old plumbing ran. He knew that the sub-basement had been converted from a coal storage room in 1974, a fact that had caused him migraines for two decades.

"North wall," Gerald said, opening his eyes.

"Twelve feet from the corner. There's a sealed-off utility shaft behind the plaster.

It connects to the old heating flue.

It's not on the current System map because it was bricked up before the Core Registration."

Pendergast stared at him. "You're betting your life on a hunch."

"It's not a hunch. It's a violation record from 1974. I filed it. I remember the citation number."

Before Pendergast could respond, Gerald moved. He walked toward the north wall.

He didn't run.

He walked with the steady, plodding pace of a man carrying a clipboard.

The gold light intensified, pressing against his skin.

*Warning: Unauthorized structural alteration detected.*

The text floated in his vision. Red. Aggressive.

Gerald reached the wall. He placed his hand against the cold brick. He focused his Core energy not on attack, but on *surveying*. He pushed his consciousness into the masonry, looking for the void he remembered.

There.

A pocket of air. A hollow space.

He didn't try to break the wall. That would take too long, and it would create debris, which was a tripping hazard and a code violation. Instead, he targeted the mortar joints surrounding the hidden shaft.

*System Command: Excavate.*

The spell was weak. It was meant for digging soft earth, not cutting through decades-old brick. But Gerald wasn't digging for treasure. He was digging for air.

The mortar cracked. Small pieces of brick fell away.

Above him, the voice boomed. **"Hutchins, Gerald. Core ID 442. You are currently non-compliant with Section 4, Subsection C: Minimum Air Exchange Rates.

Current rating: 41/100. Penalty imminent."**

A red aura coalesced around the dungeon's spawn list. The water leak, which had been threatening to short out Gerald's electrical mana conduits, expanded. The water turned acidic. The System was punishing the lack of ventilation by making the environment hostile to life.

Whiskers squealed and scurried under a shelf. The mold spores in the corner witherred, turning gray and brittle.

"Move it," Pendergast yelled. He was standing by the sealed wall, holding his pen like a sword, though he knew it was useless against a god. "The Maw is breaking through!"

A chunk of the sealed wall flew off. A black tentacle, slick and writhing, pushed through the gap. It snapped at the air, testing the barrier.

Gerald ignored it. He focused on the hole he was making.

He widened the breach. He used a *Telekinesis* cantrip he'd bought with the last few points from his last victory. He pulled a single brick out. Then another.

The air pressure changed. A gust of stale, dusty air blew out of the hole, swirling into the dungeon chamber.

It wasn't much. But it was movement.

The red aura around the spawns flickered. The penalty meter paused.

*Recalculating...*

Gerald pulled out another brick. Then another. He formed a small vent.

*Compliance Rating: 43/100.*

It was still a failing grade. It wasn't enough.

The Maw's tentacle grew bolder. It slammed against the remaining stones, cracking them. The seal was failing.

"Gerald," Pendergast said, his voice tight. "You need more air. Or you need to cite it."

Gerald paused. His hand hovered over the pile of bricks.

"Use the vent," Pendergast said, pointing at the hole Gerald had just made.

"It's not just for air. It's a connection to the outside. The library above is public space.

Public space has a code."

Gerald looked at the vent. He looked at the ceiling. He looked at Pendergast.

"The library is not a dungeon," Gerald said.

"No. It's not. But the vent connects to it. And under the System's 'Shared Infrastructure' clause, if a dungeon utilizes public infrastructure, it must adhere to public standards."

Gerald's mind clicked. A loophole. A tiny, bureaucratic loophole.

He didn't have time to argue the merits. He had to act.

He pointed at the vent. "System Command: Designate Feature."

The vent glowed blue.

*Feature Added: Ventilation Shaft.* *Classification: Shared Infrastructure Interface.*

*Warning: This feature connects to Non-Core Zone. Liability Waiver Required.*

Gerald ignored the waiver. He pointed at the Maw's tentacle, which was now fully through the seal, writhing in the center of the room.

"System Command: Citation."

Pendergast's eyes went wide. "Who are you citing?"

"The Maw," Gerald said.

"You can't cite a god!"

"I'm citing the violation of the Shared Infrastructure Interface," Gerald said, his voice gaining strength. "The Maw is attempting to breach a public access point. This is a Section 12 violation: Unauthorized Entry into Public Service Channels."

The System processed the command.

*Citation Generated.* *Target: Entity 'The Maw'.* *Violation: Trespass on Public Utility.* *Penalty: Structural Instability.*

The tentacle froze. It didn't stop moving, but it rotted.

The black flesh turned gray and flaky.

The System, bound by its own logic, recognized the violation. The Maw was attacking a public utility. The penalty was applied.

The tentacle slumped. It fell to the floor, dissolving into ash.

The seal held. The gap was clear of intruders.

Gerald exhaled. He looked at the vent.

*Compliance Rating: 48/100.*

It was still failing. But the immediate threat of acid water and withering spawns had stopped.

The gold light in the ceiling intensified. The voice returned.

**"Violation noted. But this does not absolve the Core of its own deficiencies. Hutchins, Gerald. Prepare for Physical Inspection."**

Pendergast lowered his clipboard. "He's coming in. Not scanning. Walking in."

Gerald looked at the sealed wall. He looked at the vent. He looked at his hands. They were covered in brick dust.

"I need to clean this place up," Gerald said.

"You have ten seconds before he's here," Pendergast said.

"I can't clean it in ten seconds."

"Then you better be able to talk your way out of it."

The floorboards above creaked. The sound was heavy. Deliberate.

Gerald stood in the center of his dungeon. The water leak was still dripping, creating a puddle that reflected the golden light. The mold spores were still gray. The single rat, Whiskers, emerged from under the shelf, trembling.

A hole opened in the ceiling.

Not a hole in the wood. A hole in the air.

A man stepped down from the ceiling. He wore a suit made of gray stone. His face was featureless, a smooth mask of marble. In his hand, he carried a clipboard made of obsidian.

He landed softly. No sound.

He looked at Gerald. He looked at the Maw's ash. He looked at the vent.

"Gerald Hutchins," the Inspector General said. The voice sounded like grinding stones.

Gerald straightened his back. He brushed the dust off his jacket. He picked up his clipboard.

"Inspector General," Gerald said. "Welcome to my facility."

The Inspector General took a step forward. The air grew cold.

"You cited a god," the Inspector General said.

"I cited a violation," Gerald corrected.

The Inspector General tilted his head. The marble face remained blank, but the eyes glowed with a faint, blue light.

"Your Compliance Rating is 48," the Inspector General said. "That is a failing grade. Why have you not been condemned?"

"I am currently undergoing remediation," Gerald said.

The Inspector General looked at the vent. He looked at the hole in the wall.

"Remediation requires resources," the Inspector General said. "You have none.

You have no gold.

You have no mana crystals.

You have three mold spores and a rat."

"I have a plan," Gerald said.

The Inspector General took another step. He was now five feet away.

"Show me," the Inspector General said.

Gerald looked at his dungeon. It was a disaster. It was a violation. It was a death trap.

But it was *his* violation.

Gerald looked at Pendergast. Pendergast looked back, his face pale.

Gerald looked at the Inspector General.

"I need a permit," Gerald said.

The Inspector General stopped.

"A permit," the Inspector General repeated.

"For expansion," Gerald said. "I am applying to expand my dungeon footprint into the adjacent parking lot."

Pendergast choked. "You're trying to dig up the library parking lot?"

"It's the only place with sufficient ventilation and no existing structural load," Gerald said, speaking fast. "If I can expand, I can improve airflow. I can fix the rating. I can meet compliance."

The Inspector General stared at him. The silence stretched.

Then, the Inspector General raised his obsidian clipboard.

**"Application Denied."**

Gerald felt a sharp pain in his chest. A debuff.

"Reason?" Gerald asked, his voice steady.

**"Zoning laws prohibit subterranean expansion in this district. The library is a heritage site."**

Gerald's mind raced. Heritage site.

He looked at the walls. He looked at the old brick. He looked at the 1974 citation number he remembered.

"Then I apply for a variance," Gerald said.

The Inspector General paused.

**"Variances are for emergencies. You do not qualify."**

"I do," Gerald said. He pointed to the sealed wall. "That entity outside the wall?

It's not just a monster. It's a Class A Bio-Hazard.

It violates the Environmental Protection Act of the Dungeon System.

If I don't contain it, it spreads. If I don't expand to build a proper containment facility, the city is at risk."

He was bluffing. Partially.

The Inspector General looked at the ash of the tentacle. He looked at Gerald.

He tapped his clipboard.

**"Provisional Variance Granted. Duration: 24 hours. If you fail to secure the perimeter by then, your Core is destroyed."**

Gerald nodded. He didn't smile. He didn't breathe a sigh of relief. He simply wrote it down.

"Thank you, Inspector," Gerald said.

The Inspector General turned. He floated back toward the hole in the ceiling.

"Prepare for re-inspection in 24 hours," the voice said.

The gold light faded. The cold air dissipated.

Gerald stood alone in the center of his broken dungeon.

Pendergast walked over. He looked at the vent. He looked at the ash.

"You just bought us twenty-four hours," Pendergast said. "To do what?"

"To build a wall," Gerald said. "A real one. Not a magical seal. A wall that meets code."

Pendergast looked at the rubble pile. "We don't have the materials. We don't have the crew."

"We have the parking lot," Gerald said. He walked toward the vent. He leaned in, looking up into the darkness of the utility shaft.

He could hear cars driving above. He could hear the wind.

He looked back at Pendergast.

"Get the truck," Gerald said. "We're going shopping."

Pendergast blinked. "Shopping?"

"Lumber," Gerald said.

"Concrete. Rebar.

We're building a compliant egress route through the parking lot.

And when the Inspector General comes back, he won't find a dungeon."

He paused.

"He'll find a construction site. And you can't audit a construction site that hasn't been handed over yet."

Pendergast stared at him. Then he grinned. It was a sharp, dangerous grin.

"You're going to bury the parking lot," Pendergast said.

"I'm going to fill it with concrete," Gerald said. "And then I'm going to cite the Maw for illegal dumping."
