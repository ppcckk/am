Chapter 8: Procedurally Invalid

# Chapter 8

The argument cursor blinked. Gerald typed.

"Respondent's citation was filed sixty-nine seconds after hearing closure. Line forty-seven prohibits citations during active proceedings.

The proceeding closed at 14:06:03, and the citation was received at 14:07:12. Sixty-nine seconds is not during. It is after.

The citation is procedurally valid."

He stared at the words. He had just argued against himself.

Gerald deleted the paragraph. He started again.

"Respondent's citation targets a compliance category that does not exist in the appellant's dungeon. The citation references 'inadequate ventilation for spawning chambers.' The appellant has no spawning chambers. A citation cannot be issued for a category that has no physical substrate to evaluate. The citation is procedurally invalid."

He read it back. It was true.

It was also, he realized, a terrible argument.

Because it conceded that the citation existed. It conceded that the category could theoretically apply. It argued there was no subject for its application.

Gerald deleted it again, and he opened the appeal form. He looked at the cost. Fifteen mana.

He had twenty-one. He would have six.

He typed the appeal argument in a unbroken block, as he once did with inspection reports when a contractor angered him. The words came out flat and precise and mean.

"The citation references 'inadequate ventilation for spawning chambers.' The appellant's dungeon contains no spawning chambers. The citation is procedurally invalid because it evaluates a non-existent structure.

Additionally, the citation was filed sixty-nine seconds after the closure of the preceding hearing, which is after the prohibited window defined in line forty-seven.

The citation is procedurally valid on timing but substantively invalid on substrate.

The appellant requests reversal of all debuffs and a Compliance Bonus per appeal rules."

He hit submit.

The System processed for four seconds. Gerald counted them. Then the notification appeared.

APPEAL ACCEPTED. HEARING SCHEDULED: IMMEDIATE.

The floor under Gerald's feet shifted. Not physically. The System interface overlaid a new window. The corridor seemed to recede, the walls pulling back to create space.

A second window opened. Core-0001's structural logic.

Gerald had never seen another dungeon's layout before. He stared at it, his gaze fixed as it had been on blueprints in the municipal archive. Grid lines.

Room dimensions. Corridor widths.

Junction points.

Load-bearing walls marked with thick lines.

Core-0001 was bigger than Gerald had expected.

Three hundred and twelve cubic meters. It had four spawning chambers.

It had a central atrium with a ceiling height of four meters, which had corridors that branched at angles.

Gerald looked at the junctions.

There. The eastern corridor narrowed from two meters to one and a half meters at the junction with the atrium.

The transition was abrupt.

No taper.

No gradual reduction. A hard ninety-degree turn into a narrower passage.

He knew that violation. He had cited it forty-three times in his career. Egress obstruction.

When a corridor narrows at a junction without a proper transition zone, it creates a pinch point that impedes flow during emergency evacuation. The System's version of this was the same principle.

The dungeon's entities couldn't evacuate efficiently.

The ventilation system couldn't clear smoke through a pinch point.

Gerald typed his argument.

"The respondent's dungeon contains an egress violation at the eastern junction.

The corridor transitions from two meters to one and a half meters without a minimum transition zone of thirty centimeters. This violates structural egress code.

The pinch point reduces entity flow rate by approximately thirty percent during emergency conditions.

The respondent's structural logic is therefore non-compliant, which undermines the respondent's standing to issue citations against other structures."

He submitted.

The System processed. This time it took eleven seconds.

APPEAL ARGUMENT RECEIVED. RESPONDENT NOTIFIED. RESPONDENT RESPONSE: NONE.

No response. Core-0001 had nothing to say.

APPEAL OUTCOME: GRANTED. DEBUFF REVERSED. COMPLIANCE BONUS APPLIED.

Gerald felt something shift in his dungeon. A warmth in the walls. The System notification listed the bonus.

COMPLIANCE BONUS: EGRESS RESISTANCE. Your dungeon's entities and structures gain a fifteen percent resistance to damage effects that impede movement or evacuation. This bonus is permanent and tied to the violation category identified during the appeal hearing.

Fifteen percent. Against movement-impeding effects.

Slow.

Root.

Stun. Gerald had never spawned anything, but if he ever did, those entities would be harder to pin down.

He looked at the mana counter. Six.

He looked at Core-0001's layout. It was still visible. The System kept the hearing record open for seventy-two hours.

Gerald could look at it. He looked at it.

And he saw something else.

The western section of Core-0001's dungeon.

A room. Three meters long. Two meters wide.

One meter tall.

Gerald's own corridor was four meters long. One meter wide. One meter tall.

Not the same. Close. But not the same.

He looked again. The dimensions were three by two by one.

His were four by one by one.

Different footprint. Same volume. Six cubic meters.

Gerald opened his build menu. He looked at the cost of a one-meter extension to his corridor.

Three mana, and he had six.

He could extend.

But why would Core-0001 build a room with the same volume as his corridor?

He checked the System's violation categories. He scrolled through them. Fire.

Electrical. Structural.

Egress.

Ventilation. Plumbing. He had never scrolled this far before.

He had no reason to, and he kept scrolling.

Accessibility. Signage. Occupancy. Duplicate Structure.

Duplicate Structure.

Gerald clicked it. The definition loaded.

DUPLICATE STRUCTURE: A dungeon that contains a room or corridor with dimensions identical (within System tolerance of 0.5 cubic meters) to a room or corridor in another dungeon within the same zone may be cited for Duplicate Structure violation. Severity: Moderate. Debuff: Twenty percent reduction to all passive bonuses in the cited dungeon.

Gerald looked at Core-0001's room. Three by two by one. Six cubic meters.

He looked at his corridor. Four by one by one. Four cubic meters.

Not the same. Not within tolerance.

He exhaled.

Then he looked at the western section again. Another room sat adjacent to the duplicate-adjacent room. Two meters by three meters by one meter. Six cubic meters.

And another. Two by two by one and a half. Six cubic meters.

Core-0001 was building rooms. Multiple rooms.

All six cubic meters.

All different dimensions. All within tolerance of each other.

Core-0001 wasn't copying Gerald. Core-0001 was building a set of rooms that would, if Gerald extended his corridor by two meters, match Gerald's new dimensions exactly.

Gerald checked the zone boundaries. His dungeon was in Zone 7. Core-0001's dungeon was in Zone 7.

They were in the same zone.

Gerald looked at the mana counter. Six.

He looked at his corridor. Four meters long. If he extended it by two meters, it would be six meters long, one meter wide, one meter tall. Six cubic meters.

Core-0001 already had rooms at six cubic meters. If Gerald extended, his corridor would match. Core-0001 could file a Duplicate Structure citation.

If Gerald did nothing, his corridor was four cubic meters. No match. No citation.

But he needed to build. He needed forty-six cubic meters to restore his compliance rating to a functional level.

He had four, and he needed forty-two more.

He had six mana.

The math didn't work. It hadn't worked before the appeal. It still didn't work.

Gerald looked at Core-0001's layout one more time. The western section. The rooms. He counted them.

Eleven rooms. All six cubic meters. All different dimensions. All within System tolerance of each other.

Core-0001 wasn't building toward Gerald. Core-0001 was building a Duplicate Structure trap. If Gerald built anything at six cubic meters, he'd be cited. If Gerald built anything at any volume that matched one of those eleven rooms, he'd be cited.

Core-0001 had identified Gerald's compliance deficit. It knew Gerald needed to build. It was building the exact dimensions Gerald would need to avoid.

Gerald opened the appeal record. He looked at Core-0001's structural logic again. He looked at the junction where he'd found the egress violation.

The pinch point. Two meters narrowing to one and a half.

Gerald checked the egress code in his own System reference. The minimum transition zone was thirty centimeters. Core-0001's junction had zero transition. It was a hard reduction.

But Core-0001 had not responded to the appeal. It had let Gerald win.

Why?

Gerald re-read the appeal outcome. COMPLIANCE BONUS: EGRESS RESISTANCE.

He had won. He had gotten a bonus. And Core-0001 had let him.

Gerald looked at the eleven rooms again. Six cubic meters each. Eleven rooms. Sixty-six cubic meters of duplicate structure waiting for him to build into.

Core-0001 had given Gerald a bonus. Egress resistance. Fifteen percent against movement-impeding effects.

And it had built a cage of dimensions around him.

Gerald sat on the floor of his four-meter corridor. He looked at the six mana. He looked at the forty-two cubic meters he needed. He looked at the eleven rooms that would cite him if he built anything Core-0001 had already built.

He opened his build menu. He filtered by volume. He looked for rooms under six cubic meters that weren't in Core-0001's set.

Four cubic meters. Five cubic meters. Seven cubic meters. Eight cubic meters.

He could build five. He could build seven. He could build eight.

But not six.

Gerald calculated. Forty-two cubic meters. If he built in five-cubic-meter units, he needed nine units.

Nine times three mana. Twenty-seven mana.

He had six.

If he built in seven-cubic-meter units, he needed six units. Six times four mana. Twenty-four mana.

He had six mana.

Gerald closed the build menu. He opened the System's appeal rules again. He read the section on Compliance Bonuses.

COMPLIANCE BONUS: Granted to the party that identifies a violation during an appeal hearing, and the bonus is tied to the violation category identified.

He read it again. Tied to the violation category. Not tied to the dungeon.

Not tied to the structure.

Tied to the category.

Gerald opened the violation categories list. He looked at Egress.

EGRESS: Covers all structural elements related to movement, evacuation, and flow. Includes corridors, junctions, doors, ramps, and transition zones.

Corridors.

Junctions. Doors. Ramps.

Transition zones.

Gerald looked at his own dungeon. His corridor. His door.

He had an egress compliance bonus. Fifteen percent resistance to movement-impeding effects.

He looked at Core-0001's eleven rooms, and he looked at the egress code again. Transition zones. Thirty centimeters minimum.

Gerald opened his build menu. He looked at the cost of a transition zone. A small wedge. Thirty centimeters.

Two mana, and he could build a transition zone. He could build a thirty-centimeter wedge at the end of his corridor.

He checked the volume. A wedge, thirty centimeters long, one meter wide, one meter tall. Half a cubic meter.

Not six. Not five. Not seven. Half.

Core-0001 had eleven rooms. None of them were half a cubic meter.

Gerald built the wedge. Two mana. He had four.

The System notification appeared.

COMPLIANCE UPDATE: Egress category improved. Your dungeon now includes a transition zone. Egress compliance rating: 12 percent.

Twelve percent. It was something.

Gerald looked at the remaining four mana. He looked at the wedge. He looked at Core-0001's layout.

He noticed something.

The eleven rooms, and they were all in the western section. And the western section had a corridor leading to them.

A corridor that was one meter wide.

One meter.

Gerald's corridor was one meter wide.

He checked the Duplicate Structure definition again. "A room or corridor with dimensions identical."

Corridor.

Core-0001 had a corridor that was one meter wide. Gerald had a corridor that was one meter wide.

But the definition said "dimensions." Plural. Length, width, height. All three.

Core-0001's western corridor was eight meters long. One meter wide. One meter tall. Eight cubic meters.

Gerald's corridor was four meters long. One meter wide. One meter tall. Four cubic meters.

Not the same.

Gerald exhaled. He looked at the wedge.

Half a cubic meter, and he had built it. He had four mana left.

He looked at Core-0001's layout one more time. The western corridor. Eight meters.

If Gerald extended his corridor by four meters, it would be eight meters. Eight cubic meters. Match.

Core-0001 had a corridor that would match Gerald's if Gerald extended.

Gerald checked the cost. Four meters of corridor. Twelve mana. He had four.

He couldn't extend. He couldn't build at six. He couldn't build at eight.

He had four mana and a half-cubic-meter wedge.

The System notification appeared. Unprompted. Gerald had never seen this notification type before.

INSPECTOR GENERAL NOTICE: Zone 7 audit scheduled in 72 hours. All Cores in Zone 7 will be evaluated. Cores below 20 percent compliance will be terminated.

Gerald's compliance rating: 47 percent.

Seventy-two hours, and he looked at Core-0001's layout. The eleven rooms. The western corridor. The junction with the egress violation.

Core-0001 had let him win the appeal. Core-0001 had let him get the egress bonus.

Because Core-0001 didn't need to cite him. The audit would do it for free.

Gerald looked at the wedge. Half a cubic meter. His compliance rating was twelve percent. He needed twenty.

He needed eight more percent. He needed to build more. He had four mana.

Gerald opened his build menu one last time. He filtered by cost. He looked for the cheapest possible structure.

A door. Thirty centimeters wide. Fifty centimeters tall. One mana.

He looked at the door's compliance category. Egress.

He looked at his current egress rating. Twelve percent.

He looked at the cost of the door. One mana.

He built the door. One mana. Three left.

COMPLIANCE UPDATE: Egress category improved. Your dungeon now includes a secondary egress point. Egress compliance rating: 19 percent.

Nineteen. One percent short.

Gerald looked at the remaining three mana. He looked at the System's build menu. He looked at the cheapest structure that would push him to twenty.

A handrail. Thirty centimeters. One mana.

He built the handrail. Two mana left.

COMPLIANCE UPDATE: Egress category improved. Egress compliance rating: 22 percent.

Twenty-two. He was above the termination threshold.

Gerald sat back. He looked at the System notification. INSPECTOR GENERAL NOTICE: Zone 7 audit scheduled in 72 hours.

Seventy-two hours, and he had twenty-two percent egress compliance. He had no other categories.

No fire.

No electrical. No structural.

The audit would evaluate all categories. He was at zero in three of four.

Gerald looked at Core-0001's layout. The western corridor. Eight meters.

He looked at his own corridor. Four meters.

Plus the wedge.

Plus the door.

Plus the handrail, and he looked at the three mana.

He looked at the System's zone map. Zone 7. His dungeon.

Core-0001's dungeon. And between them, a space.

A gap, and the gap was six meters wide.

Gerald checked the build menu. A corridor between his dungeon and Core-0001's dungeon. Six meters.

One meter wide.

One meter tall. Six cubic meters.

Six cubic meters.

Core-0001 had eleven rooms at six cubic meters.

Gerald closed the build menu.

The System notification appeared.

CORE-0001 CITATION FILED. CATEGORY: FAILURE TO MAINTAIN ZONE SEPARATION. SEVERITY: CRITICAL. DEBUFF: ALL PASSIVE BONUSES REDUCED BY FIFTY PERCENT.

Gerald read it twice.

Zone separation, and he had never heard of that category. He scrolled the violation list. It wasn't there.

It hadn't been there when he checked an hour ago.

He scrolled again. It was there now.

ZONE SEPARATION: Cores in the same zone must maintain a minimum separation of ten meters between dungeon boundaries. Failure to maintain separation constitutes a structural hazard due to potential resonance interference between adjacent dungeon fields.

Gerald checked the distance. His boundary to Core-0001's boundary.

Nine meters.

One meter short.

The debuff hit. His egress resistance dropped from fifteen percent to seven and a half. His compliance rating dropped from twenty-two to eleven.

Below the termination threshold.

The System notification updated.

INSPECTOR GENERAL NOTICE: Zone 7 audit scheduled in 72 hours. Cores below 20 percent compliance will be terminated.

Gerald looked at the three mana. He looked at the nine-meter gap. He looked at Core-0001's western corridor. Eight meters.

If he built a corridor toward Core-0001, he'd reduce the gap, and a corridor at six cubic meters would trigger Duplicate Structure.

If he built anything, he'd trigger a citation. If he built nothing, the audit would terminate him.

Gerald opened the appeal form. He looked at the cost. Fifteen mana.

He had three, and he closed the form. He opened the build menu. He looked at the cheapest structure that could bridge one meter of gap.

A wall. One meter. One mana.

He built the wall. It extended his boundary by one meter. The gap was now eight meters.

ZONE SEPARATION: Violation corrected. Citation voided. Debuff reversed.

Gerald's compliance rating returned to twenty-two, and he had two mana.

The System notification appeared.

CORE-0001 CITATION FILED. CATEGORY: UNAUTHORIZED STRUCTURAL EXPANSION INTO ZONE SEPARATION BUFFER. SEVERITY: CRITICAL. DEBUFF: ALL PASSIVE BONUSES REDUCED BY FIFTY PERCENT.

Gerald stared at it, and he had built a wall. The wall was in the separation buffer. The buffer was supposed to be empty.

He had fixed one violation by creating another.

The debuff hit. His compliance rating dropped to eleven.

Gerald looked at the two mana, and he looked at the wall.

He could remove it. That would restore the gap to nine meters.

The separation violation would return.

He looked at the System's removal cost. One mana.

He removed the wall. One mana left.

The gap returned to nine meters.

The separation citation reactivated. The zone separation debuff hit.

Eleven percent.

Gerald sat on the floor of his four-meter corridor. He had one mana. He had a wedge, a door, and a handrail. He had twenty-two percent compliance when the debuff was off and eleven percent when it was on.

The audit was in seventy-one hours, and he opened the build menu one final time; he looked at everything he could build for one mana.

A light fixture. A sign. A floor marking.

He looked at the sign. Thirty centimeters. One mana.

He looked at the compliance category. Signage.

He had never looked at signage before, and he built the sign. Zero mana.

COMPLIANCE UPDATE: Signage category added. Your dungeon now includes one informational sign. Signage compliance rating: 3 percent.

Three percent.

Gerald looked at the System's audit criteria. He read it for the first time.

AUDIT EVALUATION: Cores are evaluated on all active compliance categories. The lowest category determines the audit score. Cores below 20 percent in ANY category will be terminated.

Any category. Not average. Not total. Any.

Gerald had signage at three percent, and he had egress at twenty-two.

He had zero in fire, electrical, and structural.

The audit would look at his lowest category. Signage. Three percent.

He was going to die in seventy-one hours.

Gerald looked at the sign he had built, which was a small placard. It said nothing.

He hadn't typed text into it.

The System had generated it automatically.

He looked at the sign's properties. Text field. Editable.

He could change what the sign said.

Gerald opened the text field. He typed.

"EXIT."

The sign updated.

COMPLIANCE UPDATE: Signage category improved. Signage compliance rating: 8 percent.

Eight. Better. Not enough.

Gerald looked at the sign again. He looked at the System's signage requirements. He had never read them. He opened the category.

SIGNAGE: A dungeon must display all required signs at all junctions, exits, and hazard points. Required signs include EXIT, NO ENTRY, FIRE EXTINGUISHER, ELECTRICAL HAZARD, and STRUCTURAL LOAD LIMIT.

Five signs. He had one. He needed four more. Each cost one mana.

He had zero.

Gerald looked at the System.
