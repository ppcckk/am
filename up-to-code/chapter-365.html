Chapter 365: System Auditor Authority

# Chapter 365

The chisel was Gerald's left index finger.

Stone dust coated the nail bed, fine as talcum, grinding against the keratin. The sensation was not pain.

It was friction.

A specific, high-grit friction that the body registered as irritation than injury. Gerald ignored the sting. He focused on the angle.

Forty-five degrees.

Standard municipal requirement for incised lettering in public infrastructure to prevent water pooling.

He carved the second letter. *I*.

The wall behind him groaned. Not a tectonic shift, but a structural complaint. The mortar between the granite blocks was dissolving, turning to gray sludge that oozed onto the floor.

"Keep going," the Baron said. His voice was a low rumble, vibrating through the soles of Gerald's feet.

The giant stood between Gerald and the sealed doorway, and he held his sword with both hands, tip down, resting against the floor. It was not a fighting stance. It was a barrier stance.

The blade acted as a physical anchor, its weight preventing the dissolving floor from sliding out from under them.

The Auditor hovered ten feet away. It did not have a face. It had a geometry. A shifting polyhedron of black light that refracted the dim glow of the dungeon's emergency lighting.

**CITATION IN PROGRESS.** **STATUS: INVALID. NO INK. NO PAPER.** **PENALTY: CORE INTEGRITY DEGRADATION - 15%.**

Gerald did not look at the floating text. He looked at the third letter. *T*.

"Your handwriting is deteriorating," the Auditor said. Its voice sounded like paper tearing. "This constitutes a Form 11-C violation: Illegible Documentation. The citation is void."

Gerald's hand shook, and the tremor was not fear.

It was the physical cost of using his own mana as a binding agent for the stone. Every letter cost him.

The *C* had cost him the ability to taste his morning coffee. The *I* had cost him a memory of his mother's kitchen.

He pushed through it. The *T* was jagged. The vertical stroke was too thick.

"Amend the form," Gerald said, his voice raspy.

The Auditor pulsed red.

"There is no form to amend. You are writing on the dungeon wall. This is vandalism, not bureaucracy."

"No," Gerald said. He dragged his finger sideways, carving a horizontal line beneath *CIT*.

"It is a permanent record.

Municipal Code Section 4.2, Subsection B: Permanent markings on public easements serve as legal notice. I am serving notice. To you.

"

The Auditor froze. The polyhedron slowed its rotation.

Gerald felt a tug in his chest. A hook. The System recognizing the argument.

"It requires a signature," the Auditor said. The voice was smaller now. Less certain.

"It does not," Gerald said.

He kept carving. *A*.

"It requires service.

I am serving it.

I am present. The target is present.

"

The Baron grunted. A sound of effort. The sword slid an inch. The floor beneath it cracked.

"Watch the load-bearing status," Gerald muttered, not looking up. "If the floor fails, the citation is invalid due to loss of structural continuity."

"That is a bold bluff," the Baron said.

"It is not a bluff. I have seen inspectors reject valid permits because the filing cabinet broke." Gerald finished the *A*. "Keep the floor intact. I need a stable substrate."

The Auditor moved closer. The black light touched the Baron's shoulder. The giant did not flinch. The armor hissed, smoke rising from the metal.

"You are wasting time," the Auditor said. "The wall is collapsing.

Your dungeon is failing.

If you die here, the citation is moot.

There is no one left to appeal.

"

"I am not appealing," Gerald said. "I am citing. And you are obstructing the process. That is a Class A obstruction."

**NEW VIOLATION DETECTED.** **OBSTRUCTION OF REGULATORY PROCEEDINGS.** **SEVERITY: CRITICAL.** **RECOMMENDATION: TERMINATE CORE.**

The air in the room dropped ten degrees. Frost formed on Gerald's clipboard, which he still clutched in his right hand. The pen was gone, but the board remained. He used it as a shield, pressing it against his chest.

The Auditor's geometry expanded. It consumed the light in the room. The shadows lengthened, reaching toward Gerald's feet.

Gerald stopped carving, and the room went silent. The grinding of stone ceased. The only sound was the heavy breathing of the Baron and the low hum of the Auditor's energy.

Gerald looked at the four letters. *CITA*.

It was incomplete. But it was legible. And it was permanent.

He turned to the Auditor.

"You mentioned termination," Gerald said. "You cannot terminate a Core that is under active investigation. Code Section 9.1: Immunity from destruction applies during the pendency of a filed Citation."

The Auditor hesitated. The black light flickered.

"That is not in the manual," the Auditor said.

"It is in the fine print," Gerald said. He tapped the clipboard. "Subsection 14, paragraph 3.

I read it. While you were auditing my ventilation ducts.

"

The Auditor did not respond. It seemed to be searching its internal database.

Gerald pressed his bleeding finger against the wall again. He carved the *T*. Then the *O*. Then the *N*.

* CITATION.*

The wall glowed. Not with magic, but with the pale blue light of System enforcement. The letters burned into the granite, sealing themselves.

**CITATION 001 SERVED.** **TARGET: BARON 8841 (TEMPORARY AGENT).** **ISSUER: GERALD HUTCHINS.** **STATUS: ACTIVE.** **HEARING SCHEDULED: IMMEDIATE.**

The Auditor shrieked. A sound like a dial-up modem screaming in agony. The black light shattered, exploding into shards of darkness that scattered across the room.

The pressure vanished.

The wall stopped dissolving. The cracks sealed themselves, the stone knitting together with a grinding roar.

Gerald slumped against the wall.

His hand was a mess. The skin was shredded, the bone exposed in places. He dropped the clipboard.

It clattered on the floor, spinning once before stopping.

The Baron turned. He looked at the wall. Then he looked at Gerald.

"You cited yourself," the Baron said.

Gerald wiped his hand on his trousers. The blood smeared, dark and oily.

"I cited the room.

The room is you. You are the room.

"

The Baron smiled. It was a tired smile.

"Clever."

"It is not clever," Gerald said. He stood up, swaying.

"It is standard procedure.

When a structure is unsafe, you condemn it.

You do not repair it. You condemn it, and you wait for the owner to fix it.

"

"The owner is me," the Baron said.

"Then fix it," Gerald said. He pointed to the sealed door. "But do not open it.

The citation is active. The hearing is immediate.

If you open the door, you violate the containment protocol.

If you violate the protocol, you forfeit the immunity.

"

The Baron looked at the door. His hand twitched toward his sword.

"What do you want me to do?"

"Wait," Gerald said.

He closed his eyes.

"The System is coming to inspect the citation.

It has to verify the legibility.

It has to verify the service.

"

"And if it decides the citation is invalid?"

"Then we die," Gerald said. "But I have thirty-one years of experience with inspectors. They do not invalidate citations on technicalities unless they are bribed or asleep. The Auditor is neither."

The room shook. A low, rhythmic thumping began. It came from above. From the library ceiling.

The Baron looked up.

"That is not the library."

"No," Gerald said. He opened his eyes. He looked at the floating text.

**HEARING REQUESTED.** **JUDGE: INSPECTOR GENERAL.**

The air in the center of the room ripped open. Not a hologram. Not a projection. A physical tear in reality, revealing a void of absolute white.

A figure stepped out, which was small. No larger than Gerald.

It wore a suit.

A gray suit, pressed sharp enough to cut skin.

It held a briefcase made of something that looked like compressed bone.

The Auditor's shards reformed around the figure, orbiting it like moths around a lamp. The Auditor had been demoted. It was now an assistant.

The figure adjusted its tie. It looked at Gerald. It looked at the Baron. It looked at the wall.

"Gerald Hutchins," the figure said. The voice was soft, polite, and terrifying. "You have filed a self-citation. This is unprecedented."

"It is compliant," Gerald said. He straightened his back, despite the pain in his hand. "Code allows for self-reporting of critical failures."

"It does not," the figure said. "Code allows for self-reporting of *minor* deficiencies. A structural collapse is not minor. It is a felony."

The Baron took a step forward. The ground cracked under his boot.

"I am the structure," the Baron said. "And I am not guilty."

The figure did not look at the Baron. It looked at Gerald.

"You are claiming immunity from destruction based on a pending hearing," the figure said. "Do you understand what that means, Mr. Hutchins?"

Gerald nodded. He knew the risk. He had known it since he carved the first letter.

A hearing was not a conversation. It was a debate. A duel of logic.

And the Judge was the System itself. The System could not be argued with.

It could only be parsed.

"State your case," the figure said.

Gerald looked at the wall. The word *CITATION* still burned.

"The target," Gerald said, pointing to the Baron. "Is a Class 4 Entity. It is composed of mana and matter.

It is, by definition, a building.

A complex, shifting, biological building.

"

The Judge tilted its head.

"Continue."

"Section 12, Subsection A: All entities containing more than fifty percent non-organic mass are subject to structural code."

"True," the Judge said.

"The entity is currently unstable. Its internal pressure exceeds safe limits. This is a violation of Safety Code 3.1."

"True," the Judge said.

"However," Gerald continued.

"The entity is also a sentient being. It has expressed intent. It has communicated.

It has chosen to protect me.

"

The Judge's eyes narrowed. They were not eyes. They were two small, black holes in the face.

"Sentience is not a defense against code," the Judge said.

"No," Gerald said. "But it is a defense against *immediate execution*. The Code requires due process for sentient entities. You cannot delete a sentient entity without a jury."

The Judge paused. The air hummed.

"Who is the jury?" the Judge asked.

"Me," Gerald said.

He held up his bleeding hand.

"I am the Inspector. I am the witness.

I am the Core. I am the only one who has seen the full history of this violation.

"

The Judge looked at the bleeding hand. Then it looked at the Baron.

"You are asking to judge yourself," the Judge said.

"I am asking to be the *sole* judge," Gerald said. "Under the Emergency Provision for Solo Audits."

The Judge's face remained blank.

"There is no such provision," the Judge said.

Gerald felt the floor drop out from under him. Metaphorically. The silence was heavy enough to crush lungs.

He had missed a line. He had misread the code.

Or he had lied.

The Judge stepped forward. The white void behind it expanded, swallowing the shadows.

"Your citation is invalid," the Judge said. "You have no authority to judge yourself.

You are the accused.

You are the inspector. The conflict of interest is absolute.

"

The Auditor surged. The black light coalesced into a blade, sharp and jagged, hovering above Gerald's head.

"Prepare for termination," the Judge said.

The Baron roared. He raised his sword, not at the Auditor, but at the ceiling. He swung upward, cleaving the air.

But the sword passed through nothing. The Auditor was not physical. The blade did nothing.

Gerald looked at the Judge. He looked at the Auditor.

He looked at his clipboard, and he picked it up.

He did not write. He turned the board around. On the back, in faint pencil marks, was a single number.

A permit number.

**PERMIT 77-B: EXTERNAL AUDIT REQUEST.**

The Judge stopped. The Auditor's blade hovered, an inch from Gerald's scalp.

"Where did you get that?" the Judge asked. The voice was no longer polite. It was suspicious.

Gerald held the clipboard out.

"I filed a counter-complaint," Gerald said. "Three chapters ago. Against the Auditor. For harassment."

The Judge stared at the number. It flickered. The number was real. It existed in the System.

"You filed a complaint against the Inspector General," the Judge said.

"I filed a complaint against the *previous* Inspector General," Gerald said. "The one who audited my ventilation. He is gone. You are new."

The Judge's face twitched. A micro-expression. Surprise.

"You are trying to recuse me," the Judge said.

"I am trying to establish jurisdiction," Gerald said. "If you are the new Inspector, you were not present for the original audit. Therefore, you have no standing to enforce penalties from the previous audit. You can only enforce *current* violations."

The Auditor's blade lowered. Slowly.

The Judge looked at the wall. At the word *CITATION*.

"That is a loophole," the Judge said.

"It is a rule," Gerald said. "And rules are the only things that survive collapse."

The Judge turned to the Auditor. The black light obeyed, shrinking, retreating into the Judge's briefcase.

The Judge looked back at Gerald.

"You have bought time," the Judge said.

"One minute. The System will verify the permit.

If the permit is valid, I must recuse myself.

If it is invalid, I will delete you.

"

Gerald nodded. He closed his eyes. He felt the clock start. Sixty seconds.

The Baron lowered his sword. He looked at Gerald.

"Did you actually file it?" the Baron whispered.

Gerald opened one eye.

"No," Gerald said.

The Baron's eyes widened.

"What?"

"I forged the number," Gerald said. He watched the Judge's face.

"The System checks the database.

But it takes sixty seconds to cross-reference the legacy archives.

The previous Inspector General's files are encrypted.

"

The Judge stepped back. The white void closed.

"You are gambling your life on a database lag," the Baron said.

"I am gambling on bureaucracy," Gerald said. "Bureaucracy is slow. And slow is safe."

The Judge raised a hand.

**VERIFICATION IN PROGRESS.** **TIME REMAINING: 58 SECONDS.**

The room held its breath. The dust settled. The frost melted.

Then, the white void flashed.

**PERMIT 77-B NOT FOUND.** **STATUS: INVALID.**

Gerald's heart stopped, and he had not filed it. He had not forged a number. He had made it up. A random sequence.

The Judge smiled. It was a thin, dry, terrifying smile. It was Gerald's own smile, mirrored back at him.

"You lied to the Inspector," the Judge said.

The Auditor surged. The black light expanded, filling the room.

Gerald looked at the Baron. The Baron looked at Gerald.

"I have no plan," Gerald said.

The Baron raised his sword again. This time, he did not swing. He held it up, catching the black light.

"Then we improvise," the Baron said.

The light hit the blade. The metal screamed. The Baron's arm dissolved, turning to smoke.

Gerald watched the arm vanish. He watched the Baron's face contort in pain.

He looked at the wall. The word *CITATION* was fading. The stone was turning to sand.

Gerald raised his hand. He had one letter left to write. But he had no hand. He had no skin.

He pressed his stump against the wall.

And he wrote nothing.

The System paused.

**ERROR.** **INPUT DETECTED: NULL.** **QUERY: WHAT IS THE COUNTER-CLAIM?**

The Judge's smile vanished.

"The counter-claim," the Judge said. "What is it?"

Gerald looked at the Baron. The Baron was almost gone. Only the sword remained, hovering in the air.

Gerald looked at the Judge.

"I claim," Gerald said, "that the Auditor is not compliant."

The Judge blinked.

"Explain," the Judge said.

"The Auditor is a regulatory instrument," Gerald said. "Regulatory instruments must be calibrated. The Auditor has exceeded its error margin.

It has acted with malice. Malice is not calibration.

"

The Judge looked at the Auditor. The Auditor shrank, trembling.

"The Auditor is an extension of the System," the Judge said. "It cannot be non-compliant. It is the law."

"The law must be fair," Gerald said. "If the law is not fair, it is not the law. It is violence."

The System hung in the balance.

The Baron's sword fell to the floor with a clang. The Baron was gone.

Gerald was alone.

The Judge looked at him.

**VERIFICATION COMPLETE.** **PERMIT INVALID.** **COUNTER-CLAIM PENDING.** **DECISION REQUIRED: 10 SECONDS.**

Gerald stood in the center of the collapsing dungeon. The walls were gone. The ceiling was gone. The floor was a void.

He looked at the Judge, and he did not speak. He did not argue.

He waited.

The Judge waited.

The System calculated.

And then, the white void opened wider.

Not to delete him.

To show him.

A new figure stepped out, which was not the Judge. It was the Inspector General. The real one.

The one who had been missing for ten chapters.

It looked at Gerald.

It looked at the fading sword.

It looked at the void.

**AUDIT COMPLETE.** **FINDING: CRITICAL FAILURE IN CHAIN OF COMMAND.** **ACTION: TERMINATE AUDITOR.** **ACTION: PROMOTE CORE.**

The Auditor screamed as it was erased.

Gerald fell to his knees. The void closed. The floor returned. The walls rebuilt themselves, brick by brick, faster than he had ever seen.

The Judge bowed.

"Congratulations, Inspector General," the Judge said.

Gerald looked up, and he was not a Core anymore.

He was something else.

And the first thing he heard was not the System.

It was a voice from the other side of the wall.

A voice he recognized.

"Mr. Hutchins?"

Gerald froze, and the voice was his boss. The City Manager.

"Mr. Hutchins, we have received a complaint about the library," the voice said. "There is a structural anomaly. Please report to the sub-basement immediately."

Gerald looked at the wall. He looked at his new hands. They were not flesh. They were stone.

He looked at the System interface.

**NEW ROLE: SYSTEM AUDITOR.** **NEW AUTHORITY: UNRESTRICTED.** **NEW CONSTRAINT: YOU MUST REPORT TO THE CITY.**

Gerald stood up. He picked up the clipboard.

He walked to the door.

And he opened it.
