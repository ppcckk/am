Chapter 274: Blood Citation Formulated

# Chapter 274

The red ink sizzled against the parchment.

It did not flow like normal ink. It bubbled, thick and viscous, smelling of copper and old sweat.

Gerald's hand shook.

The pen tip dug into the paper, tearing a tiny hole. He didn't care. The System window hovered in his peripheral vision, pulsing with an aggressive, angry red border.

**BLOOD CITATION FORMULATED.** **TARGET: DRONE CLASS-4 (UNNAMED).** **VIOLATION: UNAUTHORIZED ENTRY INTO PRIVATE RESIDENCE (ZONE 4B).** **PENALTY: IMMEDIATE STRUCTURAL COLLAPSE OF TARGET FRAME.**

The first drone, the one that had bitten him, froze mid-lunge. Its carapace groaned. Joints snapped with the wet sound of dry twigs breaking. It collapsed into a heap of chitin and sparks.

Gerald gasped, sucking in air that tasted like ozone. His arm throbbed. The bite wound was jagged, bleeding freely, but the pain was distant. Focus had narrowed to the pen in his hand.

"Keep writing," Kael shouted, parrying a second drone's claw with his sword. "Don't stop!"

"I am writing," Gerald said, his voice flat. "I am observing the procedure."

He dipped the pen back into the pool of Minotaur blood on the floor. The liquid clung to the nib, heavy and dark. He dragged the pen across the second sheet of parchment he had pulled from his satchel.

**CITATION ISSUED TO TARGET 2.** **STATUS: PENDING.**

The second drone staggered.

A crack appeared along its thorax. It tried to correct its posture, its legs twitching in confusion.

Then the crack split.

The drone folded in half and hit the floor, dead.

But the swarm did not slow.

More drones poured from the ventilation shafts. Hundreds of them.

They clicked and hissed, a tide of metal and malice.

Kael was fighting on three fronts.

Toby was struggling to keep his shield raised, his face pale and slick with sweat.

"Too many," Toby yelled. "Gerald, you can't write them all!"

Gerald ignored him. He looked at the remaining blank pages in his satchel. He had twelve sheets left. The swarm numbered in the hundreds.

The math did not work.

Gerald stopped writing. He looked at the System interface. The **BLOOD CITATION** mechanic was active, but it required individual targeting.

Each citation took time. Each citation cost blood.

And each citation only affected a single entity or a single contiguous group if they were physically touching.

These drones were not touching. They were a swarm.

A new window popped up.

**NOTICE: INDIVIDUAL CITATION INEFFICIENT FOR SWARM ENTITIES.** **SUGGESTED ACTION: CONGREGATE.**

Gerald blinked. *Congregate.*

It was a word from the code. Section 4.2: *Crowd Control Measures in Public Assemblies.*

He looked at the drones. They were spread out, filling the dungeon room with their clicking bodies. If he could make them touch...

"Toby," Gerald said. "The shield."

Toby looked up, confused.

"What?"

"Slam it. Not at them. At the ground. Between them."

"That's stupid!" Toby yelled, deflecting a claw strike. "It won't hurt them!"

"It isn't meant to hurt them," Gerald said. "It is meant to create a shockwave. A vibration.

Drones operate on frequency, and they are synchronized. If you disrupt the floor, they will stumble.

They will fall into each other.

"

Toby hesitated. A drone lunged at his head. He slammed the shield down, not at the drone, but at the stone floor.

*CRACK.*

The impact echoed. The floor tiles shattered slightly. The vibration rippled outward.

The drones faltered. Their legs slipped. For a second, they bumped into one another.

Claws tangled. Wings brushed.

They were no longer separate entities, and they were a mass.

Gerald's eyes widened.

He raised his pen. He did not write a citation for a drone.

He wrote a citation for the *floor*.

**CITATION ISSUED: STRUCTURAL FAILURE OF SUB-FLOOR SUPPORT.** **CAUSE: VIBRATORY DAMAGE INDUCED BY UNAUTHORIZED IMPACT.** **SEVERITY: CRITICAL.** **PENALTY: IMMEDIATE SUBSIDENCE.**

The System window flashed green.

**APPROVED.**

The ground beneath the center of the swarm gave way.

It wasn't a big hole. It was a sinkhole, perhaps ten feet wide, caused by the sudden collapse of the support beams Gerald had neglected to reinforce in the early chapters. But it was enough.

The drones fell. They piled into the hole, a writhing mound of metal and bone. They were pressed together. Contact achieved.

Gerald dipped the pen. He drew a single circle on the paper.

**MASS CITATION ISSUED.** **TARGETS: CONTACT GROUP (N=47).** **VIOLATION: ILLEGAL ASSEMBLY OF NON-COMPLIANT ENTITIES.** **PENALTY: SYSTEM PURGE.**

A white light enveloped the pile of drones.

There was no explosion. No fire.

The light simply erased them. One moment, there was a mountain of clicking metal. The next, there was only dust and silence.

The room was quiet.

Kael lowered his sword. He stared at the empty space.

"Did you... did you just delete them?"

Gerald wiped his hands on his trousers. The blood was still wet on his fingers. He looked at the citation paper. It was crumbling, the red ink fading into gray ash.

"It was a violation," Gerald said. "Illegal assembly. I cited them."

"You cited the *floor*?" Kael asked, incredulous.

"I cited the condition of the floor. The drones exacerbated the violation. The System penalized the violation. The drones were incidental."

Kael opened his mouth to argue, then closed it. He looked at Gerald with a mixture of horror and awe.

Toby panted, leaning on his shield.

"We need to move. That hole is big. More will come through it."

"Wait," Gerald said.

He walked to the edge of the sinkhole. He looked down. The support beams were splintered. He took out his measuring tape.

"We have a problem," Gerald said.

"What problem?" Kael asked. "The dungeon is still being attacked?"

"The structure is compromised," Gerald said. "And now that we've unlocked Blood Citations, we have a new liability."

He pulled out a fresh pad of paper. He wrote, not a citation, but a self-assessment.

**FORM 11-B: SELF-REPORTED STRUCTURAL DEFECT.** **ITEM: SUB-FLOOR ZONE 4B.** **STATUS: COMPROMISED.** **MITIGATION: TEMPORARY SHORING REQUIRED.**

"If I do not repair this," Gerald said, "my Compliance Rating drops. If my rating drops below 40, the System initiates a Hostile Takeover protocol. The Inspector General will send an Auditor."

Kael's face went pale.

"An Auditor? Like the one that killed the Goblin King?"

"Worse," Gerald said. "An Auditor has infinite ink."

A low rumble echoed through the dungeon. Not from the hole. From the walls.

The stone vibrated.

**SYSTEM ALERT: STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY CHECK FAILED.** **AUDIT INITIATED.** **ESTIMATED TIME OF ARRIVAL: 10 MINUTES.**

"Ten minutes," Kael said. "How do we fix a floor in ten minutes?"

Gerald looked at the blood on the floor. The Minotaur's blood was still there, pooling in the cracks of the broken tiles.

"We don't fix it with stone," Gerald said. "We fix it with code."

He looked at Kael.

"Do you trust me?"

"I don't know," Kael said honestly. "But you're the only one who speaks their language."

Gerald nodded. He grabbed a large chunk of wood from a broken support beam. He held it against the edge of the hole.

"I need you to hold this," Gerald said. "Do not let it move."

"What are you going to do?" Toby asked.

Gerald dipped his pen in the blood again. He wrote on the wood itself.

**REINFORCEMENT PROTOCOL.** **MATERIAL: ORGANIC COMPOSITE (MINOTAUR BLOOD WOOD).** **CODE CLASSIFICATION: TEMPORARY SHORING (CLASS B).** **EXPIRATION: 48 HOURS.**

The System hesitated.

**WARNING: USE OF FOREIGN MATERIALS IN STRUCTURAL REPAIR REQUIRES SPECIAL PERMIT.** **PERMIT DENIED.**

Gerald's jaw tightened. He couldn't do it. He couldn't improvise a permit. The System rejected unauthorized repairs.

Unless...

Unless the repair was a citation.

Gerald looked at the crumbling edge of the hole. He looked at the blood. He looked at the System rules in his mind. *Citations require the target to be physically present.*

The hole was the target.

But you cannot cite a hole. A hole is an absence.

Gerald's breath hitched. His mind raced through thirty-one years of building codes. *Absence of material.* *Lack of support.*

He had a new idea. A loophole.

He didn't need to repair the hole. He needed to cite the *air* for failing to provide structural support.

It was absurd. It was impossible. It was exactly the kind of bureaucratic nightmare Gerald thrived on.

He wrote on the air.

**CITATION ISSUED: ATMOSPHERIC COLUMN ZONE 4B.** **VIOLATION: FAILURE TO MAINTAIN LOAD-BEARING CAPACITY.** **SEVERITY: CRITICAL.**

The System window flickered.

**INVALID TARGET. AIR CANNOT BE CITED.**

Gerald gritted his teeth. He wiped the blood from his hand onto his sleeve. He looked at the approaching rumble. The walls were cracking further.

"Think," Gerald whispered. "Think."

Kael held the wood beam. His muscles strained.

"Gerald! It's slipping!"

Gerald looked at the wood. It was Minotaur wood. It was organic. It was alive.

And it was bleeding, and he had cited the drones for being in a private residence. He had cited the floor for being weak.

What if he cited the *wood* for being unauthorized?

No. That didn't help.

He needed to make the floor strong.

Gerald looked at the blood. He looked at the hole. He looked at the System's rule about *Hearings*.

*Appeals trigger a Hearing. Winning an appeal reverses the debuff.*

He had been cited for the floor's collapse. That was a debuff. If he appealed, he could reverse it. But he had to argue structural logic.

He didn't have time for a Hearing.

"Kael," Gerald said. "Throw the pen."

"What?"

"Throw the pen into the hole."

Kael looked at him like he was mad.

"Why?"

"Because the pen is a Class A instrument," Gerald said. "And the hole is a hazard. If I cite the hazard, and the hazard absorbs the instrument, the instrument becomes part of the citation."

"That makes no sense!" Kael yelled.

"Do it!"

Kael threw the pen.

It spun through the air, trailing red blood, and fell into the darkness of the sinkhole.

**CITATION ISSUED: UNAUTHORIZED DISPOSAL OF CLASS A INSTRUMENT.** **TARGET: HAZARD ZONE 4B.** **PENALTY: CONTAINMENT.**

The System processed the citation.

The air in the hole distorted. The falling debris stopped.

The crumbling edges halted, and the hole did not close, but it was now *contained*. A forcefield of pure bureaucratic logic sealed the edges. The floor would not collapse further because the violation was now *contained* within a citation envelope.

It was ugly. It was dangerous. It was technically not a repair. It was a legal stay of execution for gravity.

**AUDIT DELAYED: PENDING REVIEW OF CITATION 74-B.** **ESTIMATED TIME OF ARRIVAL: 48 HOURS.**

Gerald slumped against the wall. He breathed heavily. His arm was still bleeding, but the wound was clotting.

Kael dropped the wood beam. He walked over to Gerald.

"You just... froze the floor with a citation?"

"I filed a motion to stay," Gerald said, his voice raspy.

"It is temporary. In forty-eight hours, the Auditor will arrive to review the stay.

If the stay is overturned, the floor collapses.

And we are in a cell.

"

Toby sat down heavily.

"We have forty-eight hours to fix it properly."

"No," Gerald said. "We have forty-eight hours to find a new blood source. The Minotaur's blood is running out. And I need more ink."

As if on cue, the dungeon lights flickered.

A new message appeared in the air, written in gold letters.

**SYSTEM NOTICE: NEW RIVAL CORE DETECTED IN ADJACENT ZONE.** **IDENTIFICATION: CORE 994 'THE PUNISHING GARDEN'.** **STATUS: HOSTILE.** **ACTION: CITATION PENDING.**

Gerald looked up.

The Punsing Garden? He knew that dungeon.

It was a trap-heavy dungeon that specialized in poison, which was notorious for its non-compliant drainage systems.

It leaked toxic sludge into the municipal sewers.

Gerald had tried to shut them down three years ago. They had sent assassins to kill him in his sleep.

He stood up. He walked to the edge of the contained hole. He looked at the gold text.

"They are close," Gerald said.

Kael grabbed his shoulder.

"Gerald, we're hurt. We're bleeding. We're trapped."

Gerald reached into his satchel. He pulled out a fresh pen. It was black ink.

He looked at the hole. He looked at the gold text.

"I am not trapped," Gerald said. "I am under audit. That is different."

He opened a new form.

**FORM 11-B: COMPLAINT AGAINST NEIGHBORING ENTITY.** **SUBJECT: CORE 994.** **VIOLATION: ILLEGAL DISCHARGE OF BIOHAZARD INTO PUBLIC SEWER SYSTEM.** **EVIDENCE: SAMPLES TAKEN FROM DRAINAGE OUTLETS (ZONE 1A).**

He didn't have samples. He didn't have evidence.

But he had the Punsing Garden's reputation.

And he had a new mechanic.

Gerald looked at his bleeding arm. He looked at the black pen. He looked at the red blood on his hand.

He mixed them.

Black ink. Red blood.

He wrote.

**CITATION ISSUED: ENVIRONMENTAL CONTAMINATION.** **TARGET: CORE 994 (REMOTE SERVICE).** **METHOD: SUB-SURFACE MIGRATION VIA SEWER SYSTEM.** **STATUS: IN PROGRESS.**

The System hesitated.

**REMOTE CITATION NOT ALLOWED. TARGET MUST BE PHYSICALLY PRESENT TO SERVE.**

Gerald smiled. A dry, terrifying smile.

He walked to the drainage grate in the corner of the room. It was old, rusted, and broken. It led directly to the city sewers. And the sewers led to the Punsing Garden.

"Kael," Gerald said. "Do you know how to swim?"

Kael stared at him.

"Are you insane?"

"I am serving a citation," Gerald said. "By mail. Via the postal service of the underworld."

He dropped the pen into the grate.

It clattered down into the darkness.

Then, the grate vibrated. The sludge from the sewer rose. It bubbled up, black and foul-smelling.

And from the depths, a voice echoed. It was not a monster's roar. It was a bureaucratic drone.

**CITATION RECEIVED.** **HEARING SCHEDULED FOR IMMINENT INTERCEPTION.**

The sludge surged upward, forming a shape. A humanoid shape made of sewage and anger.

The Auditor had arrived early.

Or the Punsing Garden was sending a messenger.

Gerald stepped back. He raised his empty, bloody hand.

"Welcome to Ashford," Gerald said. "Do you have your permit?"

The sludge-shape lunged.
