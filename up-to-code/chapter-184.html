Chapter 184: One Minute Left

# Chapter 184

The server room smelled of ozone and burnt plastic. It was a smell Gerald knew well. It was the scent of a Class B electrical violation, a smell that usually preceded a fine of two hundred and fifty dollars and a mandated six-month inspection cycle. Here, the smell preceded the end of the world.

The entity hovered in the center of the aisle, a twisting knot of void that defied the geometry of the racks surrounding it. It did not have eyes, but Gerald felt its attention. It was looking for the weak point in the structure. It was looking for the non-compliant element.

Gerald did not look at the entity. He looked at the floor.

A thick bundle of fiber-optic cables ran across the concrete, taped down with black electrical tape that was peeling at the edges. The tape was old. The tape was brittle. The tape was a trip hazard.

"Gerald, move!" Kaelen shouted from the doorway, his bow drawn but shaking in his grip.

Gerald ignored him. His fingers moved with the muscle memory of thirty-one years. He pulled a clipboard from his inventory slot, though he had no inventory. The System provided one because Gerald had requested one in Chapter 12, citing the necessity of on-site documentation for liability protection.

**Form 14-A: Immediate Hazard Abatement Order.**

He scribbled the details. *Subject: Unsecured wiring crossing pedestrian egress path. Severity: Critical. Violation: Municipal Code 7.4.2 (Trip Hazards in Emergency Egress Routes).*

The System chime rang out. It was louder this time. A physical force.

**Citation Issued.** **Target: Localized Reality Distortion (Unregistered Entity).** **Violation: Obstruction of Egress Route.** **Penalty: Structural Destabilization (30 seconds).**

The entity froze. Not from fear, but from the sudden loss of friction.

The floor beneath its shifting form seemed to tilt, not physically, but logically.

The entity's hold on reality loosened. It slid sideways, crashing into a server rack. Sparks showered from the broken metal.

Kaelen stared. "What did you do?"

Gerald didn't answer.

He was already filling out the next form. His handwriting was small, cramped, and perfectly legible.

This was the only thing that steadied his hands when the ground shook.

He was not a hero. He was an inspector. Heroes fought the dark.

Inspectors made sure the lights were up to code so the dark couldn't win by default.

The entity shrieked. It was a sound like metal tearing. It reformed, pulling itself upright with a desperate, clawing motion. The void around it pulsed, trying to reassert its existence.

Gerald stepped over the tangled cables. He walked straight toward the main server bank. The air grew colder. Frost crystallized on the server fans.

**System Notice: Ambient Temperature Drop Detected. Potential HVAC Failure.**

Gerald tapped his temple. "File a complaint," he muttered.

**Complaint Registered: Inefficient Thermal Regulation.** **Dungeon Core Status: Active.** **Current Violation Count: 12.**

Twelve.

He had been working on this building for three hours.

The System loved him. Or rather, the System loved that he was finding things wrong.

The more things wrong, the more Gerald could cite. The more he cited, the more the entity was forced to operate within a framework of rules it did not understand.

The entity lunged. It was faster than Gerald expected. It swept a tendril of black mist toward his chest.

Gerald didn't dodge. He held up his clipboard.

The mist hit the cardboard. The cardboard did not burn.

It did not explode.

It simply ceased to be part of the entity's attack vector. The System had flagged the clipboard as *Official Documentation*. Attacks on official documentation during an active audit were prohibited by the Inspector General's protocols.

The mist dissipated against the clipboard.

"Try again," Gerald said. His voice was steady. "But note that attempting to destroy evidence of a violation constitutes an Obstruction of Justice. That is a Class A felony in the System."

The entity paused.

It seemed to be processing the logic. It was a creature of chaos, of raw mana and hunger.

It did not know what justice was.

It did not know what a felony was. But it knew that its power was draining. The Citations were working.

They weren't hurting it with fire or lightning. They were hurting it by forcing it to comply with a structure it couldn't fit into.

Gerald walked past it. He walked toward the central breaker panel.

"You're going to kill us," Kaelen whispered. He was backing away, toward the library stairs. "Gerald, that thing eats reality. You can't fine it."

Gerald ignored him. He reached the breaker panel. It was open. The main disconnect switch was missing.

This was it. The core problem.

The entity wasn't attacking the dungeon. It was overloading the mana currents because there was no load. The dungeon was a conduit, but the conduit had no resistance. The energy was building up, threatening to blow out the entire district, not just the library.

Gerald looked at the empty space where the disconnect switch should have been.

He needed a breaker. He needed a way to interrupt the current. He needed to create a fault.

But you can't create a fault if you don't have a switch.

He looked around the server room. There were no tools. There were no spare parts. There was only the entity, the cables, and the silence of a system about to fail.

Then he saw it.

Kaelen's bow. The Ranger had dropped it in his panic. A composite bow. Made of carbon fiber and synthetic string.

Gerald picked it up.

**Item Acquired: Composite Bow.** **Material Analysis: Non-conductive. High tensile strength.**

A breaker switch had to be non-conductive to prevent arcing. It had to be able to snap the connection.

Gerald looked at the empty slot in the panel. He looked at the bow.

He broke the bow.

He snapped the limb. The sound was sharp, like a gunshot in the quiet room. He took the rigid, carbon-fiber piece and held it against the terminals.

"Hey!" the entity hissed. It was moving closer now, sensing the change in the energy flow. *"Stop. You are interfering with the natural decay."*

Gerald held the carbon piece steady. "Natural decay is a violation of structural integrity if it causes premature collapse. This is a managed shutdown."

He pushed the carbon piece into the empty slot. It didn't fit. The slot was designed for a copper blade, not a bow limb.

The entity surged. The mana current spiked. The lights in the server room flickered and died.

Gerald felt the heat rise in his chest.

His Core was vibrating.

If the current overloaded the room, the dungeon would collapse. The library above would fall.

The city block would be vaporized.

He needed to bridge the gap. He needed to make the carbon touch both terminals.

He looked at the entity. The void was inches away.

"Kaelen," Gerald said. He didn't turn around. "The bow string."

Kaelen was frozen. "What?"

"The string," Gerald repeated, his voice flat. "It's braided. It's strong. Use it to tie the limb to the terminal."

"That's stupid," Kaelen yelled. "It won't work! It's a bow, not a fuse!"

"It's a conductor," Gerald said. "And you're holding the ends. Do it."

Kaelen moved. He ran forward, sliding on the icy floor.

He grabbed the carbon limb Gerald held.

He grabbed the other end of the string. He tied it to the second terminal with shaking hands.

The connection was made.

A spark jumped. Small. Blue. Harmless.

The entity screeched. *"No! The circuit is closed! The energy is trapped!"*

Gerald looked at the panel. The carbon limb was glowing. It was heating up. It was acting as a resistor, slowing the flow, forcing the excess mana to dissipate as heat.

But it wasn't enough. The entity was still pouring energy into the system.

Gerald opened a new form. His hands were numb. His vision was blurring.

**Form 99-Z: Emergency Circuit Breaker Installation (Provisional).** **Reason: Imminent Catastrophic Overload.** **Materials: Improvised. (See attached photos: Bow Limb, String, Duct Tape).** **Approval Request: Immediate.**

He submitted it.

The System waited.

The entity loomed over Gerald. It raised a hand made of shadows. It could crush him. It could delete him before the System responded.

Gerald closed his eyes. He didn't pray. He thought about the code. He thought about the specific wording of the variance he had filed ten minutes ago. *Exigency due to non-compliant existential threat.*

The chime rang.

**Request Approved.** **Provisional Installation Accepted.** **Warning: Device is not UL Listed. Warranty Void.**

The glow from the bow limb intensified. The heat became unbearable. The carbon smoked.

The entity screamed as its energy was drained, not absorbed, but *vented*. The mana flowed through the improvised circuit, through Kaelen's string, into the building's grounding wire, and out into the earth.

The floor beneath Gerald's feet shook. Not with the force of an attack, but with the force of discharge.

The entity's form unravelled. It was losing cohesion. The Citations were stacking up. Each second that Gerald held the circuit open added another violation to the entity's record.

**Violation 13: Unauthorized Energy Discharge.** **Violation 14: Failure to Provide Overcurrent Protection.** **Violation 15: Operating Without a Permit for High-Voltage Modifications.**

The entity shrieked, a sound of pure frustration. It tried to pull back, but the current had it. It was caught in the loop.

Then, a new voice.

Not the entity's. Not Gerald's.

A deep, resonant voice that came from everywhere and nowhere. It sounded like the grinding of tectonic plates.

**Inspector General: Audit Initiated.** **Subject: Entity #9099 (Self-Declared).** **Status: Critical Non-Compliance Detected.** **Action: Immediate Seizure.**

The air in the room changed. The cold vanished. The heat remained.

A hand appeared in the air. Not a hand of shadow, but a hand of light. It was massive. It reached down and grabbed the entity.

The entity struggled. *"No! This is not in your jurisdiction! I am a free agent!"*

**Inspector General: Jurisdiction Confirmed. Entity is operating within a registered Core's footprint without a Lease Agreement. Violation of Municipal Zoning Laws.**

The hand crushed the entity.

It didn't explode. It folded. The void collapsed inward, turning into a small, dense ball of dark matter. The light hand held it for a moment, then dropped it.

The ball hit the floor. It didn't bounce. It just sat there, humming.

Silence returned to the server room.

Gerald slumped against the breaker panel. The bow limb crumbled to ash in his hand. Kaelen was on his knees, gasping for air, his hands blistered from the string.

Gerald looked at the ball of dark matter.

He stood up. His legs felt like lead. He walked over to the ball. He pulled out his clipboard.

He wrote a receipt.

**Item Received: Seized Asset.** **Description: Concentrated Mana Density (Unstable).** **Location: Ashford Municipal Library, Sub-basement, Server Room.** **Custodian: Gerald Hutchins, Municipal Building Inspector.**

He looked at the label. *Custodian.*

He hadn't destroyed it. He hadn't deleted it. He had impounded it.

And as he looked at the label, he saw something else. A small, glowing tag attached to the ball.

**Tag: Property of the Inspector General's Office.** **Status: Pending Disposal Hearing.** **Location of Hearing: The Void Between Worlds.** **Time: Immediate.**

Gerald stared at the tag.

He couldn't leave it here. The System wouldn't let him. It was his responsibility now. He had accepted custody.

If he didn't go to the hearing, he would be cited for Negligence.

If he went to the hearing, he would be in the Void.

Kaelen looked up, his face pale. "Gerald. What is that?"

Gerald picked up the ball. It was heavy. It pulsed against his palm.

"It's evidence," Gerald said.

He looked at the door. The library stairs were still open. The way out was clear.

But the tag was burning hot.

He had to go. Not because he wanted to. But because the code required it.

He opened his mouth to speak, but the System beat him to it.

**Hearing Location Locked.** **Transporting Defendant.**

The floor beneath Gerald disappeared.

There was no sensation of falling. There was no wind. There was only the sudden, absolute absence of gravity.

Gerald Hutchins, Municipal Building Inspector, fell out of the world.

He fell into the Void.

And in his hand, he still held the clipboard.

The last thing he saw before the darkness took him was Kaelen's mouth forming a silent word.

*Run.*

Gerald didn't run. He checked the time.

The hearing was scheduled for nine o'clock.

It was currently eight fifty-nine.

He had one minute.
