Chapter 397: The Pen Returns

# Chapter 397

The pen was gone.

Gerald looked at his right hand. Five fingers. Empty palm. The phantom weight of the ballpoint was still there, a ghostly pressure against his thumb and forefinger, but the object itself had been deleted along with his dungeon, his rat, and his dignity.

White stretched in every direction. No horizon. No floor. Just a luminous, featureless plane that hummed with a low-frequency vibration he could feel in his teeth.

**[Void State Active.]** **[No Infrastructure.]** **[No Spawns.]** **[No Authority.]**

The text floated in the air, crisp and judgmental. Gerald stared at it.

He tried to summon a clipboard.

Nothing happened. He tried to summon a measuring tape. The air remained stubbornly empty.

Panic, cold and sharp, pricked at the base of his spine. He forced it down. Panic was inefficient. Panic did not pass inspection.

He took a step.

His foot landed on solid ground, though there was no ground. He took another.

The sensation of walking persisted, a cognitive trick the System was forcing upon him.

He needed structure. He needed a baseline.

He walked.

One step. Two steps. Three.

He counted them. He measured them by feel.

Approximately thirty inches.

Standard stride, and he adjusted. Twenty-four inches.

More precise.

The white void resisted. It rippled around his ankles like thick fog. He pushed through it, maintaining a steady cadence. Left, right, left, right.

*Find the corner,* he told himself. *Every room has a corner. Even a void.*

He walked for what felt like ten minutes. The hum in his teeth grew louder.

A shadow appeared ahead.

Not a dark shadow, but a darker shade of white. A distortion in the light.

Gerald stopped, and he reached into his pocket for a pencil. Gone. He reached for his clipboard. Gone.

The distortion sharpened. It took the shape of a doorway.

Not a library door.

A steel fire door. The kind found in commercial basements.

It had a push bar, which had a kick plate. It had no handle on the inside, which was a code violation in several jurisdictions unless it was an exit-only door, but there was no exit signage visible.

Gerald approached it. He ran a hand along the frame. Cold metal. Real.

He pulled the push bar.

The door swung open.

Beyond it lay a corridor. Gray concrete walls. Fluorescent lights buzzing overhead. A smell of damp earth and ozone.

Gerald stepped through.

The door slammed shut behind him. The sound was final. A heavy, industrial *clunk* that echoed down the hallway.

**[Location: Ashford Municipal Library, Sub-Basement Sector 4.]** **[Status: Rebuilding...]** **[Compliance Rating: 0/100.]** **[Current Penalty: Structural Instability (Minor).]**

Gerald exhaled. The air was stale. It tasted like dust and old paper.

He looked left. The corridor extended into darkness. He looked right. It ended at a brick wall.

This wasn't a void anymore. It was his dungeon. Or the skeleton of it.

A tremor ran through the floor. Cracks spiderwebbed across the concrete.

*Structural Instability.*

He couldn't fix what he couldn't see. But he could inspect.

Gerald moved down the corridor. His steps were light, controlled.

He scanned the walls for moisture.

No weeping.

Good. He checked the ceiling height.

Eight feet, six inches. Low, but within the minimum for a basement storage area, provided the egress width was sufficient.

The corridor was four feet wide. Too narrow for two-way traffic, but acceptable for single-file evacuation if the exit was clear.

A shadow detached itself from the far end of the hall.

Gerald stopped. He didn't raise his hands. He didn't cast a spell. He reached for his badge.

It wasn't there, and he reached for his voice. It was there.

"Stop," Gerald said.

The shadow froze. It was a goblin. Small, green-skinned, wearing a loincloth that offered zero protection from falling debris. It held a rusty shiv.

Gerald squinted.

"Identify yourself."

The goblin tilted its head. It made a chittering sound.

"I don't speak Goblin," Gerald said flatly. "I speak Code. And you are trespassing on private municipal property."

The goblin sneered. It lunged.

Gerald didn't move. He waited. The goblin closed the distance, shiv raised.

**[System Alert: Hostile Intent Detected.]** **[Spawn List: Empty.]** **[Defense: None.]**

The shiv came down.

Gerald sidestepped. It was a simple shift of weight, a move he'd used to dodge swinging pipes during inspection walkthroughs. The goblin stumbled, its momentum carrying it forward into the wall.

*Thud.*

The goblin shook its head, dizzy. It turned, eyes wide with surprise.

Gerald looked at the wall. The impact had cracked the plaster.

"Structural damage," Gerald noted. He pointed at the goblin.

"You are liable for repairs.

Form 7-C. Property Damage. Sign here.

"

He gestured at the air where a form should have been.

The goblin screamed and attacked again. This time, it was faster. It slashed at Gerald's forearm.

Pain. Hot and sharp.

Blood welled on his skin.

**[Health: 98/100.]** **[Warning: Bleeding.]**

Gerald looked at the cut. It was clean. A laceration, not a puncture.

He looked at the goblin. It was panting, drool hanging from its chin. It was preparing for another charge.

Gerald needed a tool. He didn't have a pen. He didn't have a badge.

He had a broken wall, and he stepped back. The goblin charged.

Gerald kicked the base of the wall. Hard. With his heel.

The plaster, already cracked by the previous impact, gave way. A chunk of concrete the size of a brick fell out, exposing the rebar behind it.

The goblin, expecting a solid wall, ran right into the exposed rebar.

It shrieked. The rusty metal spiked through its shoulder.

Gerald stepped forward. He grabbed the goblin's arm. It was slick with sweat and blood.

"Unlicensed biological entity," Gerald said. "Improper use of load-bearing structures."

He twisted.

The goblin flailed. The shiv skittered across the floor.

Gerald kicked the shiv away, and he pinned the goblin against the remaining wall. The goblin's eyes bulged.

It couldn't breathe.

The rebar was holding it, but Gerald's grip was tighter.

"System," Gerald said. "Query: Can I detain this entity?"

**[Yes. Detention requires physical containment or legal binding.]** **[Note: You have no legal binding authority in Void State.]** **[Suggestion: Use Environmental Hazards.]**

Gerald looked at the hole in the wall. Behind the rebar, he could see the darkness of the original void. The place where his dungeon had been.

"Environmental hazards," Gerald muttered.

He looked at the goblin. The goblin looked back. It whined.

Gerald released the arm. He stepped back.

The goblin slumped, gasping. It tried to pull the rebar out of its shoulder. It failed. It looked at Gerald, terrified.

"Go," Gerald said.

The goblin blinked. It scrambled to its feet, dripping blood, and ran down the corridor, away from Gerald, toward the deeper darkness.

Gerald watched it go, and he looked at the hole in the wall. The void was seeping through. Black mist curled around the rebar.

He couldn't let that happen. If the void entered his dungeon, it would eat the rest of the structure.

He needed to seal the breach.

He didn't have mortar. He didn't have concrete.

He had the blood on his arm, which was a stretch. A massive, bureaucratic stretch. But Gerald had once sealed a water leak with chewing gum and a prayer because the city budget for plumbing was frozen.

He pressed his bleeding arm against the hole.

"System," he said. "I am applying a temporary patch. Material: Biological Sealant."

The System paused.

**[Material: Blood.]** **[Category: Organic.]** **[Compatibility: Low.]** **[Effect: Minor Structural Reinforcement (1 hour duration).]**

The blood smeared against the rebar. The black mist hissed, then receded.

The hole didn't close, but it slowed.

The air stopped smelling like ozone.

It smelled like copper.

Gerald pulled his arm back. The cut was scabbing over.

He leaned against the wall, breathing hard.

One goblin. One breach. He had survived. Barely.

A sound came from the other end of the corridor. Heavy footsteps. Clanging metal.

Gerald straightened. He wiped the blood on his trousers.

A figure emerged from the shadows, which was not a goblin. It was a knight.

Heavy plate armor. A tower shield. A lance that glowed with faint blue light.

The knight stopped ten feet away. It looked at Gerald. It looked at the hole in the wall. It looked at the blood on the floor.

"Where is the core?" the knight asked. The voice was muffled by a visor, deep and resonant.

Gerald adjusted his collar. He stood up straight.

"I am the Core," Gerald said.

The knight laughed. It was a dry, harsh sound.

"You look like a man. And you're bleeding."

"I am a man," Gerald said. "I was an inspector. Now I am a Core. The distinction is administrative."

The knight lowered its lance. It pointed it at Gerald's chest.

"This dungeon is cursed. The air is wrong. The geometry is impossible. I am here to purge it."

Gerald looked at the lance. The blue light pulsed.

"That lance is enchanted," Gerald said. "Lightning damage."

The knight nodded.

"Divine lightning."

**[Warning: High-Voltage Hazard Detected.]** **[Current Electrical Code Compliance: 0%.]** **[Debuff: You are vulnerable to Lightning Damage.]**

Gerald's stomach tightened. He remembered the rule. Electrical code compliance grants lightning resistance. He had zero compliance.

He needed to change the category. Fast.

"Put the weapon down," Gerald said. "You are citing the wrong entity."

"I am citing the dungeon," the knight said. "And you are the dungeon."

"No," Gerald said. He pointed at the ceiling. "Look up."

The knight looked up.

The fluorescent lights above them were flickering. One of them exploded. Glass rained down.

The knight flinched, raising its shield.

"Gross negligence," Gerald said. "Failure to maintain electrical fixtures. That is a Class B Violation."

The knight looked back at Gerald.

"What does that mean?"

"It means," Gerald said, "that if you strike me with that lance, the feedback loop will ground out through the floor. And you, standing in a puddle of blood and condensation, are grounded."

The knight looked at the floor. There was, indeed, a pool of blood near Gerald's feet. It had spread.

The knight shifted its weight. It looked uneasy.

"Step away from the hazard," Gerald ordered. It was his voice. The voice that had stopped contractors from pouring concrete in the rain.

"Step away from the puddle. And surrender your weapon."

The knight hesitated.

Gerald didn't wait. He lunged.

Not at the knight. At the wall.

He grabbed the loose chunk of concrete from the breach. He hurled it at the knight's helmet.

It wasn't a powerful throw. But it was accurate. The concrete struck the visor.

*Clang.*

The knight staggered. The lance dipped.

Gerald spun. He grabbed a pipe that ran along the wall. It was loose. He ripped it free.

He swung it like a bat.

It connected with the knight's knee.

The metal bent. The knight screamed.

"Insufficient structural support!" Gerald yelled. "Your armor is compromised!"

The knight fell to one knee. It tried to raise the lance.

Gerald jumped. He slammed the pipe into the knight's shoulder. Again. And again.

*Clang. Clang. Clang.*

The knight roared. It swung the lance blindly.

Gerald ducked. The lance sparked against the wall, sending a shower of blue sparks into the air.

The sparks hit the pipe in Gerald's hand.

Pain. Blinding, white-hot pain.

The pipe heated up instantly. It became a conductor.

Gerald dropped it, and he scrambled back. His hand was blistered.

The knight stood up. It was limping. The lance was still glowing. It looked angrier now.

"You are a pest," the knight said.

"I am an inspector," Gerald corrected. He cradled his burned hand against his chest. "And you are out of code."

**[System Alert: Combat Engagement Active.]** **[Adventurer 'Kaelen the Pure' is attacking.]** **[Note: Kaelen the Pure was previously Cited for Fire Safety Violations.]**

The knight froze.

Gerald looked at the text box. He hadn't seen the name. He hadn't seen the citation history.

But the System knew.

The knight's lance flickered. The blue light dimmed.

**[Debuff Applied: Citation Recall.]** **[Effect: Lightning Damage Reduced by 50%.]** **[Reason: Prior Unresolved Violation.]**

The knight looked at its lance in confusion. It tried to channel energy. The light sputtered.

Gerald stared at the knight. Then he stared at the System prompt.

A loophole, and the knight had been cited before. The System remembered. The citation had weakened its current power.

Gerald had been cited by Kaelen, or perhaps the other way around? No, the prompt said *Kaelen* was previously cited.

Gerald smiled. A thin, tight smile.

"Your appeal was denied," Gerald said. "Didn't you read the fine print?"

The knight roared. It charged.

Gerald ran, and he didn't run away. He ran toward the breach in the wall. The black mist was swirling again.

If he could get the knight into the void...

The knight was slow. The armor was heavy. The knee was injured.

Gerald skidded to a stop at the edge of the hole. The rebar was still there. The blood seal was holding.

The knight reached the corridor. It saw the void. It stopped.

It looked at Gerald. It looked at the void. It looked at the System messages floating in the air, which only Gerald could see.

**[Warning: Void Proximity.]** **[Recommendation: Retreat.]**

The knight stepped forward. It raised the lance.

Gerald waited.

"Come and get it," Gerald said.

The knight thrust the lance.

Gerald sidestepped.

The lance passed through the space where Gerald's chest had been. It hit the rebar.

*Zap.*

Blue electricity arced from the lance, through the rebar, into the blood seal, and into the void.

The void drank it.

The black mist pulsed. It grew.

The knight pulled the lance back. It was trembling. The air around the lance was crackling with residual energy.

Gerald looked at the knight's face, visible now through the cracked visor. Young. Arrogant. Terrified.

"You're grounding your weapon," Gerald said. "Into a non-conductive medium. That's dangerous. Very dangerous."

The knight looked at the void. It looked at Gerald.

It took a step back.

"System," Gerald said. "Query: Can I issue a Citation for Reckless Endangerment of Infrastructure?"

**[Yes.]** **[Target: Kaelen the Pure.]** **[Violation: Using magical discharge in a structurally unstable zone.]** **[Penalty: Stat Debuff: Agility -10%.]**

A red text box appeared over the knight's head.

**[Citation Served.]**

The knight stumbled. Its leg buckled. It fell hard, the armor clattering against the concrete.

It tried to stand. It couldn't. Its legs wouldn't cooperate.

Gerald stood over him. He looked down at the fallen hero.

He didn't kill him. He didn't have the tools to kill him.

But he had the tools to hold him.

Gerald reached into his pocket. He didn't have a pen.

But he had a sharp piece of rebar, left over from the breach.

He picked it up, and he walked toward the knight.

The knight scrambled backward, claws scraping on the floor.

"Don't," the knight whispered. "Please."

Gerald stopped. He looked at the knight. He looked at the void.

He looked at the empty space where his pen used to be.

He drove the rebar into the floor, right between the knight's legs.

The knight froze.

"Stay there," Gerald said. "I need to fix the wall."

He turned his back on the knight, and he worked.

The void was expanding. The knight was trapped.

And somewhere, in the distance, a new sound echoed.

Not footsteps.

Sirens, and the Inspector General was coming.

Gerald's hands shook. He picked up a chunk of concrete.

He had to finish the wall. Before the IG arrived. Before the deletion protocol re-engaged.

He had to build something. Anything.

He placed the concrete in the hole.

It didn't fit, and he tried again.

It slipped.

The sirens grew louder. The air turned red.

**[Audit Imminent.]** **[Time Remaining: 60 Seconds.]**

Gerald looked at the knight. The knight looked at him.

"Help me," Gerald said.

The knight stared.

"I said help me," Gerald repeated. "Pick up that rock."

The knight didn't move.

Gerald closed his eyes. He counted.

One. Two. Three.

He opened his eyes. The red light was blinding.

He reached for the knight's lance. It was still warm.

He grabbed it.

"System," Gerald said. "Query: Can I use this as a form?"

**[No.]** **[Suggestion: Improvise.]**

Gerald looked at the lance. He looked at the wall.

He jammed the lance into the hole.

It fit. Perfectly.

The blue light stabilized the concrete. The rebar held.

The wall stopped crumbling.

The sirens stopped.

Silence.

Gerald slumped against the wall. He was exhausted. His hand throbbed.

A new text box appeared.

**[Improvised Infrastructure Accepted.]** **[Compliance Rating: 1/100.]** **[New Passive: Lightning Resistance (Temporary).]** **[Status: Audit Passed (Barely).]**

Gerald laughed. It was a dry, hacking sound.

He looked at the knight, and the knight was still on the floor. The red light had faded.

"You're under arrest," Gerald said.

The knight looked at him with pure hate.

Gerald smiled.

He pulled the lance out of the wall.

And then he heard it.

A soft, wet sound.

Behind him.

Gerald turned.

The hole in the wall wasn't sealed. It was open. And from the void, a single, pale hand had emerged.

It was not a goblin's hand, which was a human hand.

With long, elegant fingers.

And it was holding a pen.
