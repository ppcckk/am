Chapter 119: Audit of the Debt Collector

# Chapter 119

The crystal shard pulsed with a sickly violet light, hovering in the air between Gerald’s Core and the ragged figure. The System’s red warning text burned across his peripheral vision, pulsing in time with his own mana drain.

*Violation Detected: Core Theft Attempt.* *Status: Active.* *Progress: 3%.*

Gerald did not panic. Panic was inefficient.

Panic violated the protocol for emergency structural failures.

Instead, he felt the cold, familiar numbness of administrative paralysis settle over him. This was not combat. This was not a monster encounter.

This was an unauthorized excavation on private municipal property.

The figure stepped forward. The rags were filthy, stiff with old blood and mud, but the movement was precise.

Deliberate.

The shard in its hand wasn’t just a weapon; it was a conduit. A bridge. It was pulling Gerald’s newly acquired mana—the mana gained from the merger with the neighboring dungeon—through a loophole he hadn’t even known existed.

“Wait,” Gerald said. His voice echoed through the new, spacious corridors of his expanded dungeon.

The acoustics were terrible, and he needed to install sound-dampening panels, which was a violation of noise ordinance section 4.2.

The figure ignored him. The progress bar ticked up. *4%.*

Gerald tried to spawn a rat. Nothing happened. The rat was still dead, or perhaps confused, but more importantly, Gerald’s control over his spawn list was fractured by the theft.

His mana was being siphoned away faster than he could replenish it. If the theft reached 100%, his Core would be hollowed out.

He would cease to exist.

Or worse, he would become a vessel for whatever this thing was.

He looked at the figure. Small and hunched, it looked like it was woven from shadow and hunger, and it was standing in the stairwell. The stairwell that led to the library above.

The library. The Ashford Municipal Library.

Gerald’s mind snapped into focus, and the library was public property; the library was under the jurisdiction of the City of Ashford.

And Gerald Hutchins, even as a dungeon Core, was still technically an employee of the City of Ashford.

He had not been fired. He had merely been… restructured.

The figure raised the shard higher. *5%.*

Gerald didn’t attack. He didn’t spawn a spider. He didn’t try to crush the intruder with falling debris. He reached into his internal inventory, not for a weapon, but for the one thing he had always carried, even as his body dissolved.

His clipboard.

Or rather, the System representation of his clipboard. It manifested in the air before him, a translucent blue rectangle floating in the void of his Core.

“Hey,” Gerald said. He kept his voice flat, bored. The voice of a man who has cited three hundred contractors for using the wrong grade of lumber. “Do you have a permit for that?”

The figure paused. The violet light flickered. For a second, the theft process stalled.

*5.1%.*

“Permit?” the figure rasped. Its voice sounded like dry leaves scraping together. “I am taking your heart, Gerald.”

“You are operating heavy machinery within a public access corridor,” Gerald said, pointing a spectral finger at the stairwell. “That requires a Class 3 Excavation Permit.

You do not have one.

I can see it in your inventory.

It’s empty.”

The figure hissed. “This is a dungeon. There are no permits.”

“Wrong,” Gerald said. He tapped the clipboard. “Section 9, Subsection C: *Inter-Core Resource Transfer.* Any transfer of mana, matter, or structural integrity between distinct Core zones requires a Joint Venture Agreement filed with the Inspector General’s office. Without the agreement, the transfer is classified as theft of public utilities.”

The figure’s eyes narrowed. “You’re stalling.”

“I am conducting an inspection,” Gerald corrected. “And I am noting a violation.”

The System froze. The red *Violation Detected* message hung in the air, but it didn’t progress. The theft was paused.

Not stopped, but paused. A grey box appeared in Gerald’s vision.

*Inspection in Progress.* *Target Status: Pending Review.*

The figure snarled, trying to push more mana through the shard, but the System held it in limbo. The inspector’s authority was overriding the combat mechanic. For now.

Gerald knew this was a gamble.

The Inspector General hadn’t approved this specific interpretation of the rules. The rule about Joint Venture Agreements was obscure, buried in the appendices of the Core Compliance Handbook, a document most cores never read. But Gerald had read it.

All four thousand pages.

Twice, and he had to justify the citation. If he couldn’t prove the violation, the pause would lift, and the theft would resume; this time, it wouldn’t stop.

“Show me the shard,” Gerald said.

The figure hesitated.

It looked at the shard, then at Gerald. It was confused. Cores fought.

Cores spawned monsters. Cores did not file paperwork.

Gerald waited. He could feel his mana bleeding away, a slow, cold trickle. He needed to buy time. He needed to find the angle.

*Why is this thing here?* The merger had just happened. The neighbors were dead or fled. Who knew Gerald’s location? Who knew he had just gained enough mana to be worth stealing?

The figure took a step forward. It didn’t retreat. It advanced, stepping onto the first landing of the stairwell, and the crystal shard glowed brighter, straining against the System’s pause.

*Pause Failing.* *Time Remaining: 10 seconds.*

Gerald’s heart—or the metaphorical equivalent—pounded. He needed a second violation. A stronger one. Something that would trigger an automatic quarantine.

He looked at the stairwell walls, and they were old.

Stone. Crumbling.

The merger had expanded his dungeon, but the upper levels, the ones connected to the library, were still part of the original structure.

The library.

A sign hung above the stairwell door. A faded, yellowed sign that read *EMERGENCY EXIT.*

But the door itself was blocked. Not by rubble, but by the figure. It was standing directly in the egress path.

Gerald’s eyes widened. He looked at the sign. He looked at the door. He looked at the figure.

“Obstruction,” Gerald whispered, and he raised his hand. The clipboard flared with white light.

“Section 11, Paragraph 4: *Egress Pathways must be kept clear of obstructions at all times.* The presence of an unauthorized entity blocking a primary exit constitutes a life-safety hazard of the highest order.”

The System message flashed.

*Violation Detected: Egress Obstruction.* *Severity: Critical.* *Action: Automatic Quarantine.*

The air in the stairwell turned grey. The violet light of the shard sputtered and died.

The figure shrieked, a sound of pure rage, as the System slammed an invisible barrier against its back.

It wasn’t a wall of stone. It was a wall of *code*, and the door behind it fused shut, the stone knitting itself together with supernatural speed.

The figure was trapped.

*Pause Lifted.* *Theft Attempt: Terminated.*

Gerald exhaled. His mana flow stabilized. The drain stopped.

But the figure was still there.

Pinned between the fused door and the quarantine barrier; it struggled, clawing at the stone, but it couldn’t break through.

The System had enforced the code, and the exit was blocked, so the entity was contained.

Gerald floated closer. He didn’t feel triumphant. He felt tired. And hungry.

“Who sent you?” Gerald asked.

The figure spat a glob of black ichor. “The Inspector General doesn’t care about your little games, Hutchins.”

Gerald stiffened. “The Inspector General?”

“You think you’re smart?” the figure hissed. It was small, pitiful, but its words carried weight.

“You think you can cite the world into submission?

You’re just a building.

A box. And boxes can be broken.”

“I am compliant,” Gerald said.

“Compliance is a lie,” the figure said. Its eyes were wild, feverish. “There is only power.

And the System is just a cage for the weak. You’re in it, Gerald.

You’ve always been in it.

You just never realized you were locked in.”

Gerald frowned. That wasn’t a code violation. That was philosophy. And philosophy was not enforceable.

“Why did you wait for the merger?” Gerald asked.

“Because you were weak,” the figure said. “And you are still weak. You just have more walls now. More walls to crack.”

The figure stopped struggling. It looked at Gerald with a sudden, terrifying calmness.

“I’m not trying to steal your Core,” it said softly. “I’m trying to open it.”

Gerald felt a chill that had nothing to do with the temperature. “Open it?”

“The merger created a seam,” the figure said.

“A structural weakness. You expanded too fast.

You didn’t wait for the curing time.

Concrete needs to cure, Gerald. You know that. You’ve cited contractors for rushing the pour a hundred times.”

Gerald looked at the new walls. They were smooth.

Perfect.

But when he looked closer, with his inspector’s eyes, he saw the hairline fractures.

Microscopic. Invisible to the average adventurer.

But visible to a Core, and the figure was right. He had rushed the merger. He had prioritized expansion over structural integrity. He had violated the curing code.

*System Alert: Structural Integrity Compromised.* *Warning: Seismic Activity Detected in Zone 4.*

The ground shook.

It wasn’t a big shake. Just a tremor. But it was centered on Gerald’s Core.

The figure smiled. It didn’t try to escape. It didn’t try to attack. It just sat down in the middle of the quarantined stairwell, cross-legged, and waited.

“The next tremor will be stronger,” the figure said. “And the one after that.

Your walls will crack.

Your ceiling will fall. And when you are buried under your own code, I will be waiting.”

Gerald looked at the cracks. They were spreading.

He looked at the figure. It wasn’t a thief. It was a saboteur. Or a test.

*New Quest Detected: Stabilize Structural Integrity.* *Deadline: 60 Seconds.*

Gerald had sixty seconds to fix the foundation of his dungeon before it collapsed on itself. He didn’t have enough mana. He didn’t have enough materials. He had a rat, a dead rat, and a quarantined intruder who was laughing at him.

He looked at the clipboard. He looked at the cracks.

He could try to reinforce the walls with mana. It would take too long. It would drain him dry.

He could try to evacuate. He had no legs. He was the building.

He looked at the figure. The figure knew about the curing time. It knew about the seams. It was exploiting his haste.

Gerald realized something else. The figure wasn’t attacking him. It was *auditing* him.

Just like the Inspector General.

Gerald raised his hand again. Not to cite the figure. But to cite himself.

“Violation,” Gerald said.

His voice shook. He hated it.

He hated admitting fault.

It felt like vomiting. “Self-Citation: Rushed Construction.”

The System paused.

*Self-Citation Accepted.* *Penalty: Immediate Structural Reset.* *Warning: This action will revert Dungeon Expansion to Pre-Merger State.*

Gerald looked at his dungeon. The new, beautiful, compliant corridors. The fresh air. The space.

If he accepted the penalty, he would lose everything he had gained. He would be small again. Weak again. Vulnerable to the neighbors, the adventurers, the dragons.

But if he didn’t, the walls would fall. And he would die.

The ground shook harder. A chunk of ceiling plaster fell, crashing near the rat.

*Time Remaining: 15 Seconds.*

The figure watched him. It didn’t mock him anymore. It watched with a predator’s interest. It wanted to see if Gerald would choose survival over pride.

Gerald closed his eyes. He thought of the code.

He thought of the thousands of buildings he had inspected.

He thought of the one time he had cut a corner, the one time he had been too tired to measure a beam correctly.

The building had stood, but barely. And he had never forgiven himself.

He opened his eyes.

“Accepted,” Gerald said.

The System flashed white.

*Processing...*

The walls started to pull back. The new corridors dissolved into dust. The ceiling lowered.

The space shrank. The mana that had filled Gerald’s lungs was sucked back into the dark, void-like emptiness of the pre-merger footprint.

Gerald screamed. It was a silent scream, internal and vast. He felt himself shrinking, compressing, folding in on himself.

The figure stood up. The quarantine barrier flickered and vanished as Gerald’s Core weakened.

The figure walked toward Gerald’s Core. It held out its hand.

“Good,” the figure said. “Now you are small. Now you are manageable.”

It reached for Gerald’s light.

But Gerald was faster, and he didn’t attack the figure. He didn’t try to fight it.

He used the last of his shrinking mana to do one thing.

He filed a Form 11-B.

*Unlicensed Biological Entity.*

But this time, he didn’t target the figure, and he targeted the *air*.

The air in the stairwell.

*Violation: Illegal Combustible Material.*

The air in the library above was filled with old books. Paper. Dust.

Gerald didn’t ignite it. He didn’t need fire. He needed pressure.

He collapsed his dungeon further. He squeezed the space tighter than the System allowed. He violated the minimum occupancy code. He crushed himself.

The pressure wave exploded outward.

Not from his Core. From the stairwell.

The door to the library, which had been fused shut, blew off its hinges.

The shockwave hit the figure. It was small, fragile, made of rags and bone. The pressure change was too much. The figure staggered, its balance broken.

It fell backward, into the library above.

Gerald, now tiny, now cramped, now compliant with the old, small footprint, watched the door slam shut again, this time locked by the System’s automatic security protocol triggered by the breach.

The figure was outside.

Gerald was inside, and he was small. He was weak. His Compliance Rating had plummeted to 30/100.

But he was alive.

And he had a new problem.

The door to the library was open. And now, something else was coming down the stairs.

Not the figure.

Something larger. Something with four legs and a tail that swept the steps.

A dragon.

But it wasn’t attacking. It was sniffing the air.

*New Entity Detected: Ashford Municipal Dragon.* *Identity: Registered Asset.* *Status: Here to Collect.*

Gerald looked at the System message.

*Collect?*

He hadn’t ordered anything, and he hadn’t filed a purchase order.

He was being audited.

The dragon opened its mouth.

And Gerald realized the figure hadn’t been trying to kill him, and the figure had been trying to save him.

Because the Inspector General wasn’t coming, and the debt collector was.

And the debt was in Gerald's name.
