Chapter 374: Appeal to the Rat

# Chapter 374

The fluorescent tube in the holding cell flickered at a frequency that violated municipal health code section 4.2, subsection B. Gerald Hutchins counted the flashes.

Three slow pulses.

A stutter. Then total darkness for two seconds. He closed his eyes and counted the dark interval.

One Mississippi.

Two Mississippi. The light returned.

His wrists ached.

The handcuffs were standard issue, but the locking mechanism had a slight defect in the internal tumbler, causing a micro-friction that chafed the skin at an angle consistent with improper maintenance schedules. He could feel the roughness of the denim against his wrists. He could feel the cold steel biting into his ulnar nerve.

It was a distraction. Distractions were inefficient.

A metal tray slid through the slot in the door, and the sound was harsh, scraping against the concrete floor.

Gerald opened his eyes. The tray held a styrofoam cup of lukewarm coffee and a sandwich wrapped in wax paper.

No label. No nutritional information. No expiration date printed in legible font.

Gerald did not eat. He picked up the cup. He inspected the rim.

"Officer," Gerald said. His voice was flat, devoid of the tremor that had plagued him earlier that morning. "This cup lacks a recycling symbol. Is this facility compliant with the Ashford Waste Reduction Ordinance of 2018?"

The guard on the other side of the glass shifted his weight. He did not look at Gerald. He stared at the wall behind Gerald's head.

"Eat the sandwich, Hutchins," the guard muttered.

"I am not eating unlabeled biological matter," Gerald replied. "Where is the ingredient list? Where is the allergen warning? Under what authority do you assume I do not have a peanut allergy?"

"I don't care if you have a peanut allergy. You're a Class-5 Threat. You don't get special treatment."

Class-5 Threat. The designation was absurd.

Gerald was fifty-four years old, and he had a bad knee. He had a mortgage with a fixed rate that had been locked in since 1998. He was not a threat.

He was a nuisance.

The door buzzed. It did not open.

A voice crackled through the intercom, which was not the guard. It was deeper, smoother, and carried the distinct cadence of someone who had memorized the law than just skimmed it.

"Gerald Hutchins. You are being visited by your legal counsel. Please remain seated."

Gerald straightened his spine. He adjusted his tie, which was still ripped and stained with drywall dust.

He looked at the door, which was a steel door, reinforced, with a peephole at eye level.

The hinge pins were visible. They were not secured.

A simple pry bar could remove them.

The door opened.

A man stepped in. He was young, perhaps thirty, wearing a suit that was too large for his frame.

His tie was loose.

His hair was unkempt. He carried a briefcase that looked like it had been dragged through a swamp.

This was Arthur Penhaligon.

Gerald's lawyer. Arthur was a bad lawyer.

He was known for losing cases, for forgetting to file motions, and for once, accidentally advising a client to punch a judge.

But Arthur was also the only person who had ever listened to Gerald's forty-page manifesto on the proper grading of municipal drainage ditches.

Arthur sat down across from Gerald. He placed the briefcase on the table. It emitted a low, grinding hum.

"Don't touch it," Arthur said.

Gerald did not touch it. He looked at Arthur's hands. They were shaking.

"You're here late," Gerald said.

"I'm here because the System won't let anyone else in," Arthur said. He opened the briefcase.

Inside, there were no papers. Instead, the interior of the case was lined with glowing blue runes. Floating above them was a translucent interface, a hologram that flickered in and out of existence. It displayed a single, blinking red box.

**HEARING REQUEST: PENDING.** **SUBJECT: HUTCHINS, GERALD.** **CHARGES: DESTRUCTION OF PUBLIC INFRASTRUCTURE (MAJOR), UNLICENSED CORE MANIPULATION, ASSAULT ON LAW ENFORCEMENT (NON-LETHAL).** **PENALTY: CORE DISSOLUTION AND ETERNAL VOID.**

Gerald stared at the red box. His heart rate remained steady.

He had calculated the probability of this outcome, which was high.

Too high.

"Read it," Gerald said.

Arthur swallowed. He pulled a small, physical notebook from his pocket. It was a spiral-bound journal, the kind used by elementary school teachers. He flipped it open.

"I filed the injunction," Arthur said. "But the System rejected it. It says the destruction of the orb was not a public safety measure because the orb was classified as 'Chaos Artifact,' not 'Public Infrastructure.'"

Gerald frowned. He reached into his pocket. He had no pockets.

He was wearing a jumpsuit. He reached into the fabric of the jumpsuit, finding a hidden seam he had stitched himself while waiting for transport.

He pulled out a small, folded piece of paper, which was a receipt. A parking ticket. From yesterday.

"Show them the ticket," Gerald said.

Arthur looked confused.

"The parking ticket?"

"Look at the timestamp," Gerald said.

"I was parked in the library lot at 8:00 AM. The orb appeared at 8:15 AM.

The destruction occurred at 8:45 AM.

I was on the clock. I was performing an inspection.

"

"That doesn't matter," Arthur said. "The System doesn't care about your employment status. It cares about the damage rating."

"The damage rating is subjective," Gerald said. "And it is flawed."

Gerald pointed to the holographic interface.

"Look at the charge for 'Unlicensed Core Manipulation.' What is the specific violation code?"

Arthur squinted at the blue runes.

"Code 77-Alpha: Unauthorized alteration of dungeon topology without prior filing of Form 11-C."

"Correct," Gerald said. "Now, look at the charge for 'Assault on Law Enforcement.' Code 99-Delta: Physical intimidation of peace officers."

"I don't see the loophole," Arthur said.

Gerald picked up the styrofoam cup. He crushed it in his fist. Dust fell onto the table.

"The orb was not destroyed by me," Gerald said. "It was destroyed by the environment."

Arthur blinked.

"Gerald, you kicked it into a drain."

"I did not kick it. I nudged it.

The drain was already active, and the drain was already a violation.

When I nudged the orb, it fell. The orb did not fall because of my force.

It fell because the drain's suction exceeded the orb's structural integrity. The orb was defective.

"

Arthur stared at him.

"You're arguing the orb was defective?"

"I am arguing that the *drain* was defective," Gerald corrected. "The drain was not rated for solid matter larger than three inches.

The orb was four inches.

The drain failed. The orb fell. The fall caused the crack.

The crack caused the dissolution.

I did not destroy the orb. I reported a malfunctioning piece of municipal infrastructure.

"

Arthur's eyes widened. He looked at the hologram. The red box flickered. A new line of text appeared, scrolling rapidly.

**ANALYZING CLAIM...** **CHECKING INFRASTRUCTURE RECORDS...** **MATCH FOUND: DRAIN 4-B, LIBRARY SUB-BASEMENT. RATED FOR LIQUID ONLY.** **ERROR LOG: ORB INTAKE EXCEEDED CAPACITY.**

Gerald leaned forward.

"The System's own records show the drain was non-compliant. I did not assault the officers. I directed them away from a hazardous area.

I pointed at the officer. I did not touch him.

Pointing is not assault.

It is communication.

"

The hologram pulsed. The red box changed to orange.

**CHARGE 1: REVISED. DAMAGE CAUSED BY INFRASTRUCTURE FAILURE. LIABILITY ASSIGNED TO MUNICIPAL MAINTENANCE DIVISION.** **CHARGE 2: REVIEWING.**

Arthur let out a breath. It was a long, shaky exhale. He rubbed his face with both hands.

"You did it," Arthur whispered. "You actually did it. You cited the city."

"I cited the failure," Gerald said. "The city is not the failure.

The drain is the failure. The city owns the drain. The city is negligent.

I am merely the inspector who noticed.

"

The door buzzed again. This time, it opened fully.

A man walked in, and he was tall. He wore a suit that was perfectly tailored, black, with a silver stripe running down the side of the leg. He did not carry a briefcase.

He did not carry a weapon.

He carried a clipboard.

He looked at Arthur. He looked at Gerald. He looked at the hologram.

"Mr. Penhaligon," the man said. His voice was soft, like velvet dragged over gravel. "Please leave the room."

Arthur stood up. He grabbed his briefcase. He looked at Gerald.

"Gerald," Arthur said.

"Don't let him make you sign anything. Don't sign anything. If they offer you a deal, it's a trap.

It's always a trap.

"

"Go," Gerald said.

Arthur left. The door closed. The lock clicked.

The man with the clipboard walked to the table. He sat down opposite Gerald. He placed the clipboard on the table. It was empty.

"Gerald Hutchins," the man said. "I am Inspector Vane. I am from the Office of the Inspector General."

Gerald looked at the empty clipboard.

"You have no forms," he said.

"I do not need forms," Vane said. "I have the truth."

Vane tapped the table.

The hologram changed.

The orange box disappeared. In its place, a black box appeared.

It was smaller than the previous notifications. It was darker.

**NOTICE OF APPEAL DENIED.** **REASON: JURISDICTIONAL CONFLICT.**

Gerald felt a cold chill run down his spine. It was not fear. It was the sensation of a draft in a poorly sealed window.

"What does that mean?" Gerald asked.

"It means," Vane said, "that while you successfully argued the drain was faulty, you failed to argue the most important point. You are a Dungeon Core.

You are not a citizen.

You are a legal entity separate from the human who housed you. The human, Gerald Hutchins, was arrested. The Core, which we will call 'The Basement,' was not arrested.

The Basement was released.

"

Gerald looked at his hands. They were still handcuffed. But they felt lighter.

"The Basement has been granted provisional status," Vane said. "It is now under the protection of the Inspector General. This means you cannot be destroyed. But it also means you are now under my direct supervision."

Vane picked up the clipboard. He wrote something on it. He tore off the page and slid it across the table.

Gerald looked at the paper. It was a Form 11-C.

**APPLICATION FOR PERMANENT DUNGEON LICENSE.** **CONDITIONS: MUST SUBMIT WEEKLY COMPLIANCE REPORTS. MUST ACCEPT INSPECTIONS WITH 48 HOURS' NOTICE. MUST PAY A TAX OF 15% OF ALL GENERATED LOOT.**

Gerald stared at the form. The tax was exorbitant.

The conditions were burdensome.

But they were legal. They were clear. They were in black and white.

"Why?" Gerald asked.

"Because you are useful," Vane said. "The other Cores are messy.

They are chaotic, and they do not follow the code.

But you... you measure things. You file reports.

You cite violations. You are the only Core in this district who understands the value of structure.

"

Vane leaned forward.

"There is a new threat, Gerald.

The Superior you fought?

They were not alone. There is a network. A guild of chaotic Cores.

They are planning to merge their dungeons, and they are going to create a mega-dungeon. A single, unregulated, lawless zone in the center of Ashford.

It will violate every code in the book.

It will kill thousands. It will destabilize the entire regional System.

"

Gerald's hands clenched. The metal cuffs dug into his wrists. He could feel the pulse in his thumb.

"What do you want?" Gerald asked.

"I want you to audit them," Vane said. "I want you to find the violations.

I want you to issue Citations.

I want you to shut them down.

One by one. By the book.

"

Gerald looked at the Form 11-C. He looked at Vane.

"If I refuse?"

"Then you remain in prison. And the mega-dungeon is built. And the city burns."

Gerald picked up the pen from the table. He did not sign immediately. He examined the ink.

It was blue. He examined the paper.

It was acid-free, and he examined the signature line. It was marked.

He signed his name. His handwriting was neat. His signature was identical to the one on his driver's license, his mortgage documents, and his employee evaluations for the past thirty-one years.

Vane smiled. It was a thin, cruel smile.

"Welcome to the Inspector General's office, Gerald," Vane said. "Your first assignment is due tomorrow."

Vane stood up. He walked to the door. He paused.

"Oh, and one more thing," Vane said. He pointed to the corner of the cell.

In the corner, sitting on the concrete floor, was a rat. It was the same rat from the library. It was still holding the green shard.

But now, the rat was wearing a tiny, black suit.

"The System has assigned you a partner," Vane said. "It's a rat. It's an idiot. But it's up-to-code."

The rat stood up. It bowed.

Gerald looked at the rat. He looked at the form. He looked at Vane.

"Does it have a permit?" Gerald asked.

Vane opened the door.

"It has a license. Barely.

It's running on an expiring visa.

Try not to let it get cited.

"

The door slammed. The lock clicked.

Gerald sat alone with the rat. The rat scurried over to the table. It climbed onto the chair. It sat facing Gerald.

It placed the green shard on the table. Then, it pulled a tiny, rolled-up scroll from its suit pocket. It unrolled it.

Gerald leaned forward. He read the scroll.

It was a citation.

**CITATION #: 001** **ISSUED TO: GERALD HUTCHINS** **VIOLATION: FAILURE TO WEAR PROPER SAFETY GEAR DURING DUNGEON OPERATION.** **FINE: 1 LOOT CRYSTAL.**

Gerald stared at the rat. The rat stared back. It winked.

Gerald's eye twitched. He reached for the pen. He did not pay the fine. He wrote an appeal.
