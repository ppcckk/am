Chapter 52: Eleven Minutes to Propagate

# Chapter 52

The wall groaned again. Not the structural kind Gerald liked. The kind that meant force was going somewhere it shouldn't.

He felt the northeast corridor compress by two centimeters. His spawn list updated in real time. The mold spores were dying. The rat had relocated to the southeast quadrant, which was technically a violation of its own habitat parameters, but Gerald didn't have time to care.

Margaret's Citation was propagating. He could feel it.

The debuff rippling outward from the target Core's position like a pressure wave through water.

Four Major violations. Seventy-two percent. The math was clean.

The execution was not.

The wall groaned again. Another centimeter.

He pulled up the propagation timer. Fourteen minutes until the debuff reached full effect. Fourteen minutes during which Margaret's spawns were still at full strength, still pressing against his northeast corridor with the force of a Core that had not yet felt the weight of its own non-compliance.

He had fourteen minutes to hold.

Gerald pulled up his spawn list. Three mold spores.

One water leak.

One rat. He had also, over the past thirty-one chapters of his new life, added a corridor guard (a reanimated piece of drywall that moved when someone touched it), two electrical hazards that were technically compliant with the System's definition of hazardous condition and therefore counted as legitimate spawns, and a handrail that he had installed personally and which the System had, to his genuine surprise, classified as a defensive structure.

The handrail was doing its job. It was in the northeast corridor.

Margaret's spawn, a serpentine thing made of compressed soil and broken ceramic, had hit it three times and been thrown back each time.

The handrail's compliance rating was 94, which was, technically, the most compliant object in Gerald's dungeon.

But the handrail was one object. Margaret had eleven spawns in the northeast quadrant. The soil-serpent was just the first one to reach the corridor, and the others were behind it, compressing the earth, pushing.

Gerald opened his Citation Authority panel. 49/70. Twenty-one more.

He opened the audit notification. 71:43:12. He had lost sixteen minutes since submission. The clock did not care about his wall.

He needed to solve the audit problem. But he could not solve the audit problem while Margaret was still trying to crush his northeast corridor.

He made a decision. He would hold the corridor with the handrail and the electrical hazards, and he would research the audit while the wall held.

He opened the System's regulatory documentation. He had read it four times. He was about to read it a fifth.

The relevant section was buried under a heading called Inter-Core Citation Interaction Protocols, subsection 7.3, which Gerald had previously skimmed because it appeared to deal with simultaneous multi-Core Citations. It was not that. It was worse.

"Coordinated Citation Activity" was defined as any instance in which two Cores with a Compliance Rating differential of 38 or more points engage in Citation-related interaction within a 24-hour window. The trigger was not the Citation itself. The trigger was the *interaction*.

The message Margaret had sent. The attached manifest.

The fact that she had identified her own violations and forwarded them to him.

Gerald had been an idiot. He had known the rating differential rule. He had known Margaret was below 50.

He had known he was above 88.

He had submitted the Citation anyway because Margaret was going to die and he had not thought about the interaction clause.

He scrolled down. A mitigation provision existed. Subsection 7.3.1: "Self-Reported Compliance Deficiencies filed prior to or concurrent with the triggering Citation shall reclassify the interaction as Internal Compliance Review, voiding the audit trigger."

A Self-Report, and he could file a Citation on himself. If he did it before the audit completed, the System would reclassify the whole thing as him reviewing his own compliance. No audit.

No Inspector General.

The cost was permanent.

A Self-Reported violation was not a temporary debuff.

It was a permanent reduction to his Compliance Rating, and he would be admitting, on the record, that his dungeon was deficient.

The System would hold him to it. His rating would drop. His spawns would be weaker.

Forever, and he did the math.

He needed to self-report enough violations to reclassify the interaction. The documentation did not specify a minimum. It said "deficiencies," plural.

At least two.

Possibly more, and he looked at his own dungeon.

He looked at his Compliance Rating. 89. He had worked for that number. Thirty-one chapters of measuring, installing, filing.

He had gone from 12 to 89. Every point had cost him something.

He could not afford to lose points.

His spawn list was already pathetic.

A rat. Mold.

A handrail. If his rating dropped below 80, the handrail's compliance bonus would degrade. It would stop being a defensive structure.

The northeast corridor would have nothing.

But if he did not self-report, the Inspector General would arrive in seventy-one hours and find him in violation of the Inter-Core Interaction Protocol.

The penalty for that was not a debuff. It was Citation Authority revocation. Permanent.

He would never cite another Core again, and he needed two violations. He needed to find two that were real, that he could self-report, and that would not immediately collapse his dungeon.

He started walking.

The northeast corridor was holding. The handrail had caught a second soil-serpent and thrown it into the electrical hazard, which had discharged with a sound like a staple gun and reduced the serpent to a pile of vitrified glass.

Margaret's spawns were adapting, and they were learning the corridor's geometry; the third one was going to come around the handrail.

Gerald walked the southeast quadrant. He checked the water leak.

It was compliant.

He had installed a proper drainage channel with a 2% slope, a cleanout access point, and a vent stack.

The System gave him 96 for plumbing. He could not self-report plumbing.

He checked the electrical panel. 91. He had used the correct gauge wire, proper junction boxes, GFCI protection in wet areas. No violations there.

He checked the fire suppression system. It was a bucket. He had filled it with water and placed it next to a fire extinguisher he had found in the library's storage room.

The System had given him 74 for fire safety, which was generous. The bucket was not a suppression system.

It was a bucket.

But the System had graded it as a suppression system because it contained water and was placed within ten feet of an ignition source.

That was a violation, and he could self-report it. It would cost him 74 points in fire safety.

His overall rating would drop.

His fire resistance passive would degrade.

But he had no fire spawns. He had no enemies who used fire. The fire resistance passive was currently providing zero practical benefit.

He flagged it. One violation down.

He needed one more, and he walked the west corridor. He checked the ventilation.

He had installed a passive ventilation system using the library's original HVAC ductwork, which he had accessed through a sealed crawlspace.

The System had given him 82 for ventilation.

It was adequate. Not great.

Adequate, and he could not self-report ventilation without dropping below the threshold that kept his mold spores alive. The mold needed the ventilation. If he reported it as deficient, the System would correct it, and the correction would increase airflow beyond what the mold could tolerate.

He needed something else.

He stood in the center of his dungeon and looked at the ceiling. The library floor was above him.

He could feel it.

The weight of the stacks, the weight of the people walking above, the weight of the building itself. He had never checked the load path between the library and his dungeon. He had assumed the library's foundation transferred to the earth, and his dungeon was simply a cavity in the earth.

He pulled up the structural documentation, and he had filed it in chapter fourteen. He had used the library's original foundation plans, which he had found in the municipal archives.

He had confirmed that his dungeon sat below the foundation's depth.

He had confirmed that the library's footings were above his ceiling.

He looked at the numbers again.

The library's footings were at a depth of 1.8 meters. His ceiling was at a depth of 2.1 meters. The gap between them was 0.3 meters. That was the thickness of his ceiling slab.

He had assumed the ceiling slab was a simple concrete pour. He had assumed it was uniform. He had never actually measured it from below.

He looked up. The ceiling was smooth.

Grey.

Unremarkable, and he had looked at it a hundred times.

He pulled out his measuring tool. He pressed it against the ceiling and took a reading.

2.1 meters from the floor. Correct.

He moved three meters east. Took another reading.

2.1 meters, and he moved three meters north.

2.4 meters.

He stopped. The ceiling was thicker in the north section. Three centimeters more than the south section.

That was not a uniform pour. The slab had variable thickness, causing uneven load distribution and creating a point of concentrated stress in the north section where thin met thick.

He had never flagged it. He had never checked it. He had assumed uniformity because the library's plans showed a uniform foundation.

But the library's plans were from 1974, and the building had been renovated in 1991. Gerald knew this. He had inspected the 1991 renovation permits.

He had never cross-referenced them with his dungeon's ceiling because his dungeon did not exist in 1991.

The ceiling was not what the plans said it was. The load path was not what he had assumed. A stress concentration point sat in the ceiling's north section. If Margaret's spawns pressed hard enough against the north wall, they could transfer force upward through the slab into the library's foundation.

He could self-report this. It was a real violation.

Structural, Major category.

It would cost him 40 points in structural compliance. His physical damage reduction passive would degrade by 40%.

But it was real. He had missed it.

He flagged it. Two violations. Enough to reclassify.

He opened the Self-Report form. He selected the fire suppression violation. He selected the structural load path violation. He hovered over Submit.

A notification appeared. Not from Margaret. Not from the System.

From a Core he did not know.

"CITATION SERVED. Violation: Unauthorized Structural Modification (Major).

Citation Authority: Core 6114.

Target: Core 1.

You have been cited. Appeal window: 48 hours.

Note: Target is currently under Regulatory Supremacy Audit. Appeal rights suspended per Section 7.3.4."

Gerald read it twice.

Core 6114 had cited him. For unauthorized structural modification.

He had modified his own ceiling, and he had added a handrail, a drainage channel, an electrical panel. He had done all of it with permits he had filed himself, and the citation was not about those things.

It was about the ceiling.

About the load path. About the fact that Gerald had, in chapter fourteen, installed a support column in the center of his dungeon without checking whether the ceiling slab above it could bear the additional point load.

He had done it in chapter fourteen. He had never checked.

And now Core 6114 knew. Core 6114 had been watching. Core 6114 had filed a Citation using a violation that Gerald himself had not identified.

The citation would debuff him.

Major violation. 18% reduction to all spawns. The handrail would go from 94 compliance to 77. The electrical hazards would degrade.

The mold would die.

And his appeal rights were suspended. Because of the audit. Because of the audit he was about to void.

Gerald looked at the Self-Report form. He looked at the Citation from Core 6114. He looked at the audit timer. 71:19:44.

If he submitted the Self-Report, the audit would be voided. His appeal rights would be restored. He could appeal Core 6114's Citation.

But the Self-Report would permanently lower his rating, and the Citation from Core 6114 would apply immediately, before the appeal. He would take the debuff. He would take it for 48 hours until he could appeal.

Forty-eight hours with an 18% debuff. With Margaret still pressing. With his northeast corridor held by a handrail that would drop to 77 compliance.

Or he could submit the Citation first. Void the audit. Restore his appeal rights. Then appeal Core 6114's Citation and win.

But the appeal required a Hearing.

A Hearing required him to argue structural logic against Core 6114.

And the citation was about his ceiling. About a load path he had not verified.

About a support column he had installed without checking, and he did not know if he could win that argument.

The northeast wall groaned. The handrail caught a fourth spawn. The electrical hazard discharged. The wall held.

Gerald looked at the Self-Report form. He looked at the Citation from Core 6114. He looked at the ceiling above the support column.

He had never measured the slab thickness at that point, and he pressed his tool against the ceiling, directly above the column.

The reading came back.

0.9 meters.

The slab was half the thickness he had assumed. The column was bearing a point load on a section of ceiling that was already the weakest part of his dungeon.

The stress concentration was not theoretical, which was already happening. The library above was already loading down through that thin section, and the column was adding to it.

If he appealed the Citation and lost, the System would remove the column. The library's foundation would transfer its load directly onto his ceiling slab at a point that was 0.9 meters thick instead of 2.1. The slab would crack. The library above would settle.

If he did not appeal, the Citation's debuff would weaken the column by 18%. It would still stand. But it would be weaker. And the stress would continue to accumulate.

Gerald opened the Hearing preparation panel, and he pulled up the structural calculations. He pulled up the 1991 renovation permits.

He pulled up the original 1974 foundation plans.

He pulled up the load calculations for the library's east wing.

He had six hours to build an argument that would prove the column was structurally sound. He had a ceiling that was not what the plans said it was. He had a rival Core who had watched him for thirty-eight chapters and found the one thing he had missed.

He opened a new file. He titled it: Hearing Brief, Core 1 vs. Core 6114.

He typed the first line.

"I am not going to pretend the column is compliant. I am going to prove that removing it would cause a greater violation."

The northeast wall groaned. The handrail caught a fifth spawn.

Margaret's pressure was increasing, and the debuff had not yet reached full propagation.

He had eleven minutes.

He kept typing.
