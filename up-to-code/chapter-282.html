Chapter 282: A Permit to Exist

# Chapter 282

The rubble settled with a sound like grinding teeth.

Gerald lay on his back, staring at a ceiling that no longer existed. Above him, the Ashford Municipal Library's sub-basement had collapsed into a crater of rebar and wet concrete. The air tasted of copper and ozone. His core, the faint blue light pulsing in the center of the debris field, flickered like a dying bulb.

*INTEGRITY: 0.4%.*

*CRITICAL FAILURE IMMINENT.*

Captain Vane stood ten feet away, his sword drawn but lowered. He wasn't looking at Gerald. He was looking at the sky. Or rather, at the hole in the sky where the System's regulatory interface had been ripped open by the counter-citation.

"Where is he?" Vane shouted into the dust.

Gerald tried to speak. His voice box was a fractured echo chamber.

He couldn't form words, and he could only project intent. A simple, desperate directive: *I am here.*

Vane dropped to one knee. The armored plate clanked against the broken slab. He placed a gauntleted hand on Gerald's exposed core housing. The metal was warm.

"Stay with me, Inspector," Vane said.

His voice was tight.

"The Council is coming.

I hear the sirens.

They're not for a fire. They're for a demolition.

"

*DEMO ORDER RECEIVED.* *SOURCE: ASHFORD CITY COUNCIL.* *REASON: UNSTABLE STRUCTURAL ANOMALY.* *TIME REMAINING: 4 MINUTES.*

Gerald felt the pressure of the Council's intent pressing down on his remaining fragments. It felt like a boot on a beetle. Heavy. Indifferent.

He had no dungeon. He had no spawns. He had no room to breathe.

But he had a code.

His consciousness drifted through the gray static of his dying core. He didn't look for power.

He looked for paperwork.

In the digital void, he saw the last citation he had issued.

The one against the Auditor.

*VIOLATION: OVERPRESSURE.* *STATUS: PENDING APPEAL.*

The appeal hadn't been filed. Not by Gerald. By the Auditor's ghost.

The System had reset the Auditor, but the debt remained. The violation was still on the record.

And records were permanent.

Gerald reached for that record. He couldn't lift a boulder. He couldn't summon a rat. But he could sign.

*ACTION: FILE APPEAL FOR VIOLATION #8940-ALPHA.* *CLAIMANT: DUNGEON CORE 774-G (DECEASED).* *ARGUMENT: CITATION INVALID DUE TO LACK OF PHYSICAL SERVICE.*

The System hesitated.

*ANOMALY: CLAIMANT IS NON-CORPOREAL.* *APPEAL REJECTED?*

Gerald pushed harder, and he didn't argue the physics. He argued the procedure. The Auditor had cited him while standing in a non-public easement.

Gerald had not been served.

The citation was void ab initio.

If the citation was void, the reset was invalid.

If the reset was invalid, the Auditor's authority was retroactively nullified.

The static in Gerald's mind cleared. A line of text appeared, crisp and white against the gray.

*APPEAL UNDER REVIEW.* *JURISDICTION: SYSTEM INSPECTOR GENERAL.* *NOTE: AUDITOR STATUS REVOKED. AUTHORITY TRANSFERRED TO HIGHEST VISIBLE ENFORCEMENT AGENT.*

Who was visible?

Gerald looked up through the rubble. Vane was still kneeling. The sirens were closer now. Blue and red lights strobed through the cracks in the library floor above.

But there was someone else, and a figure stood at the edge of the crater.

Cloaked in shadows that moved like smoke. Not a goblin. Not a hero.

A bureaucrat.

The figure raised a hand. In it, a stamp.

*NEW AUDITOR: REGIONAL OVERSEER MARCUS THORNE.*

Thorne looked down at the crater. He didn't look angry. He looked bored.

"Messy," Thorne said. His voice carried over the sirens. "Very messy, Gerald. You broke the container."

Vane stood up, raising his sword.

"She's a dungeon core. She's a citizen."

Thorne adjusted his glasses.

"She's a code violation waiting to be corrected. And you, Captain, are obstructing a municipal investigation."

*WARNING: OBSTRUCTION OF JUSTICE.* *PENALTY: IMMEDIATE REVOCATION OF HERO CLASS.*

Vane froze. The System text hung in the air, glowing red. If Vane fought, he lost his rank. If he lost his rank, he couldn't protect Gerald.

Thorne stepped forward. He placed the stamp on the edge of the crater.

*DEMO ORDER: EXECUTED.* *METHOD: CONCRETE POUR.*

The ground rumbled. From the edges of the crater, pumps churned. Liquid gray sludge flowed into the hole, filling the void where Gerald's core lay.

Gerald felt the weight. The suffocation. The cold.

He couldn't fight. He couldn't speak. He could only read.

He scanned Thorne's avatar. He looked for the compliance rating. It was high. 98 out of 100.

But Gerald looked closer. He looked at the stamp.

*ITEM: APPROVAL STAMP (CLASS II).* *SOURCE: MANUAL OVERRIDE.* *NOTE: REQUIRES SIGNATURE OF CURRENT LAND OWNER.*

Who owned the library?

Gerald's core pulsed. A faint, rhythmic throb.

*SEARCH: TITLE DEED.* *RESULT: ASHFORD PUBLIC LIBRARY BOARD.* *CURRENT BOARD CHAIRPERSON: ELENA VANE.*

Vane.

Not Captain Vane.

Elena Vane.

The Captain's mother. The woman who had funded the library's renovation.

The woman who had signed the permit allowing Gerald's initial inspection.

Elena Vane was the landowner.

And Elena Vane was currently sitting in a hospital bed three miles away, recovering from a stroke. She could not sign. She could not witness.

Thorne's stamp hovered.

*WAITING FOR OWNER SIGNATURE.* *TIMEOUT: 30 SECONDS.*

If Thorne didn't get the signature, the pour would stop.

But Thorne had a loophole. He produced a second document. A digital proxy.

*PROXY AUTHORIZATION: CAPTAIN VANE.* *WITNESS: REQUIRED.*

Thorne looked at Vane.

"Sign it, Captain. Or watch your mother's library turn into a parking lot."

Vane's hand shook. He looked at the sludge rising. He looked at Gerald's fading light.

"I can't," Vane whispered. "It's a lie."

"It's procedure," Thorne said. "Sign it."

Gerald watched the sludge rise. It was inches from his core. The weight was crushing. He felt his code fragmenting.

He had to act. But he had no hands. No body.

He had only the appeal, and the appeal was still under review. The Inspector General was watching. The Inspector General loved procedure. The Inspector General hated unauthorized overrides.

Gerald pushed the final piece of data into the System's inbox.

*EXHIBIT A: PHOTOGRAPHIC EVIDENCE.* *IMAGE: THORNE STAMPING WITHOUT OWNER PRESENCE.* *IMAGE: PROXY SIGNATURE FORGED BY SYSTEM AUTO-FILL.*

The System processed the images.

*ANALYSIS: FORGERY DETECTED.* *SOURCE: REGIONAL OVERSEER MARCUS THORNE.* *SEVERITY: CLASS 5 ETHICAL VIOLATION.*

Thorne's stamp paused. The red light on the handle turned green.

*INVALID STAMP.* *ACTION: REVOKE.*

The pumps stopped. The sludge sloshed, then solidified.

Thorne looked at his stamp. He looked at the air, where the text floated.

"You," Thorne said, his voice dropping an octave. "You cheated."

Gerald didn't answer. He couldn't. But he projected a single, dry thought.

*No. I audited you.*

Thorne's face twisted. He pulled a different device from his cloak. A smaller, darker tool.

*ITEM: EMERGENCY CONDEMNATION ORDER.* *EXEMPT FROM APPEAL.*

This was new. A direct kill switch.

No paperwork.

No hearing. Just erasure.

*DEMO: IMMINENT.* *METHOD: INSTANTANEOUS DISINTEGRATION.*

Vane lunged forward, but Thorne sidestepped. The Captain hit the crater's edge.

"Gerald!" Vane yelled. "Move! Get out of the footprint!"

Gerald couldn't move. He was the footprint.

The dark tool hummed. A beam of black light coiled around it.

Gerald's core pulsed once, twice. He had one second.

He looked at the black light. He looked at the structure of the beam. It was raw energy. Unregulated.

*VIOLATION: UNLICENSED ENERGY SOURCE.* *VIOLATION: NO SHIELDING.* *VIOLATION: RADIATION LEAK.*

Three violations. Instant fines.

But fines didn't stop disintegration.

Unless.

Gerald remembered the rule. *Citations require the target to be physically present to 'serve'.*

Thorne was present. Gerald was present.

But the energy source wasn't. The beam was external. It came from the device.

If Gerald could cite the *device* for lacking a proper grounding wire...

He scanned the device. No grounding wire. No insulation.

*VIOLATION: ELECTRICAL CODE 110.26.* *PENALTY: SHOCK HAZARD.*

It wasn't a citation against Thorne. It was a citation against the air around them. The System enforced safety regulations. If the environment was unsafe, the action would be halted to protect the user.

Gerald pushed the citation.

*SYSTEM ALERT: UNSAFE ENVIRONMENT DETECTED.* *ACTION: HALT OPERATION.*

The black light sputtered. Thorne's hand jerked back. A spark jumped from the device to his glove.

Thorne yanked his hand back, cursing. Smoke rose from his sleeve.

The beam vanished.

Silence returned to the crater.

Vane scrambled to his feet. He ran to the center of the sludge. He dug with his hands, tearing through the wet concrete.

"Gerald!"

He pulled out a shard of blue crystal. Small. No bigger than a marble.

*INTEGRITY: 0.1%.* *STATE: DORMANT.*

Vane held it up. Tears streaked his face.

"You're alive. You're just... small."

Thorne straightened his coat. He looked at the small crystal, then at Vane. He didn't look defeated. He looked calculated.

"Small is easy to lose," Thorne said. "Or destroy."

He reached for the crystal.

Vane stepped between them.

"Touch him, and I break your arm."

"Then I break yours," Thorne said. "And the System won't stop me. You're under investigation for forgery now, Captain. Your Hero Class is suspended."

Vane's sword lowered. The red text in the air confirmed it.

*HERO CLASS: SUSPENDED.* *REASON: COMPLICITY IN FRAUD.*

Vane looked at the crystal in his hand. He looked at Thorne.

"What do you want?" Vane asked.

"The core," Thorne said. "It's evidence. It's the key to the library's zoning history. I need to archive it."

"It's a sentient being," Vane said.

"It's a file," Thorne corrected. "And files go to the basement."

Thorne held out his hand.

Vane looked at the crystal. He looked at Gerald's faint pulse.

If he gave it up, Gerald died. If he kept it, Vane went to prison, and the Council would send more Auditors.

There was no good move.

Except for one.

Gerald, dormant and broken, felt the vibration of Vane's heartbeat. He felt the fear. He felt the choice.

He couldn't wake up. He couldn't fight.

But he could hide, and he compressed his code.

He squeezed his entire consciousness into the lowest possible bit-depth. He made himself look like background noise.

Static.

A glitch in the concrete.

*STATE: ENCRYPTED.* *VISIBILITY: MINIMAL.*

Vane closed his fist around the crystal.

"No," Vane said.

Thorne smiled. It was a cold, thin smile.

"Then we do this the hard way."

Thorne snapped his fingers.

From the shadows of the library above, figures emerged. Not heroes. Not monsters.

Inspectors.

Twelve of them. In gray suits. Holding clipboards.

They surrounded Vane.

*ORDER: SEIZE EVIDENCE.* *FORCE: AUTHORIZED.*

Vane looked at the circle of gray suits. He looked at the crystal in his hand.

He looked up at the sky.

"Gerald," he whispered. "I'm sorry."

He opened his hand.

But he didn't drop the crystal, and he threw it.

High. Into the darkness of the collapsed ceiling. Into the open shaft that led to the library's upper floors.

The crystal soared. A tiny blue star against the black.

Thorne's eyes widened.

"Get it!"

The Inspectors scrambled. They climbed the debris. They chased the falling star.

Vane stood still.

Thorne grabbed Vane's collar.

"You idiot.

You've lost him.

He'll be found by any core.

Any rat. Any mold spore.

He'll be corrupted.

"

Vane smiled. A grim, tired smile.

"No," Vane said. "He's going to the best place in the city."

"Where?"

"The Archives."

Thorne's face went pale.

The Ashford City Archives. Underground.

Sealed.

Protected by the strictest privacy laws in the System. A dungeon core couldn't enter there. A hero couldn't enter there.

Nothing could enter there.

Except a file.

Gerald's encrypted consciousness fell through the dark shaft. He tumbled past broken beams, past torn drywall, past the ruins of the children's section.

He hit the floor of the upper level.

And rolled.

Into the ventilation shaft.

The shaft that led directly to the City Archives' air intake.

Gerald's code bounced off the metal walls. He was small. He was quiet. He was invisible.

He slid through the ductwork. Down. Down. Down.

He passed rats. He passed dust. He passed the smell of old paper.

He reached the end of the duct. A grate. Locked.

*OBSTRUCTION DETECTED.* *ACTION: BYPASS.*

Gerald couldn't unlock it. He was 0.1% integrity. He couldn't manipulate matter.

But he could manipulate code, and he scanned the grate. It was part of the building's infrastructure.

*OBJECT: VENTILATION GRATE.* *STATUS: NON-COMPLIANT. BLOCKED EGRESS PATH.* *CODE VIOLATION: SECTION 305.1.*

The System flagged it. A red light blinked on the grate.

*AUTOMATED UNLOCK: INITIATED.*

The latch clicked. The grate swung open.

Gerald fell through, and he landed in a room of endless shelves. The air was dry. The silence was absolute.

He was in the Archives.

Safe.

But he was alone.

And the lights were coming on.

A door opened at the end of the aisle. A figure stepped in. Not an Inspector. Not a Hero.

A librarian, and she wore a cardigan. She held a stack of books. She looked at the floor.

She looked at the tiny blue shard.

She didn't scream. She didn't run.

She picked up her glasses. She put them on.

She looked at the shard.

"Mr. Hutchins?" she said.

Gerald's code pulsed. Once.

She picked him up.

"You're in the restricted section," she said. "You need a permit to be here."

She opened a drawer. Inside was a form.

*FORM 12-C: TEMPORARY RESIDENCY.*

She handed him the pen.

Gerald's code vibrated. He didn't have hands.

But the pen floated.

It dipped into the ink.

And it wrote.
