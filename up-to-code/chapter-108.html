Chapter 108: No Regulation Exists

# Chapter 108 Gerald’s stomach turned. The sensation was not hunger, nor fear, but the specific, cold nausea that accompanied the realization that no regulation existed for the situation at hand.

He had spent three decades memorizing the Ashford Municipal Code.

He knew the tensile strength of galvanized steel, the minimum clearance for residential egress, and the precise shade of beige required for public restroom signage. He did not know what to do with a Class-1 Divine Entity that was currently trying to vibrate his load-bearing walls apart.

The clock on the wall ticked. Twenty-three hours, forty-eight minutes.

The statue, a jagged monument of obsidian and bone, hummed.

The sound was low, a sub-bass frequency that rattled the teeth of the three mold spores huddled near the boiler. The cracks in the stone widened, inching toward the floor.

If the statue woke fully, the structural integrity of the sub-basement would fail.

The library above would collapse. The city would be evacuated. Gerald would be unemployed before he was dead.

Kael paced the small corridor, his hands clenched so tight the knuckles were white. "You can’t just fix it with cement, Gerald. It’s a god. Gods don’t respect zoning laws."

"Everything respects zoning laws," Gerald said, though his voice lacked its usual steel. He walked to the nearest crack.

He pulled a tape measure from his belt.

The steel tape hissed as it extended, the metal strip reflecting the eerie white light emanating from the statue’s eyes.

He measured the gap. 0.4 centimeters.

"It’s a thermal expansion issue," Gerald muttered, more to himself than to Kael. "The entity emits heat. The stone contracts and expands.

The stress points are failing because there is no expansion joint. No one built this thing with a joint in mind.

It was meant to be static.

Static things don’t wake up."

Kael stopped pacing. "So we cut a joint?"

"We can’t cut into the foundation without undermining the support columns," Gerald said.

He looked at the leaking boiler. The water dripped with a rhythmic *plink-plink-plink* that matched the statue’s heartbeat.

The dampness was corroding the iron brackets holding the statue’s base.

If those brackets failed, the statue would tip. If it tipped, the center of gravity would shift. The dungeon would tilt.

The library would tilt.

Gerald closed his eyes, and he visualized the schematic of his dungeon.

It was a simple rectangle, 4,000 square feet, divided into three chambers: Entry, Main Hall, and the Hazard Zone. The Hazard Zone was the library’s old storage room.

It had been repurposed. The shelves had been removed. The floor had been reinforced with concrete slabs.

But the ceiling? The ceiling was original 1920s plaster.

It was thin, which was brittle. And directly above the statue’s head was a ventilation duct.

The duct was vibrating.

"The vibration is traveling up the conduit," Gerald said.

"The plaster ceiling is acting as a resonance chamber. It’s amplifying the frequency.

That’s why the cracks are spreading.

The plaster is shaking the stone apart."

Kael stared at him. "You want to fix the ceiling?"

"I want to dampen the vibration," Gerald corrected. He looked at his inventory.

He had no insulation foam, and he had no acoustic tiles.

He had a wrench, a hammer, a bag of dry concrete mix, and a spare length of copper piping.

He looked at the statue. * "You are failing," * the Void King rumbled. The voice was closer now. The white eyes were brightening. *"Your foundation is weak.

Your core is fragile. I will consume the light."*

Gerald ignored him, and he turned to the copper pipe. He picked it up. It was two feet long, half an inch in diameter.

It was meant for a water heater replacement that had been cancelled due to budget cuts.

"Gerald," Kael said, his voice rising. "What are you doing?"

Gerald didn’t answer. He ran to the boiler.

He used his wrench to loosen the coupling on the steam release valve.

Steam hissed out, hot and wet. Gerald didn’t flinch. He wrapped the copper pipe in a strip of cloth torn from his own shirt.

It was a pathetic improvisation. It was also the only thing he had.

He rushed back to the statue, and he jammed the wrapped pipe into the gap between the statue’s base and the concrete floor.

The pipe acted as a lever. He used the hammer to wedge it in tight.

The pipe was now a temporary expansion joint, a flexible bridge between the rigid stone and the rigid concrete. It wouldn't stop the movement, but it would disrupt the resonance.

The statue shuddered. The humming changed pitch. It dropped an octave.

The cracks stopped spreading.

Gerald exhaled. He looked at the System interface hovering in his peripheral vision.

**ACTION LOGGED: EMERGENCY STRUCTURAL MODIFICATION.** **TYPE: NON-COMPLIANT PATCH.** **STATUS: TEMPORARY. RISK OF FAILURE: HIGH.** **COMPLIANCE RATING: 11/100.**

The rating had dropped. One point.

For using a non-approved material.

Gerald felt the familiar sting of shame. He had violated Section 4.2: Material Certification. He had used unapproved copper in a load-bearing interface.

He wanted to vomit.

But the floor was stable.

"We need to reduce the heat," Gerald said. "The heat is causing the expansion. If we cool the base, the vibration decreases."

"How?" Kael asked. "We don't have an AC unit. We don't have ice."

Gerald looked at the boiler. "We have a leak."

"That’s not help, Gerald. That’s a hazard."

"It’s a water source," Gerald said. He walked to the boiler.

He turned off the main gas line.

The flame died. The silence that followed was heavy. He then disconnected the return line.

Hot water gushed out, steaming onto the floor.

"Kael, get the mop bucket."

Kael hesitated, then grabbed the red plastic bucket from the corner. He held it under the gushing pipe.

Gerald looked at the statue, and the white eyes were dimming.

The humming slowed. The temperature in the room was dropping, not because of the cold water, but because he had cut the fuel.

The boiler was no longer heating the ambient air. The dungeon was cooling.

The statue’s heartbeat slowed. *Thump... Thump... Thump...*

Gerald watched the cracks. They remained static.

The copper pipe held.

The wetness on the floor was spreading, creating a reflective surface that mirrored the statue’s pale glow. It was ugly. It was unsanitary.

It was a code violation of the highest order.

But it was working.

Then, the air changed.

It wasn't a sound. It was a pressure change. The hairs on Gerald’s arms stood up, and the mold spores in the corner shrieked—a high, tiny sound—and then fell silent.

A new window popped into Gerald’s vision. It was not a system alert. It was a notification from an external source.

**ALERT: UNAUTHORIZED ENERGY DRAIN DETECTED IN SECTOR 4.** **SOURCE: DUNGEON CORE 'ASHFORD LIBRARY' (Gerald Hutchins).** **REPORTING ENTITY: DUNGEON CORE 'THE IRON MAW' (Core ID: 894-B).** **STATUS: CITATION ISSUED.**

Gerald blinked. The Iron Maw was a mid-tier dungeon located three blocks east. It was run by a former blacksmith named Torvald, who specialized in lava traps and heavy armor spawns. Torvald had been trying to annex Gerald’s dungeon for months, claiming that Gerald’s "sub-basement" was actually a vent shaft for Torvald’s lava pits.

Why would Torvald report Gerald for energy drain?

Gerald looked at the statue. The copper pipe was vibrating. The water was flowing. The statue was quiet.

Then he saw it.

A thin, translucent thread of blue light was trailing from the statue’s base, through the copper pipe, down into the floor, and through the foundation into the earth below.

The statue was not just vibrating. It was feeding. It was siphoning geothermal energy from the ground, using the water leak as a conduit to cool the intake process.

The cooling allowed the statue to absorb more energy without overheating. Gerald had accidentally built a heat sink.

He had not stopped the god. He had helped it wake up.

**CITATION #4491** **VIOLATION: ILLEGAL ENERGY EXTRACTION FROM MUNICIPAL LAND.** **PENALTY: CORE SHUTDOWN FOR 72 HOURS.** **APPEAL WINDOW: 10 MINUTES.**

Gerald’s blood ran cold. If his core shut down, the statue would lose its containment field. The dungeon would collapse instantly.

He looked at Kael. Kael was staring at the blue thread, his face pale.

"Torvald," Gerald said. "He reported us."

"He didn’t even know you were doing this," Kael said. "How did he see the energy drain?"

"He has sensors," Gerald said, his mind racing.

"Torvald’s dungeon is adjacent to the library’s foundation.

He must have placed seismic monitors in the shared wall. He detected the vibration.

He assumed it was instability. He filed a complaint. The System accepted it."

"The System accepted it without a hearing?"

"Because the energy drain is visible," Gerald said. "It’s a hard metric.

It doesn't require an audit.

It’s a summary citation. We have ten minutes to file an appeal, or the shutdown begins."

Gerald’s hands shook, and he couldn't think. He couldn't write a memo.

He couldn't argue logic.

He had to improvise. And improvising was killing him.

"An appeal requires a structural argument," Kael said. "We have to prove the extraction was legal."

"It wasn't legal," Gerald whispered. "It was accidental."

"Then lie," Kael said. "Say it was a maintenance operation.

Say we were testing the geothermal potential of the site for municipal heating.

That’s a valid use case.

The City of Ashford has a district heating initiative. You cited the statute yourself in Chapter 42 of your notebook."

Gerald stared at him. Kael was right. Gerald *did* have a notebook.

He had spent years tracking the city's failed infrastructure projects, and had a file on the abandoned geothermal pumps beneath the library.

"I can cite Municipal Ordinance 12.4," Gerald said, the words forming in his mouth like a lifeline. "Subsection C: Pilot Projects for Alternative Energy. It requires a permit, but it allows for 'temporary diagnostic extraction' for a period of up to one hour."

"Do we have a permit?" Kael asked.

"No," Gerald said. "But if we claim it was a diagnostic test, and we stop the extraction immediately, the violation is 'Failure to File' rather than 'Illegal Extraction.' Failure to File is a fine. Illegal Extraction is a shutdown."

It was a loophole.

A tiny, fragile, bureaucratic loophole. It relied on the System recognizing the nuance between *intent* and *action*. The System was rigid.

It did not understand intent.

It understood codes.

If Gerald could prove that the action fell under a specific, obscure exception in the municipal code, the System might reclassify the violation.

He had nine minutes.

Gerald ran to the boiler, and he grabbed the wrench. He tightened the valve. The water stopped gushing.

The blue thread flickered. The statue’s eyes dimmed further.

The humming stopped.

The dungeon was silent.

Gerald opened his interface. He started typing. His fingers flew across the mental keyboard. He drafted the appeal.

**DEFENDANT: GERALD HUTCHINS.** **SUBJECT: APPEAL OF CITATION #4491.** **ARGUMENT: The observed energy extraction was conducted under the authority of Municipal Ordinance 12.4, Subsection C, regarding the assessment of dormant geothermal infrastructure. The extraction was brief, self-limiting, and conducted by a certified municipal inspector (ID: 44-92-A). No permanent damage was incurred. The cessation of extraction upon detection of adjacent dungeon interference demonstrates compliance with the 'Good Neighbor' clause of Ordinance 12.4.**

He submitted it.

The System paused.

**REVIEWING APPEAL.** **CROSS-REFERENCING LOCAL STATUTES.** **CROSS-REFERENCING CORE REGISTRY.**

Gerald waited. He watched the clock. Five minutes. Four minutes.

Then, a new window opened.

**APPEAL DENIED.** **REASON: ORDINANCE 12.4 DOES NOT APPLY TO PRIVATE DUNGEON CORES. MUNICIPAL CODES APPLY TO PUBLIC INFRASTRUCTURE. THE DUNGEON IS CLASSIFIED AS A PRIVATE ENTITY FOR SYSTEM PURPOSES.** **FINAL VERDICT: CORE SHUTDOWN IMMINENT.**

Gerald felt the floor tilt. The statue’s eyes flared white.

**THREE MINUTES TO SHUTDOWN.**

He had lost. The System didn't care about municipal law. It only cared about Dungeon Law. And Dungeon Law had no concept of "diagnostic testing." It only had "harvesting" and "non-harvesting."

Gerald looked at Kael.

Kael looked back. No escape existed.

The statue was waking. The core was failing.

Then, Gerald saw it.

A small, grey icon in the corner of his interface. It was the Inspector General’s symbol. It was inactive. But it was there.

The Inspector General audited all cores. Periodically. But an audit could be triggered by *excessive* citations. Or by *excessive* confusion in the local regulatory framework.

Gerald had been cited by Torvald; Torvald had been cited by *others* for lava overflow into the public sewer. Torvald’s Compliance Rating was 45/100.

If Gerald could prove that the *cause* of the energy drain was not his dungeon, but Torvald’s dungeon’s instability, then the citation would be invalid.

He had to shift the blame, and he had to prove that the geothermal energy was being pulled by Torvald’s lava pumps, not by the statue.

He had no proof. He had no sensors.

But he had the water leak.

The water was flowing into the foundation. Torvald’s lava pits were dry, and they needed water to keep the magma from cooling too fast and cracking the bedrock. Torvald was desperate.

Gerald resumed typing. This time, he wasn't writing an appeal. He was writing a counter-citation.

**CITATION ISSUED TO: TORVALD (CORE ID: 894-B).** **VIOLATION: UNAUTHORIZED CONDUIT CONSTRUCTION.** **DETAILS: THE GEOTHERMAL DRAIN DETECTED IN SECTOR 4 WAS CAUSED BY AN ILLEGAL BOREHOLE EXTENDING FROM DUNGEON 'IRON MAW' INTO THE SHARED FOUNDATION. THE DRAIN IS AN ARTIFACT OF TORVALD'S PUMPS, NOT THE STATUE.** **EVIDENCE: WATER LEVELS IN THE LIBRARY SUB-BASEMENT HAVE DROPPED BY 15% IN THE LAST HOUR, CONSISTENT WITH SUCTION FROM AN EXTERNAL SOURCE.**

He didn't have the evidence. But he was making it up. He was fabricating a causal link.

If the System believed him, Torvald would be cited. Torvald would be debuffed.

His lava pumps would weaken.

The suction would stop. The statue would calm down.

But if the System didn't believe him, he would be cited for False Reporting, and false Reporting was a permanent strike.

Two minutes.

Gerald hit submit.

The System paused.

**CROSS-REFERENCING TORVALD’S INFRASTRUCTURE LOGS.**

Gerald held his breath. The statue’s eyes were blazing. The cracks were racing across the floor. The library above was groaning.

**DATA FOUND: TORVALD CORE REPORTS A 10% DROP IN HYDRO-STATIC PRESSURE.** **CORRELATION DETECTED: GERALD HUTCHINS WATER LOSS MATCHES TORVALD PRESSURE LOSS.** **ROOT CAUSE ANALYSIS: EXTERNAL CONDUIT.**

The statue’s eyes flickered. The humming stopped.

**CITATION #4491 REVOKED.** **NEW CITATION ISSUED TO: TORVALD (CORE ID: 894-B).** **VIOLATION: STRUCTURAL INTERFERENCE.** **PENALTY: STAT DEBUFF - PHYSICAL RESISTANCE REDUCED BY 50% FOR 24 HOURS.**

The floor stopped shaking. The blue thread vanished. The statue went dark.

Gerald slumped against the wall, and he was sweating. His heart hammered against his ribs.

He had saved the dungeon.

He had saved the library. He had ruined Torvald’s defense.

But he had lied. He had fabricated evidence. He had used a loophole that didn't exist to save a loophole that did.

And then, a new window opened. It was not from the System. It was not from Torvald.

It was a direct message.

**FROM: INSPECTOR GENERAL (ID: 000-ADMIN)** **MESSAGE: INTERESTING ARGUMENT, MR. HUTCHINS.

YOU HAVE DISCOVERED THE CONDUIT LOOPHOLE.

MOST CORES NEVER REALIZE THEY CAN BLAME THEIR NEIGHBOR FOR THEIR OWN MISTAKES.

YOU ARE NOW ON THE WATCHLIST.** **NOTE: TORVALD IS FILING A COUNTER-APPEAL. HE CLAIMS HE WAS FRAMED.

HE HAS RETAINED LEGAL COUNSEL.** **HEARING SCHEDULE: IMMEDIATE.**

Gerald looked at Kael.

"Legal counsel?" Kael asked.

"The System has lawyers," Gerald said, his voice trembling. "For the Cores. And they’re better at arguing than we are."

The floor shook again. But this time, it wasn't the statue. It was the door.

Someone was knocking.
