Chapter 423: Non-Compliant Egress

# Chapter 423

The falling body did not hit the floor.

Mira caught it.

She dropped the trowel. Her hands, still covered in plaster dust, shot upward.

She grabbed the ankle of the plummeting figure, and the weight was substantial, dead weight, and it dragged her down. Her boots skidded on the slick concrete. Kael lunged forward, catching the stranger's other leg.

They braced against the shifting floor, muscles locking, tendons straining against the sheer mass of the intruder.

"Get him off!" Mira grunted, her teeth clenched.

The stranger swung wildly. A gauntlet, heavy with plate steel, clipped Kael's shoulder.

The young man cried out, buckling.

The stranger kicked free, landing on all fours like a feral dog. He scrambled backward, eyes wide, pupils blown black. He was wearing armor that cost more than Gerald's entire life's salary.

A knight. Or something close to one.

"Where is he?" the knight screamed. "Where is the Core?"

Mira stared at him. Her chest heaved.

"Who?"

"Gerald Hutchins!"

The name hung in the air, thick with dust.

Mira looked at Kael.

Kael looked at the ceiling, where the hole was widening, splintering wood and drywall raining down. The library above was collapsing into the dungeon.

The structural integrity of the municipal building was failing. Fast.

"Kael, the ladder," Mira said, her voice flat. "Block the hole. Before more fall in."

Kael hesitated. He looked at the knight, who was now standing, drawing a sword that hummed with a purple, unnatural light. The air tasted like ozone and burnt hair.

"That sword," Mira said. "It's not licensed. It's not registered. And he's trespassing on a non-public easement."

The knight swung. The blade sliced through the air, aiming for Mira's neck.

She didn't move.

The sword stopped an inch from her skin. A translucent blue barrier shimmered into existence, holding the blade in place. The System interface flashed in Mira's peripheral vision.

**VIOLATION: UNSAFE USE OF WEAPON IN NON-COMBAT ZONE.** **CITATION ISSUED.**

The knight's arm went limp. The sword clattered to the ground. The man gasped, clutching his shoulder as if struck by a hammer. He fell to his knees, retching.

"Get up," Mira commanded. She didn't shout.

She spoke with the weary authority of a woman who had seen a thousand safety violations and filed the paperwork for every single one.

"You are cited for reckless endangerment of a public infrastructure. You will remain on the ground until a supervisor arrives."

The knight looked up, terrified.

Not of Mira. Of the invisible weight pressing down on his limbs.

The debuff was severe.

His strength stat had been zeroed out by the citation.

Kael crawled to the hole. He wedged the ladder under the jagged edges of the floor.

The wood groaned.

It wouldn't hold.

The library's weight was too great. The ceiling was tearing apart.

"He's not coming," Kael whispered. "Gerald is gone. He fused with the foundation. He's gone."

Mira ignored him. She walked to the wall where she had written *INSPECTION COMPLETE*. She touched the ink.

It was wet. Still fresh.

"He's not gone," she said. "He's working."

She closed her eyes, and she reached out with her mind, pushing past the pain, past the fear, searching for the signal. The dungeon's pulse. Usually, it was a steady rhythm.

A heartbeat.

Now, it was erratic. Stuttering.

Like an engine trying to turn over with a flooded cylinder.

*Push*, she thought. *Push the load. Distribute the weight. Use the handrails.*

The dungeon responded. Slowly.

The shifting floor stopped.

The knight on the ground stopped retching. He stared at the walls. The cracks in the plaster were sealing themselves.

Dust was being sucked back into the masonry. The falling debris froze in mid-air, suspended by an invisible grid.

"System," Mira said aloud. "Status of structural compliance."

A window popped up in her vision. Red text. Blazing red.

**COMPLIANCE RATING: 12/100.** **CRITICAL FAILURE: LOAD-BEARING WALLS COMPROMISED.** **IMMINENT COLLAPSE IN: 14 MINUTES.**

"Fourteen minutes," Kael said, his voice rising. "We can't fix this in fourteen minutes. The library is gone. The support beams are snapped."

Mira looked at the knight.

"Who are you?"

"Sir Aldous," the man wheezed.

"Order of the Golden Key.

We... we came to destroy the Core. The System flagged him as a rogue entity.

An anomaly."

"He's an inspector," Mira said. "He's not an anomaly. He's a bureaucrat."

"He's a threat," Aldous spat. "He cited the High Dungeon of Xylor. He cited the Iron Keep. He shut down the entire Eastern Sector's dungeon network for 'inadequate emergency lighting.' The Council is terrified."

"The Council is sloppy," Mira said. "They don't maintain their egress routes.

They don't check their fire extinguishers, and they rely on heroics. Heroes die. Codes save lives.

"

She turned to Kael.

"Do you have the Form 11-B?"

Kael blinked.

"The Unlicensed Biological Entity form? Yes.

I have it.

It's in my pack.

"

"Give it to me."

Mira took the form. It was a physical object, a piece of parchment that existed only because Gerald had manifested it.

She pulled a pen from her pocket.

The bent pen, and she smoothed it out.

The knight looked confused.

"What are you doing?"

"Processing a temporary permit," Mira said.

She wrote. Her handwriting was small, cramped, and precise.

**TEMPORARY STRUCTURAL SUPPORT AUTHORIZATION.** **APPLICANT: ASHFORD MUNICIPAL LIBRARY.** **TYPE: EMERGENCY BRACING.** **METHOD: ANCHORED TO FOUNDATION CORE.**

She signed her name. She added Gerald's name. She stamped it with the seal of the Ashford Department of Building Inspection, which she had also manifested from the dungeon's memory.

The parchment glowed.

The System accepted it.

**PERMIT APPROVED.** **CONDITION: TEMPORARY. 1 HOUR DURATION.** **COST: 500 MANA POINTS FROM APPLICANT RESERVE.**

The mana drained from Mira's reserves. She felt cold. Her knees knocked together. But the effect was immediate.

Thick, iron rods sprouted from the dungeon walls. They shot upward, piercing the floor, wedging themselves into the library's shattered joists above. They locked together, forming a lattice of steel.

The ceiling stopped tearing. The falling debris halted, then rose, pulled back up by the iron rods.

The library was held together by Gerald's code.

"Look at that," Kael breathed. "He's holding it. He's actually holding it."

Aldous struggled to stand, and the debuff was fading as the citation's timer ran down, but he was weak.

"It's a trick. The Core is dead.

This is residual energy.

"

"It's not residual," Mira said. "It's active. He's filing reports in real-time."

Suddenly, the air in the basement grew heavy. The temperature dropped. Frost formed on the iron rods.

A voice echoed through the dungeon. Not Mira's voice. Not Kael's. A voice that sounded like grinding stone.

**AUDIT IN PROGRESS.** **INSPECTOR GENERAL: VALORIS.**

Mira froze.

The Inspector General, and she had heard the name once. It was the highest authority in the System. The entity that enforced the rules Gerald had broken. The one who had issued the warning that led to Gerald's downfall.

Valoris did not appear as a person. It appeared as a presence.

A pressure.

The walls groaned. The iron rods shuddered.

"Get out," Mira said to Kael.

"I can't leave you," Kael said.

"The audit isn't for us," Mira said. "It's for him. It's for Gerald."

The pressure intensified. The knight, Aldous, vibrated.

His armor rattled, and he screamed, clawing at his face.

"It's burning me! The audit!

It's stripping my stats!

"

Mira grabbed Aldous by the collar.

"Stop moving!

If you violate the audit protocol, you will be erased.

Stand still. Submit to inspection.

"

Aldous went rigid. He stared ahead, eyes rolling back.

Mira looked at the wall. The glowing vein of metal was pulsing again. But this time, it was red. Angry red.

Gerald was fighting the Audit.

But he was alone, and he was in the walls. He had no body.

No spawn list.

No defensive structures. He was just a set of rules in a 4,000-square-foot box.

And the Audit was bringing everything it had.

Mira realized something. A loophole. A tiny, bureaucratic crack in the armor of the System.

The Audit was happening *inside* the dungeon, and the Audit was subject to the dungeon's local code.

Mira pulled out her pen again. Her hand shook. She looked at the Form 11-B she had just used. Then she looked at the knight.

"Kael," she said. "Hand me the knight's sword."

Kael stared at her.

"What?"

"The sword. Give it to me."

Kael kicked the sword toward her. It slid across the floor, humming with that purple light.

Mira picked it up. It was heavy. Unbalanced. Dangerous.

"Valoris," she said. Her voice echoed in the sudden silence.

The pressure stopped. Just for a second.

**INSPECTOR VALORIS: IDENTIFY YOURSELF.**

"I am Mira," she said. "Acting Inspector for the Ashford Municipal Library Dungeon."

**ACTING INSPECTORS ARE NOT AUTHORIZED TO CONDUCT AUDITS.**

"I am not conducting an audit," Mira said. "I am conducting a counter-claim."

She held up the sword.

"This weapon," she said. "It was dropped by Sir Aldous during a Code Enforcement Action.

It is currently evidence.

Evidence cannot be destroyed by an Audit.

Evidence must be preserved for the Hearing.

"

The System paused.

**EVIDENCE LOGGED.** **ITEM: SWORD OF THE GOLDEN ORDER.** **STATUS: PRESERVED.**

Valoris hesitated. The red glow in the walls dimmed slightly.

"But you are violating the Chain of Custody," Valoris's voice boomed. "You did not log the retrieval time."

Mira smiled. It was a thin, dangerous smile.

"I am logging it now," she said. She wrote on the back of the permit.

**RETRIEVAL TIME: NOW.** **WITNESS: GERALD HUTCHINS.**

The wall pulsed. A single, clear tone rang out. Like a bell.

Gerald had witnessed it.

The Audit stalled.

But the pause was brief. The pressure returned, stronger than before. The iron rods buckled. The ceiling above groaned, a sound like a mountain breaking.

Mira looked at Kael.

"The ventilation," she said. "Gerald's first violation was inadequate ventilation. If we fix the ventilation, the Audit loses one of its primary grounds for immediate termination."

Kael looked at the broken shafts in the wall.

"We can't fix the shafts. They're crushed."

"We don't need the shafts," Mira said. "We need airflow. We need to create a new egress."

She pointed to the hole in the ceiling. The one the knight had fallen through.

"That hole is non-compliant," she said. "But if we install a temporary ventilation duct... we can cite the Audit for blocking emergency airflow."

It was insane. It was dangerous. It required her to go up into the collapsing library.

Mira looked at the knight. Aldous was still rigid, vibrating under the pressure. His eyes were clear now. He looked at Mira.

"Do it," he whispered. "Save him."

Mira nodded. She looked at Kael.

"Stay here," she said. "Keep the knight alive. If he dies, the evidence is destroyed, and the case is lost."

"And you?" Kael asked.

Mira looked at the ladder. It was still wedged under the hole. It was shaking.

"I'm going to file a variance request," she said.

She climbed the ladder, and the rungs were slippery with dust. The wood splintered under her grip. Above her, the hole in the ceiling was a jagged maw of darkness and falling plaster.

She pulled herself up.

Her head broke through the floor.

She was in the library.

The room was a disaster. Bookshelves had toppled. The roof was sagging, beams cracking under the strain. The sky was visible through gaps in the tiles.

Mira stood in the center of the ruin. She looked around.

There were no books. Just dust. And shadows.

And in the center of the room, standing amidst the wreckage, was a figure.

Not Gerald.

Another Core, which was a small, glowing orb, hovering above a pile of rubble. It pulsed with a sickly green light. It had no name tag.

No level. Just a single word above its head.

**PARASITE.**

The Parasite looked at Mira. It didn't speak. It just laughed. A high, shrieking sound that vibrated in her teeth.

Mira reached for her pen. It wasn't there. She had left it in the basement.

She had no weapon. No form. No citation.

The Parasite drifted toward her. The floor beneath her feet cracked. The library was about to fall.

Gerald was below, holding the world up with sheer willpower.

And Mira was alone, armed with nothing but a trowel and a dead man's memory.

The Parasite extended a tendril of green light.

Mira backed up. Her heel hit a broken shelf. She stumbled.

She looked down at the hole in the floor. She looked at the green light closing in on her face.

She made a choice, and she didn't fight.

She jumped.

She dove back into the hole, back into the basement, back into the dark.

The Parasite followed.

And as Mira fell, she heard Gerald's voice. Not in her head. Not in the walls.

Right behind her.

"Your egress route," Gerald said, "is non-compliant."

The darkness swallowed them both.

She was falling faster than the Parasite.
