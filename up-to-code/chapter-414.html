Chapter 414: The Audit Begins

# Chapter 414

The knock came again.

*Clang. Clang. Clang.*

It did not echo. The sound died instantly against the iron rail, swallowed by the vacuum of the void beyond the handrail.

Gerald Hutchins did not turn around. He kept climbing.

His right foot found the next step, then the next.

The muscles in his calves burned, but he ignored them. He ignored the sweat pricking his hairline. He ignored the fact that the air in the stairwell tasted like copper and old ozone.

He had a job to do.

The roof access door was locked.

It always was. Gerald fumbled for the key ring in his pocket, his fingers clumsy.

He found the correct key, and he found the wrong one first. He tried the wrong one. It scratched against the metal.

He tried the correct one. The lock turned with a stiff, reluctant *click*.

He pushed the door open.

The sky above Ashford was wrong, which was not blue.

It was the color of a wet sidewalk under a streetlight. Gray, flat, and featureless.

The wind did not blow. It hovered.

Gerald stepped out.

The roof of the Municipal Library was a flat expanse of tar paper and gravel. Usually, it was cluttered with HVAC units, bird droppings, and the occasional discarded soda can.

Today, it was bare.

Clean. Sterile.

In the center of the roof stood a single object, which was a desk.

Not a dungeon construct. Not a magical illusion. A standard-issue municipal office desk, oak veneer, four legs, one drawer. On it sat a lamp, a pen, and a stack of papers.

Behind the desk sat a figure.

It wore a suit that fit too well. The tie was knotted with geometric precision.

The face was smooth, lacking pores, lacking expression.

It looked at Gerald with eyes that were simply two black dots on a white page.

"Inspector Hutchins," the figure said. Its voice sounded like paper tearing.

"Inspector General," Gerald replied. He walked toward the desk.

He did not run.

Running was undignified.

Running suggested panic. Gerald did not panic.

Gerald filed.

"You are late," the General said.

"I am on time," Gerald said. "You summoned me to the roof. I arrived at the roof. The timing is irrelevant to the jurisdiction."

"The jurisdiction is absolute." The General picked up a sheet of paper. "Let us begin the audit."

Gerald pulled up a folding chair from nowhere. The System materialized it for him, a simple metal frame. He sat. He placed his hands on his knees.

"State your name and Core ID," the General said.

"Gerald Hutchins. Core ID 001-ASH-442."

"Compliance Rating?"

"Pending."

"Pending is not a number, Inspector."

"Pending is a status," Gerald said. "Until the audit is complete, the rating is not finalized. You cannot penalize a pending status. That is a violation of Procedural Fairness, Subsection 4, Paragraph C."

The General paused. The black dots of its eyes narrowed. It looked down at the paper.

"You are correct," it said. "That is an oversight. We will correct it."

The General waved a hand. The air shimmered. A new form appeared on the desk.

*System Notice: Temporary Rating Suspension.* *Reason: Audit in Progress.* *Duration: Until Final Determination.*

Gerald exhaled. His shoulders dropped a fraction of an inch.

"Good," Gerald said. "Now, proceed."

"Let us review the incident at the Library Sub-Basement," the General said. It picked up another document. This one was thick, bound in red tape. "The incident involving Core Jarek of the Obsidian Spire."

"I cited him," Gerald said.

"You issued a Stop Work Order," the General corrected. "You halted his regeneration cycle. You disabled his mana vents. You caused three of his elite spawns to suffocate due to blocked egress routes."

"They were not egress routes," Gerald said. "They were decorative archways without handrails. The height differential exceeded four feet. Under the Municipal Safety Code, which the System adopts for all sub-terrestrial structures exceeding ten thousand square feet, any vertical transition above four feet requires a guardrail of at least forty-two inches."

"The Obsidian Spire operates under the Ancient Dungeon Code," the General said.

"The Ancient Dungeon Code was repealed by the System Update 12.4," Gerald said. "I have the memo.

I filed it myself.

Section 9, Clause 2: 'All prior local ordinances are superseded by the Global Building Standard unless grandfathered by an Exemption Certificate.' Jarek did not have an Exemption Certificate. I checked. His inventory list showed no certificates.

Only loot."

The General flipped a page.

"Jarek claims his dungeon is a living organism. He argues that a handrail is an impediment to organic flow. He argues that the System's definition of 'structure' is too rigid."

"Organic or not, if a human can walk into it, it must be safe for a human," Gerald said. "If a human trips and falls in a dungeon, and the dungeon has no handrail, the dungeon is liable. If the dungeon is liable, the Core is negligent. If the Core is negligent, the Core can be cited."

The General tapped the pen on the desk. *Tap. Tap. Tap.*

"This is… aggressive," the General said.

"It is accurate," Gerald said.

"Let us look at the citation you filed against the Maintenance Department."

Gerald blinked.

"I did not cite the Maintenance Department."

"You did," the General said. It slid a form across the desk.

It was a blank piece of paper, but when Gerald looked at it, words appeared in his mind. He saw the text. He had written it in his head while walking up the stairs.

*Violation: Unauthorized Alteration of Public Infrastructure.* *Subject: Ashford City Maintenance Dept.* *Penalty: Citation Level 1.* *Reason: Failure to repair handrail in Sector 4 within 48 hours of reported damage.*

"I cited them for negligence," Gerald said. "The handrail was loose.

I reported it, and they did not fix it. They are part of the System's physical infrastructure because their work maintains the boundaries of the dungeon footprint. Therefore, they are subject to the same compliance standards as the Core.

"

The General stared at him.

"You are attempting to regulate the regulators," the General said.

"I am attempting to ensure safety," Gerald said. "If the maintenance crew fails to fix a rail, and an adventurer falls, the liability falls on the Core.

If the Core pays the liability, the Core loses resources.

If the Core loses resources, the Core becomes weak.

If the Core becomes weak, the dungeon collapses. If the dungeon collapses, the System loses a data point.

Therefore, I am protecting the System's interest. I am saving you money, Inspector General.

"

The General's pen stopped moving, which was a new argument. Gerald had never used it before.

It was a loophole.

The System operated on efficiency. Gerald had just framed safety compliance as financial optimization.

The General looked at the blank paper. Then it looked at Gerald.

"Interesting," it said.

"It is logical," Gerald said.

"Very well," the General said. "We will accept the premise for the sake of the audit. Now, the void."

The General gestured to the edge of the roof. Beyond the gravel, the air rippled. The knock had stopped.

"Something is out there," the General said. "It is not a Core. It is not a spawn. It is not a glitch."

Gerald looked at the ripple, and he felt a cold weight in his stomach. He knew what it was.

He had seen it in the early days, before he had fixed the ventilation.

It was the absence of code. The place where rules did not apply.

"It is the Unwritten," Gerald said.

"It is the Anti-Code," the General said. "It resists structure. It resists definition. It eats Compliance Ratings."

"It ate Jarek's dungeon," Gerald said.

"No," the General said. "Jarek's dungeon was closed by your Citation.

The Unwritten did not eat it, which was *waiting*.

It feeds on ambiguity. Your Citation created a legal vacuum.

Jarek was cited, but he did not pay. He appealed. The appeal is pending.

While the appeal is pending, his status is undefined. Undefined things attract the Unwritten.

"

Gerald frowned.

"I did not tell him to appeal."

"I advised him," the General said. "It is standard practice."

"Then you created the vacuum," Gerald said.

"Perhaps," the General said.

"Or perhaps you created the bait. You are a beacon, Inspector. Your obsession with rules draws things that hate rules.

They come to test your boundaries.

They come to see if you can define them.

"

The ripple in the air grew larger.

A shape formed. It was jagged. It looked like a tear in a photograph.

It had no name. It had no level.

It had no hit points, which was a Violation.

Not a violation of a rule. A violation of reality.

The General leaned forward.

"We have two options, Hutchins."

The General held up a finger.

"Option one: You accept the Citation you filed against yourself. The System will revoke your Core Status.

You will be decommissioned. Your dungeon will be converted to a public park. The Unwritten will have nothing to anchor to.

It will dissipate.

"

Gerald looked at the desk. He looked at the pen.

Option one was safe. It was clean. It was up to code.

"Option two?" Gerald asked.

The General held up a second finger.

"You define the Unwritten. You file a Citation against it. You force it to comply.

You force it to have a form, a number, a rating.

If you can make it legal, you can destroy it with the same rules you used on Jarek. But you must serve the Citation in person.

You must touch it.

And if it resists—if it breaks your definition—you will not just lose your Core. You will lose your mind. The Unwritten will erase your memory of every rule you have ever known.

"

Gerald stood up.

His legs felt weak. He gripped the edge of the desk. The wood was warm.

"I have to serve it?" Gerald asked.

"Yes," the General said. "Physical presence is required for service.

You must walk to the edge.

You must hand it the paper.

You must read the violation aloud.

"

Gerald looked at the void. The jagged shape was closer now. It pulsed.

It looked like static on a television screen, but three-dimensional. It hurt to look at.

Gerald reached into his pocket. He pulled out a pen.

He looked at the General.

"I need a form," Gerald said.

The General smiled. It was a horrible smile. Too many teeth.

"You draft it," the General said. "You are the Inspector. You know the code better than I do."

Gerald sat back down. He picked up the pen. His hand shook. He forced it to stop.

He looked at the blank paper in front of him, and he thought about thirty-one years of inspections. He thought about the mold in the library basement. He thought about the water leak. He thought about the rat.

He thought about Jarek.

He thought about Mira, downstairs, tracing the ghost of the law in the paper.

Gerald wrote.

He did not write a standard citation. He did not write a Stop Work Order.

He wrote a *Definition of Entity.*

**Subject:** The Unwritten / The Anti-Code. **Classification:** Unlicensed Non-Structural Hazard. **Violation:** Failure to maintain distinct boundaries. **Penalty:** Immediate Demolition.

Gerald stood up. He walked to the edge of the roof.

The void was ten feet away. Then five. Then two.

The shape reached for him. It had no hands. It had only absence.

Gerald raised the paper.

"I cite you," Gerald said.

His voice was steady.

"For lack of definition. For lack of form.

For lack of code.

"

The shape paused.

It tilted. It seemed to consider the words.

*Clang.*

The paper in Gerald's hand glowed.

The shape screamed. It was a sound without pitch. It shattered the windows of the library below.

Gerald felt a pain in his head. Sharp. Sudden. Like a wire being pulled out of his brain.

He forgot the name of his first supervisor, and he forgot the width of a standard door.

He forgot the number of the form he had just written.

The shape lunged.

Gerald slammed the paper against the void.

The paper touched the nothingness.

And for a second, the nothingness had edges, which had a shape.

It had a code.

Then it exploded.

Not into pieces. Into *silence.*

Gerald fell backward onto the gravel. He gasped for air. He clutched his head.

The General was gone, and the desk was gone.

The roof was normal. The sky was blue. The wind blew.

Gerald lay on the roof, staring at the sky, and he tried to remember what he had done.

He knew he had served a Citation. He knew he had won.

But the details were foggy. Blurry. Like a photograph left in the sun.

He sat up. He looked at his hand.

The paper was gone.

In its place, burned into his palm, was a number.

*0.*

Gerald stared at it.

Zero.

Not a rating. Not a violation count.

Zero, and he looked down at the roof access door. It was locked.

He stood up. He walked to the door. He opened it.

He walked down the stairs.

The stairs creaked, and he reached the sub-basement.

Mira was there. She was standing by the handrail. She was holding the blank paper.

"You're back," she said.

"Yes," Gerald said.

He looked at the handrail. It was perfect.

He looked at the void beyond it.

There was no void.

There was only a wall. A plain, gray wall.

But on the wall, scratched into the concrete, were words.

*Case Closed.*

*Appeal Denied.*

*Next Audit: Immediate.*

Gerald touched the wall, and he felt nothing. No warmth. No magic. No presence.

Just concrete.

Mira stepped forward.

"Gerald?"

"I am here," Gerald said.

"You're different," Mira said. "You look… empty."

Gerald looked at his hand. The *0* was fading.

"I need to file a report," Gerald said.

"On what?"

Gerald opened his mouth, and he tried to find the word.

He tried to find the form number.

He tried to find the rule.

He searched his mind.

It was clean. Sterile.

He had won, and he had destroyed the Unwritten.

But he had lost the part of himself that knew why.

"I don't know," Gerald whispered.

Mira's eyes widened.

Gerald turned to the stairwell, and he walked up.

He walked out.

He walked into the library.

The patrons looked at him. They did not see a Dungeon Core. They did not see a hero.

They saw a man who had forgotten his own name.

And in his pocket, a new form appeared, which was not blank.

It was already filled out.

**Recipient:** Gerald Hutchins. **Violation:** Existence without Purpose. **Penalty:** Deletion.

Gerald looked at the form.

He did not feel fear, and he felt relief.

Because he finally had a rule to follow.

He had to delete himself.

He walked to the exit.

He did not look back.

But behind him, in the sub-basement, the handrail rusted.

Fast.

In seconds, it was red and flaking.

The water leak returned.

The mold bloomed.

The rat squeaked.

And from the darkness, a single, sharp knock came from the inside of the wall.

*Clang.*

Not the void.

Not the Unwritten.

Something else.

Something that had been waiting for the code to fail.

Mira picked up the rusted handrail.

It snapped in her hands, and she looked at the door.

Gerald was gone.

And the System had just begun the audit.
