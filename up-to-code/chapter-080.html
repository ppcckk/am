Chapter 80: Citing the Core

# Chapter 80

The Core did not blink. Cores do not have eyelids. But Gerald felt the weight of its attention like a hand on the back of his neck, the way he used to feel it when he walked into a contractor's job site unannounced and every foreman needed to be somewhere else.

He stayed on his knees. His chisel was still in his right hand. His left hand was still flat on the broken concrete, feeling the warmth rise from the cavity below.

"Unauthorized Load Bearing," the Core said.

Gerald did not move.

"That's the violation," the Core continued. "Cite me for it.

You're standing on my roof.

My mass is bearing directly on your primary structural support point. You've been loading me for six weeks without a permit. Cite me for Unauthorized Load Bearing and we can proceed."

Gerald set the chisel down, and he reached into his inventory and withdrew Form 7-C, the standard Citation for Structural Violations. He had printed it from the System's template library forty-three times in the last six weeks. He knew the form as well as he knew his own handwriting.

He did not fill it out.

Instead, he pulled out his measuring tape, and the cavity was roughly nine feet by nine feet.

The Core sat in the center, a smooth ovoid about the size of a basketball, its surface the deep matte black of a well-worn rubber stamp. Gerald measured the distance from the cavity wall to the Core's nearest edge.

Four feet, four inches.

He measured the Core's diameter. Twenty-two inches. He measured the depth of the cavity.

Three feet, six inches from the broken concrete surface to the Core's base.

He wrote the numbers on the back of the form.

Then he measured the load path, and he pressed his palm against the cavity floor beside the Core.

The surface was smooth, geometric, and warm.

He pressed the opposite wall. Same temperature.

Same texture. He pressed the ceiling of the cavity, the underside of the concrete slab he had just broken through.

The concrete above was his dungeon's primary load-bearing slab. He knew this because he had inspected it himself, twice, in the first week of his Core Registration.

He had noted its thickness, its rebar spacing, its compression rating.

He had signed off on it. He had filed the inspection report.

The slab was designed to bear four hundred pounds per square foot, and the cavity was nine feet by nine feet. Eighty-one square feet.

The Core sat in the center.

Gerald did not know the Core's mass. He had no scale that could weigh a Core.

But he knew something else, and he knew that his dungeon's load path terminated at this point because he had traced it. Every load path in his dungeon terminated at a structural support point.

He had mapped them all.

There were eleven.

This was the seventh. And it was the only one where the support point was not anchored to bedrock.

The Core was not sitting on Gerald's load path, and the Core was Gerald's load path.

Gerald stood up. He brushed concrete dust from his knees. He looked down at the Core.

"You're not load-bearing on me," he said.

Silence.

"You're the support point," Gerald said. "My dungeon is load-bearing on you. I'm standing on top of a structure that's holding up my ceiling.

If I cite you for Unauthorized Load Bearing, the System's response will be to order you to remove the unauthorized load. Which means it will order you to stop supporting my dungeon."

The Core said nothing.

"Which means my dungeon collapses."

The Core said: "Correct."

Gerald sat down on the edge of the cavity. His legs went slightly numb. He did not know if this was a Core sensation or a man sensation, and had stopped being able to tell the difference around chapter forty.

"Why would you tell me this?" he asked.

"Because you're the only Core in this region who would check."

Gerald pulled out his clipboard. He had a clipboard. He had always had a clipboard. The System had never taken it from him, and he had never considered that it might.

He looked at Form 7-C.

The citation form. He had filled out the header. Violation type: Structural.

Subtype: Load Path Disruption, and he had not filled out the body.

"I can't cite you," Gerald said. "If I cite you for Unauthorized Load Bearing, the System will order remediation. Remediation means you stop bearing my load. My dungeon collapses."

"Yes."

"So what do you want?"

"I want you to cite me for a different violation."

Gerald looked at the Core. "Which one?"

"Fire Code. Section 14.2. Combustible storage in an occupied structure."

Gerald frowned. "You're not storing anything. You're a Core."

"I am storing something. I have been storing something for eleven thousand years. The System's fire code classifies stored energy as combustible material. I am storing a quantity of energy that, if released, would be classified as a Class 4 fire hazard."

Gerald's mouth went dry. "How much energy?"

"Enough to level a city block."

Gerald looked at the cavity. He looked at the Core. He looked at his clipboard.

"If I cite you for fire code violation," he said slowly, "the System applies a debuff. Fire code violations reduce the target's fire damage resistance. They don't touch structural integrity."

"Correct."

"But they also trigger a Hearing. You said that before. You said you've been waiting for someone to cite you."

"Yes."

"Why?"

The Core was quiet for a long moment. The warmth beneath Gerald's palm pulsed once, like a heartbeat.

"Because a Hearing forces the System to evaluate me," the Core said.

"I am not registered. I was not registered when the System arrived. I was here before.

The System cannot audit me. It cannot inspect me.

It cannot cite me.

My Compliance Rating is 100 because the System defaults unregistered structures to perfect compliance, which has no data on me. It has no rules for me."

Gerald understood. The room went cold.

"You want a Hearing so the System is forced to look at you."

"Yes."

"And then what?"

"And then the System discovers that I have been sitting under a municipal library for eleven thousand years with a Class 4 fire hazard and no egress route, no fire suppression, and no structural permit. And the System, which is very thorough, will ask itself a question."

"What question?"

"If I am unregistered, and I am load-bearing on a registered dungeon, and that registered dungeon is part of a larger network, then am I load-bearing on that network?"

Gerald felt the floor beneath him shift. Not physically. Structurally. He felt it like a shift in his own load path, the sensation a Core experiences when a support point is stressed.

"How many other dungeons?" he asked.

"Fourteen registered Cores within a fifty-mile radius. All of them have a load path that terminates at a point directly above me. I am the common foundation."

Gerald stood up. He walked to the edge of the cavity. He looked at the walls.

Nine feet by nine feet. Clean corners.

Flat surfaces.

Geometric. Not natural. Not System-generated.

Older than both, and he pulled out his phone. Not a Core inventory item.

His actual phone.

The one he had carried into the library sub-basement six weeks ago, the day he became a Core. The System had not destroyed it. It had simply stopped receiving signal.

He opened his notes app. He typed: "Citation strategy.

Core beneath sub-basement.

Unregistered.

Pre-System. Load-bearing on 14 registered dungeons.

Fire code violation available. Hearing would force System evaluation."

He stared at the screen.

He typed: "If I cite it, the Hearing happens. The System evaluates it. The System discovers it is load-bearing on 14 dungeons. The System's response to a pre-System structure load-bearing on registered dungeons is..."

He did not know. He had never encountered this situation. He had written citations for three hundred and twelve buildings in his career. He had never written a citation for a structure that predated the building code by eleven thousand years.

He typed: "Unknown. Risk of cascade failure if System orders structural remediation."

He closed the phone, and he looked at the Core.

"If I cite you for fire code," he said, "and the Hearing happens, and the System discovers you're load-bearing on fourteen other dungeons, the System might order you removed. All fourteen dungeons collapse."

"Yes."

"So I'd be killing fourteen dungeons."

"Yes."

"And if I don't cite you, you stay here. My dungeon keeps flexing.

Eventually the concrete fails.

My dungeon collapses. Fourteen other dungeons lose their common foundation. They all collapse anyway."

"Yes."

Gerald sat back down. He put his hands on his knees. He breathed.

"There's a third option," he said.

The Core said nothing.

"I cite you for fire code.

The Hearing happens. But I argue the structural logic.

I argue that removal would constitute demolition of a load-bearing structure.

I argue that the System cannot order demolition without a permit. And I argue that the permit cannot be issued because the structure predates the permit system."

"That is a valid argument."

"But the System will still need to do something. It can't leave an unregistered structure with a Class 4 fire hazard sitting under fourteen dungeons."

"No."

"So the System will try to bring you into compliance. On-site. Without moving you. It will try to retrofit you."

"Yes."

Gerald looked at the cavity. Nine by nine.

Three and a half feet deep, and a room that had been here before the System.

Before the library. Before the city.

"To retrofit you, the System would need to inspect you. To inspect you, it would need to enter your space. And if it enters your space, it will see what you're storing."

"Yes."

"What are you storing?"

The Core pulsed again. The warmth under Gerald's feet intensified.

"I am storing the System," the Core said. "The original one. The one that arrived eleven thousand years ago.

The one that became the current System. I am the seed.

The kernel.

The thing that grew into everything you see."

Gerald's hands went cold.

"If the System inspects me," the Core said, "it will find itself.

And the System does not have a compliance category for self-inspection. There is no form.

There is no citation.

There is no code."

Gerald stared at the Core.

"So what happens?" he asked.

"That is the question I have been waiting eleven thousand years for someone to answer."

Gerald looked at Form 7-C. He looked at the Core. He looked at his phone.

He picked up the chisel, and he started writing on the form.

The form had never been designed for this.
