Chapter 289: Counsel for the Rat

# Chapter 289

Darkness swallowed the sound of falling.

Gerald Hutchins hit water.

It was not clean water. It was thick, cold, and smelled of rotting organic matter and industrial runoff.

The impact stole the breath from his core-body, a sensation that translated into a sharp, metallic tang in his mental buffer.

He didn't have lungs, but he had the memory of lungs. The phantom sensation of drowning clutched at his logic centers, threatening to flood his processing capacity with panic.

Panic was inefficient. Panic did not cite violations.

*SYSTEM ALERT: STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY COMPROMISED.* *ANCHOR POINT LOST.* *CURRENT LOCATION: ASHFORD MUNICIPAL SEWER LINE 4B.* *STATUS: UNLICENSED EXCURSION.*

The text flashed in red across his peripheral vision, pulsing like a migraine.

Gerald ignored it. He focused on the immediate data stream.

His dungeon footprint had shifted.

The 4,000 square feet were no longer a sub-basement. They were now a irregular, twisting tube of masonry and concrete, buried beneath the city's infrastructure. The library was gone.

Martha was gone. The light was gone.

He tried to spawn a rat.

*ERROR: SPAWN RESTRICTION ACTIVE. VIOLATION: BIOLOGICAL ENTITY OUTSIDE PERMITTED ZONE.*

Gerald paused.

The water lapped at his lower limits.

The troll's trick had worked. By dragging his core into the sewer network, the troll had moved him out of his jurisdictional comfort zone.

The sewers were not public buildings. They were utility corridors. And utility corridors had different codes.

*FORM 7-C: UTILITY EASEMENT INSPECTION.*

The thought was a reflex. A hard-wired instinct born of thirty-one years of municipal service.

Gerald didn't know the sewer code by heart, and he didn't have to. The System provided the reference material. A mental index unspooled in his mind, listing regulations for pipe diameter, flow velocity, and manhole accessibility.

The problem was access.

He was a Core.

He could perceive the immediate radius of his dungeon, which was currently a cramped, wet tunnel. He could not see the troll.

He could not see the library, and he could only see the dark, slime-coated walls surrounding him.

Then, a ripple.

The water shifted. A shape emerged from the gloom upstream.

It was not the troll, which was larger.

Broader. A massive, armored crab-like creature, its shell fused with rusted iron pipes.

It clicked its claws against the concrete, the sound echoing in the tight space.

*ENEMY DETECTED: SEWER CRAB (ELITE).* *LEVEL: 45.* *THREAT LEVEL: HIGH.*

Gerald's Compliance Rating dropped from 47 to 42. The proximity of an unpermitted biological hazard was a liability.

The crab charged.

Gerald did not dodge. He couldn't move his core.

He could only manipulate his dungeon's internal geometry, and he focused on the wall to his left. It was a standard concrete block, roughly eight feet from his center. He pushed his will into the mortar.

*RE-CLASSIFY: LOAD-BEARING STRUCTURE.*

The wall groaned. The concrete hardened. The crab slammed into it, but the impact didn't shatter the barrier. It bounced off, clicking furiously.

Gerald analyzed the attack.

The crab's claws were designed to crush. They exerted force perpendicular to the surface.

But the sewer wall was curved, and the force had dispersed.

*LOG ENTRY: INCIDENT 89-204.* *SUBJECT: IMPACT ABSORPTION VIA CURVILINEAR DESIGN.* *RECOMMENDATION: INSTALL BOLLARDS.*

He didn't have bollards. He had the water.

He opened a valve in his mental schematic, targeting a nearby junction box.

*ACTION: FLOOD ZONE B.*

The water level in his immediate vicinity rose. It wasn't enough to drown the crab—it was an aquatic creature—but it was enough to obscure the crab's footing. The creature slipped, its claws scrabbling against the slick algae.

But the troll was still out there. The master key had opened the door, but it hadn't locked it. The connection to the sewer network remained active. The troll could send more minions.

Gerald needed to close the door, and he couldn't just seal the entrance. That would trap him in a hole. He needed to legally terminate the easement. He needed to cite the sewer system itself.

But you couldn't cite a city utility. You cited the entity operating it.

*QUERY: OPERATOR JURISDICTION.*

The System returned a blank. The sewers were public works. Public works didn't have Cores. They had municipal employees.

*ERROR: INVALID TARGET.*

The crab recovered. It swung a claw, cracking the concrete.

*SYSTEM ALERT: STRUCTURAL DAMAGE. HP DOWN 5%.*

Gerald's hands—or the conceptual equivalent of them—trembled. He looked at the water.

He looked at the pipes running along the ceiling, and he remembered the blueprint.

The secret passages. The ones never inspected.

If he couldn't cite the operator, he could cite the infrastructure for being unsafe.

*FORM 12-K: HAZARDOUS MATERIAL STORAGE.*

He scanned the water. It wasn't just waste. It contained traces of mana-crystals from the nearby dungeon raids.

Residue. Unlicensed alchemical waste.

*VIOLATION: UNAUTHORIZED CHEMICAL WASTE IN PUBLIC WATERWAY.*

This was a federal violation. It was outside municipal code. It was outside Gerald's jurisdiction.

But it was a violation.

And if he could get the attention of the right authority, they would shut it down.

He needed to serve a citation. But he needed a target. The water wasn't a target. The pipes weren't a target.

Then he saw it. A maintenance hatch, rusted shut, embedded in the side of the tunnel. It had a serial number. And behind the serial number, a small, glowing rune.

*RUNE OF OWNERSHIP: PRIVATE CONTRACTOR.*

*ENTITY: GRIMWATER CONSTRUCTION CO.*

A private contractor. They had a Core. Or at least, they had a registered business entity that the System recognized as a legal person for zoning purposes.

Gerald's eyes narrowed. This was the loophole.

The troll hadn't used a public key, which had used a private one. Grimwater Construction. They must have installed the master key to bypass city inspectors.

They were cutting corners.

*GENERATE DOCUMENT: CITATION 289-A.* *VIOLATION: UNLICENSED ACCESS POINT.* *VIOLATION: BYPASSING MUNICIPAL INSPECTION.* *VIOLATION: DANGEROUS INFRASTRUCTURE (SEWER GAS LEAK DETECTED).*

Gerald drafted the text. He didn't write it out.

He compiled it, and he used the System's templates. He formatted it with the precision of a man who had spent decades learning the exact font size for margin errors.

*SUBJECT: GRIMWATER CONSTRUCTION CO.* *ADDRESS: 400 BLOCK, SECTOR 7.* *PENALTY: TEMPORARY LOCKOUT. STRUCTURAL WEAKENING. ASSET FREEZE.*

Now he had to serve it.

The crab charged again. It smashed into the wall, creating a crack. Water sprayed.

Gerald didn't attack the crab. He attacked the crack.

*ACTION: REINFORCE JOINT. SEAL BREACH.*

The crack sealed, but the pressure built up behind it. The water had nowhere to go.

*SYSTEM: PRESSURE CRITICAL.*

*ACTION: DIRECT PRESSURE TO CITATION TARGET.*

Gerald couldn't throw water at the contractor. He couldn't throw water at the troll. But he could throw water at the rune.

The rune was on the hatch. The hatch was part of the infrastructure. If the infrastructure was cited, the rune would react.

He focused his will. He pushed the water against the hatch. The rusted metal groaned. The rune flared.

*ATTEMPTING SERVICE OF PROCESS.*

*ERROR: TARGET NOT PHYSICALLY PRESENT.*

Gerald froze. The rule still applied. Citations required the target to be present to be served.

He couldn't reach Grimwater. They were probably in their office, miles away.

The crab broke through the wall. It lunged for his core.

Gerald dodged. Not physically.

He shifted his anchor point, and he moved two feet to the left.

The crab's claw passed through the space he had occupied.

*MANEUVER: MICRO-TRANSLATION.* *COST: HIGH.*

He was running out of energy. The dungeon was small. The water was cold. The enemy was big.

He needed a proxy. Someone present.

He scanned the tunnel. He looked for any entity with a legal standing.

Then he saw it. A rat.

Not his rat. A wild rat. It was perched on a ledge, watching the fight.

It had a tag on its ear. A city sanitation tag.

*ENTITY: RATTUS NORVEGICUS.* *STATUS: CITY SANITATION VERMIN (DE-EMPHASIZED).*

The rat was a biological entity. It had been processed by the city. It had a file number.

Gerald stared at the rat. The rat stared back, its eyes reflecting the dim glow of the System interface.

This was insane. It was against every protocol. It was a misuse of biological assets.

But the rat was present. And the rat was associated with the sanitation department, which was associated with the sewer system, which was associated with the contractor who installed the key.

Chain of custody.

If he cited the rat for being in an unauthorized zone, the citation would bounce up the chain.

It would notify the sanitation department. The sanitation department would inspect the zone. The inspection would reveal the unauthorized access point.

The unauthorized access point would be cited, which was a bureaucratic ping-pong match. It was slow. It was inefficient.

But it was the only way.

*GENERATE DOCUMENT: CITATION 289-B.* *SUBJECT: VERMIN (RAT).* *VIOLATION: TRESPASSING.* *VIOLATION: INTERFERENCE WITH MUNICIPAL INFRASTRUCTURE.* *NOTIFICATION OF DERIVATIVE LIABILITY: SANITATION DEPT.*

Gerald served the citation. He didn't hand it over. He projected it into the rat's mind.

The rat squeaked. It didn't understand. But the System did.

*SYSTEM ALERT: CITATION RECEIVED.* *PROCESSING DERIVATIVE LIABILITY.* *ALERTING SANITATION DEPARTMENT.*

The tunnel shook.

Not from the crab. From above.

The heavy iron hatch rattled. Then, a voice. Muffled, distant, but clear.

"Hey! What's going on down there? I'm getting a noise complaint from the surface!"

A man's voice. A city worker.

The crab froze. It looked up at the hatch.

Gerald looked at the hatch.

*ACCESS GRANTED: INSPECTOR AUTHORITY.*

The hatch burst open. Light flooded in. And with the light, a figure dropped down.

It wasn't a hero. It wasn't a mage.

It was a sanitation worker in high-vis orange. He had a clipboard.

He landed in the water, splash-landing with professional grace. He looked at the crab. He looked at Gerald's core. He looked at the rat.

He pulled a walkie-talkie from his belt.

"Dispatch, this is Unit 4. I have a Code 3 in Sector 7. Unauthorized biological entity and... wait, is that a Dungeon Core?"

Gerald waited. This was the gamble. If the worker was corrupt, he was dead. If the worker was diligent, he was saved.

The worker frowned. He looked at Gerald's floating, glowing core. He looked at the citation tag that now hovered over the rat.

"Hey," the worker said, pointing his pen at Gerald. "You're the one who filed the noise complaint about the library sub-basement? The one about the handrails?"

Gerald pulsed a single, affirmative tone.

"Right. I got your file. You're the guy who cited the dragon for fire safety?"

Gerald pulsed again.

The worker laughed. A dry, rusty sound.

"Well, well. Look what the cat dragged in." He turned to the crab.

"Hey, buddy.

You're in violation of Ordinance 402-B. No pets in the sewer. That's a $500 fine.

"

The crab clicked its claws, confused.

"And you," the worker pointed at Gerald. "You're in violation of Ordinance 101-A. Unlicensed excavation. You're not supposed to be moving your dungeon."

Gerald's heart sank.

The worker raised his clipboard.

"I'm shutting this down. Both of you. You're both in breach of contract."

*SYSTEM ALERT: MUNICIPAL SHUTDOWN INITIATED.* *CORE DORMANCY IMMINENT.*

Gerald's light flickered. His thoughts slowed. The water drained away, sucked out by a vacuum pump from above.

He was being shut down. Not by the troll. By the city.

The troll's trick had worked too well. It had brought in the ultimate authority. The bureaucracy.

And bureaucracy did not care about code. It cared about forms. And Gerald had filed the wrong form. He had cited the contractor, not the city.

The worker stamped his form.

*STAMP: DENIED.*

Gerald's vision went black.

But in the darkness, a new window opened.

*SYSTEM MESSAGE: APPEAL WINDOW OPEN.* *TIME REMAINING: 00:09:59.*

Gerald had ten minutes, and he had to appeal to the Inspector General.

But he was offline. He was being shut down.

He couldn't argue. He couldn't speak.

Unless he could borrow a voice, and he looked at the rat. The rat was still there, squeaking in the draining water.

The rat had a voice. A legal voice.

*ACTION: POWER OF ATTORNEY.* *SUBJECT: RAT.*

Gerald poured his last energy into the rat. He didn't make it fight. He made it speak.

The rat stood up on its hind legs. It opened its mouth.

And in a voice that sounded like grinding gravel, it screamed.

"APPEAL! I DEMAND A HEARING!"

The worker froze.

The crab froze.

The System paused.

*HEARING INITIATED.*

Gerald felt his consciousness fading. He was becoming nothing.

But the rat was there, and the rat was the appellant.

And the appellant had a right to counsel.

Gerald was the counsel.

He had nine minutes left. And he had to win an argument against the entire municipal government, using a rat as his mouthpiece, while his core was being dissolved.

He started typing.
