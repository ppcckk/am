Chapter 165: Wet Ink Citation

# Chapter 165

Gerald pressed his thumb against the wet citation form to prevent the ink from smearing. Not a tremor. A hum. The specific, low-frequency thrum of unlicensed mana trying to force its way through load-bearing reality.

Gerald didn't look at the countdown. He looked at the target coordinates.

Sector 1. Sub-basement Level B. Directly beneath the circulation desk. Directly beneath his own core housing.

The notification flashed red against his mind's eye, demanding immediate action. **ACTION REQUIRED: CITATION OR PURGE.**

He had thirty seconds before the System's automated purger locked onto the unregistered entity. If he didn't serve a valid Citation, the Mother of All Bugs would be deleted. And if it was deleted, the structural integrity of Gerald's entire dungeon—built atop it, anchored to it—would collapse into the void.

He couldn't cite it for being unregistered. That was the trap. Unregistered meant it had no address, no footprint, no legal standing in the System's zoning laws. You couldn't cite a ghost for violating a building code it didn't technically exist under.

Gerald's pen hovered.

*Think, Gerald.*

He had spent thirty-one years learning that buildings do not care about intent. They care about measurements.

He closed his eyes and reached out with his sensory tendrils, not to attack, but to measure. He pushed past the protective wards of his own core, sinking his awareness into the chaotic, swirling mess that was the Mother of All Bugs.

It was a knot of raw data. A tangle of legacy code from the pre-System era, tangled around the foundation of the Ashford Municipal Library. It pulsed with erratic energy, trying to expand, trying to spawn entities that the current System didn't recognize.

*It's not a dungeon,* Gerald realized. *It's a leak.*

A massive, unauthorized expansion of spatial volume. The bug wasn't hiding. It was leaking into the adjacent structural supports of his own dungeon, weakening the ceiling joists of Sector 1.

The clock hit **4:50:00**.

Gerald's hand moved. He didn't write a description of a monster. He didn't write

"Unregistered Entity."

He wrote: **VIOLATION: Unauthorized Structural Modification.**

The System paused. This was a new violation type. It hadn't appeared in his usual list of citations.

**VIOLATION ACCEPTED.**

**SUB-CATEGORY: SPATIAL OVERLOAD.**

**PENALTY: IMMEDIATE CESSATION OF SPAWNING. STAT DEBUFF: 50% REDUCTION IN MANA GENERATION FOR 24 HOURS.**

The hum in the floor dropped an octave. The chaotic swirling slowed. The Mother of All Bugs, vast and ancient and angry, felt the weight of the law slam down on it like a concrete slab.

But Gerald wasn't done, and he had cited the bug. He had saved his own foundation.

But the System had flagged the citation as *self-serving*.

A Core citing a parent structure to protect itself required a counter-measure. A balancing act. The System's Inspector General was watching.

The audit trail was already forming.

A new prompt appeared, grey and bureaucratic.

**COUNTER-MEASURE REQUIRED.** **OPTION A: PAY 50,000 MANA.** **OPTION B: ACCEPT 10 MINUTES OF SYSTEM AUDIT.** **OPTION C: ISSUE A SECONDARY CITATION TO A NEIGHBORING CORE FOR COMPENSATORY DAMAGE.**

Gerald didn't have 50,000 mana. He had barely enough to keep the lights on.

He couldn't afford the Audit. The Inspector General had been circling him for weeks, looking for the one thing Gerald had kept hidden: the fact that his dungeon's Compliance Rating was artificially high because he had manually overwritten the ventilation calculations three times.

That left Option C, and he had to cite someone else. Right now. In the next ten seconds.

Gerald scanned the proximity map. The library dungeon was small, but it was surrounded by the chaotic sprawl of the Ashford Adventurer's Guild, which was technically a neutral zone, and the darker, more aggressive dungeons of the Old Market District.

There was one dungeon within range. One that had been pestering him for weeks with low-level trespassing attempts.

**CORE: 'THE GRINDING PITS'.**

It was a mid-tier dungeon, specialized in physical combat and high-damage traps. Its owner was a Core named Vargus, a bruiser who believed that code compliance was for cowards.

Gerald focused on Vargus's dungeon, located three blocks east, and he didn't have time to send a scout. He didn't have time to wait for a violation to occur naturally.

He had to create one.

Gerald pulled a thread of his own mana, a thin, fragile strand of compliance energy, and extended it into Vargus's dungeon. Not to attack. To *measure*.

He found the entrance to The Grinding Pits. It was a wide, open archway. No doors. No egress signage.

*Too easy.* Vargus knew the rules. He'd cited Gerald for

"Aggressive Zoning" last week. Vargus had patched his fire exits. He had installed emergency lighting.

Gerald scanned deeper. He looked for the structural supports. The load-bearing pillars that kept Vargus's central arena from collapsing under the weight of the spawned golems.

He found them. Three massive pillars of obsidian.

But one of them was leaning.

Not much. A fraction of a degree. Maybe two-tenths of a degree. In a normal dungeon, this would be within the tolerance margin for

"organic settling."

But Gerald wasn't a normal inspector. He was a man who had once fined a coffee shop for a wobbly table leg that measured 1.5 millimeters out of true.

He checked the tolerance levels for Vargus's dungeon category: **COMBAT-ORIENTED, TIER 3.**

The allowed deviation for Tier 3 structural supports was 0.5 degrees, and the pillar was leaning at 0.8 degrees.

*Why?*

Gerald pushed deeper, his consciousness brushing against Vargus's core. He felt Vargus's panic.

The Core knew, which had been trying to correct the lean, burning mana to reinforce it, but it wasn't enough. The foundation beneath the pillar was soft. The ground was settling.

Vargus was hiding it. He had covered the leaning pillar with decorative tape. A cosmetic fix. A non-compliant patch.

Gerald felt a surge of professional disgust so pure it tasted like copper in his mouth.

He didn't hesitate.

**CITATION ISSUED.**

**TARGET: CORE 'THE GRINDING PITS'.** **VIOLATION: STRUCTURAL FAILURE IMMINENT. UNCORRECTED LOAD-BEARING DEVIATION.** **EVIDENCE: PILLAR 3, SECTOR 2. DEVIATION 0.8°. MAX ALLOWED 0.5°.** **STATUS: CRITICAL.**

The notification didn't just pop up. It exploded across the System interface, bright gold and undeniable.

**CITATION SERVED.**

Vargus's dungeon shuddered. The debuff hit instantly.

**PENALTY: 40% REDUCTION IN STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY FOR ALL SPAWNED ENTITIES.** **PENALTY: IMMEDIATE COLLAPSE RISK FOR NON-COMPLIANT ARCHITECTURE.**

Across the city, three blocks away, a sound like thunder rolled through the streets.

The ceiling of The Grinding Pits didn't just crack. It gave way.

Gerald heard the scream of the Core over the System link. It was a digital shriek, raw and terrified.

**WARNING: TARGET STRUCTURAL FAILURE DETECTED. DO NOT ENTER.**

The cost was paid. The balancing act was complete. The System accepted the secondary citation as compensatory damage. The Audit was suspended.

Gerald slumped back into his core housing, his energy reserves drained to critical levels. The lights in his dungeon flickered, dimming from a warm amber to a sickly brown.

He had saved his home by destroying his neighbor, and he looked at the list of violations remaining. The number hadn't gone down. It had gone *up*.

**VIOLATIONS REMAINING: 14,000,000.**

One new violation had been added. The collapse of The Grinding Pits had created a debris field. That debris field was now an unregistered hazard.

Gerald rubbed his temples. His hands were shaking again. Not from exhaustion this time. From adrenaline.

A new message blinked in the corner of his vision.

**SYSTEM ALERT: INSPECTOR GENERAL 'MOTHER_OF_ALL_BUGS' HAS FILED A MOTION TO DISMISS.**

Gerald froze, and he had cited the Mother of All Bugs. He had cited it for Unauthorized Structural Modification.

But the Mother of All Bugs wasn't a bug. It was a feature. It was the root kernel of the System.

And it had just appealed his citation.

**HEARING INITIATED.** **PARTIES: GERALD HUTCHINS (CORE ID 404) VS. SYSTEM KERNEL (ID 001).** **LOCATION: THE VOID BETWEEN DATA.** **TIME REMAINING TO PREPARE: 00:59:00.**

Gerald stared at the text, and he couldn't argue with the Kernel. He couldn't cite the Kernel. The Kernel *was* the law.

But he had won against the Mother of All Bugs by measuring its footprint. He had applied the rules of physical structure to an entity that claimed to be abstract.

If the Kernel was a building, it had walls. It had egress. It had code.

And Gerald Hutchins had never, in thirty-one years, met a building he couldn't shut down.

He stood up. His core body was weak, flickering, but his mind was sharp.

He opened his inventory. He didn't pull out a weapon. He pulled out a tape measure.

The void between data was white. Silent. Empty.

Gerald stepped onto the white ground.

Opposite him, the air shimmered. A figure formed. It wasn't a monster. It wasn't a hero.

It was a man in a grey suit. Faceless. Holding a clipboard.

The Inspector General.

"Gerald Hutchins," the figure said. Its voice was the sound of a printer jamming. "You are cited for excessive bureaucracy."

Gerald didn't speak. He looked at the figure's feet.

The Inspector General was standing on a platform. A single, floating platform.

Gerald looked at the edges of the platform.

No railing, and he looked at the gap between the platform and the surrounding void.

Ten feet.

He looked at the floor of the platform.

Smooth. Polished. Slippery.

Gerald pulled his tape measure out. He didn't measure the distance. He measured the friction coefficient of the surface.

"Your Honor," Gerald said, his voice flat, dry, and terrifying. "This platform lacks a compliant fall protection system."

The Inspector General paused. The faceless head tilted.

"There is no fall protection in the Void," the figure said.

"The Void is a workplace," Gerald said. "O.S.H.A. applies."

The System chime rang out, sharp and clear.

**VIOLATION DETECTED: LACK OF GUARDRAILS IN HIGH-WORK AREA.** **SEVERITY: FATAL.**

The white floor beneath the Inspector General's feet cracked.

Gerald hadn't attacked the Judge. He had cited the Judge's own jurisdiction for non-compliance.

The crack spread. The platform groaned.

Gerald smiled. It was a thin, painful expression.

He hadn't just saved his dungeon. He had just sued the Universe.

And the Universe had no lawyer.

The platform shattered.

Gerald was thrown backward, not by force, but by the sudden absence of ground. He fell into the white nothingness, the citation form still clutched in his hand, the ink still wet, the clock ticking down to zero.

He didn't scream, and he checked his watch.

Right on time.
