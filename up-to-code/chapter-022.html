Chapter 22: Four Degrees of Tilt

# Chapter 22

Gerald held the sign at 15 centimeters above the mounting bracket and watched the legibility meter tick upward. Eight meters.

Eight-point-five.

Nine. He adjusted the angle by two degrees. The bracket was designed for a sign that faced the corridor head-on, but the System's legibility algorithm, as Gerald had spent the last three hours discovering, measured the perpendicular distance from the sign face to the nearest point on the egress path.

Not the straight-line distance from the wall.

The perpendicular distance from the sign face, and he tilted the sign down four degrees. The bracket groaned. The meter read nine-point-three.

He tilted it another two degrees. Ten-point-one.

Gerald lowered his hand and stared at the number.

Four degrees of tilt. That was all it had taken. He had been trying to make the sign bigger, brighter, bolder.

The answer was to angle it so the System's measurement line intersected the sign face at a right angle than an oblique one. He had been solving the wrong problem.

He moved to the next sign, and the east corridor had eleven egress markers.

He needed all eleven at ten meters. At four degrees of tilt each, he could do it in under an hour.

He had done six. Five left.

He was halfway through the seventh when the notification arrived, which was red. Red meant enforcement action.

*Counter-Citation filed by Core #4471 (Verdant Hollow). Provision: Joint Inspection.

Target: Core #1193 (Hutchins Sub-Basement).

Alleged violations: inadequate fire suppression (severity 3), non-compliant egress signage (severity 2), unlicensed biological entity (severity 1). Total severity: 6. Joint Inspection window: concurrent with Inspector General audit.

If target's total severity exceeds appellant's total severity, all prior Citations issued by target against appellant are voided and target receives proportional debuff.*

Gerald read it twice. Then he sat down on the concrete floor, which was not something he did, and read it a third time.

Verdant Hollow. The plant-themed dungeon four blocks east.

Gerald had cited it eleven days ago for blocking its fire exits with root growth.

Severity 4. The Citation had killed two of its thorn-sentinel spawns and reduced the regeneration rate of its remaining entities by 30 percent. Verdant Hollow had appealed.

It had lost. Gerald had won the Hearing by demonstrating that the root growth constituted a "permanent obstruction of a required egress route" under the System's fire code equivalent.

And now Verdant Hollow had filed a Counter-Citation.

Gerald had not known that was possible, and he had read the rules.

He had read them carefully. The rules said Citations could be appealed.

They did not say Citations could be countered.

He pulled up the System's full regulatory text. He searched for "Counter-Citation." It was there.

Buried in a subsection about "reciprocal enforcement provisions" that he had skimmed because it was under the heading "Disputes Between Cores of Equivalent or Lower Rating." Verdant Hollow's rating was 58.

Gerald's was 67. Equivalent or lower. The provision applied.

Gerald closed the text, and he opened it again. He searched for "Joint Inspection." It was a procedural option available when the Counter-Citation alleged violations that overlapped with the original Citation's subject matter.

Fire exits.

Gerald had cited Verdant Hollow for blocked fire exits. Verdant Hollow was citing Gerald for inadequate fire suppression. Both were fire code.

The overlap triggered the Joint Inspection provision automatically, which meant the Inspector General would audit both dungeons at the same time.

The findings would be compared.

Whichever Core had the higher total severity would lose. Gerald's violations, if confirmed, would total 6.

Verdant Hollow's original violation, severity 4, was already on record, and gerald had cited it for additional violations since then. Root growth blocking a secondary exit, severity 2.

Missing emergency lighting, severity 1. Total on record: 7.

So Gerald was at 6. Verdant Hollow was at 7. Gerald was lower. Gerald should win.

Unless the Inspector General found more.

Gerald looked at his dungeon. He looked at the fire suppression requisition, still pending. He looked at the east corridor, where five signs still needed tilting. He looked at the rat, which was sitting in the middle of the main corridor, not in a designated habitat.

Unlicensed biological entity.

Severity 1. That was already on Gerald's list. The rat was the rat.

It had been there since day one.

He had tried to relocate it to a "designated habitat," which was a corner of the sub-basement he had partitioned with drywall, but the rat had refused to go there. It preferred the corridor.

Gerald had not been able to compel it.

If the Inspector General noted the rat in the corridor, that was a violation. Severity 1. Gerald's total would be 7.

Verdant Hollow's total would be 7. A tie.

The provision did not specify what happened in a tie. Gerald searched for it. The text was silent.

He searched for "tie" in the regulatory body. Nothing. He searched for "equal severity." Nothing.

This was a gap. A genuine gap in the rules.

Gerald's mouth went dry, and he had spent thirty-one years finding gaps in the municipal code.

Gaps were not neutral. Gaps were where things went wrong.

Gaps were where the contractor who built the addition without a permit stood in the doorway and said "well, the code doesn't specifically say I can't do this." And the inspector who did not know how to fill the gap let it stand. And then the addition collapsed.

He needed to be below 7. He needed to fix the rat.

He walked to the rat. The rat looked at him. It sat in the middle of the corridor with its paws tucked under its chest and its tail curled around its body in a posture of complete indifference.

Gerald crouched. "I need you to go to your habitat."

The rat blinked.

"Your habitat is approximately four meters in that direction. It is partitioned. It has a nesting substrate. It meets the spatial requirements for a single biological entity of your size class."

The rat groomed its whiskers.

Gerald stood up. He went to his filing cabinet.

He pulled out Form 11-B, the one he had been unable to file for the rat because the System did not recognize the rat as a "spawned entity" under its definitions.

The rat had been there before Gerald became a Core. It was a pre-existing biological entity. The System's spawn registry did not cover it.

But the System's habitat requirements did. The notification that had listed "unlicensed biological entity" as a violation did not specify whether the entity was spawned or pre-existing. It simply said the entity was not in a designated habitat.

Gerald had been trying to file a spawn registration. He should have been trying to file a habitat designation.

He pulled out a form he had never used.

Form 7-A. Designation of Existing Biological Entity Habitat.

It was in the appendix, and he had not read the appendix.

He filled it out. Entity: rat.

Species: Rattus norvegicus.

Habitat location: northeast corner, partitioned enclosure, 1.2 square meters.

Substrate: shredded cardboard. Access: open corridor.

He filed it.

The System accepted it.

A notification appeared. *Designation confirmed. Entity is now in compliant habitat. Violation resolved.*

Gerald checked his severity total. It updated. The rat violation dropped. His total was now 5.

He was below 7. He was safe.

He should have felt relief. He did not feel relief. He felt the specific cold sensation of having found a gap and then realized that someone else could find it too.

Verdant Hollow had filed a Counter-Citation under a provision Gerald had not known existed, and the provision existed because the System had written it.

The System had written it because it expected Cores to find gaps.

The System expected this, and the Inspector General was coming in forty-four hours. Gerald had five violations on record, total severity 5. Verdant Hollow had seven. Gerald was safe on the severity comparison.

But he was not safe on the audit itself.

The Inspector General would note new violations. If the Inspector General noted two more violations at severity 1 each, Gerald would be at 7.

If it noted a severity 2 violation, Gerald would be at 7.

If it noted anything at severity 3 or above, Gerald would be at 8.

He needed to be compliant. He needed to be clean. He needed to pass the audit with no new findings.

He looked at his fire suppression requisition. Still pending.

He had no fire suppression system, which was a severity 3 violation.

It was already on his list. It was part of his 5.

If the Inspector General confirmed it, it would still be part of his 5; it would not increase his total because it was already counted.

But what if the Inspector General noted it as a different violation? What if it noted "absence of fire suppression" as a new finding than confirming the existing one?

The severity would be the same.

The total would be the same. It would not matter.

Unless the Inspector General noted a violation that Gerald did not know he had.

Gerald paced the dungeon slowly, as he once paced buildings before inspections. He looked at the walls.

He looked at the floor.

He looked at the ceiling. He checked the handrails. He checked the lighting.

He checked the door widths. He checked the egress paths.

He checked the ventilation grates, and he found a crack in the east wall.

Three millimeters wide. Running from the floor to a height of about sixty centimeters.

It was not structural. He was almost certain it was not structural. But he could not prove it was not structural without a professional assessment, and he could not get a professional assessment because he was the professional and the System did not allow Cores to hire outside contractors.

He pulled out a caliper. He measured the crack.

Three millimeters, and he measured it at the top. Two millimeters. He measured it at the bottom.

Four millimeters.

He opened his reference material. The System's structural code did not specify a maximum crack width.

It specified a maximum deflection, which he could not measure without instruments he did not have.

It specified a maximum moisture intrusion, which he could measure by checking for water staining. No water stains appeared.

He checked for water staining. None existed.

He closed the reference material. He did not know if the crack was a violation.

He did not know if the Inspector General would flag it.

He could not fix it without materials, and he could not prove it was not a violation without instruments.

He left it.

He went back to his filing cabinet. He pulled out Form 14-C, the Temporary Compliance Extension he had filed. He read the acceptance notification.

It granted him a 72-hour extension on "pending resource allocation." It did not specify which resources. It did not specify which pending items.

It was a blanket extension.

Gerald read it again. Blanket extension. Pending resource allocation. All pending items.

He pulled out his fire suppression requisition. He checked the status. Pending.

He checked the extension.

It covered it, and he pulled out his ventilation upgrade request. Status: pending. Extension: covered.

He pulled out his electrical panel replacement order. Status: pending. Extension: covered.

Every pending item in his dungeon was covered by the extension. The Inspector General could not audit a pending item and call it a violation because the extension meant it was "temporarily compliant pending resource allocation." The extension was a shield.

Gerald sat with this for a moment.

Then he felt the cold sensation again, and the extension was a shield, but it was a shield that applied to pending items. What about items that were not pending?

What about the crack in the wall? No requisition existed for the crack.

No order was pending; the crack was not pending either.

The crack was a finding.

If the Inspector General found the crack and classified it as a structural violation, the extension would not cover it. Severity 2. Gerald's total would be 7. Tie with Verdant Hollow.

Gerald needed to create a pending order for the crack. He needed to file a repair requisition. If the repair was pending, the crack was covered.

He pulled out Form 6-F. Repair Requisition. He filled it out.

Location: east wall, main corridor. Description: linear fissure, 3mm maximum width, no moisture intrusion, no visible deflection.

Requested action: professional structural assessment and repair if warranted, and he filed it.

The System accepted it. Status: pending.

Gerald checked his extension. It covered all pending items. The crack was now pending. The crack was covered.

He exhaled.

Then a new notification appeared. White. Informational.

*Joint Inspection provision updated. Appellant (Core #4471) has filed a Supplementary Counter-Citation. Additional alleged violations: structural fissure (severity 2), ventilation deficiency (severity 1).

Updated total severity: 10.

Joint Inspection comparison now: Target total vs. Appellant total.*

Gerald read it. He read it again.

Verdant Hollow had added the crack.

Verdant Hollow knew about the crack. Verdant Hollow had filed a Supplementary Counter-Citation alleging the crack as a violation. The severity was 2.

Verdant Hollow's total was now 10.

But Gerald's total was still 5. The crack was pending. It was covered. It should not count.

Unless the Supplementary Counter-Citation overrode the extension. Unless the Joint Inspection provision allowed the appellant to allege violations that were pending, and the Inspector General was required to assess them regardless of extension status.

Gerald searched the text. He searched for "pending" in the Joint Inspection section. He searched for "extension" in the Joint Inspection section.

The text was silent.

He searched for "Joint Inspection" and "extension" together. Nothing.

He searched for "Joint Inspection" and "pending" together. Nothing.

Another gap.

Gerald sat still, and the gap meant that no one knew whether the extension protected him during a Joint Inspection.

If the Inspector General followed the extension rules, Gerald was at 5 and safe. If the Inspector General followed the Joint Inspection rules and ignored the extension, Gerald was at 7 and tied.

If the Inspector General assessed the crack independently and found it worse than Gerald's estimate, Gerald was at 8 and lost.

He had forty-four hours. He could not fix the crack.

He could not remove it, and he could not prove it was not a violation; could only hope that the Inspector General read the extension as a shield.

Or he could change the comparison.

Gerald opened the citation log.

He looked at Verdant Hollow's record. Total severity 10.

He could add to it.

If he could find another violation at Verdant Hollow, he could cite it. If he cited it before the Joint Inspection, the severity would increase. Verdant Hollow's total would go from 10 to 11 or higher.

Gerald's total would need to be lower than that.

But Gerald could not enter Verdant Hollow's dungeon.

Citations required physical presence.

Gerald could not leave his dungeon. He was a Core.

He was the dungeon, and he looked at the citation log again. He looked at the provision text. "Citations require the target to be physically present to serve."

Physically present. Not in the dungeon.

Present.

Gerald had served Citations by sending his spawn entities into the target's dungeon; the rat had been his first attempt. The mold spores had been his second.

The water leak had been his third.

Gerald looked at the water leak. It was a slow drip from a pipe junction in the northeast corner.

It had been there since before Gerald became a Core, which was not a spawn. It was not a biological entity. It was a plumbing failure.

But it was mobile. Gerald could direct it. He had never tried to direct it. He had treated it as a violation to be fixed, not as an entity to be deployed.

He looked at the water leak. He looked at the direction of Verdant Hollow.

Four blocks east, and the water leak was a drip.

It was not a stream. It was not a flood.

It was a slow, persistent drip that Gerald had been meaning to fix for three weeks.

He could not send a drip four blocks east, and he could send it through the plumbing.

Gerald pulled up his dungeon map. He traced the water lines.

His dungeon sat beneath the Ashford Municipal Library.

The library's plumbing connected to the municipal water main. The municipal water main ran east along Birch Street. Verdant Hollow's dungeon footprint, as Gerald had mapped it from his Citation data, sat beneath the east end of Birch Street.

The water main connected them.

Gerald opened Form 6-F again, and he filled out a new requisition. Location: northeast pipe junction.

Description: slow leak, approximately 2 milliliters per minute.

Requested action: redirect flow to municipal main, east branch.

He filed it.

The System accepted it. Status: pending.

Gerald stared at the notification. He had just requested that his water leak be redirected into the municipal water main that ran beneath Verdant Hollow's dungeon.

He had not cited Verdant Hollow, and he had not entered Verdant Hollow.

He had not served anything.

He had filed a plumbing requisition.

And if the water reached Verdant Hollow's dungeon, it would create a moisture condition. Moisture intrusion. That was a violation.

Gerald could cite it if he could serve the Citation. He could not serve it.

He was not physically present.

But the water would be physically present. The water was part of Gerald's dungeon. The water was Gerald.

Gerald looked at the provision text again. "Citations require the target to be physically present to serve."

The target. Not the Core. The target.

The target of the Citation.

If Gerald cited Verdant Hollow, the target was Verdant Hollow's dungeon. Verdant Hollow's dungeon was physically present.

It was four blocks east, which was not moving. It was a fixed location.

The provision did not say the Core had to be present, and it said the target had to be physically present. The target was the dungeon. The dungeon was always physically present.

Gerald had been misreading the provision. He had been assuming it meant he had to go there.

It did not, which meant the target had to exist in a fixed location.

Which all dungeons did.

He had been unable to cite Verdant Hollow because he had been trying to serve the Citation in person. He had never tried to serve it through the water.

He opened the Citation form. He filled it out. Target: Core #4471, Verdant Hollow.

Violation: moisture intrusion via uncontrolled water flow. Severity: 1.

Service method: physical delivery via connected municipal infrastructure, and he signed it.

The System processed it.

The notification came back in four seconds.

*Citation served. Target notified. Target has 24 hours to appeal.

Joint Inspection comparison updated: Appellant total severity: 11.

Target total severity: 5.*

Gerald was safe, and he was at 5. Verdant Hollow was at 11. The gap was six.

Even if the Inspector General found three new violations at Gerald's dungeon, he would still be lower.

He allowed himself to exhale.

Then the notification came.

*Core #4471 (Verdant Hollow) has filed an appeal.

Hearing scheduled: 12 hours.

Hearing format: structural logic debate. Appellant's argument: Gerald Hutchins' water redirection constitutes an unlicensed modification of municipal infrastructure, which is itself a violation under System code Section 14.2.

If appellant wins the appeal, the Citation is voided, the severity reverts to 10, and Gerald Hutchins receives an additional violation at severity 2 for the unlicensed modification.*

Gerald read it. He read it again.

Section 14.2. He had not read Section 14.2.

He searched for it, which was there. "Unlicensed modification of shared municipal infrastructure by a Core constitutes a violation of severity 2."

He had just modified the municipal water main.

He had filed a plumbing requisition, but the requisition was pending. Pending meant approved.

Pending meant compliant.

But the Citation form itself, the act of serving the Citation through the water main, was not a plumbing repair. It was a modification. The water main now carried a directed flow.

That was a modification. And it was unlicensed because Gerald had not filed a Form 6-F for the Citation delivery.

He had filed a Form 6-F for the leak redirection, but the Citation delivery was a separate act.

He had created a new violation. Severity 2. His total was now 7.

And the appeal hearing was in twelve hours. If he lost, his Citation would be voided, his total would revert to 7, and he would have an additional severity 2 violation on record, bringing his total to 9.

Verdant Hollow's total would revert to 10, and he would be at 9. Verdant Hollow at 10. He would win by one point.

But the Inspector General was coming in forty-four hours. If the Inspector General found anything at all, Gerald would be at 10 or higher. He would lose.

Gerald looked at the twelve-hour window, and he looked at the appeal. He looked at the hearing format.

Structural logic debate. He had won the last hearing. He had won it by proving the fire exits were non-compliant.

He could win this one.

But he needed to prove that the water redirection was not a modification.

He needed to prove it was a repair. A repair is not a modification. A repair restores existing function.

A modification changes function.

The water main's function was to carry water. Gerald had not changed that function.

He had directed the flow.

Direction is not function. A pipe that carries water east is still a pipe that carries water.

He pulled out Form 6-F. He looked at the language.

"Redirect flow to municipal main, east branch." Redirect.

Not modify.

Not alter. Redirect.

He pulled out the municipal code, the real one, the paper one from his old job. Section 4.1. "Flow direction is an operational parameter, not a structural modification." He had written that section himself.

Thirty years ago. He had written it himself.

The System's code was different from the municipal code. The System's code was Section 14.2. It said modification. The municipal code said flow direction was operational.

The hearing was a structural logic debate.

Both Cores would argue structural logic. Gerald could argue that flow direction was not structural. He could argue that the water main's structure was unchanged.

The pipe was the same pipe, and the joints were the same joints. The pressure rating was the same.

Only the direction of flow had changed, and direction was not structure.

He could win.

But Verdant Hollow would argue that the System's definition of modification was broader than the municipal definition. The System said "unlicensed modification." It did not define modification. It left the definition to the hearing.

Gerald would need to define it. He would need to argue that modification required a change to the physical structure of the infrastructure, not a change to its operational parameters. He would need to cite the municipal code. He would need to cite his own work.

He opened his filing cabinet. He pulled out the old binder. Thirty-one years of code interpretations.

He flipped to Section 4.1. His handwriting.

His interpretation.

His definition, and he had defined modification for the City of Ashford; he had not defined it for the System. But the hearing was a structural logic debate. Structural logic was his domain.

He had twelve hours. He had a definition. He had a precedent. He had a thirty-year-old binder full of interpretations that he had written with his own hand.

Gerald sat down at his desk. He opened the binder. He started preparing.

At hour four, he found the page he needed. Section 4.1, paragraph 3.

His handwriting. His interpretation. "Modification of shared infrastructure requires alteration to the physical envelope of the structure.

Operational parameters including but not limited to flow direction, pressure variance within rated tolerance, and temperature fluctuation within rated tolerance do not constitute modification."

He had written it. He had signed it. He had filed it with the City of Ashford on March 14, 1993.

He had a precedent. He had a definition. He had the hearing.

At hour nine, a new notification appeared. White. Informational.

*Hearing format updated.

Appellant has requested a "Code Origin" challenge. Under this provision, both Cores must demonstrate the origin of their cited code interpretation. If the interpretation originates from a System-registered authority, it is binding.

If it originates from a non-registered source, it is inadmissible.*

Gerald stared at the notification.

The City of Ashford was not a System-registered authority. Gerald's code interpretation was not a System-registered interpretation.

It was a municipal document, which was thirty years old. It was his handwriting.

It was inadmissible.

He had prepared for a structural logic debate. He had prepared

He had prepared for a structural logic debate. He had not prepared for a jurisdictional challenge.

Gerald removed his glasses. He cleaned them on his shirt. He put them back on. He read the notification again.

The City of Ashford was not a System-registered authority. He had known this.

He had always known this, and the System was not a municipality.

The System was not a state. The System was not a federal agency.

It was, as far as Gerald could determine, a self-executing regulatory framework with no parent body. No charter. No enabling legislation.

Which meant it had no registered authorities.

Which meant no interpretation was admissible.

Which meant the hearing was, by its own procedural logic, a nullity.

Gerald wrote this down. He underlined it twice. He stared at the underlining.

He had three hours, and he opened a new page. He wrote: "Query: If the System has no registered authority, can any interpretation be admitted?" "Query: Is the System itself a registered authority?" He wrote: "Query: Who registered the System?"

He had no answers. He had questions. He had a binder full of questions that were not, strictly speaking, answers.

At hour eleven, his Compliance Rating dropped from 67 to 66. The ventilation penalty had been applied.

He felt it as a faint pressure behind his eyes, like the beginning of a sinus infection.

He noted it. He did not address it. He lacked time to address it.

At hour eleven and forty minutes, a new notification appeared.

*Hearing begins in twenty minutes. Appellant's Code Origin challenge is scheduled for the first procedural phase. Appellant has submitted the following origin claim: "System Core Registration Protocol v.4.2, Section 7(b), as applied to the Respondent's Core Registration event." *

Gerald read it twice.

The appellant was claiming that Gerald's own Core Registration event—the moment Gerald's body had dissolved into a dungeon footprint, the moment Gerald had filed a structural deficiency report on his own reflection—constituted a System-registered authority. The appellant was arguing that Gerald's interpretation, submitted at the moment of registration, had been accepted by the System. So binding.

Gerald sat still, and the appellant was using Gerald's own filing against him.

Gerald had registered. Gerald had submitted an interpretation.

The System had accepted it.

The appellant was now claiming that Gerald's interpretation was admissible under the provision that made Gerald a Core, which was elegant. It was, structurally, sound.

Gerald had thirty minutes. He had a binder. He had a definition. He had a precedent.

He did not have a counter-argument, and he had never been outmaneuvered in a hearing; had never lost a citation. He had never, in thirty-one years of municipal compliance work, been unable to find the page that answered the question.

The notification appeared.

*Hearing begins.*
