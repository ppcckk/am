Chapter 421: Served Through Wall

# Chapter 421

The first citation went through the wall.

Not physically. The brick held firm, solid and unmoving under the relentless hammering of the rain.

The sound, however, was unmistakable.

A dull, resonant *thud* that vibrated in Gerald's molten core, distinct from the structural stress of the collapsing library above. It was the sound of a formal notice being served.

Gerald did not look up from the schematic he had drawn in the mud. His stylus, a piece of rebar sharpened to a point, hovered over the blueprint of the eastern corridor.

"Stop," he said, his voice flat. "Stop hammering."

Mira, standing knee-deep in the flooded sub-basement, lowered her clipboard. Her face was streaked with grime, her eyes wide with a mixture of exhaustion and disbelief.

"I didn't do it," she said.

"Then who did?"

"The System."

Kael, the party leader whose sword was currently acting as a temporary prop for a sagging support beam, groaned.

"Gerald, we don't have time for bureaucratic noise. The water is rising. If we don't reinforce the foundation before the next tremor, the whole library comes down on our heads."

Gerald ignored him, and he tapped the schematic with a muddy finger.

"Violation 402-C: Unlicensed Interference with Core Operations.

The hammering is not a physical attack.

It is a procedural challenge. It implies I am unable to manage my own facility without external coercion.

"

"It's a spy," Kael said, pointing to the crack in the foundation where the shadow had vanished. "That thing that ran out? It brought friends. Or it brought the System's attention."

"The shadow was a spy," Gerald corrected, not looking up. "The hammering is a Citation.

There is a difference, and a spy is a pest.

A Citation is a legal obligation.

"

Another *thud*. This one louder. The water in the basement rippled, sending small waves lapping against Gerald's base.

Mira stepped forward, her boots squelching.

"Gerald, look at me. The Inspector General is gone.

He gave you time. You spent it.

You are now vulnerable.

If you ignore the Citations, the penalties stack. We know this. You taught me this.

"

"I am not ignoring them," Gerald said.

He finally looked at her. His eyes were dull, the glow of his core dimmed by the exertion of holding the library's structure together.

"I am processing them.

There is a difference in the paperwork.

"

He reached into the wet earth and pulled out a form. It wasn't paper. It was a slab of obsidian, etched with glowing red runes. The text was tiny, cramped, and aggressively bureaucratic.

**NOTICE OF NON-COMPLIANCE: EXCESSIVE DEPENDENCY ON EXTERNAL STRUCTURAL SUPPORTS.** **PENALTY: 15% REDUCTION IN STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY.** **APPEAL WINDOW: CLOSED.**

Gerald stared at it. His fingers tightened around the obsidian until it cracked slightly.

"Excessive dependency," he muttered. "I am supporting the library.

The library is not part of the dungeon, which is municipal property.

I am merely providing… incidental stabilization.

"

"It's a trap," Mira said. "You're trying to save the library. The System is punishing you for it.

That's the loophole they used. You're a dungeon core, but you're acting like a contractor.

The system doesn't recognize the distinction.

It sees you holding something that isn't yours.

"

Kael slammed his hand against the support beam.

Dust rained down from the ceiling.

"So stop holding it!

Let the library fall!

It's not your problem!

"

"It is my problem," Gerald said, his voice dropping an octave. "The library is the public domain.

Public domain requires public safety standards.

If I let it collapse, I am liable.

Not just to the System. To the city.

To the people who worked there. To the children who read there.

"

"You're a rock," Kael said, frustrated. "You don't care about children. You care about code."

"I care about the code because the code protects the children," Gerald snapped. "Do not confuse the tool with the purpose."

Mira sighed, rubbing her temples.

"Gerald, look at the numbers.

Your Compliance Rating is dropping.

If it hits zero, the dungeon collapses. If the dungeon collapses, the library falls. And if the library falls, you're still dead.

The logic is circular.

"

Gerald looked at the obsidian slab. He turned it over. On the back, in fainter runes, was a footnote.

**SUB-CLAUSE 9: DELEGATION OF LIABILITY VIA THIRD-PARTY GUARANTEE.**

He blinked. "Delegation."

"What?" Mira asked.

"Delegation of Liability. Via Third-Party Guarantee." Gerald's mind raced, though his face remained impassive.

"The System penalizes me for holding the library.

It views the library as an external load. But if the library is guaranteed by a third party, the load is not mine. It is theirs.

"

"Who is the third party?" Kael asked.

"The City of Ashford," Gerald said.

Mira stared at him.

"The City is bankrupt, Gerald. The mayor fled. The council is disbanded."

"The legal entity remains," Gerald said. "And the insurance policy remains."

He looked at Kael.

"You have a guild charter, correct?"

Kael frowned. "Yes.

The Iron Vanguard.

Registered with the Adventurers' Guild.

We're licensed, bonded, and insured for dungeon exploration.

"

"Are you insured for structural collapse caused by adjacent dungeon instability?"

Kael paused. He pulled a small, leather-bound book from his pack. He flipped through the pages, his finger tracing the lines.

"Clause 44-B: 'Liability for collateral damage resulting from environmental hazards within the dungeon perimeter, provided the party is acting under official exploration mandates.'"

Gerald pointed at the book.

"Read it again. Focus on 'environmental hazards' and 'collateral damage.'"

Kael read it. His eyes widened.

"It covers… water damage.

And structural failure. As long as we're here to 'explore.'

"

"We are not exploring," Mira said. "We are repairing."

"We are *documenting*," Gerald said. "Exploration includes documentation of hazards. If you document the hazard of the collapsing library, you are exploring. And if you are exploring, you are bonded."

He turned to Mira.

"Get your clipboard. Write it up. 'Hazard Assessment: Structural Instability of Adjacent Municipal Structure.

Mitigation Strategy: Temporary Support by Core Entity.

Liability Transfer: Activated via Party Insurance Policy.'

"

Mira looked at him like he had grown a second head.

"You want to shift the liability for the library's collapse onto the adventurers' insurance?"

"I want to file a Form 11-C: 'Third-Party Assumption of Risk.'" Gerald said.

"It's a standard provision for public works projects involving private contractors. The System recognizes private contractors. Adventurers are private contractors.

The System recognizes insurance. The System recognizes liability transfer.

"

Mira opened her mouth to argue, then closed it. She looked at Kael.

Kael looked at his book. He looked at Gerald. Then he looked at the sagging ceiling.

"If this works," Kael said slowly, "we're not just saving the library. We're getting paid for it."

"Insurance doesn't pay for repairs," Gerald said.

"It pays for damages.

But it prevents the penalty. The penalty is what kills us.

The money is incidental.

"

Mira wrote. Her pen moved fast, scratching against the wet slate.

"'Party Iron Vanguard assumes liability for structural support provided by Core Entity 001, per Clause 44-B of the Adventurers' Guild Code.'"

She finished. She held up the slate.

Gerald took it. He pressed his thumb into the wet surface, leaving a muddy print. He focused. He pushed his will into the slate, forcing the System to recognize the transaction.

Nothing happened.

The rain continued to hammer the library. The water continued to rise.

Gerald frowned. He tried again. He pushed harder, his core flaring with a dull, orange light.

"Why isn't it working?" Mira asked.

"The System is lazy," Gerald said. "It doesn't like new filings.

It likes pre-existing categories.

We are trying to create a new category. We are trying to make adventurers 'contractors.' The System sees them as 'threats.'

"

"Can you change their classification?" Kael asked.

"I can't change your class," Gerald said. "But I can change the environment."

He looked around the basement. He looked at the water. He looked at the debris.

"Kael," Gerald said. "Stand in the center of the room. Hold your sword up. Point it at the ceiling."

"Why?"

"Because you're pointing at a hazard. That's 'threat assessment.'"

Kael sighed, but he did it. He raised his sword, pointing it at the sagging beam.

Gerald closed his eyes. He reached into the System's code, not with force, but with suggestion. He didn't fight the rules. He used them.

*Definition: Hazard.* *Source: Structural Failure.* *Responder: Adventurer.* *Action: Mitigation.*

He felt the System hesitate, which was a glitch in the matrix, a tiny crack in the logic. The System wanted to punish Gerald for holding the library.

It wanted to fine him.

But Kael was holding the sword. Kael was the one interacting with the hazard.

If the hazard was being handled by the party, Gerald was not handling it. Gerald was merely… providing the ground.

The obsidian slab in Gerald's hand glowed. The red runes faded. New runes appeared, white and clean.

**LIABILITY TRANSFER ACCEPTED.** **PENALTY SUSPENDED.** **CORE INTEGRITY STABILIZED.**

Gerald exhaled. The tension in his core eased. The water stopped rising.

Kael lowered his sword.

"Did we just… scam the System?"

"We filed a claim," Gerald said. "It's not a scam. It's a deduction."

Mira slumped against the wall, sliding down into the mud.

"You're terrifying. You know that?"

"I'm efficient," Gerald said. He looked at the ceiling.

The beam was still sagging, but it wasn't moving faster, and the pressure had equalized.

"The library is safe. For now."

"For now?" Kael asked.

"The spy is still out there," Gerald said. "The rivals know we're weak. The Inspector General is watching.

And now that we've used a loophole, the System will close it. We have to act before it does.

"

He looked at Mira.

"We need to finish the repairs. Not just the basement. The whole library.

We need to make it up to code.

Fully."

Mira looked at him.

"You want to rebuild the library?"

"I want to make it *compliant*," Gerald said.

"If the library is compliant, the System has no jurisdiction over it. It becomes municipal property. Pure and simple.

No dungeon. No core.

No audit."

Kael laughed, a harsh, barking sound.

"You're trying to delete yourself."

Gerald didn't smile. He didn't frown. He just looked at the hole in the ground.

"I'm trying to retire," he said. "With benefits."

The rain stopped.

Not gradually. Instantly. The clouds parted, not because of the weather, but because the System had decided the scene was complete.

A new sound echoed through the basement. Not a thud. A chime.

A golden ticket materialized in the air, floating just above Gerald's core.

**NOTIFICATION: AUDIT COMPLETE.** **FINDINGS: 3 VIOLATIONS RESOLVED. 1 VIOLATION PENDING.** **PENDING VIOLATION: UNAUTHORIZED USE OF EXTERNAL ENTITY AS LIABILITY SHIELD.** **PENALTY: CORE FRAGMENTATION.** **DEADLINE: 1 HOUR.**

Gerald looked at the ticket. His hand trembled.

Mira saw his face.

"Gerald?"

"The loophole worked," Gerald said. "But the System noticed."

Kael stepped forward.

"What does it mean? Core fragmentation?"

"It means I break," Gerald said.

"It means I stop being one thing. I become many.

I become scattered. I lose my coherence. I lose my voice.

"

He looked at his hands. The mud on his fingers was glowing faintly. The light was spreading, moving up his arms.

"I have one hour," Gerald said. "We need to fix the library.

We need to make it compliant.

If we do, the violation is cured. If we don't…

"

He looked at Mira.

"I will not be here to tell you that."

Mira stood up. She wiped her face.

"Then we don't waste time."

She grabbed Kael's arm.

"Get the ladder. Get the cement.

Get the wood.

We have sixty minutes to rebuild a library.

"

Kael looked at Gerald.

"Can you help us?"

Gerald looked at the rising glow on his arms. He looked at the library above.

"I can give you the blueprints," Gerald said. "But I can't build. I can only… observe."

He closed his eyes.

The light consumed him. Not violently. Quietly.

The golden ticket dissolved.

And in the silence that followed, Gerald Hutchins was no longer a single point of light. He was a thousand.

He was everywhere in the basement, and he was in the cracks.

He was in the dust. He was in the water.

He was still there. But he was not one.

And the clock had started.
