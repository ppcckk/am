Chapter 295: Wet and Slippery

# Chapter 295

Gerald ran.

His legs did not pump like a sprinter's. They pumped like a man late for a quarterly review.

Efficient.

Rhythmic. Hateful of wasted energy. The stone floor of the sub-basement shifted beneath him, the geometry of the dungeon groaning under the weight of the City Manager's unauthorized dissolution.

Walls were vanishing into mist, erasing rooms that had been inspected and approved last Tuesday.

He needed a corridor. Any corridor. One that wasn't currently being deleted from existence.

Behind him, the City Manager stood frozen, the ballpoint pen still clutched in his hand like a talisman of bureaucratic absurdity. The System's logic loop held. *Improper Instrument of Record.* The error message pulsed in the air, red and angry, but it did not stop the Mayor's agent. It merely paused the destruction. For now.

Gerald turned a corner that had not existed ten seconds ago. A new wall had risen, blocking the path back to the library.

Good.

Structural integrity was holding, albeit barely. He checked the corner. Sharp angle.

No protective bumper. Not up to code for high-traffic public access, but acceptable for a dungeon core under siege.

He marked it mentally.

Sandra, and he needed to find Sandra before the loop broke. The System would resolve the contradiction eventually. It would decide that a pen was not a form, or that a citation was not a weapon, and then it would resume deleting the dungeon. Or worse, it would delete Gerald for creating an illegal loop.

A rat skittered across his path.

It froze, confused by the shifting architecture. Gerald did not look down.

He stepped over it. The rat was his spawn, but it was not his concern. Not yet.

He found the ventilation shaft. It was narrow, meant for mold spores, not men.

But Gerald was fifty-four, and he had spent thirty years squeezing into crawl spaces.

He shimmied into the gap, his clipboard scraping against the metal ducting. The air here was stale. Humidity at eighty percent.

A violation, and he noted it, even as he crawled. *Ventilation inadequate for human occupancy. Recommend immediate remediation.*

He emerged into the storage room.

Or what used to be the storage room. Now it was a void. A jagged hole in the floor where the archives should have been.

Sandra was not there.

Instead, standing on the edge of the void, was a figure made of shadow and bad decisions.

It wore the livery of the Ashford Guild of Adventurers.

High collar.

Polished boots. The boots were scuffed, though.

A violation of guild dress code, section 4, subsection B.

"Mr. Hutchins," the figure said. The voice was smooth, cultured, and entirely unwelcome.

Gerald stopped crawling. He stood up, brushing dust off his trousers. He did not raise a weapon. He raised his clipboard.

"Who are you?" Gerald asked.

"I am Valerius Vane," the figure said. He bowed, a stiff, mechanical motion. "I am a Core of the Third Tier. And I am here to collect your debt."

"I have no debt."

"You have a Compliance Rating of 47. You are operating below the minimum threshold for municipal integration. The System has flagged you for asset forfeiture. I have been appointed as the receiver."

Gerald looked at the void. The walls were still dissolving, slow but steady. The loop was weakening. He could feel it in his teeth, a low hum of static.

"You are not a receiver," Gerald said. "You are a trespasser on private municipal property."

Vane smiled. It was not a nice smile.

"The System recognizes all Cores as sovereign entities.

Your 'municipal' status is a technicality. A glitch. I intend to fix it.

"

Vane raised a hand.

The shadows in the room coalesced. They formed a shape.

A large, jagged shape.

A golem, but poorly constructed. Asymmetrical limbs. A head that was too small for the torso.

Gerald's eye twitched.

"That is not a golem," Gerald said. "That is a pile of rubble with delusions of grandeur."

"It is my spawn," Vane said. "It is level thirty. It will crush you."

"It has no center of gravity," Gerald said. He pointed to the figure.

"Look at the left leg.

It is thinner than the right, which has no support structure. It is not load-bearing.

It is a hazard.

"

Vane blinked. "What?"

"If it takes one step, it will collapse," Gerald said. He pulled a pen from his pocket.

A fountain pen.

Proper ink. Proper nib.

"I am citing this entity for Structural Instability.

Violation 12-B: Improper Weight Distribution.

"

Vane laughed. "You cannot cite my spawn. It is part of my dungeon."

"Everything in my dungeon is subject to the Code," Gerald said. "And you are in my dungeon. Therefore, your spawn is subject to the Code."

He wrote. The scratch of the nib was loud in the silence.

**System Alert: Citation Issued.** **Target: Valerius Vane's Spawn.** **Violation: Structural Instability.** **Penalty: Immediate Collapse.**

The golem took a step, which did not attack. It did not roar.

It simply folded.

The left leg buckled under its own weight. The torso slumped forward. The head, too small to support the neck, snapped down.

The whole structure collapsed into a heap of stone and shadow dust.

Vane's smile vanished.

"What did you do?"

"I applied physics," Gerald said. "And code. Mostly code."

Vane stepped back, and he looked at the pile of rubble.

He looked at Gerald. His face was pale.

"You cheated."

"I measured," Gerald said. "You built poorly. I cited it."

The hum in Gerald's teeth grew louder. The walls around them flickered. The void in the floor widened.

"You cannot hide here," Vane said. "The Mayor knows you are alive.

He knows you are resisting, and he will send more than me. He will send the Inspector General.

"

Gerald paused.

The Inspector General.

The System's auditor. The one who checked everything. The one who could not be tricked by pen loops or structural nitpicks.

Gerald looked at the clock on the wall. It was missing. He looked at his watch. 2:14 AM.

"The audit is not until next quarter," Gerald said.

"It is now," Vane said.

"Because you broke the rules. You created a loop.

The System detected an anomaly.

It is escalating.

"

Vane straightened his collar.

"I am leaving.

But you are trapped, and the dungeon is being seized.

You will be dismantled.

"

"You have not served the citation," Gerald said.

"I don't need to be served. I know I am cited."

Vane stepped into the shadows. He vanished.

Gerald was alone, and the void was spreading. The storage room was gone. The ventilation shaft was caving in. The mold spores were dying, their small, fuzzy forms turning to gray ash.

Gerald ran again.

He needed Sandra. He needed to find her before the system finished the seizure. If she was gone, the dungeon would be empty.

An empty dungeon was just a hole. A hole could be filled.

Or paved over, and he ran to the main corridor. The handrails were gone. He slid down a slope, grabbing at jagged stones.

His hands bled.

He did not care.

He reached the intersection. The main hall.

Sandra was there, and she was sitting on a bench.

A real bench. Wooden. Sturdy.

Rated for three people, but currently holding only one. She was reading a book.

"Gerald," she said. She did not look up. "You are late."

"The City Manager tried to erase us," Gerald said. He was breathing hard. His heart hammered against his ribs.

"I know," Sandra said. She turned a page. "I heard the walls screaming."

"We need to move."

"We are not moving," Sandra said. "This is my home. You promised to fix the plumbing. You have not fixed the plumbing."

"The plumbing is gone," Gerald said. "The dungeon is being seized."

Sandra looked up. Her eyes were not confused. They were sharp. Dangerous.

"Seized by whom?"

"By the Mayor. And Valerius Vane."

Sandra closed her book.

"Vane. He is a slumlord."

"He is a Core," Gerald said.

"He is a bad landlord," Sandra said. She stood up. She was small, but she filled the room. "Did you cite him?"

"I cited his spawn. It collapsed."

"Good," Sandra said. "But he will appeal. He will use the loophole."

"What loophole?"

"The Appeal of Jurisdiction," Sandra said. "If Vane claims that his spawn was not in your jurisdiction, the Hearing will be delayed. And during the delay, the seizure continues."

Gerald felt a cold knot in his stomach. He had not thought of that. He had thought of physics. He had not thought of legal technicalities.

"He will claim the dungeon is not municipal," Gerald said. "He will say it is a private dungeon."

"And you will say it is public," Sandra said. "Because you have handrails. You have signage. You have a parking lot."

"I do not have a parking lot," Gerald said.

"Make one," Sandra said.

"There is no space."

"Excavate," Sandra said. "Fast."

Gerald looked at the void. The void was large. It could fit four cars. Maybe six.

"I need a permit," Gerald said.

"You are the Core," Sandra said. "You are the Authority. Issue the permit."

Gerald looked at his clipboard. It was blank. The previous citations had faded. The System was resetting the forms.

He pulled out his pen, and he wrote.

**Permit Issued: Temporary Parking Structure.** **Location: Sub-basement, Zone 4.** **Capacity: 12 Vehicles.** **Condition: Immediate Excavation.**

The ground shook. Stone cracked. The void widened. A ramp formed, sloping down into the darkness.

It was ugly. It was uneven. The grade was too steep. The turning radius was too tight.

But it was a parking lot.

Sandra nodded.

"Acceptable."

A sound came from the darkness of the new ramp. Heavy footsteps. Metal on stone.

A vehicle rolled into view. It was not a car. It was a carriage made of bone and iron. Pulled by two skeletal horses.

Valerius Vane stepped out, and he was not alone.

Behind him stood three other Cores. Their auras were thick, suffocating.

They were high-tier. Ancient.

Vane smiled. "You built a parking lot."

"It is a requirement for public access," Gerald said. "You cannot seize public infrastructure."

Vane stepped forward. He placed a hand on the bone carriage.

"I am not here to seize the dungeon. I am here to evict you."

He pulled a document from his coat. It was thick. Yellowed. Sealed with black wax.

"Gerald Hutchins," Vane said. "You are being audited by the Inspector General. Now."

Gerald looked at the document. The System flickered.

The loop was gone, and the pen had not worked. The parking lot had not worked.

The Inspector General was here.

But the IG was not present. Vane was holding the notice.

"Service by proxy," Gerald said. "Invalid."

"Valid if the proxy is a sworn officer of the Court," Vane said. "I am a sworn officer."

"You are a slumlord," Gerald said.

"I am a Core of the Third Tier," Vane said. "I have authority."

The three other Cores stepped forward, and they raised their hands. The air grew heavy.

Gravity increased.

Gerald's knees buckled.

Sandra stepped in front of him.

She did not look at the Cores. She looked at Gerald.

"Did you check the exit?" she asked.

"The exit is sealed," Gerald said.

"Did you check the emergency exit?" Sandra asked.

Gerald looked at the wall behind Vane. There was no door. There was no handle.

"I failed to install the emergency exit," Gerald said.

Sandra sighed. It was a long, disappointed sigh.

"You never check the fire code," she said. "You only check the zoning."

"I am a building inspector," Gerald said. "Not a fire marshal."

"Today you are," Sandra said. She reached into her pocket. She pulled out a key. A small, brass key.

She threw it to Gerald.

It landed at his feet.

"The fire suppression system," Sandra said. "It is manual. It is located in the ceiling of Zone 4. You have to activate it manually."

"There is no system," Gerald said.

"There is," Sandra said. "I installed it. Last week. While you were measuring door widths."

Gerald picked up the key. It was cold.

He looked at Vane. Vane was laughing. The Cores were advancing.

Gerald looked at the ceiling. High above, near the library floor, was a pipe. A single, red pipe.

He ran, and he ran toward the wall. He did not have a ladder. He did not have a harness.

He jumped.

His hands grabbed the pipe. His feet dangled.

The pipe was rusty, which was not rated for climbing.

It was not up to code.

It held.

Gerald pulled himself up. The Cores shouted. Magic crackled around his ankles.

He kicked them away. He reached the valve.

He turned the key.

The pipe groaned. Water rushed through it. Not clean water. Brown, sludgy water.

The ceiling burst.

The water fell. It hit Vane. It hit the Cores. It hit the bone carriage.

The water was not just water, which was the dungeon's own waste. The sewage from the library above. The water that Gerald had failed to filter.

The water that had caused the mold.

The Cores screamed. They were not undead.

They were elemental, and they were made of earth and shadow. Water dissolved them.

Vane's skin bubbled.

"You poisoned us!"

"It is not poison," Gerald said, hanging from the pipe. "It is non-compliant discharge. Violation 14-A: Improper Waste Disposal."

The water spread. It flooded the parking lot. The Cores retreated, slipping in the slime.

Vane retreated last. He looked at Gerald with pure hatred.

"This is not over," Vane said.

"It never is," Gerald said.

The water level rose. It covered Gerald's feet. He was hanging above it.

Sandra walked through the water. She did not get wet. The water parted around her.

She looked up at Gerald.

"You cited the sewage," she said.

"I cited the disposal," Gerald said.

"The System will fine you," Sandra said.

"I know," Gerald said.

"Good," Sandra said. "Now we have leverage."

She pointed to the door. The real door. The one that led to the library. It was open.

"The Inspector General is coming," Sandra said. "And he does not like wet floors."

Gerald climbed down. He landed in the water with a splash.

He looked at the open door. He looked at the flooded dungeon.

He looked at Sandra.

"We have to leave," Gerald said.

"We cannot leave," Sandra said. "The dungeon is flooded. It is a health hazard. We have to clean it up before we evacuate."

"Clean it up?" Gerald asked. "With what?"

Sandra smiled. It was a small, dry smile.

"With the heroes," she said. "The ones who are coming to loot us. They can help mop."

A horn blew. Distant. Muffled by stone and water.

Adventurers.

Gerald groaned. He hated adventurers. They never read the signs. They never respected the handrails.

But if he cited them for improper footwear in a wet zone, maybe they would listen.

He picked up his clipboard. It was soaked. The ink had run.

He wiped it on his trousers.

"Lead the way," Gerald said.

Sandra pointed to the stairs.

Gerald followed, and he did not look back at the water. He did not look back at Vane's retreating shadow.

He looked at the stairs.

They were wet.

They were slippery.

They had no non-slip coating.

Gerald pulled out a new form, and he wrote.
