Chapter 117: Cite Me

# Chapter 117 The Inspector General did not look like an inspector. He looked like a tax audit that had gained sentience and decided to murder the taxpayer.

He stood in the center of Gerald’s ruined sub-basement, untouched by the cracked concrete, the pooling black sludge, or the faint, sulfurous haze of the hazardous spill. His charcoal gray suit appeared to absorb the dungeon's ambient light rather than reflect it.

His face was a perfect oval of smooth, poreless skin, devoid of expression.

He held a clipboard. Not a tablet. A clipboard.

With paper.

And a fountain pen that hovered above it, scratching invisible notes into the air.

Gerald remained seated on a pile of rubble he had identified as structural debris from the previous ceiling collapse. He adjusted his glasses. They were cracked, a spiderweb fracture across the left lens that distorted the Inspector General into a jagged, angular monster.

"Core 8940-G," the Inspector General said.

The voice was not loud, but it vibrated in Gerald’s molars. "You have filed a Citation against a non-Core entity. A human law enforcement officer.

Specifically, Officer Dale Miller, Badge Number 492."

Gerald cleared his throat.

It felt like swallowing gravel.

"The citation was valid. Section 15.2, Duty to Notify.

The officer was on premises. He observed a Class IV hazardous spill, and did not notify the municipal hazardous materials unit within the mandated five-minute window.

He attempted to escalate the situation via radio. This constitutes a procedural delay that increased the risk of civilian exposure."

The Inspector General tilted his head, and the motion was fluid, almost liquid. "Officer Miller is not a Dungeon Core. He is not part of the System’s administrative lattice.

He is a biological entity operating under municipal statute.

Your jurisdiction does not extend to the city of Ashford’s police force."

"Jurisdiction," Gerald said, forcing the word through his dry tongue, "is defined by presence and exposure.

The spill is within my dungeon footprint. The officer is within the egress route. The hazard is emanating from my core.

Therefore, I am the Responsible Party, and the Responsible Party has the right to enforce compliance within the immediate zone of liability.

If I do not cite the delay, I am liable for the consequences of that delay.

I cited the delay to mitigate my own liability."

The Inspector General paused. The fountain pen stopped moving.

"You are attempting to use the Code of Civil Liability to bypass the System’s jurisdictional boundaries," the Inspector General said. "This is a novel interpretation."

"It is the only interpretation," Gerald said. "If I do not hold the responder accountable for the response time, then the response time is irrelevant.

If the response time is irrelevant, then the hazard is unmanaged. If the hazard is unmanaged, my Compliance Rating drops further. I cannot allow my Compliance Rating to drop.

I have spent three hundred and forty-two days building this dungeon to code. I will not lose it to a police procedural error."

The Inspector General looked at the black sludge. It was spreading toward the library’s main support pillars.

"And what," the Inspector General asked, "is the penalty for the officer?

He has no stats. He has no debuffs. He is flesh and bone.

How do you enforce a Citation against a man who does not exist in the System?"

Gerald felt a cold sweat prickle at the base of his neck. This was the trap. He had known it was a trap when he pressed *Serve*, but he had needed the distraction. He had needed the police to look at the sludge, not at him.

"I do not enforce it against him," Gerald said. "I enforce it against the System’s record of him."

The Inspector General’s eyes narrowed. "Explain."

Gerald pulled up his interface. The text was blurry through the cracked lens, but the logic was sharp. He had been studying the fine print for six hours while waiting for the IG to arrive. He had found it in Appendix Z, Section 9, Subsection C: *Administrative Cross-Referencing.*

"The System logs all non-Core entities that interact with a Core," Gerald said. "When Officer Miller entered my dungeon, he generated a Presence Log.

When I served the Citation, the System accepted it because the conditions of the violation were met.

The Citation is now part of his Presence Log. It is a mark on his record. It does not change his physical stats.

It does not slow his feet. It does not reduce his health."

Gerald stood up. He walked to the edge of the sludge, staying within the marked yellow tape he had installed around the perimeter three years ago.

"But," Gerald continued, "it changes his *Clearance Status*. Officer Miller now has a pending Compliance Violation on his record. According to the System’s Inter-Operability Clause, any entity with a pending Compliance Violation is restricted from entering any Core with a Compliance Rating above 50. My rating is currently 88."

The Inspector General stared at him.

"That means," Gerald said, "that Officer Miller cannot re-enter this dungeon.

He cannot pursue me, and he cannot search for evidence. If he steps back inside, the System will reject his entry.

It will treat him as a trespasser. And since he is not a Core, the System’s defense protocols—my traps, my mold, my confused rat—will treat him as an unregistered hostile entity. The System will kill him to protect my dungeon.

Not because I want it to. But because the Citation forces it to."

Silence stretched in the basement. The water drip from the ceiling echoed like a hammer strike.

The Inspector General looked down at his clipboard. He made a checkmark.

"You have weaponized bureaucracy," the Inspector General said.

His voice held no anger. Only a strange, cold appreciation. "You have created a legal paradox that forces the System to choose between protecting a human life or upholding a Core’s rights.

The System will protect the Core.

You have effectively issued a death threat to a police officer by filling out a form."

"It is a warning," Gerald said.

"He will not re-enter. He will call HAZMAT. HAZMAT does not have a Presence Log.

HAZMAT is a contractor. Contractors have different rules."

"HAZMAT," the Inspector General repeated.

"You assume the City will send HAZMAT?

You have cited the police. The police will not call HAZMAT if they believe the threat is contained by the Core.

They will wait. They will watch. And when they realize the Core is hostile, they will not send cleaners.

They will send a strike team."

Gerald’s stomach dropped, and he had calculated the System’s logic. He had not calculated the humans’ logic.

"If they send a strike team," Gerald said, "and they enter without a Citation... they are not violating Section 15.2. They are violating... nothing. They are just attacking."

"Precisely," the Inspector General said.

"The Citation protects you from *procedural* negligence. It does not protect you from *assault*. You have bought yourself time, Gerald Hutchins.

You have cited the delay.

But you have not cited the weapon."

The Inspector General turned to leave. He dissolved into smoke at the doorway, leaving the clipboard floating in the air for a second before it vanished.

Gerald was alone, and the sirens were still wailing outside. Muffled, distant, but getting closer.

He looked at the black sludge. It was eating the concrete. It was eating the rebar. It was eating the foundation.

He needed a defense. He needed a barrier that worked against physical force, not just compliance.

He pulled up his build menu.

*Current Resources: 420 Mana. 150 Structural Integrity Points.*

*Available Constructs: Wall, Door, Trap, Hazard, Spawner.*

He could build a wall.

But a wall was static. If the police brought breaching charges, the wall would fail. He could build a trap.

But traps required line-of-sight and activation triggers. If the police wore helmets, thermal goggles, armor...

Gerald looked at the mold spores hiding in the corner. He looked at the single rat, which was gnawing on a piece of drywall.

He looked at the Citation interface.

*Violation: Failure to Report Hazardous Spill.* *Status: Pending Appeal. Time Remaining: 00:04:12.*

The appeal window was closing. If the System upheld the citation, the debuff would apply to Officer Miller’s *record*. If the System rejected the citation, the citation would be void.

Gerald needed the citation to stay. He needed it to be a permanent stain on the officer’s record, not just a temporary debuff.

But he also needed to survive the next five minutes, and he looked at the ceiling.

The hole where the ceiling had collapsed was a perfect rectangle, four feet by four feet.

Above it, he could see the library’s second floor. The support beams.

The HVAC ducts, and he had no fire code compliance. He had no electrical code compliance. He had barely passed structural code.

But he had *Ventilation*.

Section 7.1: Air Exchange Rate.

Gerald had spent weeks fixing the ventilation. He had installed fans. He had ducted fresh air from the library above; had ensured the air flow was laminar, consistent, and free of recirculation.

He looked at the ducts. They ran directly above the black sludge.

An idea formed. It was dangerous. It was reckless. It required him to break a rule he had never broken before.

He could *over-pressurize* the ventilation system.

If he pushed more air into the dungeon than the exhaust vents could handle, he would create a positive pressure environment. The air would try to escape, and it would push out through every crack, every hole, every gap in the door frames.

It would not hurt the police. It would not create a fire. It would not create a flood.

But it would make it impossible to breathe if the air filters were compromised.

And the air filters were compromised. The black sludge was evaporating. The fumes were toxic. If he pushed the air *in*, he was forcing the toxic fumes *out*.

Unless...

Unless he reversed the flow.

If he reversed the fans, he could suck the air *out* of the dungeon, creating a negative pressure zone. The air would rush in from the library above, through the hole in the ceiling.

That would pull the toxic fumes up into the library.

It would poison the librarians. It would kill the students. It would make the City of Ashford responsible for a biological hazard in a public building.

Gerald felt sick. Literally.

His stomach churned, which was against every rule he had ever upheld, and it was an ethical violation of the highest order. It was a war crime.

But it would shut down the police response.

If the library was contaminated, the police could not enter. They would have to evacuate the building. They would have to call the CDC. They would have to seal the area.

They would not come down here. They would wait.

Gerald looked at the fan controls, and they were manual. He had installed them himself.

They were painted yellow.

Safety yellow.

He looked at the clock.

00:03:45 on the appeal, and he looked at the hole in the ceiling.

He looked at the rat.

The rat squeaked. It looked at him. It looked like it was judging him.

Gerald closed his eyes. He thought of the fire marshal who had cited him for a missing smoke detector in 2014.

He had appealed, and he had lost.

He had paid the fine. He had felt shame.

He was not a criminal. He was an inspector.

But an inspector’s job was to ensure safety.

If the police entered, they would die. If he did nothing, they would die.

If he reversed the flow, the librarians might get sick. But they would not die. The library had automatic fire suppression.

It had HVAC systems that could filter particulates. It was safer above than below.

He had to choose, and the sirens were louder now. Boots were hitting the stairs. Heavy, tactical boots. Not rubber-soled patrol boots.

SWAT.

They were here.

Gerald opened his eyes. He walked to the fan control panel. He placed his hand on the lever.

The System message flashed.

*Warning: Reversing Airflow violates Section 7.4: Directional Consistency. Penalty: Immediate Structural Instability Rating Reduction (-15). Potential for Ceiling Collapse: High.*

He didn’t care about the penalty. He cared about the outcome.

He grabbed the lever.

"Dispatch," a voice crackled from the tunnel entrance.

"This is Sergeant Kowalski. We are entering the sub-basement. Core is active.

Hostile."

Gerald looked at the tunnel. He saw the flashlights. The barrels of rifles.

He looked at the ceiling, and he pulled the lever.

The fans screamed. The air rushed backward, whipping the black sludge into a mist. The dust from the ceiling poured down.

The police hesitated at the threshold.

Then, the ceiling groaned.

A crack appeared in the main support beam. A jagged, white line running down the concrete.

Gerald watched the beam. He watched the crack.

He had to hold the airflow. He had to keep the pressure negative. He had to keep the toxic air from rising into the police team, but he had to keep it from filling his own dungeon too.

It was a balancing act. A structural compromise.

The crack widened. Dust fell.

The Sergeant shouted, "Pull back! It’s unstable!"

They retreated. Just an inch.

Gerald held the lever. His hand shook.

The System flashed red.

*Structural Integrity: 42%. Critical Failure Imminent.*

He looked at the black sludge. It was no longer pooling. It was floating in the air, a cloud of particulate matter.

He looked at the police. They were backing up the stairs.

He had bought them time. But he had broken his home.

The ceiling beam snapped.

Not the whole ceiling. Just a section. A chunk of concrete the size of a microwave fell into the sludge. Splash.

The police ran.

Gerald was alone again.

But then, a notification appeared. Not from the System.

From his *Citation* interface.

*Appeal Accepted.*

*Target: Officer Dale Miller.*

*Result: Citation Void. Violation Not Found.*

Gerald stared at the text.

Void?

Why void?

He had proved the violation. He had cited the delay.

Then, a new message.

*Reason for Void: Jurisdictional Error. Subject was not present at time of Citation.*

Gerald looked at the tunnel entrance.

Officer Miller was not there, and the Sergeant had been there.

Gerald had cited the *wrong person*. He had assumed the first boot was Miller. It was Kowalski. Kowalski had no Presence Log entry for the initial entry because he had entered *after* the citation was served.

Gerald felt the blood drain from his face, and he had wasted the Citation; he had triggered the negative pressure. He had damaged his structure. And the legal shield he had built was gone.

The System message changed.

*Compliance Rating: 12/100.*

*Warning: Multiple Critical Violations Detected.*

*Action: Core Termination Initiated.*

Gerald looked up. The ceiling was cracking all over. The fans were screaming. The sludge was rising.

And from the shadows of the library above, a new voice spoke.

"Mr. Hutchins," a woman said.

She stepped into the light from the hole in the ceiling.

She was not police. She was not HAZMAT.

She was wearing a suit. A blue suit. With a badge that glowed with a faint, purple light.

*System Enforcement Division.*

"You failed to cite the correct entity," she said. "And you have violated the Structural Code of the Core’s Home Domain. You are under arrest."

Gerald looked at the woman. He looked at the falling ceiling.

He had one option left, and he could surrender.

Or he could claim that the woman, who was now in his dungeon, was trespassing.

He raised his hand. He pointed at her.

"Violation," Gerald whispered. "Failure to Wear Appropriate Footwear in a Hazardous Zone."

The woman smiled. It was not a nice smile.

"Cite me," she said. "Try it."

Gerald looked at his interface. He had no Mana left. He had no Citations left.

He had nothing but his hands.

And the ceiling was falling.
