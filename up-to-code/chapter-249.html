Chapter 249: Compliance Violation

# Chapter 249

The heavy oak door to the Library's main floor shuddered. Not from wind. From impact.

Gerald Hutchins did not flinch. He stood at the base of the stairs, his clipboard held against his chest like a shield, his pen capped and waiting. The dust from the ceiling drifted down onto his grey cardigan, settling in the creases of his collar. He brushed it off with two fingers, checking his cuff for contamination before looking up.

Above the floorboards, the shouting grew louder. Metal scraped against wood. A table overturned.

"Compliance violation," Gerald muttered, not to Linda, but to the air itself. "Unpermitted structural modification of public space. Noise ordinance breach."

Linda, the library's head archivist, was crouched behind the circulation desk. She held a stapler like a dagger. "Gerald, they're killing the fiction section."

"They are trespassing on a Class 4 Restricted Zone," Gerald corrected, his voice dry and flat. "I am currently drafting the detention order. If you wish to assist, please ensure the liability waivers are pre-filled. Section 4, Clause C: Assumption of Risk regarding sentient furniture."

Linda stared at him. "The chairs are trying to eat them."

"Then the chairs are non-compliant with the Public Safety Act," Gerald said. "I will issue a citation to the seating arrangement.

Focus on the humans, Linda.

We need their energy signatures. We need souls to pay for the Emergency Repair Permit."

The crash above intensified. A thud. Then silence.

Gerald checked his internal clock. 4:12 PM.

The library closed at 5:00. They had forty-eight minutes to process the intake before the evening shift arrived.

If the evening shift saw adventurers bleeding on the rug, there would be an inquiry.

Gerald hated inquiries. Inquiries led to paperwork that had no end.

He pulled a fresh form from the stack in his hand.

**FORM 7-A: AUTHORIZATION FOR ADVENTURER DETENTION AND EXPLOITATION (EMERGENCY CLAUSE 19)** **SUBJECT:** Unlicensed Intrusion / Hazardous Biological Entity **STATUS:** Pending Review

He walked up the stairs. One step.

Two.

The wood groaned under his weight.

He stopped at the top landing, looking through the crack in the door.

Three figures stood in the center of the reading room. One in heavy plate armor, one in leather with a bow, and one in robes that smelled of ozone and burnt hair. They were confused.

They were looking at the walls, which had begun to weep water. They were looking at the floor, which had developed a subtle, spiraling tilt that defied Euclidean geometry but adhered strictly to Gerald's new drainage plan.

The armored man shouted something. The wizard pointed a glowing staff at the ceiling.

Gerald opened the door.

"Excuse me," he said.

The three adventurers spun around. The wizard raised his staff. The knight drew a sword. The archer loosed an arrow.

It struck Gerald's clipboard. The paper absorbed the kinetic force, crumpling but holding. The arrow clattered to the floor.

"Please cease unauthorized projectile discharge within a federal building," Gerald said. He tapped his pen against the clipboard. "You are in violation of Municipal Code 14-B: Prohibited Discharge of Energy Weapons in Non-Combat Zones."

The wizard blinked. The glow on his staff flickered out. "Who... who are you?"

"Gerald Hutchins.

Inspector." Gerald held up a badge that did not exist until he decided it did. "This library is under renovation. You have entered a construction zone without a hard hat.

You have failed to sign the liability waiver.

And you have damaged public property."

He pointed to the overturned table.

"That is a fineable offense."

The knight stepped forward. "We are here to clear the dungeon. We sensed a rise in mana. We are heroes."

"You are trespassers," Gerald said. "And you are hurting the building."

He snapped his fingers.

The floor beneath the knight shifted.

Not violently. Precisely. A tile slid two inches to the left, disrupting the knight's stance.

Simultaneously, a bookshelf rotated, blocking the archer's line of sight. The wizard tried to cast, but the air in the room thickened, the humidity spiking as Gerald adjusted the ventilation system to 'Mold Prevention High.'

The wizard coughed. He gagged.

"Gross," the knight said, trying to swing his sword at a wall that was no longer there.

Gerald walked into the room.

He did not run.

He did not shout. He walked with the measured pace of a man who knew his route.

He approached the knight, who was struggling to regain balance on the tilting floor.

"Sir," Gerald said. "Please submit to a background check."

The knight swung. The sword passed through where Gerald's head had been a microsecond before.

The blade hit the bookshelf.

The shelf did not break. It absorbed the impact, shuddering, and then redirected the force back through the floor.

The knight's knees buckled. He dropped to all fours, gasping.

The archer tried to flee toward the exit.

The doors slammed shut. Not locked.

Just closed.

Gently. But firmly.

The wizard raised his staff again. "I will incinerate you!"

"Fire safety violation," Gerald said. He pointed to the nearest smoke detector.

It blinked red.

"You are triggering the suppression system without a permit.

This is a Class A felony."

A mist sprayed from the ceiling. Not water. It was a cold, viscous gel that smelled of lavender and industrial cleaner.

It hit the wizard's staff, shorting out the magical conduit. The wizard dropped the staff, sliding on the wet floor.

Gerald looked at the three of them.

They were incapacitated. Not dead. Not maimed.

Just... detained.

He pulled a pen from his pocket.

"Linda," he called out. "Bring the waivers. And the soul extraction forms."

Linda hurried in, her face pale. She held a stack of papers. "Gerald, they're people."

"They are unpermitted entities causing structural damage," Gerald said. He knelt beside the knight. "Sir, you are being charged with three counts of trespass, one count of illegal combat within municipal limits, and one count of endangering public infrastructure. Your penalty is labor and energy contribution."

The knight looked up, blood trickling from his nose where the impact had jarred him. "You... you can't just..."

"I can," Gerald said. "I have the authority. The System recognizes my Compliance Rating as the governing law of this zone. You are in my jurisdiction."

He touched the knight's shoulder.

A notification popped up in Gerald's vision.

**TARGET DETECTED.** **CLASS: WARRIOR (ELITE).** **SOUL VALUE: 12.** **PROPOSED USE: EMERGENCY REPAIR PERMIT (PARTIAL PAYMENT).** **APPROVE?**

Gerald stared at the number. Twelve souls.

He needed one hundred.

He had three adventurers. If they all paid, he might get sixty. Not enough.

He looked at the wizard. The wizard was trying to crawl away, slipping in the lavender gel.

"Value?" Gerald asked the air.

**MAGE (MID-TIER).** **SOUL VALUE: 18.**

**ARCHER (LOW-TIER).** **SOUL VALUE: 8.**

Total: 38 souls.

Gerald frowned. He looked at the cracks in the ceiling.

They were widening.

The library's foundation was shifting.

If he didn't fix it in the next hour, the basement would collapse into the sewer lines. A sewer line collapse meant a health inspection.

A health inspection meant Gerald had to fill out Form 9000. He dreaded Form 9000.

"Not enough," he said aloud.

Linda handed him the waiver. "Gerald, what are you doing?"

"Calculating leverage," he said. He turned to the knight.

"Sir.

You have a party. You have equipment. You have a reputation.

The System places a premium on 'High-Value Social Assets.' If you agree to sign a ten-year contract to serve as a 'Guest Lecturer' in this dungeon's 'Combat Simulation Zone,' the System will grant you a 'Hero's Immunity' waiver.

This waiver converts your trespass fine into a 'Contribution Credit.'"

The knight frowned. "A lecture?"

"About how not to enter a building without checking for load-bearing warnings," Gerald said. "The System rewards educational compliance. It counts as 'Public Service.'"

The knight hesitated. "If I sign... I get out?"

"You get processed," Gerald said. "And your debt is offset. But you must remain in the building until the audit is complete. That could take days."

The wizard, hearing this, stopped crawling. He looked at Gerald. "Days?"

"Standard processing time for high-value assets," Gerald said. "Unless you want to pay the fine in immediate soul energy. Which you can do right now."

The wizard went pale. "No."

"Then you sign," Gerald said. He held out a pen.

The wizard reached for it. His hand shook. He signed.

**TRANSACTION COMPLETE.** **DEBT OFFSET: 18 SOULS.** **CREDIT BALANCE: -82 SOULS.**

Gerald moved to the archer. The archer signed faster, terrified of the gel.

**TRANSACTION COMPLETE.** **DEBT OFFSET: 8 SOULS.** **CREDIT BALANCE: -74 SOULS.**

Gerald turned to the knight. The knight looked at the two unconscious forms of his companions. He looked at Gerald. He looked at the cracking ceiling.

"I need a lawyer," the knight said.

"You are in a dungeon," Gerald said. "The legal system is suspended. Only the Code applies."

Gerald held out the pen.

The knight took it. He signed.

**TRANSACTION COMPLETE.** **DEBT OFFSET: 12 SOULS.** **CREDIT BALANCE: -62 SOULS.**

Gerald looked at the number.

Sixty-two souls short. The ceiling groaned. A large chunk of plaster fell, missing Gerald's head by inches.

He did not move. He just noted the location on his map.

"Linda," he said. "Did we miss anyone?"

Linda checked her clipboard. "There was a rogue. He stayed by the door. He's... he's gone."

Gerald looked at the entrance. The doors were still closed. But the window behind them was broken. Shattered from the outside.

A face appeared in the broken window.

It was not a human face.

It was a goblin's face, but larger. Wearing spectacles.

Holding a hammer.

"Hey!" the goblin shouted. "You didn't file the permit for the noise!"

Gerald walked to the window. He looked at the goblin. He looked at the broken glass.

**VIOLATION DETECTED:** **UNLICENSED DEMOLITION.** **PENALTY: 200 SOULS OR IMMEDIATE INCINERATION.**

Gerald smiled. It was a thin, tight smile.

"Linda," he said. "Cancel the waivers."

"Gerald?"

"We don't need the adventurers anymore," Gerald said. "We have a contractor."

He opened the window.

The goblin blinked. "What? I'm going to sue you for property damage!"

"You are trespassing on a private easement," Gerald said. "And you have just committed a felony by breaking the glass. The fine is two hundred souls. You have until I finish this sentence to pay."

The goblin looked at the hammer in his hand. He looked at the dark interior of the library, where the floor was beginning to boil with black water.

"I have... a coupon?" the goblin stammered.

Gerald pulled out a new form.

**FORM 11-C: INVOLUNTARY LABOR CONTRACT (FOR MINOR INFRACTIONS).**

"Sign," Gerald said. "Or burn."

The goblin signed.

**TRANSACTION COMPLETE.** **DEBT OFFSET: 50 SOULS.** **CREDIT BALANCE: -12 SOULS.**

Gerald looked at the number. Twelve short.

He looked at the three adventurers, still slumped on the floor, unconscious but alive. He looked at the goblin, who was now holding the hammer and looking terrified.

"Twelve souls," Gerald said. "Linda, go to the basement. There is a rat."

Linda's eyes widened. "The rat? Gerald, that's... that's a living creature."

"It is a non-compliant biological entity," Gerald said. "It has no permit.

It is a pest.

Pest control fees are deducted from the dungeon's general operating fund. If the fund is negative, the dungeon core may liquidate assets."

He looked at Linda.

"The rat is an asset."

Linda turned and ran toward the stairs.

Gerald looked at the ceiling. The crack was now a fissure. Dust was falling in sheets.

He pulled out his pen. He filled out the final section of the Emergency Repair Permit.

**PAYMENT METHOD: ASSET LIQUIDATION.** **ASSET: MUS (LOCALIZED POPULATION).** **VALUE: 1 SOUL (ESTIMATED).**

He looked at the number.

Twelve rats. Or one rat, twelve times?

No.

The System valued souls based on complexity. A rat was one soul. A human was twelve.

A goblin was fifty.

He needed twelve rats. Or one human.

He looked at the knight. The knight was waking up.

Gerald walked over to the knight. He crouched down.

"Sir," Gerald said. "You have breached the contract. The penalty for breach is forfeiture of one's essence."

The knight's eyes went wide. "I... I didn't know!"

"Ignorance of the code is no defense," Gerald said.

He reached out and touched the knight's forehead.

The System chime sounded. Loud. Harsh.

**ERROR.** **CORE PROTECTED BY HEROIC IMMUNITY WAIVER.** **LIQUIDATION DENIED.**

Gerald froze. His hand hung in the air.

He had forgotten the waiver.

He had signed them into a contract, not just detained them.

The contract protected them from immediate execution. It locked their souls.

They were safe. But they were useless for payment.

Gerald stood up. He looked at the ceiling. The fissure was splitting. Water was pouring in now, mixing with the dust, turning the floor into mud.

He had twelve souls short. And the building was going to collapse.

Then, a new notification appeared. Not from the repair permit. From his inbox.

**INCOMING TRANSMISSION: INSPECTOR GENERAL.** **SUBJECT: ANOMALY DETECTED IN SECTOR 4.** **NOTICE: AUDIT SCHEDULED IN 10 MINUTES.**

Gerald looked at the timer. Ten minutes.

He looked at the goblin, who was still holding the hammer.

"Hey," the goblin said. "You're bleeding."

Gerald touched his nose. Blood. He hadn't noticed the headache. The strain of holding the structure together was breaking his own mind.

"Help me," Gerald said to the goblin.

The goblin hesitated. "Do what?"

"Hit the load-bearing wall," Gerald said. "I need to collapse the east wing to redirect the foundation stress. It will buy us thirty seconds."

The goblin looked at the wall. "You want me to break it?"

"I am authorizing it," Gerald said. "Under Emergency Protocol 7. Sign the waiver."

He held out the pen.

The goblin looked at the collapsing ceiling. He looked at Gerald's blood-stained shirt. He looked at the three unconscious adventurers who were about to be crushed.

The goblin took the pen.

He signed.

And then he swung the hammer.

The wall exploded.

Not out. In.

The floor dropped. Gerald fell with it, down into the dark, into the mud, into the waiting mouths of the things he had not yet named.
