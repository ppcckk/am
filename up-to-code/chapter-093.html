Chapter 93: Code Without Softening

# Chapter 93

The ventilation shaft in sub-level three had collapsed at 0600. Gerald knew this because the mold spores stopped growing, which meant the humidity dropped below the threshold they needed, which meant his only remaining biological asset was entering dormancy. He stood in the corridor and watched the spores contract into pale, dry husks along the ceiling.

He had forty-eight hours before the audit, and the notice had arrived as a System-wide broadcast, the kind that appeared in every Core's interface simultaneously. INSPECTOR GENERAL ADVISORY: All Cores within Administrative Zone 4 are subject to unscheduled Structural Integrity Audit within 48-hour window. Cores failing audit will be subject to immediate suspension pending review.

Gerald read it three times. He had never received an audit notice before.

He had never even seen one.

The Inspector General's audits were supposed to be periodic, announced weeks in advance, with remediation windows. This was not that. This was a shakedown.

He pulled up his Compliance Rating.

Fifty-two. The number sat in his interface like a wound that wouldn't close.

At forty, the System would begin stripping his structural bonuses.

At twenty, it would start removing his permanent resistances. At zero, it would unmake him.

He had been at seventy-two an hour ago. Twenty points gone for looking at a Registry entry he wasn't supposed to see.

Gerald walked to the collapsed shaft and measured the gap with his interface calipers. Sixty-one centimeters.

The code required eighty for any corridor exceeding four meters in length.

His corridor was six meters long, and he was in violation.

He had been in violation for three weeks and hadn't noticed because the shaft had been intact. Now it was not, and the violation was active, and the audit was coming.

He could fix it. He could widen the shaft, install a proper ventilation fan from his spawn materials, re-route the water leak to provide moisture for the mold. It would take time he didn't have and materials he didn't have, because his Compliance Rating was suppressing his spawn production.

Gerald sat down on the floor of his own dungeon and thought about this for a long time.

Then he opened his interface and started looking for a different approach, and the audit notice had a procedural header. Gerald read it as he had read every notice in thirty-one years of municipal work: for its omissions, not its content.

The audit scope was undefined. No criteria listed. No inspector identified. No scheduling confirmation.

He pulled up the Inspector General's procedural code. Section 7, Subsection C: "Structural Integrity Audits shall be preceded by a minimum 14-day notice period, shall identify the auditing entity by Core number, shall define the scope of inspection in writing, and shall provide the audited Core with an opportunity to remediate identified deficiencies prior to the audit date."

Fourteen days. Gerald had been given two.

No inspector identified. Gerald had no idea who was coming.

No scope defined. He didn't know what they were going to check.

No remediation window. He couldn't fix anything before they arrived.

The audit was procedurally invalid.

Gerald felt something warm move through his interface. Not satisfaction. Something closer to the feeling he used to get when a contractor showed up for a final walkthrough with a clipboard and a smile and thirty minutes of preparation. The feeling of knowing the answer before you asked the question.

He opened a new form. Not a Citation. He had not used this form before, but it sat in his interface like all others: waiting for anyone who knew where to look.

FORM 19-G: Procedural Deficiency Report.

Gerald had filed Form 19-G twice in his life. Both times against the City of Ashford's own planning department, for failing to provide adequate public notice before rezoning hearings. Both times the reports had been dismissed.

He started filling it out.

REPORTING CORE: 7-4412 (Gerald Hutchins) SUBJECT OF REPORT: Inspector General, Administrative Zone 4 DEFICIENCY TYPE: Procedural Non-Compliance SPECIFIC VIOLATION: Audit notice issued with insufficient notice period (2 days vs. Required 14 days), failure to identify auditing entity, failure to define inspection scope, failure to provide remediation window. RECOMMENDED ACTION: Audit voided pending re-issuance with full procedural compliance.

He submitted it, and the response came in eleven seconds.

INSPECTOR GENERAL: Form 19-G received. Procedural Deficiency Report acknowledged. Audit notice under review. Pending determination.

Under review. Not voided. Not acknowledged as valid. Under review.

Gerald watched the notice sit there. He had perhaps six hours before the audit arrived regardless of whether his report was accepted. The Inspector General could simply ignore the procedural objection and proceed.

The System was not a court. It was a system, and systems did not always follow their own rules.

He needed something more than a form, and he went back to the ghost Core. 9-0002.

Suspended. No directory entry. No public record.

The suspension had occurred at the exact moment Gerald had registered.

Gerald pulled up the suspension record. He had accessed it once already and been cited for it.

He did not care, and he was already at fifty-two. One more citation would put him at thirty-two, still above the debuff threshold of forty. He could afford it.

The suspension record was sparse. A timestamp.

A Core number.

The word "SUSPENDED." No reason listed.

No hearing record. No citation preceding it.

Gerald stared at that last part. No citation preceding it.

Every suspension he had ever seen in the System documentation required a citation first. The process was: Citation issued, Core responds or fails to respond, Citation upheld, Compliance Rating drops, Core hits threshold, suspension follows. It was a chain.

A paper trail. The same paper trail Gerald had followed in every inspection of his career.

9-0002 had no citation. No paper trail. No process.

It had been suspended by executive action. By the Inspector General's office directly, without going through the citation system at all.

Gerald's hands were steady. His interface was steady. The dungeon around him was not. The water leak had resumed, but at half pressure, and the mold spores were still dormant, and the handrails in his east corridor were cold to the touch because his structural bonuses were degrading.

He opened Form 19-G again.

REPORTING CORE: 7-4412 SUBJECT OF REPORT: Inspector General, Administrative Zone 4 DEFICIENCY TYPE: Procedural Non-Compliance (Enforcement Action) SPECIFIC VIOLATION: Suspension of Core 9-0002 executed without preceding Citation, without Hearing, without Compliance Rating threshold trigger. Suspension constitutes unenforceable executive action in violation of Enforcement Chain of Custody, Section 3, Subsection A. RECOMMENDED ACTION: Suspension voided. Core 9-0002 reinstated pending proper citation process.

He submitted it.

This time the response took four minutes.

INSPECTOR GENERAL: Form 19-G received.

Report filed under Restricted Review. You are hereby notified that further access to Core 9-0002's records constitutes a second violation of Procedural Compliance Code, Section 12, Subsection D. Citation issued.

Penalty: Compliance Rating reduced by 20 points.

Compliance Rating adjusted from 52 to 32.

Thirty-two. Below the threshold.

Gerald felt it immediately.

The floor beneath him shuddered. A crack appeared in the west corridor wall, thin as a hair, running from floor to ceiling. His permanent resistances were beginning to strip.

The fire resistance he'd earned from installing proper fire suppression was fading. He felt it like a fever breaking in reverse: a slow withdrawal of something that had become part of him.

The mold spores went fully dormant. The water leak stopped. The handrails went from cold to brittle.

Gerald sat in the center of his dungeon and watched his own walls crack.

Then a new notice appeared. Not a System broadcast. A direct message.

CORE 9-0001 (ACTING INSPECTOR): You are filing reports against the Inspector General's office. You are citing the enforcement body. Do you understand what you are doing?

Gerald read it. He did not respond. He was thinking about the crack in his west corridor wall and the fact that it was widening.

Another message.

CORE 9-0001: You have one Citation remaining before your dungeon enters Critical Compliance Failure.

At that point, all spawned entities will be terminated, all structural bonuses will be removed, and your Core will be subject to involuntary dissolution.

You have approximately six hours before the audit arrives regardless of your Procedural Deficiency Reports. You will fail the audit.

You will be suspended. You will be erased.

Gerald typed a response.

CORE 7-4412: I have filed two Procedural Deficiency Reports. Both are pending review. If both are accepted, the audit is voided and the suspension of 9-0002 is voided. I am following procedure.

CORE 9-0001: Procedure is what the Inspector General writes. You cannot cite the hand that writes the rules.

Gerald stared at that message. He thought about thirty-one years of inspections. He thought about the time he had cited the mayor's office for improper signage on a public building and been reassigned to inspecting parking lots for two years. He thought about how the rules were supposed to protect the people who followed them.

He typed:

CORE 7-4412: That is not a procedural objection. That is an admission.

The response came instantly.

CORE 9-0001: It is a warning. The Inspector General has authorized emergency measures. The audit is not coming. It is already here.

Gerald looked up.

The crack in his west corridor wall was no longer thin. It was a gap.

Through it, he could see something that was not his dungeon, and a corridor that was not his corridor. A floor that was not his floor. The space between his dungeon and the adjacent structure was dissolving, and something was moving through it.

His interface flickered. A new notice.

INSPECTOR GENERAL: Emergency Audit initiated.

Auditing entity: Core 9-0001 (Acting Inspector). Scope: Full structural compliance.

Remediation window: None.

Begin immediately.

Gerald stood up. His dungeon was cracking apart around him, his Compliance Rating at thirty-two, his spawns dead or dying, his resistances stripped. The Acting Inspector was walking through his walls.

He had one Citation left. One.

If he used it and it was upheld, he would be at twelve.

If he used it and it was reversed on appeal, he would be at fifty-two.

If he did not use it, the audit would find him in violation of at least three categories he could already identify, and the resulting citation would put him at twelve or below.

Either way, he needed to use it, and he looked at the gap in his wall. He looked at the shadow moving through it. He looked at his interface.

He opened the Citation form. He typed the target.

CORE 9-0001 (ACTING INSPECTOR).

The System paused. A message appeared.

SYSTEM: Citation target is an Enforcement Entity. Citations against Enforcement Entities require a minimum Compliance Rating of 60 for validity. Your Compliance Rating is 32. Citation cannot be served.

Gerald read it twice. Then he understood. He could not cite the Inspector.

The System would not let him. The enforcement body was protected from the tool it used to enforce compliance.

The shadow in his wall was closer now, and he could see its shape. It was not a creature. It was a corridor.

A long, straight corridor with handrails that met code and lighting that met code and a floor that was perfectly, immaculately level.

The Acting Inspector's own dungeon was pushing into his.

Gerald looked at his Compliance Rating. Thirty-two.

He looked at the Citation form, and he looked at the requirement. Minimum Compliance Rating of 60 to cite an Enforcement Entity.

He needed twenty-eight points. He needed them now.

He looked around his dungeon. The cracks.

The dead spores.

The cold handrails.

Everything stripped. Everything failing.

Then he looked at the gap in his wall. At the perfect corridor beyond it. At the handrails that met code.

Gerald opened his building permit log. He had never used this function. It was meant for tracking his own construction projects. But it could also, he realized, track the construction of adjacent structures if they were within his dungeon's detection radius.

He pulled up the log. He found the Acting Inspector's corridor. He found its construction date. He found its permit number.

The permit was valid. The construction was legal. Everything was up to code.

Everything except one thing.

Gerald measured the gap in his own wall, and the point where the Acting Inspector's corridor was breaching his dungeon's boundary. He measured it with his interface calipers.

The breach was a structural modification to a shared wall. A load-bearing wall. The Acting Inspector was cutting through a load-bearing wall to create an access corridor.

No permit. No structural assessment. No engineering review.

Gerald's hands moved. He filled out the Citation form again. Not against Core 9-0001. Against the action itself.

CITATION TARGET: Structural modification to shared load-bearing wall, no permit, no engineering assessment, no structural review. VIOLATION TYPE: Structural Code, Section 4, Subsection B: "No load-bearing wall shall be modified, penetrated, or removed without a valid Structural Modification Permit and a signed engineering assessment."

SERVED TO: Core 9-0001 (Acting Inspector), physically present at point of violation.

The System processed it.

SYSTEM: Citation issued. Target is physically present at violation site. Citation valid.

Penalty: Compliance Rating reduced by 15 points. Target Compliance Rating adjusted from 98 to 83.

The perfect corridor in his wall shuddered. The handrails flickered. The lighting dimmed by a fraction.

Gerald had cited the Acting Inspector. He had cited the enforcement body. And it had worked.

But the penalty was fifteen points.

Not enough. Not even close to enough. The Acting Inspector was still at eighty-three.

Gerald was still at thirty-two, and the audit was still active. The corridor was still pushing through his wall.

And then Gerald noticed something else.

The Citation had been valid. The System had accepted it. Which meant the requirement for a minimum Compliance Rating of 60 did not apply to citations against structural violations committed by Enforcement Entities during active operations.

It only applied to general citations, and the loophole was specific. The loophole was narrow. And it was the only thing Gerald had.

He looked at the gap in his wall. The Acting Inspector's corridor was still advancing. And Gerald was already holding the Citation form open, his finger hovering over the next entry.

He had one Citation left, and he looked at the corridor. He looked at the wall. He looked at the floor.

The Acting Inspector's dungeon was pushing through Gerald's load-bearing wall.

That was one violation.

But the floor. The floor of the new corridor was lower than Gerald's floor.

It was a slope. A ramp. Without handrails.

Gerald measured it. The slope exceeded the maximum gradient for a corridor without handrails.

The handrails were present on one side only, and the other side was open. A drop.

He typed the code number and did not soften it.
