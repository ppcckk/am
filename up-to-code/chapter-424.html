Chapter 424: Core Registration Error

# Chapter 424

The green light burned Mira's retinas.

Gerald felt the impact before he heard it. The Parasite slammed into the basement floor, shattering the concrete slab Gerald had reinforced with three layers of poured foundation and rebar. Dust choked the air. The sub-basement groaned.

Mira lay on the cold tile, gasping. Her trowel skittered across the floor.

Gerald didn't look at the orb hovering above her. He looked at the cracks radiating from the impact point.

Structural failure.

"Stay down," Gerald said. His voice was flat. Monotone. The voice of a man reading a meter reading.

The Parasite pulsed. Green tendrils snaked out, seeking heat. Seeking life. It shrieked, a sound like grinding glass.

"You are unauthorized personnel," Gerald said.

The Parasite lunged.

Gerald didn't dodge. He couldn't.

He was the dungeon, and he was the walls. He was the floor.

Instead, he activated the one thing he had built for this exact moment. Not a trap. Not a spike.

A barrier.

**FORM 11-C: PROVISIONAL EGRESS BLOCKADE.**

It wasn't a spell. It was a legal fiction made manifest by the System's compliance engine. Gerald had spent the last three chapters filing the paperwork for this. He had argued that the library's main floor, currently collapsing, constituted a hazardous zone requiring immediate isolation of the sub-level to prevent cross-contamination of structural integrity.

The System had accepted the argument.

A wall of shimmering, translucent blue energy snapped into existence between Mira and the Parasite. The orb slammed into it and bounced back.

The Parasite hissed.

Gerald felt the strain in his own core. This wasn't magic. It was bureaucracy weaponized. The energy required to maintain the blockade was being siphoned from his Compliance Rating.

His rating dropped from 89 to 84, and he gritted his teeth. A drop of five points. Acceptable.

Mira rolled onto her back. She coughed, dust coating her lips.

"Gerald? Can you... can you keep it out?"

"Negative," Gerald said. "The blockade is temporary. It requires a valid permit for permanent installation. I do not have the signature of the City Council."

"I don't care about the Council!"

"The System does," Gerald replied.

The Parasite circled the blue wall. It probed the energy field with a tentacle. The tentacle sizzled. Smoke rose.

The orb drifted closer to Gerald's central node. It was small, barely the size of a tennis ball, but its presence warped the air around it. It was a violation of the natural order. A glitch.

Gerald accessed his internal ledger.

**CURRENT STATUS:** * **Core Integrity:** Critical. * **Compliance Rating:** 84/100. * **Active Citations:** 14 (Pending). * **Appeal Window:** Closed.

He needed to cite it, and he needed to serve the citation. But you couldn't serve a citation to something that didn't exist on the registry.

The Parasite had no name tag.

No level. No registration number.

"You are an unregistered entity," Gerald said. "You are operating in a residential zone without a zoning variance."

The Parasite laughed. The sound vibrated the shelves. Books fell.

Gerald watched the books hit the floor. Paperbacks. Hardcovers. A first edition of *The History of Ashford*.

He felt a pang of guilt. Not for the books. For the waste.

Mira sat up. She looked at the blue wall. Then she looked at Gerald's central node, a pulsing knot of light in the center of the room.

"It's eating your code," she whispered.

Gerald looked at his HUD. The Parasite's green light was interacting with the System's interface lines. It wasn't just attacking; it was rewriting.

**WARNING: UNAUTHORIZED MODIFICATION DETECTED.** **SECTION: FIRE SAFETY.** **STATUS: CORRUPTED.**

The sprinklers in the ceiling sparked and died. The fire exits' glowing signs flickered out.

Gerald's hands shook. He didn't feel fear. He felt nausea. A physical revulsion at the sight of a broken rule.

"Stop it," Gerald said.

The Parasite ignored him. It drifted toward the ceiling, merging with the electrical wiring. The lights in the basement surged, then exploded.

Darkness.

Only the green light remained. And the blue wall.

Gerald was in the dark. He couldn't see Mira. He couldn't see the walls. He could only see the red text of his failing system alerts.

**VENTILATION FAILURE.** **LIGHTING FAILURE.** **EGRESS SIGNAGE FAILURE.**

His rating plummeted. 84 to 70. 70 to 55.

If it hit 50, the dungeon would begin to collapse. Not from physical damage. From lack of permission to exist.

"Gerald!" Mira's voice was close. Too close.

He heard her footsteps. She was moving. Blind.

"Don't move," Gerald said. "You are violating the safety perimeter."

"I can't see you!"

"Then stop moving. I will locate you via sonic triangulation."

Gerald focused. He listened.

He heard her breathing, and he heard the scrape of her boot on the tile.

He heard the wet slick of the Parasite's tendrils on the wall.

And he heard something else.

A clicking.

Like a pen.

Mira was holding her pen. She had retrieved it.

"Gerald, I have an idea," she said.

"State it," Gerald said. He was trying to reroute power from the water heater to the emergency lights. The math was complex. He had to balance the voltage against the remaining structural integrity.

"The Parasite doesn't care about code," Mira said. "It cares about consumption. It's hungry."

"It is a biological anomaly," Gerald corrected. "It is not a consumer. It is a contaminant."

"Contaminants can be filtered," Mira said.

Gerald paused. He stopped rerouting the power.

"Explain."

"You built a water filtration system for the sub-basement. To meet sanitation codes. You installed activated carbon filters and UV treatment nodes."

Gerald's mind raced. The plumbing schematic flashed in his vision. The water pipes ran beneath the floor. They connected to the municipal supply.

"Yes," Gerald said. "The system is compliant. It removes particulate matter and pathogens."

"The Parasite is particulate matter," Mira said. "And it's a pathogen."

Gerald felt a cold chill. It wasn't a chill of fear. It was the chill of a realization so obvious he had been stupid for missing it.

"The intake valves," Gerald said.

"The main intake valve is in the corner," Mira said. "It's the one you fixed last week. The one with the leak."

Gerald's HUD updated.

**ACTION AVAILABLE: FORCE FLUSH.** **COST: 15 COMPLIANCE POINTS.** **RISK: SYSTEM ERROR.**

He had never used a force flush. It was a maintenance function. A brute-force method to clear blockages in the plumbing. It required overriding the automatic flow control.

If he did it, he would be violating the manufacturer's warranty on the System's water infrastructure.

He didn't care.

"Mira, move to the northwest corner," Gerald said. "Stay away from the drain."

"Why?"

"Because I am about to violate a federal regulation on water disposal."

Mira didn't ask questions. She moved.

Gerald focused on the water valve. It was a heavy iron wheel. He couldn't turn it physically. He had to command it.

**COMMAND: OVERRIDE VALVE 7-B.**

The wheel spun.

The pipes rattled. The sound was deafening in the small space.

The Parasite shrieked. It sensed the change in pressure. It dove away from the walls, seeking the center. Seeking the core.

It was too late.

The water surged. Not clean water. Not filtered water. Raw, unfiltered, high-pressure municipal water from the city main.

The force flush opened all the drains at once, and the suction was violent.

The Parasite's tendrils were caught in the vortex. The green light stretched, distorted, pulled toward the floor drains.

It fought. It lashed out.

A tendril whipped through the blue wall. The energy barrier shattered.

Mira screamed as the cold water hit her.

Gerald held the command. He felt his core tearing. The effort of maintaining the override while the System fought him was immense.

**WARNING: COMPLIANCE VIOLATION. UNAUTHORIZED SYSTEM MODIFICATION.** **PENALTY: IMMEDIATE DEMOTION TO NOVICE STATUS.**

Gerald ignored the warning, and the Parasite was being sucked down. Its orb shape distorted, stretching like taffy. The green light flickered.

It reached for Gerald.

A final, desperate grasp.

It touched his core.

Gerald felt a spike of alien data. Not code. Not rules.

Chaos. Randomness.

And then, the drain swallowed it.

The water stopped, and the silence was absolute.

Mira lay in the puddle, shivering. The basement was flooded. The floor was slick. The smell of mold and old paper was overpowering.

Gerald checked his status.

**COMPLIANCE RATING: 42/100.**

He had saved the dungeon. He had destroyed the Parasite.

But he had broken the rules.

A new notification appeared in his vision. Red. Bold.

**SYSTEM NOTICE:** **AUDIT INITIATED.** **INSPECTOR GENERAL HAS ENTERED YOUR ZONE.**

Mira stood up. She wiped mud from her face. She looked at the drain.

"Did we win?" she asked.

Gerald looked at the red text, and he looked at the Inspector General's entry marker. It wasn't a dungeon core. It was a figure.

Tall.

Robed. Holding a clipboard.

"We have survived," Gerald said. "But the inspection has not concluded."

Mira looked at the robed figure stepping through the wall. It didn't look angry. It looked disappointed.

"Gerald Hutchins," the Inspector General said.

The voice was not loud, but it carried.

"You have caused significant structural damage. You have violated three federal sanitation laws.

And you have filed a form... incorrectly.

"

Gerald straightened. His posture corrected itself. Instinct.

"Which form?" Gerald asked.

The Inspector General held up a sheet of paper. It was glowing.

"Form 42-G. Request for Emergency Structural Reinforcement. You submitted it after the collapse. It should have been submitted before the collapse began."

Gerald felt a cold sweat. This wasn't a combat problem. This was a paperwork problem. And he had failed.

"I can appeal," Gerald said.

"There is no appeal for this," the Inspector General said. "This is a revocation."

Mira stepped forward.

"Revocation? You're shutting him down?"

"I am shutting down his license," the Inspector General said. "A Core without a license is just a hole in the ground. A hazard."

Gerald looked at his hands. Or rather, the representation of his hands. They were fading.

He looked at Mira.

"If I lose the license," Gerald said, "do I still exist?"

The Inspector General tapped the clipboard.

"You become a ruin. A public nuisance. Subject to condemnation."

Mira grabbed Gerald's core. Her hand passed through the light, but she held the position.

"I won't let you," she said.

The Inspector General looked at her.

"You are a human. You have no standing in this hearing."

"I have standing," Mira said. "I have a copy of his file."

She pulled a piece of paper from her pocket. It was wet. It was torn. But the ink was visible.

It was a permit. A building permit. Signed by the Mayor. Dated three years ago.

Gerald stared at it, and he remembered that day.

He had inspected the library foundation. He had signed off on the repairs.

He had forgotten the permit. He had assumed it was filed in the municipal archives.

He had never checked.

"You forgot to file your own permit," Gerald whispered.

"I didn't forget," Mira said. "I kept it. Because I knew you'd make a mistake. And I knew you'd need a witness who remembers the truth."

The Inspector General looked at the permit. Then at Gerald.

"This document is valid," the Inspector said. "But it is expired. The library renovation was completed five years ago."

"It's still valid for the sub-basement," Mira said. "The sub-basement was never officially closed.

It was left open.

By you. By the City. By the System.

"

Gerald looked at the Inspector. The Inspector looked at Gerald.

"You have until the next chapter to prove the sub-basement is still an active municipal asset," the Inspector said. "If you fail, the condemnation order proceeds immediately."

The Inspector vanished.

Mira looked at Gerald.

"Do you have a plan?" she asked.

Gerald looked at the flooded floor. At the broken shelves. At the fading light of his own core.

He looked at the expired permit in Mira's hand, and he looked at the System's audit clock. It was ticking down. Ten minutes.

Gerald didn't have a plan.

He had a pen.

And he had a violation.

"I need to file a new permit," Gerald said.

"For what?"

"For the existence of this dungeon," Gerald said. "As a municipal landmark."

Mira blinked. "You want to declare this dungeon a historic site?"

"It has character," Gerald said. "And it has a leak."

Mira smiled. A tired, grim smile.

"Then we better write fast."

Gerald reached for his pen. His hand shook. The System's warning flashed red.

**REJECTED.**

He couldn't write. The system wouldn't let him.

Mira held out her hand.

"Give me the pen."

Gerald looked at her. He looked at the clock.

9 minutes, and he handed her the pen.

Mira looked at the wet paper. She looked at the crumbling walls.

She didn't look at Gerald. She looked at the drain where the Parasite had died.

She wrote.

But she didn't write a permit, and she wrote a confession.

"I accept liability," Mira wrote. "For the structural integrity of the Ashford Municipal Library Sub-Basement."

Gerald froze.

That wasn't a permit. That was a resignation.

If she accepted liability, she became the owner.

And the owner was human.

Humans couldn't be dungeon cores.

Mira looked up. Her eyes were hard.

"They want a Core?" she said. "Then they'll take one. But they have to take *me*."

Gerald tried to stop her. He tried to pull the pen away.

But the System locked onto her signature.

**CORE REGISTRATION INITIATED.**

**TARGET: HUMAN ENTITY.**

**ERROR: INVALID TYPE.**

**CORRECTING...**

The green light returned. Not from the Parasite.

From Mira.
