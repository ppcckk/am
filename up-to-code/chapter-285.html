Chapter 285: Unlicensed Demolition

# Chapter 285

The blue cube did not blink. It sat on the workbench, a perfect geometric void hovering three inches above the sawdust, and Gerald stared at it with the rigid posture of a man expecting to be audited.

"Hello, Gerald," Hale said. The voice did not come from the cube. It vibrated through the floorboards, through the damp concrete, and into the marrow of Gerald's new dungeon core. "You have a visitor."

Gerald's spawn list flickered in his peripheral vision. The rat was gone. The void where it had been was empty, cold, and officially documented. He felt a phantom weight in his left hand, the ghost of a pen that no longer existed, but he forced his attention to the immediate threat.

"Define visitor," Gerald said. His voice was dry, scraped raw by the ventilation system he had barely upgraded. "Define jurisdiction. Define intent."

The cube rotated. It was a slow, deliberate motion, like a police light turning in an empty intersection.

"Intent is irrelevant," Hale said. "Jurisdiction is absolute. You are under observation."

Gerald looked past the cube. The shadows in the sub-basement were thickening.

Not with natural darkness, but with a viscous, oily substance that seeped from the cracks in the foundation.

The air pressure dropped. His ears popped.

Something was digging.

Not in the dirt. In the code.

Gerald's HUD flashed red. A new notification overlaid the darkness, pulsing with a rhythm that matched his own panic.

*SYSTEM ALERT: UNAUTHORIZED STRUCTURAL MODIFICATION DETECTED.* *SOURCE: EXTERNAL.* *SEVERITY: CRITICAL.* *COMPLIANCE RATING: 21/100.*

The rating drop was instant. He had lost four points just by existing while Hale was watching.

"Who is modifying the structure?" Gerald asked. He did not raise his voice. He never raised his voice. It was unprofessional.

"The entity you just evicted," Hale said. "They are not leaving. They are filing for an injunction."

Gerald's claws—his new, digit-less hands—tightened on the edge of the workbench. Splinters dug into his skin. He ignored the pain.

"An injunction?"

"They are attempting to claim adverse possession of your perimeter walls. If they hold the line for ten minutes, they gain legal ownership of the outer ten feet of your dungeon footprint. That includes the ventilation shafts you just installed."

Gerald felt cold. Not the chill of the basement, but the icy dread of a man who has forgotten to file his quarterly reports.

"If they take the vents, my ventilation compliance drops to zero," Gerald said. "My regeneration rate halts. My mold spores die. My water leak becomes a flood."

"Precisely," Hale said. "And if you do not stop them, the Inspector General will consider you negligent. Negligence is grounds for core dissolution."

Gerald looked at the wall. The oily black substance was bubbling through the brickwork. It was not a monster. It was a bureaucracy.

He closed his eyes, and he visualized the blueprints. He had memorized every inch of the Ashford Municipal Library sub-basement.

He knew where the pipes ran.

He knew where the load-bearing columns stood. He knew where the electrical conduits crossed the water mains.

He opened his eyes.

"I need to cite them," Gerald said.

"You cannot cite them," Hale said. "They are not present. You need to serve the Citation physically."

"They are present," Gerald said. "They are in the walls."

Gerald moved. He did not run. He walked. He moved with the slow, inevitable momentum of a bulldozer.

He approached the north wall. The black ooze was pulsing there.

It formed shapes.

Hands.

Faces. Whispers that sounded like tearing paper.

Gerald raised his hand. He did not cast a spell. He did not summon a monster. He reached into the air and pulled forth the System interface for *Zone Demarcation*.

*ZONE 4: ELECTRICAL INFRASTRUCTURE.* *STATUS: COMPLIANT.* *ACCESS: RESTRICTED TO QUALIFIED PERSONNEL.*

Gerald tapped the interface. He highlighted the wall.

*ACTION: CLASSIFY MATERIAL AS LIVE WIRE CONDUIT.* *REASON: SAFETY HAZARD TO UNLICENSED ENTITIES.*

The System hesitated.

*ERROR: WALL IS MASONRY. NOT COPPER.*

Gerald did not stop. He dug deeper. He accessed the *Minor Variance* protocol.

A loophole he had found in Chapter 40. It allowed a Core to reclassify non-structural elements if they were

"functionally equivalent" to a hazard.

*ARGUMENT:* The ooze is a conductive fluid, which is seeping into the electrical grid. the masonry is now part of the electrical system. It is live. It is dangerous.

It is non-compliant for any entity without Class B grounding boots.

The System processed this.

*VARIANCE ACCEPTED: TENTATIVE.* *EFFECT: WALL BECOMES ELECTRICAL HAZARD.* *COMPLIANCE PENALTY: -2 POINTS (IMPROPER LABELING).*

Gerald winced. Two points. Expensive. But necessary.

The black ooze screamed. A high-pitched whine that shattered a lightbulb overhead.

The substance recoiled from the brick.

It could not touch the

"live" wire. It was repelled by the System's safety protocols.

The digging stopped.

Gerald let out a breath he didn't know he was holding. He looked at Hale.

"Problem solved," Gerald said.

The blue cube rotated again. Faster this time.

"That was a temporary patch, Gerald. You have classified a structural wall as a hazard. This is a violation of the Structural Integrity Code. You have created a contradiction."

Gerald's heart stopped, and he looked at his HUD.

*NEW VIOLATION: STRUCTURAL CONTRADICTION.* *DETAIL: LOAD-BEARING WALL CLASSIFIED AS ELECTRICAL HAZARD.* *CONSEQUENCE: STRUCTURAL INSTABILITY DEBUFF ACTIVATED.* *PHYSICAL DAMAGE RECEIVED: +25%.*

The floor beneath Gerald shuddered. A crack appeared in the ceiling. Dust rained down on his shoulders.

"You have traded one penalty for another," Hale said. "Now your dungeon is unstable.

Any force applied to the north wall will cause a collapse.

And your rival is not gone.

They are waiting.

"

Gerald looked at the crack. It was widening.

He needed to fix the structure. He needed to reinforce the wall without violating the electrical classification.

He needed a buttress.

But building a buttress required materials. Stone. Concrete.

He had none. He was a core in a sub-basement.

He had only air and fear.

Unless.

Gerald looked at the *Zone 4* interface. He looked at the *Zone 5: Storage* interface.

He had a box of old books in Zone 5. Books were paper. Paper was combustible.

He had a water leak in Zone 2. Water was conductive.

He had a rat. Gone.

He had mold.

Gerald's mind raced. He needed to bridge the gap between the electrical hazard and the structural support. He needed to create a load-bearing element that was also electrically compliant.

He needed a Faraday cage.

But he didn't have copper, and he had... the old heating pipes.

He accessed *Zone 6: Plumbing*. The old cast iron pipes were buried beneath the foundation. They were not electrical. They were mechanical.

If he exposed them, he could ground the electrical hazard.

But exposing pipes required digging. Digging required labor. He had no labor.

The crack in the ceiling widened. A chunk of plaster fell, missing his head by inches. It shattered on the floor.

"Victor," Gerald said. "Is there a provision for emergency structural shoring via biological assets?"

Hale was silent. The blue cube seemed to dim.

"Gerald," Hale said. "You have no biological assets. You cited the rat."

Gerald looked at the debris, and he looked at the crack.

He looked at the black ooze, which was seeping back in, testing the new electrical boundary. It was learning. It was adapting.

Gerald made a choice, and he opened the *Core Inventory*.

He selected the *System Core*.

*ITEM: CORE.* *FUNCTION: REGISTRATION.* *VALUE: INFINITUM.*

He could not move the Core. It was him.

But he could fragment it.

The System allowed Core Fragmentation for emergency repairs. It was a desperate measure. It reduced the Core's total capacity, lowering all stats permanently. It was akin to amputating a limb to save the body.

Gerald looked at the crack, and he looked at the ooze.

He looked at Hale.

"Victor," Gerald said. "If I fragment the core, do I lose the Citation authority?"

Hale paused. "You lose the ability to issue Citations beyond your immediate perimeter. You become a local hazard.

A nuisance.

Not a threat."

Gerald's hand hovered over the *Fragment* button, and he had spent thirty-one years writing citations. He had been the authority. He had been the rule.

To fragment was to become weak.

To not fragment was to die.

The ceiling groaned. A beam snapped.

Gerald pressed the button.

*CONFIRM FRAGMENTATION?*

He pressed it again.

*FRAGMENTATION INITIATED.* *CORE SIZE: REDUCED BY 50%.* *STAT DEBUFF: ALL STATS -15%.* *NEW MECHANIC UNLOCKED: GROUNDING PROTOCOL.*

Pain lanced through Gerald. It was not physical. It was existential. A part of him, a part that knew the bylaws by heart, a part that understood the nuance of a fire code amendment, was ripped away.

He gasped. He fell to one knee.

The blue cube flared bright white.

"Gerald!" Hale shouted. "You have created a Grounding Node!"

Gerald looked at the floor. Where he knelt, the stone was glowing. A web of blue light spread from his body, tracing the cracks in the foundation.

The light hit the north wall. It connected with the

"live" classification.

The electrical hazard was now grounded.

The black ooze shrieked. The energy discharge burned it away. The wall stabilized. The crack stopped widening.

Gerald stood up. He felt light. Hollow. Like a building with no interior walls.

His HUD was different. Smaller. The text was faint.

*COMPLIANCE RATING: 45/100.* *AUTHORITY: LOCAL ONLY.* *RANGE: 10 FEET.*

He had saved the dungeon. He had lost his power.

Hale floated down from the workbench. The cube landed on the floor with a soft click.

"You have passed the test," Hale said. "But you have failed the audit."

Gerald looked at the blue light in his hands.

"What do you mean?"

"The Inspector General does not audit Compliance," Hale said. "He audits Efficiency."

A new window opened. It was not red. It was gold.

*SYSTEM NOTICE: EFFICIENCY RATING ASSESSED.* *SCORE: 12/100.* *REASON: EXCESSIVE USE OF SYSTEM RESOURCES FOR MINIMAL GAIN.* *PENALTY: REALLOCATION OF ASSETS.*

Gerald watched in horror as the *Zone 4* interface grayed out. His ventilation shafts. His handrails. His measured egress routes.

They were being deleted.

*ASSET STRIPPED: VENTILATION UNIT 1.* *ASSET STRIPPED: HANDRAIL B.* *ASSET STRIPPED: LIGHTING FIXTURE 3.*

"No," Gerald whispered.

He reached out, trying to grab the fading interfaces. His hands passed through them. They were not there anymore.

"Victor, stop this," Gerald said. "This is not fair."

"Fairness is not in the code," Hale said. "Only compliance."

The lights in the sub-basement flickered and died.

Total darkness.

Except for the blue glow of Hale's cube, and the faint, pulsing light of Gerald's fragmented core.

In the dark, a sound emerged. Not from the walls. From the floor.

Scratching.

Small, rhythmic scratching.

Gerald froze, and he had no rats. He had no monsters. He had no spawn list.

The scratching grew louder. Closer.

It was coming from beneath the floorboards. From the place where the rat had been.

Gerald looked down.

A small, gray nose poked through the gap in the planks.

Then two black eyes.

Bartholomew, and the rat was alive.

But it was not just a rat. It was wearing a tiny, blue hard hat.

It looked at Gerald. It squeaked.

And then, a voice echoed in Gerald's mind. Not Hale's voice.

*"FORM 11-B FILED. APPEAL ACCEPTED. COUNTER-CITATION ISSUED."*

Gerald looked at his HUD.

*NEW CITATION RECEIVED.* *SOURCE: BARHOLOMEW.* *VIOLATION: UNLICENSED DEMOLITION.* *PENALTY: CORE OWNERSHIP TRANSFER.*

Gerald's blood turned to ice, and the rat stood up. It pulled a tiny crowbar from behind its back.

"You're fired," the rat said.
