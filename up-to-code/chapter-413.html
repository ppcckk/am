Chapter 413: Served to the Void

# Chapter 413

The air in the Ashford Municipal Library sub-basement tasted of copper and ozone. Dust motes danced in the shafts of light that pierced the ceiling, illuminating the jagged tear where reality had failed to hold together. From the vantage point of the crumbling support beam, Mira watched the fall.

She had expected a monster. She had prepared her shield for a dragon, or perhaps a shadow-stalker. What she had not prepared for was a middle-aged man in a wrinkled suit, falling into the abyss with his clipboard clutched to his chest like a holy relic.

"He's falling," Mira shouted. "He's falling and he's holding paper!"

Her party members, a rogue and a mage, stared. The rogue, Jarek, raised his bow. The mage, Elara, began a somatic component for a levitation spell, but hesitation clouded her eyes.

"He's not a monster," Elara whispered. "He's... he's an inspector."

"Does it matter?" Jarek asked, his voice tight. "The whole dungeon is collapsing. The void is eating the foundation. If he hits the ground, he's paste."

Mira stepped forward, her boots crunching on loose gravel.

"Look at the fall trajectory. It's wrong."

She was right. Gerald Hutchins was not plummeting.

He was drifting.

The System, confused by the sudden shift in status from 'Rogue Core' to 'Falling Entity,' had paused his acceleration. He hung in the air, suspended by a glitch in the physics engine, his limbs splayed like a starfish. His eyes were closed.

His lips moved silently.

"Is he praying?" Elara asked.

"No," Mira said, squinting. "He's reading."

The void below them churned. Massive shapes, twisted and eyeless, pushed through the membrane of the world.

They were the unbound monsters, the ones released by Gerald's Stop Work Order on the city.

They snapped at the edges of the collapsing platform, their jaws wide enough to swallow a horse. But they did not attack Gerald. They seemed confused by the static energy radiating from him.

"He's not moving," Jarek said, lowering his bow. "Should we pull him up?"

"We can't," Mira said. "The platform is gone. There's nothing to stand on."

A low hum vibrated through the floor. The library above groaned, the structural integrity of the municipal building failing under the weight of the supernatural intrusion. Books rained down from the upper floors, fluttering like confused birds.

Then, a single sheet of paper detached from Gerald's clipboard. It floated upward, defying gravity, and landed face-up on a shard of floating concrete near Mira's boot.

Mira picked it up. It was a standard municipal citation form, Form 7-C, for *Unauthorized Structural Modification*.

The fill-in-the-blanks were complete.

*Violation:* Failure to maintain load-bearing continuity in dimensional boundary. *Severity:* Critical. *Penalty:* Immediate cessation of collapse.

Gerald's eyes snapped open. He did not look at the monsters. He did not look at the void. He looked at the citation in Mira's hand.

"Did you accept service?" Gerald's voice was thin, reedy, but it cut through the roar of the approaching horde.

Mira blinked. "What?"

"The citation," Gerald said. He was still hanging in the air, but his posture had changed.

He stood upright, though there was no ground beneath his feet.

"I issued a Stop Work Order on the collapse itself.

But it requires a recipient. The City is non-compliant.

The System is non-compliant. You...

" He pointed a trembling finger at Mira. "You are a representative of the public interest.

By holding the notice, you have acknowledged receipt.

"

Mira looked at the paper. Then she looked at Gerald.

"If I don't take it," Mira said slowly, "does the city fall?"

"The void expands at four cubic meters per second," Gerald said flatly. "Current rate of structural failure is exponential. If you do not acknowledge the Stop Work Order, the collapse will encompass the entire municipal district within ninety seconds. The citizens of Ashford will be consumed by unlicensed biological entities."

Jarek cursed. "He's bluffing."

"He is a Dungeon Core," Elara said, her face pale. "He doesn't bluff. He calculates."

Gerald reached into his breast pocket, and he produced a second form.

"This is a Motion to Appeal the Suspension of Gravity. I am requesting immediate stabilization of the local physics engine based on the precedent of *Hutchins v.

The Void*, Case File 413-A.

"

Mira felt a cold sweat prickle her neck. She looked at the approaching monsters. They were gaining speed. The library walls were buckling inward.

"I'll take it," Mira said.

She held out the first citation.

Gerald didn't move.

"Service of process must be physical. You must stamp it."

Mira fumbled in her pack for her adventurer's seal—a small, iron stamp used for guild contracts. She stamped the paper.

The air around Gerald shimmered. The System, bound by the rigid logic of its own code, had no choice.

The citation was accepted, and the anomaly was noted. The collapse halted.

Gerald dropped.

He hit the fragment of concrete with a heavy thud, rolling before coming to rest on his back. The floating debris settled. The roar of the monsters outside faded into a confused murmur, as the breach in the world sealed itself with a sharp *pop*.

Silence returned to the sub-basement.

Mira stared at the stamp on her seal. It was smoking.

"He's alive," Jarek said, holstering his bow. He sounded disappointed.

Gerald sat up. He adjusted his tie. He looked at his clipboard, which was now intact. He checked his watch.

"14:03," he muttered. "Late."

He stood up, his knees cracking audibly. He looked at Mira, then at the party, then at the dark corners of the room where the monsters had been.

"You have been served," Gerald said.

Mira felt a strange pressure in her head. A System notification, visible only to her.

**[NOTICE: PUBLIC ADVISORY ISSUED.]** **[RECIPIENT: MIRA OF THE SILVER TIDE.]** **[STATUS: WITNESS TO STRUCTURAL REPARATION.]** **[BENEFIT: TEMPORARY RESISTANCE TO CHAOS MAGIC (30 MINUTES).]**

Mira rubbed her temples.

"Did I just get buffed for being a bureaucrat?"

Gerald walked over to a cracked section of the wall. He pulled a tape measure from his pocket. He extended it, clicking it against the stone.

"This crack," Gerald said, ignoring her. "It is 4.2 millimeters wide. The acceptable tolerance for a load-bearing wall in a Zone 4 seismic district is 2.0 millimeters."

He pulled a pen from his pocket. He wrote something on the clipboard.

"Excuse me," Mira said, stepping forward. "The City is destroyed. The System is angry. You're... you're measuring a crack in the wall."

Gerald looked up, his expression one of profound confusion.

"The City is not destroyed.

The City is *under review*.

There is a distinct legal difference, and a destroyed city is an asset loss. A city under review is a pending liability.

"

He turned back to the wall.

"Now, if you'll excuse me, I need to determine if this fracture constitutes a Section 301 violation or a Section 302 violation. The penalties are vastly different."

Jarek walked over to Gerald.

"Listen, buddy. You're a Core.

You're supposed to fight. You're supposed to spawn monsters.

You're supposed to defend the dungeon.

"

Gerald looked at Jarek.

"I have been a municipal inspector for thirty-one years. I do not fight.

I inspect.

And this dungeon," he gestured around the sub-basement, "is currently failing its fire safety inspection due to the presence of open voids in the floor."

"Open voids?" Elara asked.

"The breach we just closed," Gerald said.

"It is a hole in the floor. It has no guardrail. It has no warning signage.

It is a trip hazard.

"

He walked to the edge of the pit. He looked down into the darkness.

"I need to install a handrail," Gerald said.

"You can't build a handrail over a void," Mira said. "It's not solid matter."

"Everything is matter if you apply the correct building code," Gerald said. He tapped his clipboard. "Section 12-B: *Emergency Egress Structures*. If a void is present, and it poses a danger to life, a temporary barrier must be erected within ten minutes of discovery."

He closed his eyes. The air around him hummed.

**[SYSTEM ALERT: CORE STATUS: ROGUE.]** **[ACTION: CREATING TEMPORARY STRUCTURE.]** **[COST: 500 MANA, 500 STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY.]** **[WARNING: INSUFFICIENT RESOURCES.]**

Gerald's face remained blank. He opened his eyes.

"Insufficient resources," he said. He looked at Mira. "Do you have lumber?"

Mira looked at her pack.

"I have... a spare shield plank. It's oak."

"Is it treated for rot?" Gerald asked.

"It's... mostly."

"Close enough for temporary compliance," Gerald said.

He pointed at the edge of the pit.

"Place it there. Span the gap.

I will certify it.

"

Mira hesitated.

"If I put a plank over a void that's eating reality..."

"The void is no longer eating reality," Gerald said. "The Stop Work Order is active. The void is currently a pending legal dispute. It is safe to build on."

Mira pulled the plank from her pack. It was a broken piece of her old tower shield. She walked to the edge of the pit. She placed the plank across the gap.

It hovered there, supported by the residual magic of Gerald's citation.

Gerald walked over. He knelt. He placed his hand on the plank.

"Certified," he said.

The plank glowed with a faint blue light.

**[STRUCTURE ADDED: TEMPORARY GUARDRAIL.]** **[COMPLIANCE RATING: 48/100 (+1).]** **[NEW UNLOCK: HANDRAIL SPAWN PROTOCOL.]**

A small, metallic sound echoed through the room. From the edge of the plank, a small, iron post sprouted. Then another. Then a horizontal bar formed between them.

In five seconds, a perfect, code-compliant handrail surrounded the pit.

Jarek stared at it.

"Did... did he just spawn a fence?"

"He spawned a safety barrier," Gerald corrected. He stood up and dusted off his trousers. "Now. Regarding the monsters."

He walked to the center of the room. The air was still thick with the smell of ozone.

"The territorial rules were suspended," Gerald said. "That means any creature can enter this space. But it also means that any creature entering this space is subject to the laws of the space."

He opened his clipboard to a new page.

**[NEW CITATION DRAFT: FORM 99-Z: UNLICENSED RESIDENCY.]**

"Who is the nearest dungeon core?" Gerald asked.

Mira shook her head.

"I don't know. The map is corrupted."

"The map is never corrupted," Gerald said. "It is simply incomplete."

He closed his eyes. He focused. The System interface appeared before him, a shimmering grid of red lines.

"There," Gerald said, pointing to a faint pulsing dot to the north. "Core 892. The 'Gnoll Warren.' It is three hundred meters from this location."

"How do you know?" Elara asked.

"It is listed in the Municipal Zoning Database," Gerald said. "Under 'Unpermitted Residential Structures.' I have been trying to get them to move out of the park for twenty years."

He wrote.

"I am issuing a Citation for *Failure to Maintain Setback Distances*," Gerald said. "The Gnoll Warren is within the required thirty-meter setback from the Ashford Library. This is a Class 2 violation."

He tore the page off. He held it up.

"Who is willing to serve this?"

Mira looked at the paper. She looked at the handrail. She looked at Gerald.

"You're crazy," Mira said.

"I am thorough," Gerald said. "There is a difference."

He handed the citation to Jarek.

"Run north," Gerald said. "Find the entrance to the Gnoll Warren.

Post this on the front door.

Do not enter. Do not fight. Just post it.

"

Jarek took the paper.

"And if they attack me?"

"Then you are an accomplice to a Class 2 violation," Gerald said. "And you will be cited alongside them."

Jarek swallowed. He looked at Mira.

Mira nodded. "Go."

Jarek ran.

Gerald turned to Mira.

"While he is gone, we must address the ventilation. The air quality in this sub-basement is poor. It violates the Minimum Ventilation Standards for Public Assembly."

"We're in a dungeon," Mira said. "There is no public assembly."

"There is now," Gerald said. "You are here. The System has recognized you as a public interest stakeholder. Therefore, you are a member of the public."

He walked to a corner of the room. He pulled out a small, gray box from his pocket. It was a portable air quality monitor.

"Readings are at 400 parts per million CO2," Gerald said. "The limit is 1000 for short-term exposure. But for long-term dungeon habitation, the limit is 400. We are at the threshold."

He placed the monitor on the floor.

"I need to install a mechanical ventilation system," Gerald said. "Do you have any ductwork?"

Mira stared at him.

"Gerald. You just saved the city by citing the apocalypse.

You just built a fence over a void.

You just sent my rogue to post a citation on a gnoll den. And you're worried about carbon dioxide?

"

Gerald looked up, his eyes sharp.

"If the air is bad, the spawns are weak," Gerald said. "If the spawns are weak, the dungeon fails. If the dungeon fails, the City pays the fine. I do not want to pay the fine."

He tapped his pen.

"Find me a fan," Gerald said. "A large one. Industrial."

Mira looked at Elara. Elara shrugged.

"I have a wind spell," Elara said.

"Does it meet OSHA noise regulations?" Gerald asked.

Elara blinked.

"Does what?"

"The wind spell," Gerald said. "Is it quiet? If it exceeds 85 decibels, I cannot use it. It would create a noise violation, which is a separate citation, and I cannot issue two citations for the same incident without a hearing."

Elara sighed. "It's a gentle breeze. Maybe 40 decibels."

"Acceptable," Gerald said. "Cast it at the north wall. I will direct the flow to the ventilation shafts."

Elara cast the spell. A breeze swirled through the room. The dust settled. The air felt cooler.

Gerald watched the air monitor. The numbers dropped. 390. 380. 370.

**[COMPLIANCE RATING: 49/100 (+1).]**

Gerald allowed himself a small smile. It was a thin, tight expression, but it was there.

"Good," Gerald said. "Now, the water leak."

He pointed to the ceiling. A steady drip of water was falling from a crack in the concrete.

"Water intrusion," Gerald said. "Section 7.

It damages the structural integrity.

It creates a slip hazard.

It promotes mold growth.

"

He looked at Mira.

"Do you have a bucket?"

Mira looked at her pack.

"I have... a potion bottle."

"Is it large enough?"

"It holds two liters."

"Temporary containment," Gerald said. "Place it under the drip."

Mira placed the bottle.

Gerald watched the water drip into the bottle. *Drip. Drip. Drip.*

He pulled out his clipboard.

**[CITATION: FORM 7-Q: UNCONTAINED WATER SOURCE.]**

"I will issue this to the Library Maintenance Department," Gerald said. "They are not a Core, but they are a responsible party. The System allows citations against non-cores if the violation impacts a core's compliance rating."

He looked up.

"Where is the library manager?"

Mira pointed to the stairs.

"Probably upstairs. Trying to survive."

"Go get him," Gerald said. "Tell him Gerald Hutchins is here. Tell him he has been cited for failure to provide adequate drainage."

Mira looked at the stairs. She looked at Gerald.

"And if he refuses to come down?"

"Then I will cite the building itself for *Obstruction of Access*," Gerald said. "And I will call the Inspector General."

Mira froze. "The Inspector General?"

Gerald's face went rigid.

"I do not wish to call the Inspector General. It is a very stressful process. But if the maintenance department refuses to cooperate, I have no choice."

The air in the room grew heavy. The System interface flickered.

**[WARNING: INSPECTOR GENERAL PROXIMITY DETECTED.]** **[DISTANCE: 2 KILOMETERS AND CLOSING.]**

Gerald looked at the notification. His hand shook slightly. He clenched his fist to stop it.

"He is coming," Gerald whispered.

"Why?" Mira asked.

"Because I cited the City," Gerald said. "And I just cited the maintenance department. That is a pattern.

The Inspector General does not like patterns. He likes order.

"

Gerald looked at the handrail. He looked at the water bottle. He looked at the air monitor.

"I have to go," Gerald said.

"Go where?" Mira asked.

"To the roof," Gerald said. "The Inspector General will audit the highest point first. It is standard procedure."

He turned and walked toward the stairs. His steps were steady, but his face was gray.

"Mira," Gerald said, without looking back. "If he asks you anything, tell him nothing. Say only: 'The code is the code.'"

He reached the stairs. He climbed.

Halfway up, he stopped, and he looked down at Mira.

"And if he asks why I did it?" Gerald said. "Tell him I was following the rules."

He continued climbing. The stairs creaked under his weight.

Mira watched him go. She looked at the handrail. She looked at the citation paper Jarek had left behind, fluttering in the breeze of Elara's spell.

She picked it up, which was blank.

Gerald had already torn it out.

But the impression of the writing remained in the paper. A ghost of the law, etched in carbon.

Mira traced the words. *Stop Work Order.*

She looked at the void. The hole in the world was gone. In its place was a perfect, iron handrail.

And then, from the darkness beyond the handrail, a new sound.

Not a roar. Not a scream.

A knock.

Three sharp raps on the iron rail.

*Clang. Clang. Clang.*

Mira froze.

The handrail vibrated.

Something was on the other side.

Something that had been waiting in the void.

And it had just been served.
