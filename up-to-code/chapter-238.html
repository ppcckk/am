Chapter 238: Forged Permit Signature

# Chapter 238

The municipal parking structure of Ashford Public Library did not look like a construction site. It looked like a war zone.

Sergeant Miller kicked aside a twisted rebar spike that had erupted from the asphalt like a black metal flower. His flashlight beam cut through the dust, illuminating a crater that shouldn't have existed. The concrete slab beneath his boots vibrated with a low, subterranean hum.

"Command," Miller said into his radio, his voice tight. "I've got a sinkhole.

Or a bomb site.

I can't tell. It's eating the parking lot."

Static crackled in his ear. "Hold position, Miller. We're sending Hazmat. Do not approach the perimeter."

Miller didn't hold. He stepped closer.

At the edge of the crater, the asphalt curled upward like burning paper. Beneath the broken surface, Gerald Hutchins stood in a trench of exposed earth, wearing a hard hat and a high-visibility vest that was three sizes too large for his slight frame. He held a clipboard in one hand and a laser level in the other.

Miller raised his rifle. "Hey! You! Step away from the hole!"

Gerald didn't look up. He adjusted the tripod legs of the laser level, squinting at the red dot dancing on a vertical column of wet concrete.

"Move it, old man!" Miller shouted.

Gerald tapped his pen against the clipboard. "You are standing in a designated work zone, Officer. Please relocate to the safety barrier. You are obstructing the pour line."

Miller blinked.

The absurdity of the statement short-circuited his tactical instinct. He lowered the rifle an inch.

"What pour line?

There is no pour line. There is a hole in the city."

"There is a foundation," Gerald corrected, not looking up. "And it is failing. Check your shoes."

Miller looked down. His boots were sinking into a gray slurry that smelled of sulfur and wet cement. The ground beneath him was liquefying.

"Back up!" Miller yelled, stumbling backward as the slurry pulled at his soles.

Gerald sighed. It was the sigh of a man who had spent three decades explaining to contractors why their load-bearing beams were actually decorative supports.

"System," Gerald muttered. "Cite.

Immediate Hazard.

Violation 402.14: Unsecured Trench.

Severity: Critical."

A chime echoed in the air, not from a speaker, but from the stone itself. A translucent blue box hovered above the crater, visible only to Gerald.

**CITATION ISSUED.** **Target: The Maw (Dungeon Core).** **Violation: Uncontrolled Subterranean Expansion.** **Penalty: Structural Integrity -15%. Egress Width Reduced by 40%.** **Duration: Permanent until remediation.**

The ground shuddered. A distant roar, deep and guttural, erupted from beneath the asphalt. It sounded like a thousand tons of rock grinding against itself.

Miller fell backward, landing hard on his ass. "What the hell was that?"

Gerald finally looked at him. His face was streaked with gray dust. His eyes were dead, flat, and professional.

"That," Gerald said, "is a citation. You may file an appeal with the System, but I recommend you check your structural logs first. Your dungeon's load-bearing walls are now rated for light pedestrian traffic only."

Miller scrambled to his feet, backing away toward the perimeter tape. "System? What System? Who are you?"

"Gerald Hutchins. Municipal Building Inspector. Badge number 74-B." Gerald pointed at the crater.

"And you are trespassing on a construction site without a permit. I am writing you up."

Miller stared at the man, then at the churning earth, then at the impossible blue box fading into the dust. He ran.

***

Gerald watched the police officer flee. He felt no triumph. He felt the familiar, dull ache of a job half-finished.

He turned back to the trench.

The Maw, the rival dungeon core that had been trying to expand into the library's sub-basement for weeks, was not dead. It was merely inconvenienced. The Citation had weakened its structural integrity, causing its tunnels to collapse under their own weight, but it was still alive.

Still breathing.

Still hungry.

Pendergast emerged from the shadows of the library's service entrance. He carried a bag of quick-setting cement mix and a shovel that looked like it had been used for grave digging.

"The cops?" Pendergast asked.

"Retreating," Gerald said. "They are confused. Good. Confusion buys time."

"Time for what?" Pendergast dumped the cement mix into the slurry.

"We need more concrete. The Maw is pushing back. I can feel it.

It's trying to seal the breach."

Gerald looked at the laser level. The red dot was flickering.

"The Maw is using a Shadow-Weave patch," Gerald said. "It's a cheap fix. Non-compliant."

"Can you stop it?"

"I can't stop magic," Gerald said. "I can stop bad code."

He walked to the edge of the trench. The darkness below writhed. Tendrils of black smoke curled up, seeking purchase on the exposed rebar.

Gerald raised his clipboard. "System. Query: Definition of Structural Void."

**DEFINITION:** A structural void is any space within a load-bearing assembly that exceeds maximum allowable dimensions for its material composition. Voids create stress points.

"Correct," Gerald said.

"The Maw's tunnel is a void.

It is not supported by pillars. It is not braced.

It is a hole in the earth with no code compliance."

He looked at Pendergast. "Do we have rebar?"

Pendergast pointed to a pile of rusted metal rods leaning against a dumpster. "Enough for a cage. Not a wall."

"A cage is a support structure," Gerald said. "System.

Issue Citation: Lack of Lateral Support.

Violation 403.2. Severity: High."

Another chime. Another blue box.

**CITATION ISSUED.** **Target: The Maw.** **Violation: Absence of Lateral Bracing in Tunnel Section 4.** **Penalty: Tunnel Collapse Probability Increased by 60%.** **Status: Pending Review.**

The ground groaned. The black tendrils retracted, hissing.

Pendergast grabbed the rebar. "You're making it collapse on itself."

"I am enforcing minimum safety standards," Gerald said. "If the Maw wants to exist in my jurisdiction, it must support its own weight. Otherwise, it is a hazard."

"But it will attack us if it collapses," Pendergast said, wrestling a long rod into the mud. "If it dies, its debris will flood the library."

"Not if we build a containment zone first," Gerald said.

He pulled a roll of heavy-duty plastic sheeting from his vest. It was meant for painting, not dungeon defense. It snapped taut as he unrolled it.

"System," Gerald said. "New Mechanic: Provisional Barrier."

**QUERY:** Provisional Barrier allows for temporary sealing of hazardous zones using approved materials. Cost: 10 Mana per square foot. Duration: 4 hours.

"Accept," Gerald said.

Blue light spread from his hands, coating the plastic sheeting. The sheeting hardened, turning opaque and rigid. It became a wall, but a temporary one. A wall that existed only as long as Gerald maintained his concentration.

"Help me with this," Gerald said.

Pendergast grabbed the other end of the sheeting. They stretched it across the top of the trench, anchoring it to the rebar rods. The Maw shrieked beneath them. The blue light flickered, straining against the pressure from below.

"It's heavy," Pendergast grunted.

"It's code," Gerald said through gritted teeth. "Code is heavy."

The Inspector General's audit was due in twenty hours.

Gerald needed the library to look like a construction site, not a dungeon. A construction site had permits.

A construction site had workers.

A construction site had liability insurance. A dungeon had monsters.

If he could prove the library was under active, compliant construction, the Inspector General would be forced to pause the audit until the project was finished. It was a loophole Gerald had discovered by reading the fine print of the Municipal Code while the System tried to kill him.

*Section 104.2: Active Construction Sites are exempt from immediate occupancy audits if a valid Building Permit is posted and a Site Supervisor is present.*

Gerald was the Site Supervisor. He had written his own permit. It was forged, technically, but the System recognized the act of writing it as a valid administrative action.

"More rebar!" Gerald shouted.

Pendergast ran to the dumpster. He dragged three more rods into the trench.

A sudden impact shook the ground. A claw, massive and chitinous, punched through the plastic sheeting.

Pendergast fell back.

Gerald didn't move. He stared at the claw. It was dripping with acidic slime.

"Violation," Gerald said. "Unpermitted Biological Entity."

The claw twitched.

"System. Cite. Violation 501. Unauthorized Organism."

**CITATION ISSUED.** **Target: Maw Spawn (Elite).** **Penalty: Stat Debuff: Chitin Hardness -50%.** **Effect: Instant.**

The claw cracked. It splintered like dry wood. The Maw screamed, a sound that shattered windows in the library above.

Gerald pulled a hammer from his tool belt. He walked to the broken claw.

He hit it.

Once.

Twice. Three times.

The chitin shattered further. The spawn retracted, screaming in pain and confusion.

"Get the concrete," Gerald said. "Now."

Pendergast stared at the broken claw. Then at Gerald. Then at the hammer.

"You're hitting it," Pendergast said.

"I am removing non-compliant waste," Gerald said. "It is debris. Debris must be cleared."

They poured the cement. It was thick, gray, and sluggish. They shoveled it into the gaps around the rebar, smoothing the surface with trowels. The blue light of the Provisional Barrier held the weight, but it was dimming.

Gerald felt the drain. His mana was low. His stamina was lower.

His back ached. He was fifty-four years old.

He had arthritis in his left knee.

But the wall was rising.

It wasn't a dungeon wall. It was a retaining wall. It was ugly, utilitarian, and perfectly square.

"System," Gerald said. "Post Permit."

A small, laminated card appeared in his hand. It read: **BUILDING PERMIT #9942. PROJECT: LIBRARY SUB-BASEMENT EXPANSION.

SUPERVISOR: G.

HUTCHINS. STATUS: ACTIVE.**

He pinned it to the wet concrete.

The air shifted. The heavy, oppressive aura of the dungeon flickered. For a second, the area smelled of wet cement and diesel fumes instead of rot and fear.

Pendergast laughed. It was a nervous, jagged sound. "You did it. You made it look like a job site."

Gerald looked at his watch. Six hours until the Inspector General returned.

"We have six hours," Gerald said. "We need to finish the egress route. And we need to find the source of the water leak."

"The leak?" Pendergast asked. "The Maw is causing the leak."

"No," Gerald said. He looked at the blue box in his vision.

**SYSTEM ALERT: STRUCTURAL ANOMALY DETECTED.** **Source: Non-Dungeon Origin.** **Classification: Municipal Infrastructure Failure.** **Note: This is not your responsibility. But failure to address will result in Joint Liability.**

Gerald frowned. "It's not the Maw."

He walked to the corner of the trench. He dug his trowel into the mud. He hit something hard.

He dug faster.

Pendergast approached. "What is it?"

Gerald cleared the dirt.

A pipe emerged. It was old, cast iron, and rusted through. But it wasn't from the library.

The stamp on the pipe read: **CITY OF ASHFORD. SEWER MAIN.

CLASS 4.**

"This is the city's sewer main," Gerald said.

"So?"

"It's cracked," Gerald said. "And it's leaking directly into the Maw's tunnel system."

Pendergast looked pale. "You're saying the city is feeding the monster?"

"I'm saying the city is negligent," Gerald said.

He stood up. His hands were shaking.

Not from fear. From rage.

He pulled out his pen. He opened his clipboard to a fresh page.

"System," Gerald said. "Cite. Violation 101. Negligent Maintenance of Public Infrastructure."

**ERROR: TARGET NOT FOUND.** **Target must be a Dungeon Core.**

Gerald stared at the error. He couldn't cite the city. He couldn't cite the mayor. He couldn't cite the plumbing department.

He could only cite dungeons.

But the leak was making the Maw stronger. The sludge was fueling its regeneration.

Gerald looked at the pipe. He looked at the Maw's tunnel. He looked at the permit in his hand.

A new idea formed. It was dangerous. It was stupid. It was the most professional thing he had ever thought of.

He couldn't cite the city. But he could cite the Maw for *contaminating* a public water source.

"System. Cite. Violation 304. Cross-Connection Hazard."

**QUERY:** Target is Maw Core. Violation: Introducing foreign contaminants into municipal water supply.

"Execute," Gerald said.

**CITATION ISSUED.** **Target: The Maw.** **Penalty: Mana Regen -100%. Poison Immunity Removed.** **Duration: Until Leak is Sealed.**

The Maw shrieked. This time, it wasn't angry. It was sick.

Gerald felt a wave of nausea hit him. The citation was working, but it was hurting the environment too. The poison was spreading.

"Pendergast," Gerald said. "We have to seal the pipe. Now."

"We don't have the parts," Pendergast said.

Gerald looked at the pipe. He looked at the rebar. He looked at the concrete.

"We have the code," Gerald said. "Code requires a cap."

He walked toward the library doors.

"Where are you going?" Pendergast asked.

"To the janitor's closet," Gerald said. "I need a wrench. And I need to find out who signed off on this sewer line."

He pushed open the library doors. The lobby was empty. Dust motes danced in the light.

Gerald walked to the front desk. He picked up the phone. He dialed 311.

"City of Ashford, how can I help you?" a cheerful voice answered.

Gerald closed his eyes. He took a breath.

"This is Gerald Hutchins, Building Inspector," he said. "I need to report a Code Violation.

Class 4 Sewer Main, Ashford Public Library.

It is leaking into a hazardous containment zone. I am requesting an immediate shutdown order."

There was a pause.

"Sir, do you have a work order number?"

Gerald opened his eyes. He looked at the phone. He looked at the dust.

"No," Gerald said. "But I have a Citation. And I am serving it."

He hung up.

The phone rang immediately.

Gerald didn't answer. He walked to the janitor's closet. He kicked the door open.

Inside, he found a wrench. And a map of the city's sewer system.

The map was wrong. The lines didn't match the walls.

Gerald unfolded the map. He traced the lines with his finger. The sewer main wasn't just leaking. It was rerouted.

Someone had moved the pipes.

And the signature on the permit for the rerouting was not a city engineer.

It was a name Gerald recognized.

It was his own.

Gerald stared at the map. He had never signed a sewer permit.

But the handwriting was identical.

The phone rang again.
