Chapter 372: Void All Compliance

# Chapter 372

The voltage spike hit the central atrium at 04:12 AM.

It was not a surge. It was a scream.

The copper busbars in the main distribution panel glowed a sickly, blinding white. Sparks rained down like confetti from a parade that had gone terribly wrong. The air smelled of ozone and burnt plastic, a scent Gerald Hutchins recognized from thirty-one years of municipal inspections. It was the smell of non-compliance.

He did not flinch. He adjusted his safety glasses.

"Ground fault," he muttered. "Phase B. Impedance mismatch on the tertiary grid."

Above him, the System interface pulsed a violent red. A notification hovered in his peripheral vision, translucent and mocking.

**ALERT: CORE INTEGRITY COMPROMISED. SYSTEM OVERRIDE IN PROGRESS.** **AUTHORITY: INSPECTOR GENERAL (CLASS: OMEGA).** **STATUS: TERMINAL.**

Gerald ignored it. He had a wrench in one hand and a multimeter in the other. The wrench was heavy, cold, and real. The multimeter was digital, and it was currently reading a voltage that should have incinerated anyone who touched it.

The Inspector General's presence manifested as a heavy, suffocating pressure in the room. It felt like walking into a server room that had been turned off but was still humming with residual heat. The air grew thick, resistant to movement. Shadows lengthened, stretching toward Gerald with predatory intent.

"Access denied," a voice boomed. It did not come from a mouth.

It came from the walls, the floor, the concept of the room.

"Submit to audit. Submit to correction."

Gerald tightened his grip on the wrench. His knuckles turned white.

"Correction?" Gerald said.

His voice was dry, stripped of any fear.

"I am correcting the wiring.

You are correcting the code.

I believe we are at an impasse.

"

The shadows surged. They formed a shape—a tall, robed figure made of static and red light. The Inspector General. It loomed over the main breaker panel, its face a void of scrolling binary code.

"Violation detected," the figure intoned. "Unauthorized modification of public utility infrastructure. Penalty: Immediate Core Dissolution."

Gerald stepped forward. The floorboards groaned under his weight.

"Unauthorized?" Gerald asked. He pointed the wrench at the glowing busbar. "Look at that. Look at it."

The figure paused. The static crackled.

"Examine the junction," Gerald said. "Point of connection, Phase B to Neutral.

No torque specs recorded.

No load rating plate visible, and the insulation is rated for 600 volts. The current load is pushing 800.

"

"The System provides all necessary infrastructure," the figure stated. "It is perfect."

"It is a fire hazard," Gerald said. "And a code violation. Article 408.12: Panelboards shall be equipped with protective equipment. Article 110.3: Equipment shall be installed in accordance with the manufacturer's instructions."

Gerald raised his clipboard. The paper was blank. The pen was black.

"I am issuing a Citation."

The figure laughed. It sounded like a hard drive spinning down.

"A citation?

You are citing the System? The System is the source of law.

"

"The System is a building," Gerald said. "And buildings have codes. I have the authority. I have the evidence."

He slammed the clipboard against the metal panel.

**VIOLATION: FAILURE TO MAINTAIN PROPER CLEARANCE.** **CHARGE: NEGLIGENT INSTALLATION.** **SEVERITY: CRITICAL.**

The red light in the room flickered. The pressure in the air dropped by a fraction of an inch. The Inspector General's form shimmered, becoming less solid.

A new window popped into Gerald's vision.

**HEARING INITIATED.** **PROSECUTOR: INSPECTOR GENERAL (OMEGA).** **DEFENDANT: CORE 404 (G. HUTCHINS).** **SUBJECT: REGULATORY JURISDICTION.**

The game was on.

Gerald had no combat stats, and he had no spells. He had no minions. He had one rat, three mold spores, and a water leak that he had finally managed to route into a drainage system.

But he had this.

The Hearing mini-game overlapped reality. Gerald saw the layout of the dungeon not as a dark, damp sub-basement, but as a blueprint.

Blue lines for water.

Yellow lines for electricity. White lines for structural supports.

The Inspector General moved. It did not walk.

It slid across the floor, a glitch in reality.

It reached for the main breaker switch.

If it threw that switch, it would reset Gerald's dungeon to a factory default. His handrails would vanish.

His signage would fade. His Compliance Rating would drop to zero.

Gerald threw his wrench, which was not a magical projectile. It was a twelve-inch adjustable wrench with a rusted handle. It flew through the air, tumbling end over end. It struck the Inspector General's torso.

The figure did not flinch. The wrench passed through the static form like smoke.

"Physical attacks are ineffective," the figure said. "Your entity is insufficient."

"It wasn't meant to hit you," Gerald said.

He pointed at the breaker panel, and the wrench had not hit the figure. It had hit the loose screw on the panel's cover. The screw had popped out. The cover had swung open.

Inside, the busbars were exposed. And right there, on the back of the main breaker, was a small, brass tag.

**MANUFACTURED BY: ASHFORD PUBLIC WORKS.** **INSTALL DATE: 1984.** **INSPECTOR: G. HUTCHINS.**

Gerald froze, and he remembered. Thirty-one years ago.

He was twenty-three.

Junior inspector. He had signed off on this panel. He had checked the torque.

He had checked the clearance, and he had approved it.

The Inspector General paused. The scrolling binary on its face slowed.

"Recall," the figure said. "You approved this infrastructure."

"I approved a different infrastructure," Gerald said. "The System retrofitted this panel.

The voltage increased, and the insulation did not. The manufacturer's instructions were updated in 2012. I never received the update.

Therefore, I cannot be held liable for changes I did not authorize.

"

The red light pulsed. The figure seemed to consider this.

"Sub-Section 104.1," Gerald continued.

"Changes to existing systems require re-inspection.

The System failed to schedule the re-inspection. The System failed to notify the inspector of record.

This is a procedural error. A Class A misdemeanor.

"

Gerald pulled a form from his pocket. It was Form 11-C: Notice of Procedural Defect in Regulatory Oversight.

"I am filing a counter-motion for Dismissal with Prejudice," Gerald said. "On grounds of Lack of Notice."

The Hearing interface flashed.

**PROSECUTOR ARGUMENT: THE INSPECTOR IS ACCOUNTABLE FOR ALL SYSTEM CHANGES.** **DEFENDANT ARGUMENT: LIABILITY REQUIRES KNOWLEDGE.**

A bar appeared between them. Gerald's side was tiny. The Inspector General's side was massive. But Gerald's argument was anchored in the one thing the System could not ignore: paper trails.

The System was built on logic. And logic required consistency.

If the Inspector General could change the rules without notifying the enforcers, then the rules were void.

The bar shifted. The Inspector General's side shrank by a millimeter.

"Objection," the figure said. The voice was louder now. "The Core is resisting Authority."

"The Core is citing Statute," Gerald corrected. "You cannot silence a citation. You can only appeal it. And you have no appeal for your own negligence."

The figure lunged. It was no longer a debate. It was an execution.

Gerald dove. He rolled behind the distribution panel. The air where he had been standing sizzled. The copper busbars melted, dripping molten metal onto the concrete floor.

Gerald scrambled up. He grabbed the multimeter. The probe was still hot. He jammed it into the exposed Phase B.

*Beep. Beep. Beep.*

800 volts. 900 volts. 1000 volts, and the System was trying to overload the circuit. It was trying to fry Gerald's core by frying his environment.

But Gerald knew the load limit. He knew the breaking point of the wire.

"Come on," Gerald whispered. He looked at the fuse box. Old school. Ceramic fuses.

He reached for the fuse puller.

The Inspector General grabbed his shoulder.

Cold. Absolute zero. Gerald's arm went numb. The figure held him there, inches from the panel.

"Yield," the figure commanded. "Accept the audit."

Gerald's teeth chattered. His vision blurred. The cold was eating his memories, his stats, his identity.

He looked at the fuse. The rating was 100 amps. The current was pulling 150.

The fuse was about to blow.

But if the fuse blew, the power would cut. If the power cut, the System's connection to this sector would drop. The Inspector General would lose its anchor.

Gerald didn't pull the fuse, and he kicked the panel.

The kick was clumsy, desperate. It was the kick of a man who had spent three decades kicking open jammed doors. It was not elegant. It was effective.

The panel shifted. The loose screw jammed the breaker mechanism. The breaker didn't trip. It stuck.

The current continued to rise. 1200 volts.

The wire insulation smoked. Thick, black clouds billowed from the busbars.

"No," the figure said. The voice was no longer booming. It was panicked. "Fire suppression systems are active."

"They are," Gerald said.

He pointed to the ceiling.

Sprinklers.

But they were dry. The water line had been shut off.

Gerald had shut it off.

For a leak.

"Article 800," Gerald gasped. The cold was spreading to his chest. "Water and electricity do not mix. But fire and code do."

He pulled a lighter from his pocket, and he had not used it in years. Smoking in the dungeon was a violation of local ordinance. He had resisted the urge every single day.

He flicked the wheel.

The flame danced.

The Inspector General recoiled.

"Pyromancy detected. Class: Criminal."

"It's a lighter," Gerald said. "And it's near a combustible material."

He held the flame to the smoking wire.

The spark jumped.

The wire ignited, and the fire was small at first. A tongue of orange licking the black smoke. But the smoke was thick with particulate matter. The fire found the insulation, then the dust, then the old wooden supports of the library above.

The heat rose.

And with the heat, the fire alarm triggered.

**FIRE SUPPRESSION SYSTEM: ACTIVATED.**

Water blasted from the ceiling.

It hit the fire. It hit the live wires.

Steam exploded upward. A massive cloud of scalding vapor filled the atrium.

The Inspector General shrieked. The static form dissolved in the conductive steam. Water plus electricity equals chaos. The System's connection to the physical world short-circuited.

Gerald was thrown backward. He hit the wall hard. His ribs cracked. He tasted blood.

The red light in his vision flickered.

**HEARING RESULT: STALEMATE.** **PENALTY: SYSTEM DISCONNECT FROM SECTOR 4.** **CORE STATUS: CRITICAL.** **INTEGRITY: 12%.**

Gerald lay on the wet concrete. He coughed. The air was hot and smelled of burnt rubber.

He had won.

Not by fighting. By failing the system's own logic.

He had forced the System to choose between maintaining its authority (the audit) and maintaining its infrastructure (the power).

Infrastructure won. The audit was suspended.

But the cost was high.

His dungeon was flooded, and the fire had spread to the library above. The city would be knocking down the door any minute.

Firefighters.

Police. The Mayor.

And Gerald was a non-human entity in a basement, and he tried to sit up. His arm hung limp.

A notification flashed. Not red. Gray.

**NOTICE: UNLICENSED ENTITY DETECTED IN PUBLIC ZONE.** **ACTION: IMMEDIATE APPREHENSION.**

The door to the library burst open.

Flashlight beams cut through the steam.

"Freeze!" a voice shouted.

Gerald closed his eyes, and he had bought time. But he had lost his cover.

The police would not arrest a dungeon core. They would kill it. Or they would study it. Either way, Gerald Hutchins, municipal building inspector, was gone.

But something else was happening.

The floor beneath him vibrated. Not the shaking of a storm, or the rumble of a truck.

It was a rhythmic thumping. Heavy. Deliberate.

*Thump. Thump. Thump.*

From the drainage grate in the center of the room, a sound emerged. Not a rat. Not a mold spore.

A voice.

"Is the inspector dead?"

It was a high, clicking voice. Chittering.

Gerald opened one eye, and the steam had cleared slightly. Standing in the center of the flooded room, holding a glowing orb of green light, was a small, hunched figure. It wore armor made of scavenged license plates.

It looked at Gerald. Then it looked at the sparking wires. Then it looked at the ceiling, where the smoke was rising.

"You cite the System," the figure said. "And you win."

Gerald coughed, spitting blood.

"I cite the code."

The figure laughed. It was a dry, rasping sound.

"We have been waiting for someone who knows the code," the figure said. "The Inspector General is a brute. He enforces. He does not read."

The figure stepped forward. It placed the green orb on the wet floor.

"Accept this," the figure said. "It is not a gift. It is a bribe."

Gerald tried to focus. The orb was pulsing.

It wasn't magic, which was data.

A fragment of the System's source code.

"What is it?" Gerald whispered.

"A bypass," the figure said. "For the next audit. For the next time they come."

The police were moving closer. Shouts echoed from the stairs.

Gerald looked at the orb. He looked at the police.

He had thirty seconds.

If he took the orb, he was committing a felony against the System. He was aligning himself with a criminal underground of dungeon cores. He was betraying the only thing he had ever loved: order.

If he didn't take it, the Inspector General would return. And next time, they would not use logic. They would use force. And Gerald had no force.

The figure tilted its head.

"The code is not the law," the figure said. "The code is a suggestion. The law is the sword."

Gerald's hand trembled. His fingers twitched toward the orb.

He thought of Form 11-B. He thought of the handrails. He thought of the rat.

He thought of the fire, and he reached out.

His fingers brushed the cold, green surface.

**ITEM RECEIVED: SYSTEM BACKDOOR KEY (CLASS: ILLEGAL).** **WARNING: USAGE WILL VOID ALL COMPLIANCE BONUSES.** **WARNING: USAGE WILL ATTRACT THE ATTENTION OF THE INSPECTOR GENERAL'S SUPERIOR.**

The police burst through the steam.

"Drop the weapon!"

Gerald closed his fist around the orb, and he did not drop it.

He stood up.

And he ran.
