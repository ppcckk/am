Chapter 235: Code and Rat

# Chapter 235

The fluorescent tube in the hallway flickered with a rhythm that violated the municipal electrical code.

Gerald Hutchins counted the intervals. One.

Two.

Three. Four. Five.

One.

A five-second interval between flickers indicated a faulty starter capacitor. It also indicated a localized energy drain that would, by standard definition, constitute an unauthorized modification of public infrastructure.

He did not look up from his clipboard. His pen hovered over the grid, ready to log the violation. The air in the corridor smelled of ozone and old paper, a combination that usually preceded a fire marshal's visit. Today, it preceded something worse.

A shadow detached itself from the baseboard. It moved with a fluidity that defied the static nature of shadows, sliding across the linoleum with a wet, slithering sound.

Gerald marked the flicker on his sheet. Then he marked the shadow.

**System Alert: Unauthorized Entity Detected.**

**Classification: Shadow Stalker (Variant).**

**Violation: Failure to Maintain Minimum Illumination Standards for Public Safety.**

The shadow surged forward, a mass of darkness that sought to swallow the light. Gerald did not step back. He stepped forward.

He tapped his pen against the wall. *Click.*

The sound was small, but in the silence of the closed library, it rang like a gavel.

"You are in violation of Ashford Municipal Code Section 14.2, subsection B," Gerald said. His voice was dry, carrying the weight of thirty-one years of unpaid overtime.

"Public corridors must maintain an average illuminance of thirty foot-candles.

You are operating in darkness. This is a hazard."

The shadow paused. It rippled, confused by the audacity of the inspector. It did not attack. It waited.

Gerald reached into his jacket pocket. His fingers brushed the silver key. It was warm now, humming with a low-frequency vibration that matched the flickering light.

He pulled the key out.

The shadow lunged.

Gerald did not dodge. He raised the key and held it up, catching the light from the flickering tube. The reflection hit the shadow directly.

**System Notification: Code Enforcement Action Initiated.**

**Violation: Obstruction of Egress Path.**

**Penalty: Immediate Dispersion.**

The shadow screamed. It was a silent scream, a vibration in the floorboards that rattled Gerald's teeth. The darkness fractured, breaking apart into harmless wisps of smoke that dissipated into the ventilation system.

The light stabilized. The flicker stopped.

Gerald checked his watch. 5:14 PM. Fourteen minutes since the test ended. He had less than twenty-four hours before the termination order became active, before the System stripped his core status and returned him to the mundane, powerless existence of a fired employee.

He walked to the next door. The janitor's closet.

The door was locked. A simple padlock, rusted from lack of use. Gerald produced a small key from his belt loop. He unlocked it and swung the door open.

Inside, the dungeon core's heart beat in sync with his own.

The room had changed. The floor was no longer linoleum.

It was stone, cold and damp.

The walls were lined with fungal growths that pulsed with a faint, green bioluminescence.

A single rat sat in the corner, grooming its whiskers with aggressive precision.

This was his dungeon. Four thousand square feet of sub-basement, currently rated at a Compliance Score of 48/100.

**Current Status:** **Ventilation: D (Deficient)** **Fire Suppression: F (Critical Failure)** **Structural Integrity: C (Marginal)** **Signage: A (Exemplary)**

Gerald ignored the rat. He walked to the center of the room. The air tasted of mold and regret.

He needed a new mechanic. He needed a loophole. The System had cited him for possessing an unauthorized artifact.

The key was the artifact. If he couldn't prove the key was part of his core infrastructure, he would lose his job, and likely his life.

He looked at the stone floor. There was a crack running from the center to the north wall. A structural fissure.

In a normal dungeon, this was a weakness. In Gerald's dungeon, it was a feature.

He knelt.

He placed his hand on the crack. He closed his eyes and listened to the hum of the System. He could feel the boundaries of his domain, the invisible lines that defined his authority.

They were fraying.

The test had damaged them.

He opened his eyes and spoke.

"System. File a Form 11-C."

A prompt appeared in his vision, glowing blue against the damp stone.

**Form 11-C: Request for Temporary Structural Modification for Compliance Purposes.**

**Requirement: Demonstrate that the modification serves a mandatory safety code.**

Gerald typed the request mentally. *Modify fissure 4-A. Convert into emergency exhaust vent. Justification: Improves ventilation rating from D to B.*

The System paused. The hum in the walls grew louder.

**System Error: Modification requires material resources. Insufficient stone mass.**

Gerald frowned.

He didn't have stone. He had mold. He had water.

He had a confused rat.

He looked at the mold on the walls. Thick, white, fibrous. Fungal biomass.

**System Query: Can biomass be considered a structural building material under Municipal Code 9.1?**

Gerald paused. He knew this code. He had written the commentary for it three years ago. Section 9.1, paragraph 4: *Alternative structural materials, including treated biological compounds, are acceptable if they meet the same compressive strength requirements as standard concrete.*

The mold was not treated. But the System didn't know that yet. The System operated on the letter of the law, not the spirit of the biology.

"System," Gerald said aloud. "Classify the fungal biomass as 'Treated Biological Compound.' Apply the exemption in Code 9.1."

**System Processing...**

**Classification Accepted. Material Quality: Low. Structural Integrity: Weak.**

**Modification Approved.**

The floor groaned. The crack widened. But instead of collapsing, the stone was pulled upward, woven together with the thick strands of mold. The fissure became a jagged, ugly hole in the floor.

Gerald stepped back.

A draft rushed into the room from the newly formed hole. It was cold, carrying the scent of earth and decay. But it was fresh air.

**Ventilation Rating: C.**

**Compliance Score: 51/100.**

It was a gain. A small, dirty, necessary gain.

But the hole was ugly. It was a violation of aesthetic codes.

Gerald ignored that. He had bigger problems.

A vibration in the floor. Not from the ventilation. From outside.

The walls of his dungeon shook. Dust fell from the ceiling. The rat squeaked and hid under a pile of old newspapers.

Something was digging in.

Gerald moved to the center of the room. He could feel the pressure building, a rhythmic pounding against the perimeter of his domain.

**System Alert: Perimeter Breach Detected.**

**Source: Unregistered Core.**

**Designation: The Maw.**

**Status: Aggressive Expansion.**

The Maw. Gerald knew the name. It was a dungeon core that had taken up residence in the abandoned sewer system beneath the library. It had been encroaching on his territory for weeks, sending minor pests to test his defenses.

This was not a minor pest. This was an excavation.

The wall to the east bulged. The stone cracked. Mold roots snapped and withered as the pressure from the other side forced them back.

Gerald looked at his Compliance Score. 51. Too low. He needed more points. He needed a defense.

But he had no monsters. No traps. No weapons.

He had a citation.

He pulled his pen from his pocket. He pulled a blank piece of paper from the System's inventory interface.

He wrote.

**Citation Against The Maw: "Failure to Maintain Setback Distance from Adjacent Property Lines."**

**Violation: Encroachment on Public Utility Easement (Library Foundation).**

**Penalty: Immediate Cessation of Construction and Removal of Obstruction.**

He held the paper up. He focused his intent on the wall, on the encroaching pressure.

The wall cracked further.

**System Error: Target Core Not Present in Zone. Citation Cannot Be Served.**

Gerald gritted his teeth.

The rule was strict.

You could only cite a Core if they were physically in your dungeon. The Maw was digging from outside.

It was not inside.

But wait.

The wall was failing. If the wall failed, the Maw's influence would spill over. Its energy would enter his domain.

If its energy entered his domain, was it *inside*?

Gerald looked at the cracking wall. He looked at the hole in the floor.

He had a new idea. It was risky. It was potentially fatal.

He walked to the hole in the floor. The fresh air draft was strong now. It sucked at his jacket.

He looked at the ceiling. High above, a ventilation grate. Rusted, loose.

He picked up a heavy iron bar from the corner, one he had saved for "potential structural supports."

He threw it.

The bar hit the ventilation grate. It clattered, bouncing off the metal mesh.

He threw it again. And again.

The grate rattled. Screws loosened.

The Maw's pounding grew louder. The wall gave way. A chunk of stone fell.

Through the gap, Gerald saw it. A mass of black slime, pulsing with purple veins. A tentacle, thick as a man's thigh, reaching into his dungeon.

The tentacle touched the floor.

**System Alert: Foreign Energy Detected.**

**Status: Contamination.**

Gerald grabbed the iron bar and slammed it down on the tentacle.

It didn't kill it. It just annoyed it.

The tentacle retracted. A roar echoed through the sewers.

Gerald scrambled back. He ran to the ventilation grate. He needed to break it.

He used the iron bar as a lever. He put his weight on it. The screws popped.

The grate fell.

A shaft of darkness opened above him.

And down that shaft, descending with terrifying speed, came a drone. A construct made of bone and sewer water. It had no eyes, but it sensed the breach.

It plummeted toward him.

Gerald looked up. The drone was fast. It would crush him.

He looked at the hole in the floor. The ventilation shaft.

He had a ventilation rating of C.

He had a citation ready.

He had a key.

Gerald stood in the center of the room. He did not run. He raised the silver key.

He didn't aim at the drone.

He aimed at the air.

He focused on the System's definition of "Public Access."

"System," he shouted. "Activate Protocol: Emergency Egress Route."

**System Error: No Valid Egress Route Designated.**

Gerald smiled. It was a thin, cruel smile.

"Designate the ventilation shaft as an emergency egress route. Code 17.4. Allow public transit through infrastructure."

**Processing...**

**Designation Accepted. Egress Route Open.**

**Warning: Unauthorized Travelers Detected.**

The drone was still falling.

But as it passed through the designated egress zone, the System recognized it.

Not as a monster.

As a *traveler*.

**System Alert: Traveler Violation.**

**Violation: Failure to Present Transit Pass.**

**Violation: Public Nuisance in Transit Corridor.**

**Penalty: Immediate Repulsion.**

The air in the shaft snapped. A wall of force, generated by the System's own code enforcement algorithms, slammed into the drone.

The bone-construct exploded.

Shards of calcium and slime rained down around Gerald. He shielded his face with his arm.

Silence returned to the sub-basement.

Gerald lowered his arm. He was covered in slime. His shirt was torn. His heart hammered against his ribs.

He looked at the hole in the ceiling.

He had won. For now.

But the drone had been a scout. And the Maw was still out there. And it was angry.

He looked at his Compliance Score.

**Compliance Score: 54/100.**

**New Violation Logged: Unauthorized Use of Egress Route for Defense.**

**Penalty: -5 Points.**

**Current Score: 49/100.**

He had gained a defense, but he had lost points for abusing the system. It was a net loss.

Gerald pulled out his pen. He crossed out the 49. He wrote 50.

He needed to be higher.

He looked at the rat. The rat was peeking out from under the newspapers.

Gerald walked to it.

"Form 11-B," he muttered. "Unlicensed Biological Entity."

The rat froze.

"Or," Gerald said, "I could hire you. As a Compliance Assistant. You can check for structural weaknesses. You can report violations."

The rat twitched its nose.

"If you help me, I will grant you Core Status. Minor. Limited. But valid."

The rat stood on its hind legs. It looked at Gerald.

It chattered. A single, sharp sound.

**System Query: Accept Proposal?**

Gerald nodded. "Yes."

The System accepted.

**New Entity Created: Compliance Rat.**

**Role: Structural Auditor.**

**Stats: Intelligence +2. Speed +1. Courage +1.**

The rat scampered to the wall. It sniffed the cracks. It squeaked at the broken stones.

Gerald watched it. He felt a strange sensation in his chest. Not fear. Not anger.

Purpose.

He had a team. A tiny, disgusting, effective team.

He walked to the main door of his dungeon. The library floor above was still closed. The city didn't know what had happened.

But Gerald did.

He had twenty-three hours left.

He needed to expand. He needed to fix the fire code. He needed to find the Maw's core and serve it a final citation.

He opened the door.

He stepped into the hallway.

And froze.

Pendergast was standing there.

Not the librarian. Not the man from the test.

A man in a suit. Sharp. Cold. Holding a clipboard.

He looked at Gerald. He looked at the slime on Gerald's shirt.

"Inspector Hutchins," the man said. His voice was smooth, devoid of warmth. "I am from the Department of Internal Compliance."

Gerald's hand went to his pocket. The key burned against his fingers.

"I haven't finished my report," Gerald said.

"Your report is irrelevant," the man said. He held out a card. It was black.

"Your core has been flagged for irregular activity. I am here to initiate a full audit. Right now."

Gerald looked at the card. Then he looked at the man.

"You cannot audit a core during active enforcement," Gerald said.

"Watch me," the man said.

He raised his hand.

The air in the hallway twisted.

Gerald had never seen a Human Inspector. But he knew this one was not human.

And he was alone.

Gerald tightened his grip on the pen.

He had no idea what rules applied here.

But he had a pen. And he had a rat.

And he had twenty-three hours to die.
