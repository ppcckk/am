Chapter 291: Mandatory Union Rep

# Chapter 291

The red ink hovered millimeters from the parchment.

Gerald watched it. He did not blink. His right hand, still faintly translucent and vibrating with residual mana, hovered near the table's edge. The Inspector General remained frozen, its armored head tilted at an angle that suggested profound bureaucratic confusion.

*SYSTEM ALERT: LABOR STATUS CONFIRMED.*

The stamp lifted.

Gerald exhaled. The sound was dry, like sandpaper rubbing against wood.

A new window snapped into existence before him, bordered in aggressive gold trim. It read: *MANDATORY UNION REPRESENTATION REQUIRED PRIOR TO AUDIT FINALIZATION. APPOINT REPRESENTATIVE OR SUSPEND PROCEEDINGS.*

Gerald blinked. The clock had stopped. The timer reset. A new countdown began, stretching the deadline from ten seconds to three hours.

Three hours to find a union rep, and he looked around the dungeon. The water leak dripped into the bucket. The rat chewed on a piece of drywall. The mold spores drifted lazily near the ceiling vents he had installed last week.

No one was here.

"Excuse me," a voice said.

Gerald turned. The voice came from the main corridor, near the entrance he had reinforced with steel-plated oak doors.

A figure stood in the doorway. It was not a monster.

It was a woman in a sharp gray suit, holding a clipboard.

Her skin was pale, her eyes dark and unblinking. She wore a badge that read *Ashford Local 402: Municipal Workers Union*.

Gerald felt a cold spike of terror in his gut. He had never seen a union rep in the sub-basement. He had never even seen them on the main floor.

"Who are you?" Gerald asked. His voice sounded normal. Human.

"I'm Sandra," she said.

She stepped inside, her heels clicking on the concrete. She did not look at the mold.

She did not look at the water, and she looked at Gerald.

"You filed a grievance against the System. That triggers a mandatory response protocol.

I'm here to represent your interests.

"

Gerald stared at her.

"I didn't file a grievance. I filed a labor dispute claim."

"Same thing," Sandra said. She pulled a chair from the corner—a folding chair he had used for inventory checks—and sat down. "Let's get the paperwork in order. You're claiming employment status?"

"Yes."

"With the City of Ashford?"

"Yes."

Sandra clicked her pen.

"And you're claiming that the Core Registration did not terminate your employment contract."

"I am claiming that no resignation was filed," Gerald corrected.

Sandra nodded slowly, and she wrote something down.

"Understood. Now, the Inspector General is waiting.

You need to present your case. I can't speak for you. You have to argue it.

"

"I can't argue," Gerald said. "I'm the subject."

"No," Sandra said. "You're the witness.

I'm the advocate.

But you have to provide the evidence. Do you have your employee ID?

"

Gerald patted his pockets, and he was wearing his old inspection trousers. In the left pocket, he felt the plastic rectangle.

He pulled it out.

It was still there. A small miracle.

He placed the ID on the table.

Sandra picked it up. She examined it.

"Expired?"

"Active," Gerald said. "I didn't retire. I was transformed."

"Transformation isn't a termination event," Sandra said. She looked at the Inspector General. "Subject maintains active employment status. Evidence attached."

The Inspector General's armor creaked. The red stamp remained raised, but the tension in the air shifted. The System was processing the new input.

*SYSTEM NOTICE: EMPLOYEE STATUS VERIFIED. AUDIT SUSPENDED PENDING REPRESENTATION PROTOCOL.*

Sandra smiled. It was not a warm smile. It was the smile of a bureaucrat who has just found a loophole in someone else's budget.

"Good," she said. "Now, let's discuss the violations."

Gerald frowned.

"Violations?"

"The audit list," Sandra said. She tapped the air, and the audit screen appeared between them.

"You have seventeen citations pending.

Most are minor.

But there's one big one.

"

Gerald looked at the screen. He knew them all. He had written most of them himself.

"Inadequate ventilation," he read. "Missing egress signage. No fire suppression system."

"Those are standard," Sandra said. "The big one is this."

She pointed to a line in bold red text.

*VIOLATION 14: UNAUTHORIZED MODIFICATION OF PUBLIC PROPERTY.*

Gerald looked at the line. He looked at Sandra.

"I didn't modify public property."

"You did," Sandra said. "Your dungeon footprint is beneath the library. The library is city property.

You have expanded your dungeon boundaries into the library's foundation without a permit. That's a felony, Gerald.

Not just a code violation.

"

Gerald felt the blood drain from his face.

"I didn't know."

"Municipal employees are expected to know," Sandra said. "The City Code requires a variance request for any structural change to public infrastructure. Did you file a Form 7-A?"

"No."

"Did you file a Form 7-B?"

"No."

"Then you're liable," Sandra said. She leaned forward. "The Inspector General is going to assess a fine.

A big fine.

Enough to bankrupt your dungeon. It will strip your mana reserves.

You'll be left with nothing.

The rats will die. The mold will dry out. You'll be nothing.

"

Gerald stared at the fine amount. It was astronomical.

*FINE: 500,000 MANA POINTS.*

He had a budget of twelve.

"I need a variance," Gerald said.

"You need a permit," Sandra said. "And you need to stop the expansion. Immediately."

Gerald looked at the walls. They were pulsing slightly. He had been expanding them, slowly, to accommodate the new spawn areas. He had thought it was natural growth.

"The System allows organic expansion," Gerald said.

"The System allows it," Sandra said. "The City does not.

You're in a jurisdictional conflict.

The System wants to let you grow.

The City wants to fine you into oblivion. The Inspector General is caught in the middle.

But the Inspector General answers to the highest authority. And right now, the City has more legal standing than the System, because you're an employee.

"

Gerald felt sick. His stomach churned.

"If I get fined, my Compliance Rating drops," Gerald said.

"Yes."

"And if my Compliance Rating drops, my spawns debuff."

"Yes."

"And if my spawns debuff, they die."

"Yes."

"And if my spawns die, I lose my dungeon."

"Technically, you lose your business," Sandra said. "But effectively, yes. You're done."

Gerald looked at the rat. The rat was eating drywall. It looked happy.

"I can't afford the fine," Gerald said.

"Then you need to prove the expansion was not unauthorized," Sandra said. "You need to prove that the expansion was necessary for public safety."

Gerald paused.

"Public safety?"

"Code 12.4," Sandra said. "Emergency Structural Reinforcement. If a building is at risk of collapse, modifications are permitted without prior permit, provided they are reported within forty-eight hours."

Gerald looked at the library above him. He thought about the foundation.

He had been measuring it for thirty-one years, and he knew that foundation. He knew that the clay soil beneath Ashford was shifting. He knew that the library's east wing was sinking.

He had felt it. When he became a Core, he had felt the library's weight. He had felt the stress fractures in the foundation.

He had expanded his dungeon to push back against the sinking foundation. He had reinforced the load-bearing walls from below.

He had been doing it for weeks.

"I didn't file the report," Gerald said.

"No," Sandra said. "But you can file it now. Retroactive emergency variance. You have to prove the library was at risk."

"How?" Gerald asked.

"Data," Sandra said.

"You're a Core. You have sensors.

You have logs.

Show me the logs.

"

Gerald focused. He pulled up his System log. It was a mess of mana usage and spawn counts. But buried in the data stream was the structural health monitor.

He projected it onto the air.

Sandra looked at the graph. She frowned.

"This looks like random noise."

"It's not," Gerald said. "It's the vibration frequency of the library's foundation.

Look at the spikes.

Every time the heavy trucks go down Main Street, the foundation shakes, and the library is sinking. I've been compensating.

"

Sandra traced the line with her finger.

"You're saying you built buttresses?"

"Dungeon walls," Gerald corrected. "Reinforced concrete and mana-infused stone. They're supporting the foundation from below."

Sandra looked at the Inspector General.

"Subject claims emergency structural reinforcement. Logs attached."

The Inspector General processed the data. The armor rustled.

*SYSTEM NOTICE: DATA ANALYZED. STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY CONFIRMED. VARIANCE REQUEST PROVISIONALLY ACCEPTED.*

Sandra exhaled. She put her pen down.

"Okay," she said. "That buys you time. The fine is suspended.

But you still have to file the paperwork. Properly.

With dates, measurements, and signatures.

"

"I can do that," Gerald said.

"Good," Sandra said. "But there's a problem."

"What problem?"

"The Inspector General isn't done with you," Sandra said. "The audit is still open. And while the fine is suspended, the Compliance Rating is still low.

You're at 47/100.

That's dangerous. At 40, the System starts stripping your spawn rates.

At 30, it strips your mana regeneration.

"

"I'm working on it," Gerald said.

"You're working on it too slowly," Sandra said. She stood up. "You need immediate improvements.

Fire safety. Ventilation.

Accessibility."

"I installed handrails," Gerald said.

"Handrails are not enough," Sandra said. "You need sprinklers. You need exit signs. You need ADA-compliant ramps."

"I have a ramp," Gerald said.

"It's too steep," Sandra said. "Code 14.2. Maximum slope of 1:12. Yours is 1:8."

Gerald winced.

"I thought 1:8 was okay for small drops."

"It's not," Sandra said.

"You have two hours to fix it. Or the audit resumes.

And if the audit resumes, the Inspector General will see the other violations. The ones you haven't cited yet.

"

Gerald felt a chill.

"What other violations?"

"The ones the other Cores have filed against you," Sandra said.

Gerald froze. "Other Cores?"

"Yes," Sandra said. "You've been citing them.

They're filing counter-complaints.

They're alleging harassment. They're alleging unfair competitive practices. They're alleging that you're using municipal authority to gain an unfair advantage in the Dungeon Wars.

"

Gerald looked at the rat. The rat had stopped chewing. It was looking at Gerald.

"Who filed the complaints?" Gerald asked.

"Core 404," Sandra said. "The one next door. The one you cited for fire safety last week."

"He's still there?" Gerald asked.

"He's furious," Sandra said.

"And he's not alone. He's filed a joint complaint with Core 701 and Core 882.

They're pooling their resources.

They're hiring a lawyer.

"

"A lawyer?" Gerald asked.

"A dungeon attorney," Sandra said. "They're expensive.

But they're good, and they know the System's loopholes.

They're arguing that your Citations are invalid because you're not a certified inspector within the System's jurisdiction.

"

Gerald felt the floor tilt.

"I am a certified inspector."

"Municipal," Sandra said. "The System doesn't care about municipal. The System cares about System certification. You don't have one."

"I have a badge," Gerald said.

"A badge is not a license," Sandra said. "You're a hobbyist to them. An amateur. They're going to crush you on procedure."

Gerald looked at the clock. One hour and forty-five minutes left.

"I need to fix the ramp," Gerald said.

"Fix the ramp," Sandra agreed. "And fix the ventilation. And fix the fire suppression. And then you need to appeal the counter-complaints."

"On what grounds?"

"Procedural error," Sandra said. "The complainants did not serve you with proper notice of the joint complaint. Under System Rule 9, joint complaints require individual service.

They served you as a single entity. But you are an employee.

You are a person.

You have rights.

"

Gerald nodded.

"I can argue that."

"You have to argue it," Sandra said. "And you have to do it fast. The Hearing is scheduled for tomorrow."

"Tomorrow?" Gerald asked.

"Yes," Sandra said.

"Core 404 requested an expedited hearing. The System granted it.

You have one hour to prepare your defense.

Then you have to appear in the Court of Appeals.

"

Gerald felt his hands shake. He clenched them.

"I don't know how to argue in court," Gerald said.

"You know how to argue code," Sandra said. "That's what courts are. Code. You'll be fine."

She turned to leave.

"Wait," Gerald said. "What about the fine?"

"It's suspended," Sandra said. "For now. But if you lose the Hearing, the fine returns. And it's doubled."

Gerald looked at the clock. One hour and forty-four minutes.

He looked at the ramp. It was too steep.

He looked at the ventilation. It was clogged.

He looked at the fire suppression. There was none.

He looked at the rat.

"Hey," Gerald said.

The rat squeaked.

"Do you know how to mix concrete?"

The rat tilted its head.

"Good," Gerald said. "Start mixing."

Sandra opened the door. She paused.

"Gerald," she said.

"Yes?"

"If you win this Hearing, the City is going to want a word with you."

Gerald felt a new kind of fear. Not fear of the System. Fear of the HR department.

"Why?" he asked.

"Because you just used your job title to fight a god," Sandra said. "And the City doesn't like employees who think they're independent."

She closed the door.

Gerald stood alone in the silence. The clock ticked.

He looked at the ramp. He looked at the tools. He looked at the rat, which was now dragging a bucket of cement toward him.

He picked up a trowel, and he had one hour.

He had a lawsuit.

He had a god trying to crush him.

And he had a rat who could mix concrete.

Gerald took a breath. He squared his shoulders.

He walked to the ramp.

"Let's cite this," he said.

He raised the trowel. He looked at the steep slope.

"Violation 14.2," he muttered. "Improper gradient."

He swung the trowel. He chipped away at the stone.

But as he worked, he heard a sound. Not from the corridor. From the walls.

A scratching.

Not a rat.

Something bigger.

The walls of his dungeon were bleeding. Black, viscous fluid seeped from the cracks. The Compliance Rating on his HUD flickered.

*WARNING: UNAUTHORIZED BIOLOGICAL INFILTRATION DETECTED.*

Gerald stopped. He looked at the wall.

The scratching grew louder.

It wasn't a monster breaking in, which was his own dungeon.

His dungeon was rebelling.

The mold spores were swirling into a shape. The water leak was forming a face.

The rat stopped mixing. It looked at Gerald. It looked at the wall.

The rat squeaked. A high, piercing sound.

*"It's waking up,"* the rat seemed to say.

Gerald dropped the trowel.

The wall bulged.

And then, the door to the dungeon exploded inward.

Not from the outside.

From the inside.

Core 404 stood in the doorway. But it wasn't alone.

He was holding a sword made of pure, black void-energy. And behind him, standing in the shadows, was Sandra.

She was smiling.

But this time, the smile was not bureaucratic, which was hungry.

"Surprise," Sandra said. "I work for them."
