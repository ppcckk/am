Chapter 329: The Missing Stamp

# Chapter 329

The ink was still wet. It smeared under the pad of Kael's thumb, a blue-black smear that looked obscenely bright against the crumbling concrete of the ceiling.

Above them, the smoke figure of the Inspector General hovered, its form coalescing from the dust motes and the sheer, crushing weight of administrative judgment.

The ceiling groaned, a sound like tectonic plates shifting in the basement, but the crack stopped spreading. It hung there, frozen in a jagged line of gray plaster, waiting for a ruling.

Kael stared at the paper. His hand trembled, not from fear, but from the sheer absurdity of holding a fountain pen while a god of bureaucracy watched.

"I cannot," Kael whispered. "I don't have the stamp."

Gerald did not blink. He reached into the pocket of his vest, a vest that had materialized from the dungeon's core code, and pulled out a small, rubber-stamped seal. It was dull, gray, and smelled of old office carpet.

"You do not need the stamp," Gerald said.

"You need the signature. The stamp is for the record.

The signature is for the authority.

Sign it."

The smoke figure drifted closer. Its face was a blur of shifting documents, a mosaic of rejected applications and denied appeals. A voice emerged, not from a mouth, but from the vibration of the air itself.

*"Unauthorized variance request. Procedural violation. Citing for lack of proper seal."*

The air in the room grew thick. Kael's breathing became labored. The System was tightening the screws, squeezing the dungeon's structural integrity until it popped.

Gerald stepped forward. He placed the gray rubber seal on the floor. He picked up the fountain pen from Kael's hand, prying it from the adventurer's grip.

*"Improper handling of municipal instruments,"* the voice droned. *"Additional citation pending."*

Gerald ignored it. He looked at the ceiling crack. He looked at the smoke.

"Section 12, Subsection C," Gerald said, his voice flat. "In the absence of a primary seal, a secondary seal may be applied by the acting manager, provided the variance is for the immediate preservation of public infrastructure."

He pressed the pen to the paper. He wrote *Hutchins*. Then he pressed the rubber seal down. *Thump.*

The smoke figure halted. The vibration in the air ceased.

*"Processing variance,"* the voice said. It sounded annoyed. *"Checking Guild Charter. Checking Municipal Contract. Checking Employee Status."*

Kael rubbed his hand, looking sick.

"What did you just do?"

"I filed a retroactive permit for your presence," Gerald said, smoothing the paper. "You are now a temporary structural consultant for the City of Ashford. You are part of the operational infrastructure. The Inspector General cannot shut down active municipal operations without a prior 90-day notice and a public hearing."

*"Hearing scheduled for immediate review,"* the System announced.

The smoke figure did not leave. Instead, it expanded. The single crack in the ceiling widened, splitting into a network of fractures that spread across the dungeon floor. The walls peeled away, revealing not stone, but layers of gray tape and red warning flags.

*"Audit Initiated. Compliance Check: Structural Integrity.

Ventilation.

Fire Suppression.

Zoning."*

Gerald's stomach turned over. It was a physical sensation, a lurch of nausea that had nothing to do with the impending collapse and everything to do with the word *Audit*. An audit meant they were looking at everything.

Every corner. Every pipe.

Every spore.

"Kael," Gerald said, turning his back on the smoke. "Do not breathe deeply. The air filtration system is currently under review."

"Under review?" Kael choked out.

"It means we do not have a permit to exhale."

Gerald turned to his dungeon. It was no longer a place. It was a case file.

**System Notice: Audit Phase 1. Structural Review.**

A panel appeared in Gerald's vision. It was not a combat interface. It was a spreadsheet.

Columns labeled *Load Bearing*, *Egress Width*, *Material Durability*.

Rows populated with his dungeon's current stats. They were red.

All of them.

His dungeon had been built in a hurry, expanded through necessity, patched with code loopholes. Now, the System was reading the fine print of every patch.

*Violation: Corridor 4B exceeds maximum deflection limits by 3%. Penalty: -15% to all physical defense stats for all spawned entities.*

Gerald felt the dungeon's walls shudder. Outside, in the upper levels, he could hear the screams of the mold spores as they were crushed by their own weakened mass. The rats were dying. The water leak was becoming a flood, the pressure unregulated.

He needed to fix it. He needed to pass the audit. But he didn't have the materials. He didn't have the time.

He had the forms.

Gerald walked to the center of the room. He closed his eyes. He reached into the System menu, not for the *Spawn* tab, but for the *Documentation* tab.

It was a hidden tab, accessible only to Cores with a Compliance Rating above 50, or those who had filed a specific number of appeals. Gerald had filed four hundred and twelve appeals.

The tab was open, and he selected *Form 77-Z: Temporary Structural Reinforcement via Administrative Overlap.*

It was a loophole. It allowed a Core to claim that a non-structural element was, in fact, structural, provided it was documented as such prior to the audit. Gerald had documented the library's support pillars as part of his dungeon's perimeter three years ago, during a dispute with the city council. He had argued they were load-bearing for the *dungeon*, not just the library.

He selected the pillars. He clicked *Apply*.

*Warning: False declaration. Penalty: +20% Audit Severity.*

Gerald clicked *Accept*.

The penalty hit him like a hammer.

The ceiling groaned again, louder this time. But the walls stopped shaking.

The deflection dropped. The red text turned yellow.

*Structural Integrity: Pass (Conditional).*

The smoke figure drifted toward the next room. The ventilation shaft.

*Audit Phase 2. Air Quality Review.*

Gerald followed. He had to stay with the auditor. If he left the audit zone, the connection would break, and the penalty would become permanent.

The ventilation shafts were his weak point. He had installed them to meet minimum code, which meant they were barely adequate. The System now checked for *efficiency* and *maintenance logs*.

*Violation: Filter Change Log Missing. Penalty: -50% to All Regen.*

Regen was the lifeblood of his dungeon. Without it, his spores would dry out and die. His dungeon would become a tomb.

Gerald ran to the ventilation hub. It was a small, cramped closet in the corner of the dungeon, filled with dusty fans and duct tape. He grabbed a stack of papers from his inventory. They were blank.

He wrote, and he wrote dates. He wrote times.

He wrote *Filter Changed: 14:02 PM*.

He wrote *Filter Checked: 09:15 AM*. He wrote *Duct Cleaned: Last Century*.

He was forging records. It was illegal. It was a crime in the municipal world. It was a capital offense in the Dungeon Core world.

But the System did not care about the truth. It cared about the *record*.

He pasted the forged logs onto the ventilation unit. The System scanned them.

*Log Verified. Compliance: 100%.*

*Penalty Removed.*

Gerald gasped for air. His hands were shaking. He had just committed fraud against a god.

The smoke figure moved to the fire suppression system.

*Audit Phase 3. Fire Safety.*

This was the hard one.

Gerald's dungeon was underground. It had no sprinklers.

It had no fire exits, which was a fire trap.

The System highlighted the violations.

*Violation: No Sprinkler System. Penalty: -100% to Fire Resistance.*

If the dragon above attacked, or if a fire elemental spawned nearby, Gerald would burn instantly.

*Violation: Egress Route Obstructed. Penalty: -100% to Speed.*

His dungeon was a maze. If an adventurer got trapped, they would be stuck. If Gerald himself got trapped, he would be stuck.

Gerald looked at the walls. He looked at the dragon's shadow above.

He had no water. He had no pipes. He had no doors.

But he had Kael.

"Kael," Gerald said. "Take off your armor."

"What?"

"Your armor. It is metal. It is reflective. It is a fireproof barrier."

"I need it to survive!"

"You are a municipal worker," Gerald snapped. "You are wearing protective gear. Where is your safety inspection tag?"

Kael scrambled to his belt. He pulled out a small, laminated card. The Adventurer's Guild Safety Badge.

Gerald grabbed it. He walked to the nearest wall. He pinned the badge to the stone.

*System Prompt: Fire Protection Device Detected.*

Gerald looked at the smoke figure.

"I claim that this device," Gerald said, pointing at the badge, "is part of the fire suppression system. It is a passive thermal barrier. It reflects radiant heat. It meets the minimum requirement for Class B fire resistance in a sub-basement environment."

The smoke figure hovered. It scanned the badge. It scanned the dungeon.

*Calculation: Badge Coverage Area: 0.04 square feet.* *Requirement: 4,000 square feet.* *Compliance: 0.001%.*

*Penalty: -99% Fire Resistance.*

Gerald felt the heat rise. The dragon's breath was leaking through the ceiling. The air was turning into an oven.

It wasn't enough, and he needed more coverage. He needed the whole dungeon to be covered in badges.

He had three adventurers in the dungeon. Two were dead. One was Kael.

He had ten rats. They had no badges.

He had mold. Mold had no badges.

Gerald looked at the water leak. The water was pooling on the floor. It was rising.

*Violation: Water Damage to Electrical Systems. Penalty: -50% Lightning Resistance.*

The dragon's lightning breath was coming next. Gerald would be electrocuted before he could even think.

He was failing. The audit was tearing his dungeon apart, layer by layer.

He looked at the smoke figure. It was waiting for the final penalty. The Cumulative Failure.

*System Alert: Audit Failed. Core Demotion Imminent. Dungeon Reversion to Raw Mana.*

If he was demoted, he would cease to exist. He would become a pool of energy. The library would collapse. The city would burn.

Gerald's mind raced. He needed a wildcard. He needed a violation so severe, so absurd, that the System would pause to process it. A violation that required an appeal.

He looked at the Inspector General. He looked at the dragon. He looked at the library above.

He opened his menu. He went to the *Appeals* tab.

He didn't file an appeal for the fire. He didn't file an appeal for the water.

He filed an appeal against the *Inspector General*.

*Form 99-A: Jurisdictional Challenge.*

*Claim: The Inspector General is currently located within a municipal building (Ashford Library) during business hours. The Inspector General has not filed a visitor's pass. The Inspector General has not undergone a security check. The Inspector General is conducting an audit on public property without a warrant.*

*Requested Action: Suspend Audit. Issue Citation for Trespassing and Unauthorized Inspection.*

Gerald clicked *Submit*.

The System froze.

The smoke figure stopped. Its face shifted, the documents rearranging themselves into a single, stark image: A stop sign.

*Processing Jurisdictional Challenge.*

*Scanning for Warrant.*

*Scanning for Visitor Pass.*

*Scanning for Business Hours.*

The clock on the dungeon wall ticked. It was 2:14 PM. The library was open.

*Warrant: Not Found.* *Visitor Pass: Not Found.* *Business Hours: Active.*

*Violation: Unauthorized Presence in Public Infrastructure.*

The smoke figure shrank. It became smaller, less imposing. The crack in the ceiling stopped growing. The heat receded.

*Audit Suspended. Pending Hearing.*

Gerald slumped against the wall. His heart hammered against his ribs.

He had bought himself time.

A few minutes.

Maybe less.

But the dragon was still up there. And the Inspector General was still in the room, now citing *Gerald* for filing a frivolous appeal.

*Warning: Frivolous Filing. Penalty: -50% to All Stats. Duration: Until Hearing Concludes.*

Gerald was weak. His dungeon was weak. The dragon was coming down.

Kael walked over. He looked pale.

"Did we win?"

"We bought time," Gerald said. "We have to go to the hearing."

"To the hearing?" Kael asked. "Where?"

Gerald pointed at the floor.

"To the System's Courtroom. It's under the library's main branch. The records room."

Kael looked at the floor.

"There's no door."

"There is," Gerald said. "But we have to break the law to get in."

He pulled out his pen. He drew a door in the air.

*System Prompt: Unauthorized Portal Creation. Citation Incoming.*

Gerald stepped through the drawn door. Kael hesitated, then jumped after him.

They landed in a room that looked like a library basement, but darker. The shelves were filled not with books, but with jars of preserved failures. Screaming faces in amber. Shattered cores in glass.

In the center of the room sat a desk. Behind the desk sat a figure made of pure light. It was not the Inspector General. It was the Judge.

And on the desk, in front of the Judge, sat a file, and the file was labeled: *Hutchins, Gerald. Core ID: 8942. Status: Under Review.*

Gerald walked to the desk. He placed his hand on the file.

"I am here to appeal," Gerald said.

The Judge looked up. Its eyes were blank white orbs.

"You are not here to appeal," the Judge said. "You are here to be sentenced."

From the shadows behind the Judge, a figure stepped forward. It was small, wiry, and wearing a suit made of shadows. It held a clipboard.

"Mr. Hutchins," the figure said. Its voice was smooth, oily.

"I am your prosecutor. And I have a new charge for you."

The figure opened the clipboard.

"Charge: Fraudulent Permitting. Charge: Obstruction of Justice. Charge: Endangerment of Public Infrastructure."

Gerald looked at the charge list. It was endless.

"But here is the interesting part," the prosecutor said, smiling. "You are not the only one on trial."

The prosecutor gestured to the door.

The door opened.

Walking in was not an adventurer. It was not a monster.

It was a woman. She wore a suit. She held a stamp. She looked exactly like Gerald.

"Meet your replacement," the prosecutor said. "The City of Ashford has filed a motion to replace you. This core has passed the municipal bar exam. And she has a permit for everything."

Gerald stared at the woman. She smiled. It was a perfect, rule-abiding smile.

"I have your file, Gerald," she said. "And I have your violations. All of them."

The Judge raised a gavel.

"Hearing in session," the Judge boomed.

Gerald looked at Kael. Kael looked at the woman.

"You have to sign the permit," Gerald whispered to Kael. "Not for me. For her."

Kael froze.

The Judge slammed the gavel.

"Guilty."
