Chapter 280: Gavel of the City

# Chapter 280

The gavel sound was not a sound at all. It was a System notification chime, pitched to the frequency of a wooden mallet striking a block of oak.

Gerald Hutchins wiped slurry from his chin. His left eye was swollen shut. The left side of his dungeon—the eastern quadrant, housing the primary ventilation shafts—had collapsed into a sinkhole of raw mana and wet concrete. He stood in the center of what remained, ankle-deep in runoff that smelled of ozone and old library paper.

*TARGET: CITY OF ASHFORD.* *STATUS: UNDER REVIEW.* *VIOLATIONS DETECTED: 14.* *SEVERITY: CRITICAL.*

The text hovered in his vision, translucent and red. It burned. Not metaphorically. The red light physically warmed the air around his core.

He looked up. The hole in the ceiling was still there, but the rain had stopped. Or rather, it had frozen mid-air, suspended in droplets the size of golf balls. Time had slowed to accommodate the audit.

Gerald tried to move. His right leg buckled.

The structural integrity of his own body was at twelve percent.

He had overextended. The citation had worked, technically. He had served the City.

But the City was not a building, which was a sprawling, unpermitted sprawl of infrastructure that resisted definition.

A hand grabbed his collar.

Gerald flinched, expecting claws. Expecting the Auditor.

It was a claw, technically. But it was clean. Polished. Wrapped in a silk sash.

The creature before him was four feet tall, covered in scales that shifted color from emerald to midnight blue. It wore a monocle over its left eye and a vest made of woven spider silk. It held a tiny, leather-bound notebook.

"You," the creature said, its voice like gravel grinding in a blender. "You are out of order."

Gerald blinked. The water in his lungs burned.

"State your name," Gerald rasped. "And your permit."

The creature tilted its head. It did not answer the permit question. It answered the name question.

"Lord Vexis.

Core of the Obsidian Spire. Adjacent jurisdiction.

" Vexis tapped a claw against Gerald's chest, right over the core. "You just cited the Municipal Government.

Do you have any idea what you've done?

"

"I cited the City for failing to maintain public easements within two hundred meters of a registered dungeon core," Gerald said.

It was a lie, and the citation had been vague, desperate, and likely invalid. But the System had accepted it.

That was all that mattered.

Vexis laughed. It was a dry, rattling sound.

"The City is not a dungeon.

You cannot cite a non-dungeon entity. That is a fundamental axiom. You have triggered a Paradox Error.

"

*ERROR: PARADOX DETECTED. RESOLUTION REQUIRED.*

The text in Gerald's vision flickered. The red turned to a pulsing purple.

"If the System cannot process the citation," Vexis continued, circling Gerald with predatory grace, "it will default to the nearest valid target. And since you are the source of the citation..."

Vexis opened its notebook. A page turned by itself.

"The System will reclassify you. Not as a Core. But as the violation itself."

Gerald felt the floor drop away. The water rose. The walls groaned, a sound of tearing metal and snapping timber.

*RECLASSIFYING... 10%... 20%...*

Gerald reached for his clipboard, which was gone. Dissolved in the expansion.

He reached for his pen.

Gone.

He had no tools. He had no paperwork. He had only his knowledge.

And his anger.

"Stop," Gerald said.

Vexis paused. "Or?"

"Or I will cite you."

Vexis sneered.

"In your current state?

You are drowning.

Your mana reserves are negative.

You cannot even spawn a rat, let alone serve a Citation to a Tier 4 Core.

"

"I don't need to spawn anything," Gerald said. He stood up. The water was at his waist.

He pushed against the sinking sensation, fighting the buoyancy of his own failing structure.

"I just need to see you."

Vexis stepped back, wary.

"Why?"

Gerald pointed a shaking finger at the creature's feet.

"Your left claw," Gerald said.

Vexis looked down.

"What about it?"

"It's not touching the ground."

The creature froze. Its right claw was planted on the stone floor of Gerald's dungeon. Its left claw was hovering, two inches above the surface, balanced on the tip of its toe.

Gerald's eyes narrowed. He didn't look at Vexis's face. He looked at the gap between claw and stone.

*SYSTEM PROMPT: OBSERVE STRUCTURAL ANCHORAGE.*

Gerald focused. He didn't cast a spell. He didn't attack. He inspected.

In the System, every entity had a footprint.

A point of contact with reality. For most cores, this was the core itself. For spawn entities, it was their feet, or roots, or wheels.

If an entity was not anchored, it was transient.

Ephemeral.

Unless it was cheating.

Vexis was using a levitation enchantment to avoid disturbing the floor, likely to preserve the aesthetic of his own dungeon's entryway, which had bled into Gerald's space during the boundary expansion. He was standing in Gerald's dungeon, but he was not *in* Gerald's dungeon. He was hovering above it.

"You are trespassing on a private easement," Gerald said. His voice was steady. Deadpan. "And you are failing to maintain proper ground-level contact for load-bearing stability in a Zone 3 seismic area."

Vexis's monocle flashed.

"That is a nonsense interpretation."

"Section 4, Paragraph C of the Ashford Municipal Code," Gerald recited.

He didn't look at Vexis. He looked at the System interface, forcing his will into the audit window.

"Entities occupying public or semi-public thoroughfares must demonstrate full weight distribution to prevent tripping hazards.

You have not done so.

"

*ANALYZING...*

The purple text halted. The reclassification stopped at 45%.

Vexis's scales bristled.

"You are trying to use a building code against a magical entity."

"I am using the only code that matters here," Gerald said.

"The System's code.

You are not anchored. Therefore, you are not present.

Therefore, you cannot appeal a Citation, because you are not a valid target for service.

"

Vexis snarled.

"I am right here."

"Prove it," Gerald said. "Touch the floor.

Fully.

With all four limbs. Or accept the Citation for Non-Compliant Egress Stability.

"

Vexis hesitated.

The arrogance of the Tier 4 Core warred with the logic of the System. If he touched the floor, he became a valid target.

If he didn't, he was a ghost.

A glitch.

The System hated glitches.

*VIOLATION CONFIRMED: NON-ANCHORED ENTITY.* *CITATION ISSUED.*

A red stamp materialized in the air. It slammed into Vexis's chest.

The creature shrieked. The spider-silk vest unraveled. The scales lost their sheen, turning a dull, sickly gray.

*DEBUFF APPLIED: STABILITY REDUCTION - 90%.*

Vexis's legs buckled. He crashed onto the wet stone, his claws skidding, unable to find purchase.

He scrambled, but the System was now enforcing the physics of his violation.

He was slipping, and he was weak.

Gerald didn't gloat. He didn't celebrate. He walked—slowly, painfully—toward the fallen Core.

"Get up," Gerald said.

Vexis glared at him from the puddle.

"You cheated."

"I inspected," Gerald corrected. He pulled a piece of wet, crumpled paper from his pocket. It was a receipt from the library gift shop.

Useless. He tore it in half anyway.

"I need a witness," Gerald said. "For the City Council."

Vexis tried to summon a spell. A bolt of dark energy crackled in his palm, but it fizzled out, destabilized by the debuff.

"I will kill you."

"You will try," Gerald said.

He looked up at the frozen rain.

"But you are currently cited. Any aggressive action taken while under Citation status constitutes Obstruction of Regulatory Justice.

That adds a second violation.

Do you want the penalty multiplier?

"

Vexis went still, and the System was already calculating the cost. Gerald could feel it—a pull in his own mana, a hunger for the Core's essence. If Vexis attacked, Gerald could drain him.

Not with magic. With bureaucracy.

The rain fell again. Time resumed.

The ceiling collapsed.

Not the whole thing.

Just a section.

A chunk of concrete and rebar fell toward Gerald. He didn't dodge.

He raised his hand.

*STRUCTURAL CODE COMPLIANCE: ACTIVE.* *PASSIVE RESISTANCE: 15%.*

The concrete hit his raised arm. It shattered. Shards of drywall and stone bounced off his skin, which glowed with a faint, geometric lattice of light. The impact staggered him, but it didn't kill him.

He had built that resistance. He had spent three hundred hours measuring load-bearing walls in his own dungeon. He had reinforced the beams with mana-infused steel. He had filed the permits.

Now the permits were saving his life.

Vexis scrambled to his feet, slipping and sliding.

"This isn't over.

The Inspector General is coming.

I have allies. The Council has allies.

"

"Good," Gerald said. He coughed, spitting blood and dust. "I'll need their names for the witness list."

He turned away from the Core. He walked toward the remaining intact section of his dungeon—the eastern office. It was the only part left that still had a door.

Behind him, Vexis screamed in frustration, the sound echoing off the broken walls.

But the creature didn't follow. It couldn't.

The debuff was too severe.

It was stuck in the mud, trying to re-anchor itself, trying to understand how a 47-rated compliance score had just dismantled a Tier 4 powerhouse.

Gerald reached the door. He pushed it open.

Inside, the room was flooded. The desk was floating. The filing cabinets had burst open, spilling papers into the water.

Gerald waded in. He grabbed a dry file from the top shelf of a cabinet that had miraculously stayed upright.

*File: Ashford City Council Agenda - Next Meeting.*

He opened it. The paper was soggy, but the ink held.

*Item 4: Budget Allocation for Dungeon Defense Initiative.* *Proposed by: Mayor Halloway.* *Sponsor: Lord Vexis (Consultant).*

Gerald read the line twice. Vexis wasn't just a neighbor.

He was on the payroll, and the City had hired him.

They had invited him in.

Which meant the City was not just negligent. It was complicit.

Gerald's heart hammered against his ribs. This changed everything. A citation for neglect was a civil matter. A citation for hiring an unlicensed, code-violating entity to attack a registered municipal asset was a felony.

Under the System's laws, felonies were punishable by total dungeon deletion.

But the City had legal immunity. Unless...

Unless Gerald could prove the Council knew. He needed a meeting. He needed to get into the City Hall.

He looked at his reflection in the shattered mirror on the wall. His face was gray. His eyes were hollow. He looked like a ghost.

But a ghost with a citation, and a knock came from the doorframe. Not a physical knock. A mental ping.

*Gerald Hutchins.*

It was the Auditor. She wasn't in the room. She was in his mind.

*The Council meeting is in two hours.*

Gerald gripped the wet file. His knuckles turned white.

"I can't walk there," he said aloud. "My dungeon is gone. I have no legs. I have no mana."

*You don't need legs,* the Auditor's voice echoed. *You need a vehicle.*

Gerald looked around the flooded room. There was a boat. A small, plastic model boat that a child had left on the desk years ago. It was useless.

*Not that boat,* the Auditor said. *The other one.*

Gerald frowned. He looked down at the water.

Beneath the surface, glowing faintly, was the carcass of the first goblin he had ever cited. It had dissolved into the floorboards, but the System remembered it. It was still part of his dungeon.

*The goblin was cited for trespassing on public easement.*

*it was ejected.*

*But it was not destroyed.*

Gerald's eyes widened, and he reached into the water. His hand passed through the sludge and found cold, hard scales.

He pulled.

The goblin rose. It was bloated, gray, and lifeless. Its eyes were closed. But it was holding onto something.

A keycard.

The keycard to the City Hall maintenance entrance. The one the goblin had stolen when it tried to scout Gerald's dungeon, months ago. Gerald had forgotten it existed. He had been so focused on the egress signage that he hadn't noticed the theft.

The goblin hadn't kept it. He had dropped it in the drain.

Gerald stared at the keycard. It was rusted. It was broken.

*ITEM: MAINTENANCE KEYCARD (DAMAGED).* *FUNCTION: ACCESS TO SUB-LEVEL 1.*

It was garbage. It wouldn't open the main doors. But it would open the service elevator. The one that led directly to the Council chambers.

Gerald pulled the goblin's hand. The flesh tore away, leaving the keycard in his grip.

He held it up to the light.

If he went to the meeting, he would be seen. He would be exposed. He was a monster in the eyes of the Council.

They would send the Adventurers. The Paladins.

The Mages.

But if he went to the meeting as a citizen, with a citation in hand and a witness who had been cited for violating the code the City sponsored...

He looked at the Auditor's faint shimmer in the air.

"If I go," Gerald said. "The City will declare me hostile."

*Yes,* the Auditor said. *But you will have standing.*

*Standing is all you need to file a grievance.*

Gerald pocketed the keycard. He turned his back on the drowning dungeon. He looked at the hole in the ceiling, where the rain was still falling, washing away the evidence of his own destruction.

He didn't feel hope. Hope was inefficient. He felt the cold, hard weight of duty.

He had a violation to prove, and he had a meeting to attend.

And he had no time left.

Gerald stepped into the dark water. He didn't swim. He sank. He let the current take him, toward the drains, toward the pipes, toward the City beneath the City.

The last thing he saw before the darkness took him was the System text, flashing in time with his heartbeat.

*COUNTDOWN TO HEARING: 01:59:59.*

The gavel banged again.
