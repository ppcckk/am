Chapter 90: Scheduled Audit

# Chapter 90

Gerald was already moving when the rat died.

He felt the spawn's tiny life wink out like a bulb burning through its filament, and he did not mourn it. He had three seconds of grief at most, and he spent them calculating.

His Compliance Rating sat at 84.

One point below the threshold. The demolition timer read twenty-eight hours and forty-seven minutes. The Acting Inspector's debuff ticked at 8%, seventy-two hours remaining, which meant Gerald had roughly forty-three hours before the Inspector recovered and could return to full capacity.

He had twenty-eight hours. The math did not work. Not directly.

Gerald pulled up the citation he had filed against the Acting Inspector, and the finding was clean. Section 14.2, subsection C, load path interruption.

The Inspector had certified the ceiling plate as structurally unsound.

That was a finding of fact, entered into the System's permanent record, timestamped, irreversible.

Gerald read it again. Then he read it a third time.

Then he pulled up Section 9.

Section 9 was the remediation chapter, and it was the chapter that governed what happened when a deficiency was formally noticed.

Gerald had read it forty times over the past sixty chapters.

He had annotated it, and he had cross-referenced it with Sections 3, 7, 11, and 14. He knew it as well as he knew the layout of his own home.

Section 9.4. Remediation Grace Period.

When a deficiency is formally noticed by an authorized inspector, the affected structure enters a mandatory remediation window of seventy-two hours. During this window, no enforcement action, including demolition, may proceed against the noticed condition. The window exists to allow the responsible party an opportunity to cure the deficiency before punitive measures are taken.

Gerald read it four times, and the Acting Inspector had just made a formal finding. A certified deficiency. On Gerald's ceiling.

The finding was in the record. It was timestamped.

It was authorized.

Gerald's ceiling was now a noticed deficiency.

Which meant Gerald had seventy-two hours of remediation grace.

The demolition timer froze.

Gerald watched the numbers stop. Twenty-eight hours and forty-four minutes. Then forty-three.

Then they stopped.

The System's interface pulsed once, twice, and the timer converted to a different format; it now read REMEDIATION WINDOW: 71:59:47.

Gerald exhaled.

His spawn list was empty. The water leak was still running, still costing him points. His rating was 84.

He was still one point short. But he had seventy-two hours instead of twenty-eight.

And he had a noticed deficiency that he could cure, and he could cure the ceiling.

He had already reinforced it. The steel plate was in place.

The load path was shifted. The citation was still active against the Inspector. If Gerald could get the System to accept that the noticed deficiency had been cured, the grace period would end, but the demolition order would also be moot.

The condition that triggered it would no longer exist, which was clean. It was procedural. It was exactly the kind of thing Gerald had done three hundred times in thirty-one years of municipal work. You get cited, you fix the thing, you request re-inspection, you close the case.

Gerald pulled up the cure request form. He filled it out. He attached the citation documentation as evidence of the cure. He submitted it.

The System accepted the filing. A new notification appeared.

RE-INSPECTION SCHEDULED: 48 HOURS.

Forty-eight hours.

Not seventy-two. The System had compressed the window because Gerald had already completed the remediation. That was fine.

Forty-eight hours was more than enough.

Gerald allowed himself one full breath. Then he went back to work on his rating.

He needed one point. One point to get back above 85.

He had been at 85 for eighty-nine days, and the streak was broken, yes, but he still needed to be above the threshold to survive the re-inspection. If the Inspector came back and found Gerald at 84 again, the demolition would resume.

Gerald catalogued his violations. Ventilation: compliant.

Fire suppression: compliant.

Egress signage: compliant.

Handrails: compliant. The water leak was the problem.

It was dragging his structural integrity score down by two points. If he could seal it, he'd gain back enough to hit 86, maybe 87.

He found the leak in the northeast corner; a crack in the foundation, three millimeters wide, seeping groundwater into his floor slab.

He had known about it for weeks.

He had not fixed it because fixing it required him to excavate the surrounding area, which would temporarily reduce his floor area, which would temporarily reduce his rating. It was a net loss in the short term for a net gain in the long term. Gerald had always preferred the long term.

Not today, and he excavated.

He sealed the crack with compacted gravel and a waterproof membrane he pulled from his inventory. He backfilled.

He compacted.

The water stopped.

His rating ticked up. 84. 85. 86, and he was above threshold. The streak was still broken, but he was alive.

He had forty-eight hours until re-inspection.

He had a cure on record.

He had a citation against the Acting Inspector that would expire in roughly seventy hours, which meant the Inspector would be weakened for the entire duration of the re-inspection.

Gerald sat in the dark of his sub-basement and felt, for the first time in six chapters, something close to confidence.

Then the notification came.

CLAIM FILED: GERALD HUTCHINS DUNGEON FOOTPRINT.

Gerald stared at it. He opened the claim. The filing was from a Core he did not recognize.

The name read: THRESHOLD OF HOLLOW KINGS. The claim was filed under Section 22.1, Abandonment and Condemnation.

Section 22.1 allowed any Core to file a claim on a dungeon footprint if the current owner's Compliance Rating had fallen below the threshold for more than twelve consecutive hours. Gerald's rating had been below 85 for approximately two hours. The claim should not have been valid.

Gerald read the filing again, and the claimant had attached a timestamp showing Gerald's rating had dropped below 85 at the moment of the appeal denial. That was correct. But the claim had been filed within minutes.

The twelve-hour window had not elapsed.

Gerald pulled up the claim's metadata. The filing was valid. The System had accepted it. Which meant either the claimant had inside knowledge of Gerald's rating drop before it happened, or the claimant had filed during the exact window when Gerald's rating was technically below threshold but before the remediation grace period had been invoked.

The timing was surgical. Someone had been watching. Someone had known the appeal would fail, had known Gerald's rating would drop, and had filed within the narrow gap between the drop and the grace period activation.

Gerald pulled up the claimant's profile, and the System gave him a name, a tier rating, and a dungeon footprint size.

Threshold of Hollow Kings was a Tier 3 Core.

Its dungeon occupied roughly twelve thousand square feet, which was three times Gerald's footprint, and it was located in the adjacent district, roughly two hundred meters from Gerald's northeast corner.

Gerald had never cited it. He had never interacted with it. He had not even known it existed.

He checked the claim's status. It was pending.

The System would adjudicate in twenty-four hours.

If the claim was accepted, Gerald's footprint would be transferred to Threshold of Hollow Kings.

His dungeon would be dissolved. His spawns, his compliance work, his entire sixty-chapter project, would be absorbed into a Core he had never heard of.

Gerald ran the numbers. Twenty-four hours until adjudication. Forty-eight hours until re-inspection.

The claim would be decided before the re-inspection. If the claim was accepted, there would be no re-inspection.

There would be no Gerald, and he pulled up Section 22.1 in full. He read it line by line. The abandonment threshold was clear.

Twelve hours below the Compliance Rating threshold.

Gerald had been below for two hours. The claim was procedurally invalid.

But the System had accepted the filing; that meant the System had either made an error or had a different interpretation. Gerald read the acceptance notice again. It cited a provision he had not previously encountered.

Section 22.1, subsection F. Expedited Condemnation. If a Core files a claim within one hour of a documented Compliance Rating drop below threshold, the twelve-hour waiting period is waived. The claim is accepted for immediate adjudication.

Gerald felt cold.

Subsection F. He had never seen it. He had read Section 22.1 forty times. It had not been there before.

He pulled up the version history of the code.

The System displayed a changelog. Subsection F had been added at the exact timestamp of the appeal denial. The same moment Gerald's rating dropped.

The same moment the Acting Inspector rendered its decision, and the Acting Inspector had added the subsection.

Gerald sat still.

The Acting Inspector was an inspector.

It was a regulatory body. It could amend the code.

It had amended the code, in real time, to create a tool that would allow a rival to claim Gerald's dungeon. The Acting Inspector had not demolished Gerald directly. It had created the conditions for someone else to do it.

That was not hostile. That was an inspector.

Gerald pulled up the claim again. Twenty-three hours and fifty-one minutes until adjudication. He needed to invalidate the claim, and needed to find a procedural flaw in the filing itself, not in the code.

He read the claim document line by line. The claimant's name was registered.

The footprint coordinates were correct, and the filing fee was paid. The timestamp was accurate. The subsection F citation was correct.

Everything was clean.

Everything except one thing.

Gerald checked the claimant's Compliance Rating.

The System displayed it. Threshold of Hollow Kings: 91.

Above threshold.

No active citations. No pending hearings.

Gerald checked the claimant's spawn list. The System displayed it.

Fourteen entities.

Three of them were classified as "unlicensed biological entities." Gerald recognized the classification immediately.

He had invented it. It was his own Form 11-B, the one he had tried to file in chapter one and could not.

The claimant had unlicensed spawns.

Gerald pulled up his citation authority. He was within range. He could serve a citation. He could cite Threshold of Hollow Kings for unlicensed biological entities, for three separate violations, each one carrying a debuff proportional to severity.

But a citation would not stop the claim. A citation would debuff the claimant. It would not invalidate the filing.

Unless.

Gerald pulled up Section 22.1 again. Subsection B. A claim is invalid if the claimant is under active citation at the time of filing.

Gerald read it twice. The claimant had filed while not under citation. But if Gerald could serve a citation now, before adjudication, and the citation was retroactive to the moment of filing...

No. Citations were not retroactive. They applied upon service.

Gerald read subsection B again. He read it a third time. He read the surrounding subsections.

He read the definitions section. He read the cross-references.

There. Section 22.1, subsection B, second clause. "A claim is invalid if the claimant is under active citation at the time of filing, or if the claimant incurs a citation within the adjudication window that is related to the subject property."

Related to the subject property.

Gerald could cite Threshold of Hollow Kings for unlicensed biological entities. Those entities were spawns. Spawns were part of a Core's structure.

If Gerald cited the claimant for violations related to its own structure, and the citation was served within the adjudication window, the claim would be invalid.

It was not a citation against the claim itself. It was a citation against the claimant's structure, which happened to be the subject property of the claim.

The claim was about Gerald's footprint, and the citation was about the claimant's footprint. Those were different properties.

Unless the claimant's spawns had entered Gerald's footprint. Gerald checked his intrusion log.

Three days ago, two of Threshold of Hollow Kings' spawns had breached Gerald's northeast boundary.

They had been repelled by his compliance enforcement.

But they had been inside. They had been on his property.

The spawns were related to the subject property. They had been on it. They had violated it.

Gerald pulled up the citation form. He filled it out. Three counts of unlicensed biological entity trespass.

He attached the intrusion log as evidence. He marked the citation as related to property under active claim, per Section 22.1 subsection B.

He needed to serve it. The target had to be physically present.

Gerald checked the claimant's location. The Core was two hundred meters from his northeast corner. Outside his range. He could not serve it.

He had twenty-three hours and forty-one minutes.

The Core would not come to him. He could not go to it. He was a sub-basement.

He did not move.

Gerald stared at the citation form. He stared at the range limitation. He stared at the intrusion log.

Then he looked at the water leak he had just sealed. The crack in the foundation. The one he had excavated. The one that connected his floor slab to the groundwater table.

The groundwater table ran under both properties.

Gerald pulled up his structural map. He traced the water table. It passed beneath his northeast corner and continued, two hundred meters east, beneath Threshold of Hollow Kings' primary chamber.

He could not move. But he could extend.

A citation required the target to be physically present, and the target was a Core; a Core was not a body. A Core was a structure.

A structure had boundaries. Gerald's structure had a boundary.

The water table connected them.

Gerald pulled up Section 3.12. Shared infrastructure. When two structures share a common element, the shared element is considered part of both structures for the purposes of compliance enforcement.

The water table was shared infrastructure. It was part of Gerald's structure. It was part of Threshold of Hollow Kings' structure. It connected them.

Gerald could cite the water table. The water table was inside his dungeon. The water table was also inside the claimant's dungeon. If he cited the water table, and the citation was served on the shared element, it was served on both structures simultaneously.

The target would be physically present.

Gerald filled out the citation.

One count: shared infrastructure violation, Section 3.12, unlicensed biological contamination of shared water table; the three spawns had entered through the water table.

They had contaminated it. The contamination was a violation of the shared element.

He served it.

The System accepted. The citation hit.

Threshold of Hollow Kings' Compliance Rating dropped by four points.

Its spawns weakened. The three unlicensed entities were flagged for removal.

And the claim status changed.

CLAIM INVALIDATED: CLAIMANT UNDER ACTIVE CITATION RELATED TO SUBJECT PROPERTY.

Gerald watched the claim dissolve. The System's interface pulsed once and went quiet.

He exhaled, and he checked his rating. 86. Stable.

He had forty-seven hours until re-inspection.

He had a cure on record. The Acting Inspector was still debuffed. The demolition was paused.

Then a new notification appeared.

AUDIT NOTICE: INSPECTOR GENERAL.

Gerald's hands went cold.

The Inspector General. The one that periodically audited all Cores.

The one that had never appeared in any of his sixty-nine chapters, which had never intervened.

It had never sent a notice. It had been silent.

Until now.

The notice was timestamped. It was scheduled. It was arriving in six hours.

And it was not an audit of Gerald, which was an audit of the Acting Inspector.

He began preparing a file for the Acting Inspector.
