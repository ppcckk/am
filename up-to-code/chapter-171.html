Chapter 171: Cite the Reason

# Chapter 171

The hand was gray, desiccated, and possessed of too many joints.

It gripped the edge of the fractured foundation slab with a strength that defied its withered appearance.

Gerald Hutchins did not scream. He did not panic. He looked at the hand, then at the obsidian shard burning in his other palm, and then at the ceiling of his sub-basement dungeon, where the mold spores were drifting lazily through the stagnant air.

"Stop," Gerald said.

The voice came out flat, dry, and professional.

The hand froze. The fingers twitched. A low, gurgling sound emanated from the darkness below the slab, a sound like wet gravel grinding against bone.

Gerald stepped forward, his boots crunching on the ash of the burnt Mites. He held the shard away from his body, letting the heat dissipate into the cool, damp air of the cellar.

"I am Gerald Hutchins," he said. "Municipal Building Inspector, City of Ashford.

Department of Public Works.

Code Enforcement Division. I am requesting you identify the entity operating this unauthorized excavation.

"

The hand did not move.

Instead, a head rose from the hole in the floor. It was a grotesque thing, half-formed, lacking skin, resembling a raw lump of clay that had learned to hate. Two milky eyes swiveled upward. They locked onto Gerald.

A voice projected from the hole, not through the air, but directly into Gerald's mind. It sounded like tearing paper.

*Who are you?*

"I am the Inspector," Gerald repeated.

He pulled a small, laminated badge from his vest, though he knew the System did not care about municipal credentials. It was a habit.

A reflex.

"You are in violation of Subsection 14.2, Unauthorized Subterranean Structures. You have breached the primary load-bearing foundation of the Ashford Municipal Library. This is a Class A Hazard."

*We seek the Core,* the voice said. *The fire. The heat.*

Gerald glanced at the obsidian shard. The purple light pulsed in rhythm with the creature's heartbeat.

"There is no Core here," Gerald said. "This is a residential compliance zone.

Specifically, a library sub-basement, and the occupancy load is zero.

The ventilation is inadequate. The egress routes are non-compliant.

You are trespassing on a private easement owned by the City of Ashford.

"

*The heat,* the voice insisted. *We smell the fire essence. We want it.*

Gerald looked at the shard. He looked at the creature.

He realized the lie he had told the System, the one he had burned into the air with his own breath, was now a physical vulnerability.

He had tried to hoard the fragment, to integrate it silently. The System had marked him. The neighbors had smelled him.

And now this thing wanted what he had stolen.

Gerald lowered his hand. The heat in his palm faded, replaced by a cold numbness that spread up his wrist. He ignored it. He reached into his vest pocket and pulled out a standard-issue citation pad.

"I am issuing a Stop Work Order," Gerald said. He ripped the yellow paper off the pad. He did not write it down. He simply held it up.

The System text flashed in the corner of his vision.

**ACTION: ISSUE CITATION** **TARGET: UNIDENTIFIED ENTITY (CLASS 3 SUBTERRANEAN)** **VIOLATION: UNAUTHORIZED EXCAVATION / STRUCTURAL DAMAGE** **PENALTY: IMMEDIATE PHYSICAL REPULSION**

The creature hissed. The hand on the slab tightened.

*You cannot stop us,* the voice said. *We are part of the deep. We are the foundation.*

"The foundation is cracked," Gerald said.

"And the crack is your fault. Section 7.4: Structural Integrity.

If a dungeon core damages the host structure, it triggers an automatic stability penalty.

Look at your walls.

"

The creature looked. The dark tunnel behind it, the one leading into the earth, was groaning. Dust fell from the ceiling. The walls, which had been solid stone moments ago, were now webbed with fractures that glowed with a faint, sickly orange light.

*No,* the creature whispered. *No, the walls are fine.*

"Your Compliance Rating is dropping," Gerald said. "I can see it.

It's in the air, and the System is auditing this violation in real-time.

Every second you stay here, your structural integrity degrades by four percent. If you pull your head out fully, you will collapse.

And if you collapse, the debris will crush you. And then the library above will be condemned. And I will have to file a Form 19-C: Catastrophic Failure.

Do you know how much paperwork that is?

"

The creature's eyes widened. The milky white of its irises seemed to clear, revealing a frantic, animal intelligence.

*Stop the decay,* it begged. *Give us the heat. We will leave.*

Gerald hesitated, and the obsidian shard was still in his hand. He could feel the pull, the seductive warmth of the fire essence. It would solve his vulnerability.

It would make him strong.

But it would also be theft.

Gerald Hutchins did not steal. He inspected. He measured. He cited.

He looked at the creature. He looked at the cracking floor.

"I will not give you the heat," Gerald said. "That would be an unlicensed transfer of hazardous materials. It violates Environmental Code 33."

The creature snarled. The hand jerked forward.

Gerald raised the citation pad.

"Serve," he said.

The System responded instantly.

A ripple of blue light expanded from Gerald's position. It hit the creature like a physical wall.

The gray hand flew back into the hole.

The head slammed against the edge of the slab. The creature shrieked, a sound of pure agony.

**CITATION SERVED.** **TARGET STATUS: STUNNED (3 SECONDS).** **STRUCTURAL PENALTY APPLIED: -15% INTEGRITY.**

The ceiling above the hole groaned. A large chunk of concrete broke loose and fell into the darkness. The creature scrambled back, trying to retreat into the tunnel, but the walls of its own domain were closing in. The fractures were spreading, branching out like lightning.

Gerald walked to the edge of the hole. He looked down into the abyss.

"Exit," he commanded. "Through the nearest egress route.

Which, currently, does not exist.

You are required to create one.

Immediately."

The creature whimpered. It was trapped. Its tunnel was collapsing.

If it stayed, it died. If it came out, it was exposed to Gerald's inspection.

*Help us,* the voice said. *Fix the wall.*

Gerald looked at the obsidian shard. Then he looked at the mold spores drifting down from the ceiling.

"I am an inspector," Gerald said. "I do not repair. I identify defects."

He turned his back on the hole.

"However," he added, pausing, "if you can demonstrate a viable exit path within the next sixty seconds, I may consider a temporary variance for emergency egress. But you must provide the materials. I do not have materials."

The silence in the sub-basement was absolute, save for the dripping of water from a broken pipe.

Then, a new voice cut through the air. It was smooth, cultured, and amused.

"Mr. Hutchins. Always so pedantic."

Gerald did not turn around, and he recognized the voice. He had cited this man three chapters ago. Core 412.

The Fire Mage.

The neighbor whose essence Gerald was currently holding in his hand.

Gerald turned slowly.

Standing in the center of the room, where the mold spores had congregated into a thick, yellow haze, was a figure. It was humanoid, formed entirely of smoke and ember. It wore the semblance of a suit, but the lapels were made of flame.

Core 412.

"You have my fragment," the Core said.

The voice did not come from a mouth; it came from the air itself.

"I felt it. The theft.

The absorption attempt. You failed, of course.

But you tried."

"I found a hazardous material," Gerald said.

He kept his voice level. He did not hide the shard.

He held it up, letting it glow in the dim light.

"It was unmarked. Unlabeled.

No safety data sheet. I was attempting to contain it per OSHA regulations.

"

Core 412 laughed. The sound was like a log popping in a fireplace.

"OSHA," the Core said. "You think you can cite me with OSHA? I am the System. I am the Code."

"The Code is the Code," Gerald said.

"And you are in my dungeon. Without an appointment. Without a permit.

You are blocking the egress route.

"

The smoke-figure drifted closer. The heat radiating from it was intense. Gerald's skin prickled, but he did not step back. He stood his ground.

"You have two choices, Inspector," Core 412 said. "Return the fragment. Or I will burn this room to the bedrock. And then I will dig through the rubble to find you."

Gerald looked at the hole in the floor. The creature below was still struggling, its tunnel collapsing further. The walls were groaning, a deep, resonant vibration that shook Gerald's boots.

"If you burn this room," Gerald said, "you trigger a Category 5 Fire Code Violation. The penalty is total Core shutdown.

You have a Compliance Rating of 82.

If you cause a structural collapse during a fire event, your Rating drops below 50. You die.

"

Core 412 paused. The flames around its face flickered.

"You are bluffing," the Core said.

"I never bluff," Gerald said. "I inspect."

He pointed to the ceiling.

"Look up."

Core 412 looked, and the mold spores were not just drifting. They were clustering. They were forming a dense, yellow cloud directly above Gerald. And they were glowing.

**SYSTEM ALERT: BIOLOGICAL HAZARD DETECTED.** **VIOLATION: UNCONTROLLED SPAWN GROWTH.** **ACTION: IMMEDIATE QUARANTINE PROTOCOL.**

A barrier of white light snapped into existence around the room. A containment field. It sealed the exits. It sealed the vents.

Core 412 tried to move through the barrier. It hit the light and hissed.

"You trapped us," the Core said. "You trapped both of us."

"I quarantined the hazard," Gerald corrected. "The hazard is you. The hazard is the fire. The hazard is the unlicensed biological entity below."

Gerald walked to the center of the room. He placed the obsidian shard on the floor.

He kicked it toward the hole in the floor.

The shard slid across the concrete, coming to rest on the edge of the abyss. The purple light pulsed.

"Take it," Gerald said.

Core 412 stared at him.

"What?"

"Take the fragment," Gerald said. "It is yours.

I am filing a Form 11-B: Return of Unlicensed Biological Entity.

You are reclaiming your property.

But you must do it now. Before the quarantine timer expires.

Because if the timer expires, the System will purge this room. Fire, mold, and all. Including me.

"

Core 412 looked at the shard. Then it looked at Gerald.

"Why?" the Core asked. "You were going to absorb it. I felt your intent."

"Intent is not action," Gerald said. "And I am not a thief.

I am an inspector.

And right now, the only way to keep this dungeon open is to clear the violation. Which means returning the stolen goods.

"

Core 412 drifted toward the hole. The smoke of its body thinned. It looked tired.

"If I take it," the Core said, "I will have to leave.

The quarantine field prevents me from staying. And if I leave, I lose my foothold in your dungeon.

My scouts are confused.

My tunnels are blocked.

"

"Then leave," Gerald said. "You have thirty seconds.

The quarantine is a hard countdown.

If you are still here when it ends, you burn.

If you are gone, you live. And I keep my Compliance Rating intact.

"

Core 412 hovered over the shard. The heat from its body warped the air. The shard glowed brighter, responding to its master.

"You are a fool, Hutchins," the Core said. "You could have been powerful. You could have been like us.

Strong. Free."

"I am free," Gerald said. "I am following the code."

Core 412 snarled. It dove into the hole.

The smoke poured into the abyss. The fire essence swirled around the obsidian shard. The shard lifted into the air, pulled by the Core's will. It disappeared into the darkness below.

The heat in the room dropped.

The mold spores dissipated, the yellow glow fading.

The barrier of white light flickered.

Gerald stood in the center of the room, alone. He looked at his hands. They were shaking. He clenched them into fists.

Then, a new sound.

Not from below. Not from the shadows.

From the ceiling.

A knock.

Three distinct, polite raps.

Gerald looked up. The concrete ceiling of the library above him was intact. But the sound had come from within the stone itself.

The System text appeared in the air, large, bold, and undeniable.

**INSPECTOR GENERAL AUDIT INITIATED.** **TARGET: CORE 001 (GERALD HUTCHINS).** **LOCATION: ASHFORD MUNICIPAL LIBRARY SUB-BASEMENT.** **TIME REMAINING: 00:05:00.** **NOTE: THE AUDIT IS NOT A TEST. IT IS AN INQUISITION.**

Gerald stared at the text.

Five minutes, and he had no dungeon. He had no defenses. He had no materials.

He had only a citation pad, a broken foundation, and the memory of a lie.

The knock came again. Louder.

And then, the voice of the Inspector General did not whisper. It did not project. It spoke through the walls, through the floor, through the bones of the earth.

"Mr.

Hutchins," the voice said. It was cold, sharp, and infinitely old. "You have been cited for a violation that does not exist in the code.

You have committed an act of *heroism*.

Explain yourself. Or be deleted.

"

Gerald looked at the hole in the floor. The darkness was gone. The creature was gone. Core 412 was gone.

He looked at his empty hands, and he looked at the ceiling.

He took a deep breath.

"I am ready," Gerald said.

The floor beneath him dissolved.

Not into darkness.

Into light.

And a chair.

Gerald sat down.

Across from him, a figure sat. It was not a dragon. It was not a monster.

It was a bureaucrat.

It wore a gray suit. It held a pen.

"Start talking," the bureaucrat said. "And do not cite the code. Cite the reason."
