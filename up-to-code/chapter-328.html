Chapter 328: Retroactive Permit

# Chapter 328

The dragon did not roar. It screamed.

The sound was not a vocalization. It was the tearing of stone, the snapping of iron beams, and the wet, heavy collapse of a structural load that had been violated for too long. Ashford City's zoning laws had been ignored for centuries, but the beast had taken it upon itself to rewrite them with fire.

Kael, a level forty Paladin of the Silver Order, watched the wall dissolve.

He had spent the morning polishing his pauldrons. He had spent the afternoon buffing his sword until it reflected his own terrified face.

He had expected a dungeon, and he had expected goblins. He had expected a puzzle box that required a specific sequence of clicks to open.

He had not expected the ceiling of the sub-basement to become a mouth.

The debris rained down in chunks of drywall and concrete.

Kael raised his shield, but the impact wasn't physical. It was regulatory.

The air pressure in the room dropped so that his ears popped.

A translucent blue window shimmered into existence above the rubble, pulsing with an angry red light.

**VIOLATION: UNAUTHORIZED STRUCTURAL MODIFICATION.** **SOURCE: EXTERNAL ENTITY.** **PENALTY: IMMINENT COLLAPSE.**

"Run," said the Rogue, Mera.

She was already moving. Her dagger was sheathed.

Her boots were laced, and she didn't waste time on heroics.

She wasted time on exit strategies. Kael, however, stood rooted to the spot.

His eyes were fixed on the blue text. He was a rule-follower, too, in his own way. He followed the rules of the Guild, the rules of combat, the rules of honor.

This text broke all of them.

The dragon's head pushed through the hole.

Scales the color of burnt copper scraped against the exposed rebar. A claw, the size of a dining table, hooked onto the edge of the floor and pulled. The dungeon shuddered.

Dust choked the air.

Kael raised his sword.

"For the Light!"

"Move!" Mera shouted, grabbing his arm. "It's not an enemy! It's a building inspector!"

Kael shook her off. He charged.

He struck the dragon's claw. The impact sent a shockwave through his bones, but the creature didn't flinch. It didn't even look at him. It was looking at Gerald.

Gerald stood in the center of the room, holding a clipboard. He was not wearing armor. He was wearing a beige cardigan and slacks that were too tight at the waist. He looked like a man who had been waiting for this appointment for thirty years.

The dragon opened its maw. Heat radiated from its throat, glowing like a furnace door left ajar.

It wasn't breathing fire, which was breathing intent. It was preparing to erase the violation.

Gerald stepped forward. He didn't raise a shield. He didn't cast a spell. He held up his left hand, palm out.

"Stop," Gerald said.

The dragon hesitated. The air shimmered. The red text on Kael's HUD flickered, then changed.

**SYSTEM ALERT: CITATION ISSUED.** **TARGET: DRAGON (UNLICENSED FLIGHT/COMBUSTION).** **STATUS: PENDING HEARING.**

The dragon's mouth snapped shut. A low growl vibrated in its chest, a sound of deep, bureaucratic annoyance. It blinked one golden eye at Gerald. It seemed confused.

Gerald adjusted his glasses. He clicked his pen.

"You are in violation of Ordinance 402-C," Gerald said. His voice was dry, flat, and devoid of adrenaline.

"Combustion within an enclosed public structure requires a Class-A Fire Suppression Permit.

You do not have one.

Furthermore, your scale density exceeds the maximum allowable mass for a residential sub-basement by four hundred percent. You are overloading the foundation.

"

The dragon huffed. Smoke curled from its nostrils.

"I am not asking for a hearing," Gerald continued. "I am issuing a summary judgment. Cessation of hostilities is mandatory until the permit application is reviewed."

The dragon looked at Mera. It looked at Kael. It looked back at Gerald.

It seemed to understand that the small, beige man was more dangerous than the paladin. The paladin could kill it.

The man could erase it from existence for failing to submit form 11-B in triplicate.

The dragon pulled its head back. The claw slipped from the floor. The debris stopped falling.

Kael stared. "What... what is this?"

Gerald turned to him. The cold smile was gone. In its place was a look of profound disappointment.

"You're blocking the egress route."

Kael looked down. He was standing in the doorway.

"Move," Gerald said.

Kael moved. He scrambled backward, tripping over his own scabbard. He landed in the dirt, gasping.

Mera caught him before he hit the floor, and she looked at Gerald with a mixture of awe and horror.

"You stopped a dragon," Mera said. "With a citation."

"I stopped a violation," Gerald corrected. "The dragon is secondary."

He walked toward the hole in the wall. The System interface hung in the air, glowing softly.

**HEARING SCHEDULED: 14:00 HOURS.** **PARTICIPANTS: CORE GERALD HUTCHINS, DRAGON ENTITY (ID: PYRE-9).** **LOCATION: INTERNAL COURT (SUB-LEVEL 4).**

Gerald tapped the air.

The window minimized, folding into his pocket. He picked up a piece of drywall from the floor. He examined the crack running through it.

He ran his thumb along the fissure.

"Load-bearing failure," he muttered. "Probably shoddy workmanship. Or poor drainage."

He looked at Kael.

"Are you here to loot?"

Kael shook his head slowly. He was still on his knees.

"We... we were looking for the Lich King."

"There is no Lich King here," Gerald said. "There is a mild moisture problem and a pending variance request for a new staircase. If you are not here to submit a variance request, you are trespassing on a non-public easement."

Mera stepped forward. Her hands were empty.

"We're adventurers. We fight monsters."

"Monsters are unauthorized biological entities," Gerald said.

He pulled a small notebook from his pocket, and he wrote something down.

"I suggest you leave before I cite you for unlicensed looting.

The fines are substantial, and they are deducted from your experience points.

"

Kael swallowed hard.

"Experience points?"

"Compliance rating affects XP gain," Gerald said, not looking up. "Low compliance means low rewards.

It's a simple incentive structure.

The System encourages order. Disorder is punished.

"

He looked up. "Are you ordered?"

Mera and Kael exchanged a glance.

Kael straightened his posture. He tried to look dignified, but he was covered in dust and trembling.

"I am a Paladin.

I follow the code of chivalry.

"

"Chivalry is not a building code," Gerald said. "Show me your permit."

Kael blinked. "My... permit?"

"To enter. To fight.

To exist within this jurisdiction.

" Gerald pointed to the floor.

"This is a private enterprise.

I am the landlord. You are guests.

Guests must register.

"

Mera sighed. She reached into her belt pouch and pulled out a scroll. It was a Quest Log. She held it out.

Gerald took it. He read it.

He frowned.

"This is outdated. This refers to the previous owner. The Lich.

He was evicted.

"

"Evicted?" Mera asked.

"Demolished," Gerald said. "Structurally unsound. I filed for condemnation."

He tore the scroll in half.

"This is void.

You have no authority to be here.

However, I am feeling generous. Or rather, I am feeling efficient. I need laborers.

You two have hammers. You have strength.

You can help me shore up this wall.

"

Kael looked at the torn scroll. He looked at the dragon, which was currently pacing outside the window, waiting for its hearing. He looked at Gerald.

"You want us to do construction work?" Kael asked.

"I want you to comply with the repair order," Gerald said. "In exchange, I will waive the trespassing fee. And I will grant you a provisional permit to exist. It's better than being cited."

Mera looked at Kael.

"Do it."

Kael hesitated. His pride was a fragile thing. But the System's red text was still pulsing in his vision. **VIOLATION: UNAUTHORIZED PRESENCE.** It was a constant, nagging itch in the back of his mind.

He picked up his shield.

"Fine. But I'm not using my hammer.

I'm using my sword. It's sharper.

"

"Acceptable," Gerald said. He pulled another form from his pocket.

"Sign here.

And here. Initial every page. No abbreviations.

"

Kael signed. He felt a strange sense of relief. The System's red text faded, replaced by a green **COMPLIANT** tag.

Gerald led them through the rubble. The dungeon was a mess, but it was a *managed* mess. Every piece of debris was tagged. Every broken pipe was labeled with a priority level.

They reached the central chamber.

The water leak from the previous chapter had been fixed, but the pipe was still dripping. Gerald had placed a bucket under it.

The bucket had a label: **TEMPORARY CONTAINMENT.

REPLACE BY TUESDAY.**

"This is the heart," Gerald said. "The Core."

He pointed to a glowing crystal embedded in the wall. It pulsed with a steady, rhythmic light.

"It needs reinforcement," Gerald said. "The dragon's arrival caused a vibration resonance.

If the crystal cracks, the dungeon collapses.

And if the dungeon collapses, the library above loses its foundation.

The city will fine me. Again.

"

He looked at Kael.

"Chisel the new supports. Fit them to the existing beams.

Use the steel rods from the east wing. Do not weld.

Welding creates heat stress.

Use bolts. Grade 8, zinc-plated.

"

Kael nodded, and he moved to the wall. He examined the beams.

They were old, but they were solid.

He could see the craftsmanship. Someone had cared for this place, even if it was hidden beneath the world.

He picked up a chisel. He worked.

As he worked, he watched Gerald. Gerald was not idle.

He was measuring, and he was calculating.

He was muttering to himself.

"Ventilation is insufficient," Gerald said. "The dragon's smoke filled the space. We need an exhaust fan.

Industrial grade. Minimum 2000 CFM.

"

He pulled out his notebook. He wrote: **INSTALL EXHAUST. SOURCE: KITCHEN HOOD (UNUSABLE).**

Mera watched him.

"Can you just... make one?"

"The System doesn't spawn appliances," Gerald said.

"It spawns resources. You have to build them. Or buy them.

Or steal them.

Stealing is a violation.

"

"Where do you get them?" Mera asked.

Gerald paused, and he looked at her. His eyes were sharp.

"The Inspector General audits all cores.

During an audit, the System opens a temporary market. You can trade Compliance Points for materials.

"

"Compliance Points?"

"Points earned by fixing violations," Gerald said.

"Or by citing others.

I have a lot of points. I cited a goblin den last week.

They had no fire extinguishers. I cited a slime pit for improper drainage. The fines were hefty.

I used the fines to buy these bolts.

"

He held up a handful of screws. They glowed with a faint blue light.

"This is the economy of bureaucracy," Gerald said. "You fight with swords. I fight with paperwork. And paperwork is cheaper."

Kael finished the first support beam. He stepped back. The crystal pulsed brighter.

"Good," Gerald said. "Now the second one. Faster. The hearing starts in ten minutes."

"What happens at the hearing?" Mera asked.

"The dragon argues its case," Gerald said.

"It will claim self-defense. It will claim territorial rights. I will argue that its wingspan violates the minimum ceiling clearance of twelve feet.

It is a flying creature in a low-ceilinged room.

That is a hazard.

"

"And if you lose?"

"I don't lose," Gerald said.

"I have thirty-one years of experience. I know the loopholes. I know the precedents.

The System loves consistency. I provide consistency.

The dragon is chaos.

"

The ground shook. A distant roar echoed from the upper levels. The dragon was getting impatient.

Gerald checked his watch.

"Two minutes."

He walked to the center of the room. He stood before the Core. He closed his eyes.

"Ready?" he asked.

Kael and Mera nodded.

They didn't know what

"ready" meant in this context. They prepared to fight.

They prepared to run. They prepared for magic.

Gerald opened his eyes. He raised his stamp.

**HEARING BEGINS.**

The air changed. The dust settled. The light shifted from the warm glow of the Core to the stark, white fluorescence of a courtroom.

A figure appeared. It was not the dragon.

It was a smaller, humanoid shape made of smoke and ash.

It wore a suit. It carried a briefcase.

**ENTITY: PYRE-9 (LEGAL REPRESENTATIVE).**

Gerald stepped forward. He did not bow. He did not greet. He opened his folder.

"Counsel," Gerald said. "Let us begin."

The smoke figure smiled. It was a jagged, terrifying smile.

"Mr. Hutchins," the figure said. "We are prepared to settle out of court."

Gerald paused.

"On what terms?"

"We will leave," the figure said. "We will cease all combustion. We will withdraw from this jurisdiction."

Gerald narrowed his eyes. This was unexpected. The dragon was not fighting. It was negotiating.

"Why?" Gerald asked.

"Because," the figure said, "we have a counter-citation."

It opened its briefcase. Inside was a single, glowing document.

**CITATION: UNLICENSED CORE EXPANSION.** **TARGET: GERALD HUTCHINS.** **VIOLATION: UNAPPROVED SUB-LEVEL CONSTRUCTION (LEVEL 5).** **PENALTY: CORE DECOMMISSIONING.**

Gerald stared at the document. His blood ran cold.

He had been building. He had been expanding. He had been adding rooms, corridors, and traps. He had been treating the System like a client.

But he had forgotten one thing, and he was the client, too.

And he had never filed the permit for the new wing.

Gerald looked at the smoke figure. He looked at Kael and Mera, who were watching with wide, confused eyes.

"If I am decommissioned," Gerald said, his voice steady, "the dungeon collapses. The library falls. The city dies."

"Then you should have filed the permit," the smoke figure said. "It is too late now."

Gerald looked at his stamp. He looked at the new corridor behind him. He looked at the window.

**System Update: Threat Detected.** **Entity: The Inspector General.** **Distance: Immediate.**

The ceiling cracked. Not from the dragon. From the System itself.

Gerald had one move left. One loophole.

He looked at Kael.

"Kael," Gerald said. "Do you have a permit to breathe?"

Kael blinked. "What?"

"If you are an adventurer," Gerald said, "you are a licensed entity. You have a license to interact with the dungeon. If you interact with the dungeon, you are part of its operational infrastructure."

Gerald turned to the smoke figure.

"I am not the owner," Gerald said. "I am the manager. The owner is the City of Ashford. And the City has a contract with the Adventurer's Guild."

He pointed at Kael.

"That man is a municipal employee. By guild charter. Section 4, Paragraph 12. Adventurers performing maintenance on public infrastructure are considered temporary municipal workers."

The smoke figure froze.

Gerald smiled. It was a cold, sharp smile.

"And if he is a municipal worker," Gerald said, "then he has authority to approve variances. He can sign the permit. Retroactively."

He held out his pen.

"Sign it, Kael. Sign it now, or we all die."

Kael looked at the pen. He looked at the cracking ceiling. He looked at Gerald.

He took the pen.
