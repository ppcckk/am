Chapter 203: Citing God

# Chapter 203

The Consortium delegation did not look like an invasion force. They looked like a municipal survey team.

Three figures stood at the threshold of the sub-basement entrance, wearing matching grey coveralls and holding tablets that glowed with faint blue runes. Their boots were polished to a mirror shine, reflecting the dim emergency lighting of the Library's lower level. Behind them, a fourth figure hovered off the ground, clutching a clipboard with white-knuckled intensity.

The lead surveyor adjusted his glasses. He scanned the hallway. He checked his tablet. He frowned.

"This doesn't match the floor plans," the surveyor said. His voice was thin, reedy. "The blueprints indicate a load-bearing support column here. There is only air."

"Structural anomaly," the floating figure muttered. He didn't look up from his clipboard.

"Or a hidden chamber.

Common in older builds. Check the seismic rating.

"

The surveyor tapped the wall. The sound was dull, hollow. He tapped again. A crack spiderwebbed across the plaster, then healed instantly, knitting itself back together with a wet, organic squelch.

The surveyor recoiled.

"Biological substrate?" he asked.

"Possible," the floating figure said. "Or a mimic. Check for heat signatures."

The surveyor pointed a device at the floor.

The reading flatlined. Cold.

Dead.

Unnatural.

"It's a Core," the surveyor whispered. "A localized, high-density Core.

But the energy signature is wrong.

It's too quiet.

Too static."

"Quiet is dangerous," the floating figure said. "Quiet means they are listening. Quiet means they are auditing."

The third member of the team, a heavy-set man with a tool belt that clinked with enchanted wrenches, stepped forward. He raised a hand, forming a gesture that pulsed with red light. A standard dungeon-breaker spell.

Simple. Effective.

Designed to shatter weak structural integrity.

"Let's see what's under the rug," the heavy man grunted.

The spell flew forward. It struck the floor.

Nothing happened.

No explosion. No shockwave. No crumbling plaster.

Instead, a new sign popped up in mid-air, hovering directly above the heavy man's head. It was a bright, cheerful yellow.

*NOTICE: UNAUTHORIZED STRUCTURAL MODIFICATION DETECTED.*

*Violation: Attempted Demolition Without Permit (Code 74-C).*

*Penalty: Immediate Cessation of Kinetic Energy.*

The heavy man's spell fizzled out like a wet match. His hand spasmed, then went limp. He looked at his palm, then at the sign, then at his colleagues.

"What did you do?" he demanded.

"We didn't do anything," the surveyor said, backing away. "The system reacted."

"The system?" the heavy man snapped. "There is no system here. This is a dungeon."

"It is a *regulated* dungeon," the surveyor corrected. "Look at the walls. Look at the ventilation grates. This place is up to code."

The floating figure lowered his clipboard. His eyes narrowed. He was the Inspector General's liaison, the one who had been tracking the anomaly in Ashford for weeks.

He stepped into the corridor.

The air grew thicker, smelling of ozone and old paper.

"Core Gerald," the figure called out. "We are here for a compliance inspection. Please submit your current zoning status."

Silence.

The heavy man rubbed his wrist.

"I can't feel my hand."

"Leave it," the liaison said. "Gerald, answer us. The Consortium requires a permit for all active dungeon operations in this district. You have not filed one."

A door opened. Not a physical door, but a section of the wall sliding aside with the smooth, hydraulic grace of an elevator.

Gerald Hutchins stood there. He was not holding a sword. He was not holding a fireball. He was holding a clipboard, a pen, and a tape measure.

He looked at the three men. He looked at the floating liaison. He did not blink.

"Your entry point is blocked," Gerald said. His voice was dry, flat. "You are standing in a fire lane."

The surveyor looked down.

"There is no fire lane marked."

"It is implied by the width of the corridor," Gerald said. "Code 88-D requires a minimum of thirty-six inches of clearance in all egress routes. Your boots are wide. You are obstructing flow."

The heavy man sneered.

"We're here to inspect. Move aside, inspector."

Gerald did not move. He clicked his pen.

"I am not the inspector," Gerald said. "I am the asset. You are the liability. And you are violating the quiet ordinance I posted ten minutes ago."

The liaison floated closer.

"Gerald, this is not a game. The Consortium has authority here. We will shut this dungeon down if you do not submit to a standard audit.

We know you issued citations to three other cores this week. We know you weaponized the building code.

"

"Correct," Gerald said.

"Then you are a rogue element."

"I am a compliant element," Gerald corrected.

"You are the ones creating disorder.

Look at your tool belt, sir. Those wrenches are not calibrated to current tension standards.

If you strike a load-bearing wall, you risk a catastrophic collapse. Do you have a liability waiver signed by the Library Board?

"

The heavy man opened his mouth to retort, but no sound came out.

A new sign appeared above him.

*NOTICE: HARMLESS NEGLECT.*

*Violation: Failure to Provide Safety Equipment (Code 12-A).*

*Penalty: Temporary Immobility.*

The heavy man's legs buckled. He collapsed to the floor with a heavy thud, his enchanted wrenches scattering across the linoleum.

The surveyor and the liaison stared at him.

"He… he just fell," the surveyor stammered.

"He violated code," Gerald said. He walked forward.

His shoes squeaked on the polished floor.

"Two of you remain. I suggest you leave. Or cite yourselves for loitering."

The liaison's face hardened, and he raised his hand. A sphere of dark energy coalesced around his fingers.

This was not a simple spell.

This was a nullification field. It was designed to strip a Core of its passive resistances. It was the Consortium's favorite tool for crushing stubborn, low-level dungeons.

"You think codes protect you?" the liaison hissed. "I am an agent of the System. I can override local regulations."

Gerald stopped. He looked at the sphere of energy. He looked at the liaison's clipboard.

"You are overriding a federal mandate," Gerald said. "Section 4, Paragraph 2 of the Dungeon Core Accord. 'No agent may interfere with a Core that maintains a Compliance Rating of 50 or above.'"

The liaison paused.

"What?"

"My rating," Gerald said. "Check your tablet."

The surveyor looked at his glowing screen. His eyes went wide.

"Rating 51," he whispered.

The liaison froze. The energy sphere flickered.

"That's impossible," the liaison said. "You had three mold spores. You had a rat. You had a leak."

"I fixed the leak," Gerald said. "I replaced the ventilation filters with HEPA-grade constructs. I reinforced the egress routes with steel beams harvested from the library's old shelving units. I am up to code."

"You're bluffing."

"I have the receipts."

Gerald raised his clipboard. A scroll of paper unspooled, floating in the air between them. It was a long, endless list of inspection dates, repair logs, and violation notices.

The liaison's face went pale. The Consortium had assumed Gerald was a glitch.

A bug.

A weak Core trying to survive by hiding in the shadows.

They had not expected him to be a bureaucrat who had spent thirty-one years memorizing every obscure clause of the municipal building code.

The liaison lowered his hand. The energy sphere dissipated.

"This changes nothing," the liaison said, though his voice lacked conviction. "We are still here. We are still inspecting."

"Then proceed," Gerald said. "But be aware. I am currently conducting a private audit.

If you disturb my workspace, I will cite you for obstruction of justice. The penalty for that is permanent stat lockout.

"

The surveyor looked at the liaison.

"Sir, we should leave. His rating is rising. I can see it.

Every second he talks, his defense stats tick up.

"

The liaison gritted his teeth. He looked at Gerald. He looked at the heavy man on the floor, who was still trying to move his legs.

"We will return," the liaison said. "With a full team."

"You are welcome to apply for a permit," Gerald said. "Application form 19-Z. Submit in triplicate."

The liaison turned. He floated toward the exit. The surveyor grabbed the heavy man by the arm and dragged him toward the door.

As they stepped out into the hallway, the liaison paused. He looked back at Gerald.

"You think you're safe because you're boring," the liaison said. "But the System doesn't care about boring. It cares about threat. And you are a threat."

Gerald watched them go. He did not smile. He did not frown. He simply made a note on his clipboard.

*Incident Report: Unauthorized Entry Attempt. Status: Repelled via Code Compliance.*

*Next Action: Increase Ventilation Capacity to Counteract Potential Smog Attack.*

Gerald turned back to his dungeon. The air was clearer now. The mold spores that had drifted up into the Library had triggered a real-world evacuation, but the Library staff had not yet returned. The silence was profound.

He walked to the center of the room. He pulled out his tape measure.

He needed to know the exact dimensions of the new void space that had formed in the center of the floor. The Consortium's nullification field had left a scar. A patch of darkness where the energy had touched the ground.

It was not damage. It was a hole.

Gerald leaned over the edge. He lowered the tape measure.

The hook caught on something below, and he pulled it up.

Attached to the hook was a small, metallic object. A key.

But not a normal key. It was made of a material that looked like frozen smoke. It hummed with a low, dissonant vibration.

Gerald held it up. The light from his Core reflected off its surface, fracturing into rainbow shards.

He had never seen this item before.

A system message popped up.

*Item Acquired: Key to the Restricted Zone.*

*Requirement: Compliance Rating 55.*

*Current Rating: 52.*

*Warning: Entering the Restricted Zone without sufficient compliance may result in immediate core fragmentation.*

Gerald stared at the key. He stared at the message.

The Consortium was coming back. With more people.

With better tools, and they would not be deterred by noise ordinances or fire lanes next time. They would bring demolition crews. They would bring lawyers.

They would bring the System's own enforcers if they could manipulate the paperwork.

Gerald needed more power. Not combat power. Regulatory power.

He needed to reach 55, and he looked around his dungeon. It was clean. It was safe. It was compliant.

But it was not enough.

He looked at the key. It pointed toward the north wall. Toward the part of the dungeon that bordered the Library's main reading room.

The Restricted Zone.

No Core entered the Restricted Zone, which was a myth.

A rumor among the younger Cores. They said it was where the System stored the original templates.

The blueprint for the apocalypse. The source code of the apocalypse.

Gerald did not believe in myths. He believed in footnotes.

He pulled out a fresh sheet of paper, and he drafted a new citation.

*Violation: Failure to Maintain Adequate Security Perimeter.*

*Penalty: Immediate Access Grant.*

*Applicability: Self.*

It was a loophole. A small, technical, bureaucratic loophole. If he could prove that the lack of access to the Restricted Zone was a security flaw in his own dungeon's design, he could cite himself for it. And the System, bound by the rules of self-citation, would have to grant him access to fix the violation.

It was illegal. Or at least, it was irregular.

But Gerald Hutchins had spent his life finding irregularities, and he wrote the citation. He signed it. He stamped it with his Core's seal.

He held the paper up to the north wall.

The wall shimmered. The concrete rippled like water.

A door opened.

Behind it was not a room. It was a void. A darkness so deep it swallowed the light from Gerald's Core.

And in the darkness, something whispered.

"Insufficient rating," the voice said. It was not the System's voice. It was older. Deeper.

Gerald tightened his grip on the clipboard.

"Then grant me the temporary waiver," Gerald said.

"Illegal."

"Code 99-O," Gerald said. "Emergency Structural Repair. Waiver granted for life-safety issues."

"Your life is not at risk."

"My dungeon is at risk," Gerald said. "And if my dungeon falls, the Library falls.

And if the Library falls, the city's power grid fails, which is a life-safety issue. Cite the city. Cite the grid.

Cite the power company.

"

The voice paused. The darkness churned.

"You are playing with fire," the voice said.

"I am playing with code," Gerald said.

The darkness parted. A staircase appeared. It spiraled down into the abyss.

Gerald looked at the key in his hand. He looked at the citation in his other hand.

He looked at the timer on his dashboard.

23 hours, 7 minutes, and he had twenty-three hours to survive the Consortium. He had zero minutes to figure out what lay at the bottom of the stairs.

He took the first step down.

The stairs were cold. The air smelled of sulfur and old blood.

At the bottom, a figure waited. It was not a monster. It was not a goblin.

It was a man, and he was wearing a suit. It was tailored, perfect. His face was obscured by a mask of shifting runes.

"Inspector Hutchins," the man said. His voice was smooth, oily. "Welcome to the Basement of the World."

Gerald looked up. He did not flinch. He did not tremble.

"Name," Gerald said.

The man tilted his head.

"You do not know who I am?"

"I do not care who you are," Gerald said. "I care about your permit. Do you have a permit to be in a non-public area?"

The man laughed. It was a terrible sound, like glass breaking.

"I am the Architect," the man said. "I wrote the code you cite."

Gerald clicked his pen.

"Then you have made a mistake," Gerald said. "And I am going to correct it."

The Architect raised a hand. The darkness surged forward, forming into tendrils of black smoke.

Gerald did not run. He did not fight.

He raised his clipboard, and he held it like a shield.

And he read.

"Violation: Unauthorized Access to Sub-Level 9," Gerald said. "Penalty: Immediate Banishment."

The smoke stopped.

The Architect froze.

"That is not a valid citation," the Architect said. "I am not a Core."

"No," Gerald said. "But you are a presence. And presence requires registration. And you are not registered."

The Architect's mask flickered. For a second, Gerald saw a face beneath it. A face that was not human. A face that was made of lines and angles and infinite depth.

"You are testing the limits of the System," the Architect said.

"I am using the System," Gerald corrected. "As intended."

The Architect lowered his hand. The smoke receded.

"Very well," the Architect said. "You have passed the first test.

But there are deeper violations here.

Deeper sins. If you go further, you will not survive.

"

"I don't need to survive," Gerald said. "I need to be compliant."

The Architect smiled. It was a thin, cruel smile.

"Then come down," he said. "And try to cite God."
