Chapter 137: Egress Route Violation

# Chapter 137

Gerald pressed his nonexistent feet against the tilting floor, measuring the slide in inches. It was leaning.

Thirty degrees off vertical. That was the math.

Gerald Hutchins didn't see it with his eyes, and he felt it through the soles of his new, nonexistent feet. The earth beneath the Ashford Municipal Library groaned, a low-frequency vibration that rattled the teeth of every living thing above ground.

To the adventurers screaming in the corridor, it was an earthquake.

To Gerald, it was a code violation.

Specifically, Section 402.1: Improper Lateral Support in High-Occupancy Subterranean Structures.

He tried to stabilize the load. He focused on the main support pillar, a massive column of fused bedrock and ancient masonry that now formed the spine of his expanded body. He pushed back against the tilt, pouring his will into the stone. It felt like trying to hold up a skyscraper with a wet napkin.

The pillar cracked.

Not a hairline fracture. A jagged, angry split that widened by six inches in a second.

Above him, the screaming changed pitch. It went from terror to the wet, crunching sound of things being crushed.

Gerald pulled back, and he couldn't fix the pillar. Not yet.

The damage was too deep, woven into the bedrock layers he had only just absorbed.

Trying to force it would snap the connection. It would kill him. And if he died, the city lost its structural integrity.

The library collapsed. The sewer mains burst.

The whole district sank into the sinkhole Thomas had created before dissolving.

He had to redirect the load, and he needed a temporary brace. A shoring wall.

But he had no materials. He had no hands. He was water, root, and concrete.

"System," Gerald said. His voice wasn't sound; it was pressure. "Status report."

**"Structural Integrity: 14%."**

**"Critical Failure Imminent: 4 Minutes."**

**"Available Resources: Insufficient."**

Four minutes.

Gerald scanned his new territory. The sewer tunnels. The basement walls. The library foundation.

Everything was failing.

Then, he saw it.

A patch of red. Bright, angry red.

Not blood.

Paint.

Deep in the adjacent tunnel, the one belonging to Malacor the Defiler.

Malacor's dungeon had always been messy. Sloppy geometry.

Exposed wiring. Rotting wood.

But now, that tunnel was leaning too.

Malacor's structural core was failing in sympathy with Gerald's. The two dungeons were physically touching. Sharing a wall.

Gerald stopped thinking about saving himself. He started thinking about liability.

Malacor's tunnel was the problem. It was pulling Gerald's structure down. If Gerald could isolate the shared wall, he could offload the weight.

But he couldn't build a wall.

Unless he used a Citation.

Not a combat citation. A structural citation.

Gerald remembered the rule. *Citations can be appealed by the target Core... triggering a Hearing.*

He hadn't used that mechanic in weeks. He had been too busy fighting, too busy expanding. But the rules hadn't changed.

If he cited Malacor's tunnel for a violation, the System would intervene. It would pause the physical collapse in that specific zone to process the paperwork.

It wouldn't save Malacor. It would freeze his decay.

And if Gerald could freeze the decay on Malacor's side, he could borrow that stability. He could use Malacor's half-failed wall as a buttress for his own.

It was theft. It was bureaucratic arson.

It was the only option.

Gerald focused. He didn't think about power.

He thought about the clipboard, and he remembered the smell of old paper. The snap of a ballpoint pen.

**"Drafting Citation..."**

The words burned in his mind.

**"Target: Sub-Level 4, Tunnel B (Malacor Core)."**

**"Violation: Critical Shear Failure in Shared Partition."**

**"Code Reference: Municipal Ordinance 12-B, Subsection 4: Adjoining Property Support Requirements."**

**"Severity: Immediate Danger to Life."**

**"Action: Temporary Structural Seizure."**

The System hesitated.

**"Target is Hostile."**

**"Citation Requires Service."**

Gerald pushed harder. He flooded the shared wall with his consciousness. He touched Malacor's water. It was slimy, cold, and tasted of mold and hate.

He didn't care about the taste, and he served the citation.

He didn't send a ghost. He didn't send a minion. He sent the *intent*. He imposed the legal reality onto the physical reality.

*You are violating code. Stop moving.*

Malacor's tunnel shuddered.

A new window appeared in Gerald's view. Not a status screen. A courtroom.

**"HEARING INITIATED."**

**"Appellant: Gerald Hutchins (Core ID 001)."**

**"Respondent: Malacor the Defiler (Core ID 449)."**

**"Issue: Emergency Structural Seizure."**

**"Time Limit: 3 Minutes."**

The floor above groaned again. The ceiling of Gerald's domain buckled. Dust rained down like snow.

**"Two Minutes."**

Malacor didn't have a human avatar, and he had never bothered to create one. He was a swarm of slime and shadow.

But the System forced a representation.

A jagged, black shape formed in the virtual courtroom. It looked like a stain on the lens of a camera.

"You steal my ground," Malacor's voice boomed. It wasn't speech. It was vibration.

"You're pulling my foundation into yours," Gerald replied. He didn't raise his voice.

He kept it flat.

Professional.

"Section 12-B. You are required to maintain independent support structures within ten feet of any shared boundary.

You failed to do so. I am citing you for endangering my infrastructure.

"

"I am stronger than you."

"Strength is not a defense to code violation. Compliance is mandatory."

Malacor's black shape pulsed. It tried to crush the hearing. Tried to overwhelm the System's arbitration with raw mana.

The System ignored it.

The System cared about the form.

Gerald checked his paperwork. He had cited the specific ordinance. He had identified the exact location of the shear failure. He had declared the emergency.

He had followed procedure.

Malacor hadn't.

Malacor's dungeon had no permits for the expansion into the sewer district. He had just dug. He had just taken.

Gerald pointed at the empty space in the filing.

"No permit," Gerald said. "No structural review.

No impact study.

You expanded into a load-bearing zone without authorization. That is why your wall is failing. That is why you are pulling me down.

"

The black shape flickered.

"Then fix it."

"I can't fix your wall. I can only stabilize my side using your existing structure. That is what the Citation allows. Temporary seizure of non-compliant assets to preserve public safety."

"Public safety?" Malacor laughed, a sound like grinding stones. "You are a dungeon."

"I am a municipal entity. Under the new charter."

Gerald didn't have the charter. He was bluffing. But the System liked bluffs if they looked like facts.

The System paused.

Then, it chimed.

**"Violation Confirmed."**

**"Malacor Core is non-compliant with Ordinance 12-B."**

**"Action: Immediate Stabilization Authorized."**

The black shape screamed.

It wasn't a scream of pain. It was a scream of loss.

The System reached into Malacor's dungeon. It didn't attack his monsters. It didn't burn his traps.

It froze his wall.

The section of tunnel that adjoined Gerald's domain turned to gray stone.

It stopped vibrating. It stopped leaning.

It became rigid.

It became *compliant*.

And Gerald grabbed it, and he poured his own failing structure into that newly rigid section. He used Malacor's frozen wall as a kickstand.

The tilt in Gerald's main hall slowed.

The cracking pillar stopped widening.

Above, the screaming adventurers stopped running. They looked at the floor. It was still slanted, but it wasn't moving.

**"Structural Integrity: 22%."**

**"Temporary Support Active."**

**"Citation Valid for 24 Hours."**

Gerald exhaled. He didn't have lungs, but the motion of relief was there.

He had bought time.

But he hadn't won.

The hearing window closed. Malacor's form dissolved, but the anger remained. A message appeared in Gerald's log.

**"Appeal Filed by Malacor."**

**"Hearing Scheduled: 10 Minutes."**

Gerald stared at it.

Malacor was appealing.

Malacor had lost the emergency citation. But he was appealing the *seizure*. He was arguing that Gerald's use of his wall was theft, not safety.

If Malacor won the appeal, the System would reverse the stabilization. The wall would unfreeze. The lean would return. And Gerald's pillar would snap.

Ten minutes.

Gerald needed to prepare for the appeal. He needed to prove that the seizure was not theft. He needed to prove it was necessary.

He needed evidence, and he needed to show the System that Malacor's violation was so severe that Gerald had no choice.

He needed to look deeper.

Gerald shifted his focus away from the courtroom and back into the physical dungeon. He extended his senses into the frozen wall. He looked at the stone that now held him up.

It wasn't just stone.

Under the gray surface, he saw veins of dark energy. Purple. Pulsing.

Malacor's dungeon wasn't just poorly built. It was actively corrupting the bedrock. It was draining the mana from the earth to fuel its growth.

That was why the wall was failing. Not because of poor engineering. Because of mana starvation.

Gerald hadn't cited Malacor for bad engineering. He had cited him for bad zoning.

If he could prove that Malacor's zoning violation had caused the *physical* damage, he might win the appeal. Or he might lose it and get fined.

But he had seen something else.

In the veins of dark energy, there was a signature.

It wasn't Malacor's, which was familiar.

Cold. Blue. Sterile.

It looked like the Inspector General's energy.

Gerald froze, and the Inspector General was gone. Thomas had killed him. Or Thomas had become him.

But this energy was still here. Still feeding Malacor.

Someone else was behind Malacor.

Someone was using Malacor to destabilize Gerald. To force Gerald into a corner. To make Gerald break code and destroy the city.

Gerald looked at the frozen wall.

The blue veins pulsed in rhythm with his own heartbeat, and he realized the truth.

Malacor wasn't the rival.

Malacor was a puppet.

And the puppet master was watching.

The next window opened.

**"Appeal Hearing Begins in: 9 Minutes."**

**"New Evidence Found: Unauthorized Mana Drain."**

**"Action: Inspect Source."**

Gerald didn't have time to inspect the source. He had to argue the appeal.

But he knew one thing.

If he argued the truth—that the Inspector General was still active, still pulling strings—the System might believe him. Or it might deem him unstable.

If he argued the lie—that Malacor was just clumsy—the System might accept it.

But the lie wouldn't stop the collapse forever, and he needed a third option.

He needed to change the venue.

Gerald looked at the System interface. He looked at the

"Hearing" tab.

He couldn't change the venue.

But he could change the *plaintiff*.

If he could get a third party involved, the appeal would require that party's consent.

The library.

The library belonged to the City of Ashford.

Gerald was a municipal core. The library was his landlord.

He could file a cross-motion.

He could argue that the City of Ashford had a vested interest in the stability of the library, and that Malacor's actions constituted an attack on city property.

The City wasn't a Core. It couldn't appeal.

But the *City* could sue.

And the System had a module for

"Civil Disputes Between Non-Core Entities."

It was obscure. It was rarely used. It was buried in the footnotes of the manual.

Gerald had read the manual. All twelve thousand pages. He had read it in the prison cell where he had transformed.

He remembered the footnote.

*Section 884: In the event of property damage caused by Core activity, the affected property owner may intervene in Core Appeals to protect their asset.*

The property owner was the City of Ashford.

Gerald didn't have a lawyer. He didn't have a mayor.

He had a pen, and he had a stamp.

He had a form.

**"Form 99-Z: Third-Party Intervention Request."**

**"Filed by: Gerald Hutchins, Acting Agent for the Municipality."**

**"Status: Pending."**

The countdown ticked down.

**"Appeal Hearing Begins in: 5 Minutes."**

Gerald held his breath. He held his water. He held his roots.

He waited for the System to accept the form, which was a gamble. A massive, stupid, bureaucratic gamble.

If the System rejected the form, Gerald lost. The wall would unfreeze. The city would fall.

If the System accepted the form, the hearing would change. It would become a three-way dispute.

And three-way disputes required a judge, and the Inspector General was the judge.

But the Inspector General was gone.

So who would judge?

The System would default to the highest-ranking active Core.

Gerald was the highest-ranking active Core, and he would be the judge of his own appeal.

If he could get the City involved, he could rule in his own favor. He could declare that the City's property was damaged, and therefore the seizure of Malacor's wall was justified as protective custody of city assets.

It was a conflict of interest.

It was illegal, which was the only way.

Gerald stared at the timer.

**"4 Minutes."**

He felt the blue veins in the wall pulse again.

They were waiting for him to fail, and he picked up the bone pen.

He signed the form.

He stamped it.

**"Submitted."**

The System paused.

**"Reviewing Third-Party Claim."**

**"Claimant: City of Ashford."**

**"Representative: Gerald Hutchins."**

**"Validity Check: In Progress."**

**"3 Minutes."**

Gerald looked up.

Through the earth.

Through the concrete, and he could feel the Mayor's office. The Mayor was asleep. The Mayor didn't care about the library. The Mayor cared about the budget.

But the System didn't care about the Mayor's feelings. It cared about the title.

Gerald was the Inspector. The Inspector represented the City.

It was a technicality.

A fragile, paper-thin technicality.

**"2 Minutes."**

The blue veins pulsed faster. They were heating up. The energy drain was accelerating. Malacor was trying to break the freeze before the hearing started.

Gerald could feel the wall straining.

**"1 Minute."**

The System chimed.

Not the hearing chime.

A different chime.

Lower. Darker.

**"Third-Party Claim Accepted."**

**"Venue Change: Municipal Court."**

**"Presiding Officer: Gerald Hutchins."**

Gerald smiled. It was a dry, cracked smile.

He was the judge. He was the plaintiff. He was the defendant.

And he was the building.

The window changed.

It wasn't a courtroom anymore, which was a desk. A simple wooden desk.

And on the desk was a file.

The file wasn't for Malacor.

It was for Gerald.

**"Audit Initiated: Self-Review."**

**"Reason: Conflict of Interest."**

**"Penalty: Immediate Suspension of Core Functions Pending Review."**

Gerald's smile died, and he had won the venue.

But he had lost the game.

The System was auditing him. Right now.

His water stopped flowing.

His roots stopped growing, and the support he had borrowed from Malacor's wall faded.

The tilt returned.

The ceiling groaned.

**"Suspension in Effect: 30 Seconds."**

Thirty seconds, which was all he had.

After that, he was powerless.

And in those thirty seconds, he had to do something that wasn't in the code.

Something illegal.

Something stupid, and he looked at the file on the desk.

He looked at the countdown.

He looked at the blue veins in the wall.

He reached out with his mind. Not to the wall.

To the adventurers.

To the screaming humans running through the library above, and they had weapons. They had magic. They had panic.

They didn't have a building inspector.

But they could hold a beam.

Gerald projected his voice. Not through the earth. Through the air vents.

"Help!"

It wasn't dignified. It wasn't professional.

"Help!"

The adventurers stopped, and they looked at the vents.

"Help!" Gerald screamed again. "Hold the door! Hold it closed!"

They didn't know what he meant.

They didn't know that the

"door" was a load-bearing partition he had just cited into existence.

They just heard the monster crying.

And for the first time, they didn't run, and they rushed to the nearest heavy oak door in the library hallway.

They pushed it shut.

They braced it with their bodies.

They didn't know they were saving the city.

They just thought they were keeping the monster in.

Gerald felt their weight.

Human weight.

Small. Fragile.

But combined?

Enough to tip the scale.

The System watched.

The Audit paused.

**"External Intervention Detected."**

**"Evaluating..."**

The blue veins stopped pulsing.

The wall held.

For one second, it held.

Then, the System finished its evaluation.

**"Audit Result: Core Gerald Hutchins is found Guilty of Conflict of Interest."**

**"Sentence: Demotion to Tier 1."**

Gerald felt his power vanish.

The water in his veins went cold.

The roots withered, and he was shrinking.

Back to the sub-basement.

Back to 4,000 square feet.

Malacor's wall unfroze.

The tilt returned, worse than before.

The city above cracked.

And Gerald Hutchins, stripped of his powers, stripped of his code, stripped of his title, looked at the file on his desk.

It wasn't an audit file anymore, which was a warrant.

**"Arrest Warrant Issued."**

**"Target: Gerald Hutchins."**

**"Authority: Inspector General (Restored)."**

Gerald looked up, and the ceiling was gone.

Above him, standing on the edge of the pit, was a figure in a white suit.

Clean.

Bright.

Smiling.

The Inspector General.

Or someone wearing his face.

"Gerry," the figure said. "We need to talk about your egress routes."
