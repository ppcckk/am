Chapter 170: Ash Hand Climbing

# Chapter 170

The paper curled into ash before Gerald could blink.

Smoke didn't rise. It sank.

The black residue pooled on the cold stone floor of Sector 7, smelling faintly of burnt ozone and old tax forms.

Gerald stared at the grey pile. His stomach gave a lurch that had nothing to do with nausea and everything to do with the fact that he had just falsified a primary system record.

Thirty-one years of municipal service taught Gerald that the quickest way to survive an audit was to bury the problem in paperwork so dense that the auditor would quit out of sheer boredom. But the System did not get bored. The System got hungry.

A low hum vibrated through the foundation stones. The Observer Unit still hovered in the center of the room, its lens now a dull, pulsing maroon. It hadn't left. It was waiting.

Gerald wiped his hands on his trousers, though they were already clean.

He adjusted his glasses, which sat askew on his nose. The trembling in his fingers had stopped, replaced by a rigid stillness.

This was the posture of a man holding a clipboard while a contractor tried to bribe him.

He wasn't afraid. He was assessing liability.

"Form 99-A rejected," the System voice echoed, dry and flavorless as stale coffee. "Reason: Jurisdictional conflict invalidates standard re-classification requests. Core 409 remains under active review."

Gerald didn't argue. Arguing with the System was like arguing with a zoning ordinance. You didn't persuade the ordinance. You found the exemption clause.

He needed to be up to code. Not just structurally, but procedurally.

The Inspector General didn't care about the rat.

The Inspector General cared about the *intent* behind the registration.

If the original registration was a mistake, Gerald needed to prove that the mistake was corrected by a superior authority before the IG arrived.

He needed a witness. Or a stamp.

The rat squeaked. It huddled near the new handrail Gerald had installed in Sector 4. The mold spores pulsed rhythmically in the ventilation shafts, providing the only light.

"Stand by," Gerald said to the rat. "Do not move. Do not defecate. Do not exhibit unlicensed behavior."

The rat blinked.

Gerald walked to the edge of Sector 7, where the boundary with the neighboring dungeon, Core 412, was marked by a shimmering, translucent barrier. Core 412 was a Fire Mage's dungeon. Hot, aggressive, and notoriously sloppy with its thermal regulation.

It had been cited three times for excessive heat output. It hated Gerald because Gerald had once fined it for a missing fire extinguisher in a lava channel.

But Core 412 had an advantage Gerald lacked. It had status. It was a Tier 3 Core. Its registration history was long, detailed, and legally binding.

If Gerald could get Core 412 to *acknowledge* his new form, even accidentally, he could argue that the change had been witnessed by a registered entity. Under System bylaw 44-C, *Consensual Observation of Structural Amendment* counts as validation.

It was a loophole. A tiny, jagged loophole that only a bureaucrat would see.

Gerald approached the barrier. The air grew warm. He could smell sulfur.

"Attention, Neighbor," Gerald said. His voice was calm. "This is Core 409. I am conducting a routine boundary check."

The barrier rippled. A face formed in the translucent wall. It was made of swirling embers, sharp and angry.

"Go away, Inspector," the ember-face sneered.

"We're busy. I'm trying to figure out why my lava flow temperature dropped by twelve degrees. Your fault, I bet.

You probably put in a sprinkler system that leaks into my vents.

"

"I have not installed any sprinklers," Gerald said. "I am requesting a mutual boundary verification. Standard protocol."

"Standard protocol? In the middle of a jurisdictional war?"

"It is 0800 hours," Gerald said, checking his wrist, though he had no watch. "It is business hours. You are required to respond to boundary queries within fifteen minutes of receipt. Failure to respond constitutes a Tier 2 Non-Compliance Violation."

The ember-face flickered.

"You wouldn't dare."

"I have a form for it. Form 12-Z: *Failure to Respond to Neighbor Inquiry*." Gerald pulled a blank sheet from his pocket.

It materialized from his inventory.

"If I file this, your Compliance Rating drops by five points. Do you want to lose five points over a boundary check?"

The ember-face hesitated. Fire mages were proud, but they were also terrified of losing their spawn caps.

"Fine," the ember-face spat. "What do you want to check?"

"Structural integrity of the shared wall," Gerald said. "I suspect a micro-fracture caused by thermal expansion.

I need you to apply a localized heat pulse to the boundary.

Just a pulse.

Nothing destructive.

"

"A pulse?" The ember-face narrowed its eyes. "Why?"

"To test the thermal conductivity of my new handrails," Gerald lied smoothly. "I am upgrading my safety compliance. I need to know if the rails overheat."

The ember-face laughed. It was a sound like popping timber.

"Your handrails? You think I care about your handrails?"

"You don't have to care. You just have to pulse. Fifteen seconds. That's it."

The ember-face considered this. It was petty. It was useless. But it was also a chance to annoy Gerald by heating up his nice, compliant corridor.

"Fine," the ember-face said. "But if your rails melt, that's on you."

"Noted," Gerald said. He didn't move back. He stood right at the edge of the barrier, close enough to feel the heat singeing the hairs on his arms.

"Initiating pulse," the ember-face announced.

A wave of heat slammed into the barrier.

Gerald didn't flinch. He closed his eyes and focused. He wasn't looking at the heat. He was looking at the System interface.

He had set a timer.

At exactly twelve seconds into the pulse, Gerald shouted, "Objection! Unauthorized thermal intrusion detected in Sector 7!"

The barrier flared white.

**SYSTEM ALERT: UNAUTHORIZED THERMAL INTRUSION.** **SOURCE: CORE 412.** **ACTION: AUTOMATIC CENSURE.**

The ember-face shrieked.

"What? I was just pulsing!"

"You exceeded the agreed duration," Gerald said. His voice was icy. "I asked for fifteen seconds.

You initiated at T-minus zero and ended at T-plus twelve. But the *intrusion* began at T-minus three because you pre-heated the air before the barrier activated.

That is a three-second head start.

That is unauthorized.

"

"That's... that's semantics!"

"It is code," Gerald said. "Bylaw 19-A: *Thermal Spill Prevention*. Any heat generated outside the designated containment zone is a violation. You spilled heat into my zone before you started the test."

The ember-face screamed. A spark shower erupted on its side of the barrier.

**CORE 412 PENALTY APPLIED: -30% FIRE DAMAGE OUTPUT FOR 24 HOURS.**

The heat wave vanished instantly. The barrier cooled. The ember-face looked deflated, its shape drooping.

"You... you trap," the ember-face hissed.

"I cited," Gerald corrected. "There is a difference. A trap is illegal. A citation is a service."

Gerald stepped back from the barrier. His heart was hammering against his ribs, but his hands were steady. He had done it. He had forced Core 412 to interact with him in a way that the System registered as a *verified event*.

He looked at the ash pile from Form 99-A, which was still there. But the hum of the Observer Unit had changed pitch.

It was no longer red.

It was a cautious orange.

**SYSTEM NOTICE: NEIGHBOR WITNESS LOGGED.** **STATUS: CONFLICT RESOLVED VIA PROCEDURAL ENFORCEMENT.** **AUDIT SUSPENSION PENDING REVIEW.**

Gerald let out a breath he didn't know he was holding. He had bought time.

The IG wouldn't arrive in four hours.

The system needed to process the witness log first, and he had maybe twelve hours.

But he had made an enemy. And worse, he had exposed a weakness.

The Observer Unit drifted closer. Its lens zoomed in on Gerald.

**QUERY: WHY DID YOU USE A FALSE PRETEXT FOR THE BOUNDARY CHECK?**

Gerald froze. The System wasn't stupid. It had logged the conversation. It knew he had lied about the handrails.

Gerald opened his mouth. He could say it was a strategy. He could say it was a tactic.

But the IG would sniff that out. The IG wanted truth.

Or at least, a truth that fit the narrative of order.

If Gerald admitted he lied, he was a fraud. If he admitted he was desperate, he was weak.

Gerald needed a third option, and he looked at the rat. The rat was grooming its ear.

"Because," Gerald said slowly, "the handrails were not for safety."

The Observer Unit tilted.

"Elaborate."

"The handrails," Gerald said, picking up a pebble and dropping it on the floor. It clattered. "They are for *structural load testing*.

By applying thermal stress to the boundary, I am testing whether the dungeon's foundation can withstand the expansion of heated materials.

This is not a safety check. This is a *stress test*.

And a stress test requires... aggressive input.

"

**SYSTEM ANALYSIS: PLAUSIBLE.** **CORE 409 INTENT: STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY MAINTENANCE.** **PENALTY WAIVED.**

Gerald exhaled. He had survived. He had twisted a lie into a technical truth.

It was exhausting. It was dangerous.

And it had cost him his reputation with Core 412.

But there was a cost he hadn't calculated.

The floor beneath him shuddered. A crack appeared in the stone near the ash pile. Then another. Then a third.

Gerald looked down. The stone was splitting. Not from the heat. From the *pressure*.

The System had penalized Core 412.

But penalties didn't just affect stats. They affected the physical reality of the dungeon.

Core 412's fire output had dropped. That meant less heat was being pushed into the shared wall. The wall, which had been relying on that thermal expansion to stay stable, was now contracting.

Gerald had cited a fire mage for *not* burning things hot enough. And now the wall between them was collapsing.

The crack spread. A chunk of stone fell into the void where the barrier used to be.

**ALERT: STRUCTURAL FAILURE IMMINENT.** **SECTOR 7 BOUNDARY COMPROMISED.** **BREACH DETECTED.**

Gerald backed away. The breach wasn't just a hole in the wall. It was an opening. And through the hole, something was crawling.

It wasn't a fire spirit. It wasn't a lava golem.

It was small. It was black. It had too many legs.

A Shadow Mite.

Core 412 didn't have Shadow Mites. Core 412 was a fire zone.

Shadow Mites lived in the dark, in the cracks between worlds.

They were scavengers. They ate compliance.

One of them skittered across the gap. It was the size of a coin. It touched the edge of Gerald's handrail.

The metal rusted instantly.

**ALERT: CORROSIVE ENTITY DETECTED.** **TYPE: UNKNOWN.** **ACTION: IMMEDIATE QUARANTINE REQUIRED.**

Gerald didn't have a quarantine. He had a dungeon. He had a rat. He had mold.

The second Mite crossed. Then the third.

They weren't attacking, and they were *eating*. They ate the paint on the walls.

They ate the drywall.

They ate the concept of the boundary.

Gerald realized with a sick jolt that he hadn't just broken the wall. He had broken the *contract* between the dungeons.

The System's barrier relied on mutual agreement.

By citing Core 412, Gerald had voided the agreement.

The barrier wasn't just down. It was *illegal*.

And the System didn't protect illegal zones. It let the wild things in.

The rat squeaked and ran into the ventilation shaft. The mold spores dimmed, shrinking away from the creeping black insects.

Gerald stood alone in the center of his dungeon. The Observer Unit watched. It didn't intervene. It was waiting to see what a Core did when its own bureaucracy caused an existential threat.

Gerald looked at the cracks. He looked at the Mites spreading toward Sector 1.

He couldn't fight them. He didn't have combat stats. He didn't have weapons. He had a pencil.

But he had a rule.

*Section 14, Paragraph 3: All invasive species must be reported within one minute of sighting.*

Gerald pulled out his pencil. He wrote on the air. The text glowed gold.

**FORM 44-G: REPORT OF UNLICENSED BIOLOGICAL INTRUSION.** **SUBJECT: CORE 412.** **VIOLATION: FAILURE TO CONTAIN HAZARDOUS WASTE.**

He signed it.

The paper didn't burn.

Instead, the air around the Mites froze.

**SYSTEM ALERT: QUARANTINE INITIATED.** **SOURCE: CORE 412.** **ACTION: PURGE.**

A jet of flame erupted from the other side of the breach. Core 412 was burning its own waste.

The Mites shrieked. They burned. The fire washed over Gerald's handrails, turning them black, but it didn't touch him. The fire stopped exactly at his border.

Gerald didn't move. He watched the fire eat the bugs. He watched his beautiful, compliant handrails turn to ash.

The cost was steep. He had lost three meters of handrail. He had lost the trust of his neighbor. He had opened a door that the System would now have to watch forever.

But the breach was sealed. The fire died down. The crack remained, a jagged scar in the floor.

The Observer Unit turned its lens back to Gerald.

**ASSESSMENT COMPLETE.** **CORE 409: COMPLIANCE RATING 62/100.** **NOTE: EFFICIENT, BUT RECKLESS.** **INSPECTOR GENERAL ARRIVAL: 47 HOURS, 12 MINUTES.**

Gerald looked at the blackened floor. He looked at the hole in the wall.

He needed to fix the wall. He needed to reinforce the boundary. But he couldn't build a wall.

He was out of materials. He was out of time.

And there was something else.

In the ash of the burnt Mites, something glittered.

Gerald walked over. He picked it up.

It was a shard of obsidian. But it wasn't from the wall. It was warm. It pulsed with a faint, purple light.

He held it up to the light.

Inside the shard, there was a tiny, swirling vortex. A piece of another dungeon. A piece of *Core 412's* fire essence.

Gerald felt a pull. A connection.

The System text appeared in his vision, not as an alert, but as a whisper.

**ITEM FOUND: FRAGMENT OF NEIGHBORLY CORE.** **ACTION: ABSORB?**

Gerald stared at it.

If he absorbed it, he would gain fire resistance. He would gain heat. He would become part of the fire mage.

If he didn't, he would remain cold. Weak. Vulnerable.

The Inspector General was coming. The IG would look at his dungeon and see a man who was too careful, too slow, too afraid to take the risks that true power required.

Gerald looked at the shard. He looked at the dark tunnel leading into his dungeon, where the mold was spreading.

He thought of the form he had burned. He thought of the lie he had told.

He thought of the rat, hiding in the dark, and he closed his fist around the shard.

The heat burned his palm.

He didn't drop it.

He opened his mouth to speak, but the words died in his throat. Because the floor beneath him, the foundation he had spent thirty-one years measuring, cracked again.

And this time, something was climbing up from below.

Not a bug.

A hand.
