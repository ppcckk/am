Chapter 116: Cited the Police

# Chapter 116 The ceiling was peeling. Not like old paint, but like wet tissue paper dissolving under a stream of acid.

Gerald Hutchins watched a slab of concrete detach itself from the support beam and crash into the mold spore colony in sector four. The spores did not scream. They simply stopped glowing. Their regenerative properties, already nerfed by the lack of ventilation, had no defense against structural annihilation.

*Structural Decay. Duration: 10 Minutes.*

The red text burned in his peripheral vision, a constant, pulsing reminder that his body was currently undergoing a demolition order. He could feel it in the phantom limbs of his dungeon core, and a tearing sensation in the lower left quadrant. A grinding friction where the main hallway used to be smooth stone.

He had won the hearing. He had paused the execution. And the System, in its infinite bureaucratic cruelty, had punished him for the ambiguity of the venue.

*Unauthorized Cross-Reality Contamination.*

Gerald’s hands, which were not hands but projections of will, flew through menus that flickered and glitched. The interface was lagging.

The red warning overlay made the blue text unreadable, and he tried to access the Maintenance Tab. The icon was grayed out. Locked.

He tried to spawn a repair golem. The spawn cost skyrocketed. The penalty for decay applied a 400% markup on all construction materials for the duration of the effect.

He had ten minutes.

His dungeon was a 4,000-square-foot sub-basement beneath the Ashford Municipal Library. If it rotted down to its foundation, he would be deregistered.

Not killed.

Deregistered. Erased from the System’s active registry. A null value.

Gerald closed his eyes. He breathed. It was a reflex he no longer possessed, but the psychological anchor remained.

*Think like an inspector. Not a hero. An inspector.*

When a building is condemned, you do not fight the condemnation. You file an appeal. You find the code section that prevents the demolition.

He scanned the *Structural Decay* notification again. It was precise.

It cited the penalty.

It cited the source.

But it did not cite the specific *method* of decay, which was the loophole.

Gerald pulled up the *Ashford Municipal Building Code, Section 8: Emergency Structural Integrity*, and the System’s underlying logic was built on real-world municipal law, twisted to fit magical metaphors. He knew that code better than he knew his own children’s names.

He searched for "Immediate Demolition."

There was no such term. The System used "Structural Decay."

He searched for "Temporary Incapacitation of Structure."

Nothing.

He searched for "Deferment of Liability Pending Audit."

There.

Subsection 4.2.1: *In the event of suspected unauthorized modification to a load-bearing entity, immediate demolition is prohibited if a qualified inspector requests a 24-hour Hold Order to verify the extent of damage. The requester must provide physical evidence of the modification’s origin within the holding period.*

Gerald stared at the text. It was buried in the fine print, formatted in a font size that suggested the System’s developers had a dark sense of humor.

He had to serve a Hold Order.

But the System had labeled him the *source* of the contamination. It considered him guilty until proven innocent. Usually, this meant he could not act against himself.

Unless he changed the venue.

The water at his feet was black now. It bubbled, eating into the floor tiles. The smell of sulfur and rotting wood filled the air. A chunk of the eastern wall collapsed, raining dust onto the confused rat that scurried away from the debris.

Gerald looked at the rat. He looked at the black water.

He looked at the notification. *Source: Core ID 8940-G.*

He was the source. He could not inspect himself.

But he could inspect the *contaminant*.

The black water. It was not just water. It was residual mana from Vane’s reality, leaking through the dimensional tear created during the hearing.

It was foreign material. Unlicensed biological and magical waste.

It was a violation.

Gerald opened his *Citation* interface.

His fingers trembled. The lag was making it hard to select options. He focused.

He had to be precise.

If he missed, the hold order would not take, and the decay would continue.

He targeted the puddle of black water.

*Violation: Unlicensed Cross-Reality Waste Disposal.* *Code Section 12.4: Improper Disposal of Hazardous Materials in Non-Industrial Zones.*

He drafted the citation.

*TO: The Contaminant (Physical Entity).* *FROM: Core ID 8940-G (Gerald Hutchins), Licensed Dungeon Core.* *NOTICE: You are hereby cited for improper disposal of hazardous materials. You must vacate the premises immediately. Failure to comply will result in forced expulsion.*

It was absurd. He was citing a puddle.

But the System loved absurdity if it followed the rules.

The citation appeared as a red stamp in the air above the water.

*Serve the Citation.*

Gerald lunged, and he did not have legs. He had spatial influence. He pushed his awareness down, through the rotting floor, into the black sludge.

It burned. The sensation was akin to touching a live wire wrapped in ice.

He slammed the virtual stamp onto the water.

*Citation Served.*

The System paused.

For a second, nothing happened. The decay continued. A support column in the north corridor cracked.

Then, the red text of the *Structural Decay* notification flickered.

*Notification: Citation Received by Local Environment. Processing...*

The black water boiled. Not with heat, but with rejection; the System was trying to expel a foreign entity that had been cited for trespassing.

The decay stopped.

The peeling ceiling halted. The grinding friction in his core subsided, replaced by a dull ache.

*Hold Order Granted. Duration: 24 Hours. Reason: Pending Removal of Cited Hazardous Material.*

Gerald slumped against the only remaining upright wall, and he gasped, a dry, ragged sound that echoed in the damp air.

He had bought time.

But he had not fixed the problem, and the water was still there. It was just... Contested.

The System was holding the demolition until it could figure out how to eject the "foreign object" without destroying the container.

He needed to get the water out. Or he needed to prove it wasn't water.

He looked at the black sludge. It was spreading. It was touching the roots of the mold spores. It was seeping into the foundation.

If he didn’t remove it, the Hold Order would expire, and the decay would resume with interest.

Gerald pulled up his inventory. He had no tools. He had a broken broom, a half-empty bag of cement, and a *Egress Sign* that he had been meaning to install in the hallway.

The Egress Sign, and he picked it up. It was a simple green rectangle. *EXIT.*

He looked at the black water.

If he designated an exit route... Did the water have to follow it?

No. That was too simple. The water was a hazard, not a person.

But wait.

*Code Section 12.4* referred to "Disposal." Disposal required a destination. A landfill. A incinerator. A sewer.

Gerald had no sewer. He had no incinerator.

He had a dungeon.

And dungeons had *spawn points*.

If he marked the black water as a spawn point...

Gerald shook his head. That would be a violation of the core integrity.

He needed to move the water. He needed to transport it out of the 4,000 square feet.

But he had no doors. The library above was sealed. The System enforced dungeon boundaries.

Unless...

The *Structural Decay* had opened new cracks. Cracks in the walls. Cracks in the floor.

Cracks that led *outside*.

Gerald looked at the northern wall, and the decay had carved a jagged hole, roughly six inches wide, leading into the dark.

That wasn't inside the library. That was inside the earth. The bedrock.

The System considered the bedrock part of the dungeon footprint. Usually.

But if he cited the *hole*.., and he walked toward the breach. The water followed, slithering like oil.

He looked at the crack. It was irregular. Non-compliant. It violated the *Sealed Envelope* rule (Section 9.1: All structural penetrations must be sealed with approved materials).

He cited the crack.

*Violation: Unapproved Structural Penetration.* *Order: Seal Immediately.*

He tried to seal it with cement. The cement wouldn't adhere. The decay effect made all adhesion fail for the next five minutes.

He tried to seal it with mana. Not enough mana.

He tried to seal it with the *Egress Sign*, and he slapped the green sign over the crack.

It didn't fit. The crack was smaller.

He needed something bigger.

He looked around. The only thing big enough was the broken broom handle.

He jammed the broom handle into the crack.

It held.

The System pinged.

*Violation: Obstruction of Egress Path.*

Gerald winced. He had just created a new violation. But it was a lesser one.

*Penalty: +5% Stamina Drain for all spawns.*

His three mold spores groaned. The rat stopped moving.

Gerald ignored them. He looked at the black water. It was pooling around the broom handle.

He needed to push it through the crack, and he couldn't push it. He was a core. He could manipulate terrain, but not liquids directly without spending mana he didn't have.

He needed a current.

He looked at the water leak in sector seven. It was dripping. Slowly.

If he could direct that leak.., and he pulled up the *Water Management* tab. It was grayed out.

*Insufficient Funds. Repair Cost: 500 Mana.*

He had 12 Mana.

He looked at the broom handle. He looked at the crack.

He looked at the black water, and he had an idea. It was terrible. It was likely illegal. It was definitely risky.

He opened the *Spawn* menu.

*Spawn: Air Ventilation Duct.* *Cost: 10 Mana.*

He placed a vent directly above the broom handle.

*Spawn: Fan.* *Cost: 5 Mana.*

He placed the fan inside the vent, and he had 3 Mana left.

He activated the fan.

It was a cheap, low-RPM fan. It blew air. Just air.

The air hit the black water. It didn't move it much. It was too viscous.

Gerald looked at the fan. It was vibrating. It was loose.

*Violation: Unsecured Mechanical Equipment.*

He cited the fan.

*Order: Secure.*

He had no screws, and he cited the fan again.

*Order: Remove.*

He deleted the fan.

He lost the airflow.

He looked at the water. It was creeping toward the main hallway. If it reached the central hub, the decay would accelerate. The Hold Order would turn into a full demolition.

Gerald closed his eyes, and he thought about the Hearing. Vane had smiled. *The Inspector General is coming. They do not like loose ends.*

Vane had said the venue was ambiguous. The hearing was paused to reconvene in Ashford.

Ashford. The city.

Gerald was beneath the Ashford Municipal Library, and the library was in Ashford.

The System had said: *We will reconvene in Ashford.*

It hadn't said *inside* the dungeon.

It had said *in Ashford*.

Gerald looked at the crack. The broom handle was jammed in it. The black water was leaking through the gaps around the wood.

If he could make the water exit the dungeon... If he could get it into the city...

He looked at his *Citation* history. He had cited the goblin. He had cited the mold. He had cited the water.

He had never cited the *city*.

But the city wasn't a Core.

Wait.

The *Inspector General* was coming.

The *Inspector General* audited Cores.

But the Inspector General was also a municipal entity.

Gerald opened the *Complaint* form. It was usually used for adventurer complaints. *The floor is too slippery. The monster is too strong.*

He filled it out.

*Complaintant: Core ID 8940-G (Gerald Hutchins).* *Respondent: Municipal Services, City of Ashford, District 4.* *Issue: Inadequate Storm Drain Capacity.* *Evidence: Black, hazardous runoff accumulating in sub-basement.*

He attached the citation of the water.

*Submit.*

The form processed.

*Error: Municipal Services are not a registered Core. Complaint cannot be served.*

Gerald growled, and he looked at the black water. It was an inch from the broom handle.

He looked at the broom handle.

He looked at the crack.

He looked at the System message: *Reconvene in Ashford.*

He realized what he had to do, and he couldn't cite the city; he could *invite* the city in.

He opened the *Event* tab.

*Event: Public Open House.* *Cost: 0 Mana (Free for new cores).* *Description: Allow public access to dungeon for 1 hour. Note: Adventurers may enter. Civilians are discouraged but not prohibited.*

He selected *Public Open House*, and he targeted the crack.

He expanded the crack. It cost 2 Mana. He only had 3.

He used the last Mana.

The crack widened. It became a doorway.

Rough.

Jagged. Dangerous.

But it led out.

Into the library sub-basement.

And into the library.

And into Ashford.

The black water poured through the new opening. It flowed onto the library floor.

The System pinged.

*Notification: Dungeon Boundary Breach. Event: Public Access.*

Gerald felt a pull. A tug.

*Hearing Reconvening: Location: Ashford Municipal Library, Sub-Basement Exit.*

The red text of the *Structural Decay* vanished.

The hold order lifted.

The decay stopped.

But the black water was outside his dungeon.

And outside his dungeon, the rules changed, and he heard sirens. Not dungeon sirens. Police sirens.

He heard footsteps. Heavy, rubber-soled boots on tile.

A voice boomed from the tunnel.

"Hey! You can't be down here! This is a restricted zone!"

Gerald looked at his dungeon, which was a wreck. The ceiling was gone in places.

The floor was cracked.

The mold spores were hiding in the corners.

But he was alive.

And he had just dumped a hazardous waste violation onto the City of Ashford's doorstep.

He pulled up his *Citation* interface, and he looked at the approaching boots.

He looked at the black puddle on the library floor.

He smiled. It was a thin, dry smile.

He wrote a new citation.

*TO: Officer on Premises.* *FROM: Core ID 8940-G.* *VIOLATION: Failure to Report Hazardous Spill.* *CODE: Section 15.2: Duty to Notify.*

He pressed *Serve*.

The boot stopped.

The officer looked down at the black sludge. He looked up at the dark tunnel, and looked at the green *EXIT* sign hanging crookedly from the broom handle.

He pulled out a walkie-talkie.

"Dispatch, we have a 10-16 at the Library sub-basement. Possible chemical spill. And... something is trying to hand me a piece of paper."

Gerald waited in the dark.

The System message appeared.

*Notification: Citation Accepted by Non-Core Entity. Appeal Window: Open.*

*Warning: External Authority Response Pending.*

Gerald sat in the ruins of his home. He had broken the fourth wall. He had cited the police.

And now, he had to defend it.

In front of the Inspector General.

Who was already walking through the door.
