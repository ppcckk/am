Chapter 142: Untrusted Release

# Chapter 142

The cage hummed at a frequency that made Gerald's teeth ache.

Brix stood outside the bars, picking at his fingernails with a dagger that had seen better days, likely in a stew, likely in a place far less sanitary than this sub-basement. The rogue didn't look like a supervisor. He looked like a man who had misplaced his keys and was currently using a weapon to pry open a vending machine.

Gerald pressed his hand against the translucent glass. It wasn't glass.

It was solidified System energy, cold and unyielding.

The cage was tight. Exactly twelve feet by twelve feet. Standard minimum occupancy for a Core in probationary status, according to Subsection 9, Paragraph 4 of the Dungeon Governance Manual.

Gerald knew this because he had memorized the Manual while waiting for the IG to arrive. He had also memorized the addendum regarding Supervisor qualifications. Brix did not meet the requirements. Brix was an Adventurer, not a licensed Code Enforcement Officer.

But the IG had said *adventurer party*. Not *adventurer*.

Gerald closed his eyes and pushed his consciousness outward.

He couldn't see his dungeon anymore. He couldn't feel the draft of the ventilation shafts or the dampness of the concrete walls.

The cage blocked his sensory range.

He was blind.

"Hey," Brix said, tapping the bars. "You alive in there? The IG said you got a heartbeat. Sounds like a drum solo, though."

Gerald opened his eyes. He didn't answer. Instead, he focused on the one thing he could still control: the interface floating in his mind's eye.

*Status: Probationary Core.* *Compliance Rating: 47/100 (Frozen).* *Supervisor: Brix (Unlicensed).* *Active Violations: 12.*

The number stared at him. Twelve.

Before the audit, it had been three.

The IG had cited him for "Structural Instability due to Unsupervised Emotional Distress." That was a new violation type.

Emotional distress wasn't code. It was a feeling.

But the System treated feelings as structural loads. If Gerald panicked, the walls shook. If the walls shook, the foundation weakened.

Gerald breathed out slowly. He forced his heart rate down. He counted backward from one hundred.

The glass stopped humming.

Brix frowned. He leaned closer. "Did you just... quiet down?"

"Compliance requires stability," Gerald said. His voice was flat.

Dry.

Like he was reading a parking ticket. "I am stabilizing."

Brix shrugged.

"Okay. Cool.

So, uh.

What do I do?"

"You monitor," Gerald said.

"For what?"

"Violations."

Brix looked around the empty dungeon space beyond the cage. There was nothing there.

No monsters.

No traps.

Just the faint outline of the library's foundation above and the hum of the electrical main.

"There's nothing here," Brix said. "It's just a room."

"It is not just a room," Gerald said. "It is a structure. And structures have rules."

Brix groaned. He sat down on the floor, crossing his legs. He pulled a apple from his pack and took a bite. The sound of the crunch was obscenely loud in the silence.

"This is stupid," Brix said. "I'm an assassin. I kill things. I don't watch things."

"You are a Supervisor," Gerald corrected. "You are responsible for my integration. If I fail, you lose your adventurer license. The IG doesn't bluff."

Brix stopped chewing. He looked at Gerald. The rogue's eyes narrowed.

He wasn't stupid. He was just untrained.

"You know that?" Brix asked.

"The Manual is public record," Gerald said. "Section 3, Appendix B. 'Consequences for Supervisor Negligence.' It's in the fine print. I read it."

Brix looked at the apple in his hand. Then he looked at the cage. He looked at Gerald.

"Right," Brix said.

He stood up. "Right. Okay.

What's the first thing I watch for?"

Gerald hesitated. He didn't have a protocol for this. He had protocols for electrical faults, for plumbing leaks, for improper egress signage. He did not have a protocol for a man who solved problems by stabbing them.

"Look for instability," Gerald said. "If the walls shake, tell me. If the lights flicker, tell me. If you feel... anxious... tell me."

"I never feel anxious," Brix said.

"You are standing in a cage with a man who can't touch you. You should be anxious."

Brix snorted. He walked to the far wall of the dungeon space, outside the cage. He ran his hand along the concrete. He tapped it with his dagger.

*Clang. Clang. Clang.*

The sound echoed.

Gerald winced. "Stop that."

"Why?"

"You are introducing percussive maintenance to a structure not designed for it."

"It's concrete," Brix said. "It's tough."

"It is not tough," Gerald said. "It is brittle.

You are creating micro-fractures.

The System will register this as a Structural Integrity violation. It will lower your score."

Brix paused. He looked at the wall. He looked at Gerald.

"My score?"

"If I am deleted, your license is revoked. If my Compliance Rating drops because of your actions, I am fined.

If I cannot pay the fines, I am deleted.

Therefore, your actions affect my survival.

And my survival affects your license."

Brix lowered the dagger. He looked unsettled.

"Okay," Brix said. "So, don't hit the walls."

"Precisely."

Brix walked back to the cage. He leaned against the bars. He didn't look at Gerald. He looked at the ceiling.

"I need to pee," Brix said.

"There is a restroom in the library," Gerald said.

"No, I mean... right here. I need to go."

"There are no facilities in the current dungeon footprint. You must leave the premises."

Brix grimaced. He crossed his legs.

"I can't leave," Brix said. "The IG said I have to stay within fifty feet of the Core. It's a tether.

If I walk out, I lose connection. If I lose connection, the cage locks down and I'm trapped in here with you."

Gerald's stomach turned over. He hated improvisation. He hated variables he couldn't measure.

"Then you must wait," Gerald said.

"I can't wait. I have a bladder."

"Then you must relieve yourself in a compliant manner."

Brix stared at him. "A compliant manner?"

"You must not contaminate the foundation," Gerald said.

"The System will flag biological waste as a sanitation hazard. That is a Code 4 violation. Health and Safety.

It will tank the rating."

Brix looked around. There was nowhere to go. The floor was bare concrete. The walls were bare concrete.

"Great," Brix said. "So I'm gonna pee on your dungeon."

"No," Gerald said. "You are going to pee in a container."

"I don't have a container."

Gerald closed his eyes. He reached into his inventory.

He had nothing.

He was a Core. He didn't carry buckets.

But he could spawn them.

*Spawn: Plastic Bucket (Non-Combustible).*

The air shimmered. A white plastic bucket materialized on the floor outside the cage.

Brix jumped back. He kicked the bucket. It skittered across the concrete.

"What the hell?" Brix shouted.

"Use the bucket," Gerald said. "It is compliant."

Brix stared at the bucket. He looked at Gerald. He looked at the bucket again.

"You're joking," Brix said.

"I am never joking," Gerald said. "I am stating facts.

Use the bucket.

Or violate sanitation code.

Your choice."

Brix grabbed the bucket. He muttered a curse word under his breath. He turned away from the cage and did his business.

Gerald watched the System log update.

*Sanitation Hazard Averted.* *Compliance Rating: 47/100.*

It was a small victory. A tiny, pathetic victory. But it was a victory.

When Brix finished, he sealed the bucket with a piece of tape he pulled from his pack. He looked satisfied.

"See?" Brix said. "Problem solved."

"It is not solved," Gerald said. "It is deferred. You now have a biohazard in your inventory. You must dispose of it at a licensed facility."

"I'll dump it in the library trash."

"That is not a licensed facility."

"It's a trash can."

"It is an unregulated disposal site. You are creating a new violation."

Brix threw the bucket into a corner. "Whatever. It's gone."

Gerald felt the tension in his shoulders. The System was quiet. For now.

But then, a sound came from above.

Not from the library. From the dungeon itself. A scratch. A scurry.

Gerald froze.

He couldn't see it. The cage blocked his vision. But he could hear it. Small claws on concrete.

Something was in the dungeon.

Brix heard it too. He drew his dagger. "Rat?"

"No," Gerald said. "Rats do not scratch with three distinct rhythms. That is a Scout."

"A what?"

"A Scout. A reconnaissance entity. Sent by a rival Core."

Brix looked up. "Who's out there?"

Gerald didn't know. The audit had been a shock. The IG had been a shock.

But a rival Core? That was normal.

That was business.

"Stay away from the cage," Gerald said.

"Why?"

"Because if the Scout attacks you, and you die, I lose my Supervisor. And if I lose my Supervisor, I am in violation of Probation Protocol. And if I am in violation, I am deleted."

Brix looked nervous. He backed away from the bars. He looked into the darkness of the dungeon space.

A shape emerged from the shadows.

It was small. Gray. It moved with a jerky, unnatural grace.

It was a goblin, but not a normal goblin.

Its skin was translucent. Its eyes were glowing blue.

It was a *Citation Golem*.

Gerald's breath hitched. He had heard of them. They were sent by high-level Cores who had figured out how to weaponize the System's own laws.

The Golem held a scroll.

It walked toward Brix.

Brix raised his dagger. "Get back!"

The Golem did not attack.

It stopped ten feet away. It unfurled the scroll. It spoke.

Its voice was the sound of paper tearing.

*"Violation Detected: Unauthorized Presence in Restricted Zone."*

Brix blinked. "What?"

*"Subject: Brix of the Iron Dagger. Charge: Trespassing on Private Easement. Penalty: Immediate Deletion."*

Brix looked at Gerald. "What does that mean?"

"It means," Gerald said, his voice tight, "that the rival Core has cited you. Not me. You."

"I'm not a Core!" Brix shouted.

*"The System does not distinguish between Core and Adventurer in matters of Property Rights,"* the Golem intoned. *"You are an entity. You are in a restricted zone. You are violating the Code."*

Brix looked at the dagger in his hand. He looked at the Golem. He looked at Gerald.

"Help me," Brix whispered.

Gerald stared at the Golem.

He knew the rules.

He knew the laws. But the Golem wasn't attacking him.

It was attacking Brix. And Gerald was trapped in a cage.

He couldn't issue a Counter-Citation. He couldn't spawn a defense. He was powerless.

Unless.

Gerald looked at the cage. The glass was humming. It was vibrating.

*Structural Instability due to Unsupervised Emotional Distress.*

The IG had cited him for that.

But what if Gerald *induced* the distress?

He couldn't panic. He had to be precise. He had to find a loophole.

The Golem stepped forward. The air around Brix shimmerred. A red glow appeared on the rogue's chest. The mark of deletion.

"Move, Brix!" Gerald shouted.

Brix didn't move. He was too scared. He was frozen.

The Golem raised its hand. A wave of force pushed Brix back. He slid across the floor.

*"Payment Required,"* the Golem said. *"The violation must be cured within ten seconds. Or the entity is removed."*

"Removed," Gerald said. "They mean killed."

Brix looked at Gerald with pure terror.

Gerald looked at the cage. He looked at the Golem. He looked at the bucket in the corner.

The bucket.

The bucket was a biohazard.

If the bucket was a biohazard, then the area around it was a Sanitation Hazard Zone.

And if there was a Sanitation Hazard Zone, then there could be no Trespassing. Because you cannot trespass on a hazard. You must evacuate it.

It was a stretch. It was a legal fiction. It was the kind of thing that would get a real inspector fired.

But Gerald wasn't an inspector anymore. He was a Core.

And he had one move left.

He focused his will on the bucket. He didn't spawn anything new. He *re-classified* the existing object.

*System Update: Object 'Plastic Bucket' reclassified as 'Active Sanitation Hazard'.*

The System flashed red.

*"Sanitation Hazard Detected. Area 4, Zone C is now designated Quarantine."*

The Golem froze. It looked at Brix. It looked at the bucket. It looked at the floor between them.

The floor between them was now Quarantine.

And Quarantine meant *No Entry*.

The Golem's scroll flared. It turned to Gerald.

*"Objection,"* the Golem said. *"The hazard was created by the Core. The Core is the source. The Core is liable."*

Gerald smiled. It was a thin, cruel smile.

"I am in a cage," Gerald said. "I am isolated. I am not the source. The Supervisor is."

Brix was standing in the Quarantine zone.

*"Supervisor is Liable,"* the Golem said. *"Supervisor must cure the hazard."*

Brix looked at the bucket. He looked at the red glow on his chest.

"I can't clean it," Brix said. "I'm not a janitor!"

*"Cure the hazard,"* the Golem repeated.

Gerald looked at Brix. He looked at the dagger.

"Brix," Gerald said. "Stab the bucket."

Brix didn't hesitate. He lunged.

His dagger pierced the plastic.

He kicked it over. He kicked it again. He broke it into pieces.

*"Hazard Cured,"* the Golem said.

The red glow vanished from Brix's chest.

The Golem bowed. It turned and walked back into the shadows.

Silence returned.

Brix collapsed against the wall. He was shaking. He was pale.

He looked at Gerald.

"You just... you just used a bucket to save my life."

"It was a legal maneuver," Gerald said.

"It was insane!"

"It was compliant."

Brix laughed. It was a hysterical, jagged sound. He put his head in his hands.

Gerald looked at the System log.

*Violation Averted.* *Compliance Rating: 47/100.* *New Notification: Rival Core 'The Void Weaver' has registered a Formal Complaint.*

Gerald read the complaint. It wasn't about the goblin. It wasn't about the bucket.

It was about the cage.

*Complaint: Unlicensed Containment of a Supervisory Entity.*

Gerald froze.

The cage wasn't for him.

The cage was for Brix.

The IG hadn't given Gerald a Supervisor. The IG had given Gerald a *hostage*.

And the Void Weaver knew it.

A message appeared in Gerald's mind. It wasn't from the System. It was a direct transmission.

*Location: Ashford Municipal Library, Sub-Basement, North Wall.* *Request: Release the Supervisor.* *Condition: I will cite your ventilation system for non-compliance with Grade-A Standards.* *Deadline: Five minutes.*

Gerald looked at Brix. Brix was still shaking. He didn't know.

Gerald looked at the glass. It was humming again.

The Void Weaver was outside. And it was threatening to shut Gerald down for a violation he hadn't even committed yet.

Gerald had to open the cage.

But if he opened the cage, Brix would leave. And if Brix left, Gerald would be deleted.

Gerald had five minutes to convince a rogue to stay in a room with a monster that could kill him, while a rival Core threatened to erase his entire existence.

He looked at Brix.

"Brix," Gerald said.

Brix looked up. His eyes were wide.

"We have a problem," Gerald said.

"Another one?"

"A bigger one."

Gerald stood up. He placed his hand on the control panel of the cage. His finger hovered over the release button.

"Do you trust me?" Gerald asked.

Brix laughed again. "No."

"Then you're the only one who can save us," Gerald said.

He pressed the button.
