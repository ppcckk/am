Chapter 156: Stamped into Darkness

# Chapter 156

The stamp came down before Gerald could blink.

It was heavy, iron, and cold. It hit the clipboard with a sound like a car crash in a library.

Gerald flinched. His hand cramped around the pen. The red ink bled through the form, staining his thumb.

**[VIOLATION: UNAUTHORIZED CROSS-JURISDICTIONAL INSPECTION.]** **[PENALTY: ALL STAT BUFFS SUSPENDED FOR 24 HOURS.]**

The text hovered in the air, glowing with a sickly amber light. Gerald stared at it.

He did not speak, and he did not breathe. His heart hammered against his ribs, a frantic bird trapped in a cage of bone and rule.

He looked up.

The entity holding the stamp was not the neighbor. It was not a monster. It was a clerk.

Tall, gray, and impossibly thin, the figure stood seven feet high.

Its skin looked like parchment, stretched tight over a skeleton of wire. It wore a suit that seemed to be made of shredded filing cabinets.

Its face was a smooth oval, devoid of eyes, nose, or mouth.

Where a mouth should have been, there was only a slot.

The slot opened.

"You are out of bounds," the Clerk said. The voice was dry, like paper sliding over paper.

Gerald tightened his grip on the clipboard.

"I have a citation to serve."

"You have no warrant."

"I have the System's rules."

"The rules are not the law." The Clerk stamped the air again. Dust fell from its sleeve.

"The law is jurisdiction.

You are in Sector Four.

You belong to Sector One.

"

Gerald forced his throat to work. His voice came out flat, clipped.

"I am following a trail of non-compliance.

The rat is a conduit. The citation was served in my territory.

I am merely delivering the appeal notification.

"

The Clerk tilted its head. The motion was jerky, mechanical.

"An appeal requires a Hearing. You have not filed a Hearing Request."

"I am filing it now." Gerald held up the clipboard. "Form 11-C. Appeal Notification.

Signed by the Core.

Witnessed by the Proxy.

"

The Clerk looked at the clipboard. The slot on its face widened. A sound came from it, a low grinding noise.

"Form 11-C is for structural appeals only."

"This is a structural violation. The rat is part of the dungeon infrastructure. It is an unauthorized modification to the neighbor's spawn list."

"Define infrastructure."

Gerald paused. He looked at the rat. The rat sat on a pile of rubble, holding its red flag. It watched Gerald with intelligent, malicious eyes.

"Infrastructure," Gerald said, "is any component that affects the Compliance Rating of a dungeon. The rat has a Compliance Rating. Therefore, it is infrastructure."

The Clerk was silent for three seconds. Then, it reached into its suit pocket and pulled out a pen. It did not write on the clipboard. It wrote on the air.

**[SUBJECT: RAT.

STATUS: UNLICENSED BIOLOGICAL ENTITY. RATING: 12/100.]** **[SUBJECT: DUNGEON. STATUS: NON-COMPLIANT.

RATING: 47/100.]**

"Your argument is flawed," the Clerk said.

"A rat is not a wall.

A wall is static. A rat is dynamic.

Dynamic entities are not subject to static building codes.

"

Gerald felt a surge of cold fury. He hated this logic. He hated the distinction. It was arbitrary, sloppy, and dangerous.

"Fire codes apply to dynamic entities," Gerald said. "Sprinkler systems detect heat. Heat is dynamic. If the rat catches fire, does the dungeon lose its fire suppression rating?"

The Clerk's head tilted further.

"Yes."

"Then the rat is subject to fire code. And fire code requires egress routes. Does the rat have an egress route?"

Gerald pointed to the hole in the wall, and the hole was jagged, dark, and leaking mercury. It was not a door. It was a breach.

"No," the Clerk said.

"Then it is a violation."

"It is a hole."

"It is an unsafe egress route." Gerald stepped forward. One step.

Then another, and he was crossing the line. He was entering the neighbor's dungeon.

The air grew heavy. The temperature dropped. The mold on the walls seemed to recoil.

"Stop," the Clerk said.

Gerald did not stop.

"Stop or I will issue a Detention Order."

Gerald raised his clipboard.

"Detention requires probable cause. I have probable cause.

The rat is sitting in a hazard zone, which is blocking a ventilation shaft. I can hear the draft. It is inadequate.

"

The Clerk moved. It did not step; it slid. One moment it was ten feet away. The next, it was inches from Gerald's face.

"You are testing the Inspector General."

"I am testing your interpretation of Section 4, Paragraph B."

"Paragraph B is repealed."

"It is not repealed in my jurisdiction."

"It is not repealed anywhere. It is merely... obscured."

Gerald felt a chill run down his spine. Obscured? That was not a legal term. That was a conspiracy.

"Show me the text," Gerald said.

"I cannot."

"Why?"

"Because you are not authorized to read the raw code."

Gerald looked at the rat. The rat chattered. It held up the red flag.

**[CITATION SERVED. APPEAL PENDING.]**

The System acknowledged the appeal. The clock started.

Gerald had bought time. But he had also walked into a trap.

The Clerk's suit rustled. Something inside it moved. A shadow, long and thin, detached itself from the Clerk's body.

"Leave," the Clerk said. "Now."

Gerald looked at the shadow. It formed into a shape. A hand. A large, gray hand with too many fingers.

He looked at the hole, and he looked at the rat.

The rat was no longer holding the flag. It was holding a small, jagged piece of concrete. A shard.

Gerald understood. The neighbor was not sending a scout. The neighbor was sending a jury.

And the Clerk was the judge.

Gerald took a breath. He adjusted his grip on the clipboard.

He did not retreat, and he did not argue.

He wrote.

"Form 11-D," he muttered. "Emergency Stop-Work Order."

The Clerk froze.

"That form requires a Level 5 Hazard."

"The hole," Gerald said, pointing at the breach. "It is open to the exterior. There is no sealant.

No flashing. No insulation.

It is a thermal bridge.

"

The Clerk hesitated.

"A thermal bridge is a Level 3 Hazard," it said.

"Not when it is connected to a mercury leak," Gerald said. "Mercury is a Level 5 Hazard. The combination creates a combined hazard rating of Level 7."

The Clerk's slot snapped shut.

"That is a calculation error."

"It is a code requirement." Gerald held out the clipboard. "Sign it."

The Clerk stared at him. The shadow-hand reached out, hovering over Gerald's shoulder. It did not touch him. It waited.

"If I sign it," the Clerk said, "the dungeon collapses."

"Only the unsafe portion," Gerald said.

"The unsafe portion is the whole thing."

"Then you cite the whole thing. I will not accept partial compliance."

Gerald tapped the paper with his pen. The sound was loud in the silence.

"I am invoking the Right of Immediate Correction," Gerald said.

The air shimmered. The System text flashed red.

**[RIGHT OF IMMEDIATE CORRECTION INVOKED.]** **[WARNING: THIS ACTION MAY VOID ALL INSURANCE.]** **[WARNING: THIS ACTION MAY ATTRACT THE INSPECTOR GENERAL.]**

Gerald did not care about insurance. He did not care about the Inspector General. He cared about the hole.

The Clerk looked at the text. It looked at Gerald. It looked at the rat.

The rat dropped the concrete shard. It scurried into the shadows.

The Clerk raised its stamp.

"Do it," the Clerk said.

Gerald signed.

The ground shook, which was not a large shake. It was a precise, surgical tremor. A localized seismic event.

To Gerald's left, a section of the wall crumbled. Not the hole. The wall next to the hole.

A large slab of concrete, ten feet by ten feet, broke free from the foundation. It floated in the air for a second, held by the System's physics, then dropped.

It smashed into the floor.

Dust billowed. Debris scattered.

The hole remained. But the wall supporting it was gone.

The hole was now an open archway, unsupported, leaning precariously toward the neighbor's dungeon.

Gerald stepped back. He protected his clipboard. He protected his shoes.

The Clerk stood in the dust cloud. It was untouched.

"You have created a new violation," the Clerk said. "Unsupported masonry."

"It is now in the process of being corrected," Gerald said. "I am filing a Form 11-E. Demolition Permit."

The Clerk stepped forward. The shadow-hand reached out again. This time, it touched Gerald's chest.

It was cold. It burned.

Gerald gasped. He looked down. The hand was pressing against his core.

"You are out of time," the Clerk said. "The hearing starts in ten seconds. If you are not present, you forfeit."

"I am present."

"You are not compliant."

"I am the most compliant thing in this room."

The Clerk withdrew its hand. Gerald staggered. He felt weak. His vision blurred.

**[STATUS: DEBUFF APPLIED. PHYSICAL DAMAGE REDUCTION LOST.]**

The Clerk smiled. Or it seemed to smile. The slot on its face widened into a jagged grin.

"Go," the Clerk said. "Before the rat eats your bones."

Gerald looked at the hole.

Beyond it, the neighbor's dungeon was dark. But it was not empty.

Lights flickered. Not torches. Not magical orbs.

Fluorescent lights.

Tube lights, flickering with the hum of dying ballasts.

Gerald recognized them. He had inspected thousands of them. He knew the sound of a failing starter.

Someone in the neighbor's dungeon had installed electrical code violations.

And now, they were on.

Gerald stepped through the hole, and he did not look back at the Clerk. He did not look at the collapsing wall.

He looked at the lights.

They buzzed. They flickered. They died.

Then, they came back on.

Brighter.

Blindingly bright.

And in the center of the room, standing under the glare, was a figure, which was not a monster. It was not a beast.

It was a man.

He wore a suit. A gray suit. A suit made of shredded filing cabinets.

But he had a face, and he had Gerald's face.

The man looked at Gerald. He raised a clipboard.

His clipboard was made of bone.

"Welcome," the doppelganger said. "To the Hearing."

Gerald tried to speak. His voice failed.

The doppelganger stamped the air.

**[DEFENDANT: GERALD HUTCHINS.]** **[CHARGES: EXISTENTIAL NON-COMPLIANCE.]**

The lights exploded.

Gerald fell into the darkness.

And in the darkness, he heard the stamp come down again.

Gerald heard the stamp come down again.
