Chapter 318: Serving the Citation

# Chapter 318

The goblin scout, designated Unit 74-B by the System's internal ledger, crouched behind a load-bearing pillar. Its vision was a grainy, monochromatic overlay, highlighting structural weaknesses in red and potential threats in orange. But here, in the sector labeled *Sector 4-G: Sub-Basement Utility Corridor*, the overlay flickered.

There were no red cracks. There were no orange threats. There was only a perfect, geometric grid of fluorescent light and a smell of industrial cleaner so potent it made the goblin's nostrils burn.

Unit 74-B tapped its claws against the concrete floor. The sound was crisp.

Too crisp.

In other dungeons, the floor yielded to weight, offering traction for ambushes. Here, the coefficient of friction was standardized. The goblin shifted its weight, trying to find a loose tile to trip an intruder.

There were no loose tiles, and the grout lines were sealed with a polymer that the System classified as *Class A Fire-Resistant*.

A low hum vibrated through the goblin's bones. Not the growl of a beast, nor the crackle of mana. It was a hum of compliance.

The goblin had been sent to map this anomaly. The Warlord of the Rotting Warrens wanted to know why three of his elite shadow-stalkers had vanished in this sector without leaving a corpse. The scouts reported that the shadows simply had not *fit*. They had been rejected by the architecture.

Unit 74-B moved forward, its dagger drawn, and the blade was a jagged shard of obsidian, illegal in most high-compliance zones. The goblin felt a sudden nausea. The air pressure had shifted.

The humidity gauge on its HUD read 45%. A comfortable level for humans.

Unbearably dry for a subterranean species adapted to 80% moisture.

Ahead, a door stood open, which was not a dungeon gate. It was a standard commercial-grade push door, equipped with a panic bar and a sign that read *EXIT*.

The sign was illuminated. It was green. It was up to code.

The goblin froze. The System's predatory instincts screamed that this was a trap.

Why was the exit so obvious?

Why was the lighting so uniform? In every other dungeon, darkness was a weapon. Here, darkness had been legislated out of existence.

A shadow fell across the threshold. Not a monster's shadow. A man's.

The goblin flattened itself against the pillar.

The figure walked into the light. He wore a hard hat.

He carried a clipboard, and he did not look like a warrior. He looked like an accountant who had just discovered a rounding error.

Unit 74-B narrowed its eyes. *Weak*, the creature thought. *Unprotected. No armor. No mana aura.*

But the goblin remembered the shadows. The shadows had died here. The shadows had been cited.

The goblin waited. It needed to see the kill. It needed to see how this small, soft thing destroyed things.

The man stopped. He didn't look at the goblin.

He looked at the ceiling, and he raised a pen.

He made a mark on the clipboard. He frowned.

He turned to his left, where a ventilation duct ran along the wall. He pulled a tape measure from his pocket. He extended it. *Click.* He measured the gap between the duct and the sprinkler head.

He wrote a number. He shook his head.

The goblin's confusion grew. This was not combat. This was... administration.

The man spoke. His voice was dry, devoid of inflection.

"Dave, note a violation under Section 8.2: Obstruction of Egress Path. Clearance is two inches below minimum."

A voice crackled over a radio on the man's belt.

"Copy, Gerald. I'm bringing the shims."

The man nodded. He didn't raise his weapon. He didn't cast a spell. He simply stood there, waiting for the correction.

Unit 74-B felt a chill that had nothing to do with temperature. The predator was not hunting. The predator was auditing. And the goblin realized, with a sinking dread, that it was part of the infrastructure being audited.

Gerald Hutchins stepped onto the bridge of beams. The darkness of *The Void* pressed against his eyes, a sensory deprivation tank made of non-existence. He could feel the pull of the null space trying to unravel his thoughts, to dissolve the concept of *Gerald Hutchins* into the background radiation of the System.

He didn't fight it.

Fighting was improvisation. Improvisation made him nauseous. Instead, he anchored himself.

He gripped the steel beam with both hands, and he focused on the cold metal, the rivets, the specific torque of the bolts he had installed himself. He recited the specifications of the bridge's load capacity in his head. *Ten pounds per square foot.

Uniformly distributed.

No live load.*

The beam held.

Arthur Penhaligon stood beside him. The lawyer was pale, his suit rumpled, his eyes darting around the abyss. He clutched a briefcase that contained three thousand pages of municipal zoning ordinances, dungeon core precedents, and a expensive bottle of antacid.

"Gerald," Arthur whispered, his voice trembling. "This isn't a hearing room.

This is a void.

The Inspector General isn't sitting at a desk.

The Inspector General is the void.

"

"Procedure is location-independent," Gerald said. He didn't look at Arthur. He looked straight ahead, where the darkness was coalescing into a shape.

The shape was vast. It had no face, only a presence that felt like a thousand years of bureaucratic indifference. The voice that had threatened him earlier boomed from everywhere and nowhere.

**"SUBMIT."**

The word hit Gerald in the chest. His knees buckled. The air turned to ash.

He tasted copper and dust. The void wanted him to kneel.

It wanted him to acknowledge that his dungeon was an anomaly, a glitch, an unauthorized construction in a reality that preferred chaos.

Gerald adjusted his hard hat. He opened his clipboard.

"Objection," Gerald said.

The void rippled. **

"YOU ARE IN MY DOMAIN. THERE ARE NO RULES HERE."**

"Article 4, Section C of the System's foundational code," Gerald said, his voice steady despite the trembling of his hands. "The Inspector General's authority is derived from the maintenance of structural integrity. The void is not a structure.

It is the absence of one.

Therefore, the General has no jurisdiction over a hearing regarding the *existence* of a structure, only its *condition*.

"

Silence. The darkness seemed to pause.

"Furthermore," Arthur added, stepping forward, his voice gaining a lawyer's rhythmic cadence. "My client's dungeon, while physically located in a spatial anomaly, is legally registered as a municipal utility. Zoning Ordinance 14-B classifies sub-basement infrastructure as public domain. You cannot erase public domain without a public hearing conducted by the City Council, not by a cosmic entity."

The void swirled. A tendril of shadow lashed out, wrapping around Gerald's ankle. It felt like cold water. It felt like forgetting.

Gerald didn't move. He didn't try to pull away. He looked down at the shadow.

"Citation," Gerald said.

The shadow hesitated.

"Citation for Non-Physical Interference with Registered Utility Infrastructure," Gerald continued. He wrote on his clipboard. *Violation 1: Unauthorized contact with public property. Penalty: Immediate cessation of interaction.*

The System, bound by its own rigid logic, recognized the citation. The shadow recoiled. It snapped back into the darkness, confused. It had tried to intimidate a bureaucrat, and the bureaucrat had treated it like a loose wire.

**"YOU CANNOT CITE ME,"** the voice growled, louder now. The beams beneath them vibrated. Dust fell from the imaginary ceiling. **"I AM THE CODE."**

"No," Gerald said. "You are the auditor. I am the inspector. And you are blocking my path."

This was the loophole. The System's code treated the Inspector General as a peer, but also as an entity subject to the same laws of physical presence as any other Core. If the General manifested to conduct the hearing, the General became *physically present*. And if the General was physically present in a space designated as *The Void*, it was occupying a space that had no foundation.

Gerald looked at Arthur.

"Read the appeal."

Arthur cleared his throat. He held up a single sheet of paper. It was not a legal brief. It was a blueprint.

"Appellant: Gerald Hutchins, Dungeon Core 4921-A.

Respondent: The Void. Grounds for Appeal: The respondent's presence constitutes a structural violation of the Void's own nature. By manifesting, the respondent has introduced mass into a zero-mass zone.

This creates a pressure differential of—

" Arthur glanced at the blueprint, "—four thousand pounds per square inch. The system must resolve this error.

Either the respondent vanishes, or the zone collapses.

"

Gerald didn't wait for the System to calculate. He acted.

He pulled a small tool from his belt. A laser level.

He pointed it at the center of the void. A red dot appeared, piercing the darkness.

"Calibration check," Gerald muttered. "Target locked."

The void screamed. It wasn't a sound; it was a vibration that shattered the glass in Gerald's hard hat. The beams beneath them groaned, twisting under the sudden influx of impossible weight. Arthur fell to his knees, clutching his briefcase.

Gerald stood firm. He looked at the red dot. He looked at the darkness.

"You have three seconds to vacate," Gerald said. "Or I will cite you for creating a hazard in a protected heritage site."

The darkness surged forward. It wasn't attacking him. It was attacking the beams. It was trying to erase the anchor.

Gerald felt the strain. His mind was a fraying rope. He could feel his memories slipping—his mother's face, the smell of coffee, the first time he passed a plumbing exam. The void was eating his history to fuel its erasure of his present.

He couldn't fight it with will. He had to fight it with code.

He reached into his pocket, and he pulled out a small, folded piece of paper. It was Form 11-B.

The form for an unlicensed biological entity. He had never been able to fill it out. He had never been able to make the System accept it.

But the void wasn't a biological entity. It was a regulatory entity.

Gerald unfolded the paper. He held it up to the darkness.

"Form 11-B," he said. "Filed under 'Other Unlicensed Phenomena.' Sub-category: Regulatory Overreach."

The System paused. The vibration in the beams stopped.

The void's voice was small now. **

"THAT FORM DOES NOT EXIST."**

"It exists if you acknowledge it," Gerald said. "And you have to acknowledge me to hear the hearing. Therefore, you have acknowledged the form. And the form requires a hearing to process the violation."

Gerald took a step forward. He stepped off the beam.

He walked into the void.

Arthur screamed.

"Gerald, no!"

Gerald didn't fall. He stood on the nothing. He was standing on a citation.

"I am conducting the inspection on-site," Gerald said. "You are now under observation. If you do not provide evidence of your authority to exist in this location, I will mark this entire sector as 'Unfit for Human Habitation' and collapse it."

The void writhed. It was trapped. It had to play by the rules, or it would destroy itself.

**"WHAT DO YOU WANT?"** the voice hissed.

"Permits," Gerald said. "For the Void.

And for the Inspector General.

And for every rival Core that thinks they can ignore the building code. I want the paperwork.

"

The darkness condensed.

It formed a seal. A golden seal, spinning in the air.

It was the Inspector General's mark, which was heavy, oppressive, and bright.

**"YOU HAVE ONE HOUR TO SUBMIT THE DOCUMENTS,"** the voice said. **"IF THEY ARE NOT IN ORDER, YOU WILL BE DELETED. IF THEY ARE, YOU WILL BE GRANTED TEMPORARY IMMUNITY. BUT THE COST IS HIGH."**

"What is the cost?" Gerald asked.

The seal pulsed. A new window appeared in Gerald's vision. It wasn't a stat screen. It was a contract.

**COST: THE LOSS OF ALL COMBAT ABILITIES FOR THE DURATION OF THE AUDIT.**

Gerald stared at the words. He had no combat abilities. He had never had them. He was a building inspector.

But he had Citations. He had the power to shut down other dungeons.

The contract offered a trade. To keep the power to cite, he had to give up the ability to defend himself. For one hour, he would be vulnerable to anything that walked into his dungeon.

And his dungeon was currently full of confused rats, mold spores, and a water leak.

But outside his dungeon, the other Cores were waiting. They had heard the vibration. They knew something was happening in the library.

Gerald looked at Arthur. Arthur was pale, but he was writing. He was taking notes.

"Gerald," Arthur said. "If you accept, you can't fight back. If a dragon walks in, you just... let it walk in."

"I don't fight dragons," Gerald said. "I measure them. If they don't fit, I cite them."

He pressed his thumb to the golden seal.

The world snapped into focus. The void vanished.

The beams vanished, and he was back in his sub-basement.

The fluorescent lights buzzed. The smell of cleaner was sharp.

But something was different.

His HUD was gone. His stat window was grayed out. The option for *Spawn Monster* was locked. The option for *Build Trap* was locked.

The only thing available was *Inspect*.

And his Compliance Rating was no longer 47. It was 100.

But a new message blinked in the corner of his vision.

**WARNING: HOSTILE ENTITY DETECTED.**

Gerald turned around.

Standing in the doorway of his dungeon was not a goblin. It was not a rat.

It was a dragon. A small one, the size of a dog, but with scales of polished steel and eyes that glowed with System light. It was a mechanical construct, a proxy for one of the high-tier Cores that had been waiting for him to show weakness.

It opened its mouth. A flame built in its throat.

Gerald had no fire resistance. He had no health bar. He had no armor.

He had a clipboard.

The dragon lunged.

Gerald didn't dodge. He stepped aside, a precise, calculated move based on the dragon's stride length, which he had measured earlier that day when the creature had passed the perimeter.

The dragon crashed into the wall. It hit the handrail he had installed three chapters ago.

*Clang.*

The dragon skidded, confused. It hadn't expected the wall to be so hard. It hadn't expected the floor to be so grippy.

Gerald looked at the dragon. He looked at the burn marks on the wall where the fire had licked the paint.

"Violation 4," Gerald said. "Open flame in a residential zone."

The dragon roared, preparing to burn him to ash.

Gerald raised his pen.

"I'm citing you," Gerald said. "And I'm serving you personally."

He walked toward the dragon. He had one minute to serve the citation before the dragon ate him.

He had never run a marathon. He had never thrown a punch.

But he had walked into a void, and he could walk ten feet.

The dragon lowered its head.
