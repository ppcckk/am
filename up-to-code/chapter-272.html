Chapter 272: Sinking into Zero

# Chapter 272

The water didn't rise gently. It erupted.

A wall of brown sludge and sewage from the municipal main line punched through the fractured concrete, filling the sub-basement chamber in seconds. The force of it knocked Toby off his feet. He slid across the slick stone floor, gasping, trying to find purchase on the slick algae. The man with the broken shield stumbled back, shielding his face from the debris.

The Minotaur roared, but the sound was cut short. The beast's hooves, heavy and iron-shod, had punched through the weakened floor plates.

It was sinking.

The creature's bulk, eight feet of muscle and horn, was dragging it down into the rising muck. It thrashed, sending waves of filthy water crashing against the remaining walls.

Gerald focused. He couldn't move his body, but he could feel the infrastructure. He was the plumbing. He was the foundation.

*Target: Structural Integrity of Chamber C.* *Action: Redirect flow.*

A blue prompt flickered in his peripheral vision.

**SYSTEM NOTIFICATION: HAZARD CLASSIFICATION UPDATED.** **WATER DAMAGE: CRITICAL.** **NEW VIOLATION DETECTED: IMPROPER DRAINAGE DISCHARGE.** **CITATION PREPARATION: 12 SECONDS.**

Twelve seconds. Not enough time to flood the Minotaur completely. It was too strong. It was pulling itself up by the rafters, its claws tearing through the support beams that Gerald had spent three weeks reinforcing with steel brackets.

If the beams broke, the ceiling came down. And the ceiling was directly above the library's third-floor reading room.

Gerald looked at the man. The adventurer was screaming something unintelligible, waving the splintered shield. He wasn't attacking the Minotaur. He was trying to keep Toby from drowning.

"Move left," Gerald said. His voice boomed through the dungeon's internal speakers, amplified by the acoustics of the flooded room.

The man froze. He looked up at the darkness, then at the sinking beast.

"Left! Into the alcove! The water level is dropping there due to the sump pump intake!"

It was a lie.

There was no sump pump. But there was a drainage grate Gerald had installed last week because he hated the smell of stagnant water.

It led to the city's storm sewer.

If he opened it, the water would rush out. But it would also create a vortex.

Gerald willed the grate open.

The water surged toward the corner. The suction was violent. The man, hearing the command and desperate for any instruction, grabbed Toby and lunged left. They hit the alcove just as the whirlpool formed in the center of the room.

The Minotaur, trapped in the center, was caught in the current. Its legs were tangled in the broken rebar Gerald had exposed earlier. The creature bellowed, a sound of pure rage, but it couldn't lift itself out. The water was draining, but the beast was anchored by its own weight and the debris.

Gerald felt the strain on the grate. The city sewer system wasn't designed to handle a sudden influx of dungeon-water mixed with biological matter. The pipes were vibrating.

**WARNING: MUNICIPAL INFRASTRUCTURE OVERLOAD.** **LIABILITY WARNING: IF DAMAGE EXCEEDS $5,000, CORE PENALTY APPLIES.**

Gerald didn't care about the penalty. He cared about the Minotaur's next move.

The beast stopped struggling. It looked at the man and the boy. Then it looked up.

It wasn't looking at them. It was looking at the ceiling.

Above them, a massive crack spiderwebbed across the stone. The Minotaur had torn through the primary support column. The structure was failing.

Gerald calculated the load. The ceiling was holding on by two secondary beams and a prayer.

If the beams snapped, the library above would collapse.

Hundreds of books.

Hundreds of patrons. Maybe even the librarian, Mrs.

Gable, who had been polite about the handrail installation last month.

Gerald couldn't let that happen. He was an inspector. He did not allow public safety hazards.

He needed to stabilize the ceiling. Fast.

He looked at the System menu. He had been saving up

"Compliance Credits" from the recent citations. He had issued four tickets in the last hour. Enough for one minor structural upgrade.

He didn't have time to build a new column. He didn't have time to install a new beam.

But he had the Minotaur.

**ACTION: UTILIZE UNLICENSED BIOLOGICAL ENTITY AS TEMPORARY STRUCTURAL SUPPORT.** **COST: 200 HP (CORE STAMINA).** **RISK: CRITICAL FAILURE.**

Gerald didn't hesitate. He reached out with his mind, grabbing the Minotaur's left leg.

The creature shrieked as Gerald's will forced the limb upward.

The muscle fiber tore. Bone cracked. But the leg rose.

Gerald slammed the beast's raised leg against the underside of the cracked ceiling.

The impact shook the room. Dust rained down. The Minotaur howled, a sound of agony that drowned out the roaring water. Its leg was jammed into the fissure, acting as a jack.

The ceiling stopped sagging.

The man stared. Toby stared.

Gerald felt his own stone body cracking in sympathy. The strain was immense. He was holding up the weight of the library with the broken limb of a monster.

The water was gone now. The room was a swamp of mud and broken concrete. The grate was clogged.

The Eye of the System, the white room from before, flickered back into existence in Gerald's mind. The verdict was coming.

**HEARING SESSION: OPEN.** **CASE: DUNGEON CORE #9942 vs. MUNICIPAL CODE.** **SUBJECT: USE OF HOSTILE SPAWN AS STRUCTURAL COMPONENT.**

The Eye hovered in the air above the mud. It was translucent, glowing with a cold, bureaucratic light.

"The defendant is accused of violating Section 4, Paragraph 12: Prohibition of Using Non-Constructive Entities for Load-Bearing Purposes," the Eye droned. Its voice was devoid of emotion.

Gerald's consciousness snapped back into the dungeon interface, and he had to argue. He had to win.

If he lost, the System would purge the dungeon.

He would die. The library would collapse anyway.

"Objection," Gerald said. His voice was calm. Flat. The voice he used when telling a contractor they had to tear out the drywall because the vapor barrier was missing.

The Eye paused.

"State your objection," it said.

"The entity in question," Gerald pointed his mental cursor at the Minotaur, which was now whimpering, its leg pinned by the ceiling and the mud, "was not used as a structural component. It was *contained*."

The Eye tilted.

"Elaborate."

"Under Ashford Municipal Code, Section 8, any object obstructing a public right-of-way must be removed or secured," Gerald said. "The Minotaur was an obstruction.

It was blocking the egress route.

I secured it.

The fact that it is currently supporting the ceiling is a coincidental outcome of proper containment protocols, not an intentional architectural modification.

"

Silence.

The man with the shield lowered his weapon. He looked at Gerald, confused.

The Eye processed the data. It scanned the dungeon's logs. It saw the citations.

It saw the handrails. It saw the fire extinguishers mounted at perfect height.

"Interpretation accepted," the Eye said. "However, the use of biological matter in structural support violates the sanctity of the dungeon's aesthetic. A fine of 500 Compliance Points is levied."

Gerald felt the points drain away. He didn't have 500. He had 120.

**DEBT NOTICE ISSUED.** **ASSET SEIZURE IMMINENT.**

The floor beneath Gerald's stone body dissolved, and the System was taking his dungeon. Piece by piece. Starting with the weakest links.

The mold spores vanished first.

Then the rat. Then the handrails.

Gerald's vision went dark at the edges. He was shrinking.

He looked at the man.

"You have to leave," Gerald said. "Now."

"We can't leave you," the man said. He took a step forward.

"I am not the dungeon," Gerald said. "I am the Core. And I am being audited for bankruptcy."

The man stopped. He looked at the shrinking walls. He looked at the Minotaur, which was still groaning, still holding up the ceiling.

"If the Core dies," the man said, "the dungeon collapses."

"Yes."

"And if the dungeon collapses..."

"The library collapses. Yes."

The man looked at the Minotaur. He looked at his broken shield. He looked at Toby, who was shivering.

Then, the man did something stupid, and he walked over to the Minotaur. He climbed onto the beast's back. He grabbed the horns.

"Hey!" the man shouted at the air. "I am appealing the seizure!"

The Eye flickered.

"Appeals are for violations," the Eye said. "Not for asset management."

"This is a violation!" the man shouted. "You are violating the Code of Conduct for Adventurer-Core Interaction! Rule 7! You cannot destroy a dungeon that is currently hosting a registered party during an active quest!"

Gerald felt a jolt. He hadn't registered the party. They had just walked in.

But the System... the System was literal.

The Eye paused. It scanned the man. It scanned Toby. It checked their status.

**PARTY STATUS: UNREGISTERED.**

"Rule 7 does not apply," the Eye said.

The man gritted his teeth, and he reached into his pack. He pulled out a small, glowing crystal. A Registration Token.

He smashed it on the ground.

**PARTY REGISTERED.** **QUEST LOG STARTED: "THE INSPECTOR."**

The Eye stopped.

The dissolving walls froze.

The debt notice hovered, suspended in time.

Gerald stared. The man had just spent his last resource, a token meant for teleporting home, to register a quest. A quest Gerald hadn't created.

The System was confused. It didn't have a protocol for an adventurer creating a quest *around* a Core's financial insolvency.

"Querying," the Eye said. "Defining parameters..."

The Minotaur groaned. The ceiling creaked.

Gerald looked at the man. The man was trembling, but he held his ground. He looked like a man who had just realized he was holding a grenade with the pin pulled.

"You idiot," Gerald said. "You don't know what you've done."

"I know I'm not leaving you here," the man said.

The Eye pulsed.

**NEW MECHANIC DETECTED: HOSTILE TAKEOVER.** **PARTY ASSUMES LIABILITY FOR CORE DEBT.** **CONDITION: PARTY MUST COMPLETE STRUCTURAL COMPLIANCE INSPECTION TO CANCEL DEBT.** **TIME LIMIT: 3 MINUTES.**

Gerald's eyes widened.

The man looked at the interface that had appeared in front of him. His face went pale.

"It wants us to inspect the dungeon?" the man asked.

"It wants us to fix it," Gerald said. "And we have three minutes before the library above us collapses because of a code violation we created to save you."

The man looked at the Minotaur. He looked at the broken shield. He looked at the crumbling ceiling.

"What do we need?" the man asked.

Gerald looked at the System map. He looked at the list of violations.

*Missing handrails. 15 citations.* *Inadequate lighting. 8 citations.* *Trip hazards. 42 citations.*

He looked at the man. He looked at Toby.

"Handrails," Gerald said. "We need to install handrails. And we need to do it in under one hundred and eighty seconds."

Toby looked at Gerald.

"Where do we get the steel?" Toby asked.

Gerald smiled. It was a thin, jagged thing.

"The Minotaur," Gerald said. "It has iron bands on its legs. And it's currently acting as a load-bearing support. If we cut them off..."

The Minotaur looked up. It heard them. It knew what was coming.

It roared.

**CITATION ISSUED: DUNGEON CORE #9942, "THE IRON MAIDEN."** **VIOLATION: INTENTIONAL SELF-SABOTAGE.** **PENALTY: IMMEDIATE TERMINATION.**

The text flashed red.

Gerald felt the cold grip of the System tighten around his mind.

The man raised his broken shield.

"Run," Gerald said.

But there was nowhere to run. The walls were gone. The floor was gone. There was only the mud, the beast, and the clock ticking down to zero.

Then the Minotaur lunged.
