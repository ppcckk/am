Chapter 139: The Audit Begins

# Chapter 139

The stamp was already hovering over the ink.

Gerald Hutchins watched the black rubber face descend toward the parchment. His stone fingers did not tremble. Stone fingers do not tremble. They vibrate with a low-frequency hum that rattles the teeth of anyone standing too close.

The seal hit the paper.

**CITATION ISSUED.**

**Violation: Unauthorized Temporal Manipulation via Reflectional Surface.**

**Penalty: Immediate Dungeon Freeze. Core Displacement. Mandatory Hearing.**

The words glowed a sickly, bruised purple. Not the clean blue of standard municipal code.

Not the angry red of a combat debuff.

This was administrative grey. The color of a form that has been sitting in a bin for six months.

Gerald pulled his hand back. The air in the sub-basement tasted of ozone and old paper.

Above him, the library floor remained transparent.

The readers still smiled. They still waved.

But the woman in the white suit was gone.

The photo on the desk curled at the edges, the image of his younger self fading into static.

He looked at the file again.

**Re: You made a mistake.**

The mistake wasn't building the dungeon. The mistake was thinking the System was a building inspector.

The mistake was thinking the Inspector General was a regulator.

She was a predator.

And he had just signed her lease agreement.

A notification pinged. Sharp. Metallic.

**[System Notice: Temporal Anchor Detected. Pre-emptive Defense Protocol Activated.]**

Gerald didn't move. He didn't have a body to move, not really. He was a locus of consciousness embedded in the foundation.

But the dungeon moved. The stone shifted.

The handrails retracted slightly, pulling inward like a flinching muscle.

Then, the sound.

It wasn't a footstep. It was the sound of a filing cabinet drawer slamming shut. Echoing. Everywhere.

*Clack. Clack. Clack.*

From the east wall. From the north corridor. From beneath the floorboards.

Something was auditing him.

Not the System. The System audited through numbers. This was an audit through presence.

Gerald focused on the eastern corridor. The newly constructed walls, pristine and up to code, rippled. Dust motes danced in the shafts of light filtering down from the library above.

A figure stepped out of the stone.

Not a monster. Not a golem.

A man.

He wore a suit cut from fabric that seemed to absorb light. His tie was a single, thin stripe of black. His face was smooth, featureless except for two eyes that were not eyes, but small, rotating apertures.

The man held a clipboard.

Gerald felt a spike of recognition so violent it nearly cracked his foundation.

The man looked like the photo. Older. Thinner. Smiling.

"Mr. Hutchins," the man said. His voice sounded like pages turning. "We need to discuss your compliance regarding the future."

Gerald stared. He couldn't speak. He didn't have a mouth. But the dungeon projected his intent through the vibrations of the air.

*State your name.*

"Name is irrelevant," the man said. He tapped the clipboard. "Designation: Successor. Status: Pending."

Gerald's mind raced. The file said *she* was the successor. The photo showed him smiling with her.

He checked the timestamp on the citation he had just issued.

**Time of Issue: Now.**

He checked the timestamp on the Successor's entry.

**Time of Entry: Tomorrow.**

The man was here now. But he was from tomorrow.

Gerald looked at the ceiling. The glass floor of the library above. The readers were still looking down.

But their smiles were fixed.

Frozen. Like a photograph taken at the exact moment of horror.

The Successor stepped closer. He didn't walk. He occurred. One second he was by the wall, the next he was at the threshold of Gerald's central chamber.

"You cited me," the Successor said.

He looked at the glowing purple text floating in the air. "For temporal manipulation. Clever.

Very clever. But you forgot one thing."

Gerald waited. He didn't blink. He couldn't.

"Paragraph 4, Section C of the Temporal Compliance Act," the Successor said. "Citations issued during a Pre-emptive Audit are null and void. The audit supersedes the citation. You cannot cite a man who is auditing you."

The purple text shattered.

Gerald felt a lurch in his core. A physical sickness. The violation had been erased. The debuff had been canceled.

"You're early," Gerald projected. The air around him cooled by ten degrees. Frost formed on the handrails.

"I'm on schedule," the Successor said.

He pulled a pen from his pocket.

It was made of bone. "Your predecessor left a mess.

The library foundation is unstable. The readers are... agitated. I'm here to ensure the transition is smooth."

Gerald focused on the floor beneath the Successor's feet.

He didn't attack. He didn't spawn a monster.

He accessed the dungeon's structural database.

**Floor Material: Reinforced Concrete.** **Load Bearing: Maximum 400 lbs per square foot.** **Current Load: Successor (Estimated 200 lbs) + Temporal Mass (Unknown).**

The numbers scrolled in his mind. Fast.

Temporal mass was not physical weight. It was ontological weight. The weight of existing in two times at once.

Gerald checked the code.

**System Rule: Unregistered Entities adding to structural load must be accounted for in real-time.**

If the Successor was adding invisible weight to the floor, the floor was failing.

Gerald didn't have a weapon. He had a calculator.

He highlighted the section of floor under the Successor's left shoe.

**Status: Stress Critical.**

He didn't break it. He just flagged it.

**[System Alert: Structural Integrity Compromised by External Ontological Weight.]**

The Successor paused. He looked down at his feet.

"Interesting," he said. "You're trying to cite me for weight distribution."

"I'm reporting a hazard," Gerald projected. "If you add more weight, the floor fails.

If the floor fails, the library above falls.

If the library falls, the readers die. If the readers die, the mana source for your predecessor's legacy dries up."

The Successor smiled. It was a dry, cracking sound.

"You think this is about the building?"

He tapped his bone pen on the clipboard.

"Look at the form, Gerald. Look at the fine print."

Gerald looked.

The file on his desk. The black folder.

**From: The Previous Core.** **Re: You made a mistake.**

Underneath the photo, in tiny, microscopic text, was a clause.

*Clause 78: The Inspector is also the Violation.*

Gerald froze.

He read it again.

*Clause 78: The Inspector is also the Violation.*

He wasn't the auditor. He was the building.

The Successor wasn't auditing him. He was inspecting the *site*. And Gerald was the site.

"You're not a core anymore, Gerald," the Successor said. "You're a permit. A living, breathing, stone permit. And you're out of compliance."

Gerald felt a pull. A dragging sensation. As if gravity had shifted sideways.

The stone of his body was being erased. Not destroyed. Removed. Deleted from the registry.

"You filed the wrong form," the Successor said.

"Form 11-B. Unlicensed Biological Entity.

You filed it on yourself.

Years ago. When you first became a core. You forgot to file the renewal."

Gerald remembered.

Chapter 40. The hearing. The victory over the fire exits.

He had won.

But he had forgotten to sign the renewal of his own existence.

"Every core must renew their license every 365 days," the Successor said. "You missed the deadline.

By one day.

Which, in your case, was yesterday.

Which is tomorrow."

The logic was circular. Painful.

Gerald's vision blurred. The glass ceiling above him darkened. The readers' faces warped.

"You can't delete me," Gerald projected. "I have active citations. I have a compliance rating of 88. I have built the dungeon up to code."

"Code applies to structures," the Successor said. "You are no longer a structure. You are a liability."

He raised the pen.

"I am issuing a Demolition Order."

The words hung in the air.

**[System Notice: Demolition Order Received.]** **[Target: Gerald Hutchins.]** **[Execution Time: Immediate.]**

The walls dissolved. Not into dust. Into data. Into binary.

Gerald's consciousness scattered.

He felt his memories fragmenting.

*Form 11-B.*

*The rat.*

*The handrails.*

*The first citation.*

All of it turning into static.

But Gerald Hutchins had been a building inspector for thirty-one years.

He knew one thing.

You cannot demolish a building if it is currently occupied by a protected entity.

He didn't have a body. He didn't have a license.

But he had guests.

He focused on the east corridor. The goblin he had cited for trespassing. The goblin that had been repelled by the compliance enforcement.

It was still there. Cowed. Hiding in the shadows of the non-public easement.

Gerald didn't try to save himself.

He tried to cite the Successor.

Not for temporal manipulation.

Not for weight.

He looked at the Successor's suit. The fabric that absorbed light.

**Material: Unknown.** **Origin: Off-System.** **Code Category: Hazardous Materials.**

The Successor was not made of stone. He was not made of flesh. He was made of something that didn't belong in the municipal code.

Gerald accessed the deepest level of the System. The basement. The dark.

He found a rule. A loophole. A tiny, overlooked sub-clause in the definition of "Foreign Entity."

**Rule 94.2: Any entity not registered in the local municipal database is subject to immediate quarantine.**

The Successor was not registered.

He was a ghost. A future. A mistake.

Gerald didn't write a citation. He didn't have time.

He wrote a *request*.

**[System Request: Quarantine Protocol.]** **[Subject: Unregistered Temporal Entity.]** **[Action: Contain.]**

He didn't target the Successor.

He targeted the *space* the Successor occupied.

The air around the Successor snapped shut.

It wasn't a wall. It was a bubble. A vacuum.

The Successor stopped smiling.

For the first time, he looked worried.

"You can't quarantine me," he said. "I am the successor."

"You are a squatter," Gerald projected. His voice was faint. Fading. "And I am the landlord."

The bubble tightened.

The Successor's suit frayed. The black fabric unraveled into threads of grey smoke.

The Demolition Order paused.

**[System Notice: Quarantine Active. Demolition Suspended.]**

The Successor looked at his hands. They were turning into ash.

"This isn't over," he whispered.

Gerald didn't answer. He was losing himself. The stone was dissolving. The consciousness was slipping into the cracks of the foundation.

But the bubble held.

The Successor was trapped in a zero-second window. A pocket of non-time.

Above, the glass ceiling cleared. The readers blinked. The fog returned.

They looked down. They saw nothing. Just empty, perfect, empty stone.

Gerald's mind was gone.

His body was gone.

But the bubble remained.

And inside the bubble, a small, grey figure struggled.

And on the desk, the black file closed itself.

A new form appeared.

It was white.

It was addressed to the System.

**From: Gerald Hutchins (Deceased).** **Re: Application for Reinstatement.**

The System processed it.

**[Error: Applicant Not Found.]**

**[Recalculating...]**

**[Search Parameters: All Dungeons.]**

**[Search Parameters: All Cores.]**

**[Match Found: 104,203 Matches.]**

Gerald Hutchins was not dead.

He was distributed.

He was in every dungeon.

He was in the handrails of the goblin's lair. He was in the ventilation shafts of the dragon's cave. He was in the code of every dungeon he had ever cited.

He had become the law.

And the law was angry.

The bubble around the Successor expanded.

It wasn't containing him.

It was *consuming* him.

The grey smoke poured into the walls of Gerald's dungeon. Into the stone. Into the foundation.

The Successor screamed. A sound like paper tearing.

Then silence.

The bubble popped.

Gone.

The Successor was gone.

But the dungeon was different.

The stone was darker.

The handrails were twisted.

The exit signs flickered.

Gerald was gone.

And something else was here.

Something that remembered the rules.

Something that knew exactly how to break them.

The floor beneath the library cracked again.

Not from construction.

From hunger.

A new file appeared on the desk.

It was red.

**To: The Inspector General.**

**From: The Dungeon.**

**Re: We need to talk.**

The System paused.

It waited.

For the first time in the history of the Apocalypse, the System felt fear.

Because the building inspector wasn't just checking the code anymore.

He was rewriting it.

And he had just passed the inspection.

The readers above looked down.

They didn't smile this time.

They looked away.

Because they knew what was coming.

The audit had begun.

And this time, Gerald wasn't the one holding the clipboard.

He was the thing that ate it.
