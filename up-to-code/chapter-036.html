Chapter 36

Gerald reached the east egress and found the appellant sitting in the antechamber.

It was not sitting. It was occupying. The distinction mattered. The antechamber was six feet wide, and the thing in it filled the width the way a body fills a bathtub, with the same uncomfortable compression. It was made of something that looked like wet clay compressed into a humanoid shape, except the proportions were wrong in a way that suggested the clay had been poured into a mold designed for something taller and then the excess had been cut away. Its head was flat. Its shoulders were too wide. Where a face should have been, there was a smooth surface with two indentations that might have been eyes or might have been structural stress points.

Gerald stopped at the threshold. He did not enter.

"You are the appellant," Gerald said.

The thing tilted its head. When it spoke, the sound came from the smooth surface where the face should have been, and it was the sound of gravel shifting.

"I am Core Seventeen. I appeal the Citation of fire egress violation, Section 7.4, Paragraph B."

Gerald opened the Hearing protocol. The interface expanded in his mind the way all System interfaces did, like a form that existed in a dimension slightly behind his own. The Hearing protocol had three phases: Identification, Argument, and Ruling. He was in Identification.

"State your name and authority," Gerald said.

"Core Seventeen. Authority: sub-basement, 800 blocks east, beneath the Ashford Industrial Park."

Gerald noted it. Industrial Park. He had never inspected that area. It was outside his jurisdiction, which meant he had never seen it, which meant he had no baseline for what it looked like. The Citation had been issued based on System-generated compliance data, not on Gerald's own inspection. That was a problem. He had cited something he had never seen.

The System interface pulsed. Phase One complete. Phase Two: Argument.

"Begin," Gerald said.

Core Seventeen's gravel-voice said: "Your Citation cites Section 7.4, Paragraph B. That paragraph applies to primary means of egress. The opening I am cited for is a secondary means of egress. Secondary egress is governed by Section 7.4, Paragraph C, which does not require the 36-inch minimum width. Paragraph C requires 28 inches. My opening is 31 inches. I am compliant."

Gerald checked. He pulled the code section from his mental library, the same way he had pulled it from physical binders for thirty-one years. Section 7.4. Paragraph B. Primary egress. Paragraph C. Secondary egress. Different requirements. Different thresholds.

Core Seventeen was right.

Gerald felt it in his stomach. Not the nausea of his flaw, not the physical illness of improvisation. Something worse. The specific, cold knowledge that he had made an error. That he had cited a violation that did not exist. That his paperwork was wrong.

He had never made an error. Thirty-one years. Not once.

The System interface showed his position in the Hearing. The argument phase was open. He could respond or concede. Concession meant the Citation was voided, the debuff reversed, and his Compliance Rating dropped by fifteen points. He was currently at 78. Fifteen would put him at 63. Below the threshold for his structural compliance passive. His physical damage reduction would drop to zero.

He had thirty minutes total. He had used four.

"Request for site inspection," Gerald said.

The interface blinked. Then it expanded. A new window opened, showing Core Seventeen's dungeon in cross-section, the way the System rendered all dungeons during a Hearing. It was a map. A blueprint. The same kind of blueprint Gerald had read ten thousand times.

Core Seventeen's dungeon was a network of tunnels beneath the Industrial Park. The tunnels were rough, unlined, and arranged in a pattern that Gerald's eye immediately rejected. Not because they were ugly. Because they were structurally illogical. Load paths that terminated in nothing. Support columns that were decorative. A central chamber with no clear egress to any secondary exit.

But Gerald was not looking at structure. He was looking at the spawn density markers. The System showed them as small dots, color-coded by entity type. There were a lot of dots in the central chamber.

"Occupancy load," Gerald said.

Core Seventeen's head tilted. "Occupancy load is not a cited violation category."

"It is now." Gerald pointed at the central chamber on the blueprint. "Your central chamber is 400 square feet. You have forty-seven active spawn entities in that chamber. The occupancy load for an assembly space of that size is thirty-two. You are fifteen entities over."

"That is not a fire code violation. That is not an egress violation."

"It is a Life Safety Code violation. Section 10.1. Occupancy load is a Life Safety Code category. It has been in every building code since 1927. You have forty-seven entities in a space rated for thirty-two. If any one of them triggers a panic response, the egress capacity is insufficient to evacuate them. The exits are not undersized. The exits are correct for the rated occupancy. They are undersized for the actual occupancy."

The interface paused. Then it updated. A new citation appeared in the Hearing record. Not the original fire egress citation. A new one. Section 10.1. Occupancy load violation. Severity: Major.

Core Seventeen's clay face did not change, but the gravel-voice shifted. Lower. "You cannot add a citation during a Hearing. The Hearing is limited to the original citation."

"Section 10.1 is not added. It is substituted. The Hearing protocol permits amended citations where the original citation is found to be erroneous and a valid violation is identified within the same inspection scope." Gerald had not known this rule existed. He was discovering it in real time, the way he had discovered most rules, by reading the fine print that nobody else bothered to read. "The original citation was erroneous. You proved that. The amended citation is valid. I am substituting."

The interface processed this for three seconds. Then it ruled.

Amended citation accepted. Original citation voided. Hearing proceeding on amended basis.

Core Seventeen's body shifted. The clay compressed further, as if the thing were trying to make itself smaller. "The occupancy load is not a valid dungeon code category. Dungeons are not assembly occupancies."

"They are if they have an assembly function. Your central chamber has a spawn concentration point. Spawn concentration is an assembly function by System definition. Section 3.2, Definitions. Assembly: a space in which entities are concentrated for a common purpose."

Core Seventeen said nothing.

"Ruling," the interface said. "Amended citation upheld. Appellant found in violation of Section 10.1. Debuff applied: spawn regeneration reduced by forty percent for the duration of the violation. Appellant has thirty days to remediate or face escalation to Inspector General review."

Core Seventeen's body began to change. The clay darkened. The structural stress points where the eyes should have been deepened. The thing was being debuffed in real time, its physical form reflecting the System's enforcement.

Gerald felt the familiar satisfaction of a correct ruling. It was brief. It was always brief. The next problem was already forming.

Core Seventeen stood. It was shorter than it had been when it entered. The compression had made it lose height. It looked at Gerald with its two stress-point eyes.

"You cited the wrong parcel," Core Seventeen said.

Gerald stopped. "What?"

"The Citation you filed. The original Citation. You identified the parcel as Industrial Park sub-basement, 800 blocks east. That is correct. But the parcel number you cited, the System identifier, is not mine. It belongs to Core Twenty-Two. I am Core Seventeen. Core Twenty-Two is my neighbor. It is four blocks west."

Gerald pulled up the Citation. He looked at the parcel identifier. He looked at the System-assigned Core ID. He looked at the address.

He had cited Core Seventeen's address with Core Twenty-Two's parcel ID. A clerical error. A transposition. He had copied the wrong number from the compliance report.

Core Twenty-Two was debuffed. Core Twenty-Two had not appealed. Core Twenty-Two was now running at reduced spawn regeneration with no recourse because it had not filed within the window.

"Where is Core Twenty-Two?" Gerald asked.

Core Seventeen began to leave. It turned at the threshold, its compressed body squeezing back through the egress.

"Core Twenty-Two is a magma core," Core Seventeen said. "It has been operating for forty-three years. It has never been cited. It does not know what a Citation is. But it felt the debuff. It knows someone did this to it."

Core Seventeen was gone. The antechamber was empty. The air was still.

Gerald stood in the doorway and looked at the Citation in his interface. The parcel number stared back at him. A single digit transposed. A mistake he had made because he had been filling out forms at 2 AM without a second check. Because he had been working alone. Because he had never had a second check in thirty-one years.

The System interface pulsed. A new notification.

Core Twenty-Two. Status: Hostile. Distance: 4.2 blocks west. Direction of travel: east. Speed: increasing.

Estimated time to Gerald's dungeon: eleven minutes.

Gerald looked at his dungeon. His handrails. His egress signage. His thirty foot-candles of compliant lighting. His 4,000 square feet of carefully measured, properly ventilated, fully compliant sub-basement.

It was not built to contain magma.

He opened his spawn list. Three mold spores. A water leak. A confused rat.

He began to type.
