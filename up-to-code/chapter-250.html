Chapter 250: Reading the Fine Print

# Chapter 250

The mud was cold. It tasted of iron and stagnant water, filling Gerald's lungs with a grit that coated his tongue.

Above, the ceiling groaned, a sound like twisting metal, as the debris from the east wing settled into a precarious heap.

The fissure had widened. Water poured from the breach, a steady, churning torrent that dragged Gerald's legs sideways.

He did not swim. Swimming was unstructured. Swimming invited chaos.

Gerald planted his feet in the slurry. He found the edge of the fallen load-bearing wall, the concrete block still vibrating from the goblin's hammer strike. The block had cracked, but it had not shattered. It sat at a forty-five-degree angle, propping up a section of the upper floor that should have been gone.

Gerald's nose bled freely now, dripping into his mouth. He spat the blood onto the concrete. It looked like rust.

**SYSTEM NOTIFICATION: STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY COMPROMISED.** **CURRENT STATUS: CRITICAL.** **TIME TO AUDIT: 08:12.**

The timer burned in his peripheral vision.

Eight minutes. Not ten.

The collapse had accelerated the audit protocol.

The System did not like irregularities.

Around him, the three unconscious adventurers lay half-buried in the mud. The goblin, still clutching the hammer, stared at Gerald with wide, unblinking eyes.

He did not move.

He did not help.

He was waiting for the end.

Gerald ignored him. He looked at the gap where the floor had dropped. Below the mud, in the darkness, something shifted.

Not water. Something with mass.

Something that moved with intent.

His sensory range, previously limited to the library's sub-basement, had expanded downward. The emergency structural collapse had broken the boundary between his dungeon and the natural caverns beneath Ashford. He was no longer inspecting a building. He was inspecting a geology lesson.

A new notification pinged, soft and polite.

**NEW VIOLATION DETECTED: UNAUTHORIZED SUBTERRANEAN EXPANSION.** **CATEGORY: ZONING / LAND USE.** **SEVERITY: HIGH.** **PENALTY: IMMEDIATE DELETION OF NON-COMPLIANT STRUCTURES.**

Gerald's heart hammered against his ribs, a frantic, uneven rhythm. This was worse than the fire code violations. This was worse than the missing handrails.

If the System deleted the non-compliant structures, it would delete the cavern.

It would delete the water. It would delete the adventurers who had fallen into it.

He had to make the chaos compliant.

He had to make the cave look like a building.

"Get up," Gerald said. His voice was flat, devoid of urgency, though his hands shook as he wiped blood from his chin. "Stand on the concrete block."

The goblin flinched. "What?"

"Stand.

On. The. Block." Gerald pointed a muddy finger at the fractured concrete.

"You are an unlicensed biological entity on a restricted construction site. If you do not stand on the designated walkway, you are a tripping hazard.

And tripping hazards are fined."

The goblin swallowed.

He looked at the darkness below, where the water churned. Then he looked at Gerald's stern, blood-streaked face.

The goblin stepped onto the block. The concrete held.

Good.

Gerald closed his eyes. He accessed the interface.

Not the combat menu.

Not the spawn menu. The *Architect's* menu. The one he had ignored for two hundred and forty-nine chapters because it required too much paperwork.

He pulled up the zoning map.

The cave was irregular, jagged, undefined. It had no coordinates.

It had no square footage.

It was legally nonexistent.

Gerald focused on the water. He focused on the mud. He focused on the jagged rock walls.

*If it is not a cave, it is a sub-basement,* he thought. *If it is a sub-basement, it requires an egress plan.*

He opened his inventory. He pulled out a stack of Form 7-C: *Temporary Egress Signage (Emergency Use Only)*.

He had printed three hundred of them last week, anticipating the audit.

He had planned to hang them on the library doors.

He had not planned to hang them on the face of a collapsing geode.

He dragged the digital form into the physical space.

The air shimmered. A neon-yellow sign materialized on the wet rock wall, ten feet above the waterline. It read: **EXIT ROUTE A. FOLLOW LIGHTS.**

The water continued to rise. The creatures in the dark shifted closer. Gerald could hear their wet, slapping footsteps.

They were blind. They navigated by scent and vibration.

The sign did not help them.

He needed a barrier.

He pulled out **Compliance Barrier: Yellow Caution Tape (Reinforced).**

He unspooled the roll. The tape stretched out, glowing with a faint, blue System light. It was not just plastic.

It was a legal boundary.

Anything that crossed it without a permit was in violation.

Gerald tied one end of the tape to the goblin's belt. The goblin yelped, trying to pull away, but Gerald held firm.

"Hold this," Gerald said. "Do not let go. If you drop it, you are personally liable for the fines."

"What fines?" the goblin squeaked.

"Unapproved entry into a restricted zone. Five hundred soul-currency. Deducted directly from your lifespan."

The goblin went pale. He gripped the tape.

Gerald pulled the other end of the tape and slapped it onto the wet rock wall, creating a straight line across the mouth of the cavern. The tape hummed. The water splashed against it, unable to cross. The creatures in the dark hit the invisible wall and recoiled, screeching in confusion.

They were not attacking a dungeon. They were attacking a code violation.

Gerald checked the timer. **05:45.**

The water was still rising, but it was contained. The tape held the boundary. The creatures were trapped on the other side, clawing at the barrier.

But the ceiling was still cracking.

The main support column in the center of the room—the one that had been damaged by the initial collapse—was leaning. If it fell, the entire floor would drop into the cavern. And the cavern was now a registered sub-basement.

If the floor fell into it, the System would interpret it as a structural failure of the core infrastructure.

Gerald needed to shore up the column.

He looked at the debris. Broken pipes. Twisted rebar. Shattered concrete.

He looked at the goblin.

"Move the hammer," Gerald said. "To the left. Against the pipe."

"Why?"

"To create a lever fulcrum."

The goblin moved the hammer.

He did not question it.

He had seen the tape stop the monsters. He had seen the sign appear.

He believed Gerald knew what he was doing.

Gerald grabbed a piece of twisted rebar. He jammed it under the leaning concrete block.

He used the hammer as a wedge.

He pushed. His muscles screamed. The headache behind his eyes was blinding, a white-hot spike of pain that made his vision blur.

He was not strong.

He was an inspector. He weighed one hundred and eighty pounds.

He had weak knees.

He had high cholesterol.

But he knew where the stress points were.

He pushed against the weakest point of the fracture. He did not try to lift the weight. He tried to redirect the vector.

The block shifted. A fraction of an inch.

The ceiling groaned. Dust rained down.

Gerald pulled out **Form 14-A: Temporary Structural Support.**

He printed it. He slapped it onto the rebar.

The paper glowed gold. The rebar hardened. It turned from twisted metal into a rigid, System-approved strut.

The column stopped leaning.

**SYSTEM NOTIFICATION: TEMPORARY SUPPORT APPLIED.** **INTEGRITY STABILIZED FOR 10 MINUTES.** **NOTE: THIS IS A BAND-AID, INSPECTOR. FIX THE REAL PROBLEM.**

Gerald wiped his face with his sleeve. The blood smeared, turning his shirt a dark, rusty brown.

**TIME TO AUDIT: 02:30.**

The water was still rising inside the tape barrier. The creatures were still screaming outside it. The adventurers were still unconscious.

Gerald looked at the cavern beyond the tape. It was dark. Unexplored. Unregistered.

The System had registered the sub-basement because Gerald had defined it. He had put a sign on it. He had put a tape line on it. He had made it *real*.

But he had not inspected it.

If the Inspector General arrived and found an unexplored, unregistered sub-basement attached to a registered dungeon, it would be a Class A violation. Unpermitted expansion. The penalty was total dungeon dissolution.

Gerald had to inspect the cavern. He had to file the paperwork. He had to prove it was compliant.

He looked at the goblin.

"You," Gerald said.

The goblin flinched. "Me?"

"You are holding the tape. You are part of the boundary. You are inside the registered zone."

"I am holding the tape," the goblin said.

"Yes. And you are breathing air from the registered zone.

That makes you an occupant.

Occupants have rights.

But they also have duties."

Gerald pointed into the dark water beyond the tape.

"Go in."

The goblin stared at him. "Are you insane?"

"I am authorized. Under Emergency Protocol 7. You are assisting in a code enforcement action. Your presence in the water is necessary to verify the depth of the standing water for the drainage report."

"I am a goblin. I don't know how to write reports."

"You know how to swim. You know how to see. I will write the report. You will provide the data."

Gerald held out his hand. In it was a small, glowing crystal. A **Depth Gauge**. He had bought it for three copper coins, expecting it to be a toy.

"Take this. Walk into the water. Hold it up. When the water reaches your waist, shout 'Waist.' When it reaches your chest, shout 'Chest.' When it reaches your neck, shout 'Neck.' Then come back."

The goblin looked at the tape. He looked at the creatures, which were now trying to tear the yellow barrier with their claws. He looked at Gerald.

"If I die," the goblin said, "you will pay my soul-debt."

Gerald paused. He looked the goblin in the eye.

"I will file a Claim of Negligence against the dungeon owner," Gerald said. "Which is you. You are the unlicensed entity.

You are the hazard. If you die, it is because you failed to wear Personal Protective Equipment.

I will note this in the citation."

The goblin's eyes narrowed. He hated being cited. He hated the paperwork.

But he hated dying more.

He took the crystal. He stepped off the concrete block. He broke the plane of the yellow tape.

The System flashed red.

**VIOLATION: UNAUTHORIZED ENTRY.** **USER: GOBLIN_04.** **STATUS: TEMPORARY WAIVER ACTIVE.**

The red light faded. The goblin stepped into the water.

It was ice cold. He shivered. He walked deeper.

"Waist," he grunted.

Gerald pulled out a clipboard. He wrote: *Water depth: 1.2 meters.*

The goblin walked further. The creatures hissed, but the tape held. The goblin was on the other side of the tape, but he was still connected to Gerald by the physical tether of the tape.

"Chest," the goblin said. His voice trembled.

Gerald wrote: *Water depth: 1.5 meters. Risk of hypothermia noted.*

The goblin reached his neck. He stopped.

"Neck," the goblin whispered. "I can't see anything. It smells bad. It smells like... meat."

Gerald's pen stopped moving.

Meat.

Not rot. Not sewage. Meat.

Something was breeding down there. Something large enough to smell like meat from twenty feet away.

Gerald looked at the timer. **00:45.**

He had thirty seconds to finish the inspection.

"Come back," Gerald said. "Now."

"No," the goblin said. He was staring into the dark. "There is... something looking at me."

Gerald felt a chill that had nothing to do with the water.

"Come back," Gerald said, louder.

"It says..." The goblin tilted his head. "It says... 'Hello'."

Gerald's blood ran cold.

Goblins did not speak the Common tongue. They spoke Goblin. The System translated their thoughts, but it did not translate their speech.

Unless the creature speaking to him was not a goblin.

Unless the creature was a Core.

**SYSTEM NOTIFICATION: FOREIGN CORE DETECTED IN SUB-BASEMENT.** **IDENTITY: UNKNOWN.** **ACTION: INSPECT OR CONQUER.**

Gerald looked at the tape. He looked at the goblin.

He did not conquer. He did not fight.

He opened his interface. He clicked on the **Citation** button.

He typed: **VIOLATION: UNLICENSED RESIDENTIAL OCCUPATION OF PUBLIC WORKS.**

He hit **SERVE**.

The citation flew through the air, glowing white, and passed through the water, through the goblin, and into the darkness.

For a second, nothing happened.

Then, the water exploded.

Not from above. From below.

A hand burst from the surface. It was not a goblin hand. It was long, pale, and covered in scales. It wrapped around the goblin's throat.

The goblin didn't scream. He just stopped moving.

Gerald pulled the tape. The goblin was dragged backward, choking, his eyes rolling white.

Gerald pulled with all his strength. He planted his feet. He leaned back.

The goblin slid across the mud, out of the water, onto the concrete block. The pale hand retreated into the dark.

Gerald fell to his knees, gasping. He looked at the goblin. The goblin was alive, but he was paralyzed. Blue lines traced his veins.

**STATUS: PARALYZED. CAUSE: PHYSICAL TRAUMA.**

Gerald looked at the water. The creature was still there. It was waiting.

**TIME TO AUDIT: 00:05.**

The timer hit zero.

The air in the room changed. The dust stopped falling. The water froze in mid-splash.

A figure materialized in the center of the room. It was tall, made of light and parchment. Its face was a blank sheet of paper with the words **INSPECTOR GENERAL** stamped on it.

It looked at Gerald. It looked at the goblin. It looked at the tape.

**AUDIT INITIATED.** **SUBJECT: DUNGEON CORE GERALD HUTCHINS.** **LOCATION: SECTOR 4 SUB-BASEMENT.** **STATUS: PENDING REVIEW.**

The Inspector General turned its blank face toward the water.

**OBSERVATION:** **UNLICENSED ENTITY DETECTED IN REGISTERED ZONE.** **ACTION REQUIRED: EVICT OR LEGALIZE.**

Gerald stood up. His legs shook. He wiped the mud from his hands. He adjusted his tie.

He looked at the Inspector General. He looked at the dark water. He looked at the paralyzed goblin.

He had to make the monster legal.

He opened his mouth.

"I invoke," Gerald said, his voice cracking, "the Right of Adverse Possession."

The Inspector General paused. Its paper face rippled.

**QUERY: JUSTIFICATION?**

Gerald looked into the dark.

"Because," Gerald said, "I am the only one here who reads the fine print."

The water rose again. And this time, Gerald knew he could not stop it.
