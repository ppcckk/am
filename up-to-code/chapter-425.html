Chapter 425: It Was Compliant

# Chapter 425

The green light pulsed in rhythm with Mira's heartbeat. It was a sickly, artificial rhythm, forced by the System's desperate correction protocol.

Gerald watched the light crawl up her arm, turning her veins into glowing circuitry.

Her face went slack, then rigid. The air in the sub-basement dropped ten degrees. Frost formed on the exposed pipes.

The clock on the wall ticked. Seven minutes.

The Parasite's death throes were over.

Its corpse lay slumped in the drain, a heap of black ichor and shattered chitin. But the dungeon was still dying.

The walls were weeping.

The floor tiles cracked in a spiderweb pattern that spread from the center of the room outward.

Gerald stepped forward. His knee popped. He ignored it. He reached into his vest pocket and pulled out a small, battered ruler.

"Mira," he said.

Mira did not answer. Her eyes were open, but they saw nothing. The green light pulsed again.

**CORE REGISTRATION IN PROGRESS.** **WAIT.** **INTEGRITY CHECK: FAILED.**

"It's failing," Mira whispered. Her voice sounded like it was coming from inside the walls. "I can't hold it. I'm not a Core."

"No," Gerald said. "You're not."

He walked to the nearest wall. The plaster was bubbling.

A chunk fell away, revealing the damp brick underneath.

Gerald placed his ruler against the exposed brick, and he measured the gap between the crack and the nearest load-bearing column.

"Forty-two centimeters," he muttered.

He turned to Mira.

"Mira. Stop resisting."

"I can't stop. It's inside me."

"It's not inside you. It's inside the dungeon." Gerald pointed to the wall. "You are the liability.

The System wants a Core. You offered yourself.

The System is trying to convert you.

But you are human. The conversion is tearing the structure apart because the foundation isn't human. The foundation is municipal.

"

Mira's breath hitched. A tear tracked through the dust on her cheek.

"What do I do?"

"Resign."

"I can't just quit."

"You can." Gerald pulled a small, folded paper from his other pocket. It was a standard Form 88-D: *Voluntary Dissolution of Private Entity.* He had printed it three hours ago, before the Parasite even broke through the ceiling.

He held it out.

"Sign it. Right now."

Mira looked at the paper. The green light flared brighter. **ERROR. ENTITY IN TRANSITION. NO ACTIONS PERMITTED.**

"It won't let her sign," Gerald said. The words tasted like copper.

He looked at the clock. Six minutes.

The floor shook.

A crack split the concrete near Mira's feet. She stumbled.

Gerald caught her elbow.

His grip was tight. He felt the cold radiating from her skin. It felt like touching a live wire.

"The System is stuck in a loop," Gerald said. "It's trying to make a human into a dungeon. It's trying to make a city building into a dungeon. It's confused."

"So let it be confused," Mira said. "It's killing us."

"No. Confusion is the tool." Gerald looked around the room.

He looked at the handrails he had installed in Chapter 12, and he looked at the emergency lighting he had upgraded in Chapter 45.

He looked at the fire suppression system he had installed in Chapter 102.

Every single thing in this room was up to code.

That was the problem.

The System didn't want a dungeon up to code. It wanted a dungeon that *was* the code. A Core that was the rules.

Gerald had spent four hundred chapters enforcing rules. He had cited dragons for lack of exits. He had fined necromancers for improper waste disposal. He had audited liches for zoning violations.

He had treated the System as a regulatory body.

But the System wasn't a body. It was a machine. And machines could be jammed.

"Mira," Gerald said. "Do you remember the first inspection? The one that started this?"

"Yes."

"You asked me if the building was safe. I said no. It had a leak.

It had mold. It was non-compliant.

"

Mira stared at him.

"So?"

"The System registered me because I was *compliant* with the code of my job. I followed the rules. I filed the report. I was honest."

Gerald looked at the crumbling wall.

"The Parasite died because it violated the ventilation code. The System killed it for poor airflow."

He walked over to the main ventilation duct. The fan had stopped spinning. The blades were clogged with the black sludge from the Parasite.

"The room is sealed," Gerald said.

"No airflow. No pressure differential. The System is trying to push power through a closed loop.

It's a pressure vessel.

"

He looked at Mira.

"We need to break the loop."

"How?"

"We cite the dungeon."

Mira blinked. "You can't cite yourself."

"I can cite the *space*." Gerald pulled his pen from his pocket. It was the same pen he had used to write the confession.

He held it up.

"The System requires a target to be physically present to serve a citation. The target must be the violation."

"The violation is the human," Mira said.

"No. The violation is the *non-compliant structure*." Gerald pointed to the sealed duct. "That duct is blocked. That is a Code 404 violation: *Obstruction of Emergency Egress.*"

He walked to the duct. He didn't try to clean it. He didn't try to fix it.

He kicked it.

The metal casing dented. The fan blade bent. The blockage shifted, but it didn't clear.

"Insufficient force," Gerald said. He looked at Mira.

"Mira.

You are the owner now.

You are the Core. You have control over the environment.

"

"I don't know how."

"You don't have to know how to control it. You have to know how to *violate* it."

Gerald picked up a heavy pipe wrench from the tool shelf. He handed it to Mira.

"Break the wall," he said.

Mira took the wrench. Her hand trembled. The green light flickered.

"What?"

"The wall is load-bearing," Gerald said. "If you break it, the ceiling collapses. The dungeon dies."

"And us?"

"And us."

Mira looked at the wrench. She looked at the wall. She looked at Gerald.

"Why would I do that?"

"Because the System cannot register a Core for a destroyed dungeon," Gerald said. "It cannot convert a human into a core for a building that no longer exists. The conversion will fail. The System will cancel the transaction."

"That's insane," Mira said.

"It's the only way to reset the registry without losing your soul," Gerald said. "We lose the dungeon. We keep the people."

The clock hit five minutes.

**CONVERSION AT 85%.** **WARNING: SUBJECT INTEGRITY CRITICAL.**

Gerald could feel his own body fading. His edges were blurring. He was becoming part of the architecture.

He looked at his hand. The fingers were translucent.

"Mira," he said. His voice was weak. "Do it."

Mira raised the wrench, and she swung.

The wrench hit the brick. A chunk flew out. Dust plumed into the air.

The System screamed. A high-pitched whine that vibrated in Gerald's teeth.

**ERROR. STRUCTURAL FAILURE IMMINENT.** **HALT OPERATION.**

Mira swung again. And again.

The crack in the wall widened. The ceiling groaned. A support beam bowed.

Gerald watched. He felt a strange calm. He had spent thirty-one years ensuring buildings stood up. Now he was ensuring one fell down.

It felt wrong. It felt right.

The green light on Mira's arm turned white. It spread to her chest.

**CONVERSION FAILED.** **RESETTING.**

The wall collapsed.

It wasn't an explosion, which was a sigh. The bricks slid down. The ceiling panels dropped.

The floor buckled.

Mira was thrown backward. Gerald caught her, but he was too light. He slid across the floor. The dust cloud swallowed them.

Gerald lay on his back. Debris rained down on his chest. He couldn't breathe. The weight was crushing.

Then, silence, and the green light was gone.

Gerald coughed. He pushed a piece of drywall off his face. He sat up.

He was in a hole. A jagged, uneven hole in the earth.

The library was gone.

The sub-basement was gone. The dungeon was gone.

Mira lay next to him. She was unconscious, but breathing. She was human. Just human.

Gerald checked his pulse. It was steady.

He looked around. The System's interface was gone.

No blue boxes.

No notifications.

No Compliance Rating, and he was just a man in a hole.

He dug them out. It took twenty minutes. His hands bled.

His back screamed. But he did it.

When they emerged into the daylight, the Ashford Municipal Library was a ruin. Smoke rose from the rubble. Fire trucks were arriving. Police tape was already being strung up.

A police officer walked over. He looked at Gerald. He looked at Mira.

"Mr. Hutchins," the officer said. "You okay?"

Gerald nodded. He brushed dust from his jacket.

"Was this an accident?" the officer asked.

Gerald looked at the hole. He looked at the wreckage.

"Structural failure," Gerald said. "Due to unauthorized modifications to load-bearing walls."

The officer frowned.

"Unauthorized modifications?"

Gerald pulled a small, crumpled piece of paper from his pocket, which was the Form 88-D. It was blank. Mira hadn't signed it.

She hadn't had to.

The System had canceled the request.

But Gerald had signed it. In his head.

He handed it to the officer.

"Permit application," Gerald said. "Denied."

The officer took the paper. He looked confused. He handed it to his sergeant.

"Mr. Hutchins," the sergeant said. "We need to ask you some questions."

"I am available for inspection," Gerald said.

***

Three weeks later, Gerald sat in a temporary office in the City Hall annex.

The library was being rebuilt. The budget was in shambles. Gerald was currently employed as a *Consultant on Post-Catastrophe Structural Integrity*. It was a fancy title for *Guy Who Says No.*

He had a stack of forms on his desk. Fifty of them. All from different contractors. All trying to rebuild the library in record time.

All cutting corners.

Gerald picked up the top form. *Form 11-B: Request for Temporary Egress Route.*

He read it. He measured the proposed width. He checked the material specifications.

He stamped it **REJECTED.**

His hand didn't shake.

Mira walked in. She was wearing a hard hat and a high-visibility vest. She looked tired. She looked alive.

"Hey," she said.

Gerald looked up.

"Hello."

"The council approved the new foundation," she said. "They want to go with the steel frame. Faster. Cheaper."

Gerald frowned.

"Steel is good. But the fire rating?"

"Class A," Mira said. "Full sprinkler system. Three exits."

Gerald nodded.

"Acceptable."

He picked up his pen. He signed the approval.

Mira watched him.

"You okay?"

"I am within code," Gerald said.

Mira smiled. It was a real smile. Not the grim, tired one from the basement.

"Do you miss it?" she asked. "The magic? The monsters?"

Gerald looked at the pile of rejected forms. He thought about the dragon with the blocked fire escape. He thought about the lich with the illegal grave site.

"No," Gerald said. "I have a new case. The new parking garage. They're using substandard concrete."

Mira laughed. "Sounds fun."

"It is necessary."

Gerald stood up. He picked up his clipboard. He walked to the door.

He paused. He looked back at Mira.

"Did you ever wonder," Gerald said, "if the System was real?"

Mira shrugged.

"Does it matter? We're here. The building's going up."

Gerald nodded. He turned and walked out into the hallway.

He passed a janitor mopping the floor. The janitor was leaving a wet spot near the exit.

Gerald stopped.

"Excuse me," he said.

The janitor looked up.

"Yeah?"

"You have a slip hazard," Gerald said. "Signage is required within five feet of the wet area."

The janitor rolled his eyes.

"It's just water, man."

Gerald didn't smile. He didn't argue. He just pointed to the corner where a small yellow sign sat on a shelf.

"Please retrieve it," Gerald said.

The janitor grumbled but walked to get the sign.

Gerald waited. He watched the janitor place the sign. He checked the placement. It was correct.

He walked away.

His steps were steady. His back didn't hurt. The pen in his pocket felt heavy, but it was just a pen.

The sun was setting over Ashford.

The library's skeleton stood against the sky. It was ugly. It was incomplete.

It was compliant.

Gerald checked his watch. It was time for his lunch break.

He went to get a sandwich.
