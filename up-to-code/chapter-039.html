Chapter 39: Undefined Judgment

# Chapter 39

Gerald was measuring the rubble.

He had a tape measure in one hand and a clipboard in the other, and the clipboard was wrong, of course.

Form 7-A was for post-inspection deficiency documentation, and the rubble was not a deficiency, which was the current state of the structure. But the clipboard was what he had, and the act of measuring was what kept his hands from shaking.

Twenty-two hours and fourteen minutes remained on the Administrative Closure timer.

The Summary Judgment notification sat in his interface like a tumor. He had read it nine times.

He had searched for it in the compliance manual, and he had searched for it in the help files, in the glossary, in the appendix on enforcement actions.

It was not there.

This was the first thing Gerald noticed. Not the judgment itself, but the absence of its documentation.

The System had issued Summary Judgment against him, but the procedure was not codified anywhere he could find.

No form number.

No procedural requirements. No service protocol.

He set down the tape measure. He pulled up the notification again. He read the text.

"Authority to bypass Hearing process and apply permanent debuff to target Core without appeal."

Bypass Hearing. Fine. He could live without a Hearing.

He had never wanted one. Hearings were for people who argued.

Gerald argued nothing.

Gerald cited.

"without appeal" was different. Without appeal meant the judgment was final upon issuance. And "upon issuance" meant the moment the notification appeared in his interface.

Which meant the judgment had been issued at a distance. Through the interface. Without Gerald's physical presence at the point of issuance.

Gerald stood still.

Every Citation he had ever written in thirty-one years of municipal service required physical presence.

You could not mail a Citation. You could not email a Citation.

You could not leave a Citation on a windshield under a wiper blade.

You had to hand it to the person. You had to say their name. You had to watch them take it.

The System's rules said the same thing. He had read it in the manual, page 214, subsection 7. "Citations require the target to be physically present to serve."

Summary Judgment was a Citation. It was a debuff applied by the System's regulatory authority, and was, in every functional sense, a Citation that had skipped the Hearing step.

But it had not skipped the service step.

Gerald opened a new document. Form 14-C.

Procedural Deficiency Objection, and he had never filed one.

He had read the form. He had memorized the form.

He had not filed one because he had never encountered a procedural deficiency in thirty-one years.

His hands were steady now, and he filled in the fields.

Target action: Summary Judgment, issued at 03:47:12. Deficiency: Action lacks proof of physical service.

Target was not present at point of issuance.

No witness of service. No signature of receipt. Requested remedy: Void action.

Reissue with proper service protocol, and he filed it.

The System accepted it.

Summary Judgment: PAUSED. Procedural Deficiency Review initiated. Service must be completed within 48 hours or action is voided.

Gerald exhaled.

Then the second notification came.

Procedural Deficiency Review: An Inspector General representative will appear at the registered footprint of Core Gerald Hutchins to complete service. Estimated arrival: 11 minutes.

Gerald looked at his dungeon. Four thousand square feet of rubble.

Torn handrails.

Smoldering wiring. A Compliance Rating of 3 out of 100.

He had eleven minutes to make his dungeon look like it had not been destroyed.

He started running, and the handrails went up first. He could not rebuild the corridors, but he could straighten the existing rails where they were bent. He could clear debris from the egress paths. He could pull the smoldering wires from the junction boxes and cap them with the rubber fittings he had ordered three weeks ago and never installed because he had been busy citing goblins.

He worked fast. He worked in the order he always worked.

Egress first.

Then fire suppression.

Then electrical. Then structural.

Eight minutes. He had cleared two corridors. The sprinkler pipes were still ruptured.

The knob-and-tube wiring was still exposed in three rooms. The rubble was still rubble.

Five minutes. He pulled a handrail straight with both hands. The metal groaned. He groaned with it.

Three minutes. He capped the last junction box. He wiped his hands on his pants.

One minute.

The air in corridor seven thickened, which did not shimmer or glow; it simply became denser, as though someone had opened a window onto a colder room. The temperature dropped four degrees.

The System's interface flickered.

A figure stood in the corridor.

It was not a person. It was not a creature.

The System had shaped it to approximate a person, similar to how a dungeon core creates a spawn from raw mana.

It wore a suit. The suit was gray. The face was the kind of gray that made you think of filing cabinets and waiting rooms and the particular silence of a government office at 4:47 PM.

"Gerald Hutchins." The voice was flat. Not unkind.

It was flat, simply a form.

"I am Inspector General representative, Designation 7714.

I am here to complete service of Summary Judgment."

Gerald straightened. He pulled out his clipboard.

"Before you serve, I need to establish the current condition of the structure for the record."

7714 tilted its head. "The Summary Judgment is already determined. The service is procedural."

"I understand. I am not contesting the judgment. I am documenting the condition of the structure at the time of service. Standard practice."

7714 was quiet for two seconds. "Proceed."

Gerald walked the corridors. He measured. He noted.

He filled out Form 7-A on his clipboard, which was the wrong form, but he did not correct it. He did not want to admit the form was wrong.

He wanted 7714 to see that he was doing documentation.

That he was a professional, and that the rubble was not a failure of management but a temporary condition being addressed.

He showed 7714 the capped junction boxes. The straightened handrails. The cleared egress paths.

"Compliance Rating is still three," 7714 said.

"It will be twelve by end of day.

Twenty-two by tomorrow morning. I have the sprinkler parts ordered.

I have the wiring rated for the load.

I have the handrails up to spec."

"Noted."

7714 pulled a document from the air. It was not paper. It was a manifest of light, dense with text. Gerald could not read it from this distance.

"This is the Summary Judgment. It applies a permanent forty percent reduction to all spawn quality, all trap damage, and all passive resistances.

It is not appealable, which is not reversible.

It takes effect upon service."

Gerald took the document. He signed it. His hand did not shake.

The debuff hit. He sensed it like the drop in pressure before a storm. A drop.

A settling. The dungeon groaned around him, and something deep in the foundation shifted.

He had lost forty percent of everything.

"Service complete," 7714 said. "Procedural Deficiency Review closed. The action is valid."

"Thank you." Gerald meant it. He meant it because the service was valid, which meant the procedure was correct, which meant the System was functioning as designed, which meant he could work within it. He could not work outside it. He could not work in a system that did not follow its own rules.

7714 did not leave. It stood in the corridor, looking at the walls.

"Inspector General note. The footprint expansion that created your current registered area was not authorized by this office."

Gerald stopped writing. "I received the authorization notification. It came through the interface. It cited Citation history as the reason."

"The authorization was generated by a different process. A boundary adjustment triggered by adjacent Core activity. Your footprint expanded into space that was already registered to another Core."

Gerald set down his clipboard.

"Which Core?"

"Core Designation: Vethrax. Registered footprint: adjacent to yours on the north boundary. Your expansion consumed approximately three hundred square feet of registered Vethrax territory."

Gerald felt something cold move through him that was not the debuff.

"That is not my fault."

"The System does not require fault for an Adverse Possession violation. The violation is the overlap. The remedy is the same regardless of intent."

"Adverse Possession." Gerald had never heard this violation type.

He searched the manual. He found it. Page 489.

Subsection 12.

"Occupation of registered dungeon space by an unauthorized Core. Penalty: immediate forfeiture of occupied space and Citation severity multiplier applied to all future Citations issued by the offending Core."

"Vethrax has already filed a complaint," 7714 said. "The complaint was processed before your footprint expansion was complete. The violation is active."

Gerald looked at the north wall. His north wall. The wall that had been rubble an hour ago and was now rubble again, because the rubble was his, and the rubble was now also Vethrax's.

"When does Vethrax arrive?"

"Vethrax is already here."

The north wall moved.

Not collapsed. Not exploded. Moved. The rubble shifted like a curtain being pulled aside, and through the gap came a sound like grinding stone, and then a shape.

It was not humanoid, which was architectural, and a column of dark stone, segmented, each segment carved with a different symbol. A hole sat at the center, where a face should have been.

The hole was not empty; the hole was full of something that moved and watched and was not a face.

"Core Vethrax," 7714 said. "You have filed a complaint. The accused is present. You may proceed."

Vethrax did not speak.

It pulsed.

The hole in its center dilated, and Gerald felt something press against his interface. A notification.

Not from the System. From Vethrax. Direct.

Unregistered. A message that should not have been possible.

It said: "You are in my walls. I will remove you. I will remove your walls. I will remove you."

Then the hole contracted.

Vethrax settled into the rubble like a stone sinking into water. It was inside his dungeon. Inside his footprint.

Inside his walls.

7714 watched. It did not intervene. It had no authority to intervene. This was a Core dispute, not a compliance matter.

Gerald looked at the timer. Twenty-one hours and forty minutes.

He looked at Vethrax, which was growing. Slowly. Segment by segment. Pushing deeper into his dungeon like a root finding water.

He looked at his clipboard. Form 7-A. The wrong form.

He turned the page. He started a new document.

Form 22-D. Emergency Boundary Dispute.

He had never filed one. He had read the form. He had memorized the form.

His hands were steady.

But his Compliance Rating was three. His debuff was forty percent. His spawn list was three mold spores, a water leak, and a confused rat; the thing in his north wall was eating his dungeon from the inside out.

He wrote the word INFESTATION at the top of the form and did not stop.
