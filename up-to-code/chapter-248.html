Chapter 248: Permitting the Teeth

# Chapter 248

The clipboard trembled in Gerald's hand. It was a steady, rhythmic tremor, like a metronome set to a tempo that induced nausea.

Linda watched it.

She had worked with Gerald Hutchins for fourteen years in the Department of Public Works. She had seen him reject a permit because the shade of beige paint on a mailbox was three percent off-spec. She had never seen him look at a staircase made of human molars with the expression of a man who had just realized he forgot to renew his parking meter.

"Gerald," Linda said. Her voice was thin. "Gerald, those are teeth."

Gerald did not look at her. He looked at the System interface hovering in his peripheral vision, a translucent overlay that only he could see. The text scrolled rapidly, a waterfall of red warnings. **STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY: CRITICAL.** **ETHICAL VIOLATION: CLASS A.** **UNLICENSED BIOLOGICAL GROWTH: DETECTED.**

He clicked his pen. The sound was a sharp *snap* in the dusty silence of the library sub-basement.

"It is not a staircase," Gerald said.

"It is an unpermitted egress route. Constructed from organic matter.

Zero load-bearing capacity.

No handrails."

Linda stared at him. "It's growing."

"I am aware." Gerald stepped forward. One foot.

Two.

The tiles beneath his shoes crunched.

He held the clipboard out like a shield. "Stand back, Linda.

I am serving papers."

The air in the pit shifted. The clicking of coins ceased, replaced by a wet, grinding sound, like stones being crushed in a mortar.

The first tooth-step of the spiral expanded, widening enough for a man to stand on.

A face emerged from the darkness below. It was not a face. It was a collection of features arranged by a committee that hated symmetry.

Eyes too wide.

A mouth that was merely a horizontal slit.

A hand reached up. It had too many fingers.

"Who is in charge of this establishment?" Gerald called out. His voice was flat, the tone he used for contractors who tried to install a drain without a trap.

The face tilted. A voice bubbled up from the pit, sounding like gravel sliding down a well. "I am the Underwriter. I am the debt."

Gerald frowned.

He checked his pen. The ink was blue. He preferred black.

He switched to a backup pen from his vest pocket.

"I am Gerald Hutchins, Inspector of Code Compliance, City of Ashford.

You have a structure here." Gerald pointed the pen at the spiraling teeth.

"It violates Section 4.12, Subsection C: Prohibited Materials in Public Access Routes. Organic matter that is not permanently fused to the substrate is considered a biohazard.

You are also missing an exit sign. And you do not have a certificate of occupancy."

The Underwriter laughed. The sound caused the teeth to shudder.

"You cite me?

I am the law. I am the audit."

Gerald felt a familiar tightness in his chest.

The panic. The paralysis.

He hated improvisation.

He hated ambiguity. But the System was feeding him data. His dungeon's Compliance Rating was dropping.

If it hit thirty, the ventilation debuff would kick in, and his mold spores would suffocate. He needed to raise his rating, or lower the enemy's.

He looked at the new dungeon's name in his interface. **THE UNDERWRITER'S VAULT.**

**STATUS: HOSTILE.** **COMPLIANCE RATING: UNKNOWN.**

Gerald's mind raced.

He couldn't fight a dragon.

He couldn't fight a lich. But he could fight a building.

He knew the loopholes. He knew the fine print. He had spent thirty-one years reading the small type where other people saw only blank space.

The System allowed citations. But citations required a target to be "physically present" and "in a state of active operation." A dungeon was a building. The Core was the landlord. If the Core was exposed, it was vulnerable.

Underwriter's Vault had no walls. It was a pit. A hole in the world.

Gerald looked at Linda. "Do you have a level?"

Linda blinked, startled out of her trance. "What?"

"A laser level. Or a spirit level. Do you have one in your bag?"

Linda fumbled at her side. She pulled out a yellow tape measure and a bubble level. "I... I have this."

Gerald took the bubble level. He walked to the edge of the pit.

The teeth were rising, forming a platform.

The Underwriter's face loomed closer. It smelled of copper and old blood.

"You are attempting to expand your footprint into a protected municipal easement," Gerald said.

He held the bubble level up to the first tooth-step. The bubble was dead center.

"The substrate is stable.

But the expansion is unauthorized."

"I own this land," the Underwriter rumbled. The many-fingered hand slammed against the library floor, cracking the linoleum.

"You own nothing," Gerald said. "The city owns the sub-basement. The library owns the air rights. And the System owns the compliance."

Gerald opened his clipboard. He didn't write a citation for the Underwriter. He wrote a citation for the *System*.

**CITATION: 11-404.** **TARGET: SYSTEM LOGIC.** **VIOLATION: CONFLICT OF JURISDICTION.** **DETAIL: The target structure (Underwriter's Vault) is built upon a protected municipal foundation without a variance permit. the target does not exist within the legal parameters of the Dungeon Zone. All structures within the Zone are invalid.**

It was a bluff. A massive, terrifying bluff.

The System did not have a legal department.

It had a code.

And code was binary. It either existed or it didn't.

The text in Gerald's vision flickered.

**PROCESSING...** **ERROR: JURISDICTIONAL AMBIGUITY DETECTED.** **HOLDING ACTION.**

The Underwriter froze. The grinding noise stopped. The teeth stopped growing.

"What have you done?" the voice asked. It was smaller now. Less gravelly.

"I have filed a variance request," Gerald said. He kept his pen poised. "Until the variance is approved by the Inspector General, your construction is suspended.

Your operations are halted. Your spawns are frozen."

The System message flashed.

**STATUS: CONSTRUCTION SUSPENDED.** **CITATION ISSUED TO: THE UNDERWRITER'S VAULT.** **PENALTY: TEMPORAL FREEZE (DURATION: 10 MINUTES).**

The pit went silent. The Underwriter's face hung in the air, a mask of frozen rage.

Linda exhaled. It was a long, shuddering breath. "You... you stopped time?"

"I stopped construction," Gerald corrected.

He wiped his glasses. "The variance request is a formality. It will be denied.

But until it is, he cannot build.

He cannot spawn. He cannot attack."

Gerald looked down at the teeth. They were still there. Still real. But they were not moving.

"We need to seal it," Gerald said.

"Seal it?" Linda asked.

"Concrete. Fast-set. We pour it over the opening. We bury the entrance."

"You can't pour concrete on a dungeon core!"

"I am not pouring concrete on a core," Gerald said. "I am pouring concrete on a non-compliant construction site. It is a standard remediation procedure."

He looked at the demolition crew's abandoned supplies. There was a mixer truck outside, idling. The driver had fled, but the engine was running. The hose was still attached.

"Go outside," Gerald said. "Start the pump."

Linda stared at him. Then she looked at the frozen face of the Underwriter. She looked at Gerald, who was already walking toward the exit, his clipboard tucked under his arm.

"Gerald," she said. "If you seal the entrance, how do you fight him later?"

"I don't fight him," Gerald said.

"I wait for the appeal. He will appeal. He will demand a hearing.

And when he comes to that hearing, he will have to stand in the light. And I will cite him for not having a fire extinguisher within thirty feet of his spawn point."

Linda nodded. She ran for the door.

Gerald remained in the basement.

He walked to the edge of the pit.

The silence was heavy. He could feel the System watching him.

The Inspector General was auditing. The audit would be thorough. It would check every line of his code.

Every rule he had twisted to create this loophole.

If the Inspector General found that Gerald had manipulated the jurisdictional logic, he would be stripped of his Core status. He would become a man again. Just Gerald. A fifty-four-year-old man with bad knees and no home.

He looked at the clipboard. He wrote one more note.

**NOTE TO SELF: CHECK FIRE EXTINGUISHER INVENTORY IN NEXT ROOM. IF MISSING, CITE SELF.**

He couldn't help it. It was muscle memory. It was the code.

A vibration hummed through the floor.

The teeth in the pit vibrated. The temporal freeze was breaking. Ten minutes was a short time.

It was not enough to pour the concrete.

It was not enough to build a wall.

Gerald looked at the stairs.

The spiral went down into the dark. He needed to go down. He needed to see the Core.

He needed to find its weakness.

He stepped onto the first tooth.

It was cold. It was sharp. It bit into his shoe.

**WARNING: BIOLOGICAL MATERIAL DETECTED.** **DO NOT PROCEED.**

Gerald ignored the warning. He stepped onto the second tooth. Then the third.

He descended into the Underwriter's Vault.

The darkness was absolute.

But Gerald could see it.

He could see the layout. He saw the corridors.

He saw the rooms. He saw the trap doors.

And he saw the violations.

The walls were too thin. The floors were slippery. The lighting was insufficient.

Gerald smiled. It was a small, dry smile.

This was not a monster's lair. This was a building code disaster.

He reached the bottom of the staircase. The Underwriter stood there.

It was tall.

It wore a suit made of shadows. Its face was still the one from the pit, but larger. More detailed.

"You are in my house," the Underwriter said.

"You are in a condemned structure," Gerald said.

He raised his pen.

"Serve," Gerald said.

The System flashed.

**CITATION: 11-405.** **TARGET: THE UNDERWRITER'S CORE.** **VIOLATION: OBSTRUCTION OF MEANS OF EGRESS.** **DETAIL: The staircase you have constructed is not wide enough to allow for the rapid evacuation of occupants in the event of a fire. Minimum width: 36 inches. Actual width: 18 inches.**

The Underwriter roared. The sound shattered the dark.

But the citation took hold.

**PENALTY: MOBILITY REDUCTION (-50%).**

The Underwriter's legs buckled. It tried to step forward, but its stride was halved. It stumbled. It crashed into a wall of gold bars.

Gerald backed away. He kept his distance. He kept his pen raised.

He saw something behind the Underwriter.

A door. A small, wooden door.

It looked out of place.

It looked like a janitor's closet.

Gerald pointed at it.

"Is that a secondary egress route?"

The Underwriter froze. Its many eyes widened.

"No," it whispered.

Gerald's heart hammered against his ribs. He knew that look. It was the look of a landlord who had just realized he had failed a smoke detector inspection.

"Open it," Gerald said.

The Underwriter didn't move.

Gerald opened his clipboard. He wrote.

**CITATION: 11-406.** **VIOLATION: LACK OF SECONDARY EGRESS.** **PENALTY: STRUCTURAL COLLAPSE IMMINENT.**

The System message turned black.

**WARNING: STRUCTURAL FAILURE PREDICTED IN 3... 2...**

The walls of the Vault groaned. The gold bars bended.

Gerald ran. He ran back up the staircase.

He didn't look back.

He didn't care if the Underwriter was screaming.

He cared about the exit.

He reached the top. Linda was there. She was holding a hose.

"Pour it!" Gerald yelled.

Linda hesitated. She looked at the pit. The walls were cracking. Debris was falling.

"Do it, Linda!"

She pulled the trigger. Concrete sprayed out. Thick, gray slurry.

It hit the teeth. It hit the stairs.

It covered the view of the Underwriter.

Gerald stood back. He watched the concrete flow. He watched it seal the hole.

The ground shook. A boom echoed from below. Then silence.

Linda lowered the hose. She looked at Gerald. She looked at the fresh concrete slab where the staircase had been.

"Did we kill it?" she asked.

Gerald looked at his clipboard. The System message was gone. The Underwriter's Vault was no longer on the map.

It was buried.

It was inaccessible.

But his own dungeon was shaking.

**COMPLIANCE RATING: 48/100.** **WARNING: VIBRATIONAL DAMAGE TO FOUNDATION.** **NEW VIOLATION: CRACKS IN LOAD-BEARING WALLS.**

Gerald looked at his walls. Cracks spiderwebbed across the plaster. The vibration from the collapse had damaged his own structure.

He looked at Linda. Then he looked at the cracks.

"We have to fix this," he said.

"How?" Linda asked.

Gerald looked at the cracks. He looked at the new System notification popping up in his vision.

**NEW OPPORTUNITY: EMERGENCY REPAIR PERMIT.** **COST: 100 SOULS.** **BENEFIT: IMMEDIATE STRUCTURAL REINFORCEMENT.**

Gerald stared at the number. One hundred souls. He had three mold spores and a rat. He had no souls.

He looked at Linda.

"Linda," he said. "Do you know any adventurers who are currently trespassing in my library?"

Linda looked around the empty room. "No."

Then a crash came from the main floor. Above them. Through the floorboards.

Voices. Shouting. The clatter of armor.

"They are in the library," Gerald said.

He walked toward the stairs that led up. His face was blank.

"We need souls, Linda. And we need them now."

He opened his clipboard. He found a blank form.

**FORM 7-A: AUTHORIZATION FOR ADVENTURER DETENTION AND EXPLOITATION.**

He looked at the form. He looked at the ceiling.

"I am going to invite them in," Gerald said.

Linda looked at the new form. Her eyes went wide.

"Gerald, you can't just trap people in your dungeon."

Gerald looked at her. His eyes were cold.

"The building is under construction," he said. "All visitors must sign a liability waiver. And if they cannot pay the fine for trespassing, they will be detained until their debt is cleared."

He picked up his pen.

"Welcome to the Ashford Municipal Library," he said. "Please mind the cracks in the floor."

He opened the door to the stairs.

And down the hall, three adventurers walked into the trap.
