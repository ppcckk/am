Chapter 331: Citing the Mask

# Chapter 331

The sludge swallowed Gerald's left shoe. It was a sensible black oxford, steel-toed and broken in, but the municipal budget for footwear had always been tight, and the replacement cost was currently irrelevant compared to the structural integrity of the City Hall annex.

He pulled his foot free with a wet, sucking sound that echoed through the collapsing sub-basement. Above him, the library floorboards groaned under the weight of three tons of displaced concrete and one angry Mayor.

"I have the permit," Gerald repeated, though his voice came out as a dry rasp. He held the dog-eared form high, his knuckles white against the crumbling parchment. The paper glowed with a faint, sickly yellow light, the System's recognition of a valid legal instrument.

The Mayor's voice did not come from the ley lines this time. It came from the doorway.

Standing on the edge of the pit, silhouetted by the sparks raining down from the severed mana conduits, was Mayor Elias Thorne. He was not alone.

Flanking him were two figures in obsidian armor that shimmered with illegal enchantments.

High-level dungeon guards. The kind that cost more than Gerald's annual salary.

"Put that paper down, Hutchins," Thorne said.

He stepped forward, his boots crunching on broken tile.

"You are a core.

You are property of the System, but you are also a resident of my city.

And I am revoking your lease.

"

Gerald adjusted his glasses. They were smudged with dust.

He cleaned them on his vest.

"A lease requires a signed agreement between landlord and tenant.

The lease was voided when you stored Class-4 Mana Crystals in a zone designated for paper storage. That is a zoning violation of the highest order.

"

Thorne laughed. It was a sharp, barking sound.

"Zoning?

You think you can cite *me*? I own this city.

I own the ley lines.

I own the air you breathe.

"

"Clause 14," Gerald said. "Hazardous material storage. Immediate condemnation authority."

"You're a rat in a hole," Thorne said.

He gestured to the obsidian guards.

"Remove him.

Break the core.

I'll rebuild the library on top of his ashes. It will be cheaper than paying the fines.

"

The guards stepped forward. Their movement was fluid, predatory. They carried maces that hummed with kinetic energy.

Gerald did not move. He tapped the air in front of him. A translucent blue window appeared, hovering just above the sludge.

"System," Gerald said. "Initiate Appeal Hearing: Case Number 99-041-B. Appellant: Mayor Elias Thorne, Personal Dungeon Core 'The Gilded Cage.' Appellee: Gerald Hutchins, Core 'Ashford Municipal Library Sub-Basement.'"

Thorne froze. The guards hesitated, their maces lowering an inch.

"You can't appeal a condemnation," Thorne said, his voice losing its boom, becoming merely annoyed. "Condemnation is final."

"Condemnation is final *if* the citation is uncontested," Gerald said. "But you contested it.

You declared jurisdiction.

That triggers a Hearing.

The System requires due process. It is in the Code.

Page 4,102, subsection D.

"

Thorne stared at him.

"There is no such page."

"Check your manual," Gerald said. "Or don't. The System keeps it. I keep the receipt."

The air in the room shifted. The dust motes stopped swirling. The rain of sparks hung suspended in mid-air. The color drained from the world, replaced by a sterile, bureaucratic gray.

**[SYSTEM NOTICE: HEARING INITIATED.]** **[LOCATION: NEUTRAL VOID.]** **[PARTICIPANTS: CORE 001 (G. HUTCHINS) & CORE 884 (E. THORNE).]** **[TIME LIMIT: 15 MINUTES.]** **[RULES: ARGUE STRUCTURAL LOGIC. WINNER GAINS TEMPORARY JURISDICTION OVER LOSER'S INFRASTRUCTURE.]**

The sludge vanished. The library vanished.

Gerald and Thorne stood on a flat, gray plane.

There was no ceiling, no floor, just an infinite expanse of gray light. Two podiums stood facing each other. Behind Thorne, a holographic projection of the City Hall's dungeon layout appeared, complex and beautiful, filled with golden corridors and mana rivers.

Behind Gerald, a crude map of his four thousand square feet of sub-basement appeared, which was ugly. It was messy.

It was compliant.

"You have ten minutes to present your case," a voice said. It sounded like a printer jamming.

Thorne straightened his tie. He looked at Gerald with pity.

"You are a fool, Hutchins.

I am not appealing. I am prosecuting.

I am arguing that your core is unstable.

Look at your layout.

" He pointed to Gerald's map.

"Your ventilation shafts are non-standard. Your egress routes are insufficient for crowd control.

You have no security protocol for the main lobby. Your Compliance Rating is barely fifty. How can you claim to have authority over a structure with a Rating of ninety-two?

"

Gerald opened his mouth. He closed it.

He opened a new window.

Not the hearing window. His personal inventory.

"I am not arguing my layout," Gerald said. "I am arguing your load-bearing capacity."

Thorne blinked.

"My what?"

"Your dungeon is built on a ley line nexus," Gerald said.

He walked to the edge of his podium.

"The Gilded Cage draws power from the intersection of three major mana streams.

The City Hall foundation was designed in 1974 by the Department of Public Works.

I inspected that foundation.

"

"You inspected a library basement," Thorne said. "Not my dungeon."

"Same bedrock," Gerald said. "Same soil composition.

Clay-heavy.

Poor drainage.

In 1974, the city added a three-story annex to City Hall without reinforcing the pilings. They used standard concrete mix.

They did not account for the thermal expansion of mana crystals.

"

Thorne's eyes narrowed.

"That is ancient history."

"It is current code," Gerald said. "Code 12-C: Structural Integrity in Mana-Intensive Zones. If a structure relies on mana crystals for power, it must have thermal expansion joints every fifteen feet. Your dungeon has none."

"I have stabilization runes," Thorne said.

"Runes are not code," Gerald said. "Runes are a patch.

A patch is not a repair, and a repair is a repair. You have violated Code 12-C for thirty years. I have the inspection reports from 1974.

I have the failure analysis from the 2012 sinkhole incident.

And I have the seismic data from last week's tremor.

"

Gerald produced a stack of papers. They materialized in his hand with a soft *thud*. He placed them on his podium.

"The tremor was not natural," Gerald said.

"It was the sound of your foundation cracking. You felt it. You felt the instability.

That is why you are afraid. That is why you sent the guards.

Not to kill me.

To silence the witness.

"

Thorne's face went pale.

"That is absurd."

"Is it?" Gerald asked. "Check your dungeon's integrity rating. Check the stress points on the eastern wing. The wing where you keep your personal treasury."

Thorne waved a hand. A new hologram appeared.

It showed the City Hall dungeon's internal stress map. Red lines pulsed along the eastern corridor. They were thick.

Throbbing. Critical.

The gray void seemed to lean in.

**[SYSTEM NOTICE: STRUCTURAL ANOMALY DETECTED.]** **[ANALYSIS: CRITICAL FAILURE IMMINENT.]** **[CAUSE: THERMAL EXPANSION OF CLASS-4 CRYSTALS WITHOUT EXPANSION JOINTS.]** **[REFERENCE: CODE 12-C.]**

Thorne looked at the red lines. He looked at Gerald. For the first time, he did not look like a Mayor. He looked like a man who had forgotten to pay his car insurance.

"How..." Thorne whispered. "How did you know about the crystals?"

Gerald picked up a piece of chalk from his podium. He wrote on the gray floor.

"I didn't know about the crystals," Gerald said.

"I knew about the concrete. Concrete cracks when it gets hot. Magic gets hot.

You put magic in concrete without room for it to stretch, which is a building code violation. I have cited it in forty-seven buildings over the last thirty years.

You are number forty-eight.

"

Thorne stepped back.

"Stop the hearing. I withdraw my jurisdiction claim."

"You cannot withdraw," Gerald said. "The hearing has begun. The System has accepted the evidence.

You are now on record as having violated Code 12-C. The penalty is not fines.

The penalty is remediation.

"

"Remediation?" Thorne said. "I have gold. I have mana. I can pay any fine."

"You cannot pay for a collapsed foundation," Gerald said. "You have to fix it. And you have to fix it now. Before the tremor hits again."

The gray void flashed red.

**[SYSTEM RULING: APPEAL DENIED.]** **[CITATION SUSTAINED: CODE 12-C.]** **[PENALTY: MANDATORY STRUCTURAL RETROFIT.]** **[TIME LIMIT: 24 HOURS.]** **[IF UNMET: CORE DESTABILIZATION AND COLLAPSE.]**

The void dissolved, and they were back in the sub-basement. The sludge was still there, but the ceiling was holding.

The guards had lowered their maces. They were looking at Thorne, not Gerald.

Thorne stood trembling. His face was the color of old parchment.

"You have twenty-four hours," Gerald said. He tucked the citation form into his vest.

"I will conduct a follow-up inspection at this time tomorrow.

If the expansion joints are not installed, I will issue a second citation. This one will be for negligent maintenance. The penalty for that is total demolition.

"

Thorne looked at the guards.

"Get out. Get me the architects.

Get the engineers.

Get everyone."

The guards hesitated. They looked at Gerald. Gerald was pulling his foot out of the remaining sludge. He stood up, brushed the dirt off his vest, and put his glasses back on.

"Leave the permit," Gerald said.

Thorne sneered.

"This isn't over, Hutchins. You have made enemies of the entire Council."

"The Council has a building code," Gerald said. "So do you."

Thorne turned and marched out of the sub-basement. The guards followed, their armor clanking softly.

Gerald was alone, and he looked around the dark, dusty room. The library above was safe.

For now.

But his own dungeon was still a mess.

The ventilation was still poor. The egress routes were still too narrow.

And now, he had a twenty-four-hour deadline to ensure his own compliance, just in case the System decided to audit *him* next.

He pulled out a small notebook. It was black, hardcover, and filled with tiny, cramped handwriting. He flipped to a fresh page.

**TASKS FOR TOMORROW:** 1. Install emergency exit signage in Sector 4. 2. Widen corridor 3 to minimum 36 inches. 3. **CRITICAL:** Locate source of water leak in Sector 2. 4. **CRITICAL:** Install thermal expansion joints in Mana Conduit 1.

He stared at item four. He did not have the mana crystals. He did not have the engineers. He did not have the time.

He had one hour before the next spawn cycle. He had three mold spores and a rat.

He needed help.

Gerald closed his notebook. He walked to the edge of the dungeon's boundary, where the stone wall met the earth of the city above. He placed his hand on the cold stone.

"System," he said. "Open Community Board."

A new window appeared. It was not a dungeon interface. It was a bulletin board. It was the Adventurer's Guild local chat, filtered by proximity.

Gerald typed. He did not use combat language. He did not use magic words. He used the language of the municipal code.

**[POST: ASSISTANCE REQUIRED.]** **[REQUEST: SKILLED ENGINEERS AND MANA-CONDUIT INSTALLERS.]** **[COMPENSATION: GUARANTEED COMPLIANCE CERTIFICATION FOR ALL WORK PERFORMED. NO LIABILITY FOR STRUCTURAL DAMAGE DURING CONSTRUCTION.]** **[NOTE: THIS DUNGEON IS UP TO CODE. YOUR WORK WILL BE INSPECTED AND APPROVED. NO FEE FOR INSPECTION.]**

He hit send, and he waited.

Ten seconds passed. Twenty.

Then, a message appeared.

**[USER: "GrimmTheSmith"]** **[MESSAGE: "Wait, you're charging zero for inspection? And you guarantee liability coverage? Is this a scam?"]**

Gerald typed back.

**[REPLY: "I am Gerald Hutchins. I have been a building inspector for thirty-one years.

I do not scam.

I cite. If you do the work right, you do not get cited. If you do it wrong, I will tell the System you did it wrong.

That is worse than a lawsuit, which is a debuff to your equipment. Do you want debuffed equipment?

"]**

A pause.

**[USER: "GrimmTheSmith"]** **[MESSAGE: "Holy hell.

You're that guy. The one who cited the Crimson Keep for fire safety violations. My friend lost his sword because of that.

You're a monster.

"]**

**[REPLY: "I am a public servant. Do you want the job?"]**

**[USER: "GrimmTheSmith"]** **[MESSAGE: "I have a crew. We can start in an hour. But we want a guarantee. If the City Council attacks us, you protect us."]**

Gerald smiled. It was not a nice smile. It was the smile of a man who knew the exact statute for self-defense against unlicensed contractors.

**[REPLY: "The City Council is currently under a mandatory retrofit order.

If they attack you, they are attacking a work site protected by a temporary construction easement, which is a felony in the System. I will cite them for harassment of licensed workers.

It will take their Compliance Rating down by ten points. Do they want ten points?

"]**

**[USER: "GrimmTheSmith"]** **[MESSAGE: "I'll bring the crew. And the drills. And the cement."]**

**[REPLY: "Bring the cement. The library needs it."]**

Gerald closed the window, and he turned back to his dungeon. The rat was peeking out from behind a pile of rubble. It looked confused. It looked hungry.

Gerald walked over to it. He did not spawn food. He did not fight it.

He walked to the corner of the room where the water leak was. He pulled a small wrench from his utility belt. He tightened the pipe. The dripping stopped.

The Compliance Rating ticked up.

**[+1 VENTILATION.]** **[+1 STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY.]**

It was not enough. It was never enough. But it was a start.

And then, the ground shook.

Not a tremor. A stomp.

The ceiling cracked. Dust fell from above. A voice boomed from the library floor, amplified by magic.

"Gerald Hutchins! Come out! By order of the Adventurer's Guild Local Chapter, you are being challenged for a Boss Clear! We are coming down!"

Gerald looked up. He looked at the crack in the ceiling. He looked at the clock.

The adventurers were here. He had no monsters. He had no traps. He had a wrench and a rat.

And he had a citation form in his pocket, and he sighed. He picked up the form.

He walked to the center of the room. He cleared his throat.

"System," he said. "Open Boss Arena."

The gray void appeared again. But this time, it was not a hearing room. It was an arena. A massive, circular coliseum made of gray stone.

And standing in the center of the arena was not Thorne, which was the Inspector General.

The Inspector General was a being of pure light and red tape. He wore a suit made of scrollwork. His face was a blank mask.

**[NOTICE: UNAUTHORIZED BOSS ARENA DETECTED.]** **[PARTICIPANTS: GERALD HUTCHINS VS. UNREGISTERED PARTY

"THE GOLDEN BLADES."]** **[RULES: SURVIVAL OF THE FITTEST CODE COMPLIANT.]** **[WARNING: THE GOLDEN BLADES HAVE BROKEN THREE ZONING LAWS ENTERING THIS SPACE. YOU MAY CITE THEM. BUT THEY MAY KILL YOU FIRST.]**

Gerald looked at the masked figure.

"I have a citation," Gerald said.

The Inspector General tilted his head.

"Show me," the mask said.
