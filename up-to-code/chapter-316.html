Chapter 316: Seal and Foundation

# Chapter 316

The lock mechanism groaned. It was not a mechanical failure; it was a System-enforcement seal.

Gerald Hutchins did not panic.

Panicking was inefficient and rarely resulted in a code-compliant outcome. He stepped back from the door, his hand hovering over the biometric scanner pad that had fused itself to the wood.

The file on the desk remained open. The header **TERMINATED** pulsed with a faint, sickly green light.

Gerald adjusted his glasses. He pulled a pen from his pocket. It was a standard-issue ballpoint, chewed at the end. He clicked it twice.

"Procedural irregularity," he muttered.

The air in the records office dropped ten degrees. Frost crept up the window panes, forming fractal patterns that looked suspiciously like compliance forms.

A voice echoed from the corners of the room. It was smooth, oily, and carried the distinct resonance of a High-Tier Core.

"Welcome back, Gerald. Or should I say, welcome to the archive?"

Gerald didn't look up, and he walked to the filing cabinet labeled **HISTORICAL INSPECTORS**. He ran his finger along the drawer handles.

One was jammed.

The other was locked.

"Show yourself," Gerald said. His voice was flat, devoid of fear, carrying the weight of thirty-one years of shutting down unsafe construction projects.

"I am already here."

The shadows coalesced. A figure stepped out from behind a stack of tax records.

It wasn't a goblin.

It wasn't a skeleton, which was a humanoid shape made of stacked paper, held together by red wax seals and glowing blue runes. The face was a blank sheet of parchment.

"Inspector Halloway," the parchment figure said. "Former Chief Inspector. Deceased. Or rather, converted."

Gerald stopped. He looked at the file on the desk. Then he looked at the parchment man.

"You are a violation," Gerald said.

"I am a legacy system," Halloway replied. "And you are currently sitting in my office. Which means you are trespassing on a protected historical site. Do you have a permit for that?"

Gerald's left eye twitched. This was the specific trigger for his professional anger.

"I have a key," Gerald said. "I am an authorized user of municipal records."

"Your authorization was revoked three chapters ago when you accepted the Core Registration," Halloway said. The paper rustled. "You are no longer a municipal employee.

You are a dungeon core. And this file?

It is not about you.

It is about what you are becoming.

"

Gerald walked to the desk. He picked up the file. The paper felt warm.

"Explain the termination status," Gerald demanded.

"It's not a termination," Halloway said.

"It's a transition. You're being audited.

The Inspector General is watching, and he saw your last Citation. He saw you cite a Level 50 Dragon for 'Inadequate Fire Suppression.' That was bold. It was also technically incorrect, because the dragon's breath weapon is magical, not chemical.

You cited a magical hazard under the chemical code.

"

Gerald frowned.

He opened the file.

The pages were blank. No, not blank.

They were white-out.

"I checked the code," Gerald said. "Section 14.2.

Subsection C.

Fire suppression systems must address all combustible materials. Magic is a combustible material if it has a thermal output.

"

Halloway laughed. It sounded like tearing paper.

"The System doesn't care about your logic, Gerald.

It cares about the spirit of the law. And you're annoying the Inspector General.

He's reviewing your entire file.

If he finds one discrepancy—one unfiled report, one missed inspection—he doesn't just fine you. He deletes you.

"

Gerald's hands tightened around the file. The edges of the paper cut into his palms.

"What do you want?" Gerald asked.

"I want you to stop," Halloway said. "Close your dungeon.

Seal the entrance.

Cease all spawn activity.

If you do this now, I can bury the audit. I can make the **TERMINATED** status vanish.

"

Gerald looked at the blank pages. He looked at the frost on the windows. He thought about his sub-basement.

The mold spores. The water leak.

The single rat.

"No," Gerald said.

Halloway's parchment face rippled.

"No?"

"I cannot close a project mid-inspection," Gerald said. "It is unprofessional. Furthermore, if I seal the entrance, I violate the Egress Code, Section 8.1, which mandates that all public and semi-public spaces must maintain accessible exits during active use."

"You aren't a public space," Halloway snapped.

"My dungeon is currently occupied by three adventurers who are complaining about the lighting," Gerald said. "Under municipal ordinance, any structure with human occupancy requires adequate illumination. I cannot seal it without causing a hazard.

And if I cause a hazard, I am liable.

Which means I am not terminating. I am correcting.

"

Halloway stepped forward. The floorboards creaked under his weight.

"You are playing games with your life, Hutchins.

The Inspector General is not a bureaucrat. He is a predator. He hunts cores that break the spirit of the System.

You broke it. You cited a dragon for a fire code violation when the System intended it to be a combat mechanic.

You turned a fight into a lawsuit.

"

"I followed the rule," Gerald said. "The rule was there. I used it."

"That is the problem!" Halloway shouted.

The paper body flared with blue light.

"The System is not a rulebook!

It is a simulation! You are breaking the immersion! You are making the game unplayable!

"

Gerald felt a chill that had nothing to do with the temperature. The **TERMINATED** text on the file glowed brighter.

"Prove it," Gerald said.

Halloway paused.

"What?"

"Prove that I am violating the spirit," Gerald said. He dropped the file.

It hit the desk with a heavy thud.

"Cite me. Right now. If you can prove I am breaking the System's intent, I will surrender my Core status.

I will dissolve.

"

Halloway hesitated. The parchment body shifted, the wax seals cracking.

"You think you can out-bureaucrat the Inspector General?"

"I think you are afraid of me," Gerald said. "Because I found the loophole."

Halloway recoiled.

"There is no loophole."

"There is," Gerald said. He pulled a small, folded piece of paper from his pocket.

It was a duplicate of the **Form 11-B**, but handwritten, and he had written it on the back of a receipt from a gas station.

"Section 4, Paragraph 9 of the Core Registration Protocol. It states that a Core is bound by the Laws of the Realm in which it was established.

Ashford is a municipality. Municipal law supersedes general dungeon law for structures located within city limits.

"

Halloway's face contorted. The blank parchment displayed a frown.

"That is archaic," Halloway hissed. "That law was repealed in the Age of Heroes."

"It was not repealed," Gerald said.

"It was superseded by the Federal Dungeon Charter, which does not apply to municipal infrastructure.

I am not a dungeon. I am a sub-basement extension of the Ashford Municipal Library.

I am public property. You cannot audit public property for private gain. You can only inspect it for safety.

"

The room went silent. The frost stopped spreading.

Halloway stared at Gerald. The blue runes on his body flickered.

"You're claiming jurisdiction," Halloway said.

"I am asserting my role as a municipal inspector," Gerald corrected. "You are not my auditor.

You are a trespasser.

You are in a city records office without a warrant. You are holding a file that does not belong to you.

"

Halloway lunged. He didn't attack with magic. He attacked with bureaucracy. A cloud of red tape erupted from his hands, whipping toward Gerald's throat.

Gerald didn't dodge. He didn't block. He raised his hand and pointed a finger at Halloway.

"**Citation 004-B:** Unauthorized use of archival documents," Gerald said. "Penalty: Immediate expulsion from municipal premises."

The red tape hit Gerald's finger and stopped. It sizzled. The System's blue interface appeared in the air, not as a notification, but as a physical barrier.

**[SYSTEM ALERT: LOCAL JURISDICTION DETECTED]** **[ACTION: CITATION ISSUED]** **[TARGET: ENTITY 'HALLOWAY']** **[STATUS: VIOLATION CONFIRMED]** **[ACTION: ENFORCEMENT]**

Halloway shrieked. The paper body unravelled. The wax seals melted.

"No!" Halloway screamed. "You can't! I am a Core!"

"You are a ghost," Gerald said. "And ghosts do not have standing in civil court."

The System pulled Halloway backward. The force was absolute. The parchment figure was dragged across the floor, leaving a trail of wet ink and ash.

Gerald watched him go, and he did not feel triumph. He felt nauseous.

The act of citing a supernatural entity had caused a spike in his heart rate that he couldn't control.

His hands were still shaking.

The door to the records office clicked. The lock disengaged.

Gerald exhaled. He walked to the door and opened it. The hallway was empty. The library was quiet.

He stepped out. He needed to get back to the sub-basement. He needed to check the fire extinguishers. He needed to make sure the handrails were secure.

But as he walked toward the elevator, the air in the hallway changed. The smell of old paper vanished. It was replaced by the scent of sulfur and ozone.

A new voice spoke. It did not come from the shadows. It came from the ceiling.

"Mr. Hutchins."

Gerald stopped. He looked up.

A large, floating eye was embedded in the ceiling tiles. It was the size of a basketball. It blinked slowly.

"Inspector General," Gerald said.

The eye narrowed.

"You have invoked municipal law to shield yourself from my authority," the voice said. It was calm.

Terrifyingly calm.

"That is a clever maneuver.

It is also a violation.

"

Gerald felt the blood drain from his face.

"Which violation?"

"**Jurisdictional Overreach.**" The eye rotated. "By claiming municipal jurisdiction, you have declared that your dungeon is no longer a dungeon. You have declared it a building. Do you understand what that means?"

Gerald's mind raced. He looked for the code. He looked for the loophole.

He found it. It was ugly.

"It means I am subject to the Building Code of Ashford," Gerald said.

"It means," the voice continued, "that you are no longer protected by the Core Safety Protocols.

You have no hit points. You have no mana shield. You have no respawn timer.

You are a building.

And buildings can be condemned.

"

The floor beneath Gerald rumbled. The library walls groaned.

"I am issuing a Condemnation Order," the voice said.

"Effective immediately. You have six hours to vacate the premises. If you remain, I will have your structure demolished.

By force."

The eye vanished.

Gerald stood alone in the hallway. The elevator dinged. The doors slid open.

Inside, the mirror reflected Gerald. But his reflection was not wearing a suit. It was wearing a hard hat.

And behind his reflection, in the mirror, the sub-basement was on fire.

Gerald looked at his watch, and he had six hours.

He looked at the elevator button for the basement.

He pressed it.

The elevator doors closed. As they did, Gerald heard a sound from the lobby. It was the sound of a sledgehammer hitting drywall.

He pulled out his phone. He dialed his foreman.

"Dave," Gerald said.

"Bring the cement truck.

And bring the steel beams. We're not fixing the leak.

We're reinforcing the foundation.

"

"For what?" Dave asked on the other end.

"Because we're building a bunker," Gerald said. "And we're doing it up to code."

The elevator descended. The numbers on the panel dropped. B1. B2.

Gerald gripped the handrail. It was compliant.

It would have to be.
