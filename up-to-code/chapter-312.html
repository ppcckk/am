Chapter 312: The Perfect Bureaucracy

# Chapter 312

The stamp was a blur of crimson ink.

Gerald didn't look at it. He looked at the grass.

It was real. The blades bent under his knees, damp and cold, smelling of ozone and wet earth.

Not the sterile hum of the System's void.

Not the damp rot of his library basement. Just dirt.

But the sky was wrong.

Clouds churned in a vortex above them, black and jagged. The Redactor's shape had dissipated into the atmosphere, but the threat remained. It hung over the field like a storm front made of bureaucracy.

Elena pulled him up. Her grip was tight, knuckles white.

"Gerald. Move."

"I'm calculating the egress." Gerald wiped mud from his cheek.

His hands were shaking. Not from fear, but from the sudden, violent absence of structure.

No walls.

No ceiling. No code.

"Calculating what?" Elena dragged him backward. "There is no egress! There is no building!"

"That's the problem."

A boom echoed from the west.

Trees splintered. A shockwave rippled through the tall grass, flattening a hundred yards of vegetation in a perfect circle.

Gerald turned. He squinted against the glare of the sun.

Something was walking out of the treeline.

It wasn't a monster. It wasn't a hero.

It was a man, and he wore a suit that looked tailored from shadow. His face was smooth, featureless, save for a single, glowing eye in the center of his forehead. In his hand, he carried a clipboard.

The clipboard was the size of a door.

**[UNLICENSED ENTITY DETECTED.]** **[CLASS: AUDITOR PRIME.]** **[THREAT LEVEL: CATASTROPHIC.]**

The text floated in Gerald's vision, red and pulsing.

Gerald blinked. The text didn't disappear.

"I have no Core," Gerald said. "I have no status."

"You have a body," the Auditor said. Its voice sounded like paper tearing. "Which means you are subject to municipal zoning."

Gerald felt a cold spike in his gut. This was new.

Auditors were internal. They checked Cores. They didn't audit humans.

Unless the human was still linked to the system's infrastructure.

Gerald looked down at his chest. The skin was whole. No glowing core. No sigil.

But when he took a step, the grass beneath him turned gray. The ground cracked. A faint, golden grid appeared under his feet, fading as he moved.

"I'm unlinked," Gerald said, his voice low. "I'm just a man."

The Auditor smiled. The eye on its forehead narrowed.

"Unlinked entities are still occupants of public domain. And public domain requires adherence to the Universal Code."

The Auditor raised the clipboard.

**[VIOLATION: ILLEGAL OCCUPANCY OF UNZONED LAND.]** **[PENALTY: IMMEDIATE ERASURE.]**

A beam of red light shot from the clipboard.

Gerald didn't think. He threw himself sideways.

The beam hit the spot where he had been standing. The grass didn't burn. It vanished. A perfect, square crater appeared in the earth, three feet deep and perfectly smooth.

Elena screamed. She fired an arrow.

The arrow shattered against a barrier of red ink that formed in the air around the Auditor.

"Stop!" Gerald yelled. He scrambled to his feet. "You can't cite me! I'm not a Core!"

"Everyone is a resident," the Auditor said. It stepped forward. The ground shook. "And you are out of compliance."

Gerald's heart hammered against his ribs. He needed a plan. He needed a code.

But he had no dungeon. No spawns. No walls to cite for fire safety.

He looked at the crater. Then he looked at the Auditor.

Then he looked at the trees surrounding the clearing.

"Zoning," Gerald muttered. "He's citing zoning."

"Run!" Elena shouted. She grabbed his arm.

"No." Gerald ripped his arm free.

He stepped toward the crater.

"Gerald, are you insane?"

"It's a hole," Gerald said. His voice was flat.

Professional.

"It's a sudden depression in the terrain.

A sinkhole."

The Auditor paused. The red light on its clipboard dimmed.

"Explain."

Gerald pointed at the perfect square.

"Section 4.12. Unstable Ground Conditions.

You just excavated a four-by-four pit without a permit. Without shoring.

Without a safety fence.

"

The Auditor tilted its head. The eye widened.

"This is an execution."

"It's a construction site," Gerald corrected. "And you're in violation of the Environmental Safety Act."

Gerald reached into his pocket. He had no forms. No stamps. No pen.

But he had his hands, and he slammed his palm against the edge of the crater.

"I am filing a verbal complaint," Gerald said. "To the Inspector General."

The System hesitated.

**[PROCESSING CLAIM...]** **[CLAIMANT: GERALD HUTCHINS.]** **[STATUS: UNLICENSED.]** **[CLAIM TYPE: CIVIL ZONING DISPUTE.]**

The Auditor's clipboard flickered. The red light stuttered.

"You cannot file," the Auditor said. "You have no standing."

"I am a resident," Gerald said. He stepped closer. "I am standing on the property.

Therefore, I am affected by the excavation.

I have standing.

"

The Auditor lowered the clipboard. For a second, the featureless face seemed to frown.

"This is a loophole."

"It's the law," Gerald said.

The System chimed. A low, resonant sound that vibrated in Gerald's teeth.

**[CLAIM ACCEPTED.]** **[HEARING INITIATED.]** **[LOCATION: PUBLIC DOMAIN.]** **[JUDGE: SYSTEM LOCAL.]**

The air around them froze. The grass stopped rustling. The birds went silent.

A courtroom formed.

Not in a building. In the mind. Or perhaps in the space between thoughts.

Gerald felt a weight press down on his shoulders. The Auditor grew taller. Its suit unraveled into rolls of parchment.

Elena was pinned to the ground by invisible forces. She struggled, but she couldn't move.

**[DEFENDANT: AUDITOR PRIME.]** **[PLAINTIFF: GERALD HUTCHINS.]** **[ISSUE: AUTHORITY TO EXCAVATE IN PUBLIC DOMAIN.]**

Gerald straightened his tie. He had no tie. He adjusted his shirt collar anyway.

"The defendant," Gerald said, "created a hazard."

The Auditor's voice boomed.

"The hazard was a weapon."

"It was a hole," Gerald said. "A hole is a hazard. Did you post signage?"

"No."

"Did you install barriers?"

"No."

"Did you conduct a soil survey?"

"I am the System."

"That's not a defense," Gerald said. He walked around the crater. He measured the depth with his eyes. Four feet.

"According to Municipal Ordinance 11-B, any excavation deeper than three feet requires a permit and a protective railing. You have neither."

The Auditor stepped forward.

"I am enforcing compliance."

"By creating non-compliance," Gerald shot back. He pointed at the Auditor's feet.

The Auditor looked down.

Where it stepped, the ground cracked. Small fissures spread out.

"You're destabilizing the foundation," Gerald said. "The public domain has a structural integrity rating. You're dropping it."

The System paused.

**[STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY: 88% AND DROPPING.]** **[CAUSE: UNAUTHORIZED MASS MANIPULATION.]**

Gerald smiled. It wasn't a happy smile. It was the smile of a man who had found a typo in a ten-thousand-page document.

"If the structural integrity drops below 50%, the area collapses," Gerald said. "You'll be responsible for the collapse of the public domain. That's a Class A felony."

The Auditor stopped.

The red light on its clipboard turned yellow.

"You are bluffing," the Auditor said.

"I'm citing," Gerald said. "Check your logs."

The Auditor froze. It scanned the air. It checked the System's internal records.

Gerald watched it. He waited.

He knew the System's blind spots. The System was a bureaucracy. It cared about liability. It cared about precedent.

If the Auditor erased Gerald, it created a precedent for uncontrolled excavation. The System would have to pay for the damage.

The Auditor hated paying.

The yellow light pulsed.

**[VIOLATION CONFIRMED: NEGLECT OF SAFETY PROTOCOLS.]** **[CITATION ISSUED TO AUDITOR PRIME.]** **[STAT DEBUFF: MOVEMENT SPEED -10%. ACCURACY -5%.]**

The Auditor roared. A sound like tearing metal.

It swung the clipboard.

Gerald ducked. The edge of the board sliced through the air where his head had been. It cut a tree in half.

The tree fell.

The impact shook the ground.

**[STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY: 82%.]**

"Keep going," Gerald whispered.

He kicked dirt into the crater. Dust rose.

"Visibility is impaired!" Gerald shouted. "You're operating in low-visibility conditions without a spotter! That's another violation!"

The Auditor hesitated. It lowered the clipboard.

"You are interfering with enforcement," the Auditor growled.

"I'm improving safety," Gerald said.

He picked up a rock, which was heavy. Rough.

Gerald threw it.

It wasn't a powerful throw. But it hit the Auditor's eye.

The eye blinked.

Gerald didn't wait. He ran.

He ran toward Elena.

"He's slow!" Gerald yelled. "The debuff is working!"

Elena broke free of the invisible pressure. She grabbed Gerald's hand.

"Where to?"

"The library," Gerald said.

"We're in a field!"

"The library is gone," Gerald said.

"But the library was built on bedrock. The field is on topsoil. The Auditor is destabilizing the soil.

The library's foundation is still down there.

"

"That doesn't make sense."

"It's the only code we have," Gerald said. "We go back. We rebuild. We cite the Auditor for trespassing on protected historical infrastructure."

Elena looked at him. Her eyes were wide.

"You're insane."

"I'm an inspector," Gerald said.

The Auditor screamed.

It lifted off the ground. The suit unraveled completely. It became a storm of red tape and black ink.

It was big now. Bigger than the clouds.

It blocked out the sun.

**[WARNING: TARGET ENRAGED.]** **[WARNING: AOE DAMAGE IMMINENT.]**

Gerald grabbed a handful of grass. He pulled it up. Roots and dirt clung to his fingers.

He squeezed it. Dirt leaked between his fingers.

"Elena," he said. "Do you remember the floor plan of the library?"

"What?"

"The sub-basement. The load-bearing columns. Where were they?"

Elena stared at him. Then she closed her eyes.

"North wall. Three columns. Center of the basement."

Gerald nodded, and he looked at the Auditor. The storm was swirling. Red lightning crackled from the ink-tentacles.

The Auditor wasn't just an agent. It was a piece of the System's code. And code had bugs.

Gerald looked at the ground, and he visualized the library. He visualized the columns.

He placed his foot on the spot where the North Wall would be.

He stomped.

Once.

Twice.

Three times.

**[ACTION: MANUAL SEISMIC RESONANCE.]** **[CALCULATING...]**

The ground hummed.

A low, thrumming vibration spread out from his feet.

The Auditor paused. The red lightning stopped crackling.

"What are you doing?" the Auditor asked.

"Testing the soil density," Gerald said. "If the Auditor creates a void, the ground will collapse. If the ground collapses, the Auditor falls into the void. And if the Auditor falls into the void..."

Gerald stomped again.

The ground cracked.

A fissure opened beneath the Auditor.

Not a small crack. A chasm.

The Auditor looked down.

It tried to move. But the debuff held. Movement speed -10%.

It wasn't enough.

The Auditor slipped.

Red ink poured out of its body, filling the chasm.

**[SYSTEM ERROR: ENTITY OUT OF BOUNDS.]** **[ATTEMPTING RECALIBRATION...]**

The Auditor screamed. A sound that broke Gerald's eardrums.

It fell.

It fell into the darkness.

The chasm swallowed the red storm.

The clouds cleared, and the sun came out.

Gerald fell backward. He lay in the grass, breathing hard.

Elena crawled over to him.

"Did we win?"

Gerald looked at the sky.

"No."

The ground rumbled.

The chasm didn't close.

Instead, something else rose from it.

Not the Auditor.

A hand.

Massive. Pale. Made of white marble.

It gripped the edge of the chasm.

Then another hand.

Then a face, and the face was Gerald's.

But older. Withered. With eyes like empty wells.

**[CORE STATUS: RESTORED.]** **[WARNING: DUPLICATE ENTITY DETECTED.]** **[SOURCE: FAILED CITATION.]**

The Gerald-face smiled.

"You cited me," it said. Its voice was Gerald's, but distorted. "But you didn't serve the papers."

Gerald scrambled backward.

"I served you!"

"You served the Auditor," the thing said. "I am the Inspector General. I was watching."

The marble hand reached out.

It grabbed Gerald's ankle.

Cold. So cold it burned.

"Your dungeon is gone," the thing said. "But your code remains."

It pulled.

Gerald dug his heels in.

"Elena! Help!"

Elena grabbed his other arm, and they pulled.

The thing pulled back.

Gerald's arm popped out of its socket. He didn't feel it. He only saw the white bone sticking through his skin.

"You have no home," the thing said. "So I will give you one."

It dragged them both toward the chasm.

Gerald looked at the darkness below.

It wasn't a hole, which was a dungeon.

A perfect, pristine dungeon. White marble floors. Gold trim. Flawless ventilation.

**[WELCOME TO THE OLYMPUS SUB-BASEMENT.]** **[COMPLIANCE RATING: 100/100.]**

Gerald screamed, and he wasn't being pulled into hell.

He was being pulled into a perfect, unbreachable bureaucracy.

And he knew, with absolute certainty, that he would never be allowed to leave.

It locked the door behind them.
