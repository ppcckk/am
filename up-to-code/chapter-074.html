Chapter 74: Thirty Seconds to Rule

# Chapter 74

Gerald pressed his palm flat against the wall and counted the studs. Fourteen inches on center. The original fire station had been built to the 1987 commercial code, which meant fourteen-inch spacing for all non-load-bearing partitions. He could feel the voids behind the dungeon's false stone where the original wood framing still stood, preserved by the System's conversion protocol like insects in amber.

Core 4471 had converted the fire station's apparatus bay into a kill corridor. It had run its dungeon walls along the interior perimeter, sealing the original bay doors, pouring new stone over the original concrete slab.

But it hadn't removed the framing.

The System never removed original structural members during Core Registration. That was in the conversion guidelines. Gerald had read them.

He had read all of them, and he counted to the third stud void, and then he reached into his clipboard and pulled out a Form 7-C, Structural Modification Request. He held it against the wall.

The System recognized the form because Gerald was a registered Core, and the form required a mana expenditure of 12 to execute. Gerald had 0 mana. He had had 0 mana for six days.

But Form 7-C was not an action. It was a notification.

It told the System that a Core was requesting permission to modify a structure.

The System would evaluate the request against existing code. If the modification would create a violation, the System would deny it and flag the existing condition as a pre-existing deficiency.

That was the loophole. Gerald didn't need mana. He needed the System to look at Core 4471's wall and see what was already wrong with it.

He held the form against the stud void and wrote: Request to install emergency egress signage per Section 1026.2 of the Life Safety Code.

The System processed it. Gerald felt the familiar tingle of a query being evaluated, and then the response came back, not as a denial, but as a flag.

PRE-EXISTING DEFICIENCY DETECTED: No egress path from enclosed kill corridor. Violation severity: Critical.

Gerald pulled out his citation pad. He began writing.

Core 4471's dungeon had a kill corridor that was, by the System's own definition, a space that trapped entities with no exit. That was a life safety violation.

Not a minor one.

Not a ventilation footnote.

A critical one. The kind that, in municipal code, would trigger an immediate Corrective Order than a citation with a 30-day compliance window.

Gerald finished writing. He signed the citation. He stamped it with his Core designation. Then he looked up at the ceiling of the kill corridor and said, "I am serving this citation on the responsible party."

The corridor's torches flickered. The dungeon shuddered.

A voice came from everywhere and nowhere. It was wet and low, like a throat full of gravel.

"You're in my corridor, 2891."

Gerald held up the citation. "Section 1026.2. No egress path from enclosed space.

You have no doors on the far end. I can hear the dead-end wall from here."

"You have no spawns. You have no mana. You are in my dungeon with a clipboard."

"I am a registered Core. I am within my rights to serve a citation on a compliant entity within range."

The torches went out. All of them. The corridor went black.

Gerald did not move.

He stood in the dark with his clipboard held in front of him like a shield, which it was not, and counted his breaths. He had inspected the fire station four times. He knew this corridor was eleven feet wide.

He knew the original apparatus bay was forty feet deep.

He knew that Core 4471 had placed its spawn chamber at the far end, because the conversion guidelines required spawn chambers to be placed at the deepest point of the registered footprint.

Something moved in the dark.

Gerald heard it. A wet dragging sound, like a body being pulled across wet stone. He did not turn his head.

He had inspected enough basements to know that turning your head toward a noise in a dark space was how people got bitten.

The dragging stopped.

Something was breathing near his left ear.

Close enough that Gerald could smell it. Copper and ammonia.

A spawn that had been fed recently.

"You can't cite me," the voice said. "You're not a compliance authority."

"I am a registered Core with a Code Compliance designation. Section 4.1 of the Inter-Core Enforcement Protocol. Any Core may serve a citation on any other Core within physical range."

"Physical range."

"I am standing in your dungeon. That is physical range."

A long silence. The breathing did not move away. Gerald could feel the heat of it against his cheek.

"You're going to die in here," Core 4471 said.

"I'm going to die in here if you kill me. But the citation is already served. The System logged it when I spoke the words. You can check your compliance record."

Another silence. Then the sound of something shifting. The dragging sound moved away. The torches came back on, one by one, starting from the far end of the corridor.

Gerald did not look at the spawn that had been breathing on him. He looked at his clipboard. The citation was logged. Timestamp confirmed.

Now came the part that mattered, and he pulled out a second form. Form 14-R, Request for Corrective Order Review.

This was not a citation.

This was a request that the System escalate the violation from citation status to Corrective Order status. A citation applied debuffs. A Corrective Order forced the violating Core to physically construct a remedy within a System-defined window.

The window for a Critical life safety violation was twelve hours.

The remedy for a missing egress path was the construction of a compliant exit. A door. A passage. Something that connected the enclosed space to the outside.

Gerald's dungeon was directly beneath the library. Core 4471's dungeon was directly beneath the fire station. The two structures were adjacent, separated by a wall of original 1987 concrete that the System had converted into a dungeon boundary.

If Core 4471 was forced to build an egress path from its kill corridor, the shortest path to the outside was through the boundary wall. Through Gerald's dungeon.

The Corrective Order would build a door into Gerald's dungeon, and a door that, once built, would be a permanent structural connection. And Gerald's disabled structures, the twelve footings and eight trusses that the Inspector General had shut down, were all anchored to the same concrete boundary wall.

If the Corrective Order cut through that wall, it would re-establish the structural interconnection.

Not by Gerald's design. By the System's enforcement.

Gerald would not be removing the interconnection.

The System would be rebuilding it as part of a mandated remedy.

Gerald submitted Form 14-R.

The System processed it. Gerald felt the tingle again, stronger this time, like a current running through his teeth.

CORRECTIVE ORDER ISSUED. Core 4471.

Violation: Critical, Section 1026.2.

Remedy: Construct compliant egress path within 12 hours.

Non-compliance penalty: Immediate Core dissolution.

Gerald let out a breath. He had done it. The order was in. Core 4471 had twelve hours to build a door into his dungeon or die.

He turned to leave. He walked toward the corridor entrance, counting his steps, eleven feet wide, forty feet deep. His footsteps echoed on the stone.

The corridor was empty now. Whatever spawn had been near him had retreated.

He made it to the boundary wall, and he pressed his hand against it; could feel his own dungeon on the other side, cold and quiet and dying. Forty-one days.

He had used six.

He had thirty-five left. But if the Corrective Order worked, the structural interconnection would be restored within twelve hours.

The library above would stabilize.

Gerald pressed his palm against the wall and felt, faintly, the vibration of construction beginning on the other side. Core 4471 was building. It had no choice.

He waited. The vibration grew. Something was cutting through the boundary wall.

Gerald could hear it now, a grinding sound, like a saw through concrete; the System was forcing Core 4471 to build, and Core 4471 was building.

The grinding stopped.

A crack appeared in the wall.

Then another. Then a section of concrete fell away, revealing a passage.

Two feet wide. Four feet high. A crawlspace, not a door.

Not compliant.

Gerald stared at it, and the passage was too small. A compliant egress path required a minimum of 32 inches of clear width and 80 inches of clear height. This was 24 by 48.

Non-compliant.

The System flagged it immediately. CORRECTIVE ORDER INCOMPLETE.

Violation remains active.

Core 4471. Time remaining: 11 hours 47 minutes.

Core 4471 had tried to cheat, and it had built the smallest possible opening and hoped the System would accept it.

The System had not accepted it.

Core 4471 would have to widen the passage.

It would have to cut deeper into the boundary wall.

Gerald watched the grinding resume. The passage widened. The vibration grew stronger. He could feel his own dungeon responding, the disabled footings and trusses beginning to hum as the structural interconnection re-established itself.

Then the grinding stopped. The torches in the corridor went out. All of them.

Gerald stood in the dark. He heard the wet dragging sound again. Closer this time. Right at the edge of the passage.

"You think this is over," Core 4471 said. Its voice came from the passage, from the crawlspace that was now three feet wide and five feet high. Still non-compliant.

Still being widened. But close enough for the voice to carry.

"The Corrective Order is in effect," Gerald said. "You have eleven hours."

"I filed an appeal."

Gerald's stomach dropped, and he knew the rules. Citations could be appealed. Corrective Orders could be appealed.

The appeal triggered a Hearing.

During a Hearing, the corrective action was suspended until the Hearing concluded.

"You can't appeal a Corrective Order. The appeal window is for Citations only. Section 4.1, subsection 3."

"Subsection 4. I filed under subsection 4. Emergency appeal. Claiming the citation was served by a non-compliant entity."

Gerald felt the cold of the wall against his back.

Subsection 4. He had never used it. He had never seen it used.

It was meant for situations where the serving Core was itself so non-compliant that the citation was invalid. Gerald's Compliance Rating was 78 out of 100.

He had no ventilation.

No egress signage.

No fire suppression, and he was, by the System's own standards, the most non-compliant Core in the region.

The Hearing commenced. Gerald felt it start, a pull in his awareness, like being grabbed by the scruff, and the System opened a channel between him and Core 4471.

Both Cores were now present in a shared space, a flat grey plane that the System generated for Hearings. Gerald could see Core 4471's presence.

It was a mass of heat and pressure, a furnace given shape.

Gerald was a clipboard and a cold room.

"The serving Core is non-compliant," Core 4471 said.

Its voice filled the grey space. "Rating 78.

No ventilation.

No egress. No fire suppression. The citation was served by an entity that itself violates the very code it claims to enforce.

The citation is invalid."

Gerald felt the System evaluating, and he felt the weight of the argument.

Core 4471 was right. Gerald was non-compliant.

That was the entire point of the Hearing. It was not a question of whether the violation existed; was a question of whether Gerald had standing to serve the citation.

Gerald opened his clipboard. He looked at the citation. He looked at the Corrective Order. He looked at his Compliance Rating, 78 out of 100, glowing in red at the top of his status screen.

Then he looked at Core 4471's Compliance Rating.

47 out of 100.

Gerald pulled out a new citation pad. He began writing.

He wrote a citation for Core 4471's own ventilation deficiencies, its missing egress signage, its inadequate fire suppression.

He wrote it in thirty seconds. He had been writing these citations for thirty-one years.

He held it up in the grey space.

"I am serving a counter-citation," Gerald said.

"Section 4.1, subsection 1. Any Core may serve a citation on any other Core within physical range.

We are both present.

I am within range."

Core 4471's presence recoiled.

"You cannot cite me during a Hearing. The Hearing is a protected proceeding."

"Subsection 6," Gerald said. "Counter-citations served during a Hearing are admissible and trigger a concurrent Hearing. The original Hearing is suspended until both Hearings conclude."

He felt the System process it. He felt the grey space shudder.

The original Hearing paused.

A second Hearing opened.

Gerald was now serving a citation on Core 4471 for the exact same violations Core 4471 was using to argue Gerald's non-compliance.

The symmetry was not accidental. Gerald had built it into his plan before he ever entered the fire station, and he had known Core 4471 would appeal.

He had known the appeal would attack his Compliance Rating, and had prepared the counter-citation before he submitted Form 14-R.

Core 4471 was now facing a citation for its own life safety violations, served during an appeal that claimed Gerald's citation was invalid because of the same violations. If Core 4471 won the first appeal, Gerald's citation was void. But the second citation was now active, and it was served by a Core that, while non-compliant, was still a registered Core with standing.

The System would have to rule on both; the ruling on the second citation would determine whether the first appeal succeeded.

Gerald sat down in the grey space, and he opened his clipboard. He started drafting his argument. He had thirty-five days on the clock.

He had eleven hours on the Corrective Order.

And he had a rival Core that was about to discover that being non-compliant was not a defense. It was a vulnerability.

But as he wrote, he felt something else.

A third presence in the grey space. Small. Quiet.

Watching.

The Inspector General, which had not left. It had been listening. And it was not here to observe. It was here to rule.

Gerald's pen stopped. He looked up. The grey space had a shape in it now, tall and thin, holding a clipboard that was larger than Gerald's entire dungeon.

"Both Hearings are consolidated," the Inspector General said.

"I will adjudicate.

Core 2891, you have thirty seconds to state your case. Core 4471, you have thirty seconds.

Then I rule."

Gerald looked at his clipboard. He looked at the citation. He looked at the Corrective Order. He looked at the thirty seconds ticking down in his awareness.

He opened his mouth.

The Inspector General raised its pen.

"Twenty-five seconds."
