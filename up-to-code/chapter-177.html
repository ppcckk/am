Chapter 177: Processing the Void

# Chapter 177

Gerald checked his clipboard twice, confirming the procedural inertia had not changed his weight. It was pressurized.

Gerald Hutchins hung in the void, suspended by nothing but the System's procedural inertia. The clipboard in his hand felt heavier than it had in the physical world. It was no longer cardboard and ink. It was a weight of pure administrative law, dense and cold.

Falling was not the right verb. He was being processed.

The Void was a non-Euclidean office space. Walls of static noise shifted like bad television reception.

There was no floor, only a grid of glowing lines that represented jurisdictional boundaries.

To his left, a shimmering wall of data scrolled past: *Ashford Municipal Library. Zone 4. Sub-basement.

Compliance Rating: Pending.*

Silas Vane stood on a floating platform of polished obsidian, smoothing his suit jacket. He looked entirely too comfortable.

Brokk was gone. The dwarf had been ejected from the proceedings the moment the hearing began, deemed an

"extraneous variable." Gerald had tried to argue that the contractor was essential to the structural integrity of the defense, but the Auditor's red eye had simply widened, and the dwarf had vanished with a confused grunt.

Now it was just Gerald and the Inspector General, and the General sat at a desk made of compressed time. It was a massive, grotesque object, carved from moments of hesitation and delayed paperwork. The Inspector General itself was a shapeless figure of smoke and spectacles, wearing a robe that seemed to be woven from red tape.

"Mr. Hutchins," the Inspector General said.

The voice did not come from a mouth.

It resonated in Gerald's chest cavity, vibrating against his ribs.

"You have filed an appeal against a Class-4 Administrative Erasure. This is... unusual."

Gerald straightened his spine. He adjusted his grip on the clipboard.

"I am exercising my right to contest," Gerald said. His voice sounded thin in the vacuum.

"You cite 'Procedural Overreach' as your primary defense," the General noted. A page turned in the air. "You claim the Auditor acted prematurely. You claim your dungeon, while low-rated, is currently undergoing remediation."

"It is not low-rated," Gerald corrected. "It is under construction."

The General paused. The static around them hissed.

"Define 'under construction' in the context of a Core that has not spawned a single hostile entity in three weeks."

Gerald looked at the scrolling data. He knew the numbers.

He had memorized them.

"Ventilation ducts are eighty percent installed.

Egress signage is pending final calibration. The load-bearing pillars are up to code, though the concrete curing process is delayed by System lag.

"

"System lag," the General repeated, amused. "You blame the infrastructure for your lack of combat readiness."

"I blame the infrastructure for your inability to measure it correctly," Gerald said.

Vane shifted on his platform.

"Objection. The defendant is insulting the bench."

"Overruled," the General said. "The Inspector General is not a court. It is an audit.

Facts are admissible. Insults are merely data points.

"

The General leaned forward. The smoke of its body coalesced into a face, sharp and angular.

"Let us look at the Citation," the General said. "You issued a Citation to Core 74-Alpha, the 'Swamp of Despair.' You cited it for 'Lack of Slip-Resistant Flooring in High-Traffic Areas.' You then cited it for 'Inadequate Drainage Leading to Biohazard Accumulation.' The cumulative penalty was a 90% reduction in agility and a permanent debuff to health regeneration."

Gerald nodded.

"It was non-compliant. The mud floors were a trip hazard. The swamp water was unfiltered.

It was a public health violation.

"

"It killed three of its elite guardians," the General said. "They slipped. Their wounds festered. They died."

"They were working in unsafe conditions," Gerald said. "I provided them with a path to safety. They chose to ignore it. That is not my liability."

Vane stepped forward.

"Mr.

Hutchins admits to weaponizing safety regulations, and he did not fight those creatures. He changed the environment so that their own existence became a violation of their own code. That is not inspection.

That is assassination by bureaucracy.

"

Gerald looked at Vane. The lawyer's smile was gone. His eyes were hard.

"I inspected," Gerald said. "The System punished."

"The System punished," the General agreed.

"But you triggered the punishment.

That is the distinction. You are not a passive observer.

You are an active agent of chaos.

"

The red text returned, hovering in the air.

**PENALTY CONFIRMED: SUSPENSION PENDING.**

"Unless," the General said, "you can demonstrate that your dungeon is, in fact, a compliant entity worthy of continued operation. Not just a building. A *dungeon*."

Gerald felt a cold knot tighten in his stomach. This was the trap.

The System did not care if a dungeon was safe. It cared if it was *functional*.

A dungeon with no monsters, no traps, and no loot was a liability, which was a drain on resources. It was a wasted potential.

"I have a dungeon," Gerald said.

"You have a sub-basement with three mold spores and a rat," the General said.

"Where is the threat? Where is the revenue?

The City of Ashford expects a return on investment for the mana injected into this zone.

You are costing us more than you are generating.

"

Gerald's hands shook. Not from fear. From rage.

He had spent thirty-one years ensuring buildings were safe. He had never been asked to ensure they were *profitable*.

"I have a plan," Gerald said.

"Show it to me," the General said. "If it does not meet the Minimum Viable Danger Standard, the demolition proceeds immediately."

Gerald looked at his clipboard. He looked at the Void. He looked at Vane, who was watching him with the hungry eyes of a man who had already calculated his fee for the post-mortem.

Gerald closed his eyes. He breathed in the static. He breathed in the pressure.

He reached into his inventory. Not for a weapon. Not for a potion.

He reached for his Form 11-B. The Unlicensed Biological Entity form.

He had never been able to file it. The System rejected it because the entity—the rat—was too small to register as a threat. The System required a threat to validate a spawn.

But the System also required *compliance* to validate a spawn.

Gerald opened his eyes.

"I am invoking Clause 44-C," Gerald said. "Voluntary Self-Regulation."

Vane frowned. "There is no Clause 44-C."

"It is in the appendix of the Dungeon Core Liability Act, subsection 9, paragraph 2," Gerald said. He spoke quickly, his voice gaining strength. "It allows a Core to temporarily reclassify its spawn list from 'Hostile' to 'Structural Component' if the spawn is integral to the structural integrity of the dungeon."

The General tilted its head.

"Explain."

"My rat," Gerald said. "It is not a mob.

It is a load-bearing organism.

It nests in the support beams.

If I remove the rat, the beams weaken. If the beams weaken, the library above collapses.

If the library collapses, the City of Ashford faces a multi-million dollar liability suit.

"

Silence.

The static around them froze.

Vane's mouth opened. He closed it. He looked at the General.

The General looked at Gerald.

"You are claiming your rat is structural," the General said.

"I am claiming that removing the rat violates the Building Code of Ashford, which requires all load-bearing supports to remain intact during inspection periods," Gerald said. "The System recognizes municipal law as a baseline for physical reality in this sector. If I demolish the rat, I demolish the library. That is a greater violation than any you have listed."

The General's smoke swirled. Calculating.

"The rat is one inch long," the General said.

"It is a keystone organism," Gerald lied. Or perhaps he wasn't lying.

He didn't know.

But he knew how to sell a lie. He knew how to make a small defect sound like a catastrophic failure.

"Without it, the mold spores lose their symbiotic anchor.

Without the anchor, the mold attacks the concrete.

Without the concrete, the foundation fails.

"

He was improvising.

His hands were sweating. His heart hammered against his ribs like a trapped bird. He hated this.

He wanted to fill out a form. He wanted to check a box.

He did not want to stand in a void and bluff the Inspector General.

But the bluff was working.

The General leaned back. The compressed time desk creaked.

"Interesting," the General said. "A symbiotic dependency chain. We have not seen that before."

Vane stepped forward, his face pale.

"This is fraud. The rat has no structural integrity.

I have reviewed the architectural plans. The beams are steel and concrete. The rat is irrelevant.

"

"Then why," Gerald asked, turning to Vane, "did you send a scout goblin into my dungeon last week?"

Vane froze.

"I did not," Vane said.

"You did," Gerald said. "You sent it to test the defenses.

The goblin was repelled by the 'Trespassing on Non-Public Easement' clause.

But before it was repelled, it interacted with the rat. The rat bit it. The goblin fled.

The rat survived.

"

Gerald looked at the General.

"The rat is an active defense mechanism," Gerald said. "It is a biological deterrent.

It is not a monster, which is a security feature. And security features are part of the building code. They are required for fire exits.

"

The General stared at Gerald. The red eye blinked. Once. Twice.

"You are twisting the definition of 'security feature,'" the General said. "But you are technically correct. Under the Ashford Municipal Code, Section 12.4, emergency egress routes must be monitored. If the rat monitors the route, it is a security feature."

Gerald held his breath.

The General raised a hand. The red text in the air dissolved.

**APPEAL GRANTED. TEMPORARY STAY OF EXECUTION.**

**CONDITION: DUNGEON MUST DEMONSTRATE FUNCTIONAL THREAT LEVEL WITHIN 24 HOURS.**

**IF FAILURE: IMMEDIATE DEMOLITION.**

The Void faded. The grid lines blurred.

Gerald felt the pull of his dungeon. The sub-basement. The smell of damp concrete. The hum of the fluorescent lights.

He was going back.

But the General's voice followed him, echoing in the dissolving dark.

"Twenty-four hours, Mr. Hutchins. Make your rat count."

Gerald landed on the concrete floor of his dungeon, and he fell to his knees, gasping. The air was stale, but it was real. He could smell the mildew. He could see the water leak dripping into the bucket he had installed three days ago.

Brokk was waiting there. The dwarf looked terrified.

"They kicked me out," Brokk said. "What happened? Did you win?"

Gerald stood up. His legs felt weak. He looked at the clipboard. The text had changed.

**DEADLINE: 23:59:59**

He looked around his dungeon. Three mold spores. A water leak. One confused rat, currently chewing on a piece of drywall in the corner.

He had twenty-four hours to turn a sub-basement into a threat that could justify its own existence. Or the System would erase him. And with him, the library above.

Vane was still watching. Gerald could feel the lawyer's gaze from the library floor above, separated by two feet of concrete. Vane was waiting for him to fail.

Gerald looked at the rat.

The rat looked back. It blinked.

Gerald opened his inventory. He removed the clipboard. He opened his spawn menu.

He selected *Mold Spore*, and he selected *Water Leak*.

He selected *Rat*.

He looked at the options. Usually, he had to choose a name, a level, a role.

This time, he didn't choose, and he typed a new command.

**COMMAND: MERGE ENTITIES.**

The System flashed red.

**ERROR: ILLEGAL MERGE.**

Gerald gritted his teeth. He tried again.

**COMMAND: DESIGNATE HYBRID.**

**ERROR: UNLICENSED HYBRID.**

He was running out of time. The clock in the corner of his vision ticked down. 23:58.

He needed something bigger. Something that could fight. Something that would not die because of a code violation.

He looked at the water leak. The mold. The rat.

And then he looked at the walls, and the walls were made of brick. Old brick. Crumbling brick.

He looked at his structural scan. The brick was non-load-bearing in the upper sections, but load-bearing in the lower foundation. It was unstable.

If he collapsed the unstable brick, the dust would fill the room. The mold would spread. The rat would suffocate.

Unless he changed the code.

Gerald closed his eyes. He remembered the Building Code. Section 8.1: Ventilation Requirements. Section 12.4: Fire Safety.

And there, buried in the fine print of Section 19: Disaster Preparedness.

*A building must contain a means of self-defense against catastrophic failure.*

Catastrophic failure.

Gerald opened his eyes. He looked at the rat.

He looked at the water leak, and he looked at the mold.

He typed.

**COMMAND: RECLASSIFY DUNGEON ZONE.**

**NEW DESIGNATION: EMERGENCY SHELTER.**

The System paused.

If his dungeon was an Emergency Shelter, it did not need to produce monsters. It needed to produce safety.

But the Inspector General wanted a *threat*.

An Emergency Shelter that trapped adventurers? That was not safety. That was entrapment.

Gerald hesitated. His hands hovered over the interface.

If he did this, he would become a villain. A kidnapper.

If he didn't, he would cease to exist.

The clock ticked. 23:55.

Brokk stepped forward.

"Gerald? What are you doing?"

Gerald didn't answer. He looked at the library ceiling. He thought about the students. The school bus.

He thought about Vane's smile, and he pressed the button.

**ZONE RECLASSIFIED: EMERGENCY SHELTER (TRAP VARIATION).**

The floor beneath Gerald rumbled. The water leak surged. The mold spores exploded outward, forming a green fog. The rat squealed and vanished into the cracks.

And from the walls, the brick shifted.

Not to collapse.

To seal.

The exits closed, and the dungeon was no longer a building.

It was a cage.

And inside the cage, Gerald Hutchins laughed. A dry, rusty sound.

He picked up his clipboard.

"Time to inspect," he said.
