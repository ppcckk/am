Chapter 401: Audit of the Viral Photo

# Chapter 401

The heavy boots stopped three feet from Gerald's desk. They did not step forward.

They did not step back.

The silence stretched, thick and suffocating, pressing against the damp concrete of the sub-basement. Gerald kept his pen poised over the notepad. He did not look up.

He knew the texture of hesitation, and he had seen it in the eyes of homeowners trying to hide unpermitted decks for fifteen years.

"Inspector Hutchins?"

The voice was young.

Too young for the brass buttons on the coat. Gerald capped his pen. He placed it parallel to the edge of the desk.

He looked up.

Three men stood in the shadow of the stairs, and they wore gray tunics with silver sashes.

The sashes bore the symbol of the Department of Internal Audit. Gerald knew this symbol.

He had printed it on his own forms for decades. He had feared it. Now, it was just another badge.

"You are aware," Gerald said, his voice level, "that this is a private administrative office."

The lead auditor, a man with a face like crumpled parchment, stepped forward. His eyes darted to the wall where the hole had been.

The *Concealment of Evidence* was holding.

The air shimmered slightly, a trick of the light. The auditor squinted.

"We are not here for the office," the auditor said. "We are here for the Core."

"Gerald Hutchins is the Core," Gerald said. "I am filing a formal complaint regarding unauthorized entry into a public library sub-basement. You are obstructing municipal operations. I can have you removed for trespassing if you do not present a warrant signed by the Inspector General."

The auditor laughed, which was a dry, rattling sound.

"Warrants are for criminals, Inspector.

We are here for compliance.

Your rating dropped to forty-two. That is critical. That is eviction territory.

"

"My rating is accurate," Gerald said. He tapped the notepad.

"I have filed three appeals against environmental hazards.

The ventilation system was compromised by external sabotage.

I have documented the damage.

"

"Sabotage is not an excuse for non-compliance," the auditor said. He pulled a scroll from his sash. It unrolled in mid-air, glowing with red light.

"This is a preliminary termination notice. You have ten minutes to rectify the structural integrity of your primary chamber.

If the rating does not rise to fifty by the end of this meeting, we will dissolve your Core.

"

Gerald felt the cold grip of panic in his stomach. He suppressed it. He replaced it with the familiar numbness of bureaucracy.

"Rectify how?" Gerald asked.

"Repair the breach," the auditor said, pointing at the shimmering wall. "Or prove it does not exist."

Gerald stood up. He walked around the desk. He moved slowly.

He wanted them to see him move, and he wanted them to feel the weight of his presence. He stopped in front of the wall.

He reached out.

His hand passed through the illusion. His fingers brushed the cold, wet stone of the actual hole.

"I am repairing it now," Gerald said.

The auditor frowned.

"The mana cost is prohibitive. You do not have the reserves."

"I have jurisdiction," Gerald said.

He turned back to the desk. He picked up his notepad. He flipped to a fresh page. He wrote.

"What are you doing?" one of the junior auditors asked.

"Filing a counter-citation," Gerald said. He did not look up.

"You are standing in a designated egress route.

You are blocking the emergency exit, which is a Class B Fire Code violation. The penalty is a five-point reduction in your administrative efficiency score.

"

The auditors froze.

"That is not how the System works," the lead auditor said. His voice lost its confidence.

"The System applies universal logic to all entities with a Compliance interface," Gerald said, continuing to write. "You have an interface. You are an agent of the System.

Therefore, you are subject to the same building codes as the structures you inspect. You are currently obstructing a life-safety feature.

"

The junior auditor looked at his feet. He shifted his weight. He was moving out of the way.

"I am ordering you to clear the egress path," Gerald said. "Now."

The junior auditor took two steps to the left. The lead auditor did not move. He glared at Gerald.

"You cannot cite us," the lead auditor said. "We are agents of the law."

"I am citing you for obstruction," Gerald said.

He tore the page from his notepad. He held it out.

"Sign this.

Or I will escalate the citation to a Class A violation, which includes a permanent audit trail on your personnel file.

"

The lead auditor stared at the paper. His hand trembled. He did not sign.

Gerald sighed. He looked at the System interface hovering in his peripheral vision.

He had not filed the citation yet, and he was bluffing. But he had calculated the probability. The auditors were mid-level functionaries.

They cared about their metrics.

"If you do not sign, I will invoke the Right of Silent Inspection," Gerald said.

The lead auditor went pale.

"You do not have that right."

"Section 4, Paragraph B of the Core Rights Charter," Gerald said. "Any Core with a Compliance Rating above forty may request an independent audit of any agent within ten meters. If the agent refuses, the audit is conducted by the System itself, and the results are binding."

The junior auditor gasped, and he grabbed the lead auditor's arm.

"Sir, if the System audits us, it will check our own compliance. We have been speeding.

We have been skipping protocol.

"

The lead auditor ripped the paper from Gerald's hand. He signed it with a jagged, angry scrawl.

**[Citation Served.]** **[Target: Auditor Kaelen.]** **[Violation: Obstruction of Egress.]** **[Penalty: -5 Administrative Efficiency.]**

The red light on the auditor's scroll flickered. It dimmed. The lead auditor staggered. He looked at his hands as if they were not his own.

"You have weakened me," the auditor whispered.

"I have corrected your behavior," Gerald said. He sat back down.

He picked up his pen.

"Now. About my rating. The breach is not a structural failure.

It is an architectural feature.

"

The auditors stared at him.

"Explain," the lead auditor said. His voice was tight. Pain was making him concise.

"I have re-zoned the chamber," Gerald said. He spoke fast, his voice gaining the rhythmic cadence of a man who had memorized every line of the municipal code.

"The hole is not a breach.

It is a ventilation intake. Specifically, it is a passive cross-ventilation shaft designed to mitigate mold growth in high-humidity environments. This is a recognized exception under Code 7.4, Subsection C.

"

The lead auditor shook his head.

"There is no such code."

"There is," Gerald said. He pointed to the System interface.

"Look at the metadata of the library's original blueprint.

The architect, Elias Vane, submitted a variance request in 1998 for a subterranean air circulation system, which was denied by the previous inspector. But the variance was granted under the 'Historical Precedent' clause because the building is a heritage site.

The hole exists. It is legal. It is required for mold suppression.

My mold spores are not a failure; they are a compliance mechanism.

"

The auditors looked at each other. The junior auditor pulled out a tablet. He scrolled through the archives.

"1998..." he muttered. "Vane... Heritage variance..."

The junior auditor's eyes widened.

"Sir. It's true. The variance is active.

The hole is grandfathered.

"

The lead auditor's face twisted. He looked at Gerald. He looked at the shimmering wall. He looked at the notepad.

"You are using a technicality to avoid dissolution," the auditor said.

"I am using the law," Gerald said. "You are trying to dissolve a compliant structure based on a misinterpretation of zoning laws. That is an abuse of authority. I am noting this in my report."

The lead auditor stepped forward. He raised his hand.

A ball of gray energy formed in his palm, which was an enforcement spell. A direct attack.

Gerald did not flinch. He knew the spell. *Silence of the Bureaucratic Void.* It disrupted mana flow. It was used to shut down uncooperative Cores.

"If you cast that," Gerald said, "you will violate the Code of Conduct for Internal Auditors, Article 12: No Physical Force Against Compliant Entities. It is an automatic termination of your contract."

The gray energy fizzled. The auditor clenched his fist.

He wanted to strike, and he wanted to crush Gerald's fragile core.

But he could not. The rules were a cage, and Gerald held the key.

"Your rating is still low," the auditor said. "Forty-two is not safe."

"It is compliant," Gerald said. "And I am raising it. I have installed a secondary ventilation fan in the adjacent shaft.

I am currently processing the paperwork for its approval. Once it is approved, my rating will rise to forty-eight.

I have three minutes until the hearing ends.

I have two minutes until the fan is installed.

"

The auditor checked his watch. The seconds ticked away.

"You are gambling," the auditor said.

"I am managing risk," Gerald said. He looked at the junior auditor. "You. You seem concerned about your personal efficiency score."

The junior auditor nodded slowly.

"If you assist me in processing this variance," Gerald said, "I will withdraw the citation against your superior. I will also file a commendation for your diligence in identifying a heritage compliance issue. That is a positive metric."

The junior auditor looked at the lead auditor. He looked at Gerald.

"Sir," the junior auditor said. "If I help him, we both survive the audit. If we fight, we both get flagged."

The lead auditor spat on the floor.

"Do not listen to him."

The junior auditor tapped his tablet. He uploaded the variance request. He moved his feet. He stepped out of the egress route.

**[Variance Request Submitted.]** **[Processing...]**

The seconds stretched.

Gerald held his breath. He watched the progress bar.

It moved slowly.

The lead auditor watched it with hatred.

"Five seconds," Gerald said.

The bar filled.

**[Variance Approved.]** **[Compliance Rating: 49/100.]** **[Critical Threshold Avoided.]**

The lead auditor slumped. The gray energy around his hand vanished.

He looked defeated, and the System had ruled.

Gerald had won.

**[Hearing Concluded.]** **[Outcome: Core Retained.]** **[Note: Auditors flagged for review.]**

The two junior auditors turned and walked up the stairs. They did not look back. The lead auditor lingered.

He stepped close to Gerald. He leaned in.

His breath smelled of stale coffee and fear.

"This is not over," the lead auditor whispered. "The Inspector General has noticed you. You are not a Core.

You are a glitch.

Glitches get patched.

"

"I am a municipal employee," Gerald said. "I am not a glitch. I am a process."

The auditor sneered. He turned and climbed the stairs. His heavy boots echoed up the dark shaft.

Gerald waited. He counted to sixty. He listened for any further footsteps. There were none.

He sat back down.

His hands were shaking. He looked at the notepad. He had written the counter-citation.

He had filed the variance. He had saved himself.

But the rating was still forty-nine. It was barely passing. And the Auditor was right. The Inspector General was watching.

Gerald looked at the hole.

The *Concealment of Evidence* was fading.

The shadow blurred. The true breach was visible again.

A jagged, ugly tear in the wall, and he needed more mana. He needed to strengthen his dungeon. He could not do it alone. He needed allies.

He opened the System interface. He searched for nearby Cores.

He filtered by Compliance Rating.

He looked for those with low scores. Those who were desperate. Those who would listen to reason.

One result appeared.

**[Core Name: The Gilded Cage]** **[Location: 200 Meters East]** **[Rating: 38/100]** **[Status: Active]**

Gerald knew that name, and the Gilded Cage was a high-end dungeon in the shopping district. It was run by a Core that specialized in luxury traps and deceptive aesthetics.

It was rich in mana.

It was poor in structural integrity. It was a perfect target.

Gerald typed a message. He used the diplomatic channel.

**[Message to Core: The Gilded Cage]** **[Subject: Structural Integrity Concerns]** **[Body: Dear Core Manager, I am writing to advise you of a potential liability. Your current fire suppression system appears to be non-compliant with Code 12.3.

I can offer a consultation to rectify this.

In exchange, I require a mana transfer of 200 units to assist in my own compliance upgrades.

Failure to address these violations may result in a citation from the Inspector General. Sincerely, Gerald Hutchins.]**

He waited. The cursor blinked.

A reply appeared.

**[From: The Gilded Cage]** **[Message: Who are you? How do you know about my fire suppression?]**

Gerald smiled. It was a thin, cold smile.

**[From: Gerald Hutchins]** **[Message: I am the Inspector. And I have seen your blueprints. Do you want to fix it, or do you want to be fined?]**

The interface paused.

**[From: The Gilded Cage]** **[Message:...How much for the consultation?]**

Gerald leaned back in his chair. He had a lead. He had a rival who was desperate. He had a way to buy mana without killing his own children.

But he needed to be careful. The Gilded Cage was not just a dungeon. It was a front for a criminal syndicate.

Its traps were not just for adventurers. They were for killing people who owed money.

If Gerald went there, he would be walking into a den of wolves. He would need a guide. He would need protection.

He looked at the door. The knight had left. But the knight had left a brass buckle on the floor.

Gerald picked up the buckle. It was warm. It was heavy.

He remembered the knight's face. The fear in his eyes. The knight had said they would kill him. The knight had left because he believed Gerald was too weak to fight.

Gerald was not weak. He was dangerous.

He typed a second message.

**[From: Gerald Hutchins]** **[Message: I will come to you.

I will inspect your dungeon. I will find every violation. But first, I need to ensure my own safety.

Do you know a knight who serves no master, but who owes a debt to the law?]**

He waited, and the reply came instantly.

**[From: The Gilded Cage]** **[Message: I know one. But he is dangerous.

He is crazy.

He follows the code to the letter. He will break your dungeon, not fix it.]**

Gerald looked at the buckle. He thought of the knight's rigid posture. He thought of the knight's fear.

The knight was not crazy. The knight was compliant.

And Gerald needed a compliant sword.

**[From: Gerald Hutchins]** **[Message: Send him to me. I will pay him in code.]**

The interface blinked.

**[From: The Gilded Cage]** **[Message: He will arrive at the library entrance in one hour. Do not be late. The Inspector General has issued a new directive.]**

Gerald frowned. He refreshed the interface.

A new notification popped up. It was red. It pulsed with urgent light.

**[System Alert: Audit Protocol Changed.]** **[New Rule: All Cores must submit to a random physical inspection by an Auditor within 24 hours.]** **[Penalty for Refusal: Immediate Dissolution.]**

Gerald stared at the text. The rules had changed.

The game had changed, and the auditors were no longer just checking paperwork.

They were coming into the dungeons. They were going to touch the walls.

They were going to break the illusions.

Gerald looked at the hole. The *Concealment* had expired. The shadow was gone. The hole was real.

He had one hour. He had to fix the hole. He had to strengthen the dungeon. He had to meet the knight.

And he had to do it all before the Auditor came to measure the door width.

He stood up. He walked to the hole.

He placed his hand on the cold stone, and he felt the draft of the outside air. It was cold. It was dangerous.

It was the only way out.

Gerald closed his eyes. He reached for the System. He searched for a way to reinforce the structure without mana. He searched for a loophole in the material science rules.

He found one.

**[Loophole Found: Structural Bonding via Organic Resin.]** **[Requirement: Fresh biological material within 5 meters.]**

Gerald opened his eyes. He looked at the floor. He looked at the drain.

He looked at the dead rat that had died in the previous conflict, which was small. It was dry. But it was there.

Gerald picked it up. It weighed nothing.

He did not feel disgust. He felt purpose.

He walked to the hole. He pressed the rat into the crack in the wall. He used the System's bonding tool.

**[Binding...]**

The rat fused with the stone. The hole shrank. The draft slowed.

**[Structural Integrity: +1.]**

It was not enough. He needed more.

He looked around the dungeon. The mold was gone. The water was gone.

Then he heard a noise.

From the top of the stairs.

Heavy boots.

But these were not the auditors. These were not the knight.

These were smaller boots. Faster.

And then, a voice.

"Mr. Hutchins?"

It was the librarian. Mrs. Gable. She had come down to check on the noise.

Gerald froze. He held the half-fused rat. He looked at the hole. It was still visible.

Mrs. Gable walked into the room.

She saw Gerald, and she saw the rat. She saw the hole.

She screamed.

Gerald looked at her. He looked at the System.

He had one minute until the knight arrived. He had one hour until the audit.

And he had a witness.

Gerald smiled.

"Mrs. Gable," he said. "I am conducting an inspection. Please do not interfere with municipal operations."

She backed away. She reached for her phone.

Gerald looked at the phone. He looked at the System.

He had a new problem. He had a new resource.

He typed a query.

**[Query: Can a Municipal Employee be cited for Unauthorized Photography in a Restricted Zone?]

**[Answer: Yes.]**

Gerald looked at Mrs. Gable.

"Put the phone down," he said. "Or I will write you up for a Class C violation. And I will make sure you never get promoted."

Mrs. Gable lowered the phone. Her hands shook.

Gerald went back to the rat. He pressed it deeper.

**[Binding...]**

The hole closed.

The draft stopped.

Gerald stood up. He brushed the dust from his vest.

He was ready for the knight. He was ready for the audit.

But he did not know that Mrs. Gable had already sent the photo.

And he did not know that the photo had already gone viral.

And he did not know that the Gilded Cage had sent not just a knight, which had sent a hunter.

Someone was already coming for him.
