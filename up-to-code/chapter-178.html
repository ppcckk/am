Chapter 178: Code Compliant Dungeon

# Chapter 178

The air in the corridor tasted like wet copper and old paper. Kael adjusted the haptic feedback on his gauntlets, checking the durability meters that flickered red at the edges of his vision. His party, a ragtag collection of high-level adventurers, moved with the practiced caution of people who had walked into ambushes and lived to complain about the loot drop.

Ahead, the dungeon entrance gaped like a wound in the city's foundation. It wasn't jagged or dark. It was perfectly square. The lintel above the door bore a plaque.

*Ashford Municipal Library Sub-Basement. Zone 4. Capacity: 40. Maximum Occupancy Load: 40.*

"Did you read that?" asked Jinx, the party's rogue, her voice trembling slightly. "It has a capacity number. Like a club. Or a church."

Kael grunted, stepping over the threshold. "Code compliant.

That's the problem.

Usually, dungeons are a mess. This place is a building."

The floor beneath their boots was not stone. It was polished concrete, sealed with a substance that smelled faintly of lemon and industrial solvent. To the left, a handrail of brushed steel gleamed under the light of bioluminescent moss, which had been pruned into geometric shapes. To the right, a water leak had been contained by a series of transparent, slanted chutes that directed the flow into a sump pit labeled *Class B Waste Water*.

"This is wrong," Jinx whispered, drawing her daggers. "Where are the monsters? Where's the trap pressure plate?"

"Pressure plates are a liability," Kael muttered, echoing a briefing he'd heard from the Guild Masters. "He uses tripwires. He cites them for lack of warning signage."

They moved deeper.

The layout was a grid. Every corridor intersected at ninety-degree angles.

There were no dead ends.

Every junction had a sign pointing toward the nearest exit, with a running time to egress calculated based on average walking speed. It was unnerving. It felt less like a dungeon and more like a museum exhibit on the architecture of safety.

Then they heard the squeak.

It was soft, barely audible over the hum of the ventilation system. Jinx froze. Kael raised his shield. From around the corner of a bookshelf—which had been reinforced with steel brackets and bolted to the wall—emerged a rat.

It was not a giant rat. It was not a plague rat. It was a brown rat, roughly the size of a large house cat, wearing a tiny, reflective vest.

"It's wearing PPE," Jinx said, horrified.

The rat stopped. It looked at them.

It did not screech.

It did not charge.

It simply stood on its hind legs, raised one paw, and pointed toward a small, laminated card taped to the floor.

Kael lowered his shield. "Don't touch it."

They approached the card. It was laminated, waterproof, and printed in bold, sans-serif font.

**NOTICE: TRESPASSING IN A RESTRICTED ZONE.** **Subject: Unlicensed Biological Entity.** **Action Required: Immediate Exit or Citation for Non-Compliant Movement.**

"Is that a threat?" Jinx asked.

"It's a warning," Kael said. He looked at the rat. The rat looked back.

Suddenly, the air shifted. A pressure change popped in their ears. The bioluminescent moss dimmed. The water in the chutes surged.

From the shadows of the ceiling, a voice boomed. It was not magical. It was amplified through hidden speakers installed in the ventilation ducts.

"Attention, adventurers. You are currently in a Zone 4 Emergency Shelter. Please maintain three-point contact with any available handrails during transit. Failure to comply will result in a Citation for Unsafe Traversal."

Jinx yanked her dagger. "I'm going to stab the speaker."

"No," Kael said, his voice tight. "He's watching. He's always watching."

The rat squeaked once, turned, and scurried back into the darkness, its reflective vest catching the light.

***

Gerald Hutchins sat in the center of his dungeon, though he had no body. He was a presence in the concrete, a consciousness spread through the wiring and the water pipes. He watched the adventurers through a network of optical sensors embedded in the smoke detectors.

They were confused. Confusion was good. Confusion meant they were checking their surroundings.

Gerald focused on the status window hovering in his mind's eye.

**ZONE STATUS: EMERGENCY SHELTER (TRAP VARIATION)** **CAPACITY: 40/40** **CURRENT OCCUPANTS: 4 (Unlicensed Adventurers)** **COMPLIANCE RATING: 12/100** **CATASTROPHIC FAILURE IMMINENT: YES**

The reclassification had worked, but only partially. The System recognized the space as a shelter, which granted it protection from being destroyed by external forces. But it also triggered a new, terrifying mechanic: *Occupancy Liability.*

If an occupant was harmed, the Core took damage proportional to the injury. If an occupant died, the Core lost a permanent stat block.

Gerald had trapped them. He had sealed the exits. Now, he had to keep them alive.

Brokk, the neighboring Core who had been trying to kill Gerald for weeks, was pounding on the dungeon's outer walls. The vibrations shook the bookshelves. The sump pump groaned under the increased water pressure caused by Brokk's earth-shaking attacks.

*He wants the space,* Gerald thought. *Brokk wants to expand his dungeon into my footprint. He's trying to break my walls.*

The adventurers were moving toward the center. They were looking for the boss room. There was no boss room. There was only Gerald.

Kael shouted into the darkness. "Show yourself! We're here for the loot!"

Gerald did not speak. He adjusted the lighting. The bioluminescent moss flared brighter, illuminating the path they were taking. He watched Jinx step toward a gap in the handrail.

*Violation: Fall Hazard.*

Gerald triggered a localized magnetic pulse in the floor tile Jinx was about to step on. Her boot stuck to the ground. She stumbled, catching herself on the rail.

"Whoa," Jinx breathed. "Slippery."

"It's not slippery," Kael said, scanning the floor. "It's magnetic. He's trying to keep us up."

Gerald pulled up the **System Manual: Section 19.4 (Emergency Shelter Protocols)**.

**Subsection C: Evacuation Drills.** *In the event of catastrophic structural failure, the Shelter must initiate an evacuation drill. All occupants must proceed to designated muster points within 120 seconds. Occupants who fail to reach muster points will be deemed 'Non-Compliant Entities' and subject to removal by Force.*

Removal by Force.

If the System enforced this, and the adventurers failed the drill, they would be ejected. Ejection usually meant being crushed by the collapsing walls or thrown into the vacuum of unformed space.

Gerald had thirty seconds left before the drill initiated.

He needed them to move. Fast. But he couldn't let them run into the danger zones.

He typed.

**COMMAND: ACTIVATE FIRE ALARM.**

The sound was deafening. A rhythmic, pulsating beep echoed through the corridors. Red strobe lights, installed in compliance with Section 12.4, flashed from the ceiling.

Jinx covered her ears. "What the hell is that?"

"Fire alarm," Kael said. "But there's no smoke."

"Look at the exits," Jinx pointed. The sealed doors were now glowing green. An arrow on the floor lit up, leading them down a side corridor.

Kael hesitated. "Trick."

"Look at the handrails," Jinx said. "They're warm. That's heat tracing. That's real infrastructure."

Gerald watched them. They were moving. Slowly, suspiciously, but they were moving.

He needed them to go faster. He needed them to reach the muster point, which was located in the small utility closet near the sump pump. It was the safest place in the dungeon.

But Brokk was hitting the wall near the muster point. The concrete was cracking.

Gerald had to choose. He could reinforce the wall, spending all his Mana, leaving the adventurers unprotected. Or he could leave it.

If the wall collapsed, the adventurers would be exposed to Brokk's spawn. They would die. Gerald would lose stats.

If Gerald reinforced the wall, the drill would continue. The System would check their position. If they were still in the corridor, they would be ejected.

He had to get them to the closet.

He looked at the rat. The rat was near the closet.

**COMMAND: DEPLOY BIOLOGICAL GUIDANCE UNIT.**

The rat squealed. It emerged from the utility closet, holding a small, glowing beacon in its mouth. It ran into the corridor, toward the adventurers.

"Look," Jinx said. "The rat. It has a light."

"It's leading us," Kael said. He looked at the green arrow on the floor. It pointed toward the closet.

They ran.

The floor beneath them shook. Cracks spiderwebbed across the polished concrete. The bookshelves rattled, their steel brackets groaning.

Brokk's voice rumbled through the walls. *"Gerald! I smell fear! I smell your little pets!"*

Gerald ignored him. He watched the adventurers sprint. Kael was in the lead, his shield raised.

Jinx was close behind. The other two, a mage and a cleric, were lagging.

The timer in Gerald's mind ticked down. *10... 9... 8...*

The mage stumbled. A piece of ceiling tile fell, narrowly missing his head.

"Get up!" Kael shouted.

The mage scrambled to his feet.

*5... 4...*

They reached the utility closet.

The door was open. Inside, the space was cramped. There was a fire extinguisher mounted on the wall.

There was a first-aid kit.

There was a small, folding cot.

Kael shoved the mage inside. Jinx followed. The cleric ducked in last.

Kael grabbed the door handle. It was heavy. He pulled it shut.

*0.*

**SYSTEM NOTIFICATION: EVACUATION DRILL COMPLETE.** **OCCUPANT STATUS: COMPLIANT.** **NO EJECTION REQUIRED.**

The shaking stopped. The alarms ceased. The green lights turned off.

Silence.

Inside the closet, the adventurers huddled together.

They looked around, confused. There was no treasure. No chest.

No boss. Just a fire extinguisher and a smell of damp earth.

"Why are we here?" Jinx asked.

Kael looked at the door. He could hear the water churning outside. He could hear the distant, fading growls of Brokk's creatures as they were repelled by the sealed door's compliance field.

"He saved us," Kael said quietly.

Jinx stared at him. "He trapped us in a closet."

"He directed us to the safest spot in the dungeon," Kael said.

"Look at the walls.

No cracks. No spawn points.

This is a panic room."

Jinx touched the wall. It was solid.

"This is insane.

This isn't a dungeon. It's a building."

"It's a Code," Kael said.

***

Gerald exhaled, though he had no lungs. The Mana drain from the reinforcement spell had left him feeling thin, stretched. He checked his status.

**COMPLIANCE RATING: 15/100**

The drill had added three points. Small, but significant.

Brokk was still outside. The vibrations had stopped, but the threat remained. Brokk was waiting for Gerald to relax.

But Gerald saw something else.

In the corner of his vision, a new notification blinked.

**NOTIFICATION: INSPECTOR GENERAL AUDIT SCHEDULED.** **REASON: UNAUTHORIZED ZONE RECLASSIFICATION (EMERGENCY SHELTER).** **TIME: 10 MINUTES.**

Gerald's digital heart skipped a beat.

An audit. The Inspector General didn't just check ratings. They checked for *system abuse*.

Using a dungeon as an emergency shelter was a loophole. It was legal, technically, because the definition of "Emergency Shelter" in the System's architecture allowed for non-hostile structures. But using it to trap adventurers? That was ambiguous.

If the Inspector General arrived, they would see the sealed doors. They would see the magnetic floors. They would see the "Trap Variation" tag.

They would declare Gerald a Violation.

And if he was a Violation, he could be demolished.

Gerald looked at the adventurers in the closet. They were waiting. Kael was examining the fire extinguisher. Jinx was trying to pick the lock on the first-aid kit.

Gerald had ten minutes.

He couldn't undo the reclassification. The drill had locked it in. The System had recognized the "Emergency Shelter" status as valid for the duration of the crisis.

He had to change the definition.

He pulled up the code. **Section 8.1: Ventilation.**

He had ignored it for so long because he had no fans. But now, the sump pump was running. The air circulation was active.

**COMMAND: RECLASSIFY VENTILATION SYSTEM.**

He typed rapidly.

**NEW DESIGNATION: LIFE SUPPORT SYSTEM.**

The System paused. It scanned the pipes. The water flow. The air exchange.

**ACCEPTED.**

Now, the dungeon wasn't just a shelter. It was a life-support unit.

**SECTION 19.1: HOSPITAL INFRASTRUCTURE.**

Gerald needed to add beds. He needed to add medical supplies. He needed to add a doctor.

He looked at the rat.

The rat was in the closet, sitting on the cot. It was gnawing on a piece of cheese that Kael had dropped.

**COMMAND: PROMOTE BIOLOGICAL ENTITY.**

The rat froze. The cheese fell from its mouth.

A beam of light, thin and precise, shot from the ceiling and hit the rat. The rat's fur stood on end. Its eyes glowed white.

**NEW DESIGNATION: MEDICAL ASSISTANT.** **SKILL SET: FIRST AID (PASSIVE), TRIAGE (PASSIVE).**

The rat squeaked, confused. It looked at its paws. Then it looked at Jinx.

Jinx gasped. "Did that rat just glow?"

Kael stepped forward, his hand on his sword. "Don't move. It's a mimic."

The rat walked over to Jinx.

It sniffed her boot. It then walked to the first-aid kit.

It used its paw to turn the latch.

The kit clicked open.

Inside were bandages, antiseptic, and a small vial of mana potion.

The rat picked up the mana potion in its mouth and offered it to Jinx.

Jinx stared at the rat. She stared at the potion.

"Is it... giving me a health potion?"

Gerald watched. The rat had just provided a medical supply.

**COMPLIANCE RATING: 18/100**

The audit was coming. Gerald had to prove his dungeon was a hospital, not a trap.

He looked at the door. The seal was holding. But outside, he heard a new sound. Not Brokk's pounding.

Footsteps. Heavy, armored footsteps.

The door to the dungeon entrance opened.

Not from the inside.

The entire sub-basement door, the one leading up to the library, had been unlocked from the outside.

Gerald's sensors flared red.

Someone was entering the library. And they were walking toward the sub-basement.

Brokk hadn't broken in. Someone else had.

A voice echoed down the stairs. It was calm, professional, and terrifyingly familiar.

"Hutchins. I'm here for your inspection."

Gerald recognized the voice. It was the only person in Ashford who spoke like that.

The Head of the City Planning Commission.
