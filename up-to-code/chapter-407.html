Chapter 407: Citation Stops Lava

# Chapter 407

The lava did not care about the citation.

It bubbled up through the cracked concrete, glowing a violent, angry orange. The heat hit Gerald's face before the smoke did. It smelled of sulfur and burning drywall. His clipboard, still clutched in his left hand, curled at the edges.

Form 7-B was floating in the air before him, rendered in translucent blue glyphs. **[Violation: Unpermitted Thermal Source. Severity: Catastrophic. Citation Pending.]**

The System accepted the claim.

For a second, the lava froze.

It hung in mid-air, suspended by the sheer weight of bureaucratic inertia. The flow halted. The bubbles stopped rising. The temperature in the sub-basement dropped ten degrees, then spiked again as the System attempted to reconcile the impossible physics with the legal framework.

Gerald stared at the frozen lava. He did not breathe. He checked the timestamp on the citation.

*Approved.*

The Inspector General lowered her glass sword. Her eyes, previously hard and unyielding, widened. She looked at the lava, then at Gerald, then at the empty space where his body used to be.

"You cited the floor," she said.

"Gerald Hutchins," Gerald corrected. "I am a municipal building inspector. I do not fight fires. I file reports on them."

The lava cracked. A fissure split the frozen mass, shooting a jet of molten rock toward the library's load-bearing column.

Gerald flinched. Not from fear, but from the violation of structural integrity.

"That is a Class A hazard," he muttered. "Non-compliant egress."

The Inspector General stepped forward. Her armor, woven from solidified light, clinked softly. She raised her hand, and a shield of geometric force snapped into place between Gerald and the lava. The molten rock splashed against it, hissing like a kettle.

"The Deep Core does not respect citations," she said. "Vane was a minor nuisance.

The entity attacking you now is a Dungeon Lord, which has no Compliance Rating because it has no walls. It is a living volcano.

"

"Then I will cite the volcano," Gerald said.

He reached for his pen. It was gone. The clipboard was gone. In its place, a floating menu of options shimmered in his peripheral vision.

**[Action Required: Immediate Mitigation.]** **[Option A: Ventilate (Cost: 50 Mana)]** **[Option B: Contain (Cost: 200 Mana)]** **[Option C: Improvise (Cost:???)]**

Gerald hated Option C.

Improvisation was a deviation from the standard operating procedure. It led to errors.

It led to leaks.

It led to lawsuits.

But the lava was moving again. The frozen state was breaking. The System, realizing that a floor could not be cited as a separate entity from the dungeon itself, had reversed the suspension.

The lava flowed.

Gerald looked at the ceiling. The library above was shaking.

Dust fell from the cracks.

If the support columns failed, the books would fall on the children's section, which was unacceptable.

He looked at the Inspector General.

"Where is the fire suppression system?" Gerald asked.

"There is none," she said. "You failed to install the sprinklers three chapters ago. You prioritized handrails."

Gerald felt a surge of irritation. The handrails were ADA compliant. The sprinklers were optional in sub-basements with less than 2,000 square feet.

He had calculated the risk. He had filed the waiver.

But the waiver did not cover active volcanic intrusion.

"Then we have to build one," Gerald said.

"Now?"

"Immediately."

Gerald closed his eyes. He reached out with his mind, touching the dungeon's architecture. He could feel the concrete, the rebar, the water pipes. He could feel the weakness in the mortar joints where the rat had chewed through the sealant last week.

He pulled mana from his reserves, which was low. Dangerously low. The fight with Vane had drained him.

The audit had taken a toll.

But he had one thing the Deep Core did not. He had the blueprints.

He had the original municipal plans for the Ashford Library, filed in 1987. He had them memorized. He knew exactly where the water mains ran. He knew the pressure ratings of every pipe.

Gerald visualized the water main. It ran beneath the floor, directly under the spot where the lava was currently pooling.

He did not try to stop the lava. He tried to move the water.

**[Command: Reroute Hydraulic Flow.]** **[Cost: 150 Mana.]**

The dungeon groaned. The pipes beneath the floor shifted. The concrete buckled, not from heat, but from pressure.

The Inspector General watched, her glass sword forgotten in her hand.

"What are you doing?"

"Installing temporary infrastructure," Gerald said.

He slammed his mental hand against the dungeon's core interface. He didn't just want water.

He wanted high-pressure steam.

The code required fire suppression to activate at 135 degrees Fahrenheit. But the code also allowed for manual override in cases of imminent structural collapse.

Gerald found the override switch in the System's hidden menu. It was buried under three layers of safety protocols, marked with red warnings. He ignored them.

He bypassed the thermostat, and he forced the water into the hottest part of the floor.

The result was instantaneous. A column of white steam erupted from the floor, blasting upward with the force of a geyser. It hit the lava jet, creating a violent hiss that sounded like the dungeon screaming.

The lava solidified. Not into stone, but into obsidian glass. The rapid cooling trapped the heat inside, but it stopped the flow.

The steam cloud filled the sub-basement, obscuring everything.

Gerald coughed. The air was hot and wet. His vision blurred.

"You used the water main," the Inspector General said. Her voice was muffled by the steam.

"I used the building's existing utilities," Gerald corrected. "It is more efficient than bringing in a fire truck."

"The water main is for drinking. And toilets."

"It is also for fire suppression. Section 8, Paragraph 4. Emergency utility diversion."

The steam cleared. The lava was now a jagged ridge of black glass, cutting across the floor.

It was ugly, which was a violation of aesthetic codes.

But it was solid.

And it was cooling.

Gerald walked over to the obsidian ridge. He touched it. It was still hot, but manageable. He pulled out a piece of chalk that had manifested in his inventory.

He drew a line on the obsidian.

**[New Feature: Obsidian Barrier.]** **[Property: Heat Resistance +50%.] **[Property: Slip Hazard.]**

Gerald frowned. The slip hazard was a problem. He made a note to install rubber mats later.

"Good," the Inspector General said. She sounded impressed, which Gerald did not trust. "But you have not defeated the attacker. You have merely cooled the projectile."

"I have mitigated the damage," Gerald said. "That is the scope of my job."

"The Dungeon Lord will not stop," she said. "It is sending more."

As if on cue, the ceiling cracked. A second projectile, this one green and acidic, punched through the concrete. It landed in the corner, near the electrical panel.

Sparks flew. The smell of ozone mixed with the sulfur.

**[Warning: Electrical Hazard Detected.]** **[Risk of Total Dungeon Shutdown: 80%.]**

Gerald's hands shook. He looked at the electrical panel. It was his pride. He had rewired it himself, ensuring all circuits were grounded and labeled.

The acid was eating through the casing.

If the panel blew, the lights would go out. The ventilation fans would stop. The dungeon would suffocate.

"Can you fix that?" the Inspector General asked.

"I can," Gerald said. "But I need time."

"You have no time."

The green blob grew larger. It was spreading across the floor, eating the drywall.

Gerald looked at the acid. He looked at the electrical panel. He looked at the Inspector General.

He had a tool. Not a spell. Not a weapon. A rule.

**[System Rule: Electrical Code Compliance.]** **[If a dungeon is fully grounded, it is immune to lightning damage.]**

The acid was not lightning. But the resulting short circuit would be.

Gerald ran to the electrical panel. The acid hissed as it touched the metal door. He could feel the vibration of the current inside. He could feel the imbalance.

He opened the panel.

The Inspector General stepped back.

"Careful."

Gerald ignored her. He saw the wires. Red for live.

Black for neutral. White for ground.

The acid had eaten through the white wire, and the ground was broken.

Without a ground, the dungeon's lightning resistance passive was active, but vulnerable to surges.

He needed to reconnect the ground.

He reached into his inventory.

He pulled out a copper wire. It was the spare he kept for emergencies.

He had bought it from the library gift shop.

It cost four dollars, and he twisted the wire around the acid-eaten terminal. The acid burned his fingers. He did not feel pain. He felt annoyance.

The connection was poor. The acid was insulating the contact.

It would not work.

Gerald looked around. He needed a conductive material that was not metal. Or he needed to remove the acid.

He couldn't remove it. He couldn't touch it.

He looked at the obsidian ridge. Obsidian was a natural insulator. But it was also a semiconductor in some contexts.

No, that was not enough, and he looked at the water. The steam was still rising. The water main had been rerouted, but there was still moisture in the air. Condensation was forming on the walls.

Water was conductive.

If he made the water conductive, he could create a path to ground.

Salt.

Where would he get salt?

The library had a break room. The break room had a vending machine. The vending machine had pretzels.

Gerald closed his eyes. He focused on the dungeon's map. The break room was three feet above him.

He could reach it, and he extended his mana, pushing it up through the floor, into the break room. He found the vending machine. He found the bag of pretzels.

He did not have hands. He had force.

He crushed the bag.

Salt rained down through the floor vents.

The Inspector General looked up, confused.

"What are you doing?"

"Gritting the floor," Gerald said.

Salt fell onto the acid, onto the wires, onto the floor.

It mixed with the condensation.

The conductive layer formed.

Gerald twisted the copper wire tighter. The salt water bridged the gap between the broken ground wire and the panel chassis.

The circuit closed.

A hum filled the room. The lights flickered, then stabilized. The fans spun up, louder than before.

The green acid hissed, reacting to the salt, but the electrical current found a new path. It flowed into the ground, away from the sensitive components.

The panel survived.

**[Compliance Bonus: Grounding Restored.]** **[Lightning Resistance Increased by 10%.]**

Gerald slumped against the wall. His mana was gone. He felt hollow. Empty.

The Inspector General walked over to him. She looked at the salt-covered floor, then at the electrical panel, then at Gerald.

"You used pretzels," she said.

"Pretzels are an acceptable source of sodium chloride," Gerald said. His voice was weak.

The ceiling shook again. This time, it was not a projectile. It was a voice.

It boomed through the concrete, shaking the dust from the rafters.

**"WHO VIOLATES MY DOMAIN?"**

The voice was deep, resonant, and angry. It sounded like rocks grinding together.

Gerald looked up. The crack in the ceiling was widening. A face was forming in the stone. It was made of magma and anger.

**"I AM MAGMA-MASTER VORAX. I AM THE HEART OF THE MOUNTAIN. I AM THE FIRE THAT CANNOT BE QUENCHED."**

Gerald straightened his spine. He adjusted his tie, though he had no tie. He adjusted his posture.

He looked at the face in the ceiling.

"You are unlicensed," Gerald said.

The face paused.

**"WHAT?"**

"You are operating a thermal extraction facility within city limits without a permit," Gerald said. "You are emitting particulate matter. You are creating a slip hazard with obsidian glass. And you are failing to maintain a safe distance from residential structures."

The face in the ceiling turned a brighter shade of red.

**"I AM THE MOUNTAIN. I DO NOT NEED A PERMIT."**

"The System requires a permit," Gerald said. He pointed to the air, where his clipboard had reappeared. It was empty, but he pointed anyway.

"Form 12-C: Unauthorized Geological Activity. Immediate Stop Work Order."

The System accepted the order.

A red barrier appeared around the dungeon. It was not physical. It was legal. It was a force field made of red tape and bureaucratic denial.

Vorax slammed into it.

The barrier held.

The Inspector General stared at Gerald.

"You cannot hold a Dungeon Lord with a Stop Work Order."

"I can," Gerald said. "If I document everything."

Vorax roared. The barrier rippled.

Gerald opened his mouth to speak, to cite the violation of air quality standards.

But the System interrupted him.

**[Alert: Citation Rejected.]**

Gerald froze.

**[Reason: Target Entity 'Vorax' is classified as 'Natural Phenomenon.' Municipal codes do not apply to weather.]**

Gerald blinked.

"No," he said.

He read the text again.

**[Natural Phenomenon.]**

Vorax was not a dungeon core. He was a geological feature that had gained sentience.

Which meant Gerald could not cite him.

Which meant Gerald could not stop him.

The barrier flickered. Vorax was pressing harder. The red tape was tearing.

Gerald looked at the Inspector General.

"Fix it," he said.

"I cannot," she said. "You are outside your jurisdiction."

"Then what do I do?"

The Inspector General looked at the crumbling barrier. She looked at the magma face, which was smiling now. A terrible, burning smile.

"You have one option," she said.

She pointed to Gerald's chest.

"You can appeal."

Gerald shook his head.

"No. Appeals are for Cores. I am a Core."

"You are a Core," she said. "But Vorax is not. He is a Monster. And the System has a rule for Monsters."

She stepped closer.

"If you can prove that Vorax is, in fact, a Dungeon Core disguised as a Mountain, you can issue a Citation."

Gerald looked at the magma face.

Vorax laughed. It sounded like an earthquake.

**"I AM NO CORE. I AM FIRE."**

Gerald looked at his clipboard, and he had to prove Vorax was a Core.

How?

He had to find the Core.

Every Core had a central room. A heart. A place where the mana flowed.

Gerald looked at the crack in the ceiling. The magma was coming from there.

But where did it go?

He looked at the floor. The water main. The rerouted water.

It had flowed into the lava, which had flowed into the heat.

Gerald closed his eyes. He listened.

Not with his ears. With his mana.

He felt the flow of the dungeon. He felt the flow of the library.

And then he felt it.

A pulse.

Deep below the library. Below the sub-basement. Below the bedrock.

A heartbeat.

Vorax was not the mountain. Vorax was the projection. The Core was hiding beneath Gerald.

Gerald opened his eyes, and he looked down.

"The floor," he whispered.

The Inspector General followed his gaze.

"Under the lava?"

"Under the lava," Gerald said. "He is right beneath me."

The barrier shattered.

Vorax's hand, made of fire, punched through the ceiling. It aimed for Gerald.

Gerald did not move, and he waited.

The hand grabbed him.

The heat was unbearable. Gerald's code interface screamed warnings. **[Integrity: Critical. Structure: Failing.]**

But Gerald did not fight, and he reached out with his mind.

He reached down.

He found the heartbeat.

He found the Core.

And he did something he had never done before, and he did not cite the violation.

He invited it.

**[System Action: Joint Inspection Request.]** **[Target: Vorax Core.]** **[Reason: Shared Easement.]**

The magma hand froze.

Vorax's face in the ceiling stopped smiling.

**"WHAT IS THIS?"**

Gerald held the hand. He held the fire. He held the pain.

"You are sharing my dungeon's foundation," Gerald said. "That makes you a partner."

He smiled. It was a thin, dry smile.

"And partners must follow the same rules."

The System processed the request.

**[Joint Inspection Scheduled.]** **[Location: The Heart of the Mountain.]** **[Warning: Entering Vorax's Domain.]**

Gerald pulled himself into the magma, and he went with the fire.

Down.

Into the dark.
