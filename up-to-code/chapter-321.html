Chapter 321: Citation for a Structure

# Chapter 321

The pen hovered in the air, a sliver of plastic between Gerald and the officer's clipboard.

"Tick tock," Gerald said again.

The officer, a young man with too much hair gel and not enough sleep, looked at the woman beside him. He looked at the clipboard. He looked at Gerald.

"Ma'am," the officer said, his voice cracking slightly. "What do I write here? 'Liability Waiver' isn't a standard form."

The woman, whose badge identified her as Deputy Director Vane of the Adventurers' Guild Compliance Division, stiffened. Her fingers tightened around the clipboard. A small crease appeared between her eyebrows.

"This is a seizure," Vane said. "Not a transfer of ownership. We are seizing hazardous materials. You are a hazardous material."

Gerald didn't blink. He kept the pen steady.

"I am a registered municipal structure," Gerald said. "Under Ashford City Ordinance 44-B, sub-section 12, any seizure of a registered structure requires a signed Liability Assumption Form from the seizing entity.

If you do not sign it, the seizure is technically illegal.

The police will not arrest you for illegal seizure. They will arrest *me* for failing to surrender. But you will be liable for every wire, every pipe, and every mold spore that falls out of my walls when you try to move me.

"

Vane's lips pressed into a thin line.

"You're stalling."

"I'm waiting," Gerald said.

"The System doesn't care about your feelings. It cares about paperwork.

You want to evict me?

You have to own me. If you own me, you own the ice. If you own the ice, you own the death that happens when the ice melts and hits the electrical grid I just repaired.

"

The officer shifted his weight.

"Ma'am, the ice is... it's spreading. I can see frost on the boots."

Vane looked down. Her boots were indeed coated in a fine layer of white. She looked back at Gerald.

"Sign it," Gerald said. "Or leave."

Vane opened her mouth. Closed it. She looked at the officer.

"Get the supervisor," she whispered.

The officer nodded and backed away, pulling his radio from his belt.

Gerald watched them go. He didn't relax. His fingers ached from holding the pen.

He turned to the ice, which had stopped spreading, but it hadn't receded. It sat in the corner of the sub-basement, a jagged mountain of blue-white crystal that hummed with a low, menacing frequency.

"Kaelen," Gerald said to the empty room. "You're clever. But you're sloppy."

He walked over to the ice. He didn't touch it. He pulled a tape measure from his pocket.

He extended the tape. He measured the base.

"Three feet wide. Two feet high."

He wrote the numbers in his notebook.

"Volume: approximately six cubic feet."

He looked up at the ceiling. The sprinkler heads were intact. The water lines were pressurized.

"If you melt this," Gerald muttered, "you flood the basement. If you flood the basement, you short the main panel.

If you short the main panel, the ventilation fans stop.

If the fans stop, the mold spores proliferate.

If the mold spores proliferate, my Compliance Rating drops below 40. At 39, the System initiates a 'Health Hazard' audit.

At 35, it shuts down the dungeon for 'sanitization.'

"

Gerald frowned.

"Kaelen didn't just attack me. He attacked my compliance rating."

He realized then that the attack wasn't meant to kill him. It was meant to make him unmanageable. To make him a liability.

The officer returned with a man in a suit. The man was older, with gray hair and eyes that looked like they had seen too many spreadsheets.

"Mr. Hutchins," the man said. "I am Director Halloway. I've been briefed."

Gerald nodded.

"Director. Good. We have a situation."

"We have a seizure," Halloway said. His voice was smooth, practiced. "The Guild has filed an emergency injunction. We are taking control of this dungeon."

Gerald looked at Halloway.

"Have you filed Form 11-C? The Emergency Hazard Abatement Application?"

Halloway blinked.

"We filed the injunction."

"The injunction is a request," Gerald said. "The abatement application is the permission.

Without the application, the injunction is just a piece of paper.

The System doesn't recognize injunctions. It recognizes forms.

"

Halloway's jaw tightened.

"You're trying to confuse us with bureaucracy."

"I'm trying to save you from a lawsuit," Gerald said.

"You see that ice? It's a Class 3 Environmental Hazard.

To abate it, you need a certified hazmat team.

Do you have one?

"

Halloway looked at the officer. The officer shook his head.

"We have combat teams," Halloway said.

"Combat teams don't have hazmat certification," Gerald said. "They have swords.

Swords don't melt ice, and they make it worse.

If you send a warrior in here, he'll hack at the ice. The shards will scatter.

The surface area will increase. The melt rate will accelerate. You'll flood the building.

The library above will collapse. The City will sue the Guild.

The Guild will sue you.

"

Halloway stared at Gerald.

"What do you want?" Halloway asked.

"I want you to leave," Gerald said. "I will handle the hazard myself. I have the tools.

I have the code.

I have the plan.

"

"You are being evicted," Halloway said. "You are losing your Core Key."

Gerald felt a cold spike in his chest.

"You can't revoke my Key," Gerald said. "I'm the registered owner."

"You're the registered owner of a Hazard Zone," Halloway said. "Under the new System Guidelines, ownership of a Hazard Zone is suspended pending inspection."

Gerald looked at the ice. He looked at Halloway.

"An inspection," Gerald said.

"Yes," Halloway said.

Gerald lowered the pen.

"An inspection requires a hearing," Gerald said.

"A Hearing for a Hazard Zone declaration. That's a Level 5 Appeal. It takes forty-eight hours to schedule.

It takes two hours to conduct. It takes one hour to rule.

"

Halloway smiled. It was a thin, predatory smile.

"Then you'll be under temporary suspension for seventy-two hours," Halloway said.

"During that time, the Guild will manage the hazard.

We will remove the ice. We will fix the ventilation.

And when the hearing happens, we will prove you are negligent.

"

Gerald felt his stomach drop.

"If you remove the ice," Gerald said, "you alter the dungeon's structure. You change the environmental balance. That's a modification. You need a permit."

"We're emergency responders," Halloway said. "We don't need permits."

Gerald looked at the System interface hovering in his peripheral vision. It was red. A flashing warning: *UNAUTHORIZED INTERVENTION DETECTED.*

He had one minute to act, and he looked at the officer.

"Officer, did you file the trespassing report?"

The officer looked confused.

"No. I was just here to assist."

"File it," Gerald said. "Right now.

Report unauthorized entry.

Report property damage. Report failure to obtain entry permit.

"

Halloway stepped forward.

"This is obstruction."

"It's my right," Gerald said. "I am a citizen. I am reporting a crime."

The officer hesitated. He looked at Halloway.

"Sir, should I?"

Halloway's eyes narrowed.

"No. You are not to engage in administrative actions while a seizure is in progress."

The officer looked down at his shoes.

Gerald felt a surge of fury. Cold, hard, and focused.

He turned to the ice, and he closed his eyes. He focused on the System.

He accessed the Dungeon Interface. He found the object labeled *Ice Block (Foreign Material).*

He selected it.

He chose the option: *Cite.*

The System paused.

*Target not owned by Core. Citation invalid.*

Gerald gritted his teeth, and he couldn't cite Kaelen's magic because it wasn't part of Gerald's dungeon yet. It was an intrusion. An attack.

But it was *in* the dungeon.

He looked at the ceiling.

The water pipes ran along the rafters. They were old.

Galvanized steel.

Prone to corrosion, and he looked at the ice. It was melting. Slowly. Droplets formed on its surface.

The droplets fell.

They hit the floor. They splashed. Some splashed onto the nearest pipe.

Gerald watched the water hit the metal, and he accessed the *Water* object. It was listed as *Leakage (Minor).*

He accessed the *Pipe* object. It was listed as *Plumbing Fixture (Aged).*

He had a loophole.

He filed a new citation. Not on the ice. On the *plumbing*.

*Citation: Failure to Maintain Corrosion-Resistant Materials in High-Humidity Zones. Code 7.4.2.*

The System processed it.

*Violation Detected. Severity: Moderate.*

*Effect: Accelerated Corrosion applied to target object.*

The pipe hissed.

A tiny crack appeared. A spray of water shot out.

Halloway jumped back.

"What did you do?" Halloway shouted.

"I applied maintenance," Gerald said. "The pipe was failing. It needed to fail faster to be replaced. You're welcome."

Halloway stared at the geyser of water.

"You're destroying your own dungeon?"

"I'm accelerating the repair timeline," Gerald said. "If the pipe breaks, it becomes a visible hazard.

Visible hazards require immediate abatement.

Immediate abatement means *you* have to fix it.

Now. Not in seventy-two hours.

Now."

Halloway looked at the water flooding the floor. It was rising.

"Stop it," Halloway said.

"I can't," Gerald said. "The citation is active.

The corrosion is spreading.

If you don't fix the pipe, the corrosion will spread to the electrical junction box above it. That's a Class 1 Fire Hazard.

"

Halloway looked at the junction box. It was sparking. Blue electricity danced across the metal.

"Fix it!" Halloway screamed at the officer. "Fix the pipe!"

The officer pulled out a wrench. He ran toward the geyser.

Gerald watched him go, and he looked at Halloway.

"You want to seize me?" Gerald said.

"You have to deal with the water first. If you don't, the electrical fire starts.

If the fire starts, the sprinklers go off.

If the sprinklers go off, the water doubles. If the water doubles, the ice melts faster. If the ice melts faster, the room floods.

If the room floods, the library collapses.

"

Halloway's face was pale.

"This isn't over," Halloway said.

"No," Gerald said. "It's just begun."

Halloway turned and walked away. His heels clicked on the wet floor. He didn't look back.

Gerald stood alone, and the water was still rising. It was ankle-deep.

He looked at the ice. It was smaller now. The heat from the sparking wire was melting it.

He looked at the officer, who was wrestling with the pipe.

"Officer," Gerald said.

The officer looked up, soaked.

"Yeah?"

"You're working without a permit," Gerald said. "I'm citing you."

The officer dropped the wrench.

"Excuse me?"

"Failure to obtain a Work Permit for Plumbing Repair," Gerald said. "Code 12.1. Penalty: Fine of 500 credits and immediate suspension of duties."

The System flashed.

*Citation Accepted.*

The officer froze. A red light blinked on his chest.

*You are currently suspended from all municipal and dungeon-related activities.*

The officer looked at Gerald with horror.

"I... I was just trying to help."

"Good intentions don't bypass code," Gerald said. "Step back. Leave the wrench."

The officer scrambled back.

Gerald walked over to the pipe. He picked up the wrench.

He looked at the crack. It was jagged. Ugly.

He looked at the System, and he accessed the *Pipe* object again.

He selected *Repair.*

*Repair requires Level 3 Core Authority. Current Level: 1.*

Gerald smiled, and he didn't have the authority.

But he had the citation.

He filed a second citation. On the *Pipe* itself.

*Citation: Structural Weakness in Load-Bearing Conduit. Code 4.1. Penalty: Temporary Reinforcement Field.*

The System processed it.

*Violation Detected. Severity: High.*

*Effect: Temporary Reinforcement Field applied.*

The pipe glowed with a faint blue light. The crack sealed itself. The water stopped.

Gerald exhaled, and he looked at the ice. It was still there. Smaller. But still there.

He looked at the ceiling. The sparks had stopped. The junction box was safe.

He looked at the door. Halloway was gone. The officer was gone.

Gerald was alone, and he looked at the ice.

He looked at his notebook.

He wrote: *Kaelen. Level 40 Core. Uses Ice magic. Weakness: Heat.*

He looked at the System.

*Compliance Rating: 51/100.*

He had gained four points, and he had lost a fight. But he had won a battle.

He felt a vibration in his chest. A deep, resonant hum.

The System was changing something.

He looked at his dungeon map, and a new room had appeared. It wasn't a room. It was a *zone*.

*Zone: Hazard Containment Area.*

Gerald frowned.

He hadn't built it.

He opened the Zone Details.

*This zone has been created by the System in response to unauthorized intervention. It serves as a penalty buffer. Any further unauthorized intervention by the Adventurers' Guild will trigger a Lockdown.*

Gerald read it twice, and he understood.

The System had just created a trap for the Guild.

If they touched him again, they would trigger a Lockdown. A Lockdown meant the Guild would be banned from the district for thirty days.

Gerald smiled. It was a small, tight smile.

He looked at the ice one last time.

"Kaelen," he said. "You're in my zone now."

He sat down on a dry patch of floor, and he opened his inventory. He took out a sandwich.

He ate it slowly.

He watched the ice melt.

He watched the water drain into the newly repaired pipes.

He watched the System update.

*New Quest Available: Investigate the Source of Unauthorized Ice.*

*Reward: Core Level Up.*

*Failure: Permanent Loss of Ventilation.*

Gerald chewed the sandwich, and he looked at the quest description.

*The ice is not just an attack.

It is a marker.

Someone is mapping your dungeon. Find the map.

Stop the mapper.*

Gerald swallowed, and he looked at the ice.

It was gone.

In its place was a small, black scroll.

He picked it up.

It was warm, and he unrolled it.

It was a map.

But it wasn't a map of his dungeon.

It was a map of the City.

And on the map, there was a red X, and the X was not in his sub-basement.

The X was in the Mayor's Office.

Gerald stared at the map.

He looked up.

The door opened.

Not the side door. The main entrance.

The big, heavy steel door that led directly to the street.

It swung open.

A man walked in, and he was wearing a suit. He was holding a briefcase.

He looked exactly like Halloway.

But he wasn't Halloway.

He was the Mayor.

Gerald stood up.

"Mr. Hutchins," the Mayor said. "We need to talk."

Gerald looked at the map in his hand, and he looked at the Mayor.

He looked at the System.

*New Enemy Detected: City Hall.*

Gerald put the map in his pocket.

"I'm busy," Gerald said. "I have a ventilation issue."

The Mayor smiled. It wasn't a nice smile.

"You don't," he said. "You have a lawsuit."

He pulled a document from his briefcase, and he held it up.

It was a subpoena.

Gerald felt his blood run cold.

He looked at the document.

*Defendant: Gerald Hutchins.*

*Charge: Negligent Endangerment of Municipal Infrastructure.*

*Plaintiff: City of Ashford.*

Gerald looked at the Mayor.

"You're suing me?" Gerald said.

"Of course," the Mayor said. "You destroyed a pipe. You caused a flood. You endangered public safety."

Gerald looked at the System.

*Compliance Rating: 51.*

*Legal Status: At Risk.*

Gerald looked at the Mayor, and he looked at the subpoena.

He looked at the empty ice puddle.

He looked at the map in his pocket.

He had to act. Now.

He had to find a violation in the City's own infrastructure.

He had to cite the Mayor.

But the Mayor wasn't a dungeon core.

Gerald looked at the System interface, and he searched for *Municipal Structures.*

He found the entry: *Ashford City Hall.*

*Compliance Rating: 88/100.*

*Status: Active.*

Gerald looked at the Mayor.

"Mr. Mayor," Gerald said. "Do you have a permit for that briefcase?"

The Mayor paused.

"Excuse me?"

"Large bags are prohibited in public areas without a baggage inspection tag," Gerald said. "Code 3.4. Failure to display a tag is a citation."

The Mayor looked at his briefcase, and he looked at Gerald.

"I don't have a tag."

Gerald smiled.

He pulled out his pen.

"Sign this," Gerald said.

He held out a blank citation form.

The Mayor stared at him.

Gerald held the form out.

The System flashed.

*Target Acquired.*

*Citation Ready.*

Gerald looked the Mayor in the eye.

"Tick tock," Gerald said.
