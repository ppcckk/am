Chapter 332: To Do List

# Chapter 332

The citation form trembled in Gerald's hand. Not from fear, but from the sheer kinetic energy of the approaching boot. Dust rained down from the cracked library floor above, coating his gray suit in fine, white powder. It was a violation of air quality standards, specifically Section 4.2 regarding particulate matter in enclosed spaces.

"The Golden Blades have broken three zoning laws entering this space," Gerald said, his voice flat and dry as old parchment. "You cited them."

The Inspector General, a towering construct of light and legal precedent, tilted his blank, mask-like face. The scrollwork of its suit rustled with the sound of a thousand pages turning.

"I have not cited them," the mask said. "You have identified the potential for citation. The act of serving requires proximity and intent."

Gerald stepped forward. The arena floor was smooth, polished stone.

Too smooth, and he noted the lack of slip-resistant tiling. Another violation. He filed it mentally, adding it to the growing list that hummed in his peripheral vision.

"They are here," Gerald said.

The air in the arena warped. The gray void tore open at the eastern edge. Three figures dropped through, landing with the heavy thud of plate mail and the jingle of excessive loot bags. The leader, a man named Kaelen whose armor was gold-plated and structurally unsound, raised a sword that glowed with unstable magical energy.

"Boss!" Kaelen shouted. "Where's the loot drop?"

Gerald did not look at Kaelen. He looked at the sword.

"Your weapon," Gerald said, pointing with the citation form. "It is emitting a localized magical field that exceeds the permissible radiation limits for public assembly spaces. Furthermore, you are wearing armor that has been modified with unapproved arcane enchantments, which voids its structural integrity warranty under the Municipal Safety Act."

Kaelen blinked.

The other two Blades, a mage and a rogue, froze. They had been expecting fireballs.

They had been expecting minions, and they had not been expecting an OSHA inspector.

"Kill him," Kaelen said, confused but aggressive.

The mage chanted. A sphere of fire coalesced in her hand.

Gerald raised his hand. Not to cast a spell. To hold up a yellow clipboard.

"System," Gerald said. "Cite target for Unlicensed Combustion Device."

The System did not respond with a sound. It responded with a sensation.

A pressure in the chest.

A tightening of the lungs.

The mage's fireball flickered, then sputtered out. The air around her grew thin.

The oxygen levels in the arena had been deemed insufficient to support such volatile reactions, given the lack of proper ventilation ducts in the arena walls.

**[CITATION ISSUED.]** **[VIOLATION: UNLICENSED COMBUSTION.]** **[PENALTY: SPELL FAILURE RATE +40% FOR 10 MINUTES.]**

The mage stared at her hands. She tried again. Nothing. Just a puff of smoke that smelled like burnt hair.

Kaelen roared, charging forward. His sword swung in a wide, clumsy arc.

Gerald did not dodge. He stepped into the swing, but not into the blade.

He stepped into the space between the blade and the body.

A gap. A structural failure in the armor's joint articulation. He had measured those gaps.

He had inspected those joints when Kaelen's party had tried to enter the dungeon lobby earlier, where Gerald had denied them entry due to a missing liability waiver.

Gerald brought the wrench up. Not to hit Kaelen. To jam the wrench into the gap in the knee joint of the golden armor.

*Clang.*

The metal shrieked. Kaelen's leg buckled. He crashed to the floor, the weight of the armor pinning him.

"Illegal modification of load-bearing components," Gerald muttered, scribbling on the form. "Section 88-C. Immediate remediation required or removal from site."

The rogue, a wiry man named Vex, pulled out two daggers. He moved with unnatural speed, a blur of shadow.

He was fast.

Faster than Gerald. Too fast.

Gerald couldn't match the speed. But he could match the code.

Vex skidded toward the center of the arena. He aimed for Gerald's throat.

Gerald kicked a small, loose tile from his pocket. It wasn't a weapon. It was a piece of flooring he had pulled up during a previous inspection. He tossed it onto the floor in Vex's path.

Vex's foot landed on the loose tile. The tile shifted. The friction coefficient dropped to zero.

Vex slipped. He flailed, trying to regain balance, but the System had already flagged the area as a 'Slip and Fall Hazard Zone' due to the lack of non-slip surfacing.

**[HAZARD ZONE ACTIVE.]** **[DURABILITY DAMAGE TO FOOTWEAR +20%.]**

Vex's boots disintegrated. He slid across the smooth stone, crashing into the base of the Inspector General's podium.

Gerald walked over to Kaelen. The leader was struggling to rise, his armor locking up because Gerald had jammed the hydraulic servos with the wrench.

"You're not fighting me," Gerald said, looking down at Kaelen. "You're fighting the building code. And you're failing."

Kaelen gritted his teeth.

"You're a Core. You should be spawning monsters. You should be trying to kill us."

"I am trying to kill you," Gerald said. "But I am doing it legally.

There is a difference.

One gets you fined by the Adventurer's Guild.

The other gets you banned from the municipal zone.

"

He held up the citation form. It was filled out. Four violations. Three citations served.

The System flashed red.

**[BATTLE CONCLUDED: CODE COMPLIANCE VICTORY.]** **[PARTY STATUS: DISQUALIFIED.]** **[REWARD: NONE. PENALTY: GUILD REPUTATION -15.]**

The three adventurers vanished, teleported back to the lobby in shame. Their gear clattered in the distance as they were ejected from the arena.

Gerald stood alone in the gray void. The Inspector General watched him.

"Efficient," the mask said.

"It was messy," Gerald replied, picking up his wrench. "The floor is uneven. I need to install leveling pads before the next party arrives."

"You have defeated an unregistered party," the Inspector General said. "This triggers a new protocol."

Gerald felt a chill that had nothing to do with the ventilation rating.

"Which protocol?"

The air around Gerald changed. The gray stone of the arena softened, turning into something warm and pulsing. The floor beneath his feet became soft, like clay.

**[SYSTEM ALERT: HIGH-RISK ENTITY DETECTED.]** **[ENTITY: THE RED REGISTRY.]** **[ACTION: INITIATING BUREAUCRATIC LOCKDOWN.]**

A new window appeared in Gerald's vision. It was not a combat window. It was a permit application. A massive, labyrinthine form with three hundred fields.

**[TO CONTINUE DUNGEON OPERATION, YOU MUST APPLY FOR A CLASS-4 HAZARD PERMIT.]** **[CURRENT STATUS: PENDING REVIEW.]** **[ESTIMATED WAIT TIME: 600 YEARS.]**

Gerald stared at the number. Six hundred years. He was fifty-four. He had thirty-one years left, if he was lucky and didn't die of boredom.

"This is a trap," Gerald said.

"It is a safeguard," the Inspector General said. "The Adventurer's Guild has filed a complaint. They claim your methods are 'confusing' and 'depressing.' They are requesting your dungeon be reclassified as a 'Non-Essential Bureaucratic Maze' and demolished."

Gerald's eye twitched. Demolished. His walls.

His handrails. His inspected load-bearing pillars.

"Objection," Gerald said.

"On what grounds?"

"Gross procedural error," Gerald said, his voice rising.

He paced, his mind racing, connecting dots he hadn't seen before.

"The Adventurer's Guild filed a complaint. But they entered my space without a permit.

They are trespassers.

A trespasser cannot file a zoning complaint against a property owner.

"

The Inspector General paused. The light of its suit dimmed slightly.

"Clarify," the mask said.

"If the Adventurer's Guild is considered a business entity operating within my jurisdiction," Gerald said, "then their entry requires a commercial access permit. They did not have one. Therefore, their presence is invalid. Therefore, any legal actions taken by invalid entities are void."

He looked at the form. The three hundred fields.

"And," Gerald added, "if you force me to wait six hundred years, you are effectively killing me. A Core cannot be suspended for longer than its natural lifespan without violating the Core Protection Act of 4th Edition, Article 9."

The Inspector General remained still. The silence stretched, heavy and thick.

Then, the form changed. The fields disappeared. A single line of text appeared.

**[RECLASSIFICATION PENDING.]** **[COUNTER-MOTION FILED: GERALD HUTCHINS VS. ADVENTURER'S GUILD LOCAL CHAPTER.]** **[HEARING SCHEDULED: IMMEDIATE.]**

Gerald let out a breath he didn't know he was holding. His hands shook. Not from fear. From the adrenaline of a man who had just bet his life on a technicality.

"You're buying me time," Gerald said.

"I am following the code," the Inspector General said. "The code is ambiguous. In ambiguity, the Core is granted a temporary stay of demolition. But you must survive the hearing."

"What kind of hearing?"

"The Adventurer's Guild has hired a Legal Core," the mask said.

"It is a construct of pure litigation. It does not fight. It argues.

It will dismantle your dungeon's existence through logical paradoxes and precedent abuse.

"

Gerald felt sick. He knew how to build things. He knew how to inspect things. He did not know how to argue law with a machine that existed only to find loopholes in other machines.

"Where is it?" Gerald asked.

The floor beneath him cracked.

But this time, it wasn't a stomp, which was a slide. A massive, mechanical ramp extended from the darkness behind the Inspector General.

At the end of the ramp stood a figure.

It looked like a suit of armor, but made entirely of stacked legal briefs. Its head was a gavel. Its hands were scales.

**[LEGAL CORE: THE ADJUDICATOR.]** **[HP: ∞ (THEORY)]** **[ATTACK: PRECEDENT CASCADE.]**

The Adjudicator stepped forward. The sound was like a library collapsing.

"Mr. Hutchins," the Adjudicator boomed.

"Your dungeon lacks a Certificate of Occupancy.

Your ventilation system is inadequate. And your spawn list contains unlicensed vermin. I move for immediate dissolution.

"

Gerald looked at the Adjudicator. He looked at the Inspector General. He looked at his wrench.

He had no monsters. He had no traps. He had a rat, who was currently hiding behind a pillar, and a citation form that was now blank.

He needed a weapon. A legal weapon.

He thought of the library above.

The Municipal Library. He had inspected it.

He knew its secrets, and he knew the hidden passages, the faulty wiring, the unauthorized renovations that the city council had covered up for decades.

If he could prove the Library was non-compliant, he could prove the Dungeon Core had been registered *by error*. If the registration was an error, the whole premise of the hearing changed. He wouldn't be defending a dungeon. He would be prosecuting the city.

He looked at the Adjudicator.

"Objection," Gerald said.

The Adjudicator raised a hand.

"Overruled. Proceed."

"You cannot dissolve me," Gerald said. "Because I am not a dungeon. I am a municipal asset."

The Adjudicator paused. The scales on its shoulders dipped.

"Explain," the gavel-head said.

Gerald reached into his pocket. He didn't pull out a citation.

He pulled out a small, blue folder, which was his original inspection report from the day he became a Core.

The report that had triggered the system.

"This report," Gerald said, holding it up. "It notes a 'Structural Anomaly' in the sub-basement. It states that the anomaly is 'inconsistent with City Code.' It does not state that the anomaly is a Dungeon Core. It states it is a *defect*."

The Adjudicator leaned forward.

"If I am a defect," Gerald said, "then I am not a Core. I am a liability. And the City of Ashford is liable for my existence.

If you destroy me, you are destroying city property without a demolition permit. And you," he pointed at the Inspector General, "you are facilitating an illegal demolition."

The Inspector General's mask tilted. The light flared.

**[LOGIC ERROR DETECTED.]** **[RECALCULATING...]**

The Adjudicator stuttered. Its pages rustled violently.

"The Core Protection Act applies to Cores," the Adjudicator said. "If Hutchins is a defect, the Act does not apply."

"Correct," Gerald said.

He stepped closer. The Adjudicator did not retreat. It was bound by logic.

It had to follow the logic, even if it led to its own defeat.

"So, what happens to a defect?"

"It is repaired," the Adjudicator said.

"Repaired," Gerald echoed. "Not destroyed. Repaired."

He looked at the Inspector General.

"You cannot dissolve me. You must *fix* me. You must bring my Compliance Rating to 100. Or you are breaking the law that created you."

The arena went silent. The dust settled.

The Inspector General looked at the Adjudicator. The two machines of light and law stared at each other.

**[SYSTEM UPDATE: OBJECTIVE CHANGED.]** **[NEW GOAL: ACHIEVE 100% COMPLIANCE RATING.]** **[TIME LIMIT: 72 HOURS.]** **[FAILURE CONSEQUENCE: TOTAL EXISTENCE ERASURE.]**

Gerald slumped. His knees hit the floor. The wrench clattered away.

Seventy-two hours. To fix a dungeon that was fundamentally broken by its own origin story. To make a Core compliant in three days.

And above him, the library was filling with adventurers, and the Guild was not done. They were angry. They were bringing lawyers.

They were bringing siege engines.

Gerald looked at the rat, who peeked out from behind the pillar. The rat squeaked, a tiny, defiant sound.

Gerald picked up his clipboard. His hands were steady now.

"System," he whispered. "Open the Blueprint Editor."

A map of his dungeon appeared. It was a mess of red warnings. Every wall, every floor, every corner was a violation.

But for the first time, Gerald didn't see chaos. He saw a to-do list.

He had three days. And he had never missed a deadline in his life.

The first siege engine fired before Gerald could finish.
