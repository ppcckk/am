Chapter 121: Subsection 4.2.14

# Chapter 121

The concrete weaved.

It was not a spell. It was not a miracle. It was a reorganization of atomic lattice structures based on the municipal standard for blast-resistant partitioning, subsection 4.2.14, paragraph C. Gerald felt the familiar nausea of improvisation, the physical rejection of doing something without a stamped permit, but he swallowed it down with the bile of necessity.

The door bowed inward.

Metal shrieked against the reinforced frame. The library above was no longer silent. The vibration of running feet became a rhythmic thud, synchronized with the pounding of Gerald's own core.

"Stop hitting it!" a voice shouted from the other side.

"Pull the lever!"

"I can't pull the lever, it's fused!"

Gerald didn't open his eyes. He kept his consciousness focused on the hinge points.

He had spent thirty-one years inspecting hinges, and he knew where they failed. He knew where they held. He poured his remaining mana into the pinholes, turning the steel into a monolithic block of compliance.

The pounding stopped.

Silence returned, heavier than before.

Then, a new sound. A mechanical whine. High-pitched. Annoying.

Gerald opened one eye.

The door wasn't being forced. It was being cut. A laser torch, glowing blue-white, sliced through the center of the metal panel. Sparks showered onto the dungeon floor, sizzling against the concrete.

Gerald checked his internal clock. Nine minutes and twelve seconds remaining.

He had no weapons. He had no traps. He had a water leak in the northwest corner and three mold spores that were currently trying to colonize the laser light.

The door fell inward with a crash.

Smoke billowed into the dungeon. Through the haze walked three figures.

They were not adventurers, and they wore gray coveralls with the Ashford City Public Works logo on the chest. One carried a clipboard.

Another held a tablet.

The third, a woman with short, severe hair and eyes that looked like they had been ironed, held a pair of bolt cutters.

Gerald sat up. His form flickered. He was barely a presence in the room, a faint shimmer of dust and intent.

The woman stepped forward. She didn't look at Gerald. She looked at the laser cut.

"Sloppy," she said. "You're leaving slag on the floor. That's a slip-and-fall hazard."

The man with the tablet looked up.

"Director Vance? The Core is right there."

Vance looked at Gerald. Her expression was one of profound disappointment.

"Mr.

Hutchins.

You have a pending appeal. You have a citation pending.

And you have just sealed a public egress route with non-standard materials.

"

Gerald tried to speak. His voice came out as a dry rasp.

"The seal... was emergency structural reinforcement."

"Unauthorized," Vance said. She tapped her tablet.

"I'm citing you for illegal fortification.

That's a Class 4 violation. It voids your self-defense provisions under Code 0.0.

"

Gerald's core shuddered. The debuff hit him instantly. His form stabilized, but his mana flow slowed. He felt heavy.

"Code 0.0 applies when the structure is under immediate threat of destruction," Gerald said.

He forced the words out, relying on the muscle memory of a thousand rejected permit applications.

"The door was compromised.

The integrity of the foundation was at risk.

I am protecting the asset.

"

"The asset is the library," Vance said. "You are the asset's tenant. You do not have the right to barricade the tenant entrance."

"I am not a tenant," Gerald said. "I am a registered Dungeon Core. Under the System Integration Act of 2024, I have rights of occupancy."

Vance smiled. It was not a nice smile.

"And under that same Act, you have a duty to comply with municipal inspection protocols.

We are here to conduct an audit.

You have locked us out. That is obstruction of a public official.

That is a Class 1 violation.

"

The man with the clipboard stepped forward.

"Director, his Compliance Rating is dropping. Look at the feed. He's at 32."

"Let it drop," Vance said.

Gerald looked at the cut door. The laser mark was fresh. He looked at the three mold spores. They were dead, scorched by the laser heat.

"Why are you here?" Gerald asked.

"The audit," Vance said. "The Inspector General is watching. If we don't verify your status as 'non-compliant and non-cooperative' within the hour, the System assumes you are rogue. Rogue cores are deleted."

Gerald felt the cold truth of it. He had assumed the audit was a review of his paperwork. He had prepared the appeal for the appeal window. He had not prepared for a physical raid on his sanctum.

"I am cooperative," Gerald said.

"You sealed the door."

"I was protecting the structural integrity against unauthorized entry."

Vance stepped closer. She pulled a tape measure from her belt. Not a weapon. A tape measure.

"Show me the wall thickness," she said.

Gerald blinked.

"What?"

"Show me the wall thickness. If you claim Code 0.0 defense, you must prove the wall was failing. If the wall was compliant, your 'defense' was an assault on the city infrastructure."

Gerald's mind raced. He knew the wall thickness.

He had measured it himself last week.

Twenty-four inches of reinforced concrete. Standard for a sub-basement.

But he had woven the door. He had added ten inches of emergency steel.

If he showed the wall, it would show the excess. The excess would prove he had tampered with the structure. Tampering voided the defense claim.

If he refused to show the wall, Vance could bring in ground-penetrating radar. That would take time. She didn't have time. She had six minutes left on the audit window.

Gerald looked at the water leak, and the water was running onto the concrete floor. The laser slag was hot. Where the water met the slag, steam hissed.

"Director Vance," Gerald said. "Before I submit to measurement, I must file a counter-citation."

Vance stopped. Her hand hovered over her tablet.

"You cannot file a citation on a city official."

"Under System Rule 14, Subsection D, a Dungeon Core may cite any entity for creating a hazardous condition that interferes with the Core's ability to maintain structural compliance."

Vance raised an eyebrow.

"Interferes with...?"

"Your laser torch," Gerald said. "It has melted the door frame. It has created a superheated zone. It is currently interacting with the active water leak in my dungeon."

Vance looked down. The steam was rising thicker now.

"The steam," Gerald continued.

"It is obscuring visibility. It is creating a slip hazard on the floor.

It is raising the ambient temperature.

I am currently experiencing thermal distress. This distress is preventing me from accurately reporting my Compliance Rating.

"

Vance looked at the steam. She looked at her team. The man with the tablet was coughing.

"Turn it off," she said.

"The torch is off," Gerald said. "The heat is in the metal.

I cannot turn off the heat in the metal, which is your responsibility.

You introduced the heat source.

"

Vance's jaw tightened.

"What do you want?"

"I want you to sign a temporary waiver of liability for thermal damage," Gerald said. "And I want you to allow me thirty seconds to cool the area using the water supply, which I will do, so that I can safely show you the wall thickness."

"That is not a waiver," Vance said. "That is an extortion attempt."

"It is a safety protocol," Gerald said. "If you force me to stand in superheated steam to prove my wall thickness, and I collapse, the audit fails. The System marks the audit as 'incomplete.' The Inspector General will step in.

Do you want the Inspector General here? He will check your laser protocols.

He will check your coveralls.

He will check your fire extinguisher.

"

Vance looked at her belt. Her fire extinguisher was empty.

Gerald saw it.

"My extinguisher is charged," Gerald said. "But I am not allowed to use it because you have not issued a permit for fire suppression equipment in this zone."

Vance stared at him. The steam was rising. Her eyes were watering.

"Fine," she said. "Cool it down."

Gerald gestured. The water leak, guided by his will, redirected. It sprayed directly onto the hot metal of the door frame.

Steam exploded outward. Vance and her team stepped back, coughing.

"Thirty seconds," Gerald said.

He watched the clock. Twenty-eight. Twenty-nine.

The steam cleared. The metal cooled.

"Show me the wall," Vance said. Her voice was ragged.

Gerald moved toward the door. His legs felt weak. The Class 4 violation debuff was grinding at his mana. He reached out and touched the concrete next to the door.

He projected his internal map, and he did not show the wall.

He showed the door.

He showed the ten inches of emergency steel.

But he overlaid it with a ghost image. A schematic. A blueprint.

"This is not a wall," Gerald said.

"This is a door assembly. Under Code 8.1, a door assembly includes the frame, the jamb, and the hardware.

The emergency reinforcement was applied to the hardware, not the structure.

Therefore, the structural integrity of the wall remains untouched.

"

Vance looked at the ghost image. She looked at the tablet. She looked at the real door.

"You're lying," she said.

"I am interpreting," Gerald said. "Code 8.1 is ambiguous regarding emergency reinforcements applied to movable elements. In the absence of clear precedent, the interpretation favors the entity that preserved the life-safety function of the egress route."

"The egress route was blocked," Vance said.

"The egress route was compromised," Gerald corrected. "By your laser.

I restored it.

By sealing it, I prevented the uncontrolled release of smoke into the library above.

I protected the public.

"

Vance opened her mouth. Then she closed it.

The tablet beeped.

The audit timer hit zero.

A golden light filled the room. The System's audit algorithm had completed its scan.

**AUDIT COMPLETE.**

**STATUS: PENDING REVIEW.**

**NOTES: DISPUTED INTERPRETATION. CORE SUSPENDED. HEARING SCHEDULED FOR 48 HOURS.**

The light faded.

Gerald slumped. The suspension meant he could not spawn. He could not regenerate. He was locked out of his own dungeon's functions until the hearing.

Vance looked at him. Her expression was unreadable.

"You bought yourself time," she said. "But you lost your mobility. You are a sitting duck."

"I am a structure," Gerald said. "Structures do not move."

Vance turned to her team.

"Get out. Tag the door. Mark it for demolition."

"Director," the man with the clipboard said. "The demolition requires a warrant."

"Get the warrant," Vance said. "I don't care. I want that door gone."

She walked toward the exit. The library above was quiet again. The dragon had long since flown off. The threat had shifted.

Gerald watched them go. The door was still standing, but it was tagged with red spray paint. *DEMO 1.*

He looked at his status.

**Compliance Rating: 15/100.**

**Active Violations: 14.**

**Pending Actions: 1 (Hearing).**

**Locked Abilities: Spawn, Regenerate, Mana Generation.**

**Unlocked Ability: Passive Observation.**

The water continued to drip. *Drip. Drip. Drip.*

Gerald closed his eyes. He had won the battle. He had kept the audit from deleting him.

But he had lost the war. He was frozen.

He was waiting.

And he had not found Unit 4.

Without Unit 4, he had no proof of authorization. Without proof, the hearing would be a formality. He would be found negligent. His dungeon would be condemned.

He needed to find Unit 4. But he was locked in his basement.

The door was sealed, and the exit was tagged for demolition.

There was no one in the dungeon to send.

Except...

Gerald opened one eye.

In the corner of the room, behind a pile of rubble from the laser cut, something stirred, which was not a spawn. It was not a monster.

It was a rat.

The original rat. The confused rat. The one he had tried to file a Form 11-B on in Chapter 1.

It was still there. Hiding.

Gerald stared at it.

The rat stared back, which was small. It was dirty. It was alive.

Gerald's mana regeneration was locked. He could not spawn a scout. He could not build a sensor.

But he could observe, and he focused his Passive Observation on the rat.

The rat scurried toward the hole in the wall where the water leak originated. It climbed into the pipes.

It disappeared.

Gerald watched the rat's perspective.

The pipes led up. They went through the floor of the library. They went into the walls of the library.

The rat was not in the dungeon anymore, which was in the building.

Gerald felt a surge of hope, cold and sharp.

He couldn't send anyone. But someone had already left.

He couldn't control the rat. But he could see what it saw.

The rat climbed through the dark, narrow pipes. It reached a junction. A maintenance hatch.

The hatch was open.

Beyond the hatch was a room. A small, cluttered room filled with old filing cabinets and stacks of blueprints.

The rat entered.

It sniffed the air.

In the center of the room, on a dusty table, sat a metal box.

The box was labeled: **UNIT 4 - ARCHIVAL.**

Gerald's core pulsed, and the evidence was there. In the library. Above him.

But he couldn't get to it. He was locked in the basement. The door was tagged for demolition.

And then, the door shook.

Not from a laser.

From a hammer.

A rhythmic, loud pounding.

*Bang. Bang. Bang.*

The red paint on the door vibrated.

"Open up!" a voice shouted. "Demolition crew! We have the warrant!"

Gerald looked at the rat's view. The rat was in the archival room.

He looked at his own view. The door was breaking.

He had to make a choice.

Stay and fight? He had no mana. He would be destroyed.

Stay and wait? The hearing was in two days. He would be destroyed before then.

Run? He had nowhere to go.

Unless...

Gerald looked at the rat, and the rat was sitting on the metal box.

If he could transfer his consciousness into the rat...

He had never done that. It was not in the manual. It was not in the code.

But Code 0.0 allowed for the preservation of the Core's essence in the event of catastrophic structural failure.

The door cracked. Dust fell.

Gerald made his decision, and he didn't try to move the rat. He tried to become it.

He pushed his awareness down, down through the concrete, down through the pipes, down into the small, dirty body of the confused rat.

The connection was weak. It was like trying to hold onto a thread in a hurricane.

But he held on.

The rat twitched.

It looked up.

Its eyes were not confused anymore.

Gerald looked at the metal box, and he opened the latch.

Gerald stepped out of the box.
