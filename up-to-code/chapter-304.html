Chapter 304: Falling into the Rat

# Chapter 304

The rain lashed against the windshield of the 2004 Honda Civic, a rhythmic drumming that did nothing to soothe the headache splitting Gerald's skull. He kept his eyes on the wet asphalt, white-knuckling the steering wheel.

His knuckles were grey.

Not pale. Grey. Like ash.

He checked the rearview mirror. No tail lights. No black SUVs. Just the blur of Ashford's downtown district sliding past in the drizzle.

Gerald pulled into the municipal parking lot. The engine died with a shudder. He sat for ten seconds, listening to the ticking of the cooling metal. Then he stepped out.

He didn't walk. He strode. The posture was rigid, spine straight, shoulders squared. It was the posture of a man who had been told he was trespassing but refused to acknowledge the authority of the person telling him so.

The steps to City Hall were slick with mud.

Gerald climbed them two at a time. His suit jacket was soaked through.

The tie was askew.

The badge on his lapel caught the grey light, tarnished and dull, and the main doors were open.

Inside, the lobby smelled of wet wool and old coffee. The security guard, Miller, looked up from his desk. He squinted.

"Mr. Hutchins?" Miller asked. "You're not on the schedule."

"I am on the docket," Gerald said. He didn't stop walking.

"File 44-B.

Emergency injunction.

Mayor's office.

"

"The Mayor is in closed session. Councilman Vance is holding the door."

"Vance can go to hell. I'm here for the signature."

Gerald reached the front desk. The clerk, a young woman named Sarah, recoiled. She stared at his hands.

They were trembling, but not from fear. They were vibrating at a frequency that made the plastic pens on her desk rattle.

"Mr. Hutchins, you can't just—"

"Form 19-A.

Request for Immediate Administrative Review," Gerald said. He slapped a packet onto the counter. It was thick.

Dense.

Paperclipped with precision.

Sarah picked it up. She opened the first page. Her eyes widened.

"This lists every structural violation in the library sub-basement," she whispered. "Since 1994."

"Since 1989," Gerald corrected.

"I filed the first one. You buried it. Then you buried the next one.

Then the next."

"I didn't—"

"Section 4, Paragraph C. Municipal Liability for Negligent Infrastructure Maintenance. If I am detained for trespassing, the City becomes the primary defendant for any structural collapse resulting from the void. Do you want to be the defendant, Sarah?"

Sarah's mouth opened.

Closed, and she looked at the security camera. She looked at Gerald.

She pressed the intercom button.

"Mayor's office? There's… someone here. He says it's about the library."

Silence. Then, a crackle.

"Send him up. Second floor. Room 2B."

Gerald walked past the desk. He did not take the elevator.

The stairs were faster, and the elevator doors were out of code.

The gap between the car and the landing was three-eighths of an inch. Too wide.

Room 2B was small.

Beige walls. A single desk.

Mayor Evelyn Thorne sat behind it, and she looked tired. She looked older than she had at the gala three weeks ago.

Across from her sat Vance. Councilman Vance was not tired. He was smug. He held a glass of scotch, though it was only ten in the morning.

"Gerald," Thorne said. "You're making a scene."

"I'm filing a motion," Gerald said. He placed the packet on the desk.

It landed with a heavy thud.

"You're trying to revoke my occupancy permit by claiming the dungeon is unlicensed municipal property.

Therefore, I am trespassing.

"

"The System registered it as your Core, Gerald," Vance said. He swirled his drink. "But the land is ours.

The foundation is ours. The air is ours.

We're freezing the asset.

You're frozen with it.

"

"The System doesn't care about your deed," Gerald said.

He pulled a pen from his pocket. It was a cheap ballpoint, blue ink.

"The System cares about the Compliance Code.

And the City of Ashford has violated it for decades.

"

Vance laughed.

"What?"

Gerald tapped the packet.

"Page one.

Fire exits.

The library's rear egress was blocked by a stack of encyclopedias in 2005.

I cited it. You ignored it.

In 2010, the electrical panel was outdated. I cited it. You ignored it.

In 2015, the load-bearing wall in the sub-basement was compromised by water damage. I cited it.

You ignored it.

"

"So?" Thorne asked.

"So," Gerald said. "The void in the sub-basement? It's not a dungeon.

Not anymore.

It's a hazard. A Class 4 Structural Hazard.

And under the Municipal Code, the City is liable for hazards it has knowingly ignored for more than five years.

"

Vance set his glass down.

"That's not how the System works."

"It's how *your* System works," Gerald said. "Because you're the ones who let the rot in. You're the ones who kept the building up while the bones rotted out.

I didn't create the void. I inspected it.

And now I'm reporting it.

"

Gerald pulled a second document from his jacket. It was shorter. Single page.

"This is a Notice of Condemnation," Gerald said.

"Signed by me. As the Inspector of Record.

The library sub-basement is structurally unsound. The City must vacate the premises. Immediately.

Or face a fine of fifty thousand dollars per day, payable to the System, which I will then use to pay off the Lien on my soul.

"

Thorne stared at him.

"You're citing the City?"

"I'm citing the City for creating the conditions that allowed the Dungeon Core to form.

If you condemn the building, the Core loses its anchor. The Lien dissolves. I'm free.

You're fined.

But you keep the city. Just not the library.

"

Vance stood up, and he walked around the desk.

"You think you can bully the City with paperwork? We have lawyers.

We have judges.

"

"We have a System," Gerald said. "And the System just issued a citation."

The air in the room changed.

The temperature dropped.

Frost crept across the windowpane. A notification box appeared in the air, hovering above the desk.

It was not visible to Vance or Thorne. It was visible to Gerald.

**NOTIFICATION:** **CITATION ISSUED.** **TARGET: CITY OF ASHFORD.** **VIOLATION: FAILURE TO MAINTAIN STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY (CODE 12.4).** **PENALTY: ASSET FREEZE. CITY FUNDS FROZEN FOR 48 HOURS.** **APPEAL WINDOW: 1 HOUR.**

Thorne gasped. She grabbed her chest.

"My card… it's not working."

"Vance?" Gerald asked.

Vance fumbled for his phone. He swiped the screen.

Nothing, and he tried again. Nothing.

"What did you do?" Vance hissed.

"I used the code," Gerald said. "You spent thirty years ignoring it. I spent thirty years learning it. There's a difference."

"You're a building inspector," Vance said. "You're a nobody."

"I'm the one who knows where the bodies are buried," Gerald said. "Literally. In the foundation."

Gerald turned to Thorne.

"Sign the condemnation order. Now.

If you don't, the System will auction the library to pay the fine.

And the void will expand. It will swallow the block. You know what lives in the void now?

My rats? No.

The Receiver's scouts.

The things that came through the cracks.

"

Thorne looked at Vance. Vance looked at the phone. He looked at Gerald.

"If I sign," Thorne said, "the library is gone. We lose the cultural center."

"You lose the library," Gerald said. "You keep the city.

You keep the money. You keep your jobs. And I keep my soul.

"

Vance stepped forward.

"And if we don't?"

Gerald pulled a third document from his pocket. It was a subpoena. Not for a court. For the Inspector General.

"Then I file this," Gerald said. "And the next audit won't be from a Receiver.

It'll be from the IG.

And the IG doesn't care about your politics. He cares about code. And the City is out of compliance by a factor of ten thousand.

"

Vance paled, and he knew the IG. Everyone did.

The IG was the one who deleted dungeons that couldn't pass inspection.

He was the one who erased cores that violated the spirit of the game.

"You're bluffing," Vance said.

"Am I?" Gerald held up the subpoena. The paper glowed with a faint blue light. The System had accepted the filing. It was real.

Thorne looked at the glowing paper. She looked at her husband, who was sitting in his office across the hall, playing golf on his phone. She looked at the window, where the frost was spreading, cracking the glass.

"Give me the pen," Thorne said.

Gerald handed it to her. Blue ink. Cheap.

She signed. Her hand shook. The signature was jagged.

**NOTIFICATION:** **CONDEMNATION ORDER ACCEPTED.** **LIEN RELEASED.** **CORE STATUS: UNANCHORED.** **WARNING: CORE WITHOUT ANCHOR IS UNSTABLE. RETURN TO DUNGEON IMMEDIATELY OR RISK DISSOLUTION.**

Gerald felt a tug in his chest. It wasn't pain.

It was gravity.

A sudden, violent pull toward the earth, and he was losing his grip on the physical world.

"You're free," Gerald said to Thorne. "Go manage your crisis."

He turned and walked out.

He didn't run this time. He walked. But he walked faster than a man should be able to walk. His feet didn't seem to touch the floor.

The lobby was empty. Miller was gone. Sarah was gone. The lights flickered.

Gerald pushed through the front doors, and the rain had stopped. The sky was black.

He stood on the sidewalk. He looked at his hands. The grey was spreading up his wrists.

His fingers were translucent. He could see the veins, but they weren't red.

They were dark.

Like ink, and he had twelve minutes before his body dissolved.

He had to get back to the sub-basement. But the library was condemned.

The doors were locked.

The police were already arriving, blue and red lights flashing around the block.

He couldn't go in the front door, and he couldn't go in the back.

He needed a breach. A violation. A way in that the City hadn't accounted for.

His phone buzzed.

**NOTIFICATION:** **NEW CASE FILED.** **PLAINTIFF: SYSTEM INSPECTOR GENERAL.** **DEFENDANT: GERALD HUTCHINS.** **CHARGES: UNLICENSED CORE IN UNSTABLE STATE.** **PENALTY: TERMINATION.**

Gerald stared at the screen. The IG had moved early.

He looked at the library. The windows were boarded up. The doors were chained.

But he remembered something. Chapter 1. The reflection in the mirror. The anomaly.

The library had a skylight. In the reading room.

It had been cracked since 2003.

Gerald had cited it, and he had ordered it fixed.

It had never been fixed.

The skylight was still open. The glass was shattered. The hole was three feet wide.

It was a code violation, which was a security breach.

It was the only way in.

Gerald looked at the police cruisers. They were parking. They were getting out.

He looked at the fire escape. Rusty. Rusted bolts. Unstable.

He looked at the alley. Deep. Dark.

He started running. Not toward the door. Toward the wall.

He leaped, and he caught the fire escape ladder. It groaned. The metal bit into his translucent hands.

He climbed. One rung. Two. Three.

A police officer shouted.

"Hey! Stop!"

Gerald didn't look down. He climbed. The void hummed in his ears.

It was louder now. It was waiting for him.

He reached the roof, and he crawled across the tar and gravel. He dragged his body, which was becoming heavy, becoming less real.

He reached the skylight.

He looked down.

The reading room was empty. The chairs were overturned. The books were scattered.

And in the center of the room, the hole in the floor was gone. The void was gone.

But there was something else.

A single rat, which sat in the center of the room. It looked up at Gerald.

It had his eyes.

Gerald hesitated. His hand hovered over the hole.

The rat squeaked, which was a sound. A real sound. Not a System notification.

And then, from the shadows behind the rat, a voice spoke.

"Welcome back, Mr. Hutchins."

It wasn't the Receiver, which was deeper. Older. Tired.

The Inspector General.

Gerald looked into the dark.

"I'm not ready," he whispered.

"You're never ready," the voice said. "That's why you're an inspector."

The floor beneath Gerald's feet gave way, and he fell.

Not into the void.

Into the rat.
