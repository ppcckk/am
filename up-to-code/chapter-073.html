Chapter 73: Structural Weak Points

# Chapter 73

Gerald ran through the sub-basement with his clipboard pressed against his chest, counting steps. Thirty-one years of counting steps. He knew the footprint intimately. Fourteen paces from the north wall to the utility closet, twenty-two from the east corridor to the stairwell, and the precise spot where floor joists ran perpendicular to the library's main load-bearing wall above.

He stopped at the east corridor. Pulled out his pen. Started writing.

If the System's structural standard required a minimum load-bearing capacity of 150 pounds per square foot and the library's current foundation provided only 90, he needed to add 60 pounds per square foot of distributed support. The library was 4,000 square feet. That was 240,000 pounds of additional load capacity.

He couldn't build a foundation in forty-seven minutes. He couldn't pour concrete. He couldn't install steel columns.

But he could spawn things.

Gerald had never spawned structural elements before. His spawn list was biological: mold, water, a rat.

The System had given him a Core Class of "Code Compliance" and a spawn budget he'd spent entirely on compliance infrastructure.

Handrails. Signage. A fire extinguisher that had cost him three days of mana regeneration.

He opened his spawn interface. Scrolled past the biological category. Past the environmental category. Found a tab he'd never opened because he'd never had a reason to.

STRUCTURAL, and the list was short.

Load-bearing pillar. Non-load-bearing partition.

Beam.

Truss. Footing. Each one had a mana cost listed in the corner.

The pillar was expensive. The footing was more expensive.

The truss was somewhere in between.

Gerald ran the numbers in his head.

Twenty-two pillars at the mana cost each. He checked his current mana pool.

Not enough. He needed a different configuration.

He ran again. Fourteen pillars, eight trusses, six footings.

That fit.

Barely. It left him with almost nothing in reserve, which meant if anything went wrong during the assessment, he'd have no mana to fix it.

He started spawning.

The first pillar materialized in the sub-basement floor with a sound like a wet cough, which was gray. It looked like concrete but wasn't.

The System called it "Structural Element, Type A, Provisional." Gerald didn't care what it was.

He cared where it went.

He spawned the second one. Third.

Fourth.

Each one placed at a point where he knew the library above had a load concentration.

Column bases. Wall intersections.

The point directly beneath the circulation desk where the librarian's heavy oak counter transferred its weight through the floor and into the foundation below.

He'd inspected that library eleven times. He knew every load path. He knew where the building carried its weight and where it didn't and where it should but didn't because of a 1987 renovation that had removed a support wall without adding a beam. That renovation had been cited three times by the city and never fixed.

Gerald placed a truss exactly where that missing beam should have been.

Twenty-six minutes left, and he moved to the east corridor; the library's east wall was a curtain wall, glass and steel, not load-bearing.

But the System's standard might not care about the distinction.

The System's standard might treat all walls as potential load paths. Gerald didn't know. He couldn't know.

He'd never read the System's structural code because it didn't exist as a document he could pull from a shelf.

He spawned a footing anyway. Redundant. Safe. It cost him mana he couldn't spare, but he'd waste it than leave a gap.

Eighteen minutes.

His clipboard buzzed.

ASSESSOR ETA: 00:14:33.

Gerald was on his sixth footing. He had eight more to place.

His mana was running low.

He felt it like a headache approaching: pressure behind his eyes, slight nausea, and the System had never told him what happened when a Core ran out of mana. He assumed it was bad.

He assumed it was bad, and he spawned the seventh footing.

Eighth.

Ninth. His hands were shaking.

Not from fear. From the specific physical revulsion of doing something without having a form to file first. He was improvising.

He was making structural decisions without a stamped drawing, without a permit, without the three-part review process that would have governed any actual construction project in the City of Ashford.

He hated it. His stomach was turning.

Twelve minutes, and he reached the north wall.

The last three footings. He spawned the tenth. Eleventh.

His mana bar read 4%.

The twelfth footing cost 5%.

Gerald stared at the number, and he looked at the spot where the footing needed to go. It was directly beneath the library's north stairwell. The stairwell was load-bearing.

If that spot was unsupported, the System's assessor would find it. The assessment would fail.

The library would be erased.

He spawned the twelfth footing.

His mana hit zero, and the world went quiet.

Not silent. Quiet.

Like the hum of fluorescent lights cutting out. Like the sound of a building settling after the HVAC system shuts off for the night. Gerald felt it in his chest.

A hollowness. A sense that something essential had been removed from him and he didn't know what it was because he'd never had it before and so he didn't know what he was missing.

His clipboard flickered.

MANA DEPLETED.

CORE STABILIZATION: MINIMUM. FUNCTIONAL CAPACITY: 12%. SPAWN: DISABLED.

CITATION: DISABLED.

Gerald straightened up. His knees ached.

He looked at the twelve footings, eight trusses, and fourteen pillars he'd placed in the sub-basement.

They looked like what they were: gray provisional structures that had appeared out of nothing; didn't look like a foundation. They didn't look like a code-compliant support system.

They looked like a man had thrown them at the floor in a panic.

Gerald picked up his clipboard. He checked the clock.

Nine minutes, and he sat down on a footing. It was cold. He put his clipboard on his knee and waited.

The air changed. Not temperature.

Not pressure.

Something else.

A shift in the quality of attention in the room, like the feeling of being watched by someone who hadn't been there a moment ago.

The Inspector General did not walk in. The Inspector General did not manifest, and the Inspector General stood there, like a building inspector who knows you cut corners.

Gerald looked up, and the Inspector General was a clipboard. That was the only way to describe it, and a floating clipboard, slightly larger than Gerald's, with a pen that wrote on its own.

The pen moved across the surface in tight, precise strokes. It was reading.

Gerald stood. His knees popped.

"Inspector General, Sub-Unit 7," Gerald said.

His voice came out steady. Thirty-one years of steady. "Gerald Hutchins.

Core Class Code Compliance.

Dungeon footprint 4,000 square feet, sub-basement, Ashford Municipal Library. I'm ready for assessment."

The pen stopped.

The clipboard tilted. Not physically. Conceptually.

A building inspector tilts his head when a contractor speaks technically accurately but structurally suspiciously.

"Your provisional compliance submission cited municipal standard," the Inspector General said.

The voice came from everywhere and nowhere.

It sounded like the hum of a fluorescent light. "Your remediation uses System standard structural elements.

This is inconsistent."

"Yes," Gerald said. "It is."

"You understand that inconsistency is itself a violation."

"I do."

"Then why?"

Gerald set his clipboard down. He pulled out his pen.

He opened it to a page he'd been writing on while he waited, which was a diagram. Hand-drawn. Of the library's load path.

Every beam, every column, every joist.

He drew it from memory after eleven inspections; the load path was fixed in his mind, like a fire marshal's egress routes.

He held it up.

"Because the System's structural standard for a mixed-use occupancy is 150 pounds per square foot," Gerald said.

"The municipal standard for the same occupancy is 120. I used 120 in my provisional submission because that's the only standard I know. But I built to 150 in my remediation because that's the standard you'll assess against.

The inconsistency is procedural. The structure is compliant."

The clipboard was silent. The pen did not move.

Gerald kept holding the diagram up. His arm started to ache.

"Your structural elements are provisionally compliant," the Inspector General said.

"They meet the threshold.

They do not meet the documentation standard. Provisional elements require stamped engineering review within thirty days of placement.

You have no stamp."

"I'm not an engineer."

"You are not."

"Then the elements will be compliant for thirty days and non-compliant on day thirty-one. That's the System's own code. Section 4.7.1. Provisional elements have a grace period."

The pen moved again. Writing. Gerald couldn't read what it was writing. The clipboard was angled away from him.

"Your reasoning is sound," the Inspector General said. "Your structural elements are provisionally compliant. The library passes."

Gerald exhaled. His shoulders dropped. The tension he'd been holding in his jaw for forty-seven minutes released and his teeth ached.

"However."

The word hung in the air like a citation notice pinned to a door.

"Your structural elements create a load path that extends beyond your dungeon footprint."

Gerald looked at the diagram in his hand. Looked at the placement of the twelfth footing. The one he'd spawned with his last 4% of mana. The one beneath the north stairwell.

He looked at the Inspector General's clipboard.

"The north stairwell load path terminates at the boundary of your dungeon footprint," the Inspector General said. "But the load itself continues north. Into the adjacent footprint. Which belongs to Core 4471."

Gerald knew Core 4471. Everyone in the district knew Core 4471.

It was the dungeon beneath the old Ashford Fire Station.

A combat-oriented Core. Aggressive. Its spawns were hostile by default.

It had tried to breach Gerald's eastern wall three times.

"You have created a structural interconnection between your dungeon and Core 4471's dungeon," the Inspector General said. "This constitutes an unpermitted structural alteration to a third-party structure."

The pen moved. Gerald heard the sound of it writing. The scratch of nib on surface.

"I am now issuing a Citation."

Gerald's clipboard buzzed. He looked down.

CITATION ISSUED. ISSUED BY: INSPECTOR GENERAL, SUB-UNIT 7.

TARGET: CORE 2891 (HUTCHINS, GERALD).

VIOLATION: UNPERMITTED STRUCTURAL INTERCONNECTION. SEVERITY: CLASS 2. DEBUFF: ALL STRUCTURAL SPAWNS DISABLED FOR 72 HOURS.

APPEAL WINDOW: CLOSED.

Appeal window closed.

Gerald read it again.

Appeal window closed. The Inspector General had issued a citation with no appeal.

That had never happened. Every citation Gerald had ever issued had an appeal window. Every citation he'd ever received had an appeal window.

He looked up at the clipboard.

"Why is there no appeal window?"

"Because the violation is self-evident," the Inspector General said. "You created it in front of me. You have no defense. The citation is final."

Gerald looked at his dungeon. At the twelve footings, eight trusses, fourteen pillars.

All of them now disabled.

All of them about to become non-structural. The library above would lose its support in seventy-two hours.

"Seventy-two hours," Gerald said.

"Yes."

"The library will be erased."

"No. The library will be non-compliant. If the library is non-compliant when the next assessment occurs, it will be erased. The next assessment is scheduled for forty-one days."

Forty-one days. Gerald had forty-one days to find a way to fix a structural interconnection he couldn't remove, using spawns he couldn't use, against a Core that had already tried to breach his walls three times.

The Inspector General's clipboard turned. The pen stopped writing.

"Citation served. Assessment closed. Have a productive day, Core 2891."

The presence vanished. The hum returned. Gerald was alone in his sub-basement with disabled structures and a library above him that was already dying.

He sat down on the cold floor. He opened his clipboard. He started writing.

He had forty-one days. He had no mana. He had no spawns. He had a citation from the Inspector General himself that he could not appeal.

And he had one advantage.

Core 4471 had a dungeon beneath the old fire station. Fire stations have a specific structural code. Gerald knew that code. He'd inspected that building four times before the System arrived.

He knew exactly where its weak points were.
