Chapter 112: Audit of the Sub-Basement

# Chapter 112 The silence in the sub-basement was heavier than the plaster that had just stopped falling.

Gerald Hutchins lay on his side on the cold concrete, his cheek pressed against a puddle of rainwater that had leaked through the newly stabilized ceiling. He stared at the notification window hovering in his peripheral vision. The text was crisp, sans-serif, and merciless.

**NOTIFICATION: TAX BILL ISSUED.** **AMOUNT: 10,000 COPPER PIECES.** **DUE: IMMEDIATELY.**

He blinked. The number did not change. He had twelve copper pieces in his inventory slot. He had three mold spores, a confused rat named Timothy, and a water leak that was currently dripping onto his head.

Kael stood over him, sword sheathed, looking at the glowing blue lines that now webbed across the walls of the dungeon like veins in a marble slab. The knight looked impressed by the structural integrity. He was not impressed by the debt.

"Gerald," Kael said. His voice was tight. "We need to leave. The city council is already here."

"I can’t leave," Gerald said. He tried to sit up.

His ribs ached.

"I’m a public utility now. Utilities don’t close. Utilities stay put and wait for customers to pay their bills or for the auditors to shut them down."

"You have twelve coppers," Kael said. "The bill is ten thousand. That’s not a deficit. That’s an execution order."

Gerald ignored him, and he pulled himself up, using a support column that now hummed with municipal authority. He went to the center and stopped.

The air smelled of ozone and wet drywall.

The System had reinforced the structure, yes. It had thickened the walls, reinforced the beams, and installed emergency lighting. But it had also attached a leash.

The leash was financial.

Gerald opened his Core Interface. He navigated past the combat logs, past the spawn management tab, and went straight to the Administrative Ledger.

Negotiation was not an option. Paying in installments was not an option. The choices were **PAY** or **SEIZE**.

If he pressed **SEIZE**, the System would take control of the dungeon. It would strip out the spawn points, dissolve the rat, and convert the space into a generic storage unit for the city’s waste management department. Gerald would be evicted from his own body.

He needed ten thousand coppers. He needed them in the next four minutes, before the Auditor’s timer for asset seizure expired.

Kael was watching the entrance. Outside, the sounds of Ashford were changing. The shouting was getting closer. The city guard was pushing back the crowd of gawking adventurers who had gathered to watch the miracle of spontaneous architectural reinforcement.

"Gerald, the guards are breaking through the library doors upstairs," Kael said. "They’re coming down. They’re not here to help. They’re here to assess liability."

Gerald typed furiously into the command line, and he was looking for a loophole.

He had spent thirty-one years finding them, and had spent the last hundred chapters building this place by the book.

He knew the code better than he knew his own heartbeat.

He scrolled through the Municipal Dungeon Tax Code.

**Section 44, Subsection B: Exemptions for Educational Infrastructure.** Gerald’s dungeon was under the library. Technically, it was part of the educational complex.

But the System had registered him as a *Public Utility*, not an educational facility.

A utility provides a service. An educational facility provides instruction.

He needed to prove he was providing a service that was essential, immediate, and currently uncompensated.

"The water leak," Gerald muttered.

"What?" Kael asked.

"The leak.

It’s been dripping since Chapter 12. I’ve been trying to fix it, but the plumbing infrastructure is pre-System.

It’s not up to code.

It’s a hazard."

Gerald opened the **Service Provision Log**. This was a new tab, unlocked only by the Public Utility status. Usually, it was empty because Gerald’s dungeon offered no services. He had no shop, no tavern, no repair bench.

But he had a leak, and he highlighted the **Water Ingress Violation**; the System had cited him for it three years ago. He had paid a fine, but he hadn’t fixed it because fixing it required breaking the floor, which required a permit, which he couldn’t afford.

Now, as a Utility, he had the right to charge for maintenance.

Gerald typed: **ISSUE SERVICE INVOICE FOR EMERGENCY STRUCTURAL STABILIZATION AND WATER RETENTION.**

The System processed it.

**INVOICE ISSUED TO: ASHFORD MUNICIPAL LIBRARY.** **AMOUNT: 0 COPPER PIECES.** **STATUS: PENDING APPROVAL.**

That wasn’t enough. Zero was not ten thousand.

He needed the city to pay him for something they already owned. Or rather, for something they were liable for.

He looked at the blue lines in the walls. The **Emergency Bracing**.

The System had deployed it to save the structure.

But who paid for the deployment?

The Core. The Core had no money.

Gerald had an idea. It was a bad idea. It was a bureaucratic nightmare. It was exactly what he was best at.

He opened the **Inter-Core Communication Protocol**; could not send a message to the city council directly; the System filtered those. But he could send a message to other Cores. Specifically, the ones that were currently in Ashford.

He had a list. **The Goblin Warrens** (Hostile). **The Crypt of Whispers** (Neutral/Evil). **The Fire Elemental Pit** (Hostile).

He selected **The Crypt of Whispers**. It was a mid-tier dungeon, rich in loot, located three miles east. Its Core was a vampire type.

It had money. Lots of it.

Gerald typed: **CITATION REQUEST: ILLEGAL SUBTERRANEAN CONDUCT.**

He waited. He had to provide evidence.

He opened the map of Ashford. He drew a line from his dungeon to the Crypt. Then he drew a line from the Crypt to the sewers.

The sewers were municipal. They were public infrastructure.

If the Crypt was accessing the sewers without a permit, it was violating the **Municipal Egress Ordinance**. Gerald had cited the sewers for blockage last year. The blockage was caused by root systems. Where did those roots come from?

He remembered a report. The Crypt’s root-based spawns had been clogging the storm drains in the East District, and he had filed a **Notice of Obstruction**. He had never followed up because it wasn't his dungeon.

Now, it was, and he attached the **Notice of Obstruction** to the Citation Request.

He added a second attachment: **Photographic Evidence of Root Intrusion in Sewer Sector 7.** He had taken these photos five years ago. They were crisp. High resolution.

Unassailable, and he typed: **TARGET: CORE 442-C (CRYPT OF WHISPERS). VIOLATION: UNAUTHORIZED UTILIZATION OF MUNICIPAL INFRASTRUCTURE FOR SPAWN LOGISTICS. PENALTY: ASSESSED AT MARKET RATE FOR SEWER ACCESS.**

He set the citation to **Immediate Execution**.

A window popped up.

**WARNING: CITATION REQUIRES PHYSICAL SERVICE. TARGET MUST BE PRESENT OR REPRESENTED BY A MANAGER ENTITY WITHIN 500 METERS.**

The Crypt was three miles away. The manager wasn't here.

Gerald gritted his teeth. He needed a proxy. A manager within 500 meters.

He looked at Kael. "Are you registered as my liaison?"

Kael looked confused. "No. I'm an adventurer."

"Gerald," Kael said, his voice rising. "The guards are on the stairs. They’re kicking the door in."

Gerald didn't look up. He was typing faster. "Kael, I need you to do something illegal. But technically legal."

"What?"

"I need you to be a Manager Entity."

"I don't have a Core license."

"You don't need one. You need a **Power of Attorney**.

The System recognizes legal representation for administrative disputes.

If I grant you Power of Attorney, you become my agent. If my agent is within range of the target, I can serve the citation."

Kael stared at him. "You want me to sign a legal document while a vampire dungeon is being fined by a building inspector?"

"Yes."

"Why?"

"Because I need the money. And I can't pay it. So I'm going to fine someone else who can."

Kael looked at the stairs. Dust was falling from the ceiling. The kick came through. Wood splintered.

"Fine," Kael said. "Do it."

Gerald opened the **Administrative Authority** tab. He selected **Grant Power of Attorney: General Administrative Powers**. He typed Kael’s name.

**SYSTEM QUERY: DO YOU CONSENT TO GRANTING FULL ADMINISTRATIVE CONTROL TO KAEL THORN?**

"YES!" Gerald shouted.

**CONTROL GRANTED.**

Gerald immediately targeted the **Citation Request** again. He set the delivery method to **Proxy Service**.

**SYSTEM ERROR: PROXY SERVICE REQUIRES MANAGER TO BE WITHIN 500 METERS OF TARGET LOCATION.**

Kael was in Gerald's dungeon. The Crypt was three miles away.

Gerald froze. He had made a mistake. He had assumed proximity to the *Core*, not the *Target*.

He looked at the map. He looked at the sewer lines. The sewer line from the Crypt ran directly under the library. It ran directly under *this room*.

The roots were there. He could feel them. The System highlighted them in red on his minimap. Thin, pulsing red lines running through the walls.

**VIOLATION CONFIRMED: ACTIVE INFRACTION IN PROGRESS.**

The citation didn't need to travel to the Crypt. The infraction was happening *here*. Inside Gerald's dungeon.

Gerald typed: **CITE CORE 442-C FOR ACTIVE TRESPASS VIA SUBTERRANEAN CONDUIT.**

He hit **EXECUTE**.

The air in the room vibrated. A low hum started, like a transformer blowing up.

The red lines in the walls glowed brighter.

**CITATION SERVED.** **PENALTY ASSESSED: 12,000 COPPER PIECES (SEWER ACCESS FEE + PENALTY).** **COLLECTION METHOD: DIRECT DRAINAGE.**

The System didn't send the money to Gerald. It didn't send a bill to the vampire.

It took the money from the source.

The roots in the walls shuddered. They were connected to the Crypt’s mana reserves. The System treated mana as a liquid asset. It siphoned it through the conduit, converted it to copper, and deposited it into Gerald’s account.

Gerald’s balance spiked.

**12 COPPERS.** **1,000 COPPERS.** **5,000 COPPERS.**

The roots started to wither. The red lines turned gray. Outside, somewhere in the East District, a vampire Core was likely screaming in confusion and pain as its primary funding source was severed.

Gerald’s balance hit **10,000 COPPERS**.

The tax notification turned green.

**PAYMENT COMPLETE.** **CORE STATUS: STABLE.**

Gerald slumped against the wall. He was alive. He had paid his tax. He had bankrupted a neighboring dungeon.

Kael looked at him with a mix of horror and awe. "You just sucked the life out of a vampire."

"I cited him for a code violation," Gerald said. He was breathing hard. "He didn't have a permit for the roots."

The door at the top of the stairs burst open.

Guard Captain Miller stormed down, flanked by four city guards. They wore the grey tabards of the Ashford Guard. Miller held a clipboard. He did not look happy.

"Who is in charge here?" Miller barked.

Gerald stood up. He straightened his tie. It was stained with plaster, but the knot was still perfect.

"I am Gerald Hutchins," he said. "Core ID 884-G. Public Utility. Municipal Dungeon."

Miller looked at the blue lines in the walls. He looked at the withered roots. He looked at Kael, who was checking his sword.

"We have a complaint," Miller said. "From the Vampire Lord. He claims you’re stealing his mana."

"He’s stealing my infrastructure," Gerald corrected. "I issued a citation.

You can review it on the Public Record, Tab 4, Section 9.

Or you can come down here and inspect the conduit yourself.

But if you inspect it, you’re entering a Public Utility Zone. You need to sign in.

And you need to wear a hard hat."

Miller blinked. "A hard hat?"

"OSHA Regulation 14-C," Gerald said. "Head protection required in all active construction or maintenance zones. This zone is currently in 'Emergency Remediation' status. If you step onto that floor without a hard hat, I’m liable, and I will sue the city for negligence."

Miller looked at his guards. None of them had hard hats.

Miller looked back at Gerald. He looked at the blue lines. He looked at the clipboard.

"You’re a public utility," Miller said slowly. "So you’re part of the city."

"Correct."

"Then you’re under our jurisdiction."

"Correct."

Miller smiled. It was a thin, cruel smile.

"Then I’m shutting you down."

Gerald felt a cold spike in his stomach. "Why?"

"Because," Miller said, pointing his pen at Gerald. "A public utility cannot have a negative Compliance Rating with the City Council.

And your Compliance Rating with the City Council is currently zero.

You didn't file your **Form 12-A: Notice of Existence** with the Zoning Board. You incorporated via the System, not via the City."

Gerald’s blood went cold, and he had incorporated via the System Auditor. He had bypassed the City Council.

"Form 12-A," Gerald whispered.

"You don't have it," Miller said. "Therefore, you are an illegal structure. I am ordering immediate demolition."

The guards raised their hammers.

Gerald looked at Kael. Kael looked helpless.

Gerald looked at the System window.

**WARNING: UNAUTHORIZED STRUCTURE DETECTED. ZONING VIOLATION.** **ACTION: DEMOLITION IMMINENT.**

Gerald had one move left, and he had the power of attorney. He had the citation authority.

But he couldn't cite the city for *not* having a form.

The city was the regulator.

Unless...

Unless the city was also a dungeon.

Ashford Municipal Library. It was a structure.

It had walls, which had corridors.

It had an exit.

Did it have a Core?

No. Libraries weren't dungeons.

But the System didn't care about what it *was*. It cared about what it *did*.

Gerald looked at the ceiling. The library was above him. If the library fell, the data node was lost. The Auditor had warned him about data nodes.

The Auditor was still watching. He had to be.

Gerald typed a command. Not a citation. A request.

**QUERY: DOES THE ASHFORD MUNICIPAL LIBRARY HAVE A CORE?**

The System paused.

**AFFIRMATIVE. CORE ID 001-L. STATUS: DORMANT. OWNER: CITY OF ASHFORD.**

Gerald stared at the text, and the library had a Core. It was dormant. It was owned by the city.

If the city owned a Core, it was subject to Dungeon Laws.

If the city owned a Core, it could be cited.

Gerald typed: **CITATION REQUEST: STRUCTURAL NEGLIGENCE.** **TARGET: CORE 001-L (ASHFORD LIBRARY).** **VIOLATION: FAILURE TO MAINTAIN LOAD-BEARING SUPPORT FOR PUBLIC UTILITY DUNGEON 884-G.** **PENALTY: ASSESSED AT COST OF REINFORCEMENT MINUS TAXES PAID.**

He hit **EXECUTE**.

The System window flashed red.

**ERROR: TARGET CORE IS DORMANT. CITATION REQUIRES ACTIVE INTERACTION.**

Gerald frowned. He couldn't cite a sleeping Core.

But he could wake it up, and he opened the **Public Access** tab; he set the library dungeon's status to **Mandatory Inspection**.

**SYSTEM QUERY: DO YOU CONSENT TO FORCE-AWAKEN CORE 001-L FOR COMPLIANCE AUDIT?**

Gerald hesitated. Waking the library Core meant waking the library itself. It meant the books might start moving.

It meant the shelves might become obstacles. It meant the city might lose control of its own public building.

But it was the only way to save his own life.

Miller raised his hammer. "Hit it," he ordered.

The guard swung. The hammer hit the load-bearing column.

The column cracked.

Gerald screamed. "YES!"

**CORE 001-L AWAKE.**

The ground shook. Not with an earthquake. With a heartbeat.

From the floor above, a low, groaning sound rose up. It sounded like thousands of pages turning at once.

The ceiling burst open. Not with debris. With ink.

Black, viscous ink poured through the cracks, covering the guards, covering Kael, covering Gerald.

And from the hole in the ceiling, a face looked down. It was made of paper. Its eyes were book covers. Its mouth was a tear.

**CORE 001-L: WHO DARES DISTURB THE STACKS?**

Miller fell back, dropping his clipboard. The guards ran.

Gerald looked up at the paper face.

"I'm your tenant," Gerald said. "And you’re in violation of section 44, subsection B. You’re blocking my egress."

The paper face tilted.

**VIOLATION CONFIRMED.**

The ink surged toward Gerald. But this time, it didn't attack. It flowed around him, forming a protective barrier.

**CITATION ACCEPTED. PENALTY WAIVED. COMPLIANCE RATING: 100/100.**

The shaking stopped. The guards were gone. Miller was gone.

Gerald stood in the center of the room, surrounded by ink, alive, and up to code.

But then the System window flashed again.

**NOTIFICATION: NEW RIVAL DETECTED.** **NAME: THE ARCHIVIST.** **STATUS: HOSTILE.** **REASON: YOU WOKE THE LIBRARY. THE ARCHIVIST WANTS IT BACK.**

Gerald looked at the ink. It was moving. It was forming hands. It was trying to grab him.

He wasn't safe. He had just invited a monster into his home.

And the monster was looking for something.

Gerald looked down at his feet. The ink was pooling there. It was trying to read him.

**QUERY: WHAT IS YOUR DEBT?**

Gerald realized with horror that the Library Core didn't just want to protect him; it wanted to know if he owed it anything.

And if he owed it... It would collect.

Gerald opened his inventory. He had twelve copper pieces.

He didn't have enough to pay, and he had to give it something else.

He looked at Kael. Kael was holding his sword, but he wasn't attacking. He was staring at the ink with fascination.

"Kael," Gerald said. "What did you bring from the Crypt?"

"Nothing," Kael said. "Just loot."

Gerald looked at the inventory tab. Kael had granted him access to his loot bag as part of the Power of Attorney.

The bag held a item. A **Vampire’s Signet Ring**.

It was worth five hundred coppers.

But the Library didn't want money. It wanted knowledge. It wanted *stories*.

The ring was a story. It was a piece of the Crypt’s history.

Gerald typed: **OFFER TO PAY DEBT: ITEM [VAMPIRE'S SIGNET RING]. VALUE: 500 COPPERS. NARRATIVE VALUE: HIGH.**

The ink paused.

**ACCEPTED.**

The ink receded. The paper face nodded.

**CORE 001-L: COMPLIANCE MAINTAINED. DO NOT DISTURB THE STACKS AGAIN.**

The face dissolved. The ink drained away into the floor.

Gerald was alone with Kael.

"You gave away the signet ring," Kael said. "That was the only evidence we had of the Crypt’s root network."

"I bought my life," Gerald said.

"For a ring?"

"For a *duty*," Gerald said.

He straightened his tie. "I’m a public utility now. I have obligations.

And apparently, I have neighbors."

Kael looked at the ceiling. "The city is coming back.

With more men.

And the Archivist is coming back. And the Vampire is coming back."

Gerald nodded. He opened his interface. He looked at his Compliance Rating.

**100/100.**

He had never been so high. He had never been so vulnerable.

He had saved his dungeon by turning it into a public service. And in doing so, he had made himself the most important, regulated, and target-rich structure in Ashford.

He looked at the spawn tab. He had three mold spores and a rat.

He needed more, and he needed security.

He needed a lawyer.

And he needed to file a motion to stay the execution of the city’s demolition order.

Gerald typed: **FILING MOTION TO STAY. GROUNDS: EXCESSIVE FORCE. VIOLATION OF PUBLIC UTILITY PROTECTIONS.**

The System accepted it.

**MOTION PENDING.** **HEARING SET FOR: 48 HOURS.**

Gerald had two days before the city tried to tear him down again. Two days to hire a lawyer. Two days to fortify his defenses. Two days to find out what the Archivist wanted.

He looked at Kael.

"We need to hire someone," Gerald said.

"Who?"

"Someone who knows how to argue with a library."

Kael laughed. It was a nervous, jagged sound.

"That’s not a profession, Gerald."

"It’s a code violation waiting to happen," Gerald said. "And I’m going to exploit it."

He looked at the door. The guards were gone. But the shadows in the corner of the room were darker than before.

Something was watching him. Not the System. Not the Library.

Something else, and the ink had left a residue on the floor. It formed a symbol. A quill pen.

The Archivist.

Gerald stepped on it.

"Next chapter," he muttered. "Hope you have your hard hats ready."
