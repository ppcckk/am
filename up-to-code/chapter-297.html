Chapter 297: Ceiling Collapse

# Chapter 297

The clipboard was the shield.

Gerald held it up, pen poised above Form 115-A. The bathroom door swung open with a wet, sucking sound. Water pooled on the tile, rising an inch higher than before.

Three figures stepped through. They were not Auditors in the traditional sense.

They were Enforcers.

Tall, faceless constructs of polished obsidian and blue light, their limbs elongated by too many joints. They moved with the jerky precision of stop-motion animation.

Sandra shifted her weight. Her sword hand hovered over the hilt.

"Stay back," Gerald said.

His voice was flat. Deadpan.

The same tone he used for contractors who ignored the zoning variance on a garden shed.

"You are entering a residential zone without a permit."

The lead Enforcer stopped. Its head tilted. A low hum vibrated in the air, shaking the toothbrush holder.

Sandra glanced at Gerald. Her knuckles were white.

"They don't have forms," she whispered.

"Everyone has forms," Gerald said. "They just haven't found the right one yet."

The Enforcer raised a hand. A beam of concentrated mana gathered at its fingertips.

Gerald didn't flinch. He wrote.

*Section 4.2: Unauthorized Use of High-Voltage Arcane Energy within Residential Confines. Immediate Hazard. Citation Issued.*

The System flashed in his peripheral vision.

**CITATION SERVED.** **Target: Class III Enforcer.** **Violation: Electrical Code Violation (Class A).** **Penalty: Temporary Mana Conductivity Nullification (Duration: 30 seconds).**

The beam fizzled. It popped like a wet match.

The Enforcer stared at its hand. Then it looked at Gerald.

It tried to recharge.

The mana didn't flow.

The air around the creature felt heavy, static, and useless.

"Electrical grounding," Gerald said, tapping the clipboard. "You're not grounded. You're a live wire in a puddle. That's a fire hazard."

Sandra smiled, sharp and quick.

"Nice."

"Don't celebrate," Gerald said, already moving. "We have ten seconds before it recalibrates its resistance. Move to the vent shaft. It's the only egress route that meets clearance requirements."

He turned and marched toward the back wall of the bathroom. The tiles there were loose. He'd noticed them three chapters ago. Now was the time to use them.

The second Enforcer lunged. Sandra caught its blade with her shield, sparks flying. The impact drove her backward.

"Hurry!" she grunted.

Gerald pried up a tile. Beneath it, the dungeon's plumbing was exposed. Copper pipes, sweating condensation.

He didn't attack the Enforcer. He attacked the infrastructure.

With a wrench he'd kept in his belt pouch—a tool that had never left his person since the transformation—Gerald tightened a valve on the cold water main. Then he loosened the hot.

The pipes groaned. Pressure spiked.

"Stand back," Gerald warned.

The third Enforcer charged. Sandra parried, but the force sent her skidding across the wet floor.

Gerald kicked the valve. It snapped.

Water erupted. Not a trickle. A jet.

High-pressure, scalding steam mixed with cold spray blasted the center of the room. The floor became a lake.

The electrical sparks from the Enforcers' failed attacks hit the water and arced wildly, bouncing off the tiles, missing Gerald by inches.

The Enforcers froze. Their sensors were overwhelmed by the sudden thermal and hydraulic shift.

"Ventilation failure," Gerald muttered, checking his internal status. "Compliance Rating dropping. 44. 43."

He didn't care about the rating. He cared about the exit.

"Into the shaft!" he shouted.

Sandra grabbed his arm. They dove into the narrow gap behind the toilet, pulling the loose tile back as best they could. The water roared outside, drowning the Enforcers' movements.

Inside the shaft, it was dark. Smelled of rust and old blood.

Gerald clicked on his headlamp. The beam cut through the dust.

"We need to reach the library's main HVAC unit," Gerald said. "If we can reroute the airflow from this dungeon into the municipal system, I can file a cross-ventilation complaint against the Auditor's client. It's a jurisdictional nightmare. They'll be tied up in paperwork for weeks."

Sandra wiped blood from her lip.

"You're going to sue them?"

"I'm going to cite them," Gerald corrected. "Suing implies money. Citation implies shame. Auditors hate shame."

They crawled. The shaft was tight. Gerald's clipboard scraped against the metal walls.

Ahead, the tunnel branched. Left was a drop into the basement boiler room. Right led toward the library's reading room.

"Right," Sandra said. "The library has more cover."

"No," Gerald said. "Left."

Sandra paused.

"Why? The boiler room is a hazard zone.

No egress signage.

It's a Class 4 Fire Hazard.

"

"Exactly," Gerald said. "If we go there, we are technically trespassing in a hazardous area. But if we *fix* it while we're there..."

Sandra frowned.

"You want to inspect the boiler?"

"I want to inspect the Auditor's client," Gerald said.

"The portal was anchored in the library sub-basement. The energy signature came from the boiler room. That's where the leak started.

That's where they stole my water.

"

Sandra looked at the dark left turn. Then at the right.

"If we go left, and it's a trap..."

"It's not a trap," Gerald said. "It's a violation. And I don't ignore violations."

He crawled left. Sandra followed, her eyes darting to the shadows.

The shaft ended at a grate. Gerald pulled it open. He dropped down into the boiler room.

It was massive. Industrial. Pipes the size of tree trunks snaked across the ceiling. The air was hot, thick with the smell of burning coal and ozone.

In the center of the room, a circle of runes glowed faintly. They were fading, but still active.

And standing around the circle were three people. Not monsters. Humans. Or something close enough.

They wore grey robes. Their faces were obscured by masks. They were packing up equipment—crystals, wires, buckets of water.

One of them turned. He saw Gerald drop from the ceiling.

"Core," the man said. His voice was smooth. Professional.

Gerald stood up. He brushed rust off his suit. He adjusted his tie.

"You're in violation of Zoning Ordinance 12-C," Gerald said. "Unauthorized excavation of municipal water mains. And you're operating a portal without a variance permit."

The man laughed. It was a dry, dusty sound.

"You're the library rat."

"I am Gerald Hutchins," Gerald said. "Inspector. Grade 4."

The man pulled a staff from his robe. It crackled with green energy.

"I am Magister Vane. And you are dead."

He fired.

Gerald didn't dodge. He raised his clipboard.

The green energy hit the clipboard. It didn't bounce off. It absorbed into the paper.

**SYSTEM ALERT: MAGICAL PROJECTILE DETECTED.** **SOURCE: UNLICENSED SPELLCASTER.** **ACTION: CITATION FOR UNREGISTERED MAGIC USE.**

The energy dissipated. The clipboard glowed for a second, then cooled.

Sandra landed behind Gerald, sword drawn.

"What did you do?"

"I registered the spell," Gerald said. "The System requires all magical effects to be registered with a licensed practitioner. Vane isn't registered. He's operating illegally."

Vane's mask twitched. He looked at his staff. It flickered.

"That's not how magic works," Vane said.

"It's how the Code works," Gerald said. He pulled a new form from his pocket. *Form 99-Z: Illegal Use of Restricted Arcane Energy.*

He wrote. Fast.

Vane stepped back.

"Stop him! Kill him!"

The other two mages raised their hands. Bolts of fire and ice shot toward Gerald.

Sandra deflected the ice. The fire ball missed Gerald, hitting a pipe. The pipe burst. Steam hissed into the room.

Gerald finished the form. He stamped it with the heavy rubber seal he kept in his breast pocket.

**CITATION SERVED.** **Target: Magister Vane.** **Violations: Three counts of Unregistered Spellcasting. One count of Property Destruction.** **Penalty: Mana Lockout (Duration: 1 Hour).**

Vane's staff went dark. The green glow vanished. The other mages' hands stopped glowing.

Silence.

Only the hiss of the broken steam pipe filled the room.

Vane stared at his staff. He shook it. He shouted a word. Nothing happened.

"You... you disabled magic?" Sandra asked, lowering her sword.

"I disabled *unlicensed* magic," Gerald said. "The System recognizes my authority as a Dungeon Core with High Compliance Status. I can suppress illegal magical activities within my jurisdiction."

He looked at Vane.

"You're out of time. And you're out of power."

Vane ripped off his mask. He was old. His face was gaunt, his eyes wide with panic.

"You can't do this," Vane spat. "The Inspector General—"

"Is currently on a coffee break," Gerald said. "And I have a hearing scheduled. In five minutes. For the theft of water."

Gerald pointed to the puddle on the floor. It was spreading. The water from the broken pipe was mixing with the stolen reserves.

"This water belongs to the City of Ashford," Gerald said. "You stole it. You broke the pipe to get it. You didn't fix it."

He pulled out his wrench.

"I am going to fix it. And you are going to pay for the repair costs. Plus penalties."

Vane looked at his two mages. They were trying to cast, but nothing worked. They were helpless.

"This is insane," Vane whispered. "You're a bureaucrat."

"I'm an inspector," Gerald corrected.

He walked toward the broken pipe. He stepped over Vane's feet.

Sandra watched, confused.

"Gerald, what are you doing? We need to run. The Auditors will be back."

"No," Gerald said. He tightened the pipe joint. He applied sealant. He checked the pressure gauge.

"We don't run," Gerald said. "We wait for the Hearing."

The air shimmered. A portal opened in the center of the room. Not the one Vane had used. A new one.

A figure stepped out. Tall. Robed in white. Carrying a gavel made of stone.

The Inspector General.

Behind him, four Auditors followed.

Vane smiled.

"See?

You're done. You cited an Auditor.

You're revoked.

"

Gerald didn't look up. He was checking the leak.

"It's sealed. Pressure holding at 40 PSI."

He stood up. He turned to face the Inspector General.

The IG looked at Vane. Then at Gerald.

"Core Hutchins," the IG said. His voice boomed, shaking the dust from the ceiling. "You have been accused of obstructing an audit."

"I have filed a counter-complaint," Gerald said. He held up a stack of papers. Twenty pages thick.

"For theft," Sandra said, smiling. "And for unauthorized portal use."

The IG looked at the papers. He didn't take them.

"The Hearing has already begun," the IG said.

Vane laughed. "See?

You're guilty.

You cited an Auditor without proper service. The seal was inactive.

"

Gerald stepped forward.

"The seal was not inactive," Gerald said. "It was misaligned. I filed Form 44-B to realign it. The filing timestamp is three minutes before your portal opened."

He pulled out a small, crumpled piece of paper. A receipt.

"Here is the proof of submission. And here," Gerald pointed to the wall, "is the physical evidence of the realignment. The bolt I tightened."

He pointed to a bolt on the wall, right next to the portal rune. It was shiny. New.

Vane's face went pale.

"I didn't see it."

"Because you weren't looking," Gerald said. "You were stealing."

The IG picked up the receipt. He examined it.

The room went silent.

The IG looked at Gerald.

"Core Hutchins," the IG said. "Your evidence is... admissible."

Vane screamed.

"No! It's a trick!

The seal was old!

He forged the timestamp!

"

"Challenge accepted," the IG said. "We proceed to the Structural Logic Hearing."

The IG raised his stone gavel.

"Core Hutchins. You must prove that the portal was anchored to your water supply. Core Vane. You must prove that the water was freely available."

Gerald looked at Sandra.

Sandra nodded. She had her sword ready. But Gerald didn't need the sword.

He needed the pipe.

"I have the water pressure logs," Gerald said. "And I have the municipal water bill for this district. It shows a spike in usage at the exact time Vane opened his portal."

He pulled out another form.

This one was different. It was red.

**FORM 77-OMEGA: CRITICAL INFRASTRUCTURE FAILURE.**

"If the portal was anchored to the water supply," Gerald said, "then the portal is part of the plumbing system. And plumbing systems are under my jurisdiction."

Vane stepped back.

"Don't you dare."

Gerald stamped the form.

**CITATION SERVED.** **Target: Magister Vane.** **Violation: Unauthorized Integration of Arcane Technology into Municipal Plumbing.** **Penalty: Complete Dissolution of Portal Anchor.**

The runes on the floor shattered. The portal collapsed inward. The energy backlash hit Vane's mages. They flew backward, crashing into the wall.

Vane stood alone. His staff was dead. His magic was gone.

Gerald walked toward him.

"You stole my water," Gerald said. "You broke my pipes. You entered my dungeon without a permit."

He stopped inches from Vane.

"I am citing you for every single one."

Vane looked at the IG. The IG said nothing. The IG watched.

Gerald pulled out his pen.

But then, the IG spoke.

"Wait."

The IG pointed at Gerald's clipboard.

"Core Hutchins," the IG said. "You cited a Class 10 Mage for plumbing violations. This is... irregular."

"It's in the code," Gerald said.

"The code," the IG said, "does not explicitly cover arcane plumbing."

Gerald froze. His pen hovered over the paper.

If the code didn't cover it... the citation was invalid.

Vane smiled. A cruel, thin smile.

"Gotcha."

Gerald looked at the clipboard. Then at Vane. Then at the broken pipe.

He had one move left, and he tore off the top sheet of his clipboard. He flipped it over.

On the back, in small, cramped handwriting, was a list.

*Addendum 12.4: Definition of Water.*

Gerald held up the page.

"According to Section 1 of the Municipal Code," Gerald said, "Water is defined as any substance capable of extinguishing fire. Arcane energy, when mixed with matter, creates heat.

It is, therefore, a fuel source.

And fuels are regulated under Fire Code Section 3.

Not Plumbing Code.

"

Vane's smile vanished.

"If I cite you under Plumbing," Gerald said, "you win. But I am not citing you under Plumbing."

He wrote a new line.

"I am citing you under Fire Code. Violation: Improper Storage of Combustible Materials within a Confined Space."

He stamped it.

**CITATION SERVED.** **Target: Magister Vane.** **Violation: Fire Hazard.** **Penalty: Immediate Incineration of Non-Compliant Assets.**

The runes on the floor didn't just shatter. They caught fire. Blue fire.

It ate the stone. It ate the portal.

It reached Vane's feet.

Vane screamed. He jumped back, but the fire was faster. It consumed his robes. It consumed his staff.

The IG watched. He did not intervene.

The fire died.

Vane was gone. Not dead. Just... erased.

His assets removed from the dungeon.

Sandra lowered her sword. Her mouth was open.

Gerald picked up his clipboard. He checked the box.

*Case Closed.*

He looked at the IG.

"Your audit is over," Gerald said. "You have no evidence of misconduct. You have a client who was illegally disposing of hazardous materials in my jurisdiction."

The IG stared at him. The stone gavel lowered.

"Core Hutchins," the IG said. "You are... problematic."

"I am compliant," Gerald said.

The IG nodded slowly. He turned to the Auditors.

"Withdraw."

The Auditors faded. The IG vanished.

Silence returned to the boiler room.

Sandra walked over to Gerald. She looked at the empty space where Vane had been.

"You burned him," she said.

"I cited him," Gerald said. "The System did the burning."

Sandra looked at him. looked at him.

"You just beat a Class 10 Mage with a fire code violation."

"He was non-compliant," Gerald said.

He turned and walked toward the exit.

"Come on," Gerald said. "We have to repair the pipe before the next audit. And I think I hear a dripping sound in the library."

Sandra followed.

But as they walked, the air grew cold. Not the cold of a draft. The cold of the grave.

A new message flashed in Gerald's vision.

**SYSTEM ALERT: COMPLIANCE RATING INCREASING.** **RATING: 52/100.** **UNLOCKED: ABILITY TO INSPECT REALITY.**

Gerald stopped, and he looked at the wall.

The wall was solid brick.

But when he looked closer, with his new ability, he saw text. Written in the mortar.

*Warning: Structural Integrity Compromised.*

Not the building.

The world.

"Gerald?" Sandra asked.

Gerald looked at the text. Then he looked at the ceiling.

More text.

And more, and the whole dungeon was covered in warnings.

"We're not the only ones being audited," Gerald whispered.

Sandra looked around.

"What do you mean?"

Gerald pointed to a crack in the ceiling. It wasn't a crack. It was a seam. A tear in the fabric of the dungeon.

And through the tear, he saw something.

Not the library.

Not the city.

A face. Huge. Looking in.

Sandra screamed.

Gerald raised his clipboard.

But the face smiled.

And then, the ceiling collapsed.
