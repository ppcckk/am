Chapter 259: Code of Death

# Chapter 259

The asphalt was hot enough to blister the soles of his shoes, but Gerald Hutchins did not notice. He was too busy looking at the curb.

Specifically, he was looking at the thirty-four-inch setback between the burning pharmacy and the adjacent sidewalk. It was non-compliant. The fire had jumped the gap because the vegetation along the curb was overgrown by six inches, violating Municipal Ordinance 14-C, Subsection B, regarding firebreak maintenance.

Sarah stood beside him, her hands trembling. She held a clipboard that had materialized out of thin air, a System artifact. Her eyes were wide, fixed on the roaring flames that consumed the storefront of *Higgins Pharmacy*. Sirens wailed in the distance, but the fire trucks were blocked by a chaotic tangle of abandoned cars and panicked pedestrians.

"Move," Gerald said. His voice was flat. "You are obstructing the egress path."

Sarah stared at him.

"My dad is in there."

"Then he should not have blocked the egress path with a display of seasonal gnomes," Gerald replied. He pulled a pen from his pocket.

It was a standard-issue municipal ballpoint, black ink, click-top.

He clicked it. The sound was absurdly loud against the backdrop of screaming glass and cracking timber.

He looked at the fire.

It wasn't just heat. It was a Core's power leaking into the physical plane.

The flames moved with intent, and they curled around the support pillars of the building, targeting the load-bearing walls with surgical precision. This was not an accident. This was a demolition order.

Gerald squinted. A translucent blue window hovered in his peripheral vision.

**NOTICE OF VIOLATION: UNLICENSED STRUCTURAL COMPROMISE** **Target:** Ashford Commercial Zone, Block 4 **Severity:** Critical **Hazard:** Intentional destabilization of load-bearing elements via magical combustion. **Action Required:** Immediate cessation of unauthorized thermal events.

"Unauthorized," Gerald muttered. He walked toward the fire line.

Sarah grabbed his arm.

"You can't go in there. It's on fire."

"It is a regulated zone," Gerald corrected. "If the fire is unregulated, it is illegal. I am going to issue a stop-work order."

He stepped past the yellow police tape. The System recognized his authority.

The tape snapped like wet paper as he walked through it.

The fire flinched.

The flames nearest to him receded, pulling back like a tide retreating from a stone.

Gerald pulled a small notebook from his vest pocket. He flipped to a blank page.

"Sarah," he said. "Dictate."

"Dictate what?" she cried, coughing from the smoke.

"Date. Time. Location. Violator identification."

"The violator is the building! It's on fire!"

"The building is the site. The violator is the entity controlling the fire. Look for the source."

Sarah looked. Through the haze, she saw it. A figure standing on the roof of the pharmacy.

A man in a suit, identical to the one in the dungeon, but larger. The static on his face had solidified into a mask of white porcelain.

He held a torch that burned with black flame.

"That's him," Sarah whispered.

"Identify him," Gerald commanded.

"I... I don't know his name."

"Then describe him. Height. Attire.

Distinctive features.

Go."

Sarah's breath hitched, and she looked at the figure.

"Ten feet tall. White suit.

Black tie. Porcelain mask.

Holding a torch that... it doesn't burn right.

It eats the light.

"

Gerald wrote it down. The words glowed faintly on the paper.

**SUBJECT IDENTIFIED: HIGH-PRIORITY CORE OPERATIVE [NAME UNKNOWN]** **ATTIRE: NON-COMPLIANT UNIFORM (LACK OF HIGH-VISIBILITY GEAR)** **WEAPON: UNLICENSED THERMAL EMISSION DEVICE**

"Good," Gerald said. He tore the page out. He held it up.

The System pinged.

**CITATION DRAFTED.** **SERVE TO TARGET?**

"Yes," Gerald said.

He threw the paper. It floated in the air, defying the wind, defying the heat. It drifted toward the man on the roof. The man swatted it away, but the paper dissolved into blue light upon contact with his gloves.

The man on the roof froze. The black flame on his torch sputtered.

A massive red stamp appeared in the sky above the pharmacy, visible only to those with System awareness.

**CITATION SERVED.** **VIOLATION: FAILURE TO MAINTAIN SAFE PERIMETERS.** **PENALTY: 15% REDUCTION IN THERMAL OUTPUT.** **DURATION: 24 HOURS OR UNTIL CORRECTED.**

The fire on the roof shrank by a third. The black flame turned an angry, dull orange. The man on the roof stumbled back, clutching his torch as if it had become heavy.

Sarah stared at the sky.

"Did you... did you just nerf the fire?"

"I cited the operator," Gerald said. "He is out of compliance. He cannot generate heat exceeding the rated capacity of a standard municipal furnace without a special permit. He does not have a special permit."

The man on the roof looked down. He pointed at Gerald. The air around him warped. A voice boomed, shaking the windows of the neighboring buildings.

*"INSPECTOR. YOU HAVE INTERFERED WITH THE NATURAL ORDER."*

Gerald didn't shout. He projected. He used the voice he had developed over thirty-one years of reading codes to people who did not want to hear them.

"Your natural order is a code violation," Gerald said. "You are operating without a variance. You are altering the structural integrity of a Class 3 Commercial Zoning district without filing Form 44-Z."

The man jumped down. He landed on the street, cracking the pavement. The shockwave knocked over a fire hydrant. Water sprayed into the air, mixing with the smoke.

The man walked toward Gerald. The crowd parted, not out of respect, but out of primal terror. The air grew cold around the man. Frost formed on the asphalt.

*"You are small,"* the man said. His voice was the sound of a filing cabinet closing. *"You are a fragment of a dead system. I am the System."*

"You are a violation," Gerald said. He took out another form. This one was yellow.

**NOTICE OF ABATEMENT: IMMEDIATE CESSATION OF HOSTILE ACTION** **COST: $5,000 PER SECOND OF VIOLATION** **LIABILITY: PERSONAL**

The man stopped. His porcelain mask cracked slightly.

"You cannot fine me," the man said. "I am a Core."

"Are you?" Gerald asked. He looked at the man's feet. They were not touching the ground. They hovered an inch above the pavement.

The man looked down at his feet. He frowned.

"Under Ashford Municipal Code 8.1, all persons engaging in public disturbance must maintain contact with the public right-of-way to ensure accountability," Gerald recited. "You are levitating. This is an attempt to evade jurisdiction. It is also a safety hazard."

Gerald stamped the yellow form. He slammed his hand against the air, treating the invisible System interface like a physical desk.

**CITATION SERVED.** **VIOLATION: EVASION OF JURISDICTION / FAILURE TO MAINTAIN CONTACT WITH GROUND LEVEL.** **PENALTY: GRAVITY INCREASE (200%).** **DURATION: IMMEDIATE.**

The man screamed. It was a digital scream, a glitching audio file of pain.

He slammed into the ground. The pavement shattered beneath him. He tried to push himself up, but his arms gave out.

His ten-foot frame buckled under the sudden weight of his own existence.

Sarah grabbed Gerald's shoulder.

"Gerald! He's moving. He's trying to cast something."

The man on the ground was gathering dark energy. A sphere of black void formed in his hand. He was preparing to unmake the street.

Gerald looked at the sphere. He looked at the surrounding buildings. He looked at the water from the broken hydrant pooling around the man's knees.

"Sarah," Gerald said. "Do you know the electrical code?"

"No!"

"Gross negligence," Gerald muttered.

He pointed at the puddle. He pointed at the sphere. He pointed at the man.

"Water and electricity do not mix. But here, we have magical energy, which is a form of ungrounded high-voltage discharge, and standing water, which is a conductive hazard. If that energy touches the water before it is neutralized, it creates a Ground Fault."

"Meaning what?" Sarah yelled over the noise of the collapsing roof.

"Meaning the energy has nowhere to go but into the source. Or the user. Or everything nearby."

Gerald didn't wait. He ran toward the man.

Sarah screamed.

"Stop!"

Gerald ignored her. He reached into his vest and pulled out his pen. He uncapped it. He held it like a conductor's baton.

He didn't attack the man. He attacked the environment.

He slammed his hand onto the wet asphalt, right in front of the struggling Core.

"System!" Gerald shouted.

"I am filing an Emergency Repair Order! The ground is compromised! The grounding system is non-functional!

I am ordering an immediate discharge of all stray energy into the municipal grid!

"

The System hesitated. It was a loophole.

A massive, dangerous, stupid loophole.

Gerald was not citing the Core. He was citing the street. He was claiming the street was broken and needed to absorb the excess energy to be fixed.

If the System accepted the premise that the street was 'broken' and 'in need of balancing,' it would siphon the energy.

The blue window flashed.

**QUERY: ACCEPT EMERGENCY GROUNDING PROTOCOL?** **WARNING: THIS WILL DAMAGE MUNICIPAL INFRASTRUCTURE.**

"Yes," Gerald said. "I am the inspector. I declare the infrastructure unstable."

**ACCEPTED.**

The black sphere in the Core's hand shrieked. It unravelled. The energy ripped out of the man's grip, flowing down his arms, into his legs, and into the wet asphalt.

The ground exploded.

Not with fire, but with light. Blue arcs of electricity jumped from the puddle, arcing between streetlamps, between cars, between the buildings. The man in the suit convulsed, his static face flickering rapidly. He was being drained, his power routed into the city's grid.

Lights in the pharmacy flickered on. Then off. Then on again, brighter than before.

The man slumped. The void around him dissipated. He was just a man now. A large, confused man in a torn suit, lying in a puddle of dirty water.

Silence fell over the street. The fire was out. The sirens were still wailing, but they were distant now.

Gerald stood there, panting. His hand was shaking. He looked at his pen. The tip was melted.

Sarah ran to him. She grabbed his face.

"You're an idiot. You're a complete idiot."

"I solved the problem," Gerald said. He sounded hollow.

"You blew up the street!"

"I saved the building. Technically. The structural integrity is questionable, but it's not on fire."

Sarah looked at the man on the ground. He was twitching.

The porcelain mask was gone.

His face was human, but blank.

Empty. Like a hard drive that had been wiped.

"Gerald," Sarah whispered. "Look at his eyes."

Gerald looked. The man's eyes were white. No pupils. Just white.

And then, from the man's mouth, a sound emerged. Not a word. A frequency.

It was the sound of a system booting up.

A new window appeared in Gerald's vision. It was red. Not blue.

Not yellow. Red.

**ALERT: INSPECTOR INTEGRITY COMPROMISED** **CAUSE: UNAUTHORIZED INFRASTRUCTURE ALTERATION** **STATUS: SUSPENDED**

Gerald blinked.

"Suspended?"

The air around him turned grey. The System's color palette drained from his vision. The handrails in the library, the blue glow of his Core, all of it faded.

He felt lighter. Weaker.

**YOU ARE NO LONGER AN AUTHORITY.** **YOU ARE A CITIZEN.** **YOU ARE SUBJECT TO LOCAL LAW.**

A police car pulled up.

The doors opened. Two officers stepped out. They looked at Gerald.

They looked at the unconscious man, and they looked at the destroyed street.

One of the officers raised his weapon.

"Step away from the suspect," the officer said.

Gerald looked at Sarah. Sarah looked at Gerald.

"He's a Core," Sarah said. "He's one of us."

"Who?" the officer asked. "I see a man in a suit and a guy holding a pen. And a woman screaming."

The officer pointed his gun at Gerald.

"Drop the pen, sir."

Gerald looked at the pen. He looked at the gun.

He realized, with a cold clarity that settled in his stomach like a stone, that the System had not suspended his powers.

It had removed his protection, and he was no longer a Dungeon Core. He was a 54-year-old man in a vest pocket, standing in front of a loaded firearm, in a city that had just been burned.

And the man on the ground was opening his eyes.

They were not white anymore. They were black. And they were looking at Gerald.

*"Run,"* the man whispered.

Gerald didn't run. He dropped the pen.

Because if he ran, he would be fleeing the scene of a crime. And he did not flee. He always stayed.

But this time, staying meant dying.

The officer fired.
