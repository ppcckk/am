Chapter 276: Non-Compliant Dive

# Chapter 276

The cuff was cold.

Gerald did not pull against it. Pulling against restraints was unauthorized physical activity outside of emergency egress protocols. He stood still, his hands held at a forty-five-degree angle to his torso, watching the man with the badge adjust his tie.

"You have the right to remain silent," the tallest agent said. He smelled of stale coffee and ozone. "Anything you say can and will be used against you in a court of municipal law."

"I am aware of my rights," Gerald said. His voice was steady. "I am also aware that your warrant is invalid."

The agent paused. He looked down at the paper in his hand, then back at Gerald.

"It's signed.

It's dated. It's specific.

"

"It lacks the proper jurisdictional stamp from the Department of Public Works," Gerald said. "Arresting a Dungeon Core for endangering public infrastructure requires a dual-authorization seal. You have only the police seal. Therefore, the arrest is procedurally defective."

"Procedurally defective," the agent repeated. He sneered. "We don't care about your paperwork, Hutchins. You're flooding the street."

"I cited the street," Gerald corrected. "Which is a different thing."

"You flooded the street."

"Because the sewers failed to comply with the updated drainage code I issued last month.

If they had met the new standards, the water would have drained. They didn't.

That is their fault.

Not mine."

The second agent, a shorter man with a thick neck, stepped forward. He grabbed Gerald's elbow.

"Walk."

Gerald did not walk. He planted his feet.

"I cannot comply with an illegal order."

"Is that a threat?" the short man asked.

"It is a statement of fact," Gerald said. "I am a municipal employee. You are interfering with an active inspection."

The tall man sighed. He pulled a small device from his pocket. It looked like a standard taser, but the prongs glowed with a soft, blue light. System energy.

"If you don't walk, we'll drag you. And if we drag you, we'll file an additional citation for resisting arrest.

That's thirty days in a holding cell.

No ventilation.

No fire suppression. You know what that does to your Compliance Rating.

"

Gerald looked at the device. He looked at the agent's hand. He noted the tremor in the man's fingers.

Fear. The agent was afraid of the System.

That was useful.

"If you use that device," Gerald said, "you are introducing unlicensed high-voltage current into a public thoroughfare. That is a Class B electrical hazard."

The tall man hesitated. His thumb hovered over the trigger.

"Let him talk," the woman said.

Gerald hadn't noticed her approach. She had come up behind the agents, silent as a ghost. She wore a suit that looked like it had been tailored in a different dimension. The fabric shifted when she moved, like oil on water.

"Inspector," the tall man said. He lowered the taser. "This is a police matter."

"It is a compliance matter," she said. She stepped into Gerald's field of vision. Her eyes were not right.

They were too symmetrical.

Too perfect. "And Mr.

Hutchins is the only person in this city who understands the difference.

"

"He cited the city," the short man said. "Look at the water. It's glowing."

"Because the water is responding to the Citation," Gerald said. "It is attempting to self-report its own violation. It is trying to flush itself out of the system.

Which is, technically, a corrective action. You should be thanking me.

I have identified the root cause of the flood.

"

"The root cause is you," the tall man said.

"The root cause is non-compliant infrastructure," Gerald said. "I merely pointed a light at it. You, however, are pointing a light at the wrong person."

The woman smiled, which was a thin, sharp expression.

"He is correct, Officer.

The Inspector General does not issue warrants through the local police precinct. We handle our own enforcement. Which means you are detaining a protected entity.

"

"Protected entity?" the short man asked.

"Gerald Hutchins is currently under a Temporary Stay of Execution," the woman said. "Pending an appeal of the original Citation against the Punsing Garden. Your warrant is void."

The tall man stared at her. He looked at Gerald. He looked at the glowing water spreading across the pavement outside the library doors.

"Who are you?" the tall man asked.

"I am the liaison," the woman said. "And I am here to take Mr.

Hutchins into protective custody.

Not for arrest. For mediation.

"

She reached out and placed a hand on Gerald's shoulder.

The cold of the handcuffs vanished. The metal clicked and fell away.

Gerald rubbed his wrists, and the skin was red, but there were no bruises. The System had healed them before the police had even touched him.

"Mediation," Gerald said. "With whom?"

"With the City," the woman said. "And with the Inspector General.

He is not happy, Gerald.

You have disrupted the equilibrium.

The sewers are not just draining. They are *fighting back*.

"

As if on cue, a gurgling sound rose from the storm drain in the middle of the street. Black water surged upward, forming a shape. It was roughly humanoid, composed of sludge and rotting leaves. It raised an arm, made of wet debris, and pointed at Gerald.

"It is citing you back," the woman said.

Gerald looked at the sludge-monster. He looked at the police officers.

"That is a biological hazard," Gerald said. "And an electrical hazard. The water is charged. Do not touch it."

"It's just water," the short man said. He took a step toward the drain.

"It is not just water," Gerald said. "It is a Counter-Citation. The System recognizes that I issued a Citation to the sewer system.

The sewer system is now issuing a Citation to *me*. Specifically, for 'Interference with Natural Drainage Processes.' If that citation sticks, my Compliance Rating drops to zero.

And if my Compliance Rating drops to zero...

"

He didn't finish the sentence. He didn't need to. The agents knew the rules.

A Core with zero compliance was deleted.

Their dungeon collapsed. Their consciousness scattered.

The tall man took a step back.

"How do we stop it?"

"We don't stop it," the woman said. "We answer it."

She looked at Gerald.

"You have to attend the Hearing. Now. Before the counter-citation is finalized."

"I am under arrest," Gerald said.

"You are not," the woman said. "You are under subpoena. And I am the process server."

She held out a hand. A small, translucent card materialized in her palm. It pulsed with a golden light.

"Take it," she said.

Gerald looked at the card, and he looked at the sludge-monster. The monster was getting larger. It was pulling more water from the ground.

The street was beginning to buckle. Cracks spread through the asphalt, glowing with faint yellow runes.

"If I take that," Gerald said, "I am admitting to the violation."

"You are admitting to the *existence* of the violation," the woman said. "You are arguing for a reduction in penalty. It is standard procedure."

Gerald's heart hammered against his ribs, and he hated hearings.

He hated arguing. He hated improvisation.

But he hated losing more.

He reached for the card.

"Wait," the tall agent said. He held up his phone.

"My captain says you can't go anywhere.

He says the mayor wants a press conference in an hour. You're the face of the disaster.

"

"The mayor is not a licensed inspector," Gerald said. "He has no authority over my professional judgment."

"He has authority over your salary," the agent said.

Gerald paused. His hand hovered over the card.

Salary, and he had not thought about the salary.

"I have thirty-one years of tenure," Gerald said. "You cannot fire me without a union grievance process. Which takes six months."

"We can suspend you," the agent said. "Without pay. For cause."

"Cause?" Gerald asked. "What is the cause?"

"Gross negligence," the agent said. "You caused a flood."

"I cited a violation," Gerald said.

"The flood is the result of the violation, not the citation. If the sewer had been compliant, there would be no flood.

Therefore, the flood is not my negligence.

It is the city's.

"

The woman laughed. It was a dry, rattling sound.

"He is right.

The liability lies with the infrastructure owner.

The City of Ashford owns the sewers. Gerald Hutchins does not.

"

The tall agent frowned. He looked at his phone. He whispered something. Then he looked back at Gerald.

"If you go with her," the agent said, "you are abandoning your post. That is a violation of municipal code 14.2, section C."

Gerald looked at the woman.

"Can you cite them?" Gerald asked.

The woman raised an eyebrow.

"For what?"

"Obstruction of a Public Official," Gerald said. "I am conducting an on-site inspection of a hazardous condition. You are preventing me from doing so. Therefore, you are in violation."

The tall agent stepped forward.

"You're bluffing."

"I am never bluffing," Gerald said. "I am stating facts. Look at your shoes.

You are standing in the runoff. You are contaminating the scene.

You are not wearing the required personal protective equipment for a biohazard site, which is a OSHA violation. And since you are municipal employees, that falls under the municipal safety code.

"

The agent looked down. His shoes were indeed wet with the glowing black sludge. He took a step back, wiping his feet on the dry pavement.

"I..." he started.

"You are in violation," Gerald said. "I am issuing a verbal Citation. You have twenty-four hours to rectify the PPE deficiency, or you will be subject to a fine of five hundred dollars per violation per person."

The short man looked at his own feet. He paled.

"Is he serious?" the short man whispered.

"He is always serious," the woman said. "He is the most dangerous man in this city, and he has never even raised his voice."

Gerald looked at the sludge-monster. It had grown to the size of a car. It was now blocking the entrance to the library.

"I need to leave," Gerald said. "To attend the Hearing."

"If you leave, I will arrest you," the tall agent said. But his voice lacked conviction. He was rubbing his wet shoe.

"And if you arrest me," Gerald said, "you will be cited for unlawful detention of a public servant. And I will appeal. And I will win. And you will be fired."

He looked at the tall agent. He looked at the short man. He looked at the woman.

"Let me go," Gerald said. "Or I will cite this entire precinct for inadequate training."

The tall agent looked at the woman. She shrugged.

"He's right," she said. "If you touch him, you touch the System. And the System doesn't like being touched by people who aren't licensed."

The tall agent lowered his phone. He stepped aside.

"Go," he said.

Gerald took the card.

The moment his fingers brushed the translucent surface, the world shifted. The street disappeared. The library disappeared. The glowing water disappeared.

He was standing in a white room, which was not empty.

Sitting across from him, at a long table made of glass, was a figure in a gray robe.

The face was obscured by a mask made of shifting geometric shapes. To the figure's left sat a smaller version of the Punsing Garden's Core, looking miserable and damp.

To the figure's right sat a woman in a blue suit.

She held a clipboard. She looked at Gerald with cold, professional interest.

"Gerald Hutchins," the figure in the gray robe said. The voice was not loud.

It was heavy.

Like a stone dropping into a deep well.

"You are here to contest a Citation."

"I am here to contest the enforcement," Gerald said.

The woman in blue looked up.

"Define 'enforcement.'"

Gerald looked at the Punsing Garden Core. He looked at the mask. He looked at his own hands.

He was holding the card. He was holding his life in his hand.

"The enforcement was premature," Gerald said. "I had not yet filed the appeal for the original Citation. The counter-citation was issued before the statutory waiting period expired. Therefore, the counter-citation is void."

The Punsing Garden Core let out a wheezing sound.

"He's lying. He skipped the appeal. He cited the sewers directly."

"I cited the sewers," Gerald said.

"I did not skip the appeal. I filed the appeal via digital submission at 09:00 hours. The timestamp is in the System logs.

You have not checked them.

"

The woman in blue tapped her clipboard. She frowned. She tapped it again.

"He is correct," she said. "The log shows a submission. It was flagged as 'High Priority' but was not reviewed due to system overload caused by... the flood."

Gerald felt a small flicker of satisfaction. A tiny, cold spark in his chest.

"The system overload was caused by the flood," Gerald said. "Which was caused by the non-compliant sewers.

Which I cited.

Therefore, the delay was not my fault. It was the fault of the infrastructure. You cannot punish me for the failure of the infrastructure.

"

The figure in the gray robe tilted its head. The geometric mask shifted. A triangle became a square.

"You are arguing," the figure said, "that the System is to blame for your actions."

"I am arguing," Gerald said, "that the System is a bureaucracy. And bureaucracies have rules. I followed the rules. You did not."

The Punsing Garden Core slammed a fist onto the table.

"He destroyed my dungeon! He killed my guards! He flooded the city!"

"He cited the guards for improper uniform standards," Gerald said. "And he cited the dungeon for inadequate ventilation. The deaths were a side effect of the debuffs. The debuffs were lawful."

The figure in the gray robe raised a hand. Silence fell over the room.

"Mr. Hutchins," the figure said.

"You have a point.

The appeal was filed.

The counter-citation was premature. I will vacate the counter-citation.

"

Gerald exhaled. He had not realized he was holding his breath.

"However," the figure continued.

Gerald stiffened.

"However," the figure said, "the flood is still happening. The city is in chaos. The System requires stability. You created the instability."

"I identified it," Gerald said.

"You exploited it," the figure said. "You used the Citation to bypass the normal enforcement channels. You are a loophole, Mr.

Hutchins. And loopholes must be closed.

"

The woman in blue looked at Gerald. Her eyes were sharp.

"Here is the ruling," the figure said. "The counter-citation is void. You are free to go. But there is a condition."

"What condition?" Gerald asked.

"You must fix the flood.

Within twenty-four hours. If the city is not compliant with the drainage code by then, you will be permanently deregistered. Not just from the System.

From the City."

Gerald looked at the table. He looked at the Punsing Garden Core. The Core was smiling now. A wicked, wet smile.

"You can't do it," the Core said. "The sewers are clogged with sludge.

The pumps are broken.

You're an inspector. You don't fix things. You just write them down.

"

Gerald looked at the figure in the gray robe.

"If I fail, I die," Gerald said.

"If you fail, you cease to exist," the figure said. "And the City will tear down your dungeon. Brick by brick."

Gerald stood up. He walked toward the door.

It was not a door, which was a gap in the white space.

He stepped through it.

He was back on the street.

The sludge-monster was still there. The police were still watching. The water was still rising.

The woman in the suit was waiting for him.

"Well?" she asked.

"I have twenty-four hours," Gerald said.

She looked at the glowing water. She looked at the crumbling asphalt.

"You can't fix the sewers in twenty-four hours," she said.

"No," Gerald said. He looked at the Punsing Garden's entrance. The light was pulsing in rhythm with the water. "But I can change the code."

The woman stared at him.

"Change the code?" she asked. "You're a Core. You don't write the code. The System writes the code."

Gerald looked at the sludge-monster. He looked at the police. He looked at the sky.

"The System writes the code," Gerald said. "But I am the one who interprets it."

He turned to the tallest agent.

"Officer," Gerald said. "I need a ladder."

The agent blinked.

"A ladder?"

"And a shovel," Gerald said. "And a permit."

"A permit?"

"For emergency excavation," Gerald said. "I'm going to dig a new trench. Right here. In the middle of the street."

The agent laughed.

"You're insane."

Gerald looked at him. He did not laugh.

"I am not insane," Gerald said. "I am compliant."

He looked at the woman.

"Get me the permit," he said. "Or I will cite you for failure to provide municipal support."

The woman hesitated. Then she pulled out a tablet. She typed something.

"You have it," she said.

Gerald turned to the Punsing Garden. The Core was watching him.

"You're making a mistake," Gerald said. "You think this is about water. It's not. It's about flow."

He looked at the sludge-monster.

"And I am going to redirect it."

He walked into the black water.

The police shouted. The woman screamed.

But Gerald did not stop. He walked until the water reached his chest. Then he dove.

Under the street, in the dark, he found the pipe. It was cracked. It was leaking. It was non-compliant.

And he was going to fix it.
