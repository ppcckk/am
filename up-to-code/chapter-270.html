Chapter 270: Appeal Denied

# Chapter 270

The clipboard materialized in the air with a soft, mechanical *thunk*.

It was not wood. It was not plastic. It was a slab of translucent blue light, hovering at a precise thirty-degree angle, waiting for a signature.

Gerald stared at it. His pen, a standard-issue municipal ballpoint with a chewed cap, trembled in his hand. Not from fear. From the sheer, audacious audacity of the request.

Appeal.

The word hung in the damp sub-basement air, vibrating against the stone walls. The System's font, a sharp, serifed typeface that Gerald recognized from the city zoning ordinances of 1998, burned into his retinas.

The woman in the suit stepped forward. The fog of the Crimson Wastes parted around her like oil slicked water. She did not walk; she glided, her heels clicking on the concrete with a rhythm that matched Gerald's own heartbeat.

"Form 77-C," she said. Her voice was the sound of paper tearing.

"Request for Administrative Review of Citation 4492-B.

Filed by Core Unit 7, Crimson Wastes. Reason: Alleged procedural error in service of Citation.

"

Gerald looked at Mara. She had frozen, her dagger still pointed at a water stain on the floor. The goblin behind her had stopped breathing, its chest a still, heaving lump.

Thirty seconds remained on the audit clock.

Gerald looked at the clock. *00:29:45*, and he looked at the woman.

She was not a monster. She was not a hero.

She was a bureaucrat.

And she was holding a legal document that could dismantle everything he had built.

If the Citation was voided, the debuffs on the Crimson Wastes would vanish. Their elite spawns would regain their full health and resistance stats. The three creatures he had effectively executed by citing their fire exits would be brought back by the System's restorative protocols, fueled by the magical energy of a vindicated appeal.

Gerald hated appeals. He hated them because they relied on interpretation, and interpretation relied on ambiguity.

Gerald lived for the black and white.

The ink on the page.

The measurement in inches, and he stepped toward the floating clipboard.

"State your ground," Gerald said. His voice was flat. Professional. He adjusted his collar, though there was no one in his office to judge his attire.

The woman tilted her head. A small, cruel smile touched her lips.

"Procedural error, Core Hutchins.

Citation 4492-B was served to a proxy unit. A scout goblin.

The target Core was not physically present at the site of service.

System Rule 4, Subsection D: *'A Citation requires the target Core to be physically present to serve.'*

"

Gerald paused, and he looked at the goblin that had been leaning on the wall.

It was still there, trembling. It was a scout.

A low-level entity.

Not the Core itself.

The Crimson Wastes Core was miles away, deep in the volcanic fissures of the northern district. It had sent a scout to harass Gerald's dungeon, not to receive paperwork.

Gerald's pen dipped. He didn't write yet. He thought.

The rule was clear. *Physically present.*

But the System also had a clause he had memorized during the long, lonely nights of his first week as a Core. A clause about jurisdiction.

"You are citing the service of the Citation," Gerald said. "Not the violation itself."

"Precisely," the woman said. "Service was improper.

Therefore, the Citation is void.

The debuffs are lifted.

Your dungeon's Compliance Rating of 47 remains unaffected by my Core's actions, but my Core's reputation is restored. And you, Gerald Hutchins, will be cited for harassment of a registered Core.

"

Mara stepped forward, her face pale.

"Gerald, you can't win this. The rule is explicit."

Gerald ignored her. He looked at the goblin. It was still leaning on the wall.

It had not moved. It had not left the premises.

The audit clock ticked. *00:29:10*.

Gerald walked to the goblin. He did not look at it with fear. He looked at it with the critical eye of a man checking if a handrail was bolted securely to a stud.

"Excuse me," Gerald said to the goblin.

The goblin squeaked.

"I… I am leaving."

"No," Gerald said. "You are not leaving. You are currently occupying a non-designated rest area. Furthermore, you are obstructing a primary egress route."

He turned back to the woman in the suit.

"Core Unit 7 claims service was improper because the Core was not present."

"Yes."

"However," Gerald said, clicking his pen, "Core Unit 7 voluntarily dispatched a biological entity into my dungeon space. By System Rule 12, *'Any entity acting under the direct command of a Core constitutes an extension of that Core's presence for the duration of its active duty.'*"

The woman's smile vanished.

"That is not in the manual," she said.

"It is in the errata," Gerald said.

"Page 402. Line 18. Added after the Great Goblin Rebellion of three months ago.

It defines 'presence' not by location, but by command chain.

Your scout is your hands. Your scout is your eyes.

Your scout is *you*.

"

Gerald held up his pen.

"I am not appealing your appeal," Gerald said. "I am amending my Citation. I am serving a second citation.

Right now. To the scout.

For obstruction of egress and unauthorized leaning on load-bearing structures.

"

The woman stared at him. The clipboard flickered. Red error messages scrolled across its surface.

*ERROR: TARGET PRESENCE CONFLICT.*

*CALCULATING COMMAND CHAIN…*

*CONFIRMED.*

The System was thinking. It was weighing the command chain against the physical distance.

Mara watched, her dagger forgotten. The goblin watched, its eyes wide.

The clock hit *00:28:00*.

The clipboard stopped flickering.

A new line of text appeared.

**CITATION 4492-B AMENDED. SERVICE VALIDATED VIA COMMAND CHAIN PROTOCOL.**

The woman in the suit recoiled. The blue light of her suit dimmed.

"This is… a loophole."

"It is code," Gerald said.

"You filed the paperwork wrong. You tried to cite me for procedural error, but you forgot to check the updated statutes on remote proxy liability.

That is not a loophole, ma'am. That is negligence.

"

He signed the amended citation. The ink glowed gold.

The effect was immediate. The air in the sub-basement shivered.

The three elite monsters of the Crimson Wastes, currently miles away, found their health bars dropping to critical levels.

The fire code violation debuff returned, sharper and more punishing than before. The System did not like being wrong. It corrected its mistake with interest.

The woman in the suit screamed. It was not a human scream. It was the sound of a filing cabinet slamming shut.

"You have violated the spirit of the law!" she shrieked.

"I have followed the letter," Gerald said. "The spirit is not enforceable. The letter is."

He looked at the clock. *00:27:30*, and the audit was still running.

He had won the legal battle, but the physical battle was not over. The Crimson Wastes were retaliating.

The fog was thickening.

Shadows were lengthening.

And then, the floor beneath Gerald shifted.

Not a tremor. A tilt.

A low groan emanated from the earth. It sounded like a building settling, but wrong. Unbalanced.

Gerald looked down.

The concrete beneath his feet was cracking.

"No," Gerald whispered. "No, no, no."

He had reinforced this area. He had installed a support pillar. He had checked the load ratings. He had done it himself.

Unless.

Unless the Crimson Wastes had not just sent a scout. They had sent a saboteur.

The woman in the suit was gone. Dissolved into the fog. But her presence lingered. And with her departure, she had left something behind.

A hole.

Not a hole in the floor. A hole in the *code*.

Gerald looked at the crack. It was spreading, racing toward the main support pillar he had installed in Chapter 15.

If that pillar failed, the entire library above would collapse.

Not just the sub-basement.

The library. The people in it.

The System did not care about the library. It cared about the dungeon. But Gerald did.

He looked at Mara.

"Mara, get the party out. Now."

"We can't leave you!"

"I am not leaving! I am fixing the violation!"

Gerald dropped his pen. He pulled a tape measure from his pocket. It snapped open with a metallic *ping*.

He ran toward the cracking floor. The goblin, sensing the shift in power, scrambled away, fleeing back toward the fog.

The crack widened. Water from the leaky pipe in the corner sprayed out, mixing with the dust.

*Warning: Structural Integrity at 12%.*

*Warning: Imminent Collapse.*

Gerald ignored the warnings. He measured the gap.

Two inches. Then four.

He needed a brace. He didn't have a brace. He had a handrail.

He ran to the nearest wall. He ripped the metal handrail from its bolts. The bolts shrieked. The metal bent.

It was not enough, and he looked at the goblin's abandoned rest area. A simple wooden bench, poorly constructed, violating fire safety standards by being too close to a heat source.

It was perfect.

Gerald grabbed the bench. It was heavy. He dragged it toward the crack.

The floor groaned again. The ceiling above them creaked. Dust fell like snow.

He jammed the bench into the widening fissure. It was a desperate, ugly fix. It violated every principle of proper bracing.

It was not anchored. It was not angled correctly.

It was a temporary patch on a catastrophic failure.

But it held.

For a second.

Then the bench splintered.

The crack exploded outward.

Gerald was thrown backward. He hit the wall, his head snapping back against the brick. Stars danced in his vision.

The floor beneath the bench gave way. A sinkhole.

Darkness yawned below.

Gerald looked over the edge. He saw not earth, but a void. A swirling, purple void that smelled of sulfur and old magic.

The Crimson Wastes were not just a dungeon. They were a leak. A breach.

And something was climbing out.

A hand. Pale, elongated, with too many joints.

Gerald scrambled back, his heart hammering against his ribs. He looked at Mara. She was frozen, staring at the void.

"The support failed," Gerald said. His voice was calm. Too calm.

Mara looked at him.

"We have to run."

"No," Gerald said. He pulled his pen back from his pocket. It was broken. The cap was missing.

He looked at the hand climbing out of the void, and he looked at the broken bench.

He looked at the System interface floating in his peripheral vision.

**STRUCTURAL FAILURE DETECTED.**

**VIOLATION: INADEQUATE SUB-STRATA REINFORCEMENT.**

**FINE: -500 COMPLIANCE POINTS.**

**PENALTY: DUNGEON LOCKDOWN.**

Gerald smiled. It was a thin, jagged thing.

"Lockdown," he whispered. "Good."

He raised his broken pen. He pointed it at the hand.

"I am issuing a citation for unauthorized excavation without a permit."

The System paused.

The hand stopped climbing.

It tilted its head, confused by the audacity of the demand.

Gerald stepped forward. He stood on the edge of the sinkhole. He did not brace himself. He did not look down.

He looked at the woman in the suit, who had reappeared at the edge of the fog, her face twisted in rage.

"You want to appeal?" Gerald shouted over the rumble of the earth. "Fine. I will appeal your entire existence. I will cite you for not having a foundation!"

He swung his pen, which did not hit the hand. It hit the System interface.

And it broke the rule.

The screen shattered. The numbers scattered. The audit clock froze.

*00:27:00*, and the world went silent.

Then, the void roared.

Not a sound. A pressure.

Gerald felt his body being pulled forward. Not by gravity. By the code itself.

The System was rewriting him, and he looked at Mara. Her eyes were wide. She was screaming, but he couldn't hear her.

The last thing Gerald Hutchins saw was the number 47 on his Compliance Rating blinking into red.

And then, he fell.
