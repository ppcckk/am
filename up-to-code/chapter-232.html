Chapter 232: Unlicensed Biological Entity

# Chapter 232

The stone fingers closed around Gerald's ankle.

He didn't scream. He didn't panic. He looked down at the grey, jagged protrusion erupting from the library's sub-basement floor, then back at the vine-core, who was currently trying to shield its leaves from the falling dust.

"Improper excavation," Gerald said. His voice was flat, devoid of fear, and entirely focused on the debris field.

"No permit filed for subsurface intrusion.

No geotechnical survey. This is a Class A violation of municipal code section 4.12."

The hand tightened. The stone groaned.

The vine-core flinched. **IT IS THE IG,** it projected, its mental voice jagged with static. **IT DOES NOT CARE ABOUT CODE. IT CARES ABOUT THE HEARING.**

"The Inspector General is part of the System," Gerald said.

He pulled a small, yellow notepad from his vest pocket. He clicked his pen.

"And the System has a building code.

Did you know that? I didn't either, until I started measuring things."

Another hand emerged from the crack. Then a third.

They were clumsy, slow, made of compacted earth and old foundation stones.

They weren't attacking with intent; they were attacking with structural ignorance.

They were trying to grab Gerald, but they were doing it with the grace of a collapsing retaining wall.

Gerald sidestepped the first grab. His hip bumped against the broken office chair. He didn't move away. He moved *toward* the crack.

"Mira," Gerald said, not turning his head. "Hold the beam."

Mira was already there. Her sword was jammed into a fissure in the overhead concrete slab, a makeshift strut. Her arms trembled. "Gerald, they're pulling you down!"

"I'm not being pulled down," Gerald said. "I'm conducting an on-site inspection of the unpermitted breach."

He pointed his pen at the leading stone hand.

**CITATION ISSUED: ILLEGAL EXCAVATION. PENALTY: 50% REDUCTION IN MOVEMENT SPEED FOR ALL ENTITY SPAWNS GENERATED BY THIS BREACH FOR THE DURATION OF THE HEARING.**

The System text flashed in his vision, bright and blue.

The stone hands froze. They didn't stop; they slowed to a crawl. The leading hand, which had been inches from Gerald's chest, now moved with the agonizing lethargy of wet cement drying in winter.

Gerald stepped back, brushing dust off his khakis. He looked at the vine-core. "See?

Compliance is power. You're generating spawns without a variance request.

The IG is just enforcing the rules you're breaking."

The vine-core's eyes widened. **I DID NOT KNOW THE RULES APPLIED TO THE IG'S PROXIES.**

"Nobody knows the rules until they get fined," Gerald said. He turned to the crack in the floor. The stone hands were still reaching, but they were sluggish. Vulnerable.

Mira wrenched her sword free from the ceiling slab. "We have to move. The ceiling is going to drop in about ninety seconds. I can feel the rebar bending."

Gerald looked up. He ignored the groaning metal above him. He looked at the stone hands.

"You're not leaving," Gerald said to the vine-core.

**WE MUST ESCAPE. THE IG IS HERE.**

"No," Gerald said. "You're the defendant. I'm the inspector.

The IG is the arbiter.

But the arbiter has a problem. The IG is inside my dungeon.

My dungeon has a Compliance Rating of 84/100.

The IG's proxy spawns have no Compliance Rating because they are unregistered entities. Under System Rule 12-C, unregistered entities that interfere with a registered Core's operation during a sanctioned Hearing are considered Trespassers on Private Property."

Gerald pointed his pen at the stone hands.

"I am invoking the Right of Egress. All unauthorized entities must vacate the premises immediately or face immediate deletion by the Local Compliance Enforcement Protocol."

The air in the sub-basement went still. The dust motes stopped dancing.

The stone hands paused. The System seemed to consider this.

A prompt appeared in Gerald's mind.

**APPEAL PENDING. TARGET: UNREGISTERED ENTITY [IG_PROXY_01]. ARGUMENT: PRIVATE PROPERTY TRESPASS DURING SANCTIONED LEGAL PROCEEDING. EVIDENCE: CORE REGISTRATION 8474-G, HEARING ORDER 992-IG.**

**VERDICT: ACCEPTED. PROTOCOL ENGAGED.**

The stone hands vibrated. Not with effort, but with dissolution. They crumbled, not into rubble, but into fine grey powder that scattered across the floor. The crack in the floor didn't seal, but the hands that had emerged from it vanished.

Mira stared at the pile of dust. "Did you just delete the Inspector General's minions?"

"I cited them," Gerald said. "There's a difference. Deletion is a combat mechanic. Citation is a legal one."

**THE IG WILL BE ANGRY,** the vine-core projected. Its voice was smaller now, less certain.

"The IG doesn't get angry," Gerald said. "The IG gets an audit trail.

And audits are boring.

Boring is good.

Boring means I don't have to fight."

He checked his watch. "We have approximately two minutes before the ceiling collapses. If you want to win this appeal, you need to prove you didn't install the beam. You need to prove the beam was installed by the City."

**THE CITY?**

"Who else would put a load-bearing beam in a library sub-basement without telling anyone?" Gerald asked. "Think. Who signed off on the renovation?"

The vine-core's leaves rustled. It was thinking.

**THE FOREMAN. HE SAID IT WAS NECESSARY FOR STABILITY. HE GAVE ME THE BLUEPRINTS.**

"Gerald," Mira shouted. "Now!"

The ceiling slab above them dipped. A chunk of concrete the size of a microwave detached and crashed onto the spot where Gerald had been standing a second ago.

Gerald didn't flinch. He grabbed the vine-core's root system.

"Move," Gerald commanded.

The vine-core resisted. **I CANNOT LEAVE THE HEARING UNFINISHED.**

"The Hearing is suspended due to hazardous conditions," Gerald said, his voice dropping an octave, becoming the voice he used when he found asbestos in a school gymnasium. "Code 74.2: Immediate evacuation required upon structural failure. We are not leaving the Hearing.

We are moving the venue to a safer location. This is a procedural amendment."

He pulled. The vine-core, confused by the legal logic, allowed itself to be dragged.

Mira scrambled up a pile of fallen shelves, jumping from one to the next. Gerald and the vine-core moved together, a strange pair of man and monster, navigating the collapsing labyrinth.

They weren't running away. They were running *toward* the ventilation shaft.

"Why the vent?" Mira yelled over the roaring collapse.

"Because the shaft is up to code," Gerald said. "And the main stairwell is not."

They reached the shaft. It was a narrow, rectangular opening in the wall, fitted with a metal ladder. The ladder was rusty, but the frame was solid.

Gerald climbed. The vine-core had to be carried. Mira held Gerald's boots as he lifted the heavy, writhing mass of roots and leaves up into the duct.

Dust choked them. The heat was rising. The fire suppression system was failing, sprinklers sputtering dry.

Gerald squeezed into the shaft, dragging the vine-core's root ball behind him. The metal groaned. The shaft was tight, designed for maintenance workers, not monsters.

"Push," Gerald said.

He pushed. The vine-core pushed back, its tendrils finding purchase on the duct walls.

Above them, the library floor gave way. The ceiling of the sub-basement was gone. The weight of the books, the desks, the entire first floor of the Ashford Municipal Library was coming down.

They were in the neck of the hourglass.

Gerald looked back down into the shaft. He saw the IG's symbol glowing in the dust below. A golden eye, watching.

It didn't speak. It just watched.

Gerald met its gaze. He didn't look away. He didn't apologize. He held up his yellow notepad, the one with the citation for illegal excavation written in clear, block letters.

He tapped the page.

*This is not over,* his expression said. *I have the logs. I have the foreman's name. And I have a copy of the original building permit, which I found in the library's basement archives three years ago. The beam was never approved.*

The golden eye flickered.

Then, a new prompt appeared in Gerald's mind.

**HEARING RESUMED. VENUE: VENTILATION SHAFT 4B. STATUS: IN PROGRESS.**

**NEW RULE ADDED: CORES MAY NOT USE STRUCTURAL COLLAPSE AS A DEFENSE DURING APPEAL. PENALTY: 100% DEBUFF TO CORE HEALTH FOR 24 HOURS.**

Gerald stopped moving.

He looked at Mira. Mira looked at him.

"Oh," Gerald said. "That's a new one."

**THE SYSTEM REACTS TO YOUR PREVIOUS MANEUVERS,** the IG's voice echoed, no longer coming from the ground, but from the walls themselves. **YOU USED TRESPASS TO STOP SPAWNS.

YOU USED EVACUATION TO STOP THE HEARING. NOW, THE SYSTEM ADJUSTS. YOU HAVE BROKEN THE CODE OF PROCEDURE.

YOU ARE NOW IN VIOLATION OF RULE 12-C.**

Gerald's vision blurred. A red bar appeared above his head.

**CORE HEALTH: 12/100.**

**DEBUFF: ALL DEFENSES REDUCED TO ZERO.**

**VULNERABILITY: CRITICAL.**

The vine-core let out a low, mournful hum. **YOU BROKE THE RULES TO WIN, GERALD. NOW THE RULES BREAK YOU.**

Gerald looked at his hands. They were shaking.

Not from fear.

From the physical sensation of his own dungeon's integrity failing. He could feel every crack in the walls, every weak joint in the ceiling. He could feel the weight of the library pressing down on him.

Mira grabbed his shoulder. "Can you fix it?"

"I can't fix it," Gerald said. "The System changed the rules. It's not a bug. It's a patch."

He looked at the vine-core. The monster was looking at him with something like pity.

**THE FOREMAN,** the vine-core said. **HE DID NOT JUST INSTALL THE BEAM. HE PLANTED A LISTENING DEVICE IN THE WALLS. HE KNEW YOU WOULD BE HERE. HE KNEW YOU WOULD CITE HIM.**

Gerald froze.

"What?"

**THE CITY WANTED YOU TO FIND THE VIOLATION. THE CITY WANTED YOU TO CITE THE CORE. THE CITY WANTED THE HEARING TO HAPPEN.**

Gerald's mind raced. The pieces clicked.

The strange timing of the renovation.

The specific placement of the beam.

The fact that the IG had shown up *exactly* when Gerald found the logs.

This wasn't a rescue. It wasn't an attack.

It was a setup.

"Why?" Gerald asked.

**TO TEST YOU,** the vine-core said. **THE SYSTEM IS AUDITING YOUR CORE. THE IG IS NOT ENFORCING THE CODE. THE IG IS ENFORCING THE TEST.**

Gerald looked at the red bar above his head. 12/100. He was vulnerable. His dungeon was failing. And he was trapped in a ventilation shaft, with a monster that trusted him and a girl who was holding on for dear life.

A new sound echoed in the shaft. Not the crash of concrete.

Footsteps.

Heavy, rhythmic footsteps. Coming down the duct.

Gerald looked up into the darkness of the shaft above him.

Two lights appeared. Not torches. Flashlights.

Then, a voice. Calm, professional, and terrifyingly familiar.

"Mr. Hutchins," the voice said. "Please step out of the shaft.

You are in violation of several safety protocols. And I'm afraid your citation has been revoked."

Gerald recognized the voice. It was the voice of the City Manager. The man who had fired him thirty-one years ago for "excessive pedantry."

The City Manager stepped into the light. He wasn't holding a weapon. He was holding a clipboard.

"Welcome back, Gerald," the City Manager said. "The inspection never really ended, did it?"

Gerald looked at the clipboard. It was a new form. Form 11-B. Unlicensed Biological Entity.

And the name on the form wasn't the vine-core's.

It was Gerald's.
