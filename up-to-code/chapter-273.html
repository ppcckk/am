Chapter 273: Blood Oath Mechanic

# Chapter 273

The mud tasted of copper and old blood.

Kael wiped the sludge from his eyes, blinking against the sudden darkness that had swallowed the corridor. He gripped his broken shield with white-knuckled desperation, the wood splintering further under the strain. The Minotaur lunged, a mountain of muscle and rage, but it did not strike him.

It struck the air.

Where the beast's horns had been, there was only a shimmering grid of translucent blue lines. A holographic barrier snapped into existence, humming with a low, electric buzz that vibrated in Kael's teeth. The Minotaur slammed into it, its momentum halted by an invisible force that felt less like magic and more like a solid wall of concrete.

The beast roared, confused, its hooves skidding on the slick floor.

Kael scrambled backward, slipping on the wet earth. He stared at the barrier, then at the figure standing ten feet away.

Gerald Hutchins stood there, holding a clipboard that shouldn't have existed in a dungeon. He wasn't holding a sword. He wasn't casting a spell. He was holding a pen.

"Stop moving," Gerald said. His voice was flat, devoid of the adrenaline pumping through Kael's veins. "You're disturbing the measurement zone."

Kael opened his mouth to scream, to ask what kind of madness this was, but the words died in his throat. The System interface hovered in his peripheral vision, red and angry.

**SYSTEM ALERT: STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY FAILURE IMMINENT.** **3 MINUTES TO REMEDIATION.**

"The handrails," Kael gasped, pointing at the shimmering grid. "What are you doing?"

"Improvising a temporary egress path," Gerald replied, not looking up from his clipboard. He scribbled something furiously. "The Minotaur is currently serving as a non-compliant load-bearing element. We are removing it from the equation."

Gerald gestured to the man with the shield.

"You.

Hold the beast.

Keep its weight off the ceiling tiles. If the ceiling fails, we all get crushed. If it doesn't, I can cite it for excessive structural reliance.

"

The man, whose name Kael couldn't remember, nodded weakly. He raised his broken shield and slammed it against the Minotaur's flank. The beast snarled, turning its attention away from Gerald.

Kael watched, paralyzed. This wasn't heroism. This wasn't combat. It was bureaucracy manifested as physical force.

Gerald walked toward the Minotaur, stepping over the mud as if it were merely an inconvenience. He produced a pair of shears from his pocket. They were rusted, municipal-grade tools, not weapons.

"Stay still," Gerald said to the beast.

The Minotaur bucked. The ground shook. Dust rained down from the ceiling, which was rapidly developing spiderweb cracks.

"Gerald," Kael whispered. "We don't have time."

"We have one hundred and forty seconds," Gerald corrected. He moved with a terrifying, mechanical precision. He approached the Minotaur's left leg. The iron bands wrapped around the beast's shins were thick, blackened by heat.

"The iron," Gerald muttered. "It's acting as a tension rod. Bad practice. But useful."

He jammed the shears under the band.

The metal groaned. The Minotaur thrashed, its leg sweeping out.

Gerald didn't dodge, and he let the leg hit him.

The impact knocked the breath out of Kael. Gerald crumpled.

His ribs must have cracked.

He didn't scream, and he simply adjusted his grip on the clipboard, pulled the lever of the shears, and kept cutting.

**DING.**

A new window popped up in Kael's vision.

**CITATION DRAFTED: CORE #9942.** **CHARGE: UNSAFE LOAD-BEARING APPENDAGE.** **PROPOSED REMEDY: SEVERANCE.**

Gerald stood up, wiping blood from his lip. He looked at the severed section of the iron band, now hanging loose. He pulled it free with a grunt. The Minotaur screamed, a high-pitched sound of agony and rage, but its left leg went limp.

With the tension released, the ceiling above the beast groaned and lowered slightly.

"Move," Gerald commanded.

Kael didn't need to be told twice. He grabbed the man with the shield and dragged him toward the only opening that hadn't collapsed. The blue grid flared, narrowing the path, forcing them to squeeze through a gap that was barely shoulder-width.

**VIOLATION: OBSTRUCTED EGRESS.** **PENALTY: +10 DAMAGE TAKEN.**

The air around them burned. Kael felt his skin blister. He cried out, stumbling forward. The System was punishing them for the act of trying to escape.

Gerald walked behind them, calm as a librarian checking due dates. He stepped through the gap without flinching. The blue light washed over him, and he seemed to absorb the penalty, his posture stiffening just slightly.

"Keep moving," he said. "The library above is shifting its weight distribution. If we stop, the floor above us becomes a guillotine."

They emerged into a wider chamber. It was a storage room, filled with crates that had never opened. In the center of the room, a single lamp flickered.

Gerald stopped. He looked at the lamp. He looked at the cracks in the walls. He looked at the Minotaur, which was dragging itself through the narrow gap behind them, leaving a trail of blood and iron filings.

"Lighting," Gerald said. "Eight citations. I missed the emergency exit signs."

He pulled a small, glowing crystal from his belt. It was a standard dungeon core resource, meant to spawn mana crystals. Gerald crushed it in his hand.

The dust hung in the air. Then, with a sharp *ping*, the dust formed a perfect, illuminated arrow pointing to the north wall.

**SYSTEM NOTIFICATION: EMERGENCY SIGNAGE INSTALLED.** **COMPLIANCE RATING INCREASE: +2.** **DUNGEON INSTABILITY: STABILIZED.**

The shaking stopped. The dust settled. The Minotaur slumped against the wall, panting, its leg a ruin.

Kael slid down a crate, gasping for air. His heart hammered against his ribs like a trapped bird. He looked at Gerald. The inspector was already writing on his clipboard again, his face pale but unreadable.

"You killed it," Kael said.

"I cited it," Gerald corrected. "Termination is the penalty for self-sabotage. The Minotaur was part of the Core.

Cutting its leg was a repair. The Core couldn't handle the repair, so it died.

"

"You could have fought it."

"Fighting is inefficient. It generates debris. Debris violates sanitation codes."

Toby, the man with the shield, sat up. He looked at the dead beast, then at the glowing arrow.

"So... we're out?"

"No," Gerald said. He pointed to the north wall. "We are not out. We are in violation of occupancy limits."

Kael frowned. "Occupancy limits?"

"Three people in a room rated for zero," Gerald said.

He tapped the wall.

"This room is a storage closet. It has no ventilation.

You are breathing out carbon dioxide.

The System registers this as air quality degradation.

"

The air felt thick, heavy. Kael felt a headache spike behind his eyes.

**WARNING: OXYGEN DEPLETION IMMINENT.** **REMEDIATION REQUIRED: VENTILATION IMPROVEMENT.**

"How do we fix that?" Toby asked. "There are no windows."

Gerald looked at the crates. He looked at the ventilation ducts running along the ceiling. They were small, circular, and clogged with centuries of dust.

"We need a fan," Gerald said.

"We don't have a fan," Kael said.

"We have the Minotaur."

Kael stared at him.

"The Minotaur is dead."

"Its lungs are intact," Gerald said. He walked over to the corpse. "The System classifies biological entities with active respiratory functions as potential mechanical assets if repurposed correctly. I can issue a Conditional Permit for 'Biological Air Filtration.'"

"That's insane," Kael said.

"It's within the letter of the code," Gerald said. He opened his clipboard. A new page appeared.

**FORM 14-C: TEMPORARY USE OF BIOLOGICAL ASSETS FOR ENVIRONMENTAL CONTROL.** **APPLICANT: GERALD HUTCHINS (CORE #001).** **ASSET: CORPSE OF CORE ENTITY #9942 (MINOTAUR VARIANT).** **JUSTIFICATION: LIFE SAFETY.**

Gerald pressed his thumb to the paper.

The System paused. For a second, Kael thought Gerald had broken it. Then, a chime sounded.

**PERMIT APPROVED.** **WARNING: UNORTHODOX PRACTICE. INSPECTOR GENERAL NOTIFICATION PENDING.**

Gerald grabbed the Minotaur's jaw. He forced its mouth open. He inserted his hand into the beast's throat, ignoring the blood, ignoring the smell. He twisted, finding the trachea.

"Push," Gerald said to Toby.

Toby didn't understand.

"Push what?"

"Air. Push air into the lungs. Use the shield to pump the diaphragm."

Toby scrambled to his feet. He looked at Gerald, then at the dead beast. He placed the broken shield against the Minotaur's chest and pushed.

The beast's chest rose. A wheeze of air escaped its mouth, traveling up into the ducts.

The ducts creaked. The dust shifted.

Gerald moved to the other side of the room, and he grabbed the ventilation grate. He pried it open with his bare hands, nails tearing. The metal bent.

He pulled the grate down, exposing the dark shaft beyond.

"Keep pumping," Gerald ordered.

Toby pushed. The Minotaur's chest rose and fell. The air in the room circulated. The headache faded.

**SYSTEM NOTIFICATION: VENTILATION IMPROVED.** **COMPLIANCE RATING INCREASE: +5.** **TOTAL RATING: 54/100.**

Kael watched, horrified and awed. Gerald was using a dead monster's lungs to power an air conditioner. And it was working.

But then, the floor beneath them shuddered.

Not from the library above. From below.

A deep, resonant hum vibrated through the soles of Kael's boots. The crates rattled. The Minotaur's body twitched, though Gerald had stopped touching it.

Gerald froze. His pen stopped moving.

He looked at the floor. A crack appeared in the concrete. Then another.

"That's not the library," Gerald said. His voice was different now. Tighter.

"What is it?" Kael asked.

Gerald didn't answer. He walked to the center of the room. He stomped his foot. The crack widened.

Below them, something was digging.

"It's not the System," Gerald said. "It's external."

A hand punched through the floor, which was black, chitinous, and vast. It gripped the edge of the hole, tearing the concrete aside. Then a second hand. Then a face.

It was an insectoid head, segmented and clicking. It wore a helmet of polished brass. It looked at Gerald with compound eyes that reflected the flickering lamp.

"Inspector," the creature clicked. Its voice was like grinding stones. "You have filed an excessive number of reports."

Gerald straightened his spine. He tucked his clipboard under his arm.

"State your name and Core designation," Gerald said.

"I am Queen Vex of the Hive-7," the creature said. "You have cited three of my drone nests for noise pollution. You have cited my Queen's Chamber for lack of privacy screens. You have cited my brood pits for improper waste disposal."

The Queen pulled itself into the room. It was ten feet tall. Its legs clicked against the floor, cracking the remaining concrete.

"I am here to appeal," Queen Vex said. "But the hearing is cancelled. The penalty for repeated harassment of a superior Core is... removal."

Gerald looked at the Queen. He looked at Kael and Toby, who were pressed against the walls, terrified.

Gerald looked at his clipboard, and he turned the page.

**FORM 11-B: UNLICENSED BIOLOGICAL ENTITY.** **STATUS: PENDING.**

Gerald ripped the page out. He held it up.

"You are trespassing," Gerald said.

Queen Vex laughed, a wet, clicking sound.

"I am a dungeon core. I exist within the System."

"You are not registered in this jurisdiction," Gerald said. "Your dungeon footprint is located in Sector 4, Grid 9. This is Sector 1, Grid 1. You have no permit to cross district lines."

"I have a travel visa," Queen Vex said.

"Show it."

The Queen's eyes narrowed. She raised a claw, pointing at her chest. A badge glowed there, a symbol of the Hive-7.

Gerald squinted. He walked closer. He pulled a small magnifying glass from his pocket.

He inspected the badge. He inspected the seal. He inspected the date.

Then he looked up.

"Expired," Gerald said.

The Queen's claws twitched.

"It was renewed last week," she hissed.

"The System requires renewal within forty-eight hours of expiration," Gerald said.

"You filed your renewal form via the Hive Mind network.

That network went down on Tuesday. Your filing was never received.

Therefore, your visa is null.

"

**SYSTEM NOTIFICATION: UNLICENSED ENTITY DETECTED.** **STATUS: ILLEGAL.** **ACTION: DEPORTATION PROTOCOL INITIATED.**

The air around Queen Vex shimmerred. It wasn't the blue grid of the barrier. It was a gray, sucking vacuum.

The Queen screamed. She clawed at the walls, but her limbs passed through the air, dragging against nothing. The System was pulling her out.

"You cannot!" she shrieked. "I am powerful! I have armies!"

"You are out of code," Gerald said. He didn't watch her go. He watched the hole in the floor.

The Queen vanished. The vacuum closed. The room was silent again, save for the wheezing of the Minotaur's corpse.

Kael slid down the wall, his legs giving out.

"You... you just banished a dungeon core."

"I enforced compliance," Gerald said. He rubbed his chest.

His face was gray.

"That took out of me. My stamina is low."

Toby looked at the hole.

"What happens now?"

Gerald looked at the ceiling. The cracks were gone. The library above was safe.

"Now," Gerald said, "we have a problem."

He pointed to the floor. The hole where the Queen had entered was still there. But beneath it, in the darkness of the earth below, lights were flickering. Hundreds of them.

Small, red, bioluminescent dots.

"Deportation triggers a retaliation clause," Gerald said. "She didn't come alone. She came with a delegation."

Kael looked at the lights. They were moving. Climbing up the shaft.

"How many?" Toby asked.

Gerald counted the dots. His lips moved silently.

"Forty-two," Gerald said. "And they are all carrying citations for you."

Kael looked at Gerald.

"For us?"

"For the dungeon," Gerald said. "The Queen filed a complaint. She claims her drones were assaulted by 'unidentified hostile entities.' She named you and Toby as the perpetrators."

**SYSTEM NOTIFICATION: CIVIL SUIT FILED AGAINST OCCUPANTS: KAEL, TOBY.** **CLAIM: ASSAULT ON DUNGEON ASSETS.** **PENALTY: FORFEITURE OF LIVES AND LOOT.**

Kael stood up.

"What do we do?"

Gerald looked at his clipboard. He looked at the rising tide of red lights.

"We file a counter-claim," Gerald said.

"On what grounds?"

Gerald pointed to the Minotaur.

"The Queen's drones are armed. They are carrying weapons. In a residential zone, that is a Class C misdemeanor."

Gerald smiled. It was a thin, jagged thing.

"But to win the suit," Gerald said, "we need a witness. Someone who can attest to the fact that the Queen was out of code."

Kael looked at the dead Minotaur.

"The witness is dead."

"No," Gerald said. "The witness is the one who killed it."

Gerald looked at Toby.

"You," Gerald said. "You pushed the chest. You operated the ventilation. You are now a certified operator of biological assets."

Toby went pale.

"I don't know how to do that."

"You don't need to know," Gerald said. "You just need to sign the affidavit."

He held out the pen.

"If you sign, you admit to trespassing in the Hive-7. You admit to assaulting the Queen. You admit to destroying a Core entity."

"Why would I do that?" Toby asked.

"Because if you sign, the System recognizes you as a combatant in a legal dispute," Gerald said. "That grants you immunity from immediate deletion. It gives you time to fight."

"And if I don't?"

"Then the System deletes you for being an unlicensed occupant before the drones even reach the room."

The red lights were fifty feet away. Then forty.

Toby looked at Kael. Kael looked at Gerald.

Gerald held the pen steady. His hand did not shake.

"Three seconds," Gerald said.

Toby grabbed the pen. He signed.

The air in the room changed. The gray vacuum vanished, replaced by a golden aura around Toby and Kael.

**STATUS UPDATE: DEFENDANTS RECOGNIZED.** **TRIAL COMMENCING.**

The first drone burst through the hole. It was the size of a dog, covered in chitin and bristling with needles.

It stopped. It looked at the golden aura. It looked at Gerald.

Gerald didn't draw a weapon. He drew a fresh sheet of paper.

"Objection," Gerald said.

The drone tilted its head.

"Relevance," Gerald continued. "You are entering a courtroom zone. You are not wearing protective headgear. This is a safety violation."

The drone clicked. It moved forward.

Gerald stamped the paper.

**CITATION ISSUED.** **VIOLATION: UNSAFE ENTRY.** **PENALTY: STUN.**

The drone froze. Electricity arced across its body. It fell, twitching.

But behind it, the next drone was already climbing. And the next. And the next.

Gerald looked at Kael.

"They are coming in waves," Gerald said. "And I am out of ink."

Kael looked at the pen. Then he looked at the Minotaur's blood, pooling on the floor.

"Improvise," Kael said.

Gerald looked at the blood. He looked at his empty inkwell.

For the first time, Gerald Hutchins looked uncertain. He reached for the blood.

His hand hovered over the red pool. He didn't touch it. He pulled his hand back.

"I cannot," Gerald whispered. "Improvisation is against protocol."

The drones lunged.

Kael grabbed Gerald's hand and forced it into the blood.

"Write," Kael yelled.

Gerald dipped the pen. His face twisted in physical pain, his brow furrowing as if he were swallowing glass.

He touched the paper.

The ink spread. But it wasn't black. It was red. And it glowed.

**SYSTEM ERROR: UNAUTHORIZED MATERIAL USED.** **CITATION INVALID.**

The first drone bit Gerald's arm.

Screaming, Gerald fell back. Kael caught him.

Toby slammed the shield into the drone's head, cracking its shell.

"Fight!" Toby shouted. "Just fight!"

Gerald lay on the floor, clutching his arm. He looked at the red-inked citation. It was fading. The System was rejecting it.

But then, a new window appeared.

**ANALYZING SUBSTANCE...** **SUBSTANCE IDENTIFIED: CORE BIO-FLUID.** **NEW MECHANIC UNLOCKED: BLOOD CITAION.** **EFFECT: TARGETS BOUND BY BLOOD OATH.**

Gerald's eyes widened, and he looked at the red text. He looked at the approaching swarm.

He wrote.
