Chapter 406: Citing the Ground

# Chapter 406

The briefcase snapped shut.

Silas Vane did not blink. He did not breathe. His eyes, void of any sclera or iris, remained fixed on the flickering bulb in the storage room.

Gerald adjusted his tie. It was a knot he had tied thirty-one years ago, and it still held.

"Ten million mana points," Gerald said. "That is a significant sum. It exceeds the annual budget of the Parks and Recreation Department by a factor of four."

Vane's smile stretched too wide, tearing the skin at the corners of his mouth.

"It is an offer of peace, Gerald. The Consortium does not enjoy litigation. We prefer efficiency."

"Peace is a state of affairs," Gerald said. He picked up his clipboard.

It was a standard-issue municipal clipboard, green plastic, warped from humidity.

"Peace requires adherence to the municipal code. Specifically, Section 14-C regarding unlicensed biological entities and Section 22-B regarding unauthorized dimensional intrusions."

The air in the sub-basement grew cold. Frost traced the edges of the unfinished handrails.

"You are a bureaucrat," Vane said. "Bureaucrats can be bought."

"I have a pension," Gerald said. "It is vested. It is stable. It does not care about mana."

Vane stepped forward.

The dark energy from the briefcase leaked out, pooling on the concrete floor like spilled ink.

"Then we will take it.

Not your pension.

Your Compliance Rating.

"

Gerald frowned.

"My rating is 47."

"It will be zero," Vane promised. "And when it is zero, the System deletes you. It does not archive. It deletes."

The clock on the wall ticked. 11:59:30.

Gerald looked at the rat. The rat was gnawing on a piece of drywall. It was not helping. It was, however, technically an employee, though its job description was vague.

"Vane," Gerald said. "You opened the briefcase."

Vane paused. His oily hand hovered over the latch.

"Yes?"

"Did you file the necessary forms for importing a Class-5 Dark Artifact into a Zone 2 Commercial Residential Hybrid?"

Vane's head tilted. The motion was jerky, mechanical.

"No."

Gerald made a note on his clipboard.

"That is a violation of Subsection 9-A. Unauthorized Import of Hazardous Materials. I am issuing a Citation."

Vane laughed. It sounded like grinding gravel.

"You cannot cite me.

I am not a dungeon core.

I am a contractor.

"

"The System does not distinguish between the entity and the agent," Gerald said. He held up the clipboard. "The System recognizes the *intent* of the transaction.

You are attempting to alter the structural integrity of my Compliance Rating. That is a construction activity.

And you have no permit.

"

A blue window popped into Gerald's vision.

**[Citation Issued: Unauthorized Import of Dark Energy.]** **[Target: Silas Vane (Agent).]** **[Penalty: Immediate debuff to Agent Strength. Duration: Until Permit is Filed.]**

Vane flinched. The dark energy sputtered. The pulsing heart inside the briefcase slowed its beat.

"What..." Vane whispered. He looked at his hands. They were trembling. "What did you do?"

"I applied the code," Gerald said.

"You cannot!" Vane shouted. The smoothness was gone from his voice. "The Consortium has immunity clauses!"

"Immunity applies to acts of war," Gerald said. "This is an act of renovation. And you are breaking the building."

Vane roared. He kicked the briefcase open. The black heart erupted, sending a shockwave of necrotic energy across the room.

It hit the unfinished handrail.

The metal groaned but held.

Gerald did not move. He had reinforced that handrail with steel brackets last Tuesday. He had inspected the welds. He knew they would hold.

The energy dissipated. Vane was on his knees. The debuff was visible now; a grey aura clung to his suit, making his movements sluggish.

"Give me the permit," Gerald said.

"I have no permit!" Vane spat.

"Then you are working illegally." Gerald took a step forward. His shoes squeaked on the concrete. "I am shutting down the site."

Vane struggled to stand.

"You are not the Inspector General. You are a Level 1 Core."

"I am the Core of this site," Gerald said. "And the site is unsafe. Evacuate."

Vane screamed. He pulled a dagger from his sleeve. It was made of shadow and bone. He lunged.

Gerald raised his clipboard.

The dagger hit the green plastic. It shattered.

Vane stared at the clipboard. Then at Gerald. Then at the clipboard again.

"That is not magical," Vane whispered.

"It is high-density polyethylene," Gerald said. "It is rated for impact resistance up to fifty pounds per square inch. Your attack had a force of approximately thirty. It failed to meet the threshold for breaking the material."

Vane scrambled back. He reached for the heart in the briefcase.

"I will burn this place down!"

"Fire suppression is required," Gerald said. "You have not installed sprinklers. You have not provided an evacuation plan. You are in violation of the Fire Code, Section 3, Paragraph 1."

The air in the room shifted. The System recognized the violation. A warning light pulsed red in the corner of Gerald's vision.

**[Violation Detected: Lack of Fire Suppression in Presence of High-Combustion Source.]** **[Automatic Enforcement: Suppress Fire Source.]**

The heart in the briefcase sputtered. It smoked.

Vane panicked. He tried to close the briefcase, but the latch was jammed by the frost Gerald's cold presence had created.

"Fix it!" Vane yelled.

"I am not a mechanic," Gerald said. "I am an inspector."

He looked at the rat.

"Hey. Stop eating the drywall. Go to the corner.

There is a bucket.

"

The rat squeaked, confused, but it moved. It scurried to the leak in the corner and began drinking.

Gerald turned back to Vane.

"Your artifact is smoking. This is a smoke detector violation. Do you have a detector?"

Vane looked around wildly.

"No!"

"Then you are in violation of Life Safety Code 4.2." Gerald pointed a pen at Vane. "I am ordering you to cease all operations immediately."

The System acted.

A wall of force slammed into Vane. It was not a magical barrier. It was a *code enforcement barrier*. It was the physical manifestation of the rule *No Open Flames Without Supervision.*

Vane was pinned against the damp wall. His suit tore. Blood oozed from his mouth.

"Help me," Vane wheezed.

"Call your lawyer," Gerald said.

"I don't have a lawyer! I have clients!"

"Then you have a liability issue." Gerald checked his watch. 11:59:55. Five seconds.

Vane's eyes widened. He realized what was happening. The audit. It was here.

The air in the room changed. The pressure dropped. The frost on the handrails turned to water. The flickering light bulb burst.

In the sudden darkness, a voice spoke. It did not come from a mouth. It came from the walls, the floor, the ceiling. It was the voice of the System itself, translated into something Gerald could understand.

"Inspector Gerald Hutchins."

Gerald straightened his spine. He held his clipboard like a shield.

"Yes?"

"Your Compliance Rating is currently 47. You have three open violations: Ventilation, Egress Signage, and Fire Suppression."

Gerald nodded.

"Acknowledged. I am in the process of remediation."

"There is an external threat," the voice said. "Silas Vane. Agent of the Consortium. He is attempting to corrupt your core."

"He is attempting to perform unpermitted work," Gerald corrected.

"Correct," the voice said. "However, the Consortium has filed a grievance. They claim you are abusing your authority. They claim your citations are frivolous."

Gerald's heart beat faster. This was the Hearing.

"They are wrong," Gerald said.

"Prove it," the voice said. "The Hearing begins now. You have sixty seconds to demonstrate that your actions were procedurally sound."

Vane, pinned against the wall, laughed.

A wet, gurgling sound.

"You have to argue logic? Against the System?

You have to prove your bureaucracy was *correct*?

"

Gerald looked at Vane. He looked at the smoking heart. He looked at his unfinished handrails.

He had to prove that his petty rules were not just rules. They were survival.

"Vane," Gerald said. His voice was quiet.

Vane stopped laughing. He glared at Gerald.

"What?"

"Look at the heart," Gerald said.

Vane looked. The black heart was cracking. The dark energy was leaking out, forming tendrils that reached for the walls.

"It is unstable," Gerald said. "It is leaking radiation. If it breaches containment, it will destabilize the local mana field. This will cause a mana surge."

Vane's face went pale.

"No. No, the stabilization matrix holds."

"The stabilization matrix is missing," Gerald said. He pointed to the briefcase.

"You removed the stabilizer crystal to fit the heart in the case.

I saw the empty slot. Form 11-C, Subsection D. Removal of safety components is prohibited.

"

Vane shook his head.

"No. I didn't. I..."

"You did," Gerald said. "And now it is leaking. And the System will not let you fix it because you are not licensed. The System will purge the leak."

"Purge?" Vane's voice rose to a shriek.

"The System deletes non-compliant energy," Gerald said. "It will delete the heart. And it will delete the source of the heart. Which is you."

Vane screamed. He tried to rip the briefcase away from him, but the enforcement barrier held him tight.

Gerald stepped closer. He was not afraid. He was annoyed. The noise was disrupting his ability to focus on the details.

"Vane," Gerald said.

"Do you understand? You broke the container.

You broke the rules.

The rules break you.

"

The System's voice returned.

"Hearing in progress. Core Hutchins presents evidence: Structural instability of target artifact due to removal of safety components. Correlation to citation issued."

The air hummed. A beam of white light shot from the ceiling, hitting the briefcase.

Vane convulsed. His body faded. The dark energy was being scrubbed away, molecule by molecule.

"Wait!" Vane yelled. "I can pay! I can fix it! Let me appeal!"

"Appeals require a valid reason," Gerald said. "Removing safety components is not a valid reason. It is a hazard."

The light intensified. Vane's suit dissolved.

His skin turned grey, then translucent, and he looked at Gerald one last time.

His eyes were terrified. Not of death.

Of erasure.

"You... are... insane," Vane whispered.

"I am compliant," Gerald said.

Vane vanished.

The briefcase dropped to the floor. It popped open. The heart was gone. Only a pile of ash remained.

The enforcement barrier dissolved.

Gerald stood alone in the dark. The only light was the emergency exit sign he had installed yesterday. It cast a faint red glow on the wet concrete.

The System voice spoke again.

"Compliance Rating: 47. Status: Stable. Citation upheld. Agent Silas Vane terminated."

Gerald exhaled. His hands were shaking. He put the clipboard down on the desk. He rubbed his temples.

"Ventilation," Gerald muttered. "Still a problem."

He looked at the rat. The rat was still drinking from the leak.

"Hey," Gerald said. "Do you want a job?"

The rat looked up. It squeaked.

"Good," Gerald said. "You are now the official Ventilation Tester. Your duty is to detect air quality issues. I will pay you in cheese."

The System flashed a new message.

**[New Employee Hired: Rat (Level 1).]** **[Role: Air Quality Monitor.]** **[Bonus: +1 Passive Detection to Hazardous Gases.]**

Gerald nodded.

"That is acceptable."

He turned to the door. He needed to find the Inspector General. The audit was not over. The voice had said *Hearing in progress*, and it had not said *Hearing Complete*.

He opened the door.

Standing in the hallway was not the Inspector General, which was a woman. She wore a grey robe that seemed woven from starlight.

Her face was unreadable, her eyes the color of polished slate.

She held no clipboard. She held a sword.

The sword was made of glass.

"Gerald Hutchins," she said. Her voice was not the System's voice. It was human, but it carried the weight of centuries.

Gerald looked at her. He looked at the sword. He looked at the clock on the wall. It was 12:01.

The audit was supposed to be at noon, which was now.

"Who are you?" Gerald asked.

The woman raised the glass sword.

"I am the Inspector General. And you have a problem, Inspector."

Gerald blinked.

"I have citations. I have employees.

I have a leaking roof.

That is normal.

"

"No," the woman said. "You have attracted the attention of the Deep Core. By citing Vane, you have triggered a Class-4 Retaliation Event."

She pointed the sword at Gerald's chest.

"The Consortium is not done with you. And neither is the System."

The glass sword glowed.

Gerald looked at his clipboard. It was empty.

"What do you mean, the System is not done with me?" Gerald asked.

The Inspector General smiled. It was a sad, tired smile.

"You cited a Contractor. But the Contractor was hired by a Dungeon Lord.

And that Dungeon Lord is auditing you right now.

Not with words.

With fire."

The floor beneath Gerald's feet shook.

**[Warning: Incoming Projectile Detected. Source: Unidentified High-Tier Dungeon.]**

Gerald looked down. The concrete was cracking.

From the cracks, lava bubbled.

Gerald looked at the woman.

"I have not installed a fire suppression system."

The Inspector General lowered the sword.

"Then you are going to need to improvise, Inspector. The code does not cover volcanic eruptions."

Gerald stared at the lava. He stared at the woman. He stared at his clipboard.

He had never been asked to fight a volcano.

But he had been asked to check for heat sources, and he picked up the clipboard.

"Form 7-B," Gerald said. "Excessive Heat Generation. I am citing the ground."
