Chapter 96: Citation Void

# Chapter 96

The Inspector General was already three corridors in.

Gerald sensed it, like a draft before its source was visible, and a cold precision moving through his dungeon like a surveyor's laser.

Not hostile.

Not warm. Simply present. Simply measuring.

His rating had dropped from 53 to 48 in the time it took to process the notification.

The Inspector General did not spawn minions. It did not build traps. It did not grow corridors. It traversed Gerald's dungeon as Gerald had once traversed a condemned Elm Street apartment building, noting each code violation with identical blank attention.

A new notification appeared.

VIOLATION IDENTIFIED: Citation #47.

Target: Core Vennick. Status at time of service: IN TRANSITION (absorption window active).

Citation void.

Penalty: Unauthorized Damage via Invalid Enforcement Action. Severity: Critical.

Gerald read it twice. Then he pulled up Citation #47 from his records.

He remembered it. Three weeks ago.

Vennick was a mid-tier Core in the eastern district.

Gerald had cited it for improper load distribution in its spawn chambers, and the debuff had reduced Vennick's spawn regeneration by forty percent. Two of Vennick's elite spawns had died within the hour.

The absorption window. Gerald had never heard of it.

He checked the System documentation. It was there, buried in a sub-section of the Core Lifecycle Protocols he had never read because it applied to Cores in their final moments. Upon absorption, the losing Core persisted for roughly ninety seconds without being subject to enforcement. During that window, any Citation served to it was void.

Gerald had served Citation #47 during that window. He had not known. The System had not warned him.

The Inspector General moved on. Another notification.

VIOLATION IDENTIFIED: Citation #31. Target: Core Dunmora. Range check: Target's primary corridor extends 2.3 meters beyond Gerald's registered boundary.

Citation served outside registered range. Severity: Major.

Gerald felt the floor shudder. His rating: 44.

This one he had known about. He had cited Dunmora for a fire code violation and had calculated the range using Dunmora's outermost spawn point than its primary corridor. He had assumed the spawn point was the relevant boundary. The Inspector General was applying a stricter interpretation.

He could argue this. He had argued this before. The System's range definition was ambiguous in the published documentation. He had relied on the ambiguity.

But the Inspector General was not the System. It was the System's auditor. And auditors did not care about ambiguity. They cared about the strictest possible reading.

A new notification.

VIOLATION IDENTIFIED: Citation #12. Target: Core Fenn. Citation frequency exceeds threshold for single-target enforcement within a 30-day period. Severity: Major.

Gerald had cited Fenn four times in twenty-six days.

Each Citation was valid. Each was for a different violation. But the frequency itself was a violation.

A rule he had never known existed.

His rating: 40, and the Inspector General was not looking at Gerald's dungeon. It was looking at Gerald's history.

Every Citation he had ever issued.

Every range calculation. Every target selection. It was auditing his enforcement record, not his structure.

Gerald opened the argument field. He typed carefully.

"Requesting clarification on Citation #31. The published range definition states: 'The Core's registered boundary is determined by the outermost point of active spawn activity.' Citation #31 was served to Core Dunmora based on its outermost spawn point at coordinates 47.2, 12.8. This point was within my registered range. The Inspector General's interpretation uses primary corridor extent, which is not the definition provided in the published documentation."

The Inspector General paused. The cold precision stopped moving.

A response appeared.

CLARIFICATION PROVIDED. Section 14.7(b) of the Core Enforcement Addendum (unpublished) defines registered boundary as the outermost extent of primary corridor structure. Section 14.7(b) supersedes the published definition in Section 9.2 for purposes of Citation range verification.

Unpublished. The rule was unpublished.

Gerald stared at the notification. The Core Enforcement Addendum.

He had never seen it, and he had never heard of it.

The Inspector General was applying rules that had never been served to him.

He typed again.

"Section 14.7(b) is unpublished. It has not been served to Core Gerald Hutchins. Under the System's own Service Requirements Protocol (Section 3.1, published), no compliance obligation attaches to a Core until the relevant regulation has been formally served.

Section 14.7(b) has not been served. Therefore it cannot be applied retroactively to Citation #31."

The Inspector General was silent for eleven seconds.

RESPONSE: Service Requirements Protocol (Section 3.1) applies to Core-to-Core enforcement. It does not apply to Inspector General audits. Inspector General audits operate under Section 1.4 of the Audit Authority Charter (unpublished), which grants retroactive application of all regulations regardless of service status.

Gerald felt something cold move through his dungeon walls.

Not the Inspector General. Something else. A pressure building in the floor.

His rating: 37, and he had one move left. He knew he had one move left because he had been writing citations for thirty-one years and he had never once seen a rule that did not contain a flaw.

Every rule had a loophole.

Every loophole had a rule that closed it. And every closing rule had its own flaw.

He typed.

"Section 1.4 of the Audit Authority Charter grants retroactive application of regulations. Section 1.4 is itself a regulation.

It has not been served to Core Gerald Hutchins.

Therefore, under the same logic the Inspector General applied to Section 14.7(b), Section 1.4 cannot be applied to Core Gerald Hutchins.

The Inspector General is attempting to apply an unserved regulation to justify the application of another unserved regulation. This is circular and invalid."

The silence this time was longer. Twenty-three seconds.

Gerald's dungeon held its breath.

RESPONSE: Accepted. Section 1.4 is unserved. Audit Authority Charter Section 1.4 cannot be applied to Core Gerald Hutchins.

Citation #31 is reinstated as valid. Citation #12 is reinstated as valid.

Citation #47 remains void due to Target Status violation (Section 14.7(b) is not required for this determination; Target Status is defined in published Section 22.1).

Gerald's rating: 43, and the Inspector General was still in his dungeon. Still measuring. Still finding.

But Gerald had found the crack. The Inspector General could not apply unpublished rules to him. Every unpublished rule was now a shield.

Gerald had read the published documentation cover to cover, and he knew every published section by number. He had spent thirty-one years knowing regulations by number.

He typed again.

"Requesting that all remaining audit findings be limited to published regulations only. Any finding based on unpublished material must be accompanied by proof of service to Core Gerald Hutchins."

RESPONSE: Accepted. Audit scope limited to published regulations.

The cold precision resumed its movement. Slower now. More careful. The Inspector General was recalibrating.

Gerald watched his rating stabilize. 43.

Not safe. Not close to dissolution threshold, which he now knew was 10. But not comfortable.

And the Inspector General was still finding things.

VIOLATION IDENTIFIED: Citation #8.

Target: Core Pell.

Citation served during Pell's mandatory maintenance window (published Section 19.3). Maintenance windows are enforcement-immune periods.

Severity: Major.

Gerald felt the floor drop a centimeter. Rating: 39.

He had cited Core Pell during a maintenance window. Section 19.3 was published.

He had read it, and he had simply not cross-referenced it against his Citation log. It was an oversight. A real one.

Not a loophole.

Not a technicality. A genuine failure of his own compliance.

The Inspector General moved to the next corridor.

Gerald followed it with his awareness, and he could feel it checking his handrails. His ventilation. His egress signage.

His dungeon was clean. His dungeon was up to code.

But his record was not.

VIOLATION IDENTIFIED: Citation #22.

Target: Core Vassik. Citation range calculation used spherical radius than Manhattan distance.

Published Section 9.2 specifies Manhattan distance. Severity: Major.

Rating: 35.

Gerald's hands were shaking. He had used spherical distance for Citation #22.

He had assumed spherical.

The System used Manhattan. He had never checked because he had never needed to. His Citations had always been close enough that the difference did not matter.

It mattered now.

VIOLATION IDENTIFIED: Citation #39.

Target: Core Lumen. Citation served to a Core whose Compliance Rating was below 5.

Cores below Rating 5 are in System custody and cannot be served Citations (published Section 27.4).

Severity: Critical.

Rating: 28.

Gerald remembered Lumen. He remembered it.

It had been a small Core.

A weak Core.

He had cited it for a ventilation violation because the violation was obvious and he had not known the target was below Rating 5. He had not checked.

He had never checked.

The Inspector General paused. A new notification appeared.

AUDIT PROGRESS: 7 of 52 Citations reviewed. Current penalty trajectory projects final rating: 14. Dissolution threshold: 10. Core Gerald Hutchins is 4 points from dissolution.

Gerald looked at the number. Four points.

He had issued fifty-two Citations.

Forty-five of them remained unreviewed. The Inspector General was finding violations in seven out of seven. The pattern was consistent.

Every Citation he had ever issued contained at least one technical flaw.

He had been sloppy. Thirty-one years of municipal inspection had taught him precision, but the System's rules were not municipal rules.

They were stranger.

More specific. More unforgiving. And he had been writing Citations for two years without ever cross-referencing the full body of published enforcement law.

He had been too confident. Too sure that his method was sound.

The Inspector General moved to Citation #9.

Gerald opened the argument field. His fingers were steady now.

The shaking had stopped.

What replaced it was worse.

The specific, cold clarity of a man who has found the error in his own work and knows exactly how much it will cost him.

He typed.

"I am filing a voluntary suspension of all active Citations pending completion of this audit. Under published Section 41.2, a Core may voluntarily suspend its enforcement authority to demonstrate good faith compliance. Suspension takes effect immediately upon filing."

The Inspector General stopped.

RESPONSE: Voluntary suspension accepted. Active Citations: 0. Core Gerald Hutchins's enforcement authority is suspended pending audit completion.

The notification was followed by another.

AUDIT SCOPE UPDATED. With enforcement authority suspended, remaining Citations are classified as historical records than active enforcement actions. Historical records are subject to audit under published Section 44.1, which applies a reduced penalty scale (50% of standard severity).

Gerald's rating ticked up. 28 to 31. The remaining forty-five Citations would be judged at half severity.

The Inspector General resumed its movement. Slower. More careful. But still finding things.

Gerald watched. He could not stop it. He could only watch and calculate and hope that the reduced scale was enough.

Then the floor beneath the Inspector General opened.

Not his floor. The Inspector General's. A corridor forming beneath the auditor itself. Going down.

A notification appeared.

NOTICE: Secondary party in this audit (Inspector General) has received a Counter-Citation. Citation source: Core Vennick. Citation #47 reinstated by Inspector General ruling is being appealed by Core Vennick under published Section 15.3 (Appeal of Void Citation Reinstatement). Hearing initiated.

Gerald stared.

Core Vennick was appealing the reinstatement of Citation #47. The same Citation the Inspector General had just declared void, and the same Citation that had been void because Vennick was in an absorption window.

If the appeal succeeded, Citation #47 would be void again. And if it was void again, then the Inspector General's ruling that reinstated it was itself an error. And an error by the Inspector General during an audit was not a violation of Gerald's compliance.

It was a violation of the Inspector General's compliance.

Gerald looked at the notification again.

HEARING INITIATED. Parties: Inspector General (appellant) vs. Core Vennick (respondent). Gerald Hutchins is listed as a third party with standing.

Gerald was being dragged into the hearing. Not as a participant. As evidence.

His Citation #47 was the subject. His actions were the reason the Inspector General had made an error.

And if the Inspector General lost the hearing, the error would be logged.

And a logged error by the Inspector General would trigger a Reciprocal Audit.

The Inspector General would be audited. And if the Inspector General failed, it would be dissolved.

And Gerald, as the third party whose actions caused the error, would be named as the source of the Inspector General's failure.

The notification appeared.

THIRD PARTY LIABILITY NOTICE: If Inspector General is dissolved due to error caused by third party, third party assumes Inspector General's remaining audit obligations. Core Gerald Hutchins will assume the Inspector General's incomplete audit of all registered Cores in the Eastern District.

Gerald read it. He read it again.

The Inspector General had an incomplete audit of every Core in the Eastern District. Fourteen Cores. Fourteen full audits. If the Inspector General was dissolved because of Gerald's Citation #47, Gerald would have to complete those audits himself.

With his enforcement authority suspended.

With a rating of 31.

Against fourteen Cores who would know exactly what had happened.

He needed all fourteen of them to sign.
