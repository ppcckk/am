Chapter 58: Voidable Enforcement

# Chapter 58

Gerald had found it at minute forty-three of his one-cycle window. He almost missed it. Almost.

The System's procedural documentation was three thousand pages long. He had read it once, cover to cover, in the second cycle after the Audit notice. He had read it again after the first Hearing. He was reading it for the third time when the footnote on page 2,417 caught him.

*Section 11.4(c): All enforcement actions undertaken by the Inspector General shall comply with the procedural requirements set forth in Sections 9 through 11 of this document. Non-compliance renders the enforcement action voidable.*

Gerald stared at it. He read it again.

Then he read Section 9.

Then Section 10. Then Section 11. Then he went back to 11.4(c).

The Inspector General had to follow its own rules, and he checked the Hearing notice he had received.

It listed a Hearing window. It listed a countdown.

It listed his current Compliance Rating and the projected post-Hearing Rating.

What it did not list was a Statement of Charges. What it did not list was a Basis of Authority. What it did not list was a Notice of Right to Appeal, which Section 10.2 required for any enforcement action carrying a Rating penalty above twenty points.

The drop from seventy-eight to thirty-eight was forty points.

Gerald opened a new document. He typed: *Citation.

Target: Inspector General.

Violation: Procedural Non-Compliance, Section 11.4(c).

Failure to provide Statement of Charges. Failure to provide Basis of Authority.

Failure to provide Notice of Right to Appeal per Section 10.2.*

He looked at the Citation form. It required the target to be physically present for service.

The Hearing. The Inspector General would be physically present at the Hearing.

Gerald printed the Citation. He folded it. He put it in his pocket.

The countdown hit zero, and the world went white. Not metaphorically. The walls of his dungeon dissolved into a flat grey plane that extended in every direction, and Gerald stood on it in the body he had not had for thirty-one years but had somehow regrown, because the System did not care about biology. It cared about compliance.

Across the plane stood a figure. It was tall.

It wore a coat that was too long and a tie that was too short.

It held a clipboard. The clipboard was the size of a door.

"Core 4401," the Inspector General said.

Its voice sounded like a filing cabinet closing. "You are before this body for review of your Citation Filing Threshold.

You have filed forty-seven Citations in the current cycle, and the System threshold is thirty. You are seventeen over. You understand the consequence."

"I understand the consequence," Gerald said. "I do not understand the authority."

The Inspector General looked at him. The look was not hostile. It was the look of a bureaucrat who has been asked to explain a form to someone who has already filled it out correctly.

"The threshold is established in Section 7.2 of the Enforcement Code. You have read the Enforcement Code."

"I have read it three times."

"Then you know the threshold exists."

"I know the threshold exists. I do not know why it was applied to me without notice."

The Inspector General's clipboard shifted. A new page appeared. "The threshold is a standing provision. It does not require individual notice."

"Section 10.2," Gerald said. "Any enforcement action that will reduce a Core's Compliance Rating by more than twenty points requires advance notice containing three elements.

A Statement of Charges.

A Basis of Authority.

A Notice of Right to Appeal. I received none of them."

The Inspector General was silent for a moment. The grey plane hummed. Somewhere in the distance, Gerald could hear the faint sound of his rat squeaking.

"Section 10.2 applies to enforcement actions initiated by the Inspector General against a Core," the Inspector General said. "The threshold is not an enforcement action. It is a systemic parameter."

"Section 11.4(c)," Gerald said. "All enforcement actions undertaken by the Inspector General shall comply with the procedural requirements set forth in Sections 9 through 11. You are undertaking an enforcement action.

You are reducing my Rating by forty points. You did not provide the required notice.

Therefore the action is voidable."

The Inspector General's clipboard shifted again. Another page appeared. Then another.

The figure's expression did not change, but something in its posture did.

It was the posture of a bureaucrat who has just realized that the person across the table has read the same memo.

"You are arguing that the threshold itself is voidable," the Inspector General said.

"I am arguing that the application of the threshold to me, in this instance, without notice, is voidable. I am not arguing the threshold does not exist. I am arguing you cannot apply it to me without following your own procedure."

The Inspector General set the clipboard down. It picked it up again. It set it down.

"Core 4401. You are correct."

Gerald felt something in his chest loosen. It was not relief. It offered the quiet satisfaction of being right beyond dispute.

"The threshold stands as a systemic parameter," the Inspector General continued.

"Your Rating will not drop to thirty-eight. However, you remain seventeen Citations over the threshold. The threshold does not require notice to exist.

It requires notice to be applied. You are currently over the threshold.

I will apply it at the end of the next cycle with proper notice.

You will have one cycle to file an appeal.

Do you understand?"

"I understand."

"The Hearing is concluded."

The grey plane began to dissolve. Gerald's dungeon reassembled around him, and the rat was where he had left it, sitting on a handrail, looking mildly concerned.

Gerald stood in his sub-basement. He reached into his pocket. He pulled out the Citation.

He had prepared it before the Hearing. He prepared it because he always knew a bureaucrat would find a procedural error and stop. He had prepared it because Gerald Hutchins did not stop. Gerald Hutchins filed the paperwork.

The Inspector General had been physically present at the Hearing. The Citation was valid.

He held it up. The System recognized the target. The text glowed faintly.

*Citation served.*

A sound came from above. Not from his dungeon. From everywhere. A deep, resonant tone, like a building settling into its foundation.

Gerald's Compliance Rating flickered. Seventy-eight. It stayed at seventy-eight.

He looked up. The ceiling of his dungeon was transparent. He could see the library above him.

He could see the floor of the library, and beneath the floor, visible only to him now, he could see the Inspector General.

It was standing in the grey plane. It was looking at him. Its clipboard was open. Its Compliance Rating was visible.

It had not had one before. Gerald had never seen it. He had not known it had one.

The Inspector General's Rating was sixty-one.

Gerald had cited it for three procedural violations. The debuff was proportional. Three violations, moderate severity. A fifteen-point reduction.

Sixty-one.

The Inspector General looked at Gerald. Gerald looked at the Inspector General.

Then the Inspector General did something Gerald had never seen it do. It smiled.

"Core 4401," it said. "You have just cited the Inspector General."

"I have."

"Do you know what happens when a Core cites the Inspector General?"

Gerald thought about this. He thought about Section 7.2. He thought about Section 11.4(c). He thought about the Enforcement Code, which he had read three times, and which did not contain a sentence about what happened when the Inspector General was cited.

"No," Gerald said. "I do not."

The Inspector General closed its clipboard.

"Neither do I," it said. "This has never happened before."

The grey plane went white.

Gerald was back in his dungeon. The rat was gone. The handrail was gone.

In their place, a new notification hung in the air, written in a font he had never seen.

*System-wide event triggered. Inspector General Compliance Rating: 61/100.

All pending audits suspended.

All pending Hearings suspended. All pending Citations frozen. Core 4401: you are now the highest-rated Core in the active audit queue.

You have been assigned the role of Interim Inspector General. Your first audit is scheduled in one cycle.

Target: all Cores currently below Rating 50.

You have forty-three targets.*

Gerald read it twice.

Forty-three targets, and he had been citing dungeons for a cycle and a half. He had cited twenty-three.

There were more. There had always been more. And now the System was asking him to cite all of them.

He looked at the rat's handrail. The rat had not come back.

He looked at the notification. He looked at his Compliance Rating. Seventy-eight. The highest he had ever been.

He opened a new document. He typed: *Audit Preparation.

Target list: forty-three Cores.

Estimated citations: two hundred and sixty. Estimated time: approximately four cycles at current filing rate.*

He stared at the number.

Two hundred and sixty Citations. He had never filed more than three in a cycle.

The threshold was thirty, and he was already seventeen over.

He would be two hundred and seventy-seven over.

The threshold applied to all Cores equally.

Gerald looked at the ceiling. Somewhere above him, the library was quiet. Somewhere below the library, forty-three dungeon cores were building things that did not meet code.

He picked up his clipboard and typed the line he had been avoiding.
