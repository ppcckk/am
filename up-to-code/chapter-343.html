Chapter 343: Rogue Core in Walls

# Chapter 343

The woman in the grey cardigan did not understand why the streetlights were flickering. She held a plastic bag of groceries in one hand and her phone in the other, her thumb hovering over the emergency dial.

The air in Ashford Square tasted like copper and wet plaster.

Around her, the buildings groaned. Not the settling sounds of old masonry, but a deep, structural shudder that vibrated through the soles of her shoes.

She looked up at the municipal library. The windows were dark, except for the sub-basement, which glowed with a sickly, pulsating blue light.

A man in a hard hat stood on the sidewalk, holding a clipboard. He wasn't a construction worker. He was too short, too bald, and his eyes were too wide. He stared at the cracks forming in the pavement like a man watching a crime scene.

"Is the building coming down?" the woman asked.

The man didn't answer, and he tapped his pen against the clipboard. Tap.

Tap.

Tap.

Another crack split the asphalt near her feet. A chunk of concrete popped up, revealing rebar that twisted upward like black fingers. The woman stepped back, gasping. The man stepped forward.

"Stay back," the man said. His voice was flat, devoid of urgency. "The expansion joint is failing."

"The what?"

"The expansion joint," he repeated. He pointed his pen at the library's foundation. "It is not designed for lateral shear stress of this magnitude. If you step on the yellow tape, you will be cited for trespassing on a restricted construction zone."

The woman looked at the tape. It was bright yellow. It wasn't police tape. It was caution tape with small, precise icons of a measuring tape and a level.

"Who are you?" she asked.

"Inspector," the man said. "Gerald Hutchins."

He turned his back on her. He walked toward the library entrance, which had buckled inward.

The door was jammed, the frame warped by an invisible force.

Gerald didn't try to open it, and he pulled a laser measure from his pocket. He pointed it at the gap between the door and the frame.

He frowned.

"This is non-compliant," he muttered.

The building shook again. A shard of glass fell from the second story. It shattered on the pavement inches from his shoes.

He didn't flinch. He just wrote a note.

***

Gerald Hutchins felt the vibration in his core.

Not in his chest. In the walls. In the plumbing.

In the concrete that formed his being.

He stood in the center of the sub-basement, now a cavernous void where the library's storage room had once been. The blue light emanating from his core pulsed in rhythm with the city's heartbeat. Outside, he could feel the weight of the buildings above him. They were not just structures; they were extensions of his code.

And they were failing.

The giant's threat had triggered a cascade failure. By reinforcing the load-bearing surface under his feet, Gerald had altered the stress distribution of the entire municipal district. The System, in its infinite bureaucratic wisdom, had interpreted this as a localized seismic event.

**[ALERT: STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY COMPROMISED ACROSS ZONE 4.]** **[VIOLATIONS DETECTED: 142.]** **[CURRENT COMPLIANCE RATING: 78/100.]**

Gerald looked at the number. It was a disaster.

A 78 was a failing grade, which meant his spawn rates were dropping, his traps were misfiring, and his defenses were weakening. But worse, the citation radius was no longer limited to the library. The System had registered the seismic event as a city-wide infrastructure crisis.

Every building in Ashford was now within his jurisdiction.

He could feel them. The post office.

The bank.

The elementary school.

He could feel the hairline fractures in their foundations, the loose wiring in their panels, the blocked fire exits in their stairwells.

It was a sensory overload. A migraine of code violations.

Gerald rubbed his temples. His headache was physical. The System was forcing him to acknowledge every flaw.

"Focus," he said aloud.

The Auditor stood nearby, her form shimmering like heat haze. She watched him with an expression of detached curiosity.

"You are breaking the city," she said.

"I am identifying its weaknesses," Gerald corrected.

"And if you cite them all? The System will collapse under the weight of the paperwork."

"The System likes paperwork," Gerald said. He pulled a fresh citation pad from thin air. The pad was thick, bound in red leather. "It thrives on it."

He looked at the post office, three blocks east. He could feel its roof sagging under the weight of snow that had never fallen. A phantom load.

The System was testing him. If he ignored it, his rating would drop further.

If he cited it, he would be claiming jurisdiction over a federal building.

But federal buildings were not exempt from municipal code. Not if they were part of the dungeon's footprint.

Gerald stepped toward the wall. He placed his hand on the concrete. He could feel the post office's ventilation shaft.

It was clogged.

A bird's nest, likely.

**[VIOLATION: OBSTRUCTED AIR INTAKE.]** **[SEVERITY: MINOR.]** **[PENALTY: 5 POINTS TO TARGET CORE, 2 POINTS TO INSPECTOR FOR SUCCESSFUL ENFORCEMENT.]**

It was small. But it was a start.

He wrote it.

**CITATION: 343-A** **TARGET: UNITED STATES POSTAL SERVICE, BRANCH 402** **VIOLATION: INADEQUATE VENTILATION PER SECTION 12.4 B.** **SERVED: IN PERSON, VIA SEISMIC RESONANCE.**

The System accepted it.

The penalty applied instantly. Somewhere in the city, a postal worker felt a sudden chill as the air in the break room stagnated. Gerald felt a small surge of energy.

His Compliance Rating ticked up to 79, which was working.

But it was slow. He had 142 violations. He couldn't write them all by hand. He needed a loophole.

He looked at the Auditor.

"Can I delegate?" he asked.

The Auditor raised an eyebrow.

"To whom?"

"To my spawns."

She laughed. It was a dry, rasping sound.

"You have three mold spores and a rat, Gerald. They are not licensed inspectors."

"I have a new spawn," Gerald said.

He hadn't.

Not yet.

But he was thinking. The System allowed Cores to spawn entities based on available resources.

What resource did he have now?

Authority, and he had 142 citations. Each one generated a small amount of *Bureaucratic Energy*. It was a new stat, introduced in the last chapter. He could spend it.

Gerald closed his eyes. He reached out with his mind, feeling the flow of energy from the citations he'd already served. He pooled it. It felt like hot coffee in his veins.

**[RESOURCES: BUREAUCRATIC ENERGY x 142.]** **[ACTION: SPAWN ENTITY.]** **[COST: 50 ENERGY.]** **[ENTITY TYPE: ADMINISTRATIVE CONSTRUCT.]**

He hesitated. The System usually spawned monsters.

Goblins.

Slimes. Things with teeth.

But Gerald didn't need teeth. He needed a pen.

**[CONFIRM?]

**[YES.]**

The air in the sub-basement thickened.

Dust motes swirled, forming a shape. It wasn't a monster.

It was a humanoid figure, tall and thin, made of stacked paper and binder clips.

Its face was a blank sheet of parchment. Its hands were oversized fountain pens.

**[ENTITY CREATED: CLERK GRADE 1.]** **[ATTRIBUTE: PUNISHMENT.]** **[ATTRIBUTE: PROCEDURE.]** **[ATTRIBUTE: NO PHYSICAL FORM.]**

The Clerk stood there, twitching. It looked around the ruin, then at Gerald.

"Order," the Clerk said. Its voice sounded like pages turning.

Gerald pointed at the wall.

"Go to the post office.

Cite the ventilation.

Then the bank.

Then the school.

"

The Clerk nodded. It dissolved into a cloud of paper, which swept through the cracks in the library's foundation and out into the city.

Gerald watched it go. He felt a drain on his energy, but also a sense of satisfaction. It was like watching his first day on the job, thirty years ago.

But the Auditor was frowning.

"You are creating a network," she said. "That is not standard protocol."

"It is a staffing issue," Gerald said. "I am overwhelmed. The System requires me to enforce code. I am providing the workforce."

"The Inspector General will not like this."

"The Inspector General likes results," Gerald said. "And I am getting results."

He checked his rating. 81, which was climbing.

But then, a new alert flashed in his vision. Red. Urgent.

**[ALERT: UNAUTHORIZED ENTRY.]** **[LOCATION: CITY HALL, SUB-LEVEL 3.]** **[SOURCE: UNKNOWN.]** **[THREAT LEVEL: HIGH.]**

Gerald froze. City Hall? That was three miles away. How could something be there?

He looked at the Auditor.

"Is this part of the audit?"

"No," she said. She looked unsettled. For the first time, her form flickered with uncertainty. "This is not in the script."

Gerald felt a tug in his core. It was a wrong feeling. A violation.

Not a code violation, but a structural one. Someone was drilling into the city's foundation.

Not with a drill.

With magic, and he focused his senses. He could feel it. A sharp, jagged pain in the earth beneath City Hall. It was piercing the bedrock.

"Who is that?" Gerald asked.

The Auditor looked at the ceiling of the ruin.

"He is a Core," she said. "But he is not registered."

"Unregistered Cores don't exist."

"Unregistered *rogue* Cores do," she said. "They operate in the blind spots. They use municipal infrastructure as a shield."

Gerald understood. Someone was using the city's buildings to hide their dungeon. They were tapping into the power grid, the water main, the sewers. They were making the city part of their dungeon.

And they were damaging it.

"Where is he?" Gerald asked.

"City Hall," the Auditor said. "He is trying to rewrite the foundations. He is turning the building into a fortress."

Gerald looked at his spawn list. The Clerk was busy. The rat was hiding. The mold spores were useless.

He needed to move, and he couldn't leave the library. If he left, the seismic instability would worsen. The library would collapse. And if the library collapsed, his dungeon would be destroyed.

But he had to stop this rogue Core. If he let him rewrite the foundations, he could change the code of the entire city. He could turn Ashford into his own dungeon.

Gerald looked at the Clerk, who was currently citing a parking meter in downtown Ashford.

"Recall the Clerk," Gerald said.

**[RECALLING CLERK... SUCCESS.]** **[ENERGY COST: 10.]**

The paper cloud rushed back through the cracks. It reformed in front of Gerald.

"New mission," Gerald said.

"Go to City Hall. Do not fight.

Do not attack.

Just serve this.

"

He wrote a citation on a blank page.

**CITATION: 344-A** **TARGET: UNKNOWN CORE** **VIOLATION: UNAUTHORIZED MODIFICATION OF PUBLIC INFRASTRUCTURE.** **NOTE: THIS IS NOT A COMPLIANCE ISSUE. IT IS A CRIMINAL ACT.**

The Clerk looked at the citation. Its parchment face remained blank.

"Serve it," Gerald said.

The Clerk vanished.

Gerald waited. He felt the energy drain from his core. He felt the connection to the city weaken.

Then, a scream echoed in his mind.

It wasn't a human scream. It was the sound of metal tearing.

**[CITATION SERVED.]** **[TARGET REACTS: HOSTILE.]** **[WARNING: TARGET IS ATTACKING THE CITATION SOURCE.]**

The source was the Clerk. But the Clerk was an extension of Gerald.

Gerald felt a sharp pain in his left arm. He looked down. His arm was cracking. Like dry mud.

The rogue Core was attacking him through the citation. He was using the legal link to send a physical backlash.

**[SYSTEM ERROR: ILLEGAL ATTACK VIA LEGAL CHANNEL.]** **[APPEAL WINDOW OPEN: 10 SECONDS.]**

Gerald gasped. He couldn't breathe. His chest was tightening. The Auditor was shouting something, but her voice was distant.

He had ten seconds to file an appeal.

If he appealed, he could stop the attack. But appeals required a Hearing. A Hearing meant he had to argue structural logic with the rogue Core.

And he was in the middle of a city-wide compliance crisis.

If he stopped to argue, the city would fall.

If he didn't appeal, his arm would shatter. And if his arm shattered, he might lose the ability to write citations.

Gerald looked at his arm. The cracks were spreading.

The Auditor leaned in.

"Do not appeal," she whispered. "If you appeal, you acknowledge their authority. You must override."

"Override?" Gerald choked out.

"Use the Inspector General's authority," she said. "Claim emergency jurisdiction."

Gerald didn't have emergency jurisdiction. The Inspector General had to grant it.

**[EMERGENCY JURISDICTION REQUEST?]** **[WARNING: THIS WILL INVITE THE INSPECTOR GENERAL TO YOUR LOCATION IMMEDIATELY.]**

Gerald hesitated, and the Inspector General was not a person. It was a force. A pure, unyielding force of order. If it came here, it might shut down Gerald's dungeon for being too chaotic.

But the rogue Core was already winning.

The pain in his arm intensified. The cracks reached his shoulder.

Gerald gritted his teeth. He raised his good hand. He pointed at the wall.

"Requesting emergency jurisdiction," he said. "Under Article 9, Section 3: Imminent Threat to Public Safety."

The System paused.

The blue light in the room turned white.

A voice boomed, not from the air, but from the walls themselves.

**[JURISDICTION GRANTED.]** **[INSPECTOR GENERAL DISPATCHING.]** **[ETA: 3 SECONDS.]**

Gerald looked at his arm. The cracks stopped spreading.

The Auditor smiled. It was a cold, terrifying smile.

"You have just invited the wolf to the sheep pen," she said.

The wall behind Gerald exploded.

Not from the outside. From the inside.

A hand, made of stone and glowing runes, burst through the concrete. It grabbed Gerald by the throat.

The rogue Core was not at City Hall, and he was in the library.

He had been hiding in the walls all along.

Gerald choked. The stone fingers tightened.

The Inspector General was arriving. But the rogue Core was already there.

And he was holding Gerald.
