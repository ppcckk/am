Chapter 221: Compliance Override

# Chapter 221

The clipboard cracked against the rat's skull.

Bone shattered. Ash scattered.

The Pyro-Morphic Rodent stumbled back, smoke pouring from its nostrils, but it did not die.

It hissed, a sound like tearing metal, and its eyes flared with renewed intensity. Gerald's hand shook, not from fear, but from the sudden, violent realization that his primary tool—the Form 11-B citation—had just failed.

A voice boomed across the sub-basement, distorted by heat and static.

"Stand down, Core 47-Alpha."

Gerald looked up.

The shadow in the corner coalesced into a figure. It was tall, wearing the grey suit of a mid-level bureaucrat, but its head was a floating geometric shape, a rotating tetrahedron of obsidian.

This was not the Inspector General.

This was worse. This was a Regional Compliance Officer.

"You are out of order," the officer said. Its voice sounded like grinding gears.

Gerald straightened his tie. He tucked the broken clipboard under his arm. "I am addressing an unlicensed biological entity that has breached the structural integrity of Zone 4."

"The entity was contained within the thermal vent system prior to your intervention," the officer replied. It floated closer, its tetrahedron rotating faster. "Your use of a non-compliant blunt instrument caused collateral damage to the ventilation shaft. You have violated Section 8, Subsection C: Improper Tool Usage in Hazardous Environments."

Gerald blinked. "My clipboard is standard issue."

"Your clipboard is broken," the officer noted. "And you struck the entity.

Striking constitutes combat.

You are not authorized for combat.

You are authorized for inspection."

The rat lunged again. Gerald sidestepped. The rat slammed into a support pillar, leaving a scorch mark.

The pillar groaned. Dust rained down from the ceiling.

"If you do not neutralize that rodent, the structural load will shift," Gerald said, his voice tight. "I am citing you for failing to assist in hazard mitigation."

The officer paused.

The tetrahedron stopped rotating. "Assistance is not granted to Cores with a Compliance Rating below 60. Your rating is 47.

You are a liability, Hutchins."

"Then fix my rating," Gerald said.

"I cannot fix what is inherently defective."

Gerald looked at the rat. It was chewing on the support pillar, trying to find purchase, trying to burn through the concrete. If it broke the pillar, the library above would collapse. The ceiling tiles above them were already sagging.

"You're right," Gerald said. "I am defective. But I am not the only problem."

The officer tilted its head. "Explain."

"Your presence here is unauthorized," Gerald said. He reached into his suit pocket.

He did not pull out a weapon.

He pulled out a small, folded piece of paper. A permit. "I filed a Notice of Inspection Request three days ago.

It was denied. So I filed a Counter-Notice of Imminent Structural Failure.

That notice requires an immediate on-site assessment by a Regional Officer."

The officer's form flickered. "The Counter-Notice was processed by AI. It was rejected."

"The processing error is on the server side," Gerald said. "I have the timestamp. I have the error log. And I have the rat."

Gerald pointed the broken clipboard at the rodent.

"That rat is not a spawn. It is a symptom.

It is a manifestation of the heat buildup in the main ventilation shaft. The shaft is non-compliant with the Municipal Fire Code, Article 12, regarding temperature regulation in subterranean archives. You know this.

The System knows this. But you are here, citing *me* for hitting it, while the root cause remains unaddressed."

The officer did not move. The air in the room grew colder, despite the rat's presence.

"You are attempting to leverage a bureaucratic loophole to avoid a citation," the officer said.

"I am attempting to leverage a bureaucratic fact to save the library," Gerald corrected.

"And if that library collapses, you will have to file a form with the Inspector General explaining why a Regional Officer failed to prevent a Class 4 Structural Event. Do you have that form pre-filled? I doubt it.

It is forty pages long."

The officer's tetrahedron spun slowly. "Show me the logs."

Gerald held out his hand. He did not speak.

He simply projected the mental link, a small, precise packet of data he had been holding in reserve.

It was not combat data. It was administrative data.

The officer absorbed the packet. It remained silent for ten seconds. The rat scurried toward a drainage pipe, trying to escape the heat of its own body.

"The error is valid," the officer said finally. "The processing queue was overloaded. The Counter-Notice was technically valid."

"Then you are now an active participant in this inspection," Gerald said. "You are not here to punish me.

You are here to assess the hazard.

That rat is a hazard.

The ventilation shaft is a hazard. The lack of a secondary egress route is a hazard.

You have three violations to address before you can issue a citation against me."

The officer looked at the rat. Then it looked at Gerald. "You are manipulating the protocol."

"I am using the protocol. There is a difference."

The rat leaped. It aimed for Gerald's throat.

The officer raised a hand. A barrier of grey light snapped into existence around Gerald. The rat slammed into it and bounced off, yelping.

"Stay back," the officer commanded.

Gerald exhaled. He adjusted his tie.

"Now that you are actively engaged, you will notice the rat is not attacking randomly.

It is targeting the thermal junction box on the far wall. That box is old. It is wired incorrectly.

It violates the Electrical Code, Section 4.

If that box fails, the resulting fire will trigger the suppression system. But the suppression system is dry.

It has not been tested in six years.

It will fail. The library will burn. The dungeon will be destroyed.

And you will be found negligent."

The officer's form solidified. It stepped away from Gerald and moved toward the wall. The tetrahedron spun, scanning the wiring.

"The wiring is non-compliant," the officer admitted.

"It is a Class A violation," Gerald said. "Immediate remediation required."

"I do not have the authority to perform repairs," the officer said.

"You have the authority to shut down the dungeon," Gerald said.

The officer froze.

Gerald walked toward the rat.

The creature snapped at his heels.

Gerald kicked it away, a clumsy, ungraceful motion. He did not enjoy it.

He hated it. But he kept walking.

"Shut it down," Gerald said. "Issue a Temporary Closure Order.

Seal the ventilation shaft.

Seal the electrical box. Seal the rat in the containment zone. Do it now, or I will file a formal Complaint of Negligence with the Inspector General, attaching this video of your inaction."

"Video?" the officer asked.

"I have been recording the ambient audio since you arrived," Gerald lied.

He had not. But the officer did not know that.

"My Core has a high-fidelity sensory array.

It is part of the new safety updates."

The officer hesitated. The System hummed in the background, a low, vibrating tension. The rat circled them, confused by the sudden shift in authority.

"A Temporary Closure Order suspends all spawn generation," the officer said. "It also freezes the dungeon's growth. Your Compliance Rating will stop improving."

"Better than a collapse," Gerald said.

"It is a severe penalty for the Core."

"It is a severe penalty for *you* if you do not act," Gerald said. "I am not asking for your help.

I am telling you what the code requires.

The code does not care about your career, Officer.

It cares about the structure. And right now, the structure is failing."

Gerald pointed to the ceiling. A crack ran through the plaster. Dust motes danced in the light of the rat's fiery eyes.

"Look at it," Gerald said. "Look at the deflection. It is over three millimeters.

It is over tolerance. You know this.

You have measured it in your head.

Why are you arguing?"

The officer turned to face him fully. The grey suit rippled.

"You are dangerous, Hutchins," the officer said.

"I am efficient," Gerald corrected.

"You are bending the rules to break the System."

"I am using the rules to uphold the System," Gerald said. "There is a difference. One leads to chaos. The other leads to paperwork."

The officer looked at the rat.

The rat looked at the officer. The rat hissed, and a stream of fire shot out, scorching the air near the electrical box.

Sparks flew.

The smell of ozone mixed with the smell of sulfur.

"Act," Gerald said.

The officer raised both hands. The air around the electrical box froze. Ice crystals formed on the wires, slowing the current. The rat screamed, confused by the sudden drop in temperature.

**SYSTEM ALERT: TEMPORARY CLOSURE ORDER ISSUED.** **TARGET: DUNGEON CORE 47-ALPHA.** **REASON: IMMEDIATE STRUCTURAL HAZARD.** **DURATION: 24 HOURS.**

The rat collapsed. The fire in its eyes dimmed. It turned into a pile of ash and bone, inert.

The officer lowered its hands. The ice melted, dripping onto the floor.

"You have won," the officer said. Its voice was flat. "For now."

"I have complied," Gerald said.

The officer faded. "The Inspector General will review this. Your Compliance Rating will be audited. If I find that you used social engineering to bypass safety protocols, you will be deregistered."

"Noted," Gerald said.

The officer vanished. The sub-basement was silent, save for the dripping water from the melted ice.

Gerald looked at the ash pile. He looked at the cracked pillar. He looked at the electrical box, now coated in a thin layer of frost.

He picked up his broken clipboard. The plastic was warped, blackened at the edges.

He could not use it anymore.

He needed a new one.

He needed to file a report on the closure. He needed to document the officer's intervention.

But as he turned to leave, the System interface pinged.

**NEW MECHANIC UNLOCKED: ADMINISTRATIVE LEVERAGE.**

**DESCRIPTION: Cores who successfully force higher-tier System entities to act via bureaucratic pressure gain access to 'Audit Shield' abilities. These abilities allow the Core to temporarily freeze the actions of rival Cores during a Hearing.**

Gerald stopped. He read the text twice.

He had not just survived. He had learned a new way to fight. Not with fire.

Not with claws. With paper.

A sound came from the tunnel entrance. Footsteps. Heavy, armored footsteps.

Gerald turned.

Standing in the doorway was a party of four adventurers.

They were wearing high-level gear. Glowing armor. Enchanted swords.

They looked at the ash pile.

They looked at the ice. They looked at Gerald.

The leader, a woman with a massive greatsword, stepped forward. She looked at Gerald's suit, his broken clipboard, his thinning hair.

"Is this the boss?" she asked.

Gerald straightened his tie. "This is the inspection site. You are trespassing on a closed zone. I am issuing you a Citation for Unauthorized Entry."

The woman laughed. "We're here for the loot. The rumors said this dungeon was tough. They said the rat was a demon."

"The rat was a non-compliant entity," Gerald said.

"It is deceased. The dungeon is closed. Leave, or I will summon the Regional Compliance Officer again.

And this time, I will cite you for obstructing an official investigation."

The woman raised her sword. "We're not leaving. We're taking the core."

Gerald looked at her. He looked at the System interface. He looked at the Audit Shield prompt.

He did not have the shield yet. He had just unlocked the mechanic. He had not activated it.

But he had the citation.

"If you take this core," Gerald said, "you take the liability.

You take the fines.

You take the audit. And you take the Inspector General's attention.

Are you sure you want to be part of the compliance record?"

The woman hesitated. Her party shifted behind her.

"Who is the Inspector General?" one of them asked.

Gerald smiled. It was a thin, dry smile. "The person who wrote the rules. And I am just getting started."

The woman lowered her sword slightly. "Tell me more."

Gerald opened his mouth. He did not speak.

Instead, he pulled out a pen. It was not a weapon.

It was a blue ballpoint pen, standard office issue.

He uncapped it. He clicked it.

The sound was tiny. But in the silence of the sub-basement, it was deafening.

**SYSTEM ALERT: AUDIT SHIELD ACTIVATION PENDING.** **REQUIREMENT: SUBMIT FORMAL COMPLAINT AGAINST PARTY OF FOUR.** **WARNING: FAILURE TO PROVIDE EVIDENCE WILL RESULT IN PERMANENT LOSS OF CORE INTEGRITY.**

Gerald looked at the adventurers. He looked at the blank piece of paper he had torn from his notebook.

He wrote the date.

He wrote the time.

He wrote the names of the adventurers, which he had pulled from their public adventurer guild profiles using a minor sensory scan.

He wrote the charge: Obstruction of Justice.

He held up the paper.

"Serve," Gerald said.

The paper floated from his hand. It did not fly to the adventurers. It flew to the ceiling, where it stuck to the blackened plaster.

Nothing happened.

The adventurers stared at him.

"That's all?" the leader asked.

Gerald looked at the paper. He looked at the System.

**ERROR: EVIDENCE INSUFFICIENT. NO WITNESSES.**

Gerald's stomach dropped. He had no witnesses. The officer was gone. The rat was dead.

"Wait," Gerald said.

The leader raised her sword again. "No witnesses? Then no citation."

She swung.

The sword came down.

Gerald did not move. He could not move.

He was paralyzed by the error message.

The sword struck his shoulder.

Pain exploded.

**SYSTEM ALERT: CORE DAMAGE DETECTED.** **HEALTH: 10%.**

Gerald fell. Blood, not black ichor, but real, hot blood, seeped from the cut. He had forgotten he was made of something more than code now. He had forgotten he could bleed.

The leader stood over him. "Pathetic," she said. "Just an old man with a clipboard."

Gerald lay on the floor. He looked at the ceiling. At the stuck paper. At the crack in the plaster.

He could not cite them. Not yet.

But he could do something else.

He reached into his pocket. He pulled out the only other thing he had.

A small, red button.

The Emergency Stop.

It was meant for the elevator. He had installed it last week, because he had noticed the elevator was non-compliant with accessibility standards. He had installed it to pass the inspection.

He had not tested it.

His finger hovered over the button.

If he pressed it, the entire sub-basement would lose power. The lights would go out.

The ventilation would stop.

The heat from the electrical box would return, unchecked.

The fire would start.

The library would burn.

The adventurers would die.

He would die.

The leader stepped closer. "Last chance to give up the core."

Gerald looked at her. He looked at the button.

He pressed it.
