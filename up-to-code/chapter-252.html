Chapter 252: Inspected by the Void

# Chapter 252

Gerald's tape measure snapped taut.

The corridor was three feet, four inches wide.

Code required thirty-six inches.

Ironfist had arrived early.

The ground trembled. Dust fell from the ceiling tiles, coating Gerald's clipboard in a fine gray powder. He did not brush it off. A dirty workspace was a safety hazard, and he would document it.

From the main entrance, a sound rolled forward. It was not a footstep.

It was a tectonic shift.

Stone grinding on stone. The air pressure dropped, popping Gerald's ears.

He adjusted his stance.

Feet shoulder-width apart. Weight centered.

This was not combat posture.

This was inspection stance. It allowed for maximum lateral stability when checking for vertical alignment.

The darkness at the end of the hall parted.

Ironfist filled the frame.

The core avatar was a construct of jagged basalt and glowing magma veins. It stood nine feet tall. Its chest cavity roared with a furnace heat that made the mold spores in the corner wilt and curl. The golem's eyes were pits of white fire.

It held a hammer. The head of the hammer was the size of a compact car.

Gerald raised his clipboard.

"Halt," Gerald said. His voice did not shake. His voice sounded like he was reading a lease agreement to a tenant who had already broken it.

Ironfist paused. The magma veins pulsed brighter.

"Inspector," the golem rumbled. The voice vibrated in Gerald's molars.

"You cited my thermal regulation systems.

My lava flows cooled.

My golems slowed. I am here to correct the error.

I will demolish this sub-basement."

Gerald looked at the hammer. He looked at the ceiling.

"You are currently trespassing," Gerald said. "And you are failing to maintain a safe distance from structural supports."

Ironfist swung the hammer.

It was a casual blow. A test. The stone floor cracked. The shockwave knocked the water leak out of the wall, spraying mist into the air.

Gerald did not flinch. He stepped to the left. One step. Precise.

The hammer missed his shoulder by two inches.

Gerald scribbled on his pad. *Uncontrolled kinetic discharge. Failure to secure tools.*

Ironfist snarled. The second swing was faster. It aimed for Gerald's torso.

Gerald sidestepped again. He watched the hammer head. He watched the trajectory. He calculated the radius of the swing.

If the hammer hit the load-bearing pillar to his right, the ceiling above would collapse. If the ceiling collapsed, the library above would sink. If the library sank, the municipal bond market would panic.

Gerald could not allow that.

He raised his tape measure.

"I am issuing a temporary injunction," Gerald said.

He slammed the tape measure against the air.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** *Violation Detected: Improper Tool Usage.* **ACTION:** *Citation Applied: Heavy Equipment Operator (Unlicensed).* **EFFECT:** *User 'Ironfist' is now subject to OSHA Standard 1910.212: Guarding of Machine Hazards.*

The air around Ironfist shimmered.

An invisible grid snapped into place around the golem's arms. The hammer slowed. The joints locked. The magma within the golem's limbs constricted, unable to expand into the necessary pressure chambers for a full swing.

Ironfist grunted. It tried to force the arm forward.

The grid held.

The System enforced the mechanical safety limits. A hammer that could crush a dungeon core was, by definition, a machine hazard. It required guarding.

"Move," Gerald commanded.

Ironfist roared. It threw the hammer aside. It lunged forward with bare stone hands.

Gerald stepped back. He pulled a roll of yellow caution tape from his inventory.

"Non-compliant entry path," Gerald muttered.

He unspooled the tape. He slapped it across the gap between the two pillars.

The tape did not stop the golem. But it triggered the boundary sensor.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** *Perimeter Breach Detected in Zone 4 (Residential Adjacency).* **ACTION:** *Citation Applied: Zoning Violation (Commercial/Industrial in Mixed-Use Zone).* **EFFECT:** *Target 'Ironfist' suffers 'Administrative Burden'. Speed reduced by 15%.*

Ironfist slowed. Its heavy steps dragged. The dust cloud thickened.

Gerald was not fighting. He was regulating.

Every move Ironfist made generated a violation.

Every swing was a noise ordinance breach. Every step was a vibration control failure.

The System loved bureaucracy.

It punished chaos with paperwork.

But Ironfist was Tier 2. It had high resistance to debuffs. The slowdown was annoying, not disabling.

The golem reached the caution tape. It ripped through it.

Gerald felt a chill. Not from the cold.

From the realization that this was a brute-force attack.

Ironfist did not care about the citations.

It cared about breaking the container.

Gerald needed to escalate.

He looked at the ceiling. The water leak was still dripping. He looked at the mold spores. They were hiding behind a loose floorboard.

Gerald opened his inventory. He bypassed the Form 11-B. He went to his 'Maintenance Requests' tab.

He selected **Request 44-C: Emergency Ventilation Failure.**

He pointed his grease pencil at the ceiling.

"I am declaring this dungeon structurally compromised," Gerald said. "Evacuation protocol is now mandatory."

Ironfist tilted its head. "Evacuation?"

"The System requires adequate air exchange rates," Gerald said. "You have generated excess heat and particulate matter. The current ventilation system is insufficient."

Gerald slammed his fist against the wall.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** *Compliance Audit: Air Quality Index Critical.* **ACTION:** *Enforce Ventilation Code 602.1.* **EFFECT:** *All internal doors in Zone 2 are forced open. Fresh air intake activated.*

The walls groaned. Hidden panels slid open. Fresh, cold air from the outside street rushed into the dungeon.

Ironfist recoiled.

The golem was fueled by internal heat. The sudden influx of cold, oxygen-rich air disrupted its thermal equilibrium. The magma veins cooled rapidly. The stone skin contracted, creating micro-fractures across its chest.

Ironfist screamed. It was a sound of grinding rock.

"You are killing me!" Ironfist shouted.

"I am ventilating the space," Gerald said. He wrote on his clipboard. *Subject failed to adapt to environmental controls.*

Ironfist lunged. It ignored the fractures. It ignored the slowness. It wanted to crush the Inspector.

Gerald saw the movement. He saw the weight shift.

He did not dodge this time.

He stepped forward. Into the radius of the attack.

This was a gamble. A calculated risk. He knew the code for 'High-Velocity Impact Zones'. He knew that if he stood in the dead zone, the System would flag the attacker for 'Unsafe Working Conditions' relative to the bystander.

Ironfist's fist came down.

Gerald raised his clipboard.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** *Bystander Injury Risk Detected.* **ACTION:** *Citation Applied: Negligent Supervision.* **EFFECT:** *Target 'Ironfist' is paralyzed for 3 seconds pending safety review.*

The golem froze.

Its fist hovered inches from Gerald's face. The heat was intense. His eyebrows singed. The smell of burning hair filled his nose.

Three seconds.

Gerald moved.

He did not run away. He ran toward the golem's legs.

He produced his grease pencil. He drew a line on the floor. Then another. He marked a circle around Ironfist's left foot.

He was not attacking the golem. He was attacking the ground.

He pulled a **Structural Integrity Check** form from his sleeve.

"Sub-grade settlement detected," Gerald said.

He tapped the form against the floor.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** *Foundation Instability Detected.* **ACTION:** *Trigger Load-Bearing Alert.* **EFFECT:** *Target 'Ironfist' experiences 'Unstable Surface' debuff. Balance check failed.*

Ironfist's leg buckled. The golem tipped. It crashed onto one knee.

The impact shook the entire sub-basement. Cracks spiderwebbed across the concrete floor.

Gerald scrambled onto the golem's back.

He pulled out a screwdriver. Not a weapon. A standard Phillips-head, six inches long.

He drove it into the joint of the golem's neck.

It was not enough to damage the core. But it was enough to trigger the 'Precision Maintenance' protocol.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** *Foreign Object in Critical Machinery.* **ACTION:** *Immediate Shutdown Sequence Initiated.* **EFFECT:** *Target 'Ironfist' enters 'Standby Mode' for 10 seconds.*

The golem went rigid. The fire in its eyes dimmed to a dull red glow.

Gerald jumped off.

He landed heavily. His knees ached. His back screamed.

He was fifty-four years old.

He was not built for parkour.

He straightened his tie.

Ironfist was locked. Ten seconds.

Gerald had ten seconds to decide.

He could issue a final citation. A demolition order. It would destroy the golem's body, but Ironfist's core would likely survive, retreating to its own dungeon. Gerald would win the encounter, but he would not end the threat.

Or he could use the loophole.

He looked at the paralyzed golem. He looked at the cracks in the floor.

He opened his inventory. He selected **Form 99-Z: Conditional Variance Request.**

He had never used this form. It was meant for builders who wanted to deviate from code for aesthetic reasons. It allowed for a temporary suspension of certain rules, provided a 'Safety Officer' certified the deviation.

Gerald was the Safety Officer.

He wrote. Fast. His handwriting was jagged, but legible.

*I, Gerald Hutchins, certify that the destruction of Core 'Ironfist' constitutes an imminent threat to the structural integrity of the Ashford Municipal Library. I request a variance from the 'Combat Damage' restrictions. I request the authority to absorb, not destroy, the hostile energy.*

He slammed the form against the golem's back.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** *Variance Request Received.* **EFFECT:** *Pending Approval.*

Ironfist's eyes flared. It was breaking the standby mode.

Five seconds left.

Gerald held his breath.

The System processed the request. It weighed the bureaucratic logic against the magical threat.

**APPROVED.**

Gerald felt a pull in his chest. A vacuum sensation.

The heat from Ironfist drained. Not into the air. Into Gerald.

Magma energy flowed through the stone, through the floor, into Gerald's hands. It was not painful. It was... warm. Like stepping out of a cold rain into a heated lobby.

The golem shrank. Its stone skin turned to dust. The fire extinguished.

Ironfist was gone.

Gerald stood alone in the ruined corridor.

He checked his watch.

The Compliance Rating in his vision ticked up.

**CURRENT COMPLIANCE RATING: 62/100.**

A notification flashed.

**NEW UNLOCK:** *Thermal Regulation Mastery (Level 1).* **PASSIVE:** *Immunity to ambient heat. +5% Fire Resistance.*

Gerald exhaled. His hands were shaking.

He had absorbed a Tier 2 Core's energy. He had broken the rules to save the rules.

He picked up his clipboard. It was scorched. The corners were burnt.

He needed to file a report. A big one.

But before he could move, the floor beneath him rumbled.

It was not Ironfist.

It was deeper. Much deeper.

A new voice echoed. It was not a whisper. It was a broadcast.

**SYSTEM WIDE BROADCAST:** **FROM: INSPECTOR GENERAL.** **MESSAGE: Audit Failed.**

Gerald froze.

**MESSAGE: Core 'Ashford Sub-Basement' has violated the 'Non-Aggression Principle' by absorbing hostile Core energy without a Permit 44-A.** **ACTION: Immediate Freeze of All Dungeon Functions.** **STATUS: Locked.**

The lights went out.

Total darkness.

Gerald tapped the side of his head. Nothing happened. The System was offline for him.

He was blind. He was cold. And he was locked out of his own house.

A sound came from the shadows. Not from the corridor where Ironfist had died.

From the water leak.

The water had stopped dripping.

Instead, it had formed a shape. A humanoid shape, made of sludge and rust.

It stood up. It was small. No taller than Gerald.

It had no face. Just a smooth, wet surface where features should be.

It raised a hand. In its hand was a stamp. A red rubber stamp.

**VOIDED.**

The figure spoke. Its voice was the sound of dripping faucets.

"You broke the seal, Inspector."

Gerald tried to move.

He couldn't. His legs were heavy. The System's lock was physical.

He was anchored to the spot.

The sludge-creature stepped forward. It walked toward Gerald.

It did not attack. It placed the stamp on Gerald's chest.

*Thud.*

Gerald looked down.

On his shirt, a red mark appeared. It was not ink. It was light.

**TICKET ISSUED: Unlicensed Energy Absorption.** **PENALTY: Dungeon Core Demotion to Tier 0.** **COUNTDOWN: 60 SECONDS.**

Gerald gasped.

Tier 0.

A rat's nest. A pile of junk.

He would lose everything. The handrails. The codes.

The order.

He looked at the creature.

"Who are you?" Gerald asked.

The creature tilted its head.

"I am the Consequence," it said. "And you have no appeal left. You used the variance form without a witness."

Gerald's heart hammered.

He looked around the dark room. He needed a witness. He needed someone to sign the form.

The goblin.

The goblin from before. It had run away.

But wait.

Gerald remembered the message. *Leave a review.*

He had asked for reviews.

He concentrated. He couldn't access the System. But he could access his memory. He could access the physical world.

He shouted into the darkness.

"I require a second signature for the variance appeal!"

Silence.

Then, a scratch from the door.

The heavy oak door creaked open.

A small figure stood there. It was wearing a hoodie. It held a phone.

It was not a goblin.

It was a kid. A human kid.

The kid looked at Gerald. The kid looked at the sludge creature.

The kid raised the phone. Flash on.

**CLICK.**

The flash blinded the sludge creature. It hissed, retreating into the drain.

The kid spoke.

"You're on Twitch, man," the kid said. "And chat just spammed the appeal form."

Gerald stared.

The kid was not an adventurer. The kid was not a goblin.

The kid was a streamer.

And the System... the System had recognized the stream as a public broadcast.

A witness.

The red mark on Gerald's chest flickered.

**APPEAL RECEIVED.** **WAITING FOR REVIEW.**

The kid pointed the phone at Gerald.

"Don't move," the kid said. "You're trending."

Gerald straightened his tie. He looked at the camera lens.

"Make sure you get the egress routes in the background," Gerald said. "It's important for the liability waiver."

The kid laughed.

Then, the screen behind the kid turned black.

A new message appeared.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** **FROM: CORE 'VOID-STAR' (SECTOR 9).** **MESSAGE: Hello, Inspector. I liked your stream.**

Gerald's blood ran cold.

Void-Star was a Tier 3. A reality-warping dungeon.

It had seen him.

It knew he was vulnerable.

And it was watching.
