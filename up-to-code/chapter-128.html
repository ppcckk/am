Chapter 128: License to Drown

# Chapter 128

The rain hammered against the reinforced glass of the Municipal Emergency Operations Center, blurring the city lights into smeared neon bruises. Captain Elara Vance pressed her forehead against the cold windowpane, watching the water cascade down the sides of the library's historic facade.

It wasn't just rain anymore.

It was a deluge. A structural breach.

"Satellite imagery confirms thermal variance in the sub-basement," the tech officer muttered, typing furiously. "Core 8940-B is still active. It's not attacking the city. It's *leaking* it."

Elara turned from the window.

Her uniform was damp, her boots squelching on the linoleum. She had spent three years hunting dungeon cores with fire and steel, expecting monsters, expecting glory, expecting something that screamed when it died.

What she got from Hutchins was worse.

Silence. Paperwork. And now, a flooded library that held the only known copies of the Ashford Founding Charters.

"Get me a line to the Inspector General's proxy," Elara said, her voice tight. "And tell the fire brigade to stop pumping water and start pumping out the books. If we don't save the records, the city loses its legal claim to the land. If the land is lost, the library's zoning status collapses."

Zoning. The word tasted like ash in her mouth.

Hutchins didn't fight with swords. He fought with code. And right now, the code was drowning them.

***

Deep below the saturated earth, the air smelled of wet concrete and old paper. Gerald Hutchins stood in the center of the collapsed ceiling, his avatar flickering as the structural integrity of the dungeon dipped into the red zone.

*Status: Critical. Compliance Rating: 34/100.* *Active Citations: 14 (Pending).* *Civil Liability: Active.*

The floor beneath him was no longer solid ground. It was a slurry of mud and debris, held together by the System's temporary cohesion fields. Above, the weight of the flooded library pressed down, tons of water seeking an escape route. Gerald could feel the pressure against his core, a physical thrumming that made his nonexistent teeth ache.

He did not panic. Panic was a violation of emotional stability protocols.

Instead, he looked at the water rising around his knees. Dark, filthy water.

Sewage.

Runoff.

The city's waste, flowing freely into his domain because he had induced a micro-fracture in the supply line.

*Alert: Unauthorized Fluid Transfer Detected.* *Violation: Sanitation Code 7.2-B – Cross-Connection Prevention.* *Penalty: -15 Agility, -10 Stealth.*

Gerald narrowed his eyes. He had anticipated this. The System punished contamination, but it also punished *inaction*.

If he let the water stagnate, he would be cited for sanitation hazards. If he drained it too quickly, he would be cited for uncontrolled discharge.

He needed a third option.

Gerald raised a hand, tracing a pattern in the mud. He wasn't casting a spell. He was drawing a drainage plan.

*Action: Initiate Emergency Percolation Protocol.* *Requirement: Installation of Permeable Membrane.* *Cost: 400 Mana.*

He didn't have the mana, and he had never had enough mana. But he had something else. He had knowledge of the city's soil composition.

The sub-basement rested on a bed of clay-rich loam, known to municipal engineers as the

"Ashford Clay Layer." It was notorious for poor drainage, which was why the library's original foundation had cracked in 1974.

But clay, when fractured correctly, became a sieve.

Gerald focused on the mud beneath his feet. He visualized the microscopic gaps between the clay particles.

He thought of the time he had denied a permit for a new parking structure because the contractor had used the wrong type of gravel.

He remembered the inspector's report: *

"Insufficient granular base layer. Risk of hydraulic uplift."*

Hydraulic uplift.

Water pushed up. If he could direct that upward pressure, he could lift the floor sections.

*Action: Induce Hydraulic Heave in Sector 4.* *Target: Sub-grade Clay Layer.* *Warning: High Risk of Total Collapse.*

Gerald pushed. He didn't use magic. He used leverage. He focused his will on the water trapped in the pores of the earth, forcing it into the larger voids he had created with his initial fracture.

The mud bubbled.

A geyser erupted three feet to his left. Then another. The floor heaved.

Above, the ceiling cracked. Not a catastrophic failure, but a controlled release. The weight of the water was being transferred, not into the dungeon's core, but into the surrounding earth. The pressure equalized.

*Notification: Pressure Equalized. Structural Integrity Stabilizing.* *New Violation Detected: Unauthorized Groundwater Alteration.* *Severity: Moderate.*

Gerald wiped his hands on his suit, the gesture automatic, useless, but comforting. The water was still there, but it wasn't drowning him anymore. It was just sitting there. A lake in his living room.

And with the water came something else.

Movement. In the murky depths.

Gerald looked down. Shapes moved in the dark. Not rats. Not mold.

Shadows, and they were flat, two-dimensional silhouettes that slid across the water's surface, ignoring gravity, ignoring the mud. They moved with the fluid grace of oil slicks.

Gerald felt a chill that had nothing to do with the temperature. He had not spawned these. He had not cited them. They were not in his registry.

One of the shadows coalesced near his core. It rose, forming a vaguely humanoid shape, its edges rippling like smoke.

"Mr. Hutchins," the shadow said. The voice was not a sound; it was a vibration in the water, transmitted directly to Gerald's perception. "You are making a mess."

Gerald straightened his tie.

"I am engaging in necessary infrastructure maintenance. State your business."

"Business," the shadow echoed. "We are the Audit Bureau."

Gerald froze. The Audit Bureau was a rumor, a ghost story told by lower-tier cores.

They weren't rivals, and they weren't monsters.

They were the System's internal police. They didn't fight dungeons; they erased them.

"You cannot be here," Gerald said, his voice steady, though his hand twitched at his side. "This is a private residential zone."

"Zoning is a matter of interpretation," the shadow replied. "And your current interpretation is... fluid."

The shadow extended a hand. Or what passed for a hand. A tendril of darkness stretched out, aiming for Gerald's core.

"If you do not cease this unauthorized hydraulic manipulation, we will revoke your Core License. We will dissolve your footprint. We will cite you for the crime of existence."

Gerald looked at the tendril. He looked at the rising water. He looked at the notification flashing in his peripheral vision.

*Civil Liability: Active.* *Audit Authority: Present.*

If he fought, he lost. He was a 47-rated core with three mold spores. These things were entities of pure administrative malice.

If he submitted, he was erased.

But Gerald Hutchins had never followed the rules when the rules were wrong. He had only followed the rules when they were *specific*.

"Your presence is not registered," Gerald said. "You are not a citizen of Ashford. You are not a Core."

"We are the law," the shadow hissed.

"Then you must comply with municipal ordinance 14-C," Gerald said. He pulled a pen from his pocket. It wasn't a real pen.

It was a construct of mana, shaped like the ballpoint he had used for thirty years.

"All entities entering a private residence must present identification and a valid business permit.

Do you have a permit?

"

The shadow paused. The ripples in its form slowed.

"We do not require permits," the shadow said.

"Then you are trespassing," Gerald said. He tapped the pen against his palm. "And you are loitering. And you are failing to observe proper hygiene standards in a public-facing workspace."

The shadow lunged. Tendrils of darkness wrapped around Gerald's legs, pulling him down into the mud.

Gerald did not struggle. He did not scream. He simply held up the pen.

*Action: File Citation: Failure to Produce Identification.* *Target: Audit Bureau Entity.* *Severity: Immediate Detention.*

The System hesitated. For a fraction of a second, the logic looped. Was an Audit Bureau entity subject to municipal code?

The System's architecture was vast, ancient, and rigid.

It prioritized local jurisdiction over abstract authority.

The shadow shrieked, a sound like tearing metal. The tendrils tightened, squeezing the air from Gerald's lungs.

But then, the System responded.

*Violation Confirmed: Unregistered Entity in Residential Zone.* *Enforcement Protocol: Repulsion Field Activated.*

A wall of pure, white force exploded from Gerald's core. It wasn't magic. It was the physical manifestation of a zoning violation. It hit the shadow with the force of a city ordinance being enforced.

The shadow was hurled backward, slamming into the muddy wall. It dissolved into smoke, screaming as it was forcibly ejected from the dungeon's boundaries.

*Entity Removed.* *Compliance Rating: 34/100.* *Note: Local Jurisdiction Enforced.*

Gerald gasped, falling to his knees in the mud. His ribs ached. His core flickered dangerously.

He had survived. Barely.

But the victory was hollow, and the shadow had been right about one thing. The Audit Bureau was coming. And they had seen him.

They knew he could manipulate the system, exploit the loopholes, turn their own bureaucracy against them.

A new notification flashed, bright and red, burning into his vision.

*Alert: Priority Audit Scheduled.* *Timeframe: 48 Hours.* *Auditor: Magistrate Vane.* *Objective: Full Compliance Review. Potential Core Dissolution.*

Forty-eight hours.

Gerald stood up.

His legs shook, and he looked around the flooded, ruined sub-basement. The mold spores were dead.

The rat was gone. The water was still rising, inches from his knees.

He needed help. Not adventurer help.

They would only make a mess, and he needed someone who understood the law better than he did. Someone who could argue with a god.

Gerald closed his eyes and reached out, not to the System, but to the city above. He reached for the one person he had ever respected, the one person who had once told him that the building code was not a suggestion, but a promise.

His old supervisor. The one who had fired him. The one who had known exactly how to cheat the system without breaking it.

Gerald focused his mana, not on a spell, but on a message. A simple, urgent, handwritten note, pushed through the System's communication layer.

*To: Director Marcus Thorne.* *Subject: Inquiry re: Ashford Library Structural Assessment.* *Body: I need a witness. Bring your badge.*

He waited.

The water lapped at his waist.

Then, the air in the dungeon changed. It didn't smell of mud anymore. It smelled of cigar smoke and expensive cologne.

A figure stepped out of the shadows.

Not a shadow. A man.

Solid, real, wearing a suit that cost more than Gerald's annual salary.

He held a cane, though he didn't need it for walking. He used it to point.

"Mr. Hutchins," Marcus Thorne said, his voice smooth, oily, and dangerous. "You certainly have a way with leaks."

Gerald looked up, water dripping from his chin.

"You're late."

"I'm not here to help you," Thorne said, stepping onto a dry patch of concrete that shouldn't have existed. "I'm here to take your license."

He smiled. It was a shark's smile.

"And then, I'm going to take the city."
