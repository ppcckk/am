Chapter 269: Hearing Waived

# Chapter 269

The citation was already bleeding ink before Gerald's mind fully registered the threat.

It hung in the air, translucent and trembling, hovering over the jagged scar in the dungeon's floor. The System interface, usually a calm, bureaucratic blue, flickered with a violent, angry red. This wasn't a standard violation notice. This was a Pre-emptive Enforcement Order.

Gerald blinked. He did not blink again. He focused on the floating text, his eyes tracing the serif font with the precision of a man who had read ten thousand building permits in his life.

**VIOLATION CODE: 14-C. UNAUTHORIZED STRUCTURAL MODIFICATION VIA NON-CORE ENTITY.**

**SEVERITY: CRITICAL.**

**ACTION: IMMEDIATE SEIZURE OF ASSETS AND CORE REVOCATION.**

**PENDING: HEARING WAIVED DUE TO FLAGRANT MALICE.**

The words "Hearing Waived" hit Gerald harder than a physical blow. His stomach dropped, a cold knot tightening below his ribs.

The System, usually a rigid arbiter of procedure, had just suspended due process, which meant someone powerful had pulled a string. Someone had decided that Gerald's dungeon was not a home, but a hazard to be abated.

Mara stood three feet away, her axe resting on her shoulder. She watched the red text with an expression of bored contempt. She had seen many things die. She had not seen paperwork die.

"They're going to take it," she said.

Her voice was flat.

"The System doesn't like being cheated.

You used a variance to fix a crack.

The Inspector General sees that as cheating.

"

Gerald ignored her. He reached out, not with his hand, but with his Will.

He grabbed the edge of the citation notice.

It felt like grabbing a hot iron bar.

The heat seared his mental fingers, but he held on. He needed to read the fine print.

He needed the footnotes.

There were no footnotes. The message was absolute.

But Gerald knew the code. He knew that every absolute rule had an exception, and every exception had a filing requirement.

He couldn't fight the seizure with force, and he couldn't fight it with magic. He had to fight it with procedure.

He looked at the surrounding darkness.

The Crimson Wastes bled into the periphery of his dungeon, a thick, crimson fog that smelled of sulfur and old blood. The fog was pressing in, eager to consume the small, gray space Gerald had carved out.

The neighboring cores were watching, and they could feel the System's attention shifting. They were waiting for the gavel to fall.

Gerald closed his eyes. He summoned his internal ledger. It wasn't a book. It was a vast, empty filing cabinet in his mind, waiting for documents.

"Form 88-Z," Gerald muttered.

Mara frowned. "What?"

"Form 88-Z. Emergency Stay of Enforcement.

Application for Immediate Relief.

" Gerald's thoughts moved fast, a blur of legalistic logic.

"The System prohibits Core Revocation without a documented opportunity for cure.

The waiver of the hearing is a procedural error if the underlying defect was remedied prior to the notice.

"

"You fixed the floor," Mara said. "It's fixed. Why does it matter?"

"It matters because the notice implies the defect is *active*," Gerald said. He opened his eyes. He stared at the red text.

"If I can prove the defect is cured, and that the cure was *contemporaneous* with the violation, the waiver becomes invalid. The hearing must be reinstated."

"That's a technicality," Mara said. She shifted her weight, her boots grinding against the rough stone. "The Inspector General doesn't care about technicalities. He cares that you broke the rules."

"The Inspector General cares about *paperwork*," Gerald corrected. He composed the mental document. It was not a spell. It was a legal argument, structured in the precise, dry language of municipal law.

**TO: SYSTEM INSPECTOR GENERAL'S OFFICE** **FROM: CORE 492-B (HUTCHINS, GERALD)** **RE: REQUEST FOR STAY OF REVOCATION PURSUANT TO STATUTORY EXCEPTION 14-C-4 (CONTEMPORANEOUS CURE)**

Gerald typed the words with his thoughts.

The System resisted. The air around him crackled with static. The red text pulsed, trying to burn the message away.

But Gerald held the structure firm, and he cited the specific subsection. He referenced the timestamp of his own variance application, filed three seconds prior to the seizure order.

*Time is the only truth in bureaucracy,* Gerald thought. *If the cure existed before the notice, the notice is moot.*

He slammed the mental document into the air.

It didn't appear as a floating pane. It appeared as a stamp. A heavy, invisible weight that slammed down onto the red citation.

*Thud.*

The red text shuddered. The aggressive pulsing slowed.

Mara took a step back. She raised her axe, not against an enemy, but against the air.

"Did you just file a motion against God?"

"I filed a motion against a clerk," Gerald said.

He was sweating. His heart hammered against his ribs, a frantic, bird-like rhythm.

"The Inspector General is a functionary.

Functionaries follow rules. If I prove the rules were broken in the enforcement, the enforcement fails.

"

The System interface froze. The red text vanished.

In its place, a single, white line of text appeared.

**MOTION ACCEPTED FOR REVIEW. PENDING DECISION.**

A timer appeared in the upper right corner of Gerald's vision. It started counting down.

*00:59:59*

"One hour," Gerald said. He exhaled, a long, shaky breath. "I have one hour to prepare the evidence for the reinstated hearing."

Mara lowered her axe, and she looked at Gerald with a new expression.

It wasn't respect, exactly. It was the wary assessment one gives to a bomb that has just been defused.

"If you lose, they take the core. You die. The dungeon collapses."

"If I win, they fine me," Gerald said. "And I have to pay the fine in mana."

"Mana you don't have," Mara pointed out.

Gerald paused. He looked at his mana bar. It was at 12%. It had dropped significantly during the fight with Mara and the subsequent repair.

He hadn't thought about the cost.

"I can borrow it," Gerald said.

Mara laughed. It was a short, bark of a sound.

"Borrow it? From whom?"

"From the neighbors," Gerald said. He turned toward the edge of his dungeon, where the gray gloom met the crimson fog.

The Crimson Wastes were a high-tier dungeon. Its core, a sentient entity of rage and fire, had been trying to expand into Gerald's space for weeks.

It had sent minions, which had tried to collapse his walls. It had failed, largely because Gerald had cited its minions for trespassing and unsafe working conditions, forcing them to retreat.

But now, with Gerald's core under threat, the Crimson Wastes would smell blood. They would try to finish the job.

Gerald walked to the boundary line. The air here was hot. It tasted of ash.

He didn't attack, and he didn't spawn a monster. He opened a direct communication channel.

This was a risky maneuver.

Most cores kept their communication channels closed to avoid detection. Gerald opened his wide, broadcasting a signal on a frequency reserved for inter-core diplomatic negotiations.

**TO: CORE 88-A (CRIMSON WASTES)** **FROM: CORE 492-B** **SUBJECT: JOINT VENTURE PROPOSAL / ASSET LEASING**

Mara watched him. She didn't speak. She knew better than to interrupt a man when he was in the middle of a filing.

The response came almost instantly. The text appeared, jagged and dripping with virtual molten lava.

**WHAT DO YOU WANT, MAGGOT?**

Gerald winced at the insult. He ignored it.

He focused on the contract terms, and he needed a loan.

But the System did not allow direct transfers of mana between cores without a binding agreement. A binding agreement required a transaction.

A service.

Gerald had something the Crimson Wastes wanted. He had a clean dungeon. A compliant dungeon.

And he had a new idea, and he looked at the rough, jagged walls of his sub-basement. They were ugly, yes.

But they were *up to code*.

They were structurally sound. They were fire-resistant, thanks to the recent variance.

Gerald drafted a proposal.

**PROPOSAL: SUBLEASE OF COMPLIANT ZONES.** **TERMS: THE CRIMSON WASTES MAY SPAWN MINIONS WITHIN THE BOUNDARIES OF CORE 492-B FOR A PERIOD OF 48 HOURS.** **IN EXCHANGE: A ONE-TIME MANA TRANSFER OF 50,000 UNITS TO CORE 492-B TO SATISFY PENDING FINES.** **CONDITION: ALL SPAWNED ENTITIES MUST BE PROVEN TO BE COMPLIANT WITH LOCAL HOUSING AND WORKPLACE SAFETY CODES PRIOR TO SPAWNING.**

Mara stared at the text. She read it twice. Then she looked at Gerald.

"You're... subletting your dungeon?" she asked. Her voice was incredulous. "To the thing that tried to eat you?"

"I'm not subletting the dungeon," Gerald said.

"I'm subletting *compliance*. The Crimson Wastes has a terrible compliance rating. 12/100.

Their minions take increased damage from all sources because their environment is hazardous.

If they spawn minions in my dungeon, those minions inherit my dungeon's passive resistances. Fire resistance. Structural integrity.

"

Mara's eyes widened.

"They'll be invincible."

"They'll be *efficient*," Gerald corrected. "And efficient minions make money. The Crimson Wastes is bleeding revenue because its minions keep dying to environmental hazards. This fixes that."

The interface paused. The molten text churned.

**YOU WOULD ALLOW MY HORDES TO WALK YOUR HALLS?**

**YES,** Gerald thought. **PROVIDED THEY FILL OUT THE NECESSARY PERMITS.**

**AND YOU GET THE MANA?**

**YES.**

**AND IF WE BREAK YOUR WALLS?**

**YOU PAY FOR THE REPAIRS PLUS A 200% PENALTY FOR UNAUTHORIZED STRUCTURAL DAMAGE. THE SYSTEM WILL AUTOMATE THE DEBUT.**

The Crimson Wastes hesitated. It was a beast of rage, but it was also a business. It calculated risk. It calculated profit.

**ACCEPTED.**

The mana transfer initiated. A torrent of red energy flooded Gerald's core.

His mana bar spiked.

It jumped from 12% to 100%.

It overflowed, spilling into his storage reserves.

Gerald gasped. The sudden influx of power felt like a drug. His vision sharpened.

The darkness of the dungeon seemed to recede. He felt stronger.

He felt *heavy*.

But the dungeon changed, and the gray gloom was replaced by a pulsing, rhythmic heat. The walls, previously jagged and cold, glowed with a faint, ember-like light. The air grew thick, heavy with the scent of sulfur.

Mara backed away, covering her nose.

"What did you do?"

"I brought in tenants," Gerald said. He felt a strange sensation in his mind. It wasn't just mana.

It was *presence*, and he could feel the Crimson Wastes' minions now. They were not in the Crimson Wastes.

They were *here*.

He looked at the far corner of the sub-basement. The shadows deepened. Two red eyes appeared.

Then four. Then twenty.

Goblins.

But not the ragged, starving goblins of the early game.

These were armored. They wore polished steel.

They carried shields that bore the Crimson Wastes' sigil, and they stood in Gerald's dungeon, looking around with confusion. They looked at the handrails. They looked at the egress signs. They looked at the smooth, fire-resistant floor.

One goblin stepped forward. It looked at Gerald. It did not attack.

"Is this place... safe?" the goblin asked. Its voice was raspy, but polite.

Gerald straightened his tunic. He pulled a small, spectral clipboard from his air.

"Define 'safe,'" Gerald said.

The goblin blinked.

"No traps? No falling rocks?"

"The rocks are load-bearing," Gerald said. "And the traps are compliant with fall-protection regulations. If you fall, you will be caught by a net. The net is rated for three hundred pounds."

The goblin looked at its comrades. They looked at each other. Then, the goblin in front bowed, a stiff, awkward motion.

"Thank you," it said. "We have been dying for years."

Mara stared at Gerald. She looked at the army of armored goblins standing in his living room. She looked at the mana bar, full and overflowing. She looked at the timer, still ticking down.

*00:45:12*

"You just invited an army into your bedroom," she said. "While the Inspector General is deciding whether to kill you."

"I invited *compliant* employees," Gerald said. He turned to Mara.

"We need to move.

The hearing is in forty minutes. I need to prep the arguments for why the Crimson Wastes' presence constitutes a 'joint venture' rather than an 'unauthorized occupation.'

"

"That's... insane," Mara said.

"It's efficient," Gerald said.

He began walking toward the exit. The goblins parted for him.

They didn't attack, and they saluted.

As Gerald walked, he felt a new notification pop up. It was not from the System. It was from the Inspector General.

**NOTICE: AUDIT EXPANSION.**

**DUE TO THE COMPLEXITY OF THE JOINT VENTURE STRUCTURE, THE INSPECTOR GENERAL WILL CONDUCT AN ON-SITE INSPECTION OF ALL JOINT ENTITIES DURING THE HEARING.**

Gerald stopped. He looked at the goblins. He looked at Mara.

The Inspector General wasn't just reviewing the paperwork. He was coming to the dungeon. He was coming to *inspect* the tenants.

If the Inspector General found a single violation among the Crimson Wastes' minions—a dented helmet, a missing safety rail, a blocked exit—the joint venture would be voided. Gerald would lose the mana. The hearing would be lost.

And the Inspector General was known for his nitpicking.

Gerald looked at the goblin with the dented helmet. It was standing by the handrail. The dent was on the left side.

Gerald's hand twitched. He pulled out a red pen.

"Excuse me," Gerald said to the goblin. "Is that helmet a Class 4 Safety Standard?"

The goblin looked up, terrified.

"It... it was issued by the Warlord."

"The Warlord is not a certified safety officer," Gerald said. He wrote on his clipboard. "Citation 14-C-Subsection 9: Improper Protective Equipment. You must replace that helmet within ten minutes, or you will be cited for non-compliant labor practices, which voids your residency permit."

The goblin scrambled backward, fear in its eyes.

Mara looked at Gerald. She looked at the clock.

*00:30:00*

Gerald wasn't preparing a defense. He was starting an inspection. Of his own allies.

He looked at Mara.

"You have twenty minutes to find every single violation in this room. If the Inspector General sees it before I cite it, I lose. If I cite it first, I control the narrative."

He turned his back on her. He walked toward a goblin that was leaning on a wall, not using a designated rest area.

"Excuse me," Gerald said. "That is a load-bearing wall. You are not authorized to lean on structural elements without a support brace. Are you holding a brace?"

The goblin shook its head.

Gerald clicked his pen.

"I'm going to need a citation for that."

Mara groaned, but she started moving. She pulled her dagger. Not to fight, but to point at things.

The audit had begun. And Gerald Hutchins was not going to let anyone—human, goblin, or god—get away with a code violation.

He looked at the door to the neighboring dungeon, the Crimson Wastes. Through the fog, he saw something else.

A figure, tall and slender, standing in the shadows, which was not a goblin.

It was not a hero.

It was a woman in a suit. A suit that shimmered with the light of the System.

She held a clipboard, and she was watching him.

Gerald stopped writing. He looked at the figure. She looked at him.

And for the first time in thirty-one years, Gerald Hutchins felt something other than annoyance.

He felt *hunted*.

The figure raised a hand. She pointed at Gerald.

She mouthed one word.

*Appeal.*
