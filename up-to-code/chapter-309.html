Chapter 309: Emergency Demolition Permit

# Chapter 309

The headlights cut through the dust motes dancing in the upper sub-basement. They swept across the exposed conduit runs, the half-installed handrails, and the pile of drywall samples Gerald had stacked by the ventilation shaft.

Elena stepped out of her sedan. She wore a heavy coat against the November chill, her hair pulled back in a tight bun that hadn't moved since 1998. She did not look at the library sign. She looked at the padlocked gate.

Gerald was already at the gate. He held a clipboard. He had not picked up the wrench.

"Form 4-C," Gerald said, extending the clipboard without greeting her. "Emergency Structural Assessment. I need you to sign as a neutral third-party observer."

Elena took the clipboard. She squinted at the header. The System font was jagged, glowing with a faint, sickly amber light.

"You called me at eleven fifteen to sign a form," Elena said. Her voice was flat. Tired.

"The floor is going to drop," Gerald said.

"The floor is concrete, Gerald. It's been there for forty years."

"Not according to the load-bearing calculations Unit 7 is currently rewriting in real-time." Gerald tapped the clipboard. "If I don't have a witness to the failure, I can't file an emergency injunction.

If I don't file the injunction, the dungeon collapses.

If the dungeon collapses, the library collapses. If the library collapses, I lose my pension. And I am not losing my pension.

"

Elena looked at him. looked at him. His face was grey, smeared with grime. His eyes were bloodshot, fixed on a point three inches above her left shoulder.

"You're not sick," she said.

"I am a Class 4 Entity," Gerald said. "I am currently under audit. Please sign."

She signed. The pen didn't touch paper. The signature appeared in the air, burning itself into the amber glow of the interface.

**[Witness Accepted. Observer Status: Neutral.]** **[Hearing Window Opened. Duration: 00:10:00.]**

Ten minutes.

Gerald led her down. He moved with the rigid posture of a man who believed slouching was a structural defect. Elena followed, her heels clicking on the concrete. The air smelled of ozone and wet mold.

In the main chamber, the air shimmered.

A tear in the fabric of the dungeon space opened. Core 42-B emerged.

It was not a monster, which was a geometric abstraction, a shifting cube of obsidian light that hovered over the central spawn point.

Gerald pulled a roll of red tape from his utility belt. He slapped it onto the floor, forming a perimeter around the cube.

"Cease all movement," Gerald said. "You are trespassing on a protected egress route."

The cube rotated. A voice echoed, not from the cube, but from the walls themselves. It sounded like grinding stones.

**[Citation Rejected. You have no authority over this space.]**

"I have a witness," Gerald said. He pointed at Elena. "And I have Form 11-B."

He pulled a second document from his belt. This one was handwritten. The ink was fresh.

Elena stepped closer, peering at the paper.

"Gerald, what is this?"

"An appeal based on Procedural Non-Compliance," Gerald said. "I am arguing that Unit 7 failed to provide a 30-day notice of intent to alter structural integrity. Which is required by Municipal Code 14.2, subsection D, which the System adopted in patch 4.03."

The cube pulsed. A red laser grid projected outward, slicing through the air toward Gerald.

Gerald did not move. He held up the clipboard.

**[System Alert: Hazardous Material Detected.]** **[Hazard: Unlicensed Reality Manipulation.]** **[Penalty: Immediate Stasis.]**

The laser grid froze. The cube shuddered. The obsidian edges cracked.

"What did you do?" Elena whispered.

"I didn't do anything," Gerald said. "The code did."

He turned to her.

"Do you see the fracture?

In the west wall?

It's diagonal.

That means shear stress. Unit 7 is trying to phase-shift the walls to escape my Citation.

But phasing requires a stable anchor point. By calling you as a witness to the *current* state, I locked the anchor. They can't move the wall without violating the Observer Clause.

"

Elena stared at the frozen laser. She looked at Gerald. For the first time in ten years, she saw something other than a boring man. She saw a weapon.

"You trapped them," she said.

"I filed a complaint," Gerald said. "They're trying to settle out of court. Literally. They're trying to move the courtroom."

The cube screamed. A high-pitched frequency that shattered the glass in the library's upper windows.

**[Hearing Suspended. Violation of Observer Clause detected.]** **[Re-evaluating...]**

Gerald checked his watch.

"Eight minutes.

If we don't finish the argument, the System defaults to the highest bidder.

Unit 7 pays in mana. I pay in structural integrity. I'm outbid.

"

"Then argue better," Elena said.

She grabbed the clipboard from his hands. She began reading the handwritten notes.

"This is gibberish.

You're citing codes that don't exist.

"

"They exist in the patch notes," Gerald said. "Page 4,002. Footnote 12."

Elena flipped through the air.

"Gerald. You're insane."

"I'm thorough," he corrected. "Look at Section 9.

Ventilation.

Unit 7 hasn't installed a fire suppression system.

They're relying on passive cooldowns. That's a Class 3 Fire Code violation.

If I cite that, their regen rate drops by 40%. They can't sustain the phase-shift.

"

Elena looked at the cube. It was straining against the red tape. Cracks spread across its surface.

"Say it," she said.

"I can't," Gerald said. "I have to serve the Citation. Physically."

He walked toward the cube.

"Gerald, stop."

"I have to hand it to them."

He reached out. The air around the cube warped.

Heat radiated from it, singeing the hair on his arms.

Gerald didn't flinch. He stepped over the red tape.

He placed the citation on the cube's surface.

**[Citation Served. Core 42-B cited for Fire Code Violation: Lack of Sprinkler System.]** **[Penalty: Regen -40%. Structural Integrity -15%.]** **[Appeal Window: Closed.]**

The cube slumped. It dropped to the floor with a heavy, metallic clang. The obsidian cracked completely. Inside, something pulsed—a weak, blue light.

Gerald turned to Elena.

"Did you see?"

"I saw," Elena said. Her face was pale. "What is this place?"

"My dungeon," Gerald said. "It's not up to code. But I'm fixing it."

The interface flashed.

**[Hearing Won.]** **[Reward: +10 Compliance Rating.]** **[Current Rating: 48/100.]** **[New Notification: Inspector General Has Flagged Your Dungeon.]**

Gerald frowned.

"Flagged? Why?"

Elena looked at the ceiling.

"Because you just changed the rules."

"What rules?"

"The System's rules," Elena said. "You used a municipal code to override a dungeon mechanic. That's not in the handbook."

"It's in the fine print," Gerald said. "Rule 1: The System is law. Rule 2: All law is subject to local ordinance. It's basic jurisprudence."

A new door opened in the east wall. It wasn't a dungeon door. It was a service elevator.

A man stepped out, and he wore a grey suit. His tie was perfectly knotted.

His shoes were polished to a mirror shine.

He held a briefcase.

"Mr. Hutchins," the man said.

His voice was smooth.

Calm.

Dangerous.

Gerald straightened his spine.

"Can I help you?"

"I am Agent Vane," the man said. "Inspector General, Regional Division 4."

Elena stepped back. She recognized the suit. Everyone in Ashford recognized the grey suits. They were the ones who came after the buildings failed.

"Agent Vane," Gerald said. "This is an active Hearing site. You are trespassing."

Vane smiled. It was a thin, bloodless expression.

"I am not trespassing. I am conducting a surprise audit."

He opened his briefcase. Inside, there were no tools. Just a tablet.

"You have cited three cores this week," Vane said. "Core 42-B, Core 19-A, and Core 08-Z."

"Violations," Gerald said.

"Aggressive enforcement," Vane said. He tapped the tablet. A holographic chart appeared in the air.

Red lines spiked upward.

"You are disrupting the economy.

Adventurers are avoiding your sector, and they say your dungeon is 'too safe.' They say it's boring. They say the traps don't kill them, they just inconvenience them.

"

"That's the point," Gerald said.

"It's not the point," Vane said.

"The point is conflict. The point is escalation.

You are lowering the threat level.

And that affects our quarterly projections.

"

Gerald's hands clenched.

"I am enforcing the code."

"You are enforcing *your* code," Vane said. "The System's code is for survival. Yours is for bureaucracy. Do you know what happens to a core that lowers the threat level?"

Vane closed the briefcase.

"Nothing," Gerald said.

"Wrong," Vane said. "They get decommissioned."

The air in the room grew cold. The red tape on the floor dissolved.

"Decommissioned?" Elena asked.

"Removed from the active map," Vane said. "Deleted. Wiped from the servers."

Gerald looked at the cube. It was still twitching.

"You can't," Gerald said. "I have a Compliance Rating of 48. I'm above the threshold."

"You're below the threshold for *Viability*," Vane said. "You are safe.

That makes you useless.

The System rewards danger.

You are providing none.

"

He turned to leave.

"Wait," Gerald said.

Vane stopped.

"I have a citation," Gerald said.

Vane turned. His eyes were cold.

"You cannot cite me, Mr.

Hutchins. I am the Inspector.

"

"You are not the Inspector," Gerald said. "You are an employee. And you just admitted to manipulating quarterly projections to influence dungeon threat levels."

Vane's smile faded.

"That is confidential System policy," Vane said.

"It's public record," Gerald said.

He pointed to the air.

"Page 12 of the Public Transparency Act. Subsection F.

'No agency may artificially inflate or deflate risk metrics to manipulate user engagement.'

"

Vane froze.

"You're accessing the Public Transparency Act?" Vane said. "That's a Level 10 Core privilege."

"I'm a Level 10 Core," Gerald said. "I gained it when I cited Core 42-B. The System upgraded me for 'Exceptional Regulatory Insight.'"

Vane looked at the tablet. The hologram flickered.

"You have ten seconds to vacate," Vane said.

"I have a witness," Gerald said. He pointed at Elena. "She saw you admit it."

Vane looked at Elena. He looked at Gerald.

"Elena," Gerald said. "Do you want to sign a deposition?"

Elena looked at the man in the grey suit. She looked at her ex-husband, standing in his dirty overalls, holding a clipboard.

She walked forward. She took the pen.

"I'm signing," she said.

Vane lunged, and he didn't move like a man. He moved like a glitch.

The air around him distorted.

He was going to attack. Not physically. Systemically.

He was going to delete them.

Gerald raised his wrench.

Not to strike.

To measure, and he slammed the wrench against the wall. *Clang.*

**[Structural Impact Detected.]** **[Vibration Analysis Initiated.]**

"Stop!" Gerald shouted. "You're violating the Quiet Hours Ordinance! It's after 11 PM!"

The System paused.

Vane halted. He hovered in mid-air, confused.

"Quiet Hours?" Vane said.

"Municipal Code 8.4," Gerald said. "No noise above 60 decibels after 11 PM. Your entrance was 85 decibels. That's a citation."

**[Citation Generated: Noise Violation.]** **[Target: Agent Vane.]** **[Penalty: Stealth -50%. Movement Speed -20%.]**

Vane dropped to the floor. He stumbled.

"This is absurd," Vane hissed. "You're citing an Inspector General."

"I'm citing a noise nuisance," Gerald said. "And now that you're slowed, I can serve the Deposition."

He handed the paper to Elena. She signed it.

**[Deposition Filed.]** **[Evidence of System Misconduct Logged.]** **[Pending Review by Oversight Committee.]**

Vane looked at the tablet. His face went white.

"You've ruined everything," Vane said.

"I've improved compliance," Gerald said.

Vane opened his briefcase. He pulled out a black drive. He crushed it in his hand.

"Then we go dark," Vane said.

The lights in the dungeon flickered. The red tape vanished. The cube dissolved into smoke.

Vane looked at Gerald.

"You have won a battle, Mr.

Hutchins.

But you have started a war. The Oversight Committee doesn't like surprises.

They like order. And you are chaos dressed as order.

"

"I am a building inspector," Gerald said.

"You are a cancer," Vane said.

He stepped into the elevator. The doors closed.

Silence returned.

Elena looked at Gerald.

"What did you do?"

"I filed a complaint," Gerald said.

"They'll kill you."

"They can't," Gerald said. "I have a hearing scheduled. The Oversight Committee has to review the deposition. It could take months."

"And then?"

"And then I'll be ready," Gerald said. He looked at the empty space where Vane had stood. "I need to install a soundproofing barrier."

He pulled out his measuring tape.

**[New Quest: Fortify the Foundation.]** **[Objective: Increase Sound Insulation by 40%.]** **[Reward: Unlock 'Bureaucratic Immunity' Perk.]**

Gerald looked at Elena.

"Can you help me install the acoustic panels? I need a second set of hands."

Elena looked at the dungeon. She looked at the System interface glowing in the dark. She looked at her ex-husband, who was currently arguing with a wall about R-value insulation.

She sighed.

"Where are the panels?" she asked.

"In the truck," Gerald said. "And Elena?"

"What?"

"Put on your hard hat. I don't want a safety violation on the first day of the audit."

She laughed. It was a short, sharp sound. But it was real.

Then the floor shook.

Not a tremor. A shift.

From the center of the room, a new tunnel opened. It wasn't dug by Unit 7. It wasn't dug by a rival core.

It was dug by the System itself.

A voice boomed from the hole. It was not the grind of stone. It was the chime of a bell.

**[Emergency Session Called.]** **[Subject: Gerald Hutchins.]** **[Charge: Inciting Unsanctioned Compliance.]** **[Sentence: Immediate Demolition.]**

Gerald looked at the hole.

"Well," he said. "That's going to require a permit."

He walked toward the abyss.

Elena followed him.
