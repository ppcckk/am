Chapter 411: Code Violation Appeal

# Chapter 411

The water was ankle-deep and rising. It tasted of copper and old dust.

Gerald did not look at the burning logo above him. He looked at the floor.

A crack in the concrete. Three millimeters wide. Running parallel to the main support beam.

"Move," Kaelen said. She grabbed his arm. Her grip was tight, bruising.

Gerald pulled away.

"Structural compromise detected," he said. His voice was flat. He pointed at the crack. "Load-bearing failure imminent in forty seconds."

Kaelen stared at him.

"We are drowning, Gerald."

"We are drowning because of a code violation," Gerald said. He crouched.

His knees popped, and the water was up to his shins now. Cold.

"If the water continues to rise at this rate, it will breach the secondary containment wall.

That wall is rated for hydrostatic pressure, not dynamic surge.

It will shatter. The entire sub-basement collapses.

"

Kaelen looked back at the Architect's hiding place. The shifting walls of the cavern were still moving, a slow, grinding churn of stone and shadow. The laugh of the entity echoed, distorted by the water.

"We need to run," Kaelen said. "Now."

"Running does not fix the leak," Gerald said.

He reached into his inventory. Not for a weapon. For a clipboard.

The System materialized the object in his hand with a soft *thump*. It was standard municipal issue. Duct tape on the corner. A pen clipped to the top.

Gerald uncapped the pen.

"What are you doing?" Kaelen asked.

"Filing an injunction," Gerald said.

He wrote. *Emergency Repair Order.

Section 402.B. Uncontrolled Water Ingress. Immediate cessation of flow required to prevent catastrophic structural failure.

Failure to comply results in immediate demolition of the offending entity.*

He held the paper up.

**[PROCESSING INJUNCTION.]**

The System's voice was different now. Deeper. It vibrated in the teeth.

**[INJUNCTION GRANTED.]** **[TARGET: ARCHITECT (UNREGISTERED CORE).]** **[STATUS: NON-COMPLIANT.]** **[ACTION: SHUTDOWN OF WATER SOURCE.]**

The roar of the water changed. It did not stop. It reversed.

A vacuum sound. Like a throat clearing.

The water level dropped two feet in a second. Then four. Then ten. The floor, slick with mud, groaned.

Kaelen slid.

Gerald braced his feet, and he did not fall. He had good traction.

Non-slip shoe soles. He had installed them in his own dungeon three months ago. A small upgrade.

A small investment.

The shifting walls stopped moving.

Silence.

Then, a sound from the darkness. Not a laugh. A cough. Dry, rattling.

"Who issued this?" the voice asked. It was smaller now. Weaker.

Gerald walked toward the center of the cavern. The water was gone. Only dampness remained. A fine mist clung to the air.

"Gerald Hutchins," he said. "Municipal Inspector. Level 12 Core."

"You have no authority," the voice said.

"I have a Citation," Gerald said. "And I have the System's new rule set. *Code is Law.*"

The Architect emerged, which was not a monster. It was a shape. A cluster of geometric lights, hovering above a pile of rubble.

It looked tired.

Dim.

"You cited the Auditor," the Architect said. "You broke the hierarchy."

"I cited a violation," Gerald said.

"The water was a hazard. Hazard requires mitigation. You failed to mitigate.

Therefore, you are liable.

"

The Architect's lights flickered.

"You think this is about water?"

"It is about water," Gerald said.

"Water is code.

Code is structure. Structure is life.

You are killing the life of this dungeon. And, incidentally, the life of the library above it.

"

Kaelen stepped forward. Her sword was drawn.

It glowed with a faint blue light.

"Gerald, don't talk to it. Destroy it."

"No," Gerald said. He raised a hand. "We are inspecting."

He walked closer. The Architect retreated. Not in fear. In calculation.

"You have been cited for three major violations," Gerald said.

He looked at his clipboard. He could not see the paper anymore.

The water had ruined the ink.

But he remembered the numbers. He always remembered the numbers.

"One: Unauthorized water mains.

Two: Lack of drainage. Three: Failure to maintain emergency egress routes.

That is a Compliance Rating of four.

If the new rule set is enforced strictly, you are marked for deletion.

"

The Architect pulsed. Red.

"Deletion is not death," the Architect said. "It is erasure.

I have existed for six hundred years. I have built civilizations in the dark. You cannot erase me with a clipboard.

"

"I can cite you," Gerald said. "I can cite you until your mana reserves are exhausted trying to appeal the fines.

I can cite you for noise pollution.

For improper waste disposal. For the smell.

"

"The smell?" Kaelen asked.

"Musty," Gerald said. "Very musty. It violates Health Code 12-C."

The Architect laughed, which was a harsh, grinding sound.

"You are amusing, Inspector.

But you are naive.

The Auditor is not gone. The Auditor is waiting. You have drawn its attention to yourself.

To *all* of us.

"

A shadow passed over the cavern. Not a cloud. A grid. A perfect, geometric grid of white light.

**[SYSTEM ALERT: INSPECTOR GENERAL APPROACHING.]** **[AUDIT WINDOW OPENING IN: 10 MINUTES.]**

Kaelen looked up. Her face went pale.

"That's not possible," she said. "Audits are scheduled. Months in advance."

"Not under the new rule set," Gerald said.

He looked at his watch, and he did not have a watch. He felt the time in his bones.

"Emergency audits are immediate. Especially when a core has violated the primary directive."

"What is the primary directive?" Kaelen asked.

"Order," Gerald said. "The System exists to create order.

The Architect creates chaos.

You create adventure. I create compliance.

"

"I create chaos?" Kaelen asked, offended.

"You fight things," Gerald said. "Fighting is chaotic. It requires improvisation. The System hates improvisation."

The grid of light descended. It touched the top of the cavern. The stone sizzled.

The Architect screamed.

"They are here. They will delete me. They will delete you."

"No," Gerald said. "They will audit me."

He turned to Kaelen.

"Leave."

"What?" Kaelen said.

"Leave," Gerald said.

He pointed to the exit corridor. The one he had widened last week.

The one with the handrails.

"Go to the library. Lock the door. Do not come back until I signal."

"I am not leaving you," Kaelen said.

"Gerald," she said. "Think.

If the Inspector General deletes you, the library collapses.

People die.

My people die."

Kaelen looked at him. Her eyes were wide. Not with fear. With anger.

"You are not a hero, Gerald. You are a bureaucrat. Heroes save people. Bureaucrats file paperwork."

Gerald looked at the Architect. The entity was shrinking. The grid was pressing down.

The air grew heavy. Oppressive.

"I am not trying to be a hero," Gerald said. "I am trying to be compliant."

He pulled out another form. This one was different. It was red.

**[FORM 11-D: VOLUNTARY SELF-CITATION.]** **[SUBMISSION: IMMEDIATE CORRECTION PLAN.]**

Kaelen stepped back.

"What is that?"

"An appeal," Gerald said. "But not a legal one. A structural one."

He walked toward the Architect. The entity shrank away.

"Touch me," Gerald said.

"What?" Kaelen asked.

"Touch the wall," Gerald said to the Architect. "The wall you built. The one that is leaking. Touch it."

"Why?" the Architect asked.

"Because I need to cite you for improper material usage," Gerald said. "And I need to cite myself for failure to inspect it sooner."

The Architect hesitated. Then, a tendril of light extended. It touched the wet stone.

**[CONTACT ESTABLISHED.]** **[LINKING...]**

Gerald felt the connection. It was cold. Sharp. It felt like biting into aluminum foil.

He focused, and he did not think about magic. He thought about load distribution. He thought about tensile strength.

He thought about the fact that this wall was made of compressed mud and hate, and it was not rated for water.

**[CITATION FILED AGAINST SELF: GERALD HUTCHINS.]** **[VIOLATION: FAILURE TO IDENTIFY SUBSTANDARD CONSTRUCTION MATERIALS.]** **[PENALTY: LOSS OF MANA REGENERATION FOR 48 HOURS.]** **[MITIGATION: ACCEPTANCE OF ARCHITECT'S STRUCTURE INTO MUNICIPAL JURISDICTION.]**

The System paused.

**[REVIEWING...]** **[ANOMALY DETECTED.]** **[MERGER PROPOSED: CORE GERALD HUTCHINS + CORE ARCHITECT.]** **[STATUS: NON-COMPLIANT MERGER.]** **[ACTION: DENIED.]**

"No," Gerald said.

He spoke the word. Not in his mind. Out loud.

"I am not merging," Gerald said. "I am absorbing."

He pointed at the Architect.

"I am citing you for trespassing on public infrastructure. Specifically, my dungeon.

You are an unlicensed entity within a regulated zone.

You have no rights. You have no property. You are debris.

"

The Architect's lights flared.

"You cannot absorb me. I am a Core."

"You are a violation," Gerald said. "And violations are removed."

He slammed his hand onto the floor. The concrete. The floor he had poured himself. The floor that was up to code.

**[EXECUTION OF CITATION.]** **[TARGET: ARCHITECT.]** **[ACTION: DEMOLITION.]**

The Architect screamed, which did not die. It unraveled.

The lights went out, one by one.

The rubble remained.

The water remained.

But the presence was gone.

The grid of light above them stopped descending. It hovered. Waiting.

**[AUDIT SUSPENDED.]** **[REASON: NO NON-COMPLIANT ENTITY FOUND.]** **[NOTE: CITATION PENDING FOR GERALD HUTCHINS.]**

**[PENDING CITATION: FAILURE TO REPORT PRIOR VIOLATION.]** **[FINE: 50% OF TOTAL MANA RESERVES.]** **[PAYMENT DUE: IMMEDIATELY.]**

Gerald's knees buckled.

The mana drained. It felt like blood leaving a wound. Fast. Too fast.

His vision blurred. The edges of the cavern turned grey.

"Gerald!" Kaelen shouted. She ran to him.

He caught himself on the wet wall. His fingers scrabbled for purchase.

"I am fine," Gerald said. His voice was slurred.

"You are bleeding from your nose," Kaelen said.

"It is a minor violation of personal hygiene standards," Gerald said. He wiped his face. His sleeve was red.

He looked at the pile of rubble where the Architect had been. Something was there. Not light. Not stone.

A book, which was bound in black leather. It had no title.

Kaelen looked at it.

"What is that?"

Gerald straightened. He felt dizzy. Weak.

He had never been this weak. Not even in the early days, when he was just a rat and a leak.

"That," Gerald said, "is the evidence."

He picked it up. It was heavy. Heavier than it should have been.

**[ITEM ACQUIRED: THE ARCHITECT'S LEDGER.]** **[CONTENTS: UNPUBLISHED CODES.]** **[WARNING: READING THIS ITEM MAY RESULT IN INSTANTIATION OF NEW RULES.]**

Gerald opened the book, and the first page was blank.

Then, words appeared. Written in ink that moved.

*Section 1: The Definition of a Hero.*

Gerald frowned. He turned the page.

*Section 2: The Cost of Compliance.*

He turned another page.

*Section 3: The List.*

It was a list of names. Thousands of them.

Dungeon Cores. Adventurers. Politicians. Gods.

And at the top, in red ink.

*Gerald Hutchins.*

Below it.

*Status: Active Threat.*

Kaelen looked over his shoulder.

"Gerald, what is that?"

Gerald closed the book. His hands shook.

He looked up at the ceiling. The grid of light was fading. But it was not gone. It was watching.

"It is a target list," Gerald said.

"For who?" Kaelen asked.

Gerald looked at her. He looked at the exit. The safe, handrailed, well-lit exit.

"For the ones who do not follow the code," Gerald said.

A sound came from the surface. Above the library.

Sirens.

Not police sirens. Not fire sirens.

The sirens of the Ashford City Hall. The alarm for a building evacuation.

Kaelen looked up.

"The library is empty. The alarm is for the building."

Gerald looked at the book. He looked at the water stains on the floor.

He looked at the hole in the ceiling where the System's logo had burned.

And then, he saw it.

Written on the wall, in the same moving ink as the book.

*Appeal Window: Open.*

*Hearing: Scheduled.*

*Adversary: The City of Ashford.*

Gerald stared at the words.

The City of Ashford was not a dungeon. It was not a monster.

But it was a structure. And it had a building permit.

And Gerald had just cited the Auditor.

Which meant, by the new rules, the Auditor had cited the City.

Which meant Gerald had to defend the City.

Or lose it, and he looked at Kaelen.

"We have to go to City Hall," Gerald said.

"To do what?" Kaelen asked.

"To file the appeal," Gerald said.

He opened the book again.

And this time, he did not close it, and he read.
