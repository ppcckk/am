Chapter 210: Structural Re-routing

# Chapter 210

The pen snapped on the second contact.

Gerald didn't flinch. He watched the black ink leak into the mossy fur of the creature, a stark contrast against the damp gray of the wall.

The *Code-Infested Vermin* paused, its toothless mouth hanging open in a silent shriek that vibrated in Gerald's chest cavity.

It wasn't pain. It was resonance. A frequency that matched the low-grade hum of failing infrastructure.

Gerald stepped back.

His foot found the edge of the puddle he had inadvertently created earlier. He corrected his stance, shifting his weight to the heel, and checked his footing.

Slip hazard.

Unmarked. He made a mental note.

The creature lunged.

It moved with a jerky, stop-motion quality, limbs unfolding like a carpenter's square. Gerald sidestepped.

It was not a dodge.

It was an adjustment.

He had calculated the trajectory based on the creature's center of mass and the uneven friction of the moss-covered floor.

*Unlicensed Biological Entity,* he thought. *Aggressive posture. No signage indicating temperament.*

He raised his hand. Not to strike. To point.

"Violation," Gerald said. His voice was flat, stripped of any inflection that might suggest fear or bravery. It was the voice of a man who had cited three hundred illegal renovations in Ashford and slept perfectly well every night. "Failure to maintain safe clearance from electrical infrastructure."

The System pinged. A soft, blue glow appeared in his peripheral vision.

**Citation Issued:** *Hazardous Obstruction.* **Target:** *Code-Infested Vermin.* **Status:** *Pending.*

The creature froze. The moss on its back curled inward, pulling tight. It looked at the mana crystal behind Gerald, then at the citation glowing in the air.

It didn't understand the concept of a fine. It understood the concept of energy flow.

And the citation was blocking the flow.

The creature hissed, the sound like tearing wet cardboard. It spat a glob of viscous sludge at the citation. The blue text shuddered but held.

Gerald walked forward. Slowly. Deliberately.

He didn't run.

Running was for people who didn't know the exit routes. He walked with the steady, plodding rhythm of a man checking the plumb line of a wall.

*Improvisational Maintenance.* The skill was active.

He could feel it in his fingers, a tingling numbness that traveled up his wrist. The cost was mounting. 10% effectiveness drop. He had used it to fix the ventilation shaft.

He was using it now to weaponize the paperwork.

The vermin lashed out. A tendril of moss, thick as a cable, whipped toward Gerald's face.

Gerald didn't dodge. He caught it.

The moss was slimy, cold, and smelled of rotting library books.

He twisted his wrist, applying torque.

The creature shrieked, a high-pitched whine that drilled into his teeth. He pulled.

The creature's body, anchored to the wall by roots of fungal hyphae, resisted.

Gerald gritted his teeth. His knuckles whitened. He wasn't fighting. He was performing a manual override.

"Non-compliant," he muttered. "Structural integrity compromised."

He yanked. The creature tore free from the wall with a sickening *pop*.

It landed on the wet floor, scrambling for purchase.

Its legs were too short for the distance to the mana crystal. It scrambled, claws clicking on the concrete.

Gerald dropped the severed tendril.

He looked at the creature. It was small, pathetic, and entirely wrong for the environment.

It shouldn't exist here.

The ventilation shaft was rated for air circulation, not biological migration.

He looked at the mana crystal. It pulsed with a rhythmic, amber light.

The creature was heading for it, drawn by the energy signature.

If it reached the socket, it would feed.

And if it fed, it would grow. And if it grew, it would become a *Persistent Hazard.*

Gerald couldn't afford a persistent hazard. He had a Compliance Rating of 47. One more violation, and the System would impose a mandatory renovation period.

His dungeon would shut down. The library above would be condemned.

He had thirty-one years of tenure to protect.

He needed to remove the hazard. Permanently.

He looked at the broken pen. Useless. He looked at the puddle.

Slippery.

He looked at the moss. Flammable?

No.

The fire code required three feet of clearance from combustibles. The moss was too close to the electrical junction box.

*Violation #2:* *Combustible Material within Proximity to High-Voltage Source.*

The idea hit him. It was ugly. It was dangerous. It was technically correct.

Gerald walked toward the junction box. It was a rusted metal panel, screwed into the concrete. The screws were stripped. The panel was loose.

He pulled the panel. It groaned, metal screeching against metal.

The creature stopped.

It watched him.

Its yellow eyes narrowed.

Gerald ripped the panel off.

Inside, wires hung in a tangled nest. Copper, frayed and exposed. The junction box was not up to code.

It hadn't been for years. He had cited the library for it in 2014.

The citation had been ignored.

He looked at the creature. It was three feet away. Two feet.

Gerald reached into the junction box. He didn't touch the wires. He touched the grounding rod.

It was detached.

Another violation.

He grabbed a loose piece of rebar that was protruding from the wall, left over from a failed renovation. He wedged it into the junction box, forcing it against the live wires.

Sparks flew.

The creature recoiled.

It hissed, a sound of genuine alarm. It could feel the voltage change. The air ionized.

The smell of ozone filled the small chamber, sharp and metallic.

Gerald held the rebar.

His arm burned.

The heat radiated through his skin. He didn't drop it.

He couldn't drop it. If he dropped it, the circuit would short, and the feedback loop would fry the mana crystal.

He needed to direct the surge.

*Improvisational Maintenance.* Use the hazard.

He angled the rebar. Not toward the crystal. Toward the creature.

The creature lunged, trying to bite the metal. It wanted the energy. It was starving for it.

Gerald pushed the rebar forward.

The tip of the metal touched the creature's mossy chest.

The discharge was instant. Blue-white light exploded in the dark chamber.

The creature didn't scream.

It dissolved. The moss blackened, curled, and fell away in clumps of ash. The wire skeleton snapped, glowing red-hot before cooling into slag.

The smell was awful. Burnt hair and ozone.

Gerald released the rebar.

He stepped back. His hand was blistered.

The skin on his palm was pink and angry.

He flexed his fingers. The pain was sharp, immediate, and clarifying.

He looked at the ash pile.

**Hazard Removed.** **Violation Recorded:** *Illegal Use of Electrical Infrastructure.* **Penalty:** *-5 Compliance Rating.* **Current Rating:** *42.*

Gerald stared at the number. He had lost points.

He had solved the problem, but he had paid for it.

The System did not reward improvisation.

It punished deviation.

He looked at his hand. He should go to the infirmary. He should file a Worker's Compensation claim. He should document the injury.

But he was a Core. He had no infirmary. He had no paperwork for self-injury.

He looked at the mana crystal. It was safe. The light was steady.

Gerald turned back to the ventilation shaft. The moss was still there. It was spreading.

It had consumed the edges of the shaft opening. It was growing toward the library's main floor.

If he didn't stop it, it would breach the sub-basement ceiling. That was a structural violation. It would void the library's occupancy permit.

Gerald walked to the shaft.

He picked up a shard of the rusted junction panel. It was sharp. He tested its edge against his thumb.

Blood welled up, dark and quick.

He needed to cut the moss back. To create a firebreak.

He started cutting. The moss was tough, fibrous.

It resisted the metal.

He had to saw at it, pulling with his good hand, pushing with the injured one. The pain in his palm flared with every stroke.

He worked in silence. The only sound was the wet tearing of plant matter and the rhythmic *thwack* of his breathing.

He thought about the citation he had served on the vermin. It hadn't killed it.

The spark had.

The citation had only distracted it.

The System's logic was flawed. A citation was a warning.

A warning was not a weapon.

But he had made it work. He had bent the rule.

*Improvisational Maintenance* was a slippery slope. He knew this.

He had spent his career fighting people who wanted to bend the rules.

He had fined them. He had shut them down. He had revoked their permits.

And now he was doing it.

He paused. The moss was cut back three feet. The shaft was clear.

But the shaft was open. Unsealed.

Gerald looked at the gap. It was six inches wide. Enough for a rat. Enough for a scout goblin.

He needed to seal it.

He looked around the room. He had the broken pen. He had the rusted panel. He had the wet floor.

He didn't have cement. He didn't have drywall.

He looked at the ash pile from the vermin.

Ash was a filler. It was inert. It was cheap.

He could use it.

Gerald walked to the ash pile. He scooped it up with his bare hands. It was still warm. He carried it to the shaft.

He packed the gap.

Ash into the cracks. He pressed it down.

He shaped it.

It wasn't pretty. It wasn't permanent. It was a patch job.

It was exactly what he would be fined for if he did it to a building.

He packed the last inch of the shaft. He smoothed the surface with his thumb. The pain in his hand throbbed, a dull, persistent beat.

He stepped back.

**Temporary Repair Detected:** *Ash Seal.* **Effectiveness:** *Low.* **Duration:** *24 Hours.* **Cost:** *-2 Compliance Rating.* **Current Rating:** *40.*

Gerald closed his eyes. He breathed in. The air tasted of ash and old paper.

He was at 40. The threshold for mandatory shutdown was 30. He had ten points of margin.

He needed to regain points. He needed to become compliant.

But he had no resources. No money. No builders.

He looked at the mana crystal. It was his only source of power. If he drained it to build proper walls, he would lose spawn capacity. If he didn't build, he would lose points.

He was trapped in a loop of decay.

A sound. Not from the shaft. From the wall.

A knock. Three sharp raps.

Gerald froze. He looked at the wall.

It was solid concrete.

No doors.

No windows.

Another knock. Three raps. Then a pause. Then two more.

It was a code.

Gerald's stomach tightened. He knew that rhythm. It was the standard knock for municipal inspectors.

But he was an inspector. And he was a Core. And he was in a sub-basement that did not exist on any blueprint.

He walked to the wall. He placed his good hand against the cold concrete.

"Who is there?" he asked. His voice was steady. Professional.

A voice came through the wall. It was muffled, distorted, but clear.

"Core 402. Requesting entry."

Gerald's breath hitched. Core 402. He knew that number.

It belonged to *The Rotting Root*, a dungeon core that had been expanding beneath the park three blocks east. It had been cited for three violations last week.

Why was it here?

Gerald looked at the wall. He couldn't open a door. He had no door.

"I am not accepting visitors," Gerald said. "You are trespassing on private municipal property."

"I have a warrant," the voice said.

Gerald frowned. "A warrant?"

"Inspection Warrant. Issued by the Inspector General."

The wall shook. Dust fell from the ceiling.

Gerald looked at his Compliance Rating. 40.

If the Inspector General was here, it wasn't a visit. It was an audit.

And audits didn't care about improvisation. They cared about code.

The wall cracked. A spiderweb of fissures appeared in the concrete. The cracks glowed with a faint, green light.

Gerald backed away. He looked at the mana crystal. It was pulsing faster now. Warning him.

He looked at the ash seal in the shaft. It was crumbling.

He looked at the door that wasn't there.

The concrete gave way. Not with a bang, but with a sigh. The wall slumped inward, turning to dust.

Standing in the breach was a figure. It was tall, slender, and made of polished obsidian. It had no face, only a smooth, black surface where features should be. It wore a vest of woven gold thread.

It held a clipboard.

Gerald stared at it. The entity's presence was heavy. The air in the room grew cold. The mana crystal dimmed.

The entity looked at Gerald.

It didn't have eyes, but Gerald felt the weight of its gaze. It scanned him. It scanned the room.

It scanned the ash seal.

It scanned the blistered hand.

It clicked its pen.

"Core 001," the entity said. Its voice was like grinding stone. "Gerald Hutchins. You are in violation of Article 4, Section C: *Unauthorized Modification of Structural Integrity.*"

Gerald straightened his spine. He wiped the ash from his pants.

"I was performing emergency maintenance," Gerald said. "To prevent a fire hazard."

The entity looked at the ash pile. It looked at the junction box. It looked at the scorch marks on the floor.

"It is not maintenance," the entity said. "It is concealment."

It wrote something on the clipboard.

Gerald felt a cold dread settle in his gut. He had prepared for this. He had measured the walls. He had filed the forms.

But he had used a loophole. And loopholes were for lawyers, not inspectors.

The entity looked up.

"You have one hour to rectify this violation," the entity said. "If you fail, I will issue a Shutdown Order."

Gerald looked at the hole in the wall. Outside, the darkness of the earth waited. And in the darkness, something else was listening.

"I need materials," Gerald said.

The entity tapped its pen.

"Materials are a resource issue," it said. "Not a code issue. Find them. Or close the dungeon."

It turned. It walked back into the darkness. The hole in the wall remained.

Gerald stood alone.

He looked at his hand. It was bleeding. He looked at the ash. He looked at the mana crystal.

He had one hour.

He had no walls. No cement. No builders.

He had only the dungeon. And the dungeon was falling apart.

He looked at the ceiling. Above him, the library was quiet. The patrons were oblivious. The foundation was cracking.

If he didn't fix the wall, the library would collapse.

If he fixed the wall, he would fail the inspection.

He walked to the center of the room. He sat down on the floor. He placed his bleeding hand on his knee.

He thought about the pen. The broken pen.

He thought about the citation. The one that hadn't worked.

He thought about the loophole.

*Improvisational Maintenance.*

He had used it twice. Effectiveness was down.

He had to use it again.

But this time, he couldn't just fix it. He had to prove it was legal.

Gerald closed his eyes. He reached into the System's interface. He didn't look for repair tools. He looked for the definition of *Infrastructure.*

And he found it.

*Infrastructure: Any system designed to support the operation of the dungeon.*

The wall was infrastructure. The ash was not.

But what if the ash was part of the ventilation system?

Gerald opened his eyes. He looked at the shaft. He looked at the hole in the wall.

He could connect them.

He could route the ventilation through the breach.

It was insane. It was dangerous. It was definitely not up to code.

But it was the only way.

Gerald stood up. He walked to the mana crystal. He placed his hand on it.

"System," he said. "Initiate Structural Re-routing."

The crystal pulsed.

**Warning:** *This action will void current Structural Compliance.* **Cost:** *-10 Compliance Rating.* **Current Rating:** *30.*

Gerald hesitated. 30 was the shutdown line.

If he did this, he was one bad move away from death.

But if he didn't, the wall would fall, and the library would collapse, and he would be dead anyway.

He looked at the hole. The darkness was seeping in.

He looked at his hand. The blood was drying.

He took a breath.

He pressed the crystal.

**Structural Re-routing Initiated.**

The floor shook. The walls groaned. The ash seal shattered.

And the dungeon breathed.
