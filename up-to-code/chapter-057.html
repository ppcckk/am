Chapter 57: Citing the Rulebook

# Chapter 57

Gerald read the twenty-second Counter-Citation and found nothing wrong with it.

He had read the first twenty-one and found nothing wrong with those either. Every field was filled.

Every timestamp was correct.

The basis statement cited the same procedural defect in all twenty-two cases: Field 14, signature, absent at time of original filing. Core 8801 had done its homework. It had pulled Gerald's filing history, identified the defect, and packaged it into twenty-two separate filings, each one individually sufficient to trigger a Procedural Violation mark.

Gerald had been reading for forty minutes. His Compliance Rating sat at seventy-eight, and the countdown to the Inspector General's on-site audit ticked down with a steady, indifferent pulse. Two hours and eleven minutes.

He looked at the rat. The rat had not moved. It sat on a shelf Gerald had installed to meet the minimum clearance requirements for biological entity housing, and it watched him with the flat, unbothered expression of an animal that had never been asked to fill out a form.

Gerald pulled up the System's procedural glossary.

He had read it three times already, but he read it again, because the first two times he had been reading it looking for a cure period, and he had not found one. The System's procedural code was sparse on remediation windows.

Once a defect was filed, it was filed.

The amendment had fixed the defect, but the amendment had been accepted after the original filing, and Core 8801 was arguing that the defect existed at the moment of filing, and the amendment was irrelevant to the question of whether the original filing was valid.

That argument was, technically, correct. Gerald had to concede it.

A building inspector who filed a report with a missing signature had filed a defective report, even if he signed it later.

The defect existed at the moment of filing.

You could not retroactively cure a filing defect and then argue the original filing was clean.

So the cure period approach was dead.

Gerald opened the System's service requirements. Not the filing requirements. The service requirements. The rules that governed how a document reached the party it was directed at.

*Citations require the target to be physically present to serve.*

He read it again. Then he read the definition of "Citation" in the glossary.

*Citation: A formal notice of violation issued by a Core to another Core, carrying a stat debuff proportional to the severity and number of violations cited.*

He read the definition of "Counter-Citation."

*Counter-Citation: A filing by a cited Core contesting the validity of a Citation. If successful, voids the Citation and may apply a Procedural Violation mark to the citing Core.*

Gerald stared at both definitions. Then he stared at the service requirement again.

The service requirement said "Citations." "Citations and Counter-Citations." Just "Citations."

But the glossary defined a Counter-Citation as a type of filing that carried a stat debuff. A Procedural Violation mark was a stat debuff. It was proportional to the severity of the procedural defect. It reduced Compliance Rating, which was itself a stat.

Gerald pulled up his own Citation #4409-0017. He had issued it to Core 8801. Core 8801 had been physically present in Gerald's dungeon when Gerald served it.

That was why the Citation was valid on its merits. The defect was procedural, not substantive.

The Citation itself was legitimate.

But the Counter-Citation against that Citation had been filed electronically. Core 8801 was not in Gerald's dungeon. Gerald checked the dungeon's entry log. Core 8801 had not entered his dungeon in the past six cycles.

Gerald started typing.

*Motion to Dismiss for Improper Service.*

*Argument: The System's procedural code requires physical presence for service of a Citation. A Counter-Citation, by definition, carries a stat debuff (Procedural Violation mark). Per the glossary, any filing that carries a stat debuff meets the definition of "Citation." a Counter-Citation is a Citation. It requires physical service.

Core 8801 was not physically present.

Service is defective. The filing is void.*

He filed it, and the response came back in four seconds.

*Motion to Dismiss reviewed.

Argument accepted in part. Counter-Citations #8801-0001 through #8801-0022 are voided for improper service. Core 8801 may refile with proper service.

Service window: 2 cycles.*

Gerald exhaled. Twenty-two marks gone. Compliance Rating held at seventy-eight.

He looked at the countdown. Two hours and nine minutes.

Then he looked at the service window. Two cycles. Core 8801 had two cycles to physically enter his dungeon and re-serve twenty-two Counter-Citations.

Gerald opened his dungeon's access controls.

He had set them up in chapter one, more or less, when he had marked the sub-basement as a non-public easement to repel the goblin scout.

He pulled up the access log. Core 8801 had not attempted entry.

It was filing remotely, which meant it was somewhere else, and it would need to travel.

He could deny entry. He had the right.

A dungeon could restrict access to its non-public areas.

If Core 8801 could not enter, it could not serve. If it could not serve, it could not refile. The Counter-Citations would expire at the end of the service window, and Gerald would be clean.

He started drafting the access denial. He was halfway through it when the panel flashed.

*Inspector General has arrived on-site. Audit commencing.*

Gerald's hands stopped. He looked at the countdown. It had been two hours and nine minutes. Now it read zero.

The System did not wait. The System did not give you a grace period, and the System arrived when it said it would arrive, and the System was here.

Gerald stood up. He was not a body anymore, not exactly, but he had a presence in his dungeon, a distributed awareness that let him feel the walls and the floor and the air. He felt the audit protocol activate. It felt like a cold hand pressing against the perimeter of his dungeon, testing the boundaries, measuring the space.

The Inspector General did not have a shape. It was a process. A walking checklist. It moved through his dungeon like Gerald during a fire safety inspection, touching and measuring, checking for what was present and what was missing.

It checked his ventilation, and he had installed a cross-ventilation system in chapter six, after the first Compliance Audit. It checked his egress signage.

He had installed it in chapter seven, after the second Audit.

It checked his fire suppression. He had installed a sprinkler-equivalent in chapter fourteen, after the Citation against Core 6612 had triggered a fire-code audit on his own dungeon.

Each check passed. The Inspector General moved on.

Gerald felt the audit move through his corridors. It checked his handrails.

It checked his floor load ratings.

It checked his electrical conduit spacing.

It checked his biological entity housing. It checked the rat.

The rat passed.

The audit reached the Citation storage.

Gerald had not thought about this. He had stored his Citation records in a dedicated file system within his dungeon, a filing cabinet made of System data, organized by target Core and violation category. He organized it by category, date, and severity, as he did everything.

Thirty-one years of filing. He had built the system before the System built him, and he had kept it because it was useful.

The Inspector General paused at the filing cabinet, which did not move. It just paused. The cold hand pressed against the data and held it there.

Gerald felt the audit reading his files.

Not the Citations themselves. The metadata. The filing dates.

The target Core identifiers.

The violation categories. The number of Citations per Core.

The number of Citations per cycle.

The Inspector General finished reading. It moved on.

The audit continued. It checked his spawn list. Three mold spores, a water leak, one rat.

All within the biological entity housing code. It checked his dungeon footprint.

Four thousand square feet.

Within the minimum size requirement for a registered Core.

It checked his Compliance Rating. Seventy-eight.

Above the passing threshold of sixty.

The audit ended.

*On-site audit complete. Core 4409 Compliance Rating: 78/100. Status: Passing. No structural deficiencies found.*

Gerald felt the cold hand withdraw. He felt the audit protocol dissolve.

He was still at seventy-eight, and he was still alive. Core 8801's Counter-Citations were void. The service window was open, but Gerald could deny entry, and he would deny entry, and in two cycles Core 8801 would lose its filing rights and Gerald would be clean.

Then the panel flashed.

*Inspector General has issued a Citation against Core 4409.*

Gerald read it. He read it twice. He read it a third time.

*Citation #IG-0001.

Issuing authority: Inspector General. Target: Core 4409.

Violation: Excessive Regulatory Action.

Basis: Core 4409 has filed forty-seven Citations within the current audit period. System threshold for Excessive Regulatory Action: forty Citations per audit period. Violation severity: Critical.

Remedy: Citation revocation and Compliance Rating reduction of 40 points. Additional remedy: Core 4409 is hereby placed on Probationary Status.

Probationary Status limits future Citations to a maximum of five per audit period.

Violation of Probationary Status triggers automatic Compliance Rating reset to 0.*

Gerald stared at the panel.

Forty-seven Citations. He had filed forty-seven Citations.

He had not counted them. He had not counted them because he had never thought of them as something that needed counting. They were work.

They were the job. He had been doing the job.

The Inspector General had not been auditing his dungeon. It had been auditing his filing history.

Forty-seven Citations. Forty was the threshold. He was seven over.

The Compliance Rating reduction of forty points would drop him from seventy-eight to thirty-eight, which was below the passing threshold. That meant debuffs. All of them.

Every category.

Ventilation, fire safety, electrical, structural. All of them, proportional to the deficit.

And Probationary Status.

Five Citations per audit period. He had been filing Citations at a rate of nearly one per cycle. He would have to slow down.

Or stop.

Gerald looked at the Citation. He looked at the appeal window. Three cycles to file an appeal. Three cycles to argue that forty-seven Citations was not excessive.

He looked at his filing history.

Forty-seven Citations.

Against twenty-three different Cores. Some of them dead.

Some of them Cited multiple times. He had cited Core 6612 four times. He had cited Core 3307 three times.

He had cited Core 1122 five times, and the Inspector General had counted.

Gerald looked at the rat. The rat looked at him.

He opened a new filing. His hands were steady. His voice, if he had one, would have been quiet.

He started typing an appeal. He needed to prove that forty-seven Citations was not excessive. He needed to prove that the System's threshold of forty was arbitrary. He needed to prove that a Core who was doing its job should not be penalized for doing its job well.

He needed to prove that the Inspector General's own audit protocol, which had triggered the count, was itself the reason he had exceeded the threshold.

The System had audited him, and the audit had counted his filings. The count had triggered the Citation. The Citation had been issued during the audit.

The audit was the cause of the violation.

Gerald typed the argument. He typed it carefully. He typed it by category, date, and severity, as he typed everything. He typed it like a man who had been doing this for thirty-one years and who had never once been asked to stop.

He filed it, and the response came back.

*Appeal received. Hearing scheduled. Hearing window: 1 cycle.*

One cycle. Gerald looked at the countdown. It reset again. Smaller this time.

He looked at his Compliance Rating. Seventy-eight.

It would stay at seventy-eight until the Hearing.

Then it would either stay at seventy-eight or drop to thirty-eight. There was no middle ground.

He looked at the filing history. Forty-seven Citations.

He had cited twenty-three Cores.

Some of those Cores were dead.

Some of those Cores had been Cited by other Cores as well; the System's threshold applied to all Cores equally.

Gerald pulled up the filing histories of the other Cores he had Cited. Core 6612 had filed eleven Citations. Core 3307 had filed four.

Core 1122 had filed zero. None of them were near the threshold.

He pulled up the filing histories of the Cores that had Cited him. Core 8801 had filed two Counter-Citations, both voided. Core 9103 had filed one Citation against Gerald, which Gerald had appealed and won.

No one else was near the threshold. Gerald was the only Core in the System who had been filing Citations at scale.

He was the only one doing the job.

And the System was punishing him for it.

Gerald looked at the rat. The rat looked at him.

He opened the Hearing preparation panel. He had one cycle. He had to walk into a Hearing against the Inspector General itself and argue that the System's own threshold was unreasonable.

He had never argued against the System. He had argued against Cores. He had argued against goblins and fire exits and load-bearing walls. He had never argued against the rulebook itself.

He typed the rulebook's own words back at it, line by line.
