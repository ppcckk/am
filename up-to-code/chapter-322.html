Chapter 322: Citing a God

# Chapter 322

The pen tip hovered over the Mayor's knuckle.

Gerald didn't blink. He didn't breathe. He just waited.

The air in the council chamber grew thick, pressurized by the weight of the System interface hovering invisibly between them. To anyone else, it was just a man holding a form. To Gerald, it was a weapon.

The Mayor's eyes darted to the briefcase. The bag was leather, brown, and bulging with documents that smelled of expensive cigars and political maneuvering.

It had no tag.

No inspection sticker. No municipal property number.

"Sign it," Gerald said again. His voice was flat, devoid of the tremor that used to accompany public speaking. That tremor belonged to Gerald the human. Gerald the Core had no vocal cords to shake.

"I'm the Mayor of Ashford," Councilman Halloway said, his voice rising an octave. "I am the plaintiff in this suit. You cannot cite me for carrying a bag."

"Code 3.4, Section B," Gerald recited.

"Public Access Control. Large personal items entering a government-sanctioned hearing room must be tagged for security clearance.

You brought it in.

You are holding it. You are in violation.

"

Halloway looked at the subpoena in his own hand. It was signed. Sealed. Delivered.

"If I sign this," Halloway said slowly, "does the lawsuit go away?"

"The Citation is separate from the Civil Suit," Gerald said. "But the Citation triggers a Compliance Audit.

A failed audit freezes all municipal asset transfers.

Including your campaign funds.

Including the city budget for the library repairs you're trying to cut.

"

Halloway paled. The color drained from his face so fast it looked like a stain.

"You're bluffing," Halloway whispered.

Gerald tapped the paper. The System pinged. A soft, green chime that only Gerald could hear.

*Target Lock: Confirmed.*

*Violation: Unlicensed Item in Restricted Zone.*

*Severity: Minor.*

*Action Required: Sign for Service.*

Gerald extended the pen.

Halloway's hand shook. He looked at the other council members. They were silent.

They were terrified. They had seen what happened to the previous Mayor when he tried to rezone the library into a parking lot.

He had been cited for

"Non-Compliant Lot Drainage." The resulting mudslide had swallowed his car.

Halloway took the pen, and he signed.

The moment the ink touched the paper, the System flared.

*Citation Served.*

*Target: Ashford City Hall (Proxy: Councilman Halloway).*

*Status: Probationary.*

*Penalty: -15% Administrative Efficiency for 24 Hours.*

Halloway dropped the pen. It clattered to the floor.

"Get out," Halloway hissed. "Get out of my office."

Gerald picked up the subpoena. He folded it neatly. Four corners aligned. No creases.

"I'll be in touch regarding the pipe," Gerald said. "You'll want to check the load-bearing capacity of your ego. It's exceeding the safety factor."

He turned and walked out.

Behind him, he heard Halloway screaming for legal counsel. Gerald ignored it. He had bigger problems.

The hallway was empty. The security guards looked at him with a mixture of awe and nausea. They knew what he was now.

They had felt the pressure when he passed them, and the air had tasted like ozone and wet concrete.

Gerald kept walking.

His steps were measured. Six inches per step. Consistent.

Predictable, and he needed to get back to the library. The Compliance Rating was rising, but barely. 52. Then 53.

The Citation had worked. It bought him time. But it didn't fix the flood.

As he exited City Hall, the sky was dark.

Rain lashed against the pavement.

Gerald pulled his collar up. The cold bit through his jacket.

He wasn't cold, not really. The dungeon core didn't feel temperature. But the human avatar he maintained did.

And that discomfort was useful. It kept him alert.

A black sedan pulled up to the curb. It didn't belong to the city. It was sleek, armored, and tinted so darkly it reflected the streetlights like obsidian.

The window rolled down.

Inside sat a woman, and she wore a suit that cost more than Gerald's entire municipal salary. Her hair was blonde, sharp, and perfectly styled. She smiled.

It was a predatory smile.

"Mr. Hutchins," she said.

Gerald stopped. He didn't approach the car. He stood on the sidewalk, hands in his pockets.

"Ma'am," Gerald said. "You are idling in a no-parking zone. The sign is up."

The woman laughed. It was a dry, crackling sound.

"Nice try, Gerald. We're not here to talk about parking."

She gestured. The front door opened. It didn't look like a normal car door. It swung outward like a vault.

"Get in. We need to discuss the flood."

Gerald's internal sensors spiked. He scanned the interior.

No dungeon signature.

No core. Just a woman. And something else.

Something dense.

"Who are you?" Gerald asked.

"My name is Elena Vance," she said. "I represent the Consortium of Independent Dungeons. We've been watching you."

Gerald's heart rate didn't increase. Cores didn't have hearts. But his avatar's face tightened.

"The Consortium," Gerald said. "I haven't heard of them."

"Because you're new," Elena said.

"And you're dangerous.

Citing a Mayor? That's not just bureaucracy.

That's political warfare. You're making waves, Gerald. And waves cause erosion.

"

She pointed at the street.

"Look down."

Gerald looked.

The rainwater wasn't draining. It was pooling. Thick, black sludge was rising from the storm drains, bubbling up with a foul odor.

*System Alert: External Interference Detected.*

*Source: Subterranean Waterway Modification.*

*Violation: Unauthorized Diversion of Municipal Runoff.*

*Severity: Major.*

Gerald looked back at Elena.

"You did this," he said.

"It's a gift," Elena said. "We're helping the city with its water management problems. By removing the city from the equation."

"You're flooding the library."

"We're testing your resilience," Elena said. "The flood wasn't an accident.

It was a test.

And you passed. Barely. But the next one won't be so gentle.

"

Gerald felt a pull.

A gravitational tug in his chest. The core deep within the library was agitated.

It wanted to vent.

It wanted to purge.

"If you're part of a Consortium," Gerald said, "then you're operating without a public permit. You're running an unlicensed utility."

Elena's smile faded.

"Careful, Gerald. We aren't a building. We're a network."

"Code 12.9," Gerald said. "Utility Infrastructure Coordination. Any private entity altering public waterways requires a Master Plan and a Joint Authority Agreement. Do you have one?"

Elena's eyes narrowed.

"We have a deal with the City."

"The City doesn't exist anymore," Gerald said. "Not for this purpose. I cited the Mayor. The authority is suspended."

Elena leaned forward. The car door clicked shut.

"You think you've won," she said. "But you've just declared war on a system you don't understand. The Consortium doesn't play by municipal codes. We play by power levels."

She rolled up the window.

The car pulled away. The tires spun, spraying water onto the sidewalk. Gerald stepped back. The sludge splashed his shoes.

He looked at the System.

*Warning: Hostile Action Imminent.*

*Target: Unknown Entity.*

*Threat Level: High.*

Gerald walked to the curb. He pulled out his clipboard. He wrote.

*Observation: Illicit water diversion.*

*Action: Immediate inspection required.*

He couldn't go home. The library was flooded.

The entry was blocked by police tape.

But he couldn't stay here, and he needed to access the core directly.

He needed to use a loophole.

Gerald turned and walked toward the nearest manhole cover. It was iron, heavy, and rusted.

He placed his hand on it, and he didn't lift it. He didn't try to open it.

He sent a command.

*Request: Temporary Substrate Access.*

*Category: Municipal Property.*

*Status: Public Domain.*

The System hesitated.

*Query: Does the user hold jurisdiction over the physical access point?*

Gerald thought. He didn't own the manhole. The city did.

But he had cited the Mayor. The Mayor's authority was suspended.

The infrastructure was in limbo.

"Jurisdiction is void," Gerald said aloud. "The asset is unmanaged. Therefore, it is fair game for inspection."

The System pulsed.

*Logic Accepted.*

*Access Granted: Temporary.*

The manhole cover didn't move. Instead, the concrete around it softened. It turned to mud. Then to air.

Gerald stepped through.

The world twisted. Colors inverted. Gravity shifted.

He was inside the infrastructure. Not the dungeon. The *pipes*.

He stood in a dark tunnel. Water rushed around his ankles, cold and fast. The air smelled of rust and old blood.

He was in the city's veins.

And he wasn't alone.

Ahead, in the darkness, something moved. A shape. Not a monster. Not a dungeon spawn.

It looked like a man made of porcelain. White, smooth, and cracking.

It stood on a dry ledge above the water. It held a wrench.

Gerald stopped.

The porcelain man looked at him. It had no eyes. Just smooth, white surfaces where eyes should be.

"Inspector," the creature said. Its voice sounded like a stone grinding on stone.

"Identify yourself," Gerald said. He didn't reach for a weapon. He reached for his citation pad.

"I am the Foreman," the creature said. "We maintain the flow."

"You are diverting flow," Gerald said. "That is a violation."

The Foreman tilted its head. Cracks spiderwebbed across its face.

"The flow is life," the Foreman said. "You stop it, you kill the city."

"The city is already dying," Gerald said. "You're helping it die faster."

The Foreman raised the wrench. It was massive. Forged from black iron.

"The Consortium pays well," the Foreman said. "And we have a deadline."

"What deadline?"

The Foreman pointed down the tunnel.

"The dam breaks in six hours," it said. "Unless you pay us to redirect it."

Gerald looked at the water rushing toward him. It wasn't just rainwater. It was charged. Static electricity danced along the surface.

*Hazard Detected: High Voltage Runoff.*

*Code Violation: Exposed Live Wires in Wet Environment.*

*Severity: Critical.*

Gerald looked at the Foreman.

"If you redirect the dam," Gerald said, "where does the water go?"

"Into the lowlands," the Foreman said. "The slums. The dungeons. The things you don't like."

Gerald's face hardened.

"That is a prohibited discharge," Gerald said. "Section 14.2. No dumping of hazardous material into residential zones."

The Foreman laughed. It was a horrible sound. Like breaking glass.

"You can't cite us, Inspector. We aren't a building. We're a force of nature."

Gerald pulled out his pen.

"Nature has regulations," Gerald said. "So do forces. And you are blocking an egress route."

He pointed at the tunnel ahead. The water was rising faster now. The static was popping louder.

"The exit is obstructed by your presence," Gerald said. "Clear it, or I will issue a Stop Work Order."

The Foreman stepped forward. The water lapped at its porcelain feet.

"You're stalling," the Foreman said. "The dam is cracking. We can hear it."

Gerald felt it too. A deep, groaning sound from far away. The earth was shaking.

He looked at his System.

*Compliance Rating: 54.*

*Current Action: Obstruction of Emergency Egress.*

*Penalty Available: Immediate Physical Removal.*

Gerald had never tried that. Physical removal was usually reserved for monsters. For intruders.

But the Foreman was blocking a public right-of-way.

Gerald tapped the air.

*Citation: Obstruction of Emergency Egress.*

*Serve: Immediate.*

The System hesitated.

*Target: Non-Standard Entity.*

*Warning: Physical Removal may result in Unstable Structural Collapse.*

Gerald didn't care.

"Do it," Gerald said.

The air around the Foreman snapped.

Invisible hands seized the porcelain creature. The Foreman struggled. Its cracks widened. Shards of white flew off its body.

"Stop!" the Foreman shrieked. "The dam! You'll break it!"

"The dam is already broken," Gerald said. "I'm just making sure it falls on the people who built it."

The System pulled, and the Foreman was ripped backward. Through the water. Into the darkness.

Silence fell.

Then, a rumble.

Not from the tunnel. From above.

The ceiling of the pipe shuddered. Cracks appeared in the concrete. Dust fell like snow.

Gerald looked up, and the library was above him. The Municipal Library.

And it was collapsing.

*Structural Integrity: 30% and Falling.*

*Event: Catastrophic Failure of Municipal Dam.*

*Impact Zone: Direct.*

Gerald had no time to run. He had no time to think.

He had to build, and he focused on the crumbling walls around him. The pipes. The steel. The stone.

*Command: Reinforce.*

*Cost: All Remaining Mana.*

*Result: Temporary Structure.*

Gerald closed his eyes. He poured everything he had into the code. He visualized the load-bearing walls.

The shear strength.

The tensile strength, and he didn't improvise. He calculated.

He built a shield.

The ceiling exploded.

Rubble rained down. Water roared.

And then, silence.

Gerald opened his eyes, and he was standing in a cube of reinforced concrete. The water was everywhere. But he was dry.

He was alive.

But he was trapped.

And outside his concrete box, a voice spoke.

It wasn't the Foreman, which was the System.

*Auditor Detected.*

*Identity: The Inspector General.*

*Status: Active.*

Gerald froze.

*Query: Why is the Inspector General auditing a collapsed dam?*

The voice continued.

*Because you didn't just cite the Mayor.*

*You cited the City itself.*

*And the City is part of the System.*

*You have violated the Prime Rule.*

*No Core may destroy the Anchor Point.*

The walls of his concrete box glowed red.

*Penalty: Deletion.*

Gerald looked at his hands. They were dissolving.

He had one chance. One loophole.

He opened his inventory, and he pulled out the subpoena. The one Halloway had signed.

He held it up to the red glow.

*Evidence of Prior Consent.*

*The Mayor authorized the action.*

*the action was sanctioned.*

*Sanctioned actions do not violate the Prime Rule.*

The red glow paused.

It hovered.

It processed.

*Logic: Flawed.*

*Correction: Mayor did not authorize destruction.*

*Re-evaluation: Pending.*

Gerald watched his fingers fade, and he had seconds.

He needed to prove the Mayor authorized the destruction.

Or he needed to prove that the destruction was necessary to prevent a greater violation.

He looked at the water. The high-voltage runoff.

If the water hit the library, it would trigger a city-wide blackout.

Blackout = Failure of Life Safety Systems.

Failure = Mass Casualties.

Mass Casualties = System Failure.

System Failure = Core Termination.

Gerald typed it. Fast.

*Argument: Destruction of Anchor Point was necessary to prevent System-wide Cascade Failure due to Electrocution of Population.*

*Calculation: Probability of Cascade: 99%.*

*Probability of Core Termination via Deletion: 100%.*

*Lesser Evil: Accepted?*

The red glow stopped pulsing.

It turned white.

*Query: Accepted.*

*Audit Status: Suspended.*

*Reason: Emergency Override.*

Gerald fell to his knees. His hands were solid again.

He was safe.

For now.

But the water was still rising. And the Inspector General was still there. Watching.

And in the distance, beyond the rubble, he heard a horn.

It wasn't a car horn, which was a trumpet.

A war horn.

Gerald looked through the cracks in his concrete shield.

Standing on the bank of the flooded street, silhouetted against the lightning, was a figure.

It was massive. Ten feet tall. Clad in armor made of fused brick and mortar.

It held a giant hammer.

And it was looking at his library.

*New Enemy Detected.*

*Name: The Demolitionist.*

*Affiliation: The Consortium.*

*Objective: Total Eradication.*

Gerald stood up. He grabbed his clipboard.

He had to go out there, and he had to cite a god.

The Demolitionist raised his hammer and walked toward Gerald’s library.
