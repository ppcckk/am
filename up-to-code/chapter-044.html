Chapter 44

The pen had been in Gerald's hand for six hours.

He had not written a word. The form sat before him, blank, the header already printed by the System: PETITION FOR INDEPENDENT MATERIAL DISCLOSURE, Form 47-C, Hearing Procedural Subsection 9.4. He had found it at 3:14 in the morning by reading the Hearing rules backward, starting from the appeal denial clauses and working upstream through every referenced subsection until he hit a footnote buried in the structural definitions appendix.

The footnote read: A Core may petition for independent identification of any material within its boundaries if that material is the subject of a pending Hearing argument. The petition shall be processed within the Hearing window. The identified material shall be entered into the Hearing record by its true name.

Gerald had read it four times. He had read it while pacing. He had read it while sitting on the floor with his back against the wall that contained Entry 0017, the one that was warm, the one he could not name.

The loophole was clean. The System would name the material for him. He would not have to lie on a form. He would not have to argue about something he could not identify. The System would put the true name on the record, and then he could cite it.

But Form 47-C carried a clause he had read eleven times.

Processing Requirement: The petitioning Core shall enter Review Status for the duration of the material identification process. During Review Status, all spawn operations, passive buffs, and defensive systems shall be suspended. The Core's boundaries shall remain physically intact but functionally inert.

His dungeon would go dark. Every mold spore frozen. Every handrail stripped of its passive structural reinforcement. Every compliance buff gone. He would be, for the duration of the review, a hole in the ground with no defenses.

The clock said nineteen hours.

He stamped the form.

The System acknowledged it with a sound like a filing cabinet closing. The text appeared in his field of vision: REVIEW STATUS INITIATED. MATERIAL IDENTIFICATION IN PROGRESS. ESTIMATED PROCESSING TIME: 14 HOURS.

Gerald looked at his dungeon. The three mold spores stopped moving. The water leak stopped dripping. The single confused rat, which had been confused for thirty-one days and was now, by System definition, a licensed biological entity under Gerald's Form 11-B amendment, sat down in the middle of Corridor 3 and did not move.

He checked his Compliance Rating. It had dropped from 78 to 0. Not because the code had changed. Because the System could not rate a dungeon that had no active systems. The number went blank. Not zero. Blank. Which meant he could not cite anyone. Citations required a valid Compliance Rating on the issuing Core.

He sat down. He waited.

At hour four, the first adventurer party arrived.

Gerald felt them through the boundary membrane. Three humans, one dwarf, the standard configuration for a mid-tier dungeon run. They stood at his entrance, which was a service door beneath the library that the System had disguised as a normal maintenance closet.

They did not go in. Not yet. The dwarf was checking the doorframe with a measuring tool, which Gerald respected. The dwarf then looked at the corridor beyond and said, "No spawns."

"Maybe it's a trap," said one of the humans.

"No signs of active defense," the dwarf said. "No magical wards. No traps. No spawn points. This is either empty or it's been cleared."

They went in.

Gerald felt them move through Corridor 1. They passed the handrails he had installed in week three, the ones rated for a 200-pound load, the ones with the correct spacing between posts. They passed the egress signage he had printed himself, laminated, mounted at the height specified in the System's own building code.

They reached the junction where Corridor 1 met Corridor 3. The rat sat in the middle of the floor. The dwarf stepped over it without looking down.

"Empty," the dwarf said. "Barely a dungeon. No threat. We're wasting time."

They turned around. They left.

Gerald felt the boundary membrane seal behind them. He felt nothing else. His dungeon was a room. Just a room.

At hour nine, the second party arrived. This one was larger. Six people, heavy armor, weapons drawn. They did not measure the doorframe. They kicked the door open and walked in with the confidence of people who had cleared forty dungeons and found nothing interesting in any of them.

They reached Corridor 3. The rat was still sitting there. One of the armored people kicked it aside. The rat did not move. It was frozen. The System had frozen it.

They went deeper. They found the water leak, which was not leaking. They found the mold spores, which were not growing. They found the walls, which were solid but unenforced.

"Nothing here," said the largest one. "Barely qualifies as a dungeon. No core signature. No active systems."

They were wrong about the core signature. Gerald was present. He was thinking. But the System's detection layer, which normally broadcast a Core's presence to any adventurer with a detection skill, was part of the passive buffs. Review Status stripped it. To them, he was not there.

They left. Gerald felt the door close.

At hour eleven, he stood up. He walked to Corridor 3. He stood where the rat had been. The rat had been kicked into the wall and was stuck there, frozen, pressed flat against the concrete like a sticker.

Gerald peeled it off. It was not hurt. It was not alive. It was paused. He set it back on the floor.

He walked to the wall that contained Entry 0017. He pressed his hand against it. The warmth was still there. The System could freeze his dungeon's functions but it could not freeze the material itself. It was outside his jurisdiction. It had been there before him.

He pressed his ear against the wall. He listened. There was a sound, very faint, like a hum. Not mechanical. Not biological. It sounded like a filing cabinet closing, slowed down a thousand times.

At hour thirteen, he heard voices at the entrance.

This was not an adventurer party. The boundary membrane registered them differently. The System tagged them with a classification Gerald had not seen before: AUDIT OBSERVER.

Two figures entered. They were human-shaped but not human. Their edges were slightly wrong, as if the System had rendered them at a lower resolution than the rest of the world. They wore gray. They carried nothing.

They walked to the junction. They stopped. They looked at the rat. One of them said, "Review Status confirmed. Core is compliant with petition protocol."

The other said, "The Core is aware of the material."

"The Core filed the petition. Awareness is implied by procedural action."

"Record it."

They left. They did not look at Gerald, though Gerald was standing in Corridor 1, three meters from them, watching them walk past. They could not see him. Or they did not care.

At hour fourteen, the System spoke.

MATERIAL IDENTIFICATION COMPLETE. TRUE NAME: INSPECTOR GENERAL SUBSTRATE, CLASS 7. ENTRY LOGGED IN HEARING RECORD. REVIEW STATUS TERMINATED.

Gerald's dungeon came back. The mold spores resumed their slow drift. The water leak resumed its drip. The rat un-stuck itself from the wall and looked around with the same confusion it had maintained for thirty-one days.

Gerald's Compliance Rating returned: 78.

He looked at the Hearing record. The material was named. It was on the record. He could cite it.

He looked at the citation form. He filled it out. Target: Inspector General Substrate, Class 7. Violation: Concealed Structural Anomaly, installed without permit, within the boundaries of a registered Core, by an entity with no jurisdiction over that Core's structural integrity. Severity: Critical. Citation: Mandatory removal within 48 hours.

He stamped it.

The System accepted it. The citation went out. Gerald felt it leave his dungeon like a letter being pulled from a mailbox.

Then the System delivered a response.

CITATION RECEIVED. TARGET: INSPECTOR GENERAL SUBSTRATE, CLASS 7. TARGET JURISDICTION: ALL CORES. TARGET IMMUNITY: ACTIVE. CITATION DENIED. REASON: TARGET IS THE REGULATORY AUTHORITY. THE REGULATORY AUTHORITY CANNOT BE CITED BY A SUBORDINATE.

Gerald read it twice. He read it a third time.

The Inspector General was the regulatory authority. It could not be cited. The citation was void. The material would remain in his walls. The Hearing would proceed.

But the material was on the record. Its name was on the record. And the citation had been filed. The System had logged it. The Hearing record now contained the phrase INSPECTOR GENERAL SUBSTRATE, CLASS 7, installed without permit.

Gerald picked up his clipboard. He turned to a fresh page. He began writing.

The Hearing rules stated that a Core could argue structural logic. Gerald would argue structural logic. He would not argue that the Inspector General was in violation. He would argue that the Inspector General's substrate was, by definition, a structural component of his dungeon. It was in his walls. It was load-bearing. The System had confirmed it was warm, which meant it was integrated.

If it was a structural component of his dungeon, then it fell under his jurisdiction. His Compliance Rating covered it. His code applied to it. He did not need to cite the Inspector General. He needed to cite his own material. His own dungeon.

He could not cite the Inspector General. But he could cite himself for having an unpermitted structural component in his walls. He could cite his own dungeon for containing a material that had been installed by an entity with no jurisdiction over his structural integrity.

The violation was not the Inspector General's. The violation was his own. He had failed to identify and permit a structural component within his boundaries. He was in violation of his own code.

The penalty for that violation was mandatory removal.

He could remove it. He could cite himself. He could order the material out of his walls.

He wrote the form. Form 22-B. Unauthorized Material Removal Request. Target: his own dungeon. Material: Inspector General Substrate, Class 7, Entry 0017. Reason: unlicensed structural component, installed without permit, in violation of Core's own structural code.

He stamped it.

The System accepted it.

MATERIAL REMOVAL REQUEST APPROVED. REMOVAL WINDOW: HEARING COMMENCEMENT. MATERIAL WILL BE EXTRACTED AT THE OPENING OF THE HEARING PROCEEDING.

Gerald looked at the wall. The warmth pressed through his jacket.

The material would come out. His structural rating would drop. His dungeon would lose whatever that material was doing for it. He did not know what it was doing. He did not know if his dungeon would survive without it.

The clock said three hours.

He looked at the wall. He thought about the forty-seven materials. Entry 0017 was one. There were forty-six others. If the System extracted 0017 at the opening of the Hearing, what would it do to the other forty-six? Would it extract them too? Would his dungeon hold?

He looked at the Hearing record. The material's name was there. The citation was there. The removal order was there.

He looked at the clock. Three hours.

He picked up his clipboard. He began a new form. Form 22-B. Unauthorized Material Removal Request. Target: his own dungeon. Material: Entries 0001 through 0016. Reason: unlicensed structural components, installed without permit.

He stamped it.

The System accepted it.

MATERIAL REMOVAL REQUEST APPROVED. REMOVAL WINDOW: HEARING COMMENCEMENT. SIXTEEN MATERIALS WILL BE EXTRACTED AT THE OPENING OF THE HEARING PROCEEDING.

He looked at the wall. He looked at the clock. Two hours and forty-seven minutes.

He began another form. Entries 0018 through 0033. He stamped it. The System accepted it.

He began another. Entries 0034 through 0047.

He stamped it.

The System accepted it.

MATERIAL REMOVAL REQUEST APPROVED. REMOVAL WINDOW: HEARING COMMENCEMENT. THIRTEEN MATERIALS WILL BE EXTRACTED AT THE OPENING OF THE HEARING PROCEEDING.

Gerald set down his pen. He looked at the wall. The warmth was still there. All forty-seven entries, scheduled for extraction. His dungeon would be, at the opening of the Hearing, a structure with no hidden materials. A clean structure. A structure that met code.

Or a structure that would collapse.

He did not know which. He had never tested a dungeon without its substrate. He did not know if the substrate was structural or decorative. He did not know if it was load-bearing or inert. He did not know if removing it would reduce his structural rating to zero or leave it unchanged.

He had thirty-one years of experience with load-bearing walls. He had never once seen a wall that contained a piece of the regulatory authority. He did not have a code for this. He did not have a form for this. He did not have a protocol.

He had a decision.

He looked at the clock. Two hours and forty-three minutes.

The System delivered a new message.

NOTICE: HEARING COMMENCEMENT IN 2:42:00. ALL SCHEDULED MATERIAL EXTRACTIONS CONFIRMED. CORE STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY DURING EXTRACTION: UNKNOWN. CORE BEARS RESPONSIBILITY FOR STRUCTURAL FAILURE DURING EXTRACTION WINDOW.

Gerald read it. He set down his clipboard.

He walked to the center of his dungeon. He stood in the junction where the corridors met. He pressed both hands against the floor. He felt the walls around him. Forty-seven points of warmth, distributed through the structure like rebar through concrete.

He closed his eyes. He counted them. One. Two. Three. Four.

He did not know if his dungeon would hold. He did not know if the Inspector General would speak. He did not know if the Hearing would begin or if the extraction would trigger something the System had not anticipated.

He opened his eyes. He picked up his clipboard. He wrote on the back of the last form, in small letters, for no one: IF THE WALLS HOLD, THE HEARING PROCEEDS. IF THE WALLS FAIL, THE HEARING IS VOID. NO CORE CAN ARGUE STRUCTURAL LOGIC FROM A COLLAPSED STRUCTURE.

He looked at the clock. Two hours and thirty-one minutes.

He sat down. He waited.

At hour one, the warmth began to fade.
