Chapter 71: Self Issued

# Chapter 71

The pen was shaking. Gerald noticed it, stopped, set the pen down, picked it up again. It was still shaking. He put it down a second time and pressed both palms flat against the clipboard until the tremor passed.

Section 4.2(b) of the Citation Validity Code stated plainly that a Core could only issue citations if its Compliance Rating met or exceeded the Minimum Enforcement Threshold of 40. Gerald had been at 12 when he issued his first citation.

He had been at 19 when he issued his fourth, and he had been at 31 when he issued his ninth. Every citation Core #2201 had challenged fell below the threshold.

Gerald had known this. He had always known this. He had simply assumed the threshold applied only to the moment of service, not the moment of issuance. The System's language was ambiguous on this point, and ambiguity was the one thing Gerald could not abide in his own paperwork.

He pulled the Citation Validity Code up in his System interface and read it again. Third time. The language was identical. "A Core shall not issue a Citation unless its Compliance Rating meets or exceeds the Minimum Enforcement Threshold at the time of issuance."

At the time of issuance.

Not service. Issuance.

Gerald had been wrong, and he had been wrong the whole time.

He set the clipboard down. The rat watched from the south wall. Gerald watched the rat. Neither of them blinked.

Then he opened a new page and started writing again.

Delegated Authority. Section 9.1(c) of the Enforcement Delegation Code.

A Core could issue citations below the threshold if it was acting under Delegated Authority from a regulatory body with Standing.

The System itself was a regulatory body with Standing.

The audit notices the System had sent Gerald were, by their own definition, "enforcement actions." If the System was conducting an enforcement action through Gerald, then Gerald was operating under Delegated Authority for the duration of that action.

It was a stretch. But it was a stretch with language to back it, and Gerald had spent thirty-one years finding language to back stretches that other people had to pay for.

He finished the response at 6:47. He filed it. The System logged it.

He walked his dungeon, repeating the same path he had taken every morning for six weeks. South corridor, handrail height, forty-two inches. North corridor, egress signage, photoluminescent, visible from all points of the corridor.

Ventilation grates, eighteen per thousand square feet. He counted them.

He always counted them.

The water leak was still in the east wall. Gerald had filed for a plumbing permit with the System's Municipal Interface seventeen days ago, and the System had not responded.

Gerald had filed a follow-up.

No response. He had filed a third.

No response.

He had considered filing a fourth but had stopped because he was fairly certain the System did not have a plumbing department.

The mold spores were in the northeast corner. They were technically not a violation.

They were a spawn.

Spawns were not violations.

But they contributed to the moisture problem, which contributed to the ventilation rating, which contributed to the Compliance Rating, which was currently 38 and climbing by fractions of a point every time Gerald installed something new.

Thirty-eight. Two below the threshold. Two below the number that would have made all his citations valid without the Delegated Authority argument.

Gerald measured the northeast corner. The mold occupied eleven square feet. He had tried to remove it.

The System classified it as a spawn entity and would not allow removal without a Spawn Relocation Permit, which required a 90-day notice period. Gerald had filed that notice on day three.

He was on day forty-one, and he finished his walk at 7:15. He made coffee. The coffee was not compliant with any health code because Gerald did not have a health code, but he drank it anyway.

At 7:30, the System delivered a new notice.

[INSPECTOR #8812 HAS ARRIVED EARLY. AUDIT COMMENCING. ALL ENFORCEMENT ACTIONS SUSPENDED UNTIL AUDIT CONCLUSION.]

Gerald looked at the notice. He looked at the clock. It was not 14 hours yet.

It was barely two hours since the second notification.

Core #2201 had used Priority Audit Authority to arrive early, and the Inspector had complied.

The air in the dungeon changed. Gerald could not describe how it changed. The System did not provide a description, and the air grew heavier, as though it had stopped moving and now resumed.

Then Inspector #8812 was there, which was not a person.

It was not a creature. The shape distorted the space around it until Gerald's eyes watered. A rectangular prism, roughly the size of a filing cabinet, floating at a height that suggested it was reading his handrails.

It had no face. It had no voice.

But when it spoke, the words appeared in Gerald's interface as a direct System message.

[AUDIT COMMENCING.

CORE DESIGNATION: GERALD HUTCHINS. COMPLIANCE RATING: 38.

PRIOR CITATIONS ISSUED: 14. CITATIONS UNDER APPEAL: 1. RESPONSE FILED: YES.

REVIEWING.]

Gerald stood still. The rat stood still. The mold did not move because mold did not move.

[DELEGATED AUTHORITY ARGUMENT: SECTION 9.1(c). EVALUATING.]

The prism rotated. Gerald watched it rotate. The rotation made a sound like a filing drawer closing, which Gerald found deeply unsettling for reasons he could not articulate.

[SECTION 9.1(c) REQUIRES: (1) REGULATORY BODY WITH STANDING, (2) ACTIVE ENFORCEMENT ACTION, (3) TEMPORAL PROXIMITY BETWEEN ACTION AND CITATION. EVALUATING TEMPORAL PROXIMITY.]

The prism stopped rotating.

[TEMPORAL PROXIMITY: CITATIONS ISSUED 41 TO 67 DAYS AFTER AUDIT NOTICE RECEIVED. MAXIMUM ALLOWED WINDOW UNDER 9.1(c): 14 DAYS.]

Gerald felt something cold move through his chest, and he had not checked the window. He had not checked the window because he had not expected to need the Delegated Authority argument. He had expected the threshold to apply to service, not issuance.

He had been wrong about the threshold, and now he was wrong about the window.

[DELEGATED AUTHORITY ARGUMENT: DENIED. TEMPORAL PROXIMITY INSUFFICIENT.]

Gerald's mouth opened. He closed it. He opened it again.

"The audit notices," he said. "They were ongoing. The enforcement action did not end. The System continued to send notices throughout the period."

[SYSTEM NOTICES ARE INFORMATIONAL. THEY DO NOT CONSTITUTE ONGOING ENFORCEMENT ACTION. ENFORCEMENT ACTION IS DEFINED AS A DISCRETE EVENT WITH A START AND END TIMESTAMP. YOUR AUDIT NOTICE HAD A TIMESTAMP.]

Gerald looked at the prism. The prism did not look back because it had no eyes. But Gerald felt the weight of its not-looking, and it was the weight of every denied permit application he had ever filed, every form returned for correction, every time a developer had looked at him with the particular contempt reserved for men who measured things.

"Then my citations are void," Gerald said.

[UNDER SECTION 4.2(b), YES. ALL FOURTEEN CITATIONS ISSUED BELOW THE MINIMUM ENFORCEMENT THRESHOLD ARE VOID. ALL DEBUFFS APPLIED TO THOSE TARGETS ARE REVERSED. ALL HEARING OUTCOMES BASED ON THOSE CITATIONS ARE VACATED.]

Gerald sat down on the floor. He did not mean to sit down.

His legs simply decided that standing was no longer a viable option.

The clipboard landed beside him. The rat moved to the east wall, which was new, because the rat had not been on the east wall before.

Fourteen citations. Fourteen debuffs.

Fourteen hearings he had won.

Fourteen sets of spawns that had died because their Core's compliance had been degraded by his paperwork.

All of it, gone. Reversed.

The targets would be back at full strength. Some of them would be at higher strength, because the System awarded bonus stats to Cores who won appeals, and Gerald had lost this appeal before it had even begun.

The prism moved to the south corridor. It measured the handrail. It measured the egress signage. It counted the ventilation grates.

[PHASE TWO: PHYSICAL INSPECTION. COMMENCING.]

Gerald stood up. He picked up his clipboard.

He did not know what else to do.

The prism measured. Gerald watched. This was the part he understood.

This was the part he had been doing for thirty-one years.

Someone else measured, and he watched, and if the numbers were wrong he wrote them down and made someone else fix them.

The prism measured the south corridor, and the handrails were compliant. The egress signage was compliant. The ventilation was compliant.

The prism moved to the north corridor. Same results.

It moved to the east wall.

The water leak.

The prism noted it.

[VIOLATION: ACTIVE WATER INTRUSION. CATEGORY: STRUCTURAL. SEVERITY: MINOR. COMPLIANCE IMPACT: -2.]

Gerald wrote it down. He always wrote it down.

The prism moved to the northeast corner. The mold. It measured the mold's footprint.

It checked the spawn classification. It noted that the Spawn Relocation Permit was pending.

[NOTED. NO ADDITIONAL VIOLATION.]

The prism moved to the center of the dungeon.

It hovered there. It was quiet for a long time. Gerald checked his clipboard.

He had not written anything in two minutes, and the prism was measuring something he could not see.

Then the prism spoke again, and its words came slower than before, as if it were choosing each one with care.

[OBSERVATION: STRUCTURAL ANCHORING OF HANDRAILS IN SOUTH CORRIDOR.]

Gerald looked at the south corridor.

The handrails were there. Forty-two inches. He had measured them himself.

He had measured them twice.

[HANDRAILS ARE COMPLIANT WITH HEIGHT AND SPACING STANDARDS. HANDRAILS ARE NOT COMPLIANT WITH LOAD PATH STANDARDS.]

Gerald frowned. "Load path?"

[HANDRAILS MUST TRANSFER LATERAL LOAD TO A STRUCTURAL ELEMENT WITHIN THE CORE'S OWN FOOTPRINT. YOUR HANDRAILS ARE ANCHORED TO THE EAST WALL. THE EAST WALL IS A SHARED STRUCTURAL ELEMENT WITH THE OVERLYING MUNICIPAL LIBRARY. THE LIBRARY'S FOUNDATION IS NOT WITHIN YOUR DUNGEON FOOTPRINT.]

Gerald stared at the east wall, and the wall was there.

It had always been there. It was the wall between his dungeon and the library's sub-basement.

He had built handrails against it because it was the nearest wall. He had checked the height. He had checked the spacing.

He had not checked the load path because in thirty-one years of municipal inspection, he had never once needed to check the load path of a handrail. The handrail transferred load to the wall.

The wall transferred load to the foundation, and the foundation was the building's. The building was someone else's. That was how buildings worked.

That was how they had always worked.

But this was not a building. This was a dungeon.

And the dungeon's footprint ended at the east wall.

And the east wall was not his. And the load path did not complete.

[VIOLATION: INCOMPLETE LOAD PATH ON HANDRAILS. CATEGORY: STRUCTURAL. SEVERITY: CRITICAL. COMPLIANCE IMPACT: -15.]

Fifteen. Gerald's rating was 38.

Minus 2 for the water leak.

Minus 15 for the load path, which put him at 21.

[ADDITIONAL OBSERVATION: ALL HANDRAILS IN ALL CORRIDORS ARE ANCHORED TO SHARED STRUCTURAL ELEMENTS. VIOLATION APPLIES TO ALL HANDRAILS. COMPLIANCE IMPACT: -15 PER CORRIDOR.

FOUR CORRIDORS. TOTAL: -60.]

Gerald did the math. 38 minus 2 minus 60, which was negative twenty-four; a negative Compliance Rating was not a number the System displayed. The System did not have a number for it.

The System had a status.

[COMPLIANCE RATING: STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY FAILURE IMMINENT. CORE HAS 72 HOURS TO REMEDIATE OR FACE AUTOMATIC COLLAPSE.]

Gerald looked at the clipboard. He looked at the handrails. He looked at the east wall, which was the library's wall, which was not his wall, which held his handrails, which held his entire compliance structure together, which was about to fall apart because he had never once in his life needed to ask who owned the wall.

The prism began to fade.

[AUDIT CONCLUDED. CITATIONS VOID.

COMPLIANCE RATING: 21.

STRUCTURAL FAILURE COUNTDOWN: 72 HOURS. GOOD DAY.]

It was gone. Gerald was alone. The rat was grooming itself on the east wall, directly below the handrail that was about to kill him.

Gerald looked at the wall. He looked at the handrail.

He looked at the System interface, which offered him two remediation options.

Option one: rebuild all handrails with independent anchors within his own footprint.

That required structural steel he did not have and could not acquire in 72 hours. Option two: expand his dungeon footprint to include the library's foundation.

He stared at option two.

To expand his footprint, he needed to claim adjacent territory. The System's territorial expansion protocol required one of three things: a Citation against the adjacent structure, a negotiated transfer, or a hostile takeover.

The library was not a dungeon. It had no Core. It could not be cited.

Could it?

Gerald opened the Citation Validity Code. He searched for the definition of "structure." The definition was broad. Any constructed entity with a footprint.

The library had a footprint. The library was a structure.

But the library had no Compliance Rating, because the System only assigned Compliance Ratings to Cores.

Unless.

Gerald searched for "non-Core structure." The System returned a entry.

[NON-CORE STRUCTURES: MAY BE SUBJECT TO AUDIT IF THEY SHARE A STRUCTURAL ELEMENT WITH A REGISTERED CORE. AUDIT TRIGGERS AUTOMATIC COMPLIANCE RATING ASSIGNMENT.]

The library shared a structural element with Gerald's dungeon. The east wall. Which meant the library could be audited.

Which meant the library had a hidden Compliance Rating that had never been measured.

Which meant Gerald could cite it.

But if Gerald cited the library, he would be responsible for its compliance.

The System's Citation framework placed remediation obligation on the citing Core. Gerald would have to fix the library. The entire library.

Four stories. Three hundred thousand square feet.

A building he had inspected in his previous life and found, as far as he knew, adequate.

Adequate.

Not compliant. Adequate was not compliant.

Gerald had never once in thirty-one years used the word "compliant" to describe a building he had inspected, because no building was ever compliant. Buildings were adequate. They were acceptable.

They were "up to code" in the loose, municipal sense that meant nobody had died yet.

The library was not up to code. Gerald knew that. He had always known that. He had simply never had the authority to say it.

He looked at the clock. Seventy-one hours, forty-three minutes remaining.

He opened a new page on his clipboard. He wrote the header.

CITATION.

Then he stopped.

Because if he cited the library, the library would become his problem. And the library was four stories of problems, and Gerald had never in his life solved a problem that was not his own, because that was not what a building inspector did. A building inspector cited the owner.

The owner fixed it, which was the system. That was the code.

No owner existed. Not anymore. Not in the System. Only Gerald remained, with his 72 hours, in a four-story building never inspected by any authority able to enforce compliance.

He wrote the citation.
