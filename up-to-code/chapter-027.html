Chapter 27

The Rating dropped to 7 and Gerald watched it happen like watching a patient bleed out on a table.

The IG scan moved through his eastern corridor at the speed of a slow walk. Each time it passed a point of interest, a number flickered at the edge of his awareness. Ventilation deficit. Negative. Electrical grounding absent. Negative. Egress signage missing at junction three. Negative. Each one subtracted. Each one was accurate. He could not dispute them.

He was running out of dungeon to scan.

The eastern corridor was the worst section. He knew this. He had known it for three weeks. The water leak from the library's plumbing infrastructure had never been repaired because he lacked the mana to seal it, and the moisture had warped two support beams and created a patch of black mold along the baseboard that the System classified as a Category 4 biological hazard.

The scan reached the mold.

RATING: 6.

Gerald felt the debuff land. His three mold spores lost their passive regeneration. The rat's movement speed dropped by fifteen percent. Everything in the dungeon got worse at once, and the scan was only halfway through.

He had eleven minutes.

The scan entered the junction chamber. Junction three. The one where the egress signage should have been. He had not installed it because he could not figure out how to manufacture a sign without a forge, and the System did not provide a template.

RATING: 5.

Gerald's hands went cold. Five was below the threshold for active spawning. At four, the System would stop generating entities entirely. At three, the dungeon would enter dormancy. At two, the Core would be flagged for termination.

He needed to raise the number. He needed to raise it fast.

The scan moved toward the junction chamber's north wall. Gerald knew what it would find there. The wall was bowing. Three degrees from vertical. Not enough to be a structural emergency, but the System did not grade on curves. It graded on numbers. Three degrees was a Category 2 deviation.

He could not stop the scan. He could not hide the wall. He could not move it.

But he could object.

The thought arrived fully formed, the way all his thoughts arrived: as a form. A specific form, with a specific number and a specific procedure. Form 19-C. Objection to Materiality of Cited Deficiency. He had filled out hundreds of these in his career. Not for himself. For property owners who wanted to argue that a violation was too minor to enforce.

He had always denied them.

Now he was them.

The scan reached the bowing wall. It flagged the deviation. RATING: 4.

Gerald filed the objection.

The System processed it. A pause. Then: OBJECTION LOGGED. Materiality review pending.

The rating did not change. The violation was still there. But it was now under review, and the IG's scan moved on without counting it.

Gerald stared at the notification. He had not expected it to work. He had expected the IG to ignore him the way he had ignored property owners. But the IG was not him. The IG was a system, and systems had procedures, and procedures had review steps.

The scan reached the water leak. Category 3. RATING: 4.

He filed another objection. OBJECTION LOGGED. Materiality review pending.

The scan reached the electrical junction. No grounding wire. Category 2. RATING: 3.

Third objection. OBJECTION LOGGED.

Three objections in three minutes. Gerald felt something tighten in his chest. Not fear. Something older. The feeling of knowing you are about to do something you would not do if you were honest. He had spent thirty-one years telling people that their violations were material. He had built his entire career on the conviction that a violation was a violation was a violation.

Now he was arguing that three of his own violations were too small to matter.

The scan reached the mold again. It had already counted it once. It would not count it twice. But the scan was looping back. The IG was re-scanning the eastern corridor.

RATING: 3.

Gerald filed his fourth objection. The mold. Category 4. He argued that the affected area was less than zero point five square feet and therefore fell below the materiality threshold for biological hazards.

This was a lie. The affected area was closer to one point two square feet. But the System did not have a measuring tool attached to the IG's scan. It had a scanner that flagged presence, not extent. Gerald was exploiting the gap between detection and measurement.

OBJECTION LOGGED.

The scan finished its loop. RATING: 3. Four violations under materiality review. If the review upheld his objections, the rating would jump back to 7. If it denied them, the rating would stay at 3 and he would be flagged for termination.

The review would take time. The IG had to process each objection against a materiality threshold. Gerald did not know what the threshold was. He had never seen it published.

The scan moved to the western corridor. Gerald's best section. Handrails installed. Lighting adequate. Egress routes clear. The scan found nothing. RATING held at 3.

The scan moved to the central chamber. The rat's nest. The System flagged it. Unlicensed biological entity, nesting in a non-designated area. Category 1. Minor.

RATING: 2.

Gerald filed his fifth objection.

This one he could not justify. The rat was a violation. It had always been a violation. He had known it was a violation since the first day. He was filing an objection on a violation he could not argue was immaterial.

OBJECTION LOGGED.

Five objections. The System displayed a counter. OBJECTIONS FILED THIS AUDIT: 5. A second line appeared below it. MAXIMUM OBJECTIONS PER AUDIT: 5.

He was at the limit. If he filed a sixth, the System would not log it. It would reject it. And rejection during an audit would be logged as a separate infraction.

The scan moved to the southern section. The section he had not finished. The section where the walls were thin and the floor was uneven and the ventilation was a single gap between two stones that let in cold air from the library's foundation.

The scan found the ventilation gap. Category 3. RATING: 2.

He could not object. He was at the limit.

The scan found the uneven floor. Category 1. RATING: 1.

Gerald felt the world go quiet. One. He was at one. The debuff hit everything. The mold spores stopped producing mana. The rat sat down and stopped moving. The dungeon was dying.

The scan found the thin walls. Category 2.

RATING: 0.

The System displayed a notification. TERMINATION PROTOCOL INITIATED. TARGET CORE COMPLIANCE RATING: 0. DISPOSITION: ARCHIVED.

Gerald read it twice.

Archived. Not destroyed. Archived. The word was careful. Bureaucratic. The System was not killing him. It was putting him in a box. A box with no dungeon, no spawns, no Core functions. A filing cabinet in the dark.

He had thirty seconds before the protocol completed.

The materiality reviews had not finished. Four of his five objections were still pending. If even one was upheld, the rating would rise above zero and the termination would halt.

Twenty seconds.

The first review resolved. The bowing wall. Three degrees from vertical. The System's materiality threshold for structural deviation was two degrees. Gerald's argument was that the deviation was below threshold. It was not. The threshold was two. The deviation was three.

OBJECTION DENIED.

Fifteen seconds.

The second review. The water leak. Category 3. Gerald had argued it was immaterial because it was a single point of failure and not a systemic drainage problem. The System's materiality threshold for moisture intrusion was any active leak affecting more than one adjacent surface. The leak touched two surfaces.

OBJECTION DENIED.

Ten seconds.

The third review. The electrical junction. No grounding wire. Gerald had argued that the junction carried less than twelve volts and therefore fell below the grounding requirement threshold. The threshold was fifteen volts. The junction carried twelve.

OBJECTION UPHELD.

The rating shifted. 0 became 1. The termination protocol paused. Not cancelled. Paused. The System was recalculating.

Five seconds.

The fourth review. The mold. Gerald had argued the area was below the materiality threshold. The threshold was zero point five square feet. The actual area was one point two.

OBJECTION DENIED.

Three seconds.

The rating was still 1. One more denial and it was back to zero. The fifth review. The rat.

Two seconds.

OBJECTION UPHELD.

The rating shifted to 2. The termination protocol entered a holding state. The System displayed a new notification.

AUDIT COMPLETE. COMPLIANCE RATING: 2. DISPOSITION: PROBATIONARY. CORE REMAINS ACTIVE PENDING CORRECTIVE ACTION PLAN.

Gerald exhaled. His body did not exist anymore, but he exhaled.

He had survived by being a liar. A specific kind of liar. A professional liar. The kind who knows exactly which violations are small enough to argue away and which ones are not. He had known, instinctively, that the electrical junction carried twelve volts because he had measured it himself three weeks ago. He had known the rat was a Category 1 violation because he had looked up the classification table. He had known the mold was too big to argue because he had measured it too.

He had known what to lie about and what not to. That was the skill. Not the lying. The knowing.

The audit timer hit zero. The IG's scan withdrew from his dungeon. Gerald felt it go, like a hand lifting off his chest.

Then a new notification appeared.

THIRD-PARTY COMPLAINT FILED AGAINST CORE: HUTCHINS. COMPLAINT ID: 4471-C. STATUS: ACTIVE. RESPONSE REQUIRED WITHIN 48 HOURS.

Gerald opened the complaint.

It was short. Three paragraphs. The first alleged that Gerald had filed five materiality objections during a single audit cycle. The second alleged that this constituted "systematic abuse of the objection mechanism to evade compliance enforcement." The third alleged that Gerald's Core had served a Citation on a non-Core entity (SURVEYOR-7) and that this Citation was therefore void and the act of serving it constituted "unauthorized interference with Inspector General operations."

The complaint was anonymous. No Core name attached. But the format was wrong. Anonymous complaints used a standard template. This one did not. This one used a numbered paragraph structure with sub-clauses and a reference to "the respondent's prior filing history."

Gerald had seen that structure before. He had seen it in the correspondence from CORE-9. The neighboring dungeon core. The one that had sent the goblin scout in the early days. The one that had tried to flood his eastern corridor with water elementals three chapters ago.

CORE-9 had a law degree. Gerald had learned this when CORE-9 filed an appeal against a Citation and included a footnote citing the System's procedural code by section number.

The complaint referenced Gerald's objection count. It referenced his Citation on SURVEYOR-7. It used a structure that only CORE-9 used.

Gerald checked the complaint timestamp. It had been filed six minutes before the audit began.

CORE-9 had known the audit was coming. CORE-9 had timed the complaint to land during the audit, when Gerald would be too busy fighting for his Rating to respond. The response window was forty-eight hours. Gerald had spent twenty-four minutes of that window trying not to be archived.

He had forty-seven hours and thirty-six minutes to respond to a complaint that alleged he was gaming the system.

He opened the response form. Form 22-D. Response to Third-Party Complaint.

The first field required him to identify the complainant. He could not. The complaint was anonymous. But the System had a procedure for this. If the respondent could demonstrate that the complaint was filed by a specific Core with a prior adversarial relationship, the complaint could be reclassified as a "Targeted Grievance" and subject to a higher evidentiary standard.

Gerald needed to prove CORE-9 filed it.

He had no evidence. The complaint was anonymous by design. CORE-9 had covered its tracks.

But Gerald had one thing. The complaint referenced his "prior filing history." Specifically, it cited his objection count during the audit. Gerald had not yet filed any objections during this audit when the complaint was written. The complaint was filed six minutes before the audit began. It could not have known his objection count.

Unless the complaint was not written before the audit. Unless it was written during the audit and backdated.

Gerald checked the filing timestamp again. 14:03:22. The audit had begun at 14:09:00. The complaint was filed before the audit. But it referenced objection counts that did not exist yet.

The complaint was fabricated. CORE-9 had guessed Gerald would file objections. It had guessed he would file five. It had written the complaint in advance and backdated it.

But backdating a System timestamp was not possible. The System did not allow it. Unless the complaint was filed through a channel that did not use System timestamps.

Gerald looked at the complaint ID. 4471-C. The "C" suffix. He had seen that suffix before. It stood for "Construct-Filed." Complaints filed by constructs did not receive System timestamps because constructs were not Core entities. They existed outside the timestamp protocol.

CORE-9 had used a construct to file the complaint. A construct with no timestamp. A construct that could observe Gerald's dungeon from outside and write a complaint in real time.

The surveyor.

SURVEYOR-7 was still in Gerald's dungeon. It had been there for twenty-four minutes. It was standing in the junction chamber, holding its retracted measuring tape, waiting for credentials it did not have.

Gerald looked at the surveyor. The surveyor looked at him.

The surveyor's writing hand was still. But its eyes were not. Its eyes were tracking the complaint notification floating in the air between them.

Gerald had forty-seven hours. The surveyor had forty-eight hours to present credentials. The surveyor did not have credentials. The surveyor would fail to comply, and the citation would be upheld, and the surveyor would be removed.

Unless the surveyor was not the one who filed the complaint.

Unless there was another construct. One Gerald had not seen. One that had been watching from outside his dungeon, writing a complaint in real time, using the "C" suffix to avoid a timestamp.

Gerald looked at the walls of his dungeon. Thin walls. Uneven floors. A ventilation gap that let in cold air from the foundation.

Somewhere on the other side of those walls, something was writing.
