Chapter 173: Citing Himself

# Chapter 173

The woman in the high-vis vest did not look at Gerald Hutchins. She looked at his clipboard. Specifically, she looked at the top right corner of the clipboard, where a small, red stamp read **NON-COMPLIANT**.

Her eyes narrowed. The crease between her brows deepened into a canyon of professional suspicion.

She wore a hard hat that had been purchased from a discount bin, the plastic still retaining the faint, acrid smell of factory molding.

Her boots were steel-toed, muddy, and heavy. She tapped a pen against her thigh, a rhythmic, nervous tic.

"I don't care what you say," she said.

Her voice was flat, devoid of the usual adventurer bravado. "I saw the permit.

It was valid for three days.

You're claiming it expired because of a 'structural integrity shift' that hasn't happened yet. That's not how physics works. That's not how the System works."

Gerald stood perfectly still. He did not blink. He did not fidget. He simply held the clipboard at eye level, blocking her view of the dungeon corridor behind him.

"Section 4, Paragraph B of the Municipal Variance Code," Gerald said. His voice was dry, like paper rustling in a vacuum.

"A variance is void if the underlying structure changes during the application window.

The load-bearing pillar moved.

Therefore, the permit is void."

"The pillar didn't move."

"It did. I have the laser measurements."

"It's a dungeon. Things move."

"Not load-bearing things. Not without a permit."

The woman, whose name tag read **BRETT**, shifted her weight. She looked at the three companions behind her. Two of them were armored knights, their plate mail gleaming with an unnatural, magical polish.

The third was a mage, hovering off the ground, hands crackling with low-level electricity. They were waiting for Brett to give the signal.

They were waiting for permission to kill.

Brett looked back at Gerald. She saw the age in his face. The wrinkles around the eyes.

The gray hair.

She saw the mildness of a man who had spent thirty-one years measuring door widths and checking egress routes.

"You're trying to trap us," Brett said.

"I'm trying to inspect you," Gerald corrected.

Brett spat on the concrete floor. It was a crude, dismissive act.

"Fine," she said.

"If the permit is void, we're trespassing. So we'll just fight you. And then we'll take the core.

No paperwork. Just violence."

She raised her hand. The knights stepped forward. The mage's lightning arced higher.

Gerald did not raise a weapon. He did not spawn a monster. He raised his free hand, holding a pen.

"Wait," Gerald said.

Brett paused. Her hand hovered in the air. The knights hesitated, confused by the interruption.

"You are wearing a hard hat," Gerald said. "But you are not wearing eye protection."

Brett touched her hard hat. "Why would I need eye protection in a dungeon?"

"Because," Gerald said, pointing the pen at the ceiling, "you are standing directly under a Class 4 Water Leak. Section 12, Subsection C of the Occupational Safety Code mandates eye protection in areas with a probability of fluid ingress exceeding fifteen percent. I measured the drip rate. It is nineteen percent."

Brett looked up. A single, large drop of water fell from the darkness above, landing squarely on her nose.

She wiped it away with her sleeve. Her face contorted in disgust.

"Are you serious?"

"I am never serious," Gerald said. "I am compliant."

Brett's eyes flashed with rage. She slammed her hand down. "Kill him."

The lead knight drew a sword. The blade was broad, heavy, and glowing with a faint, red enchantment. He lunged.

Gerald did not move. He did not dodge.

Instead, he snapped his fingers.

A red light pulsed from the walls. It was not a magical barrier. It was a System notification, projected into the air in bold, blocky letters.

**VIOLATION DETECTED: UNSAFE WORKING CONDITIONS.** **CITATION ISSUED: CODE 77-B.** **PENALTY: TEMPORARY STUN (5 SECONDS).**

The knight froze mid-stride. His muscles locked. His sword halted inches from Gerald's nose. The mage dropped to the ground, his lightning fizzling out as his hands spasmed.

Brett screamed. "What did you do?"

"I cited you for creating a hazardous work environment," Gerald said.

He wrote something on his clipboard.

The sound of the pen scratching against the paper was loud in the silent corridor. "You are creating a slip hazard.

The water on the floor. You ignored it. That is a violation of the General Duty Clause.

I am serving the citation now."

He held up a small, red slip of paper.

"Sign here, please."

Brett stared at him. The red light pulsed again, faster now. A low hum vibrated through the concrete floor.

Brett's face went pale.

Not from fear, but from recognition. She knew this game. She had played it with other Cores.

But never like this.

Never with such bureaucratic precision.

"You can't do this," she whispered.

"I have the authority," Gerald said. "I am an Inspector. You are the Subject."

Brett looked at her companions. The knight was still frozen. The mage was trying to stand, but his legs were shaking uncontrollably, a side effect of the lightning feedback loop caused by the citation.

Brett reached for her belt.

She pulled out a small, glowing stone. A Resurrection Pearl. Or maybe a Teleportation Scroll.

It didn't matter. She was going to leave.

But Gerald took a step forward.

"You didn't sign," he said.

"I'm not signing anything!"

"Refusal to sign constitutes an admission of guilt," Gerald said. "Under System Statute 9, Section 4. Your Compliance Rating is now dropping."

Brett gasped. She felt it. A sudden, sharp pain in her chest. Her health bar, visible in the corner of her vision, flickered.

**HEALTH: 80%** **HEALTH: 60%** **HEALTH: 40%**

The debuff was real. The System was enforcing the penalty.

"Stop!" Brett yelled. "We'll go! We'll leave!"

Gerald lowered his clipboard. The red light faded. The knight collapsed to the ground, gasping for air. The mage groaned, clutching his head.

Brett backed away, her eyes wide with horror. She looked at Gerald not as a weak, old man, but as a monster. A monster who used rules as weapons.

"This isn't right," she hissed.

"It is exactly right," Gerald said. "Please leave the premises. And take your hazardous waste with you."

Brett grabbed her companions. One by one, she dragged them out of the corridor, toward the exit. They stumbled, weak and debuffed.

Gerald watched them go. His hand was steady. His pen was still.

But inside, his stomach churned.

The nausea was back.

The paralysis. He had used a loophole he hadn't fully tested.

He had cited an adventurer for a workplace safety violation. It worked. But it had cost him.

He looked down at his clipboard.

The citation pad was blank.

No. Not blank.

The page had turned. But not to a new citation.

To a **NOTICE OF HEARING**.

**SUBJECT: CORE 001** **ISSUE: UNAUTHORIZED CITATION OF NON-CORE ENTITY** **STATUS: PENDING REVIEW**

Gerald's heart skipped a beat. Adventurers were not Cores. They were not dungeons. They were not bound by the Building Code.

He had applied the code to a human being.

And the System had let him.

But the System was not done with him.

He looked up at the ceiling. The hole from the previous chapter's void was gone, but the air still felt thin. The smell of damp paper was stronger now.

A new notification appeared in his vision. Not on the wall. In his mind.

**SYSTEM ALERT: INSPECTOR GENERAL AUDIT INITIATED.** **TARGET: CORE 001.** **REASON: IRREGULAR CITATION PRACTICE.** **PREPARE FOR INTERROGATION.**

Gerald swallowed. His throat was dry.

He had survived the fight. But he had triggered the audit.

He knew what an Audit was. It wasn't a battle.

It was a review of his entire history.

Every citation. Every rule he had bent. Every loophole he had exploited.

The Architects had warned him. *They are coming. And they have the permits.*

Gerald looked at the exit where Brett had fled.

He needed to prepare. He needed evidence. He needed to prove that the citation was valid.

But how do you prove a human violated the building code?

Gerald walked back into his dungeon. The sub-basement was quiet. The mold spores floated lazily in the air. The water leak dripped in a steady, rhythmic beat.

He went to his workbench. He picked up a tape measure.

He measured the distance from the wall to the exit door.

**10 feet, 4 inches.**

He checked the code. Minimum egress width for a sub-basement of this occupancy classification was **10 feet, 6 inches.**

He frowned.

He measured again.

**10 feet, 4 inches.**

He had made a mistake. A tiny, insignificant mistake. A two-inch deficit.

He had cited Brett for a violation that didn't exist in that specific spot. He had cited her for the water, but the water was just part of the environment. The real violation was the egress width.

And he had missed it.

Gerald's hands shook.

If the Inspector General found out that he had cited a human for a violation that was technically ambiguous, he would be stripped of his Core status. Or worse. Reclassified.

He needed to fix it.

He needed to prove that the two-inch deficit was actually a violation.

But the exit was already open. The adventurers were gone.

Gerald looked at the note he had received earlier. **THE BUREAU OF UNLICENSED REALITY.**

He picked up his pen.

He wrote on the back of the citation pad.

**ADDENDUM: STRUCTURAL INSTABILITY.**

He drew a diagram. A simple, crude sketch of the exit corridor.

He marked the two-inch deficit.

And then he added a note.

**DEFICIT CAUSED BY EXTERNAL PRESSURE. SEE: ADVENTURER TRAFFIC.**

He was lying. But it was a lie that sounded like code.

He filed the addendum.

**FILING COMPLETE.**

**STATUS: PENDING.**

Gerald sat back. He closed his eyes.

He could feel the System watching him. Waiting.

The Audit would begin in ten minutes.

He had to survive it. He had to argue that the two inches mattered. That the water had displaced the floor. That the adventurers' weight had compressed the sub-basement, reducing the egress width temporarily.

It was absurd.

But it was plausible.

And Gerald Hutchins was an expert in plausible nonsense.

He opened his eyes.

He looked at the water leak.

He picked up a bucket.

He mopped the floor.

As he mopped, he thought about the Architects. They wanted him to stop. They wanted him to become a monster. They wanted him to fight with claws and teeth.

But Gerald didn't have claws. He had a pen.

He had a code.

And he had a lie.

The water swirled in the bucket. Gray and muddy.

He wrung the mop.

The floor was wet.

He looked at the exit.

The door was still open.

He walked toward it.

He did not go outside. He stood in the threshold.

He looked out into the library. The lights were dim. The books were silent.

He took a deep breath.

He stepped back into the dungeon.

He closed the door.

And he locked it.

The lock clicked.

A sound like a bone breaking.

The System notification appeared.

**ACCESS DENIED.** **REASON: LOCKED FROM INSIDE.** **CODE VIOLATION: BLOCKED EGRESS.**

Gerald smiled. A small, tight, terrified smile.

He had just created a new violation. A self-inflicted one.

He was citing himself.

And he was doing it to prove that the system could be broken.

The Audit was coming.

Gerald picked up his pen.

He had three minutes left.

He wrote.
