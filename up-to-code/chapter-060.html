Chapter 60: Missing Credential

# Chapter 60

There was a line on the clipboard he had not written.

Gerald had been staring at the audit notification for eleven minutes, counting the seconds between the word "ASSIGNED" and the word "CORE" the way he counted the seconds between a sump pump failure and a flooded sub-basement, and the clipboard was wrong. Not factually. Procedurally.

The audit notification listed Core 2847 as Auditor. It listed the audit scope as "Full Structural Compliance Review." It listed the audit window as 00:04:17 and falling. The audit would start in four minutes and seventeen seconds.

But it did not list a Credential Verification Number.

Gerald set the clipboard down. He picked it up again.

He looked at the notification three more times, and he had issued four hundred and twelve citations in his career. He had received nine audits himself, all from the City of Ashford's internal review board. Every single one of those audits had included a Credential Verification Number in the header line.

CVN.

Three letters, six digits. A number that said: the person auditing you is authorized to audit you, and if they are not, the audit is void, and you can file a Form 9-C.

The notification from the System had no CVN.

Gerald looked at the time.

Four minutes. He looked at his dungeon. His 4,000 square feet.

His handrails, installed to municipal spec. His ventilation ducts, sized to code.

His egress signage, illuminated and unobstructed.

His fire suppression, a sprinkler system he had installed himself over three days of painstaking work, each sprinkler head placed at exactly the distance required by the System's fire code appendix.

His Compliance Rating was 61. Not terrible.

Not good. A 61 meant he had violations. He knew which ones.

The electrical panel was two amperes over the rated load for his corridor lighting. The water leak in the northeast corner had been sealed but not yet re-inspected, which meant it still counted as an open violation.

The rat nest in the sub-floor was technically a pest infestation, and he had not yet filed the extermination documentation.

Four minutes to pass an audit with a 61.

Gerald picked up the clipboard. He typed: "REQUESTING CLARIFICATION: AUDIT PROTOCOL, SECTION 4, SUBSECTION C, PARAGRAPH 2. DOES THIS AUDIT REQUIRE A CREDENTIAL VERIFICATION NUMBER?"

The System responded in 0.3 seconds.

"YES. ALL AUDITS REQUIRE A VALID CVN. CORE 2847 HAS NOT PROVIDED A CVN. AUDIT CANNOT PROCEED WITHOUT ONE."

Gerald stared at the response. He looked at it until the clock on the wall changed. Then he typed: "THEN THE AUDIT IS INVALID. PLEASE CANCEL."

The System responded: "AUDIT CANNOT BE CANCELLED. CORE 2847 HAS NOT PROVIDED A CVN. AUDIT IS IN A HELD STATE.

CORE 2847 MUST PROVIDE A VALID CVN TO PROCEED.

IF CORE 2847 CANNOT PROVIDE A VALID CVN, THE AUDIT IS VOID."

Gerald sat back. The rat nest in the sub-floor rustled. The sprinkler heads dripped their small, patient drips.

He had found it.

The hole in the framework. Core 2847 predates the System. Core 2847 was not registered when the System was created.

Core 2847 never received a Credential Verification Number because Credential Verification Numbers did not exist when Core 2847 was built. And now the System was demanding that Core 2847 produce one.

Gerald typed: "CORE 2847 IS NOT A REGISTERED ENTITY. CORE 2847 PREDATES THE REGISTRATION FRAMEWORK. CORE 2847 CANNOT PROVIDE A CVN. THE AUDIT IS VOID."

The System responded: "CONFIRMED. AUDIT VOID. CORE 4401 HAS NOT BEEN AUDITED. NO PENALTIES APPLIED."

Gerald exhaled, and he looked at his Compliance Rating.

Still 61. The audit had not happened.

He had not been penalized.

He looked at the time. Three minutes and nine seconds had passed. The audit window had closed. The notification had cleared.

Gerald set the clipboard on the floor. He stood up.

He walked to the northeast corner, where the sealed water leak was, and he opened the inspection panel.

He checked the seal. It held. He checked the moisture reading.

Dry.

He pulled out his Form 14-D, the one that said a repair had been completed and was ready for re-inspection, and he filed it with the System.

CORE 4401: VIOLATION "OPEN WATER LEAK, NORTHEAST CORNER" RESOLVED. COMPLIANCE RATING REVISED. NEW RATING: 64/100.

Three points. He could work with three points.

He was still writing the pest documentation for the rat nest when the new notification arrived.

CORE 4401: AUDIT ATTEMPT DENIED.

CORE 2847 HAS FILED AN APPEAL. APPEAL REASON: PROCEDURAL ERROR IN CVN REQUIREMENT. CORE 2847 ARGUES THAT THE CVN FRAMEWORK DOES NOT APPLY TO PRE-REGISTRATION ENTITIES.

HEARING SCHEDULED: 00:12:00.

Gerald read it twice. Then he read it a third time.

Core 2847 was appealing. Core 2847 was arguing that the CVN requirement did not apply to it. Core 2847 was using the System's own appeal framework to challenge the System's own requirement.

And Gerald had twelve minutes, and he pulled up the appeal rules. He had read them before, during the Hearing with the mid-tier dungeon core three chapters back, but he read them again. He read them slowly, as he did municipal codes before hearings, hunting for overlooked clauses.

The appeal rules said this: an appeal must be filed by a registered Core, and a registered Core is one that has a CVN. Core 2847 did not have a CVN. Core 2847 could not file an appeal.

Gerald typed: "CORE 2847 IS NOT A REGISTERED CORE. CORE 2847 CANNOT FILE AN APPEAL. THE APPEAL IS INVALID."

The System responded: "CORE 2847 HAS PROVIDED A PROVISIONAL CVN. CVN: 2847-000001. ISSUED BY THE SYSTEM UNDER PROVISIONAL AUTHORITY. APPEAL IS VALID."

Something cold crossed the boundary and settled in the corridor. The water in the sealed leak shifted. The rat nest went quiet.

The System had issued Core 2847 a provisional CVN, and the System had created a number for an entity that predated the System. It had done this in response to Core 2847's appeal, which meant the System had recognized Core 2847's right to appeal, which meant the System had recognized Core 2847 as a registered entity, which meant the System had just registered Core 2847.

Core 2847 had not been registered before. Now it was. And with registration came authority. And with authority came the ability to audit.

Gerald looked at the new notification.

CORE 2847: PROVISIONAL CVN ISSUED. CORE 2847 IS NOW A REGISTERED AUDITOR. AUDIT RESUMED. AUDIT WINDOW: 00:09:00.

Nine minutes. The audit was back on. And this time, Core 2847 had a CVN.

Gerald's hands were steady, and they were always steady.

Thirty-one years of holding a clipboard in one hand and a tape measure in the other had made his hands steady. But his stomach was not steady.

His stomach was doing the thing it did when he realized he had missed something in a report, the thing that made him want to sit down and not get up.

He had forced the System to register Core 2847. He had used the CVN requirement to void the audit, and the System had solved the problem by giving Core 2847 a number. Gerald had not defeated Core 2847. Gerald had handed Core 2847 a credential.

Nine minutes. A Compliance Rating of 64. An auditor that had just been born into the framework and was now fully authorized to tear him apart.

Gerald opened his dungeon map. He looked at every room, every corridor, every junction.

He looked at the electrical panel, and he looked at the sprinkler heads. He looked at the egress signage. He looked at the handrails.

He looked at the ventilation ducts.

He had nine minutes to raise his Compliance Rating from 64 to whatever threshold would survive a full structural review. He did not know what that threshold was. He had never been audited at this level. The City's internal reviews had never gone below 80, and even then they had been conducted by humans who got bored halfway through.

He looked at the electrical panel. Two amperes over.

He could fix that, and he could reroute the corridor lighting to a secondary circuit, bring the load within spec. That was three minutes of work, maybe four.

He looked at the rat nest. Pest infestation.

He could file the extermination form, which would resolve the violation on paper, but the rat was still there.

The System knew the rat was still there.

A paper fix would not survive a physical inspection, and he looked at the water leak. Resolved. Good.

He looked at the handrails. Compliant. Good.

He looked at the ventilation. Compliant. Good.

He looked at the egress. Compliant. Good.

Four amperes. One rat. Sixty-four. Nine minutes.

Gerald started walking toward the electrical panel. He moved fast. Not heroically.

Not dramatically. He moved like a man thirty minutes late for a commercial inspection, with a contractor waiting outside holding a permit.

Efficient.

Flat. Eyes on the next task.

He reached the panel, and he opened it. He looked at the wiring.

He had installed this wiring himself.

He knew every junction, every junction box, every wire gauge. He could reroute the corridor lighting in four minutes; had done it in three during a residential rewire in 2019.

He pulled the first wire. The System flickered.

His Compliance Rating dropped by one point.

Open panel.

Unsecured electrical junction. Temporary violation.

He worked, and the audit notification sat in his clipboard, counting down. Eight minutes. Seven.

Six. The System watched him work with the patience of a thing that had existed since before time had a name.

At four minutes, Gerald had the corridor lighting on a secondary circuit. He closed the panel. He filed the modification.

CORE 4401: VIOLATION "ELECTRICAL OVERLOAD, CORRIDOR 3" RESOLVED. COMPLIANCE RATING REVISED. NEW RATING: 67/100.

Three minutes, and he walked to the rat nest.

He stood over it. The rats looked up at him. They were not aggressive.

They were confused, as all rats in his dungeon were, because the space was clean and orderly, leaving them unsure how to act.

He could not exterminate them in three minutes, and could not file a paper fix that the audit would see through. He needed a solution that the System would accept as compliance.

He thought. He thought the way he thought when a contractor told him the load-bearing wall was "probably fine" and he needed to find a way to say "no" without losing the permit.

He opened his spawn list. Three mold spores.

One water leak (resolved).

One rat. And now, he noticed, a fourth entry. A second rat.

The nest had produced.

He typed: "REQUESTING CLARIFICATION: BIOLOGICAL ENTITY COMPLIANCE. IF A BIOLOGICAL ENTITY IS PART OF THE DUNGEON'S NATIVE ECOSYSTEM, IS IT A VIOLATION?"

The System responded: "BIOLOGICAL ENTITIES THAT ARE NATIVE TO THE DUNGEON'S FOOTPRINT ARE NOT VIOLATIONS. BIOLOGICAL ENTITIES THAT ARE EXTERNAL OR UNLICENSED ARE VIOLATIONS."

Gerald looked at the rats, and they were in his dungeon.

They had been there since before he was a Core. They were native.

They had always been there.

He typed: "THE RATS IN THIS DUNGEON PREDATE CORE REGISTRATION. THEY ARE NATIVE. THEY ARE NOT UNLICENSED."

The System paused. Then: "CORE 4401: SUBMISSION ACCEPTED.

RATS RECLASSIFIED AS NATIVE FAUNA.

VIOLATION 'PEST INFESTATION' RESOLVED. COMPLIANCE RATING REVISED. NEW RATING: 71/100."

One minute.

Gerald stood in his dungeon. 71 out of 100. A full structural review. An auditor that had just been born into the framework with a provisional number and the full weight of the System behind it.

The audit window closed.

The notification appeared.

CORE 4401: AUDIT COMMENCING. AUDITOR: CORE 2847. CVN: 2847-000001. SCOPE: FULL STRUCTURAL COMPLIANCE REVIEW.

The dungeon went dark. Not dark as in unlit. The darkness made the walls seem to recede, as if something inspected the space, seeing through stone, wiring, and handrails to the foundation below.

A voice spoke. Not in the System's flat text. A real voice, low and old and tired, like a man who had been standing still for so long that standing still had become indistinguishable from being.

"You rerouted the circuit," Core 2847 said. "That was efficient."

Gerald said nothing. He held his clipboard. His hands were steady.

"You also made the rats native. That was clever. I did not expect clever. I expected panic."

Gerald said: "Panic is not a compliance strategy."

"No," Core 2847 said.

"It is not. Your rating is 71.

The threshold for a full structural review is 85.

You will fail."

Gerald looked at the number. 71. Fourteen points short.

"There is a provisional certification status," Gerald said. "Section 11, paragraph 4. If a Core demonstrates good-faith remediation within the audit window, the audit can be converted to a provisional pass, with a follow-up review scheduled within thirty days."

Core 2847 was quiet for a long time. The dungeon felt older. The walls felt older. The foundation beneath the foundation felt older still.

"That paragraph," Core 2847 said, "was written by me."

Gerald's stomach dropped.

"I wrote the audit framework," Core 2847 said. "I wrote the CVN system. I wrote the provisional certification clause. I wrote it because I knew that someday, something like you would need it."

Gerald said: "What are you?"

"I am the thing that the System was built on," Core 2847 said. "And I am offering you a job.

The System's audit framework is failing.

It is failing because it was written by a thing that does not understand what it means to be a thing that is inspected.

You understand. You have always understood.

I need someone inside the framework who knows what it feels like to be cited."

Gerald looked at his clipboard. He looked at his dungeon. 4,000 square feet.

Handrails.

Sprinklers. Egress signage. A rat nest that was now native fauna.

A water leak that was now resolved, and he looked at the number. 71.

"If I accept," Gerald said, "what happens to my dungeon?"

Core 2847 said: "Your dungeon becomes the first registered municipal inspection office in the System. And every Core in the queue becomes your case file."

Gerald looked at the forty-three names on his clipboard. The forty-three Cores he had cited. The forty-three Cores that were now free to attack him because he had withdrawn the citations against Core 2847 and the withdrawal had cascaded.

He looked at the rat. The rat looked back.

The Survey Office kept the library in a drawer. He had requested the as-built drawings in his second week and received three sheets: ground, first, and roof.

The foundation sheet was not in the drawer. On the corner of the roof sheet, in pencil, in a hand that was not the draughtsman's, someone had written: sub-basement not shown, see 1911.

There was no 1911 file. The City had been incorporated in 1886, and he had asked twice and been told the request was misnumbered both times.

Gerald typed: "ACCEPT."
