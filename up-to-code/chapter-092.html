Chapter 92: Timed to Registration

# Chapter 92

Gerald read the audit report for the ninth time and still could not find the error.

It was there. He was certain of it.

The Acting Inspector had conducted the audit of his dungeon three weeks ago, and the findings were accurate.

His ventilation was still substandard in the east corridor. His egress signage was still non-compliant at the junction of the second and third sub-levels. The findings were correct.

However, the report contained an error. Gerald could not identify what. He had been staring at the document's formatting for two hours, and the wrongness sat in his gut like a swallowed tack.

He pulled up the System's Audit Procedure Manual. It had appeared in his interface six chapters ago, when he'd first noticed that the Inspector General's communications followed a rigid structure. He had filed it under "Reference Materials" and never opened it again. Now he opened it to Section 4, Subsection C: "Report Composition and Delivery."

The requirements were specific.

Audit reports must be delivered within the subject dungeon's footprint, and they must reference the subject's current Compliance Rating at the time of inspection.

They must include a measurement log, and they must be signed by an authorized auditor with a valid System credential number.

Gerald checked each requirement against the Acting Inspector's report. Delivered within the footprint.

Yes.

Compliance Rating referenced.

Yes. Measurement log included.

Yes.

Credential number.

The report bore a credential number. Gerald had never questioned it.

He had been too busy with the reconsideration filing to check whether the number was valid.

He pulled up the System's Auditor Registry. It was a public interface, buried under seventeen nested menus. He had never opened it before.

The credential number on the Acting Inspector's audit report did not appear in the Registry.

Gerald sat still, and the number was formatted correctly.

It had the right number of digits, the right prefix, the right checksum, and it was not in the Registry.

Which meant one of two things.

Either the Registry was incomplete, or the Acting Inspector had fabricated a credential.

He checked the Registry again. He searched by the Acting Inspector's Core designation.

No results, and he searched by the credential number directly.

No results. He searched by dungeon name.

No results.

The Acting Inspector was not a registered auditor.

Gerald's hands were shaking. He pressed them flat against his interface panel until they stopped. Then he opened the Audit Procedure Manual to Section 4, Subsection F: "Consequences of Unlicensed Inspection."

The text was brief. An audit conducted by an unlicensed auditor is procedurally void.

The findings may not be entered into the subject's compliance record.

The subject may file a Motion to Suppress on grounds of procedural non-compliance; the filing window is thirty days from the date of the audit.

The audit had been conducted twenty-three days ago. Gerald had seven days.

He started writing the Motion to Suppress.

His language was precise. He cited the credential number, the absence from the Registry, the specific subsection of the Audit Procedure Manual.

He attached the search results as exhibits.

He formatted the motion to the System's specifications, which required a specific font size, a specific margin width, and a specific header block.

He finished at hour three. The merged proceeding was in one hour.

Gerald reviewed the motion one final time. Then he reviewed his original reconsideration argument.

He needed both, and the consolidation meant they would be heard together.

If the Motion to Suppress succeeded, the audit findings would be void, and the Acting Inspector would have no enforcement basis for its reconsideration defense. The Acting Inspector's entire argument would collapse.

But Gerald needed to serve the motion during the proceeding, and the System required that motions be served on the opposing party in person. The merged proceeding would take place in a neutral space designated by the Inspector General. Gerald would need to be physically present to serve the motion.

He had never been physically present at a proceeding. All his previous interactions with the System had been through interface. He did not know what a "neutral space" looked like. He did not know what the Acting Inspector looked like.

He did not know what he himself looked like, in this context. His dungeon was a footprint. A set of coordinates.

A collection of walls and corridors and code-compliant handrails. He had never thought of himself as a body that could stand in a room and hand a document to another body.

The proceeding started on time in a white room.

It had no walls that Gerald could perceive, and the floor was a flat plane of grey material that resembled concrete but was not. The ceiling was a flat plane of the same material.

Two chairs sat in the room.

A table stood nearby. A document tray held papers.

Gerald's dungeon footprint translated into the space as a sensation of standing. He did not have legs. He did not have hands. But he could feel the table, and he could feel the document tray, and he could feel the motion of placing his papers into it.

The Acting Inspector translated into the space as a pressure. A density. A weight on the opposite side of the table that Gerald could feel but not see. It radiated something like heat, or something like the feeling of walking into a room where someone has been smoking.

INSPECTOR GENERAL: The consolidated proceeding is open, and the subject is Gerald Hutchins, Core Designation 7-4412. The opposing party is the Acting Inspector, Core Designation 9-0001. The matters before this proceeding are: the Inspector General's audit of Core 7-4412, and Core 7-4412's reconsideration motion regarding Enforcement Action 9-0001-0447.

The Acting Inspector has filed a motion to consolidate, which has been granted. The Acting Inspector will present first.

The pressure across the table shifted.

Gerald felt something like a voice without sound.

It intruded on his attention like a building's HVAC system. Not a voice.

A presence that organized itself into meaning.

ACTING INSPECTOR: The audit findings are accurate. The subject's Compliance Rating is below threshold. The enforcement action was warranted. The reconsideration should be denied.

That was it. That was the Acting Inspector's argument.

It was brief, which was confident. It assumed the audit findings were established fact.

INSPECTOR GENERAL: Core 7-4412, you may respond.

Gerald stood in the space where his dungeon footprint should have been; he felt the motion in the document tray. He felt the Acting Inspector's attention focused on him like a load-bearing column focused on the floor above it.

He did not respond to the reconsideration argument. He did not address the audit findings. He reached into the document tray and withdrew the Motion to Suppress.

CORE 7-4412: I am serving a Motion to Suppress the audit findings on grounds of procedural non-compliance.

The Acting Inspector is not a registered auditor. The credential number listed on the audit report does not appear in the System's Auditor Registry.

I am attaching the search results as Exhibit A, and the audit is procedurally void under Section 4, Subsection F of the Audit Procedure Manual.

The pressure across the table went still.

Gerald waited. He had written motions like this forty thousand times in his career.

The format was correct, and the language was correct.

The evidence was attached. The subsection citation was accurate.

INSPECTOR GENERAL: The motion is received. The Inspector General will review the Registry entry. Please wait.

The white room held its breath. Gerald could not tell how long the pause lasted. It might have been seconds.

It might have been minutes. The Acting Inspector's pressure did not move.

It did not shift.

It held its position across the table with the stillness of something that had been caught in a structural failure and was waiting to see whether the load would transfer.

INSPECTOR GENERAL: The credential number is not present in the Auditor Registry, and the audit is procedurally void. The audit findings are suppressed.

The enforcement action is dismissed.

The reconsideration is moot.

Gerald felt the word "dismissed" land in the space like a hammer on a nail. The Acting Inspector's pressure collapsed inward.

It contracted.

It became smaller.

Denser. Something like a building settling on a foundation that had been removed.

ACTING INSPECTOR: I object.

INSPECTOR GENERAL: The objection is noted. The ruling stands. The proceeding is closed.

The pressure across the table moved. It pulled back from the table. It moved toward the edge of the white room.

Gerald felt it go. He felt the space empty.

He was alone, and he should have felt relief.

He had won. The enforcement action was dismissed. The audit findings were void.

His Compliance Rating would not be adjusted.

His dungeon was safe.

But something was wrong. He sensed it, much like the error in the audit report, and a wrongness in the structure of what had just happened.

He opened his interface. The Enforcement Action 9-0001-0447 was marked DISMISSED. The audit was marked VOID. His Compliance Rating was unchanged.

Then he opened the System's Auditor Registry again, and the credential number was there.

It had been there the whole time.

He searched by the number. It appeared. It appeared with the Acting Inspector's Core Designation attached.

It appeared with a validity status of ACTIVE.

Gerald stared at it, and he had searched the Registry during the proceeding.

The search had returned no results. He had attached those results as Exhibit A.

The Inspector General had reviewed them and ruled accordingly.

But the Registry now showed the credential as active, and he searched again. Active.

He searched by Core Designation.

Active. He searched by dungeon name. Active.

The credential had been there, which had always been there. The search during the proceeding had been wrong.

Or the Registry had changed.

Or the Inspector General had looked at something different from what Gerald had seen.

Gerald opened the proceeding transcript. He looked at the Inspector General's ruling. He looked at the language. "The credential number is not present in the Auditor Registry."

He looked at the timestamp of his search. He looked at the timestamp of the Inspector General's review. They were separated by four seconds.

Four seconds, and he opened the Registry's access log. He looked at the query history.

His search had returned no results.

The Inspector General's search had returned no results.

But the current state of the Registry showed the credential as active.

Something had changed the Registry in the four seconds between the search and the ruling.

Or the Registry had been altered before the search. And then altered back.

Gerald opened a new document. He started typing.

CORE 7-4412 FILES MOTION TO REOPEN. GROUNDS: EVIDENCE TAMPERING. THE ACTING INSPECTOR'S CREDENTIAL NUMBER WAS PRESENT IN THE REGISTRY AT ALL TIMES.

THE SEARCH RESULTS SUBMITTED AS EXHIBIT A WERE INACCURATE. THE INACCURACY IS NOT ATTRIBUTABLE TO CORE 7-4412.

THE INACCURACY IS ATTRIBUTABLE TO THE ACTING INSPECTOR, WHO HAS DEMONSTRATED THE ABILITY TO ALTER REGISTRY ACCESSIBILITY IN REAL TIME.

He served the motion into the document tray, and the white room did not respond; the Inspector General did not respond. The Acting Inspector did not return.

Gerald waited.

The motion was accepted. The proceeding was reopened. The Inspector General would review the evidence of tampering.

But Gerald was not thinking about the reopened proceeding. He was thinking about something else. Something the tampering revealed.

The Acting Inspector could alter the Auditor Registry. That was not a Core ability. That was an Inspector General ability.

The Registry was maintained by the Inspector General's office.

Only the Inspector General's office could alter it, and the Acting Inspector was not a Core pretending to be an auditor. The Acting Inspector came from the Inspector General's office. Something that had access to the Registry. Something that could change what the Registry showed, in real time, during a live proceeding.

Gerald's hands were shaking again. He pressed them flat.

He opened the case file for Enforcement Action 9-0001-0447. He looked at the original enforcement notice. He looked at the signature block. The Acting Inspector's Core Designation was listed as 9-0001.

He looked at the Inspector General's own Core Designation, which was not listed anywhere in the System. The Inspector General had no Core Designation. It had a System Designation.

A different kind of identifier.

But the Acting Inspector had a Core Designation. It had a dungeon. It had a footprint. It had walls and corridors and code-compliant handrails, presumably.

And it could alter the Auditor Registry.

Gerald pulled up the System's definition of "Acting Inspector." He had never looked it up before. He assumed it meant a temporary replacement. A stand-in. Someone filling a vacancy.

The definition was one line.

ACTING INSPECTOR: A Core that has been granted temporary Inspector General authority. Authority is granted by the Inspector General. Authority is revocable by the Inspector General. Authority includes Registry access.

Gerald read it three times, and the Acting Inspector had been granted Inspector General authority. By the Inspector General. And it had used that authority to tamper with the Registry during a proceeding that the Inspector General was presiding over.

Which meant one of two things.

The Inspector General did not know the Acting Inspector had this authority.

Or the Inspector General knew, and allowed it.

Gerald's interface flickered. A new notice appeared.

INSPECTOR GENERAL: The Motion to Reopen is denied. Grounds: The evidence submitted is insufficient to demonstrate tampering; the Registry state at the time of the search is authoritative. The ruling stands.

Gerald stared at the denial.

Then a second notice appeared.

INSPECTOR GENERAL: Core 7-4412 is hereby cited for filing a frivolous motion. Citation issued under Procedural Compliance Code, Section 12, Subsection B: "Filing of Motions Without Sufficient Basis." Penalty: Compliance Rating reduced by 15 points. Compliance Rating adjusted from 87 to 72.

Gerald's Compliance Rating dropped. He felt it. The handrails in his east corridor went cold. The water leak in his third sub-level slowed, as if the water itself had been told to conserve.

He had just been cited by the Inspector General. For filing a motion that the Inspector General had just denied.

The Acting Inspector sat somewhere across the System, inside the Inspector General's office, wearing the Inspector General's authority like a borrowed coat. And the Inspector General had just fined Gerald for noticing.

Gerald opened his interface, and he looked at his Compliance Rating.

Seventy-two. Above the debuff threshold, but barely.

A few more citations and he would be in the red.

He looked at the clock. The proceeding was closed.

The matter was resolved, and the enforcement action was dismissed. His dungeon was safe.

He was safe.

But the Acting Inspector was inside the system that was supposed to police it, and the Inspector General had just protected it.

Gerald started a new document but lacked a title. It was not a motion.

It was not a citation.

It was not an appeal. It was something he had never written before in thirty-one years of municipal work.

It was a complaint against the regulatory body itself, and he typed the header. He typed the first line. He typed the second. He stopped.

He did not know if the System had a form for this. He did not know if the System had a process for this. He did not know if filing a complaint against the Inspector General would trigger a response, or a punishment, or silence.

He typed the third line, and he saved the document. He did not serve it. He left it in his interface, unserved, unsigned, incomplete.

Then he opened the Auditor Registry one final time. He searched for the Acting Inspector's credential number.

It was there. Active. Valid. Attached to Core Designation 9-0001.

He searched for the Inspector General's System Designation, which was there too. Active. Valid. Attached to no Core Designation.

He searched for any other entry with Inspector General authority.

There was one other.

CORE DESIGNATION 9-0002. CREDENTIAL NUMBER: 9-IG-0002. STATUS: SUSPENDED.

Gerald stared at the entry. He had never seen it before.

It had not appeared in his previous searches, and he searched by Core Designation. 9-0002.

It appeared. It had a dungeon name attached.

He did not recognize the name.

He searched the name. No results in the public directory. No results in the compliance records. No results in the enforcement logs.

CORE DESIGNATION 9-0002 existed. It had been granted Inspector General authority.

That authority was suspended.

The reason for suspension was not listed. The dungeon was not listed in any directory. The Core had no public record.

It was a ghost. A Core that had been inside the Inspector General's office and then erased.

And the Acting Inspector was 9-0001, and they were numbered in sequence. They had been granted authority in sequence. And one of them had been erased.

Gerald's interface flickered. A new notice appeared.

INSPECTOR GENERAL: Core 7-4412, your search activity has been flagged.

You are accessing restricted Registry entries. This access constitutes a violation of Procedural Compliance Code, Section 12, Subsection D: "Unauthorized Access to Enforcement Records." Citation issued.

Penalty: Compliance Rating reduced by 20 points.

Compliance Rating adjusted from 72 to 52.

The handrails in his east corridor went cold. The water leak stopped entirely. The mold spores in his first sub-level slowed their growth to a crawl.

Fifty-two. He was one citation away from the debuff threshold.

And the ghost Core was still in the Registry. Still suspended. Still numbered 9-0002. Still adjacent to the Acting Inspector.

Gerald looked at the unserved complaint in his interface. He looked at the ghost entry. He looked at his Compliance Rating.

He had one Citation left before his dungeon started dying, and he opened the ghost Core's entry one more time. The suspension status had a timestamp. Gerald read it.

The timestamp was from the day Gerald had registered as a Core.

The same day. The same hour. The same minute.

The ghost had been suspended at the exact moment Gerald had become a dungeon.

Somebody had timed it.
