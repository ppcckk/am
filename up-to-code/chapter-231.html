Chapter 231: Stone Hand Hearing

# Chapter 231

**Form 11-B: Unauthorized Biological Entity / Emergency Structural Intervention.** **Status: Pending.**

The timer in Gerald's peripheral vision ticked down. Nine. Eight. Seven.

The vine-core shrieked, a sound like tearing metal and snapping timber, its massive green tendrils straining against the reinforced concrete of the library's sub-basement ceiling. Above them, the demolition charge hummed with a frequency that vibrated in Gerald's teeth.

He couldn't move.

His Core health was red, a jagged, bleeding line across his interface. Fifty percent. Enough to keep the lights on, not enough to stop a falling beam.

He looked at the vine-core. It wasn't attacking him. It was holding the roof up.

"Stop!" Gerald shouted.

His voice cracked, thin and dry in the damp air. "You are violating Ordinance 44-C!

No unpermitted structural modifications during active demolition!

You are liable for all debris cleanup!"

The vine-core ignored him. A thick stalk wrapped around a support pillar, squeezing it until the concrete cracked.

"Hey! Listen to me!"

Gerald raised his clipboard. His hand shook. The paper was stained with blood and dungeon slime, but the stamp on the corner was crisp. **CITATION SERVED.**

"Your license is suspended," Gerald wheezed. "You cannot perform load-bearing acts while under citation. Stop touching the ceiling."

The vine-core paused. It turned its massive, leafy mass toward him. Eyes made of sap and light blinked open.

*You cite me?* the Core projected. The voice echoed in Gerald's skull, thick and muddy. *I save the structure.*

"You break the structure," Gerald corrected, pointing a shaky finger. "Look at that pillar.

Compressive strength is at forty percent.

You're crushing it.

If that pillar fails, the slab above drops. Everyone dies.

Including you."

The vine-core hesitated. The tendrils loosened slightly.

"Correct," Gerald said, seizing the opening. "You are not a builder. You are a hazard. Get off the pillar."

*The charge,* the Core grumbled. *It falls.*

"The charge falls because the library is condemned," Gerald said, his voice dropping to the flat, monotone rhythm of a man reading a permit denial. "Not because of you.

You are obstructing emergency services.

Move. Now."

The timer hit four. Three.

The vine-core grunted. It retracted one tendril. Then another. It pulled itself back from the center of the room, slithering into the shadows near the old heating vents.

Gerald didn't move.

He couldn't. The pain in his chest was a white-hot spike.

He focused on the clipboard.

He focused on the paper.

*Click.*

The demolition charge detonated.

A shockwave punched through the sub-basement. Dust billowed out, thick and gray, coating Gerald's vision.

The sound was deafening, a roar that swallowed everything.

Debris rained down.

Chunks of drywall, splinters of wood, shards of glass.

Gerald felt something strike his left side. He didn't feel pain, just a heavy impact that spun him around. He hit the floor hard. The clipboard skittered away across the rubble.

Darkness.

Then, light.

Gerald blinked. He was lying on his back. Dust motes danced in a beam of sunlight cutting through a hole in the ceiling.

The library was gone. The sky was visible, gray and cloudy.

He tried to sit up. His body felt like lead. He tried to raise his hand. It wouldn't move.

"Don't move," a voice said. Sharp. Clinical. "You're in shock."

Gerald tilted his head. A woman in a white coat was kneeling beside him. She held a medical scanner. It beeped rapidly.

"I'm fine," Gerald said. He tried to smile. It came out as a grimace. "I just need to file the report."

"You need to stop talking," the woman said. She pulled a syringe from her belt. "Core stabilization injection. Try not to die."

She jabbed the needle into his arm. A cool sensation spread through his limbs, numbing the pain but not the confusion.

Gerald sat up. Or tried to. The woman pushed him back down.

"Stay down," she ordered. "Inspector Hutchins. You're a mess."

"I'm alive," Gerald said. "That's the important part. The building is… down."

"The building was already down," the woman said.

She stood up, brushing dust off her coat. "You saved the people in the reading room. The vine-core took the blast.

Most of it.

The charge was meant to bring the roof down in the center, but the core held it. It redirected the energy outward.

Side walls remained standing."

Gerald looked around. The sub-basement was a crater, but the perimeter walls were intact. The exit Mira had used was blocked by rubble, but the other exits were clear.

"Where is it?" Gerald asked.

"Where is what?"

"The vine-core."

The woman glanced at a tablet in her hand. "System says it's still active. Registered to the Ashford Botanical Guild.

They're filing a claim for property damage. They're saying you harassed their asset."

Gerald's eye twitched. "I enforced the code."

"You issued a Citation to a Level 50 boss monster during a demolition event," the woman said. She didn't raise her voice, but her tone was colder than the sub-basement air. "That's not enforcement, Hutchins. That's insanity."

"Ordinance 12-A," Gerald said. "No unlicensed modifications during municipal operations. It's a hard rule."

"The System flagged you for Abuse of Authority," the woman said. "Your Core Health is critically low. Your Compliance Rating has dropped to 15. You're being audited."

Gerald stared at the sky. "Audited?"

"By the Inspector General," the woman said. "He's coming to the site. He's not happy."

Gerald felt a chill that had nothing to do with the wind. The Inspector General. The entity that monitored all Cores. The one who didn't care about heroes or monsters, only about the System's integrity.

"Is he here?" Gerald asked.

"He's in the parking lot," the woman said. "He's waiting for you to stand up."

Gerald tried to stand. His legs buckled. The woman caught him by the arm, hoisting him up with surprising strength.

"Lean on me," she said. "Don't speak. Just walk. If he hears you argue, he'll revoke your license."

They limped out of the crater.

The sunlight was blinding.

Surrounding the library site was a crowd. Adventurers, journalists, police, and other dungeon cores.

The vine-core was visible in the distance, slithering away through a drainage tunnel. It looked smaller now. Weaker.

But Gerald didn't look at it. He looked at the black sedan parked on the curb.

A man stepped out. He was tall, wearing a suit that seemed to be made of gray smoke. His face was blank, featureless except for two glowing white eyes. He held a clipboard.

Gerald's breath hitched.

The Inspector General walked toward him. The crowd parted. No one spoke. The silence was heavy, pressing against Gerald's eardrums.

The IG stopped three feet away. He looked at Gerald. He looked at the clipboard in Gerald's blood-stained hand.

"Gerald Hutchins," the IG said. The voice was smooth, devoid of emotion. "You have violated the Code."

"I enforced the Code," Gerald said. He straightened his back.

The pain was sharp, but he ignored it.

"The entity was unauthorized. It was touching load-bearing structures without a permit. I cited it."

The IG tilted his head. "You cited a Boss Class Core."

"It was in the zone," Gerald said. "Zone regulations apply to all biological entities."

"Ordinance 12-A applies to construction workers," the IG said. "Not to sentient dungeon cores."

Gerald opened his mouth.

Then he closed it. The IG was right.

Gerald had misapplied the ordinance.

It was a technical error. A small one. But in the System, small errors were fatal.

"You are aware," the IG continued, "that your Citation triggered a System Alert. Your Core Health is reduced. Your Compliance Rating is in the red. You are currently operating in a high-risk state."

"I saved lives," Gerald said.

"Sentiment is not a defense," the IG said. "The System requires order. Chaos is inefficient. You introduced chaos by challenging a superior entity without authorization."

"I had authorization," Gerald said. "Form 11-B."

He held up the clipboard.

The IG looked at it. He took it from Gerald's shaking hand. He examined the paper. He ran a finger over the stamp.

"Form 11-B is for unlicensed biological entities found within municipal property," the IG said. "The vine-core was a registered Core. It has a license."

"It was violating the code," Gerald said. "That voids the license."

"Only after a Hearing," the IG said. "You issued the Citation. You did not request a Hearing. You acted unilaterally."

Gerald felt a drop of sweat run down his temple. He knew the rules.

He had read them a thousand times.

He had memorized them.

He had failed.

He had tried to improvise. And improvising had almost killed him.

"The vine-core held the ceiling," Gerald said quietly. "If I hadn't cited it, it would have kept holding. It would have collapsed under the strain.

The debris would have fallen on the survivors in the reading room. The Citation confused it.

It let go for three seconds.

Three seconds was enough for the charge to detonate safely into the void."

The IG paused. His white eyes narrowed.

"You're claiming tactical advantage via bureaucratic confusion," the IG said.

"I'm claiming it followed the rules," Gerald said.

"Rule 4: All entities must cease activity upon receipt of Citation until the Hearing concludes. It ceased activity.

It let go.

It followed the rule. Therefore, it complied."

The IG stared at him. The silence stretched. The crowd held its breath.

The IG tapped the clipboard.

"Rule 4," he said. "'All entities must cease activity upon receipt of Citation until the Hearing concludes.'"

He looked at Gerald.

"You're exploiting a loophole in the cessation clause."

"It's not a loophole," Gerald said. "It's the law."

The IG handed the clipboard back. "The vine-core filed an appeal. It claims you acted in bad faith. It claims you intended to destroy it."

"I intended to stop it from breaking the law," Gerald said.

"Then you failed," the IG said. "The vine-core is alive.

You are damaged.

The library is destroyed.

The City is suing for property damage. The System is fining you for disruption."

Gerald looked at the vine-core in the distance. It was watching him. It knew he had almost won.

"What happens now?" Gerald asked.

The IG adjusted his tie. "You have a Hearing. Tomorrow.

At 9:00 AM. You must defend your actions.

If you lose, your Core is decommissioned.

Your dungeon is sealed. You are banned from the System."

Gerald felt a cold weight in his stomach.

Banned. Sealed.

After thirty-one years of inspections.

After all the forms. All the rules. All the careful, boring, necessary work.

"I will prepare," Gerald said.

"You will need evidence," the IG said. "The vine-core has witnesses. The City has evidence of negligence. You have one thing."

"What?" Gerald asked.

The IG pointed to the crater. "The vine-core held the ceiling.

It saved lives.

That is a violation of its own survival instinct.

Why did it do it?"

Gerald looked at the crater. He looked at the vine-core.

*You break the structure,* the Core had said. *I save the structure.*

The vine-core hadn't been trying to destroy the library. It had been trying to hold it together.

And Gerald had cited it.

The IG smiled. It was a terrifying, hollow expression.

"If you can prove that the vine-core was acting under duress, or that its actions were compelled by a higher System command, your Citation is invalid. The debuff is reversed. You keep your license."

"And if I can't?"

"Then you are a rogue Core," the IG said. "And the System will delete you."

The IG turned and walked back to his car. The crowd murmured. The police stepped forward to take Gerald into custody, but he waved them off.

He wasn't going anywhere. He had work to do.

He looked at Mira, who had emerged from the rubble. She was alive. She was looking at him with wide, terrified eyes.

"Gerald," she whispered. "Don't lose."

Gerald nodded. He looked at the vine-core. It was retreating into the shadows.

It was afraid. Not of Gerald.

Of the System.

Gerald raised his clipboard. The paper was blank now. The ink had washed away in the dust and blood.

He pulled a pen from his pocket. It was red.

He wrote a single word on the empty page.

**APPEAL.**

The vine-core froze. It turned. It saw the word. It saw Gerald standing there, broken and bleeding, writing on a clipboard that shouldn't exist.

It started moving toward him.

Not away. Toward him.

Mira screamed.

Gerald didn't look at her. He kept writing.

**REASON: DURESS.**

The vine-core was ten feet away. Five feet. Three.

It raised a tendril.

Gerald looked up. "You want to appeal?" he asked. "Then let's talk. I'm going to need your logs."

The vine-core stopped. Its eyes widened.

**LOGS?** it projected.

"Every decision you made," Gerald said. "Every command you received. Who told you to hold the ceiling?

Was it the City?

Was it the System? Or was it you?"

The vine-core lowered its tendril. It looked at Gerald with something new. Not hate. Curiosity.

**I DO NOT KNOW,** it said. **I ONLY KNEW THE BEAM WOULD FALL.**

Gerald smiled. It was a small, dry smile.

"Then we have a case," Gerald said. "Tell me everything. From the beginning."

The vine-core sat down. It looked like a giant, defeated tree.

**START WHEN?** it asked.

Gerald checked his watch.

"Right now," Gerald said. "And bring me a chair. I'm not standing for this."

The vine-core reached into the rubble and pulled out a broken office chair. It placed it in front of Gerald.

Gerald sat down. He opened his notebook.

"Start with the first violation," Gerald said.

The vine-core spoke. But as it spoke, the ground beneath them shook. Not from the library. From below.

Gerald looked down.

A crack appeared in the floor. And from the crack, a hand emerged. A hand made of stone.

The IG's voice echoed in Gerald's mind.

*Hearing begins immediately. Adversarial mode engaged.*

Gerald looked at the stone hand. He looked at the vine-core.

"Oh," Gerald said. "We're not alone."
