Chapter 49: Eight Minutes to Forty

# Chapter 49

Gerald's hands worked the hardware assembly with the familiarity of thirty-one years and ten thousand doors. Strike plate.

Latch bolt.

Thumb piece. The sequence was muscle memory deeper than thought. He shaped each dungeon component as he once visualized a door frame, though here intention replaced tools and System units replaced millimeters.

The latch bolt was the critical piece.

The code required a minimum throw of one-half inch for egress hardware. Gerald shaped it at three-quarters.

He had always over-built.

Over-building was not a violation. Over-building was insurance.

He mounted the thumb piece on the interior face. The exterior face required a different mechanism.

Panic hardware.

A horizontal bar that, when pressed, retracted the latch with a motion.

The code was explicit: one motion, no knowledge required, no special effort. The System had translated this into a flat panel on the exterior face that responded to any forward pressure.

Gerald tested it. He pushed the panel. The latch retracted.

The opening swung inward. He pushed again.

It swung outward.

Both directions compliant, and he pulled up the complaint response form. His Compliance Rating ticked up by two points. Not enough to matter. Enough to satisfy the specific violation.

He filed the response. The System accepted it. The Hollow's complaint was resolved.

Gerald looked at the clock. One hour and forty-seven minutes until license review.

He looked at the door, which was a good door.

It met every specification in the hardware chapter. It had a rating.

Upon completion, the System automatically assigned it a rating, as it did for everything.

Fire rating: twenty minutes. That was low. Commercial egress doors in municipal buildings were rated sixty to ninety minutes.

Twenty minutes was residential. Twenty minutes was the minimum for a single-family dwelling.

But this was not a single-family dwelling, which was a dungeon.

The System classified it as "private storage, unoccupied by persons other than Core entities." Private storage had no fire rating requirement for doors. The twenty-minute rating was a floor, not a ceiling.

It was the minimum the System would allow. It was not a requirement.

Gerald read this three times.

Then he read the definition of "egress route."

An egress route was a continuous and unobstructed path of travel from any point within a structure to a public way. The door was on the egress route, and the egress route connected to the opening that led to the library sub-basement. The library sub-basement was a public way by municipal classification.

So the door was on a path leading to a public way.

So the door was an egress door.

So the door required a fire rating appropriate to the occupancy classification of the structure it served.

Gerald pulled up the occupancy classification table. Private storage, unoccupied: no fire rating required for any element.

This was why his dungeon had never needed fire-rated anything.

His mold walls were not rated. His handrails were not rated. His floor was not rated.

None of it required a rating because the occupancy classification exempted him.

But the door was on an egress route leading to a public way. The System had recognized this. The System had assigned the door a twenty-minute fire rating because the door was now, by definition, part of a means of egress.

Gerald read the next line. He read it twice.

"Where a means of egress terminates at a door classified as a fire-rated assembly, the door shall be installed within a fire-rated wall assembly of equal or greater rating."

The door was twenty minutes. The wall around it was not rated at all.

The wall was not rated at all, and the wall was not rated at all.

Gerald looked at the wall. It was dungeon material.

It was his body.

It was mold and mineral and something that had once been the foundation of a library built in 1962. It was not fire-rated. It was not anything-rated.

It was unclassified because it had never needed classification.

Now it needed classification, and he pulled up the wall assembly requirements.

A twenty-minute fire-rated wall assembly required specific material composition, specific thickness, specific continuity from floor to ceiling to adjacent wall.

Every joint required fire-rated sealant. Every penetration required fire-rated penetration sleeves.

Every handrail attachment required fire-rated blocking behind the mounting plate.

He looked at his handrails. They were mounted directly to the wall with simple bracket fasteners.

No blocking.

No sleeves. No sealant.

He looked at the mold colonies. They were penetrations. They were unsealed. They were violations.

He looked at the floor.

The door threshold met the floor. The floor was unsealed.

The junction between wall and floor was unsealed.

The junction between wall and ceiling was unsealed.

Every junction in his egress route was a violation.

Gerald sat down on the floor. This was allowed.

He was not on the egress route when he sat, and he was three feet to the left of it.

The egress route was thirty-six inches wide and he was outside its boundary.

He pulled up the code for fire-rated wall assemblies. It was forty-seven pages long. He had never read it.

He had inspected fire-rated assemblies hundreds of times, but he had never built one. Building was different from inspecting.

Inspecting required knowledge of what should be there.

Building required the physical ability to put it there.

Could he build a fire-rated wall assembly?

The System gave him material control over his dungeon. He could shape it.

He could thicken it, and he could add layers; could he add the specific material composition the code required? Fire-rated assemblies used specific mineral compounds, specific densities, specific moisture content.

The System's material palette was dungeon material. Was dungeon material capable of meeting fire-rated specifications?

He pulled up the material properties panel.

Dungeon material had no fire rating, which had no thermal properties listed. It was, in the System's taxonomy, "undetermined." Undetermined meant the System had not tested it.

Undetermined meant the System did not know what it was.

Gerald stared at the word undetermined.

Undetermined was not compliant.

He had one hour and forty-three minutes until license review; could not build a fire-rated wall assembly in one hour and forty-three minutes. He did not know if he could build one at all. The material itself might not be capable.

He looked at the door again. The door was the problem.

The door had triggered the chain, and the door had pulled the egress route into a new classification. The egress route now had a fire-rated element, which required a fire-rated assembly, which he could not build, which meant the entire egress route was non-compliant.

Non-compliant egress was a Category 3 violation.

Category 3 violations carried automatic cessation orders. His dungeon would shut down.

His spawns would freeze.

His Compliance Rating would drop to zero.

His license would be revoked, and the Hollow had not filed a hardware complaint. The Hollow had filed a hardware complaint that, when resolved, would create a fire-rated assembly that Gerald could not support.

The Hollow had known.

The Hollow had known that Gerald would build the door because Gerald always built the door.

The Hollow had known that the door would cascade.

Gerald pulled up The Hollow's message history. He read the original complaint again. FAILURE TO MAINTAIN COMPLIANT DOOR HARDWARE ON EGRESS ROUTE. REQUESTED ACTION: IMMEDIATE CESSATION OF ALL DUNGEON OPERATIONS PENDING HARDWARE COMPLIANCE.

Pending hardware compliance. Not pending hardware installation. Pending compliance.

The Hollow had not asked him to build a door. The Hollow had asked him to be compliant.

The Hollow had known that compliance, for Gerald, meant building the door.

And the door would do the rest.

A new message appeared.

NOTICE: CORE HUTCHINS. YOUR EGRESS ROUTE HAS BEEN RECLASSIFIED. OCCUPANCY CLASSIFICATION UPDATE PENDING.

Gerald's stomach dropped.

Occupancy classification.

Not just the egress route. The whole structure.

If the egress route was reclassified, the occupancy classification of the entire structure would update.

Private storage was the lowest classification. It had the fewest requirements. If his occupancy classification changed, every requirement in his dungeon would change.

He pulled up the classification criteria. An occupancy was classified by its use.

Private storage required no human habitation, no assembly, no commercial activity.

But a means of egress leading to a public way implied that persons could enter the structure.

The System's logic was circular and inescapable. If you had an egress route, you had an occupancy.

If you had an occupancy, you had occupancy requirements.

His dungeon had an egress route because he had always had an opening. The opening had always been there.

It had never been a door before.

Now it was a door. Now it was an egress route. Now it was an occupancy.

NOTICE: OCCUPANCY CLASSIFICATION UPDATE COMPLETE.

NOTICE: NEW CLASSIFICATION: ASSEMBLY, SMALL.

NOTICE: APPLICABLE CODE: ASSEMBLY OCCUPANCY PROVISIONS, CHAPTERS 1 through 14.

NOTICE: COMPLIANCE RATING RECALCULATED.

NOTICE: CURRENT RATING: 3/100.

NOTICE: LICENSE REVIEW SCHEDULED: 41 MINUTES.

Gerald looked at the number.

Three. He had been at fourteen when The Hollow's first complaint landed.

He had been at twelve at registration.

Now he was at three. The reclassification had retroactively invalidated everything. His handrails were wrong for assembly occupancy.

His corridor widths were wrong. His mold lighting was wrong.

His water leak was now a slip hazard under assembly occupancy rules.

His single confused rat was an unlicensed biological entity in a public assembly space.

Forty-one minutes, and he stood up. He walked to the door.

He looked at it. He could remove it. He could un-build it.

If he removed the door, the opening would cease to be a door. If the opening ceased to be a door, it would cease to be an egress route.

If it ceased to be an egress route, the occupancy classification would revert.

But if he removed the door, The Hollow's original complaint would reactivate. The hardware violation would return. He would be cited again. His rating would drop further.

He could not remove the door. He could not keep the door.

He looked at the clock. Forty minutes.

He looked at the wall around the door. The fire-rated assembly. The forty-seven pages he had never read. The material that was undetermined.

He looked at the mold colonies. The penetrations. The unsealed joints.

He looked at his handrails. The bracket fasteners. The missing blocking.

He looked at the floor. The threshold. The junction.

Forty minutes.

Gerald pulled up the code for assembly occupancy. Chapter one. Life safety.

Occupant load calculation, and he had to calculate his occupant load. He had no seats.

He had no tables.

He had no stage. He had no bar. But he had a floor area.

Assembly occupancy was calculated by floor area divided by a factor, and the factor for standing space was seven square feet per person.

His dungeon was four thousand square feet.

Five hundred and seventy-one persons.

His dungeon was now rated for five hundred and seventy-one persons, and he had three mold spores, a water leak, and a rat.

But the code did not care what he had.

The code cared what he could hold. Five hundred and seventy-one persons required a minimum of four exits.

He had one opening. Four exits. Each exit required a fire-rated assembly.

Each exit required hardware. Each exit required signage.

Each exit required emergency lighting, and he could not build four exits in forty minutes. He could not build one exit in forty minutes.

He looked at the door. He looked at the wall. He looked at the clock.

Thirty-eight minutes.

A new message appeared.

NOTICE: INSPECTOR GENERAL PROXIMITY ALERT.

NOTICE: INSPECTOR GENERAL HAS INITIATED ON-SITE AUDIT.

NOTICE: INSPECTOR GENERAL ESTIMATED ARRIVAL: 12 MINUTES.

NOTICE: AUDIT SCOPE: ALL ACTIVE VIOLATIONS.

NOTICE: AUDIT SCOPE: OCCUPANCY CLASSIFICATION VALIDITY.

NOTICE: AUDIT SCOPE: LICENSE RENEWAL DETERMINATION.

Gerald read the message. He read it again.

Twelve minutes. Not forty. The Inspector General was not waiting for the scheduled review.

The Inspector General was coming now, and the Inspector General had seen the rating drop to three and had accelerated.

Gerald looked at the door. He looked at the wall. He looked at the forty-seven pages of fire-rated assembly code he had never read.

He looked at the clock.

Eleven minutes, and he made a decision.

He reached into the door and pulled it apart.

The System recognized the removal. The opening ceased to be a door. The egress route ceased to be an egress route.

The occupancy classification began to revert.

But The Hollow's complaint reactivated the instant the door vanished.

NOTICE: VIOLATION REACTIVATED. CORE HUTCHINS. FAILURE TO MAINTAIN COMPLIANT DOOR HARDWARE ON EGRESS ROUTE.

NOTICE: CUMULATIVE VIOLATIONS: 1.

NOTICE: COMPLIANCE RATING: 7/100.

NOTICE: LICENSE REVIEW IN PROGRESS.

NOTICE: INSPECTOR GENERAL ARRIVING IN 8 MINUTES.

Seven. Better than three. But not enough. The threshold for license renewal was forty.

Gerald stood in his dungeon, and he had no door.

He had no egress route. He had no occupancy classification.

He had a violation and a rating of seven and an Inspector General arriving in eight minutes with a mandate to audit all active violations.

He needed forty points. He needed them in eight minutes.

He looked at the handrails. He looked at the mold. He looked at the water leak.

He started building.
