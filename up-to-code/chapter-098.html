Chapter 98: Six Hours Passed

# Chapter 98

Gerald measured the bow at the midpoint of the corridor wall. Four centimeters. He recorded it on his clipboard in the format he had used for thirty-one years: Location, magnitude, direction of deflection, probable cause, recommended remediation.

The probable cause was obvious. Something heavy was pressing against the other side. The recommended remediation was also obvious. He could not afford it.

He set the clipboard under his arm and walked toward the bow. His dungeon floor was concrete, poured to municipal spec when the library sub-basement had been constructed in 1987. The concrete was not his. The System had registered it as part of his footprint, but it was still municipal concrete, still 1987, still showing the hairline cracking pattern Gerald had flagged on Form 7-C during his last physical inspection of the library, six years ago, when he had still been a man with a hard hat and a pension that would never vest.

The wall bowed another centimeter, and he did not flinch. Flinching was not a code violation.

Gerald pulled out his Core Credit ledger. 112 credits. The structural remediation estimate was 4,200.

The gap was not a gap.

It was a canyon. It was a geological formation. He had been a building inspector long enough to know that some deficiencies were not deficiencies.

They were the building itself, and the building was not going to fix itself, and you either condemned it or you looked the other way and prayed.

Gerald did not look the other way. Gerald wrote reports.

He turned back toward the center of his dungeon, where his spawn list sat on a stone shelf he had built to the correct ADA-compliant height. Three mold spores.

One water leak.

One rat. The rat was grooming itself on the shelf. It looked up at Gerald with the specific expression of a rodent that had never been asked to justify its presence in a public building.

Gerald opened his citation log again. Entry fourteen.

Entry fifteen, and he had been reading them for an hour, looking for reversals, and he had found four.

Four citations he could voluntarily reverse, freeing up four Cores from debuffs they had been suffering. Four Cores who would then be stronger, and who might notice that Gerald was now the weakest thing in the district.

He had stopped at entry fourteen. Not because entry fourteen was the last one he could reverse. Because entry fourteen was a citation against Core Kessick.

The same Kessick. The one whose dungeon was currently pressing through his east wall.

Gerald closed the log. He picked up his clipboard. He walked back to the east corridor.

The wall had another centimeter of bow. The concrete was not cracking. The System was holding it together, because the System recognized the wall as a load-bearing element of Gerald's dungeon footprint, and load-bearing elements could not fail without triggering a Compliance Audit, and Gerald did not need another Compliance Audit. He had enough.

He pressed his palm against the wall. He felt the pressure on the other side. Slow.

Rhythmic. Like a piston.

Then he felt something else. Heat. Faint, but present. The wall was warming.

Gerald stepped back, and he looked at the floor. Near the base of the wall, where the concrete met the foundation, a thin line of light was appearing. Not fire.

Not yet.

A seam. The wall was being cut.

He pulled out his clipboard.

He wrote: "Active construction detected at east boundary. Unpermitted. Uninspected.

No fire separation observed."

The notification came.

CONSTRUCTION ZONE DETECTED. Jurisdiction: Core Hutchins. Status: Unpermitted. Municipal oversight authority: ACTIVE.

Gerald stared at the notification. He read it again. He read it a third time.

Municipal oversight authority. Active.

The annexation was construction. Construction was subject to municipal oversight. And Gerald was the municipal authority within his own dungeon.

He walked to the seam in the wall, and he pressed his face close to it.

He could see, on the other side, a corridor. Narrow.

Rough-cut. The walls were dripping. No handrails.

No egress signage. The floor sloped at roughly four degrees toward the boundary, which meant water would pool at Gerald's wall.

He wrote faster.

"Corridor width: approximately 1.2 meters.

Minimum required width for egress in occupied structure: 0.9 meters. Compliant. However, corridor lacks fire-rated separation from adjacent structure.

No smoke barrier.

No sprinkler coverage. No emergency lighting.

Ventilation: none.

Slope: non-compliant for drainage. Electrical: none. No GFCI protection in wet environment."

He paused. He looked at the corridor again.

"Occupant load: unknown. No signage indicating maximum occupancy."

He wrote one more line.

"Recommendation: STOP WORK ORDER."

The notification came.

STOP WORK ORDER DRAFTED. Conditional Citation Type: Active Construction Halt. Requires physical service on-site. Service distance: within 3 meters of construction activity.

Gerald looked at the seam. He looked at the wall. He looked at the 112 credits in his ledger.

He walked through the wall.

Not through it physically. The System allowed it.

A Code Compliance Core could pass through any boundary within its jurisdiction for the purpose of active inspection.

Gerald felt the transition like stepping through a curtain of cold water.

His dungeon's air was stale, recycled, barely adequate. The other side was worse.

The corridor smelled of wet stone and something chemical, like solvent.

He walked forward. Three meters.

The corridor narrowed, and he could hear Kessick's construction crew working. Not goblins. Something larger.

The sound of stone being cut with tools that were not hand tools.

Mechanical. Grinding.

He reached the crew.

Four entities. Stone golems, roughly humanoid, each the size of a refrigerator. They were cutting the wall from the other side.

Their tools were spinning discs that threw sparks.

Gerald held up his clipboard.

"Stop work."

The golems did not stop. They did not have eyes. They did not have ears, and were constructs, and constructs followed their Core's instructions, and Kessick's instruction was to build.

Gerald said it again. "Stop work. This is a municipal order."

The golems continued.

Gerald read the notification. "Physical service." He stepped forward. He held the clipboard against the nearest golem's chassis. The System registered the contact.

STOP WORK ORDER SERVED. Target: Core Kessick. Violations cited: 6. Construction halt enforced for 24 hours.

The golems stopped. The grinding discs slowed, shuddered, and went still. The silence that followed was absolute.

Then the wall behind Gerald shook. Not a bow this time. A vibration. A voice, coming through the stone.

"You're in my corridor."

Gerald did not turn around. He was still facing the golems. He was still writing.

"Your corridor is in my jurisdiction. It is unpermitted. I have cited six violations. You have twenty-four hours to correct them or the annexation is void."

A pause. The vibration came again.

"You reversed my citation."

Gerald looked up. "I was considering it. I stopped at entry fourteen."

"Why?"

"Because you were about to annex me. I don't reverse citations against active threats. It's in the manual."

"There is no manual."

"There is always a manual."

The wall vibrated again.

Gerald could feel Kessick's presence on the other side.

Not a body. A pressure.

A will. The kind of will that came from a Core that had been building for a long time and had never once been told to stop.

"Twenty-four hours," Kessick said. "And then?"

"And then you correct the violations. Or you don't annex me."

"And if I correct them?"

Gerald looked at his clipboard. He looked at the corridor. He looked at the golems, frozen mid-swing.

"Then I inspect again. And I find more."

The vibration stopped. The corridor was silent.

Gerald turned and walked back through the wall.

His dungeon reassembled around him. The seam sealed. The bow remained, but it was no longer growing.

He sat down on his stone shelf. The rat moved away from him. The mold spores

drifted through their usual circulation pattern, which Gerald had not designed but had observed and found adequate.

He pulled out his clipboard. He had a form for this.

Form 7-C: Post-Encounter Compliance Reassessment, and he had never used it. He had read it four times.

He started filling it out.

Item one: Did the encounter result in structural damage to the Core's dungeon footprint?

No. He had not been damaged. He had walked through a wall that was not his wall.

He paused.

Item two: Did the Core cross into unpermitted territory during the encounter?

He looked at the clipboard. He looked at the wall.

The seam was sealed, which was his wall again.

But for approximately four minutes, he had been inside a corridor that was not his corridor. A corridor that did not have his name on any permit.

A corridor that he had entered without filing a Temporary Access Authorization.

He had not filed a Temporary Access Authorization.

His Compliance Rating ticked down.

Twelve to eleven.

Gerald set the clipboard on his knee. He stared at the number. The rat stared at him. The mold spores continued their circulation, indifferent to municipal oversight.

"Of course," he said.

He had been so thorough with Kessick's violations that he had failed to document his own. He had crossed a property line without authorization. He had entered an unpermitted structure. He had conducted an inspection without proper jurisdictional clearance.

He was, by the letter of the System's own code, in violation.

He started drafting a self-citation. He had never done this. He did not know if it was possible. He wrote the header anyway.

*NOTICE OF VIOLATION. Issuing Authority: Core Hutchins.

Subject: Core Hutchins.

Violation: Unauthorized entry into adjacent dungeon corridor without Temporary Access Authorization. Severity: Minor.*

He signed it. He filed it. He felt a small, warm satisfaction.

Then the System spoke.

Not a notification.

Not a form. A voice.

The same voice that had delivered his Compliance Audit at the beginning.

The voice of the Inspector General.

"CORE HUTCHINS. YOU HAVE BEEN SELECTED FOR A SURPRISE AUDIT. PRESENT YOUR ENTIRE COMPLIANCE FILE WITHIN SIX HOURS. FAILURE TO COMPLY WILL RESULT IN IMMEDIATE REVOCATION OF CORE STATUS."

Gerald looked at his clipboard. He looked at his dungeon.

Four thousand square feet.

Three mold spores.

One rat. A water leak.

Handrails he had installed with his own hands, and a fire extinguisher he had placed by the entrance because the code required it.

Six hours, and he had never once failed an inspection.

He stood up. He picked up his measuring tape. He started walking.
