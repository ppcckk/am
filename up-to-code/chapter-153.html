Chapter 153: Suit in the Dark

# Chapter 153

Maren Vane adjusted the focus on her scrying lens, squinting at the grey haze hovering over Ashford Municipal Library.

The lens was a cracked relic from the Second Age, expensive enough to buy a mid-sized house in the suburbs, but it still struggled with the ambient magical noise of a city that had been paved over twice. Most scryers ignored municipal districts.

They were boring.

They were stable. They were full of taxes.

Maren wasn't looking for adventure. She was looking for profit.

Her contract with the Guild of Arcane Acquisitions required a new source of refined mana crystals. The old mines in the northern sector had collapsed, leaving only dust and regret. The underground networks beneath the city were uncharted, unclaimed, and theoretically infinite.

She tapped the side of her lens. The image sharpened.

A sub-basement. Four thousand square feet of concrete and despair.

"Show me the Core," she whispered.

The lens rippled.

A shape coalesced in the center of the room. It wasn't a glowing orb.

It wasn't a pulsating heart of darkness.

It was a clipboard.

Maren blinked. She tapped the lens again. Harder.

The clipboard remained. Floating in the center of a perfectly painted hallway.

"Glitch," she muttered.

She adjusted the frequency dial, moving from Ethereal to Structural. The lens whined, a high-pitched sound that made her teeth ache.

The image shifted.

The walls became transparent.

The floor became translucent.

Inside the structure, she saw them.

Three mold spores. A water leak. A rat.

And one figure.

He stood in the center of the room, holding a measuring tape. He was wearing a high-visibility vest over a dress shirt. His tie was knotted tightly at the collar. His hair was combed with geometric precision.

Maren zoomed in. The figure's face was pale, his eyes wide and unblinking. He looked like he was having a nervous breakdown, but his hands were steady. He was measuring the distance between two electrical outlets.

Twenty-two inches.

He nodded. He wrote something on the clipboard. Then he looked up.

Maren felt a chill crawl up her spine. He couldn't see her. No one could see into the Scrying Layer unless they were a Core of Tier 3 or higher. This man was barely Tier 1.

But he was looking right at her.

His pen hovered over the paper. He didn't write. He just stared.

Maren pulled her hand away from the lens as if it had burned her. She checked the readings again. The Compliance Rating was low, but the Stability Index was off the charts.

The mana flow wasn't erratic. It was regulated.

Piped.

Inspected.

This wasn't a dungeon. It was a bureaucracy.

If she sent a raiding party in, they wouldn't find monsters. They'd find fines.

She closed the lens. Her hands shook.

"Boss," she said, turning to the shadows behind her. "We have a problem."

***

Gerald Hutchins did not have ten minutes.

He had nine minutes and forty-three seconds.

The red eye in the ceiling blinked again. A slow, rhythmic pulse that synced with the thumping in Gerald's chest.

**[DEFENSE WINDOW OPEN. FILE OR PERISH.]**

The air in the sub-basement tasted like ozone and burnt toast.

The lava had solidified into a black, glass-like obsidian that stretched from wall to wall. It was ugly.

It was non-compliant.

It blocked the egress route.

Gerald looked at the obsidian floor. Then he looked at the clipboard in his hand.

His hands were shaking. Not from fear. From rage.

He had followed the rules. The Pyre had violated the Thermal Expansion Code.

He had cited it.

The System had enforced the citation.

That was the contract. That was the law.

But the Inspector General didn't care about the law. The IG cared about liability.

"Filing a defense," Gerald said aloud. His voice sounded thin, swallowed by the damp concrete. "Where is the filing cabinet?"

No answer.

He looked around. The walls were bare. The ceiling was red. The floor was sharp.

He needed a precedent. He needed a loophole. He needed a witness.

The rat was gone.

Gerald closed his eyes. He breathed in through his nose, held for four, out for four. In through his nose.

Hold. Out.

*What would a municipal lawyer do?*

He opened his eyes. He looked at the black glass.

The violation was "Reckless Endangerment."

To prove innocence, he had to prove that his action was not reckless. He had to prove it was necessary.

The Pyre had been boiling. The heat was rising. If he hadn't cited it, the heat would have melted the library's foundation.

But the System didn't care about the library. It cared about the Core.

Gerald walked to the center of the room. He stepped onto the black glass. It was cold. Freezing.

He knelt. He pulled a pen from his shirt pocket. He opened the back of his clipboard, revealing a hidden compartment he had installed in Chapter 42. Inside was a single sheet of vellum paper, stamped with the Ashford City Seal.

It was a form he had never used.

**Form 99-C: Emergency Structural Alteration Request.**

He hadn't used it because it required a signature from the property owner. He was the property owner. He was also the tenant. He was also the inspector.

It was a conflict of interest. The System usually rejected it.

But Gerald had a new variable.

He looked up at the red eye.

"I am filing a counter-claim," he said.

The eye pulsed.

**[STATE YOUR CLAIM.]**

"Claim: Structural Necessity."

Gerald's pen moved. He wrote fast, his handwriting cramped and slanted.

*The subject (Pyre Core) exhibited critical thermal instability exceeding safe operational limits of 400 degrees Fahrenheit. Standard suppression methods (water, ice) were unavailable due to environmental constraints. Immediate cessation of thermal activity was required to prevent catastrophic structural failure of the host facility (Ashford Municipal Library).*

*The citation acted as a forced cooling agent via System-enforced Thermal Shock. The resulting obsidian formation is, per Section 14, Subsection B of the Ashford Code, classified as a 'Permanent Load-Bearing Barrier' until removed by licensed demolition crews.*

*the action was not reckless. It was a mandated maintenance procedure.*

He signed it.

**Gerald Hutchins. Inspector #402.**

He stood up. He held the paper out to the air.

**[PROCESSING...]**

**[ANALYZING STRUCTURAL LOGIC...]**

**[ERROR: CONFLICT OF INTEREST DETECTED.]**

Gerald's stomach dropped.

He had forgotten the conflict clause. The System didn't like self-approval.

**[LIQUIDATION IMMINENT. T-5 MINUTES.]**

Panic flared. Bright and hot.

Gerald looked at the walls. He looked at the ceiling. He looked at the black floor.

Then he looked at the corner of the room.

A water leak dripped from the ceiling. A steady, rhythmic *plip, plip, plip*.

He walked over to it. He placed his hand under the drip.

Water pooled in his palm.

He looked at the red eye.

"I invoke Rule 114," Gerald said.

The eye paused.

**[RULE 114 NOT FOUND IN LOCAL CODE.]**

"It's in the General Principles," Gerald said. His voice rose. "The Right of Self-Preservation for Public Infrastructure."

He pointed at the water.

"This water is municipal runoff.

It is city property. If I liquidate, the library loses its foundation. The city loses the library.

The city is my employer.

The System is an employer. Employers protect their assets."

He dropped the wet paper onto the black glass.

"I am not acting as a Core. I am acting as a Municipal Employee protecting City Assets. The conflict of interest clause applies to private entities. I am public."

Silence.

The red eye stared.

The dripping water continued. *Plip. Plip. Plip.*

**[SEARCHING...]**

**[MATCH FOUND: PUBLIC SECTOR EXEMPTION, CLAUSE 7.]**

**[EXEMPTION APPLIES TO EMPLOYEES OF MORTAL MUNICIPALITIES.]**

**[LIQUIDATION STALLED.]**

Gerald exhaled. His knees buckled. He caught himself on the wall, his fingers digging into the concrete.

He had bought time. Not freedom.

The eye blinked.

**[APPEAL ACCEPTED FOR REVIEW.]**

**[CITATION SUSPENDED PENDING AUDIT.]**

**[WARNING: INSPECTOR GENERAL DEPUTY DISPATCHED. ETA: 3 MINUTES.]**

Gerald straightened his tie. He smoothed his vest.

He didn't want an audit. He wanted a quiet office and a cup of coffee.

But the Deputy was coming.

Deputies were worse than the IG. The IG was a concept. The Deputy was a bureaucrat who enjoyed paperwork.

Gerald looked at the black glass floor. It was a violation now. It blocked egress. He had just cited himself for creating it.

He needed to move it.

He couldn't. It was fused to the foundation.

He needed a helper.

The rat emerged from a crack in the wall. It carried a piece of bread. It looked at Gerald. It squeaked.

Gerald looked at the rat.

"You're an unlicensed entity," Gerald said.

The rat dropped the bread. It tilted its head.

"I can't hire you," Gerald said. "I can't even acknowledge you."

The rat picked up the bread. It walked to the black glass. It dropped the bread on the obsidian.

It sat down. It ate.

Gerald watched it.

The System didn't register the rat as a threat. The System didn't register the rat as a citizen.

But the rat was there.

Gerald looked at the bread crumbs on the black glass.

If the rat was an entity, and it was on the glass, and the glass was a barrier...

Gerald pulled out his pen.

He wrote on the air.

**[SUB-CITATION: OBSTRUCTION OF PUBLIC ACCESS BY UNLICENSED BIOLOGICAL ENTITY.]**

The text hung in the air. It was invisible to everyone but Gerald.

The System didn't care about rats.

But the Deputy would.

If the Deputy arrived and saw a violation on the floor, the Deputy would have to clean it up before reviewing Gerald's case.

It was petty. It was spiteful.

It was exactly what a building inspector did.

Gerald smiled. It was a tight, thin smile that didn't reach his eyes.

The red eye blinked.

**[VIOLATION DETECTED: OBSTRUCTION.]**

**[ENFORCEMENT: REMOVE OBSTRUCTION.]**

The black glass cracked.

Not from heat. From the System trying to delete the obstruction.

The crack spread.

The floor groaned.

Gerald stepped back.

The glass shattered.

Chunks of obsidian flew across the room.

One piece struck the wall, leaving a black scar. Another piece landed near the rat. The rat didn't move.

It just watched.

The hole in the floor revealed the raw earth beneath.

And something else.

A pipe.

Copper. Old. Tapping against the dirt.

*Clang. Clang. Clang.*

Gerald froze.

He knew that sound.

He had heard it thirty-one years ago. In the basement of the old Town Hall. Before they paved it over.

The main water line. The one that fed the entire district.

The System hadn't just broken the floor. It had exposed the city's veins.

If the Deputy saw this, it wouldn't just be a dungeon violation.

It would be a municipal infrastructure emergency.

Gerald pulled out his phone. He dialed 911.

He didn't speak. He just held the phone up to the hole in the floor.

He let the sound of the water pipe hit the receiver.

He waited.

On the other end, a dispatcher answered.

"911, what is your emergency?"

Gerald didn't answer.

He looked at the red eye.

The eye widened.

**[COMMUNICATION ATTEMPT DETECTED.]**

**[SYSTEM INTERFERENCE PROTOCOL INITIATED.]**

The red eye turned purple.

**[WARNING: REAL-WORLD CONTACT PROHIBITED.]**

Gerald dropped the phone.

The phone fell into the hole.

It landed on the copper pipe.

*Clang.*

The phone lit up.

A notification appeared on the screen.

**Incoming Call from: Mayor's Office.**

Gerald stared at it.

The System couldn't stop a phone call from the Mayor. The System couldn't stop the city from knowing about its own infrastructure.

If the city knew, the System couldn't liquidate him quietly.

He would have to go through the courts.

And Gerald Hutchins had never lost a case in the courts.

He picked up the phone.

He answered.

"Hello, Mr. Hutchins?" The Mayor's voice was smooth, practiced. "We have a report of structural instability at the Library. Are you aware of the situation?"

Gerald looked at the red eye. The eye was shrinking. Dimming.

The System was retreating.

"Mr.

Mayor," Gerald said.

"I am currently conducting a mandatory inspection. Do not send anyone down here.

Do not send the fire department. Do not send the police."

"Why?"

"Because I have found a violation," Gerald said. "And I am going to fine it."

He hung up.

The red eye vanished.

The ceiling returned to white concrete.

Silence.

Gerald stood in the center of the ruined basement. The water pipe hummed beneath his feet.

He looked at the rat.

The rat was gone.

In its place was a small, black object.

A badge.

It was metallic. Shaped like a shield. Engraved with a single word: **AUDITOR.**

Gerald picked it up.

It was warm.

**[NEW MECHANIC UNLOCKED: SUB-INSPECTOR PRIVILEGES.]**

**[DESCRIPTION: YOU MAY NOW DELEGATE CITATIONS TO UNLICENSED ENTITIES. COST: ONE LIFE PER USE.]**

Gerald looked at the badge. Then he looked at the hole in the floor.

The water pipe was leaking.

The water was rising.

And somewhere in the darkness below, something was moving toward the hole.

It wasn't a rat.

It wasn't a goblin.

It was wearing a suit.

Gerald tightened his grip on the badge.

He had a new tool.

He had a new problem.

And the water was still rising.
