Chapter 351: Revocation of Core Status

# Chapter 351

The iron gate shrieked. It was a rusted, agonizing sound that cut through the rain, echoing off the brickwork of the abandoned textile mill in the industrial district. Gerald Hutchins did not flinch. He adjusted his grip on the Citation pad, ensuring the rubber band held the sheets tight against the damp, and stepped forward into the shadowed archway.

The air inside smelled of sulfur and rotting meat. It was the signature scent of a necromantic dungeon, the kind that usually operated with zero regard for OSHA standards.

Gerald pulled his clipboard closer to his chest, shielding it from the drizzle that had begun to seep through the roof gaps.

He looked at his watch. 11:14 PM. He had ten hours and forty-six minutes before the Inspector General rebooted.

"State your business, Inspector," a voice boomed from the darkness.

It was not a shout, but a resonance.

The words vibrated in Gerald's molars. From the shadows emerged a construct.

It was a amalgamation of bone and iron, standing seven feet tall, with hollow eye sockets that glowed with a sickly green light.

A Bone Golem,, but modified with heavy plate armor that looked like it had been scavenged from a modern tank.

Gerald clicked his pen. The sound was sharp, precise, and mundane.

"Gerald Hutchins," he said. His voice was flat, stripped of any fear or adrenaline.

He spoke the way he spoke when denying a permit for a non-compliant deck in a historic district.

"Municipal Building Inspector, City of Ashford.

I am conducting a spot-check for code violations.

"

The golem tilted its head. The joints in its neck ground together.

"Violations?

This is the Domain of Malakor. We do not care for your municipal laws.

"

"Code section 4.2, subsection B," Gerald recited, not looking up from his pad.

He was already measuring the distance between the entrance arch and the first obstruction.

"Obstruction of egress pathways. A dungeon must maintain a minimum clearance of thirty-six inches at all choke points to allow for emergency evacuation.

You have a pile of ribcages blocking the corridor.

That's a twenty-inch gap.

"

The golem took a step forward. The floor trembled.

"We do not evacuate. We conquer."

"Then you are citing a fire hazard," Gerald said.

He wrote something down.

"Fire Code 7.1. Combustible materials stored within five feet of a heat source.

Your torches are open flame. Your walls are wood.

Your floors are straw, which is not a dungeon.

It is a kiln waiting to happen.

"

Malakor laughed. It was a wet, gurgling sound that came from everywhere and nowhere. The green lights in the golem's eyes flared brighter.

"You think you can stop me with paperwork, little man? I have raised an army of the dead.

I have crushed heroes.

I will crush you.

"

"You are attempting to intimidate a public servant," Gerald noted, writing a second line.

"Assault on an official is a felony in three jurisdictions. I am currently in Ashford city limits.

You are violating state law, not just System protocol.

That means I can serve a Citation that persists even if I die.

"

Malakor paused. The golem stopped moving.

"That is... unlikely," the Core said. Its voice dropped an octave, becoming curious. "The System grants immunity to Cores during active conflict."

"Conflict is defined by the System as an exchange of hostile actions," Gerald said. He stepped closer.

The heat radiating from the golem was intense, singeing the hair on his arms, but he did not step back.

He held up his badge.

"I have not attacked you. I have not cast a spell.

I have not dealt damage. I am measuring. Measuring is not an attack.

Therefore, we are not in conflict. We are in an inspection.

"

Silence stretched between them. The rain drummed against the roof. The golem stood frozen, its logic processors likely churning through the semantic definitions of

"hostile action."

Malakor spoke again, softer this time.

"You are playing games, Hutchins."

"I am applying the rules," Gerald corrected. "You have a Class Action defense prepared. You expect me to come here and fight, to use my Citations aggressively so that you can appeal based on 'unfair targeting.' But I am not fighting. I am documenting."

The golem lowered its weapon. A jagged scythe of black iron.

"What do you want?"

"Access to the central chamber," Gerald said.

"I need to check the load-bearing capacity of the foundation. Your dungeon is sinking. I can see the stress fractures in the outer walls from the street.

If you are building a necropolis, you need proper drainage.

You have a water table issue. I can hear it dripping.

"

Malakor was silent for a long time. The green lights dimmed.

"You are insane," the Core said.

"I am thorough," Gerald replied.

"Enter," Malakor said.

"But if you attempt to seal my dungeon, I will summon every spirit in this city to haunt your office. I will make your life a living hell. You will never sleep again.

You will never eat again.

"

"Gerald ignored the threat. He stepped past the golem. The creature did not move. It watched him with a mixture of contempt and confusion.

Gerald walked down the corridor, and the floor was uneven, littered with debris.

He noted every trip hazard. He noted the lack of handrails on the staircase.

He noted the absence of fire extinguishers. He wrote it all down. The pen moved across the paper with rhythmic precision. *Click.

Scratch. Click.

Scratch.*

At the end of the hall, a heavy door of obsidian stood ajar. Gerald pushed it open.

The central chamber was vast. It was a cathedral of bones, lit by floating orbs of necrotic energy. In the center sat a throne of skulls. And on that throne sat Malakor.

He was not a monster. He was a humanoid figure made of translucent violet energy, wearing the tattered remnants of a royal robe. His face was sharp, aristocratic, and currently twisted in a sneer.

"You walked right in," Malakor said. "Like you owned the place."

"I am the inspector," Gerald said. He closed the door behind him. The latch clicked. It was a satisfying sound.

"Inspector," Malakor spat the word. "Do you know who I was? I was a Duke.

A Duke of Ashford, three hundred years ago.

I built this city. I own this land.

The System gave me this body because I refused to die.

Because I refused to let my legacy fade.

"

Gerald walked to the center of the room. He ignored the throne.

He walked to a pillar, and he placed his hand on it.

He tapped it with his knuckles. It sounded hollow.

"This pillar is decorative," Gerald said. "Not structural. You have no support beams.

The ceiling is unsupported for forty feet. This is a critical structural failure.

Section 3.1: Roof Integrity.

"

Malakor leaned forward on his throne.

"You are boring me. I could kill you with a thought. I could raise every corpse in this room and have them tear you apart."

"You could," Gerald agreed. He pulled a small device from his pocket. A laser measure.

He pointed it at the floor.

The red dot hit the black stone.

"But you haven't.

Why?"

Malakor hesitated.

"Because you are... strange. You do not fear death. You fear disorder."

"Precisely," Gerald said. He wrote in his pad. "Violation: Lack of emergency exit signage.

Section 5.4. Visibility in low-light conditions is inadequate.

Your necrotic orbs provide illumination, but they do not indicate egress direction.

This is a life-safety violation.

"

"I am not interested in life safety," Malakor said. "I am interested in power."

"Power requires stability," Gerald said. He looked up.

"You are losing Core integrity. I can see it. The air here is stagnant.

Poor ventilation. Section 6.2.

Air Quality Index is below acceptable limits.

This reduces the regen rate of your spawned entities. You are weakening your own army because you refuse to install HVAC systems.

"

Malakor's eyes narrowed.

"How do you know that?"

"I measured the air currents at the entrance," Gerald said.

"And I calculated the CO2 buildup based on the number of undead present. You have a ventilation deficiency.

It is costing you hit points per second.

Passive debuff: Reduced Regeneration. 15% penalty.

"

The Core stared at him. The violet energy of his body flickered.

"Show me," Malakor demanded.

Gerald tore the top sheet from his pad. He held it out.

"This is a Preliminary Citation.

It is not final, which is a warning. It notes that if I file a Formal Citation, the penalty will be 50% reduction in all magical regeneration.

I can serve it right now. I am physically present. The range is within ten feet.

"

Malakor looked at the paper. He did not take it.

"If you cite me," Malakor said, "I will appeal. I will drag you through the Hearing. I will prove that your definitions are subjective. That the System's interpretation of 'ventilation' does not apply to ethereal constructs."

"You will lose," Gerald said. "The System's definition of ventilation is objective.

It is a matter of cubic feet per minute of air exchange.

You have zero. I have the measurements. I have the photos.

I have the timestamp.

"

Malakor stood up. He was taller than Gerald by a head, but he looked smaller. He looked tired.

"What do you want?" the Core asked.

"I want you to join the Class Action," Gerald said.

Malakor froze.

"The Coalition?

Why would I join those fools?

They are weak. They are desperate.

"

"They are organized," Gerald said. "You are alone.

You have been hiding in this mill for ten years, and the IG is awake.

Or it will be soon. When it wakes, it will audit every Core in Ashford.

It will find your ventilation violations. It will find your structural failures. And it will strip you of your power for incompetence.

"

Malakor slumped back onto his throne.

"The IG does not audit for building codes."

"The IG audits for System Compliance," Gerald said. "And the System has a building code.

You know this.

You have read the Terms of Service. You ignored them because you thought you were above them. You are not above them.

You are subject to them.

Just like I am.

"

Gerald took a step closer.

"I can help you. I can help you fix your dungeon. I can help you become compliant.

And if you are compliant, you cannot be cited. You cannot be debuffed.

You become immune to administrative attack.

"

Malakor looked at him. The sneer was gone. Now there was only calculation.

"And what is the cost?" Malakor asked.

"I need access to your logs," Gerald said.

"Every spawn request. Every upgrade.

Every spell cast in the last decade. I need to know who else is out there. Who else has been filing complaints.

Who else is violating the code. I need to build a case against the Aggregate.

And you are the first witness.

"

"Me? A witness?" Malakor laughed, a bitter, broken sound. "I am a King."

"You are a defendant," Gerald said. "And you are currently in violation. Sign this waiver, and I will not serve the citation.

You will have twelve hours to install proper ventilation fans and reinforce your ceiling.

If you do not, I will cite you for willful negligence.

"

Malakor looked at the waiver. He looked at Gerald. He looked at the rain leaking through the roof, pooling on the floor near the throne.

"You are a monster," Malakor whispered.

"I am an inspector," Gerald said.

He waited. The clock on the wall—broken, but ticking with magical resonance—showed 11:45 PM.

Malakor reached out. His hand, translucent and cold, touched the paper. The ink shimmered. The signature appeared in glowing violet script.

*Malakor.*

"Done," Malakor said. "Now get out of my dungeon before I change my mind."

Gerald nodded. He tucked the signed waiver into his coat. He turned to leave.

"Wait," Malakor said.

Gerald stopped. He did not turn around.

"The first name on your list," Malakor said. "The one who started the Class Action. You know him?"

Gerald turned back.

"I have the name. I do not know him personally."

"He is not a Core," Malakor said. "He is a human."

Gerald felt a chill that had nothing to do with the rain.

"Explain."

"He is the one who wrote the Building Code," Malakor said.

"He is the one who created the System rules. He is the Architect. He is still alive.

And he is in Ashford. He is in the library.

"

Gerald's hand tightened on his pen. The library. The place where he had become a Core. The place where he had filed his first report.

"Why did you not tell me?" Gerald asked.

"Because I was busy trying to survive you," Malakor said.

He sat back down.

"Go, Inspector. Go to the library.

Ask for the Architect. Tell him you are here to inspect his work.

"

Gerald walked out. He did not run. He walked with the steady, plodding gait of a man who had found the source of the leak.

Outside, the rain was heavier. The streetlights flickered. Gerald looked at his watch. 11:52 PM.

Eight hours left, and he opened his car door. He got in. He started the engine.

He drove toward the library.

The streets were empty. But as he turned the corner onto Main Street, the streetlights went out. One by one. Like dominoes.

Darkness swallowed the city.

Gerald's headlights cut through the black. And in the center of the road, standing in the middle of an intersection with no traffic, stood a figure.

He was wearing a suit. A perfect, tailored suit. He held a blueprint in his hand.

Gerald slammed on the brakes. The car skidded, tires screaming against the wet asphalt. He stopped three feet from the man.

The man looked up. His face was familiar. It was the face of the man who had signed the initial Core Registration form. The man who had approved Gerald's first permit.

The Architect smiled.

"Good evening, Inspector," the man said. "I was expecting you."

Gerald gripped the steering wheel. His knuckles turned white.

He did not speak, and he could not speak. The System in his mind was screaming a warning.

*Compliance Rating: Critical.*

*Warning: Unauthorized Entity Detected.*

The Architect tapped the blueprint against his leg.

"You cited Malakor. You signed the waiver. You think you have leverage."

The man stepped closer to the car. The glass of the window fogged up with his breath.

"But you forgot one thing, Gerald," the Architect whispered. "You cannot cite the author."

The door of the car flew open. Not because Gerald opened it. Because the System forced it.

Gerald fell out. He hit the wet pavement. He tried to stand, but his legs would not move. The System had paralyzed him.

The Architect looked down at him, and he pulled a red pen from his pocket. It was not a blue pen.

It was red.

The color of a rejection.

"Your inspection is over," the Architect said. "And you have failed."

He raised the red pen.

Gerald stared up at him. He tried to reach for his Citation pad, but his hand was locked at his side. The pen was gone.

The Architect brought the pen down. Not on Gerald. On the air above him.

The sky tore open.

A new window appeared in Gerald's vision. It was not a System notification. It was a court summons.

*Defendant: Gerald Hutchins.* *Charge: Obstruction of Justice.* *Penalty: Immediate Revocation of Core Status.*

The red pen touched the sky.

And Gerald Hutchins dissolved.
