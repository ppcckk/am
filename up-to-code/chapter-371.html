Chapter 371: Citation of Treason

# Chapter 371

Gerald's pen scratched against the clipboard with the rhythmic precision of a metronome.

"Form 11-B, Subsection C, Paragraph Four," he said, not looking up. "Unauthorized use of public thoroughfare for the purpose of magical transit."

The figure in the white suit did not move. It stood in the center of the ruined atrium, its head tilted at an angle that defied spinal anatomy. A clipboard of its own hung from one hand, dripping with black ink.

"I am an Auditor," the figure said. Its voice sounded like paper tearing.

"You are in violation of Municipal Code 44.1," Gerald replied. He turned a page. "No entity may occupy a public building without a temporary occupancy permit. You have none."

"The System grants automatic occupancy to Auditors."

"Does it?" Gerald raised an eyebrow. "Show me the filing."

Auditor 7 stepped forward. The marble floor cracked under its weight.

"You are wasting time, Core 404. The hearing begins in forty-seven hours. You will be demolished."

Gerald looked up. His face was a mask of mild boredom.

"Demolition requires a signed warrant from a licensed inspector. I am the only licensed inspector within a ten-mile radius. You have not filed a request."

Auditor 7 paused. The black ink on its clipboard stopped dripping.

"You are a Core. You are not an inspector."

"I am a Core who is an inspector," Gerald said. "My registration is dual-classed. Check your database. It's in the fine print of the initial Core Registration protocol, Section 9, Clause 12."

Auditor 7 looked down at its clipboard. It flipped pages. The sound was dry and brittle.

"There is no such clause."

"There is," Gerald said. "Page 304. I read it when I registered. I have a copy."

He reached into his vest pocket. He pulled out a crumpled piece of paper. It was a photocopy of a System notification. It was stained with coffee.

Auditor 7 leaned forward. Its eyes were hollow pits.

"Prove it."

Gerald stepped closer. He held up the paper.

"You can't read it from there. Come here."

Auditor 7 hesitated. Then, slowly, it walked toward Gerald.

The other Auditors stood at the perimeter. They were watching. They were waiting.

Auditor 7 stopped two feet away. It reached for the paper.

Gerald slapped its hand away.

"Hands off. This is evidence."

Auditor 7 recoiled. Its fingers twitched.

"You are obstructionist."

"I am procedural," Gerald corrected. "You are impeding an investigation.

I will cite you for obstruction of a public official, which is a Class A felony in Ashford. And, according to System Bylaw 7, a Class A felony voids the Auditor's immunity.

"

Auditor 7's head snapped up. The hollow eyes flared with white light.

"You are bluffing."

"Am I?" Gerald tapped his clipboard.

"I have already filed the citation. It is queued for processing.

If you do not stand down, it will be served.

Your rating will drop by thirty points. Your spawn rate will halve.

"

The silence in the atrium was heavy. Dust motes danced in the shafts of light that pierced the broken ceiling.

Auditor 7 looked at the other Auditors. They looked back.

One of them, a smaller figure with a clipboard made of bone, stepped forward.

"He is correct," the small Auditor said. Its voice was higher.

Squeakier.

"Section 9, Clause 12.

Dual-class registration is valid if the candidate passed the Municipal Inspector Exam prior to Core birth.

"

Auditor 7 turned to the small Auditor.

"Verify."

"I have," the small Auditor said. "Gerald Hutchins passed the exam in 1993. He is a licensed inspector.

His Core status is secondary. His Inspector status is primary.

"

Auditor 7's jaw unhinged. It made a sound like a door slamming.

This is unacceptable.

"

"It is the law," Gerald said. "The System does not lie about laws. It only lies about facts."

Auditor 7 raised its hand. Black energy coalesced in its palm.

Then, it stopped.

The air pressure in the room dropped. Gerald's ears popped.

A new figure entered the atrium, which did not walk. It slid across the floor, hovering an inch above the ground. It was tall.

Its suit was not white.

It was grey.

Its face was featureless. A smooth oval of skin.

Auditor 7 bowed. The other Auditors bowed.

Gerald did not bow. He checked his watch.

"Inspector General," Gerald said.

The grey figure stopped. It looked at Gerald.

"Core 404," the Inspector General said. The voice was not loud. It was everywhere.

"Inspector General," Gerald repeated. "You are late. The hearing was scheduled for immediate commencement."

"The hearing is suspended," the Inspector General said.

Gerald frowned. He tapped his pen against the clipboard.

"Suspended? On what grounds?"

"On the grounds of jurisdictional conflict."

The Inspector General floated closer. It stopped three feet from Gerald.

"You are claiming to be an inspector. The System recognizes you as a Core. These are mutually exclusive states for the purpose of enforcement."

"They are not," Gerald said.

"They are overlapping. I am a Core. I am also an inspector.

I enforce the rules. You enforce the rules.

We have a conflict of interest.

"

The Inspector General tilted its head.

"Explain."

Gerald took a breath. He adjusted his glasses.

"The System grants Cores the power to issue Citations.

This is a regulatory power. It is an enforcement power.

Only licensed officials hold enforcement power. by granting me Citations, the System has implicitly granted me Inspector status. I am not a Core pretending to be an inspector. I am an inspector who became a Core because the System forced me to.

"

Auditor 7 stepped forward.

"This is sophistry. The Core power is derived from Dungeon Authority, not Municipal Authority."

"No," Gerald said. "It is derived from Compliance.

Compliance is a municipal concept.

The System adopted it from us. We defined it. You just copied it.

"

The Inspector General remained silent.

Gerald pressed on.

"Look at the rules.

Rule 3: 'A Code Compliance Core can issue Citations to any dungeon within range.' It does not say 'any Core.' It says 'any dungeon.' I am a dungeon. I am also a building.

And I am an inspector of that building.

I have the right to inspect my own building. And if I find violations in other buildings that affect mine, I have the right to cite them.

"

"That is a stretch," Auditor 7 said.

"It is a logical extension," Gerald said. "If I cite a rival dungeon, I am improving the structural integrity of the surrounding area. That is my job. That is what inspectors do."

The Inspector General looked at the black window. The timer was still ticking.

**TIME TO HEARING: 46 HOURS.**

"The hearing will proceed," the Inspector General said. "But the scope will be expanded."

Gerald blinked.

"Expanded?"

"You claim to be an inspector," the Inspector General said. "Then you will be judged as one."

"Judged how?"

"The hearing will not be a trial of your strength. It will be a trial of your code.

You will present your dungeon's compliance report.

The Auditors will inspect it.

If you fail, you are demolished. If you pass, the rival dungeons are spared.

"

Gerald felt a cold knot form in his stomach, which was new.

He had been expecting a battle. Or a debate.

This was an audit.

A real audit.

Not the automated ones the System sent. A live, human, adversarial audit.

He looked at his dungeon, which was a mess.

The sub-basement had been shattered during the last fight. The handrails were bent. The fire extinguishers were empty. The ventilation fans were stuck.

He had forty-six hours, and he could not fix it all.

He could not pass a full audit.

Unless...

Gerald looked at the Inspector General.

"I accept," he said.

Auditor 7 laughed. It was a dry, rasping sound.

"You will fail."

"Maybe," Gerald said. "But I will not be demolished. I will be fined."

The Inspector General raised a hand.

"The Hearing is adjourned until the audit date. You have forty-six hours to prepare your documentation. And you have forty-six hours to repair your structure.

The Auditors will arrive at the appointed time. They will not be gentle.

"

The grey figure turned. It faded.

"Wait," Gerald said.

The Inspector General stopped. Half of its body was already gone.

"One more thing," Gerald said. "Who pays for the repairs? The System fines me for violations. But who pays for the damage the Auditors caused?"

The Inspector General did not answer. It vanished.

Auditor 7 stared at Gerald. Its black ink was boiling on its clipboard.

"You have made an enemy," Auditor 7 said.

"I have made a procedure," Gerald said. "There is a difference."

Auditor 7 sneered. It turned and walked toward the exit. The other Auditors followed.

Gerald was alone, and he looked at the broken floor.

He looked at the cracked ceiling.

He looked at his clipboard.

He wrote a single word on the top line.

**PRIORITY.**

Then he wrote a second word.

**LIABILITY.**

He needed help, and he needed someone who knew the procedural rules. Someone who could stall the hearing.

Gerald tapped his foot.

He closed his eyes.

He thought of the map.

He thought of the white dot.

The center of the map.

The main atrium.

The library, and the library was above him.

The library was public.

The library was not his dungeon.

But the library was connected to his dungeon.

And the library had a maintenance budget.

Gerald opened his eyes, and he looked up through the hole in the floor.

He could see the library floor above.

He could see the feet of librarians.

He could see the feet of patrons.

He took a deep breath, and he climbed.

***

Gerald emerged from the sub-basement hatch into the main atrium of the Ashford Municipal Library.

The hatch door groaned. It was warped. It did not sit flush.

Gerald stepped out. He brushed dust from his vest.

The atrium was quiet. It was late. The sun was setting through the high windows.

A librarian looked up from her desk. She adjusted her glasses.

"Mr. Hutchins," she said. "You're not supposed to be down there. The sub-basement is closed for repairs."

Gerald walked to her desk. He placed his clipboard on the counter.

"I need access to the maintenance logs," he said. "For the last five years."

The librarian frowned.

"The maintenance logs are archived. You need a supervisor's signature."

"I have a supervisor's signature," Gerald said. "It's on this form."

He held up a form. It was blank.

The librarian stared at it.

"That is not signed."

"It will be," Gerald said. "Once you give me the logs."

The librarian sighed.

"Why do you need them?"

"I am conducting a structural integrity assessment," Gerald said. "I suspect the foundation of the library is compromised by underground activity."

The librarian laughed.

"Underground activity? Like what?"

"Like a dungeon," Gerald said.

The librarian stopped laughing, and she looked at Gerald's face. She looked at his uniform. She looked at the weird, jagged scar on his forehead where the Core registration had changed his body.

She knew.

Everyone knew.

The whole city knew that the library was haunted. That things came out of the basement. That rats had started walking upright.

"You're the Core," she said.

"I am the Inspector," Gerald corrected.

"You're both," she said.

"I need the logs," Gerald said.

The librarian looked at the hatch. She looked at Gerald.

She opened a drawer. She pulled out a thick, dusty binder.

She placed it on the counter.

"Page 42," she said. "The boiler replacement in 2019. It was never signed off."

Gerald flipped to page 42, and he read.

His eyes narrowed.

"Here," he said. "The ventilation upgrade in 2021. The contractor used non-compliant ductwork."

The librarian nodded.

"The city cut corners," she said. "They wanted to save money."

Gerald smiled. It was a thin, sharp smile.

"This is exactly what I needed."

He took out his pen, and he wrote.

He was not building a dungeon.

He was building a case.

The Inspector General wanted an audit?

Fine.

Gerald would give them one, and he would prove that the Library's foundation was compromised not by his dungeon, but by the City's negligence.

If the City was negligent, then the City was liable.

If the City was liable, then the System could not demolish him for the damage.

Because the damage was not his.

It was theirs.

Gerald looked at the librarian.

"Do you have the original contracts?" he asked.

"The originals are in the basement," she said. "In the archive."

Gerald froze.

The basement.

His dungeon, and he had to go back.

But the Auditors were coming.

And the timer was ticking.

**TIME TO HEARING: 45 HOURS.**

Gerald looked at the binder, and he looked at the hatch.

He looked at the librarian.

"Stay here," he said.

"Where are you going?"

"To get the contracts," Gerald said. "And to fix the handrails."

He turned. He walked toward the hatch.

He opened it, and he stepped down.

He descended into the dark.

***

The sub-basement was cold.

The air smelled of mold and ozone.

Gerald moved through the shadows, and he knew the layout.

He had measured it.

He had mapped it.

He knew where every crack was.

He knew where every leak was, and he moved quickly.

He was not fighting.

He was working.

He pulled a wrench from his tool belt.

He tightened a pipe, and he replaced a fuse.

He cleaned a vent.

He did not speak.

He did not think.

He worked, which was his job.

This was his strength.

While others fought with swords, Gerald fought with wrenches.

While others cast spells, Gerald cast regulations.

He fixed a loose floorboard, and he secured a railing.

He checked a smoke detector.

He logged the repairs on his clipboard.

Time passed.

Hours passed.

Gerald's hands were blistered. His back ached.

He did not stop, and he found the archive room.

It was in the northeast corner.

The door was locked.

Gerald used his key.

He entered, and the room was dark.

Dust covered the shelves.

Gerald turned on his flashlight.

He scanned the shelves.

He found the box, which was labeled **CONTRACTS 2015-2020**.

He opened it.

He pulled out the files.

He read them.

He smiled, and the City had hired a shell company. The company had no license. The work was shoddy. The materials were substandard.

This was not a mistake.

This was fraud.

Gerald took the files.

He turned to leave.

And then he saw it.

A shadow in the corner.

Not an Auditor.

Not a Core.

A person.

A man in a suit, and he was holding a briefcase.

He was not glowing.

He was not floating.

He was just a man.

But his eyes were wrong, and they were too still.

Gerald raised his flashlight.

"Who are you?" he asked.

The man did not answer.

He opened his briefcase.

Inside was a single sheet of paper.

Gerald stepped forward, and he looked at the paper.

It was a Citation.

But not from him.

Not from the System.

It was a Citation from the City.

**VIOLATION: UNLICENSED STRUCTURAL MODIFICATION.**

**FINE: $50,000 PER DAY.**

**DEMAND: DEMOLITION.**

Gerald looked at the man.

"You're the City," Gerald said.

The man smiled.

"We own the building," he said. "You're trespassing, Mr. Hutchins. And you're breaking the law."

Gerald looked at the Citation, and he looked at the man.

He looked at the files in his hands.

He had the proof.

But the City had the money.

And the City had the law.

Gerald's heart stopped, and he had won the System.

But he had lost the City.

The man stepped forward.

"Sign the demolition order," he said. "Or we sue you into bankruptcy."

Gerald looked at the pen the man held out, and he looked at the hatch.

He looked at the timer in his mind.

**TIME TO HEARING: 43 HOURS.**

He had no allies.

He had no time, and he had no choice.

Unless...

Gerald looked at the man's suit.

It was cheap.

The buttons were plastic, and the tie was loose.

He was not a high-level official.

He was a clerk.

A clerk who was trying to intimidate an Inspector.

Gerald took a breath, and he reached into his pocket.

He pulled out his badge.

He held it up.

"I am a municipal employee," Gerald said. "You cannot sue me personally. You can only sue the City. And the City is on both sides of this."

The clerk blinked.

"What?"

"I am the City," Gerald said. "And you are the City. We are the same entity."

The clerk frowned.

"That's not how it works."

"It is," Gerald said. "If you sue me, you sue yourself. If you demolish me, you demolish yourself. The System will punish you for self-harm."

The clerk hesitated.

Gerald stepped closer, and he placed his hand on the clerk's chest.

"I am filing a counter-claim," Gerald said. "For fraud. And for obstruction of justice."

He handed the clerk the files.

"Read them," Gerald said. "And decide if you want to go to jail."

The clerk looked at the files, and he looked at Gerald.

He looked at the pen.

He looked at the exit.

The clerk swallowed.

He dropped the pen, and he picked up the briefcase.

He turned and ran.

Gerald watched him go.

He did not chase him.

He did not celebrate, and he looked at the files.

He looked at the hatch.

He had bought himself time.

But not enough.

The Inspectors General were still coming.

And they were not clerks, and they were gods of procedure.

Gerald looked at his watch.

He had forty-three hours.

He had to prove that the System was wrong.

He had to prove that the Inspector General was violating the code.

He had to find a loophole.

A loophole that no one had ever used.

Gerald looked at the ceiling, and he looked at the library above.

He had an idea.

It was dangerous.

It was stupid.

It was against every rule in the book.

But it was the only way.

Gerald picked up his wrench, and he runned.

He ran toward the central atrium.

He ran toward the main power grid.

He ran toward the System's heart.

And he filed a new citation.

**VIOLATION: UNAUTHORIZED ACCESS TO PUBLIC UTILITY.**

**TARGET: THE INSPECTOR GENERAL.**

**CHARGE: TREASON.**

Gerald raised his clipboard.

His hands were steady.

"Let them come," he said. "I have a form for that."
