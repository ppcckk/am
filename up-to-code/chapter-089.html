Chapter 89: Reset to Zero

# Chapter 89

Gerald had the spreadsheet open before he finished reading Form 2-A the second time.

Eighty-seven days of continuous compliance. Fourteen public occupancy events. The threshold for Deemed Permanent status was ninety and fifteen. He was three days and one event short.

He pulled up Section 14.2 of the System's Structural Classification Code. The language was dense but familiar. *A structure that has maintained a Compliance Rating of 85 or above for ninety consecutive days and has recorded fifteen or more public occupancy events shall be deemed permanent without further inspection, provided no active demolition order is pending at the time of threshold achievement.*

The last clause was the problem. The demolition order was active.

It was active because it was pending.

It would only stop being pending if he got permanent classification. Circular.

Gerald closed the section and opened Section 14.2 subsection (d). *A pending demolition order may be stayed if the respondent files a timely appeal under Section 7.1, provided the appeal Hearing is conducted within the structure subject to demolition.*

He could appeal. He could force a Hearing. And the Hearing itself would count as a public occupancy event, because Section 7.1 required both parties to be physically present. That would bring him to fifteen.

He still needed three more days of compliance.

Gerald checked his Compliance Rating. 91. He had been above 85 for eighty-seven consecutive days. If he filed the appeal and the Hearing occurred within the next three days, the order would be stayed, the Hearing would register as the fifteenth occupancy event, and on day ninety he would be permanently classified.

He filled out Form 7.1.

Appeal of Temporary Structure Classification. Grounds: procedural error in classification methodology.

The System had never inspected his structure, which had classified him as temporary by default, based on the absence of a permit than the presence of deficiency. That was not how classification worked. A structure was not temporary because it lacked paperwork.

It was temporary because it was structurally unsound. His was not.

He filed it.

The System responded in eleven minutes.

Form 7.2.

Hearing Scheduled. Date: current plus thirty-six hours.

Location: respondent's premises. Presiding Officer: Acting Inspector, assigned.

Gerald noted the word "assigned." He had not requested a specific inspector. The System would pick one.

He pulled up the list of available inspectors within range.

Four remained. Three were System constructs, faceless administrative entities that processed paperwork in neutral chambers. The fourth was a Core.

Core 4471-B. Designation: "Acting Inspector." No prior inspection history. No public record. No address.

Gerald stared at the designation.

Cores did not serve as inspectors. That was not in the code.

He checked again.

Section 3.1, Inspector Qualifications. *Inspectors shall be appointed by the Inspector General from among qualified personnel.* The Inspector General could appoint anyone. The System did not specify that appointed inspectors could not be Cores.

This was new.

Gerald ran a scan for Core 4471-B. The System returned a location. It was close. Close. Directly above his dungeon footprint, offset by approximately forty feet to the north.

He had never detected it. His dungeon occupied the sub-basement of the Ashford Municipal Library.

He had mapped every inch of it, and he knew the ceiling height, the load-bearing walls, the water table depth.

But he had never mapped above his own ceiling. Why would he?

He was a sub-basement. The library above was municipal property, not a dungeon.

Except it was. Core 4471-B was a dungeon core. It had been operating above him. It had been there the whole time.

Cold moved through the dungeon the way water moves through a crack. Not fear.

Recognition.

He had spent eighty-seven days building handrails and installing fire suppression and measuring egress routes, and the entire time there had been a dungeon directly above his ceiling. A dungeon that the Inspector General had now appointed as his inspector.

The Hearing would be conducted by a rival. A rival who knew his structure from above, and a rival who could see his compliance layout, his spawn positions, his ventilation ducts. A rival who could testify against him.

With thirty-six hours left, Gerald went to work.

The first thing he did was measure his ceiling.

He had never done this. He knew the height, forty-two inches of concrete slab, but he had never examined its composition.

He ran a material scan.

Standard System concrete. No reinforcement. No rebar.

No post-tensioning, and he ran a scan on Core 4471-B's floor; the scan returned partial data, filtered by the System's privacy protocols, but it was enough. The floor above his ceiling was a slab of reinforced composite, thirty-six inches thick, with a load rating that exceeded his ceiling's capacity by a factor of three.

Core 4471-B was heavier than Gerald's ceiling could support.

Gerald checked the building code. Section 11.4, Structural Loading. *A structure shall not impose a load exceeding the rated capacity of any supporting element.* His ceiling was a supporting element. Core 4471-B's floor was imposing a load on it. The load exceeded the capacity.

This was a violation. Not his violation. The other one's.

Gerald drafted a citation.

Then he stopped.

A citation required physical presence to serve. Core 4471-B was above him, not inside his dungeon.

He could not serve it during the Hearing because the Hearing would take place in his space, and the Acting Inspector would be present as a party, not as a structure. A citation served during a Hearing would be a procedural irregularity. The System would void it.

He needed another angle, and he pulled up Section 7.1 again. The Hearing rules. *Both parties shall present evidence relevant to the classification question; the Presiding Officer shall render a determination based on structural logic and code compliance.*

Structural logic. Gerald could argue that his ceiling was failing.

That the load above it was exceeding capacity.

That his structure was at risk of collapse, and that it was, in fact, temporary, because its supporting element was overloaded.

But that would prove the System's classification correct. He would lose the appeal.

Unless he reframed it.

Gerald sat still for four minutes.

Then he opened a new form. Form 11-C.

Request for Structural Modification Permit.

Subject: Core 2201-E. Modification: reinforcement of ceiling slab to accommodate imposed load from adjacent structure.

He filed it. The System processed it in two minutes.

Approved.

Reason: modification addresses existing deficiency.

No inspection required for modifications under 500 cubic feet.

Gerald now had a permit. He could reinforce his ceiling. He could bring it up to code. He could make his structure sound.

But he needed Core 4471-B's cooperation. The load was coming from above. If he reinforced his ceiling, he was compensating for someone else's violation.

That was not how code worked. You did not fix someone else's problem by modifying your own structure.

You cited them and made them fix it.

Unless the modification itself created a new violation in the other structure.

Gerald checked Section 11.4 again. *A structure shall not impose a load exceeding the rated capacity of any supporting element.* If he reinforced his ceiling, the rated capacity increased. The load from above would no longer exceed it. The violation would be cured.

Another section existed. Section 11.9. *A modification to a supporting element that increases its capacity shall not reduce the rated capacity of any element above it.* If he reinforced his ceiling, he changed the load path. The load that had been distributed through his ceiling would now be redistributed upward.

Into Core 4471-B's floor.

Into Core 4471-B's supporting elements.

If those elements were not rated for the redistributed load, the violation would transfer.

Gerald ran the numbers.

His ceiling, reinforced, would push additional load upward. Core 4471-B's floor was rated for its own weight plus its contents, and it was not rated for the additional load from a reinforced ceiling below.

The numbers were close. Close. Within four percent.

Four percent was within the tolerance for a citation. Not enough to cause collapse. Enough to cite.

He drafted the citation, and he drafted it carefully.

He included the modification permit, the load calculations, the code section; included the date the modification would be performed.

He included the exact moment the load path would shift.

He would perform the reinforcement during the Hearing, and would serve the citation at the moment the load path shifted. The Acting Inspector would be present. The citation would be valid.

Gerald checked the clock. Thirty-four hours.

He prepared the reinforcement. It was a simple job.

Steel plate, bolted to the underside of the slab, and he had the materials. He had the permit. He had the calculations.

He did not have a crew. He was a Core. He could spawn entities, but he had only three mold spores, a water leak, and a rat. None of them could bolt steel plate to a ceiling.

He could do it himself.

Cores could manipulate their own structures. It was slow.

Painful.

Like bending your own bones. But he could do it.

Gerald started; the first bolt was set at hour twelve. The second at hour fourteen.

The third at hour sixteen.

His awareness was spread thin, focused on the ceiling, ignoring the rest of the dungeon.

The water leak spread. The rat wandered into a room he had not maintained in three days.

The mold spores multiplied in the damp.

His Compliance Rating dropped. 91. 89. 87, and he needed to stay above 85.

At hour twenty, the third bolt was set. The steel plate was flush.

The load path was shifting.

He felt it happen. A subtle change in the weight distribution above him, and a groan in the concrete, which was not concrete but System-composite, or something else.

He served the citation.

The System accepted it. Core 4471-B was cited for violation of Section 11.9. Debuff: structural integrity reduced by 8% for 72 hours.

The Hearing started six hours later.

The Acting Inspector materialized in his dungeon, which was not a faceless construct. It was a Core.

A presence.

Gerald felt it as he felt other Cores. A density. A shape.

The Acting Inspector looked at his ceiling. At the steel plate. At the citation glowing in the air between them.

"You reinforced your ceiling," it said. "During a Hearing."

"I filed a permit," Gerald said. "The modification was approved."

"You cited me during a Hearing."

"I cited you during a Hearing. That's what Section 7.1 permits.

Both parties are present.

A citation requires presence.

I have presence. I have a violation."

The Acting Inspector was quiet for a moment. Gerald could feel it scanning the citation. Checking the load calculations. Checking the permit.

"The load transfer is within tolerance," the Acting Inspector said. "Four percent."

"Section 11.9 does not specify a tolerance."

"It implies one. The System does not cite for trivial load shifts."

"The System cited you for a jurisdictional challenge that had no jurisdictional basis. That was trivial."

The Acting Inspector went still. Gerald recognized the stillness. It was the stillness of a Core calculating whether it was being trapped.

"You are not appealing the classification," the Acting Inspector said. "You are using the Hearing to cite me."

"I am appealing the classification. The citation is incidental."

"The citation is the point."

Gerald did not answer. He did not need to. The Acting Inspector could see his Compliance Rating.

It had dropped to 86. One point above the threshold.

One point above the floor that would trigger a debuff cascade.

The Acting Inspector looked at the ceiling again. At the steel plate. At the load calculations glowing in the System's interface.

"If I rule against you," it said slowly, "you lose the appeal. The demolition order stands. You have thirty hours."

"If you rule against me, you rule against a structure that is structurally sound and compliant with every applicable code section."

"And if I rule in your favor, you become permanent. And then you can cite me for the next ninety days without me being able to demolish you."

Gerald said nothing.

The Acting Inspector raised its hand. The System's interface pulsed. A determination was being rendered.

Gerald watched the numbers form, and he watched the word that appeared.

DENIED.

Gerald's Compliance Rating dropped to 84.

The threshold. The debuff cascade initiated. His spawns weakened.

The water leak accelerated. The rat died.

The demolition order reactivated. Timer: twenty-nine hours.

The Acting Inspector smiled. Gerald could not see its face, yet the curve of its awareness suggested a smile.

"You made one mistake," it said.

"You assumed I would be hostile.

I am not hostile. I am an inspector.

And you are a temporary structure with a failed ceiling."

Gerald looked up. The steel plate was still there.

The reinforcement was still in place, and the load path was still shifted. The citation was still active.

The Acting Inspector's structural integrity debuff was still ticking. 8%. For seventy-two hours.

And Gerald realized, with a clarity that felt like ice, that the Acting Inspector had just ruled his ceiling was structurally unsound. That was an official determination. A finding of fact. A certification.

He pulled up Section 14.2. *A structure that has maintained a Compliance Rating of 85 or above for ninety consecutive days.* His rating was 84. He had fallen below 85. The ninety-day streak was broken.

He was at zero.

Twenty-nine hours to rebuild from nothing.
