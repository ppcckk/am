Chapter 292: Wrong Form

# Chapter 292

The door frame splintered.

Shards of oak and iron sprayed across the concrete floor. Sandra stood in the breach, her silhouette framed by the flickering emergency lights of the corridor. The void-energy sword in Core 404's hand hummed with a frequency that made Gerald's teeth ache.

Gerald did not raise his trowel, and he raised his clipboard.

"Zone 4 breach," Gerald said, his voice flat. "No permit posted."

Sandra's smile widened. It wasn't a smile of friendship.

It was the look a predator gives when it realizes the prey is too stupid to run.

"You're still filing paperwork, Gerald? You're dying."

"I am documenting the incident," Gerald corrected.

Core 404 stepped forward. The black fluid dripping from the walls pooled around his boots. The mold spores, previously dormant, were now swirling in a thick, gray fog, obscuring the lower half of the room. The water leak had become a stream, running down the wall like a vein bursting.

*WARNING: STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY COMPROMISED. COMPLIANCE RATING: 12/100.*

The number flashed red in Gerald's peripheral vision. It pulsed in time with his heartbeat. If it hit zero, his dungeon would collapse into the municipal library basement above, crushing three floors of books and likely killing several librarians.

Gerald hated that, and he looked at the ramp he had been repairing. The gradient was still off. Violation 14.2. He had cited it himself, but the citation hadn't processed yet because the dungeon was under attack.

The rat squeaked again. It dropped the bucket of cement and scurried behind Gerald's leg.

"Kill him," Sandra commanded.

Core 404 raised the void-sword. The energy blade lengthened, sharpening into a razor of pure nothingness.

Gerald didn't dodge, and he stepped sideways, sliding his clipboard between the sword and his own torso.

*CLANG.*

The void-sword struck the clipboard. The wood cracked, but the System enforced the integrity of the document. A Citation could not be broken by force alone; it had to be appealed or resolved.

"Object obstruction in egress path," Gerald muttered.

The air in the dungeon thickened. The System responded to the violation with a localized gravity well. Core 404 stumbled. The void-sword dipped, its edge grazing the concrete floor, leaving a trench of black dust.

Gerald's heart hammered against his ribs. His hands shook, but only slightly. He needed to stabilize the dungeon before the mold consumed the support pillars.

He looked at Sandra.

"You violated the non-compete clause in our previous engagement, Sandra. I have the emails."

"I don't care about your emails," she said, stepping over the threshold. Her heels clicked on the concrete. "I care about the power source. You're leaking it."

Gerald looked at the walls, and the black fluid was seeping faster. The

"rebellion" wasn't a monster breaking in.

It was the dungeon itself rejecting its own design flaws.

The mold spores were trying to fix the ventilation by clogging it. The water leak was trying to cool the overheated core by flooding it.

They were trying to survive by killing him.

And Sandra was using that chaos to breach the sanctity of the Core Chamber.

Gerald turned to the wall. The scratching had stopped. The face in the water leak stared at him with hollow eyes.

"Stop," Gerald said to the dungeon. "Obey."

The water face ignored him.

Gerald pulled a red stamp from his belt. It was heavy, made of iron and bound by a chain.

The Stamp of Immediate Cessation, and he had never used it.

It required a violation of Category A severity.

Life-threatening instability.

He slammed the stamp onto the nearest load-bearing pillar.

*THUD.*

The sound was like a gunshot.

A shockwave of blue light rippled out from the stamp. It hit the mold spores, freezing them mid-swirl. It hit the water leak, halting the flow. The Compliance Rating froze at 12.

But the cost was immediate.

Gerald felt a sharp pain in his left arm. The System had deducted hit points from the Core to enforce the order. His left hand went numb.

"Effective," Sandra noted. She looked impressed, then disappointed. "But you can't stamp everything, Gerald.

The System allows one emergency injunction per cycle. You just spent yours.

"

She was right.

The HUD flashed: *EMERGENCY INJUNCTION EXHAUSTED. REGENERATION DISABLED FOR 24 HOURS.*

Gerald looked at his numb hand. He flexed his fingers. They didn't respond.

Core 404 recovered from the gravity well. He grinned, a jagged expression on his void-darkened face.

"One stamp. And you can't use it again."

"I don't need to," Gerald said.

He looked at the rat, and the rat was holding a small, damp piece of paper. It had retrieved it from the rubble.

Gerald reached down with his right hand, the only one that still worked, and took the paper.

It was a Form 88-C: Notice of Unauthorized Structural Alteration.

He had filed it three chapters ago against Core 404's previous expansion. It had been rejected. But the rejection notice had a timestamp. And a code.

Gerald looked at Sandra.

"You're standing in Zone 2."

Sandra frowned.

"So?"

"Zone 2 is classified as Residential-Use Sub-Basement."

"It's a dungeon," Sandra said.

"It was a library sub-basement," Gerald corrected. "And under Ashford Municipal Code Section 12, Subsection D, Paragraph 4, any entity conducting hostile operations in a Residential-Use zone is subject to Immediate Eviction by Municipal Authority."

Sandra laughed.

"You think the city can stop a dungeon core?"

"The System recognizes Municipal Authority as a valid regulatory body," Gerald said. He remembered the rule. He had read it in the fine print. *System Compliance overrides magical hierarchy if the magical hierarchy violates local zoning laws.*

It was a loophole. A tiny, bureaucratic crack in the cosmic order.

Gerald raised his clipboard. The cracked wood was still intact. He tapped the top of it three times.

"By order of the City of Ashford, I evict you from this premises."

The air in the room changed.

The System didn't care about dragons. It didn't care about void-swords. It cared about code.

A beam of white light shot down from the ceiling. It wasn't a laser. It was a grid. A physical barrier of blue-white energy snapped into existence around Sandra.

She tried to step forward. She hit the barrier.

*ZAP.*

She flew backward, skidding across the concrete. Her heels scraped against the floor.

Core 404 roared. He swung his void-sword at the barrier. The sword passed through.

But Sandra didn't, and she was human. Or mostly human. The barrier stopped her body, but not her magic.

"You can't evict me!" she shouted, struggling against the light. "I'm a guest! An invited guest!"

"You were invited," Gerald said, his voice steady despite the trembling in his legs. "Invitation revoked. Violation of hospitality laws."

The barrier tightened. It compressed around her, pinning her arms to her sides.

Core 404 looked at Gerald. His eyes narrowed. The void-energy in his sword flared.

"You're cheating," 404 said.

"I'm enforcing," Gerald replied.

He looked at the mold spores. They were still frozen. The water leak was still paused. The dungeon was holding its breath, waiting to see if the inspector would survive.

Gerald had bought them time. But not much.

The barrier would last only as long as Gerald could maintain the citation. His mana pool was draining fast. The pain in his left arm was spreading to his shoulder.

He needed to finish this, and he walked toward Sandra. The barrier moved with him, keeping her contained.

"Leave," Gerald said. "Take your sword. Take your void. Leave my building."

Core 404 stepped closer. The black fluid from the walls reached out, trying to pull Gerald back. The mold spores thawed, inching toward his ankles.

404 raised his sword.

"I'm not leaving. I'm taking the Core."

Gerald looked at the rat, and the rat was chewing on a wire. A thick, copper cable that ran along the baseboard. The electrical wiring for the dungeon's lighting system.

Gerald's eyes widened.

The Electrical Code.

If he cut the power, the lights would go out. But if he overloaded the circuit, he could trigger a fire suppression system.

The fire suppression system in his dungeon was non-functional. Violation 22. But the sprinklers were installed. They were just blocked by debris.

If he could clear the debris, he could activate the sprinklers.

But he couldn't reach them. His arm was numb.

The rat looked at him. It squeaked.

Gerald understood.

"Electrical hazard," Gerald said.

He pointed to the wire the rat was chewing.

Core 404 laughed.

"You're going to electrocute yourself?"

"No," Gerald said. "I'm citing you for creating a conductive path on a non-insulated surface."

He snapped his fingers.

The System recognized the citation.

The wire sparked.

A bolt of lightning arced from the chewed cable, jumping to the void-sword.

Void-energy and electricity do not mix well.

The sword exploded.

Core 404 screamed as the feedback loop surged through his arm. The black fluid on the walls recoiled. The mold spores scattered, burning away in the electrical fire.

Sandra hit the ground, blinded by the flash.

Gerald grabbed the trowel, and he ran to the sprinkler head above the main corridor. It was clogged with black sludge.

He swung the trowel. Once. Twice.

The sludge broke off.

The sprinkler clicked.

Water sprayed down. Cold, municipal tap water.

It hit the floor. It hit the mold. It hit the void-energy remnants.

Steam hissed.

The Compliance Rating jumped.

*COMPLIANCE: 45.*

*COMPLIANCE: 50.*

The debuffs lifted. Gerald's left arm tingled. Sensation returned, sharp and painful.

He looked up.

Core 404 was on his knees. His void-sword was gone. His arm was smoking.

Sandra was crawling toward the door. The barrier had dissipated with the chaos.

"Run," she gasped. "He's coming."

"Who?" Gerald asked.

"The Inspector General."

Gerald froze, and the Inspector General was the ultimate auditor. The entity that monitored all Cores. It had never been seen. It had only been rumored.

Why was she afraid of it?

Because the explosion had been loud. And the System logs everything.

A low hum filled the room.

Not from the walls.

From the ceiling.

The ceiling of the dungeon, which was the floor of the library above, vibrated.

Gerald looked up.

The concrete slab above was cracking.

Not from damage.

From pressure.

Something was pressing down from above. Something massive.

The dust fell.

Gerald stepped back. The rat scurried onto his shoulder.

The ceiling split open.

A hand reached through, which was white. Smooth. Featureless.

It held a clipboard.

It was larger than Gerald's. It was larger than the room.

The Inspector General had arrived.

And it was holding a citation.

Gerald looked at Sandra. She was already gone, vanished into the shadows of the corridor.

She had used him. She had used the chaos. She had let him take the hit.

And now, the true enforcer was here.

Gerald raised his clipboard. His hand shook.

"State your business," Gerald said.

The white hand did not speak. It pointed at Gerald.

Then, it pointed at the broken wire.

Then, it pointed at the wet floor.

Then, it pointed at Core 404, who was trying to stand.

A voice echoed in Gerald's mind. It sounded like paper tearing.

*VIOLATION 1.*

*UNAUTHORIZED USE OF FORCE.*

*VIOLATION 2.*

*FAILURE TO MAINTAIN SAFE WALKING SURFACES.*

*VIOLATION 3.*

*INTERFERENCE WITH FEDERAL AUDIT.*

Gerald looked at Core 404.

404 looked back. For the first time, the void-core looked terrified.

"You didn't just cite him, Gerald," 404 whispered. "You cited the event."

Gerald realized the mistake, and he hadn't just cited Core 404.

He had cited the explosion.

And the explosion was a result of his own actions.

The Inspector General didn't care about the war. It didn't care about the dungeon.

It cared about the paperwork.

And Gerald had filed the wrong form.

The white hand snapped its fingers.

Gerald's HUD flashed red.

*CORE SUSPENSION PENDING.*

*AUDIT COMMENCING.*

*PREPARE FOR IMMEDIATE CLOSURE.*

The floor beneath Gerald gave way, and he fell.

Not into the basement.

Into the audit.
