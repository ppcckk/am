Chapter 113: Mud from the City

# Chapter 113 The floor started to shake while the ink remained wet.

Gerald Hutchins did not look up from his clipboard. He looked at the vibration.

It was a low-frequency tremor, roughly four hertz, localized to the southeast quadrant of his dungeon’s primary atrium.

He knew this because he had mapped the atrium down to the millimeter during Chapter 45, and he had installed dampening foam under the floorboards during Chapter 89. This vibration was not mechanical. It was magical.

It was unauthorized.

"Kael," Gerald said, his voice flat and dry as parchment. "Is that a resonance frequency or a breach?"

Kael, the former adventurer who now served as Gerald’s reluctant security consultant and general lackey, stood by the door, his hand hovering near his sword hilt. "I don’t know, Gerald. The shadows are moving."

"Shadows do not move unless there is a light source changing position or a physical obstruction interrupting the light path," Gerald said. He tapped his pen against the clipboard.

"Unless we are dealing with a Class IV Illusion.

Which requires a minimum of three mana conductors within twenty feet. Do we have mana conductors?"

"No."

"Then the shadows are lying. Or I need to recalibrate my eyes."

The floorboards in the southeast corner buckled. Not upward, but inward.

The wood splintered, not into shards, but into a thick, viscous black sludge.

It smelled of old paper and rotting wood.

The Archivist’s signature.

Gerald stepped back. He did not run. Running implied panic.

Panic led to poor decision-making. Poor decision-making led to citations.

Gerald did not want another citation.

He already had a pending motion to stay the city’s demolition order, and he was not about to complicate his legal standing by being seen fleeing a crime scene.

"Kael, secure the perimeter," Gerald said. "Do not engage. Observe. Document."

Kael drew his sword. "Gerald, that thing is eating the floor."

"It is not eating.

It is *spawning*," Gerald corrected. He watched the black sludge rise.

It formed a shape.

A humanoid shape, but wrong. Too many joints. Too much height.

Its head was a bundle of quills bound together by wet leather.

It stepped out of the floor. The floor healed behind it. The wood knit itself back together, leaving no trace of the intrusion.

"Unlicensed biological entity," Gerald muttered. He reached for his interface. He tried to open the Spawn tab.

**ERROR: SPAWN TAB LOCKED.** **REASON: ACTIVE HEARING SCHEDULED. STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY UNDER REVIEW. NO NEW ASSETS PERMITTED UNTIL COMPLIANCE VERIFICATION IS COMPLETE.**

Gerald blinked. He tried again.

**ERROR: SPAWN TAB LOCKED.**

"This is unacceptable," Gerald said.

"I have a pending motion.

That does not suspend my operational capacity. It only suspends my ability to alter the external facade.

The interior is mine. The floor is mine. That thing is on my floor."

The Quill-Golem turned its head. It had no eyes.

It had holes where eyes should be, filled with flowing ink.

It looked at Gerald. It tilted its head.

*“Inspection,”* the Golem said. Its voice sounded like pages tearing. *“Where is the permit?”*

Gerald felt a cold spike of anxiety.

Not because the monster was threatening him, but because it was asking a question he couldn’t answer immediately. He didn’t have the permit.

He had the motion, and the motion was pending. The permit was denied.

"I have a motion to stay," Gerald said.

*“A motion is not a permit,”* the Golem said. It took a step forward.

The floor beneath its foot turned gray and brittle.

"You are operating without a current Certificate of Occupancy.

This is a violation of Ordinance 7-B: Unauthorized Dungeon Operation."

Gerald’s heart skipped a beat. Ordinance 7-B. He knew this ordinance.

He had cited three other cores for it in Chapter 60, and it was a minor violation, usually resulting in a fine.

But this wasn’t the System, which was the Archivist. And the Archivist was quoting the code back at him.

"Who are you?" Gerald asked.

*“I am the Auditor,”* the Golem said. *“And you are out of compliance.”*

Kael lunged.

His sword sparked against the Golem’s arm. The metal slid off the wet leather like oil.

Kael stumbled back, gasping.

"It’s slippery! I can’t get a grip!"

"Stop," Gerald said. "Don’t hit it.

If you damage the structure, you create a new violation.

Debris in the egress path.

Slippery surface hazard. You’re adding to the list."

Kael lowered his sword, breathing hard. "It’s going to kill us, Gerald."

"Not if we follow the procedure," Gerald said. He looked at the Golem. It was waiting.

It wasn’t attacking. It was inspecting.

It was holding the floor at 32 psi, testing its load-bearing capacity.

Gerald closed his eyes. He visualized the dungeon. The plumbing.

The wiring.

The ventilation shafts. He remembered every inch.

He had built this place to be perfect.

To be a fortress of compliance.

And then he realized the flaw.

The Archivist wasn’t attacking him with magic. It was attacking him with bureaucracy.

It was citing him.

But citations required a target, and a target had to be physically present to be served.

The Golem was in the dungeon. Gerald was in the dungeon. The rules applied.

But Gerald couldn’t spawn more troops. He couldn’t reinforce the walls. He was locked out of his own upgrade menu.

Unless.

Unless he filed a counter-citation.

To file a citation, Gerald needed to identify a violation. The Golem was standing on the floor. The floor was wood. The Golem was made of ink and leather.

Ink is a flammable liquid. Leather is a combustible material.

Fire Code.

Section 402.1: Storage of Flammable Liquids. Section 402.2: Combustible Loading.

Gerald opened his interface. He bypassed the Spawn tab. He went to the **Compliance Audit** sub-menu.

**STATUS: 100/100.** **PENDING HEARINGS: 1 (City Demolition).** **ACTIVE CITATIONS ISSUED: 42.**

He looked for the **Issue Citation** button. It was grayed out.

**CITATION ISSUANCE SUSPENDED DUE TO PENDING HEARING.**

Gerald gritted his teeth. He couldn’t cite it. He couldn’t upgrade. He couldn’t fight.

The Golem took another step. The floor groaned.

*“Your egress width is non-compliant,”* the Golem said. *“The main corridor narrows to forty-two inches at the junction with the boiler room. Minimum is forty-four inches. This is a life-safety violation.”*

Gerald froze, and he had measured that corridor. He had measured it twice. It was forty-four inches. He had installed the handrails to ensure clearance.

But the Golem was right. The handrails. The new handrails he had installed in Chapter 105, to comply with ADA accessibility standards for the public library above.

The handrails were thick. They protruded.

He had prioritized the public code over the dungeon code. He had made the dungeon too safe for the humans above, and too cramped for the monsters below.

It was a design conflict. A clash of regulatory bodies.

"I didn't know," Gerald said. It was the first time he had ever admitted he didn’t know something. It tasted like ash in his mouth.

*“Ignorance is not an excuse,”* the Golem said.

It raised a hand. The quills extended. They were sharp.

They were ready to pierce. *“This dungeon is condemned.”*

"Wait," Gerald said. He looked at Kael. "Kael, do you have a measuring tape?"

Kael looked confused. "What? Why?"

"Do you have one?"

Kael reached into his pack. He pulled out a retractable tape measure, and had carried it for years, for measuring loot bags, for checking weapon reach. It was a mundane, cheap thing.

Gerald took it. He stepped toward the Golem.

"Gerald!" Kael shouted.

Gerald ignored him. He walked up to the narrowing corridor. He held the tape measure against the wall, then against the handrail.

*Forty-two inches.*

He turned to the Golem. "You are correct. The egress is non-compliant."

*“Then you admit the violation,”* the Golem said.

"I admit the physical reality," Gerald said. "But I do not admit the jurisdiction."

The Golem paused.

Gerald looked at the floor. The black sludge was spreading. It was covering the handrails. It was covering the egress path.

"The violation you cite is based on the current configuration of the dungeon," Gerald said. His voice was gaining strength.

The dry, deadpan tone returned, anchored by the certainty of the numbers.

"However, you are standing on a temporary obstruction. A Class IV Illusionary construct. This construct is occupying the egress path.

Therefore, the effective egress width is not forty-two inches. It is zero."

The Golem tilted its head.

"Under Municipal Code 12.4, Section C," Gerald continued, speaking faster now, the words tumbling out like a machine gun, "the obstruction of an egress path by temporary materials constitutes a Priority One Fire Hazard. Priority One Hazards trigger an Immediate Abatement Order. This order supersedes all other regulatory conflicts because it addresses an imminent threat to life safety."

Gerald pointed at the Golem.

"You are the obstruction," Gerald said. "You are the hazard. Therefore, *you* are in violation of the fire code."

The Golem stood still. The ink on its face rippled.

*“I am the Auditor,”* it said, but there was a hint of uncertainty in its voice. *“I am enforcing the code.”*

"You are violating the code," Gerald said. "And since you are a foreign entity within my jurisdiction, I am not citing you for a violation. I am citing you for *being* a violation."

Gerald looked at his interface. The **Issue Citation** button was still grayed out.

Another button existed. A button he had never used. A button buried in the **Emergency Actions** sub-menu, which he had unlocked in Chapter 72 after he successfully defended a citation against a Fire Elemental Core.

**EMERGENCY ABATEMENT ORDER.** **REQUIREMENT: IMMEDIATE THREAT TO LIFE/PROPERTY.** **COST: 10% OF TOTAL MANA RESERVES. 24-HOUR COOLDOWN.**

Gerald didn’t care about the cost. He looked at the Golem. He looked at the spreading ink. He looked at Kael, who was trembling.

He pressed the button.

**ABATEMENT ORDER ISSUED.** **TARGET: UNLICENSED ENTITY.** **ACTION: REMOVAL.**

The air in the room snapped.

It wasn’t an explosion, which was a vacuum.

The System pulled the air out of the space occupied by the Golem; the ink, the leather, the quills—they didn’t burn.

They didn’t break. They simply ceased to exist.

The Golem vanished, and the floor was empty. The wood was intact. The handrails remained.

Silence.

Kael dropped his sword. It clattered on the floor. He stared at Gerald. "What did you do?"

Gerald slumped against the wall. He felt drained.

His vision blurred.

The 10% mana cost was significant. It felt like a blood donation.

"I filed an emergency order," Gerald said.

He wiped his mouth. "It’s a loophole.

The System prioritizes immediate life safety over all other regulations.

If you can prove that the *entity itself* is the violation, you can remove it without a hearing."

Kael stared at him. "You just... deleted it."

"I abated it," Gerald corrected. "There’s a difference.

Abatement implies temporary removal until compliance is restored, and the entity was the non-compliant element.

Now it’s gone. We are compliant again."

Kael laughed. It was a hysterical, broken sound. "You’re insane.

You’re actually insane. You argued with a shadow monster using building codes and won."

"I didn't argue," Gerald said. "I cited."

He looked at his interface.

**ABATEMENT ORDER COMPLETE.** **COMPLIANCE RATING: 100/100.** **MANA RESERVES: 90%.**

He was safe. For now.

But then, a new notification popped up. Not from the System. Not from the City.

It was a red text box, pulsing in the center of his vision.

**SYSTEM ALERT: INSPECTOR GENERAL AUDIT INITIATED.** **TRIGGER: UNPRECEDENTED USE OF EMERGENCY ABATEMENT AGAINST A FOREIGN ENTITY.** **STATUS: IMMEDIATE REVIEW.** **TIME TO HEARING: 1 HOUR.**

Gerald stared at the message.

An Inspector General Audit, and he had never faced one. It was the highest level of oversight. It wasn’t a hearing with a local judge or a city council, and was the System itself, judging his interpretation of its own rules.

If he lost, the Abatement Order would be reversed.

The Golem would come back. And worse, his Compliance Rating would be wiped. It would go back to zero.

The debuffs would hit.

His mold spores would die. His rat would stop moving.

The water leak would flood his core, and he had one hour.

He needed to prepare. He needed evidence. He needed to prove that the Golem was a fire hazard, not just a monster.

He looked at the spot where the Golem had stood, and the floor was clean. Too clean.

No residue remained. No ink stain. No scorch mark.

"Kael," Gerald said. "Did you see any residue left behind?"

Kael shook his head. "No. It was like it never happened."

Gerald frowned. That wasn’t right. Physical objects leave physical traces. Even magic leaves a trace.

He walked to the spot. He touched the wood. It was cold.

"Kael, bring me a UV light."

"What?"

"A UV light. From the janitor’s closet. We have one for checking water damage."

Kael ran off. Gerald waited. He felt the clock ticking in his head. One hour.

Kael returned with a small, handheld UV lamp. Gerald switched it on.

He swept the beam across the floor.

The wood glowed faintly. Normal.

He swept it across the handrail.

The handrail glowed, and he swept it across the wall.

There.

A faint, purple outline. A footprint. But not a foot.

A claw mark. Deep in the grain of the wood.

And inside the claw mark, glowing with a different, sickly green light, was a word.

**RECALL.**

Gerald froze.

*Recall.*

That wasn’t a System command. That wasn’t a code violation. That was a spell. A retrieval spell.

The Golem hadn’t been destroyed. It had been *recalled*.

By whom?

The Archivist.

The Archivist hadn’t just sent a scout. The Archivist had used Gerald’s emergency abatement as a cover. Gerald had triggered the System’s removal protocol, which had *pulled* the Golem out of the dungeon, back to its owner.

The Archivist had tricked him.

And the Inspector General was coming to ask why he had used emergency protocols to assist an enemy dungeon core in retrieving its assets.

Gerald looked at Kael.

"We don't have an hour," Gerald said. His voice was quiet. Terrified.

"What do you mean?"

"The Audit isn't a hearing. It's an arrest."

The door to the dungeon burst open.

It wasn’t a guard. It wasn’t a goblin.

It was a man in a suit. A gray suit. He held a briefcase. He wore glasses that reflected no light.

He stepped into the dungeon. He looked at Gerald. He looked at the empty spot on the floor.

"Mr. Hutchins," the man said. His voice was smooth, like polished stone. "You are under investigation for aiding and abetting a hostile dungeon core."

Gerald looked at the man. He looked at the briefcase.

"I didn't aid him," Gerald said. "I abated him."

"You removed the evidence of his presence," the man said.

He opened the briefcase.

Inside was not papers. Inside was a single, black quill.

"And you failed to report the retrieval spell. Which is a violation of Reporting Ordinance 12-C."

Gerald felt the floor drop out from under him.

**COMPLIANCE RATING: 0/100.** **STATUS: SUSPENDED.** **ACTION: CUSTODY.**

The man reached into the briefcase. He pulled out handcuffs. But they weren’t metal. They were made of light.

"Please come with me," the man said.

Gerald looked at Kael. Kael looked at Gerald. Kael looked at the sword on the floor.

Gerald looked at the man.

"I have the right to remain silent," Gerald said.

"You do," the man said. "But you also have a duty to report violations. You failed. That is why you are here."

The light handcuffs snapped onto Gerald’s wrists, and they burned.

"Next chapter," Gerald whispered, the pain making his voice thin. "Hope you have your lawyers ready."

The man pulled him forward. Gerald didn’t fight. You don’t fight the Inspector General.

But as he was dragged out of his own dungeon, Gerald noticed something.

The man’s shoes, and they were covered in mud.

Ashford mud.

Not dungeon mud.

The Inspector General was from the City.

The System wasn’t the only thing watching him.

And the City knew he was guilty before they even arrived.

He began writing the defense of a guilty man.
