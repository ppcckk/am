Chapter 233: Form 11-B

# Chapter 233

The clipboard hovered in the air, suspended by a faint, golden wireframe that pulsed with System diagnostics. To anyone else, it looked like a standard municipal form. To Gerald Hutchins, it looked like a death warrant printed on high-quality bond paper.

He stood in the ventilation shaft, his non-physical core body shimmering with static. The City Manager, a man named Arthur Pendergast who had once cited Gerald for having a coffee mug that exceeded the legal limit of fluid volume in a public workspace, did not look angry.

He looked bored.

That was worse. Anger could be argued with. Boredom implied a finality that Gerald had never seen in a code enforcement file.

Pendergast tapped the clipboard with a pen that didn't exist in the physical world. It made a sound like a gavel hitting wood.

"Form 11-B," Pendergast said, his voice echoing off the galvanized steel. "Unlicensed Biological Entity. You are currently operating without a valid business license for the entity known as 'Gerald Hutchins.' The previous license was revoked thirty-one years ago due to excessive pedantry. We are reinstating that revocation retroactively."

Gerald's hand twitched. It was a phantom limb, a construct of his will, but the muscle memory remained. He reached for a pen that wasn't there.

"This is procedurally incorrect," Gerald said.

His voice was flat, stripped of the fear that should have been there. Fear was a variable he couldn't measure, so he ignored it.

"You cannot revoke a license retroactively without a prior hearing and a notice of intent to revoke.

I have never received such a notice."

Pendergast smiled. It was a thin, bureaucratic smile.

"The System provided the notice.

It was in the fine print of your Core Registration.

Section 4, Paragraph B. *'By accepting Core Status, the Inspector agrees to be subject to the Original Employment Contract of the City of Ashford, specifically regarding disciplinary history.'* You signed it. Or rather, your body dissolved into it."

The walls of the shaft groaned. Not from age. From pressure.

The air in the tunnel tasted like copper and old dust. The Compliance Rating bar above Gerald's head flickered. 12.

It dropped to 11.

The vine-core, the sentient plant monster that had been his only ally in this shaft, recoiled. Its leaves turned a sickly brown. It had relied on Gerald's structural integrity to keep the tunnel from collapsing under the weight of the library above. Now that Gerald was being reclassified as an illegal entity, the support structure was being legally dismantled.

"Leave," Pendergast said. He pointed the pen at the girl clinging to a ledge below. Maya.

She was a rogue archer from a rival party that had tried to raid Gerald's dungeon three days ago.

She had helped him fix a water leak. Now she was collateral damage in a municipal dispute.

"Take the girl.

Leave the dungeon. We are rezoning this sub-basement for luxury condominiums."

Gerald looked at the vine-core. The monster looked back, its eyes wide with a intelligence that shouldn't have existed in a plant.

It wasn't afraid of Pendergast.

It was afraid of the silence.

If Gerald disappeared, the dungeon died. If the dungeon died, the vine-core became just a weed in a basement.

"You're not an IG," Gerald said. The realization hit him with the force of a sledgehammer. The Inspector General wore white robes.

Pendergast wore a beige suit. The System's audit protocol required white robes.

This wasn't an audit.

Pendergast tilted his head. "Does it matter? The citation is valid.

The entity is unlicensed.

The structure is condemned."

"It matters because you don't have jurisdiction over a Dungeon Core," Gerald said. He looked at the golden wireframe of the clipboard. He looked at the System interface that hovered in his peripheral vision. *Current Status: Condemned. Pending Demolition.*

He had spent thirty-one years finding the loopholes in building codes. He had found the one clause in the International Building Code that allowed inspectors to halt demolition if the building in question was deemed a "Historic Landmark" due to unique architectural features.

A Dungeon Core was not a building. It was a biological entity. But the *dungeon*—the physical space—was a building.

Gerald closed his eyes. He stopped thinking like a Core. He stopped thinking like a monster. He started thinking like Gerald Hutchins, Senior Building Inspector, Grade 4.

He looked at the beam above him. The one Pendergast had supposedly 'renovated' to trap him.

"Maya," Gerald said.

He didn't look at her. He looked at the ceiling. "Grab the vine.

Wrap it around the support beam at three meters."

Maya blinked.

Sweat dripped from her forehead into her eyes.

"What? Are you insane?

The IG is right there!"

"Wrap. It.

Around.

The. Beam," Gerald repeated. His voice was louder.

It carried the specific, icy tone he used when telling a contractor that their plumbing was a violation of health standards.

Maya moved. She didn't question it.

She had learned, in the three days since raiding this place, that Gerald did not waste words.

She vaulted onto the narrow ledge, grabbing the thick, woody tendril of the vine-core. The vine resisted, then obeyed. It coiled around the steel I-beam that crossed the shaft.

Pendergast frowned. He looked up. "What are you doing?"

Gerald opened his eyes. He focused on the System interface.

He didn't try to attack Pendergast.

He couldn't.

Pendergast was a manifestation of the System's authority, or at least, he had the authority of the City Manager, which the System recognized as a higher tier of municipal governance than a low-level Core.

Instead, Gerald accessed his own dungeon's inventory. Not the monsters. The *infrastructure*.

He pulled up the schematic for the sub-basement. It was a mess. But there was one thing in there that wasn't listed in the standard dungeon spawn list. A thing he had found during his first inspection, before the Core Registration had fully taken hold.

A load-bearing column. A remnant of the original 1920s library foundation. It was made of concrete so dense it was technically classified as 'ancient masonry' under preservation laws.

And it was directly above the shaft.

"Gerald, you can't cite me," Pendergast said. He raised a hand. The golden wireframe expanded, trying to encompass Gerald.

"Your license is revoked. You have no authority to issue citations."

"I'm not citing you," Gerald said. He looked at the vine-core. "I'm filing a complaint."

He didn't aim at Pendergast. He aimed at the beam.

He selected the 'Structural Integrity' module.

He didn't attack the beam. He *inspected* it. He forced the System to recognize the beam as a historic artifact.

He used his remaining compliance points—those meager 12 points—to pay for an Emergency Preservation Order.

The System paused.

A prompt appeared in the air.

**ACTION: EMERGENCY PRESERVATION ORDER.** **COST: ALL REMAINING COMPLIANCE POINTS.** **TARGET: HISTORIC MASONRY REMNANT (ID: #004-A).** **EFFECT: FREEZE ALL STRUCTURAL MODIFICATIONS IN RADIUS 50M.**

Gerald pressed 'Accept'.

The golden wireframe around Pendergast shattered. Not with a crash, but with a soft *pop*, like a bubble bursting.

The air in the shaft stopped moving. The pressure vanished. The dust froze in mid-air.

Pendergast looked down at his clipboard. The form was blank. The name 'Gerald Hutchins' was gone. In its place was a single word: *PROTECTED*.

Pendergast's beige suit flickered. He looked at Gerald, and for the first time, the boredom was gone. He looked confused.

"What did you do?" Pendergast asked. His voice lacked its earlier resonance. It sounded thin.

"I filed a Section 103 Preservation Claim," Gerald said. He was breathing hard, though he didn't need to.

The strain of the action was physical.

"Under the Historic Structures Act, any building component over one hundred years old cannot be altered or demolished without a unanimous vote of the Historical Society. You didn't get that vote. You didn't even file the paperwork."

Pendergast stared at him. The System rules were absolute. The City Manager was a manifestation of local authority, but the Historic Structures Act was a higher-tier law in the Ashford municipal code. The System recognized the hierarchy.

"You can't use municipal law against the System," Pendergast said, but his hands were shaking. The clipboard dissolved into pixels.

"I didn't use municipal law," Gerald said. "I used the System's own respect for precedent.

You treated this dungeon like a building.

I treated it like a protected landmark.

The System hates inconsistency. If you can't demolish it, you can't rezone it.

And if you can't rezone it, you have no authority to evict me."

The vine-core relaxed. Its leaves flushed green again. Maya slumped against the wall, gasping.

Pendergast stepped back. The darkness of the shaft seemed to pull at him.

He wasn't an enemy.

He was a glitch. A bug in the code. And Gerald had just found the exploit.

"This isn't over," Pendergast said.

He looked smaller now. Less substantial.

"The IG will see this.

The Inspector General doesn't care about historic preservation. He cares about compliance."

"Then let him come," Gerald said. "I'll have my paperwork ready."

Pendergast vanished. He didn't walk away. He simply ceased to be there, leaving behind the smell of ozone and the faint, lingering image of a disappointed face.

Silence rushed back into the shaft. It was heavy. Oppressive.

Maya slid down the wall until she was sitting on the floor. She looked up at Gerald. Her face was pale. Her arrow quiver was empty.

"You just froze the System," she whispered. "You froze the whole zone."

Gerald looked at his hands. They were trembling.

Not from fear.

From the effort of holding the Preservation Order active.

It was costing him everything. He had no compliance points left.

His dungeon was, technically, in a state of legal limbo.

He couldn't spawn monsters. He couldn't upgrade traps.

He couldn't even open a door.

The dungeon was a museum exhibit. Static. Safe.

And useless.

"We need to move," Gerald said. His voice was hoarse.

"The preservation order is temporary.

The System will correct the error. We have maybe an hour before the IG arrives to overwrite the preservation claim with a demolition permit."

Maya stood up. She looked at the exit tunnel. It was blocked by rubble from the earlier collapse.

"How do we get out?" she asked. "The tunnels are collapsed."

Gerald looked at the vine-core. The plant creature was still coiled around the beam. It looked at Gerald with an expression that might have been worry.

"We don't go out," Gerald said. He walked toward the blocked tunnel.

He placed his hand on the rubble.

It was solid stone.

He couldn't move it. He had no mana.

He had no strength.

But he had a clipboard.

He pulled a small, spectral note from his inventory. A sticky note. It had been attached to the Preservation Order. He peeled it off and stuck it to the largest rock in the pile.

The note read: *PENDING REINSPECTION.*

He tapped the note.

The rock didn't move. But the System interface flashed.

**OBJECT IDENTIFIED: UNLICENSED OBSTRUCTION.** **STATUS: NON-COMPLIANT.** **ACTION: REMOVAL ORDER ISSUED.**

The rock crumbled. Not because Gerald moved it. Because the System decided it was no longer allowed to be there.

Maya's eyes went wide. "You just deleted the wall."

"I cited it for blocking egress," Gerald said. He stepped through the gap.

The dust settled around his ankles.

"Every object in a dungeon must serve a purpose or be removed. A rock that is not a trap, a decoration, or a structural support is a hazard. I filed a hazard report.

The System cleaned up its mess."

They moved through the tunnels. Gerald was weak. His core felt thin, like parchment held together by spit. He leaned on the vine-core, which slithered ahead, clearing small debris with its tendrils.

Maya watched him. She had raided this dungeon to kill its core for loot. Now she was helping him escape.

"Why did you save me?" she asked. They were in the main lobby of the sub-basement.

The water leak was still dripping, but the water was clear.

The handrails were polished. It was the cleanest dungeon she had ever seen.

Gerald stopped at the main entrance. The door was locked.

Not with a lock.

With a seal.

The System had sealed the exit to prevent the 'illegal entity' from fleeing.

"I didn't save you," Gerald said. He looked at the door. "You were in the room.

You were a witness to a code violation. Witnesses are required to stay until the inspection is complete."

Maya stared at him. "That's insane."

"It's procedure," Gerald said. He looked at the seal. It was a bright blue barrier. It pulsed.

He couldn't break it. He didn't have the points. He didn't have the power.

But he had the vine-core. And he had the knowledge that the City Manager had lied.

The City Manager had said the renovation was a setup to test Gerald. But the IG had shown up exactly when Gerald found the logs. The logs. The old municipal records in the library's archives.

Gerald had read them while hiding in the vents.

The logs showed that the Ashford Municipal Library had been built on a sinkhole. The foundation was unstable. The 'renovation' wasn't a trap.

It was a cover-up for the fact that the library itself was condemned.

The System knew this. The IG knew this.

The IG wasn't trying to audit Gerald. The IG was trying to cover up the library's condemnation by destroying the dungeon that had discovered the truth.

Gerald looked at the vine-core. "Can you taste the air?"

The vine-core twitched. It extended a tendril to the door. It tasted the magic in the barrier.

**SPELLE: SEAL OF CONDEMNATION.** **SOURCE: CITY MANAGER / INSPECTOR GENERAL JOINT AUTHORITY.**

"It's a joint authority," Gerald muttered. "They're both signing off on it. That means they're both liable."

He pulled out his mental file cabinet. He opened a folder he had never used. It was labeled *'Liability Waivers and Indemnification Clauses.'*

He didn't have points to buy an attack. He had points to buy a lawyer.

Well, not a lawyer. A *representative*.

There was a System mechanic for this. A loophole so obscure that even the beta testers had missed it.

**SUMMON PROXY:** *When a Core is under audit by a municipal authority, they may summon a 'Third-Party Inspector' to oversee the process. Cost: 50% of all stored Mana.*

Gerald had little Mana. His dungeon was small. His spawn list was pathetic. But he had one thing the other cores didn't have.

He had the accumulated resentment of every contractor who had ever been fined by him.

He had the sheer, stubborn weight of thirty-one years of saying *no*.

He activated the Summon.

**ACTION: SUMMON PROXY.** **COST: 50% MANA.** **SUMMONING: THIRD-PARTY INSPECTOR.** **TARGET: THE NEXT AVAILABLE INDEPENDENT AUDITOR.**

The air in the lobby rippled. The blue barrier on the door flickered.

A figure stepped out of the wall. Not a monster. Not a hero.

A man in a gray suit. He held a clipboard. He wore glasses that were crooked. He looked like he hadn't slept in a week.

The figure looked at Gerald. Then at the barrier. Then at the vine-core.

"Mr. Hutchins," the figure said.

His voice was nasal.

"I am Inspector Barnaby Fisk, Independent Auditor, District 4. I have received a complaint that this facility is being illegally sealed by municipal employees."

Pendergast's face appeared on the surface of the blue barrier. It was twisted with rage.

"Fisk! Back off! This is a police matter!"

Fisk didn't look at Pendergast. He looked at Gerald.

"Mr. Hutchins, do you have a complaint?"

Gerald straightened his tie. It was a spectral tie, but he tugged it anyway.

"I do," Gerald said. "I am being denied egress from a public building by an unauthorized official. And I am being cited for a license that was revoked before I was born."

Fisk nodded. He pulled a pen from his pocket.

"Very well," Fisk said. "I am accepting the complaint. I am also issuing a Citation against the City Manager for 'Interference with a Protected Entity.' And I am issuing a Citation against the Inspector General for 'Falsification of Audit Records.'"

The blue barrier shattered.

Not from Gerald's power. From the sheer weight of Fisk's authority.

Fisk stepped through the broken door. He looked at Gerald.

"You have until dawn to appeal this citation, Mr. Hutchins. Or I will shut down the entire city block."

Gerald looked at the city block. The library. The sinkhole. The truth.

He looked at Maya. She was smiling. A terrifying, hopeful smile.

Gerald looked at Fisk.

"I don't want an appeal," Gerald said. "I want a Hearing."

Fisk's crooked glasses glinted.

"Careful, Mr. Hutchins," Fisk said. "A Hearing against the Inspector General is a one-way ticket. You either prove the System is lying, or you get deleted."

Gerald looked at the vine-core. It bloomed. A single, white flower opened on its stem.

"I've been waiting thirty-one years for this meeting," Gerald said.

He stepped out into the night.
