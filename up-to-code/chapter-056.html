Chapter 56: Signature Required

# Chapter 56

Gerald had been staring at the same field for forty minutes.

The self-inspection report format was not in his panel. It was not in any sub-menu he could find.

He had searched for "self-inspection," "internal audit," "pre-audit preparation," and "corrective action," and the only result the System offered was a line: "Self-Inspection Report (Form 7-S) available upon request to flagged Core." He had requested it.

The form had appeared. And now he was stuck on Field 14.

Field 14: Witness Signature.

The form required a licensed third-party Core to sign the self-inspection report, attesting that the flagged Core had conducted the inspection in good faith. Without that signature, the report was inadmissible. Without an admissible self-inspection report, Gerald had no way to demonstrate corrective action prior to the Inspector General's arrival.

He put his head in his hands. He could feel the specific pressure behind his eyes that meant he was about to be sick.

A notification chimed. Not the Inspector General. Not the audit countdown. Something else.

*Procedural Counter-Citation filed against Core 4409 by Core 7712 (Vexithar). Basis: Citation #4409-0034 issued without required Structural Basis Statement. Requested remedy: void Citation, apply Procedural Violation mark to Core 4409.*

Gerald read it twice. Then he sat up straight.

He had issued Citation #4409-0034 four cycles ago. It was the Citation that had debuffed Vexithar's spawn list by forty percent for six cycles. It was the Citation that had made Vexithar's dungeon collapse inward when its structural supports lost their integrity buff. It was the Citation that had killed two of Vexithar's elite golems and forced the Core to retreat from the eastern corridor.

Vexithar had appealed. The Hearing had gone badly. Gerald had won, but barely, and only because he'd managed to prove that Vexithar's load-bearing columns were undersized by three centimeters.

Now Vexithar was trying a different angle. Not appealing the substance of the Citation. Attacking its procedure.

Gerald pulled up the Citation he had filed, and he scrolled through it. Citation number.

Date.

Target Core. Violation category. Severity rating.

Debuff applied. Signature.

No Structural Basis Statement appeared, so he reviewed his other filed Citations. None of them had one.

He checked the System's documentation on Citation format. He had never looked at it carefully. He had always assumed the format was the same as his municipal citations, because the System had always accepted his municipal citation format. He had never once considered that the System might require something additional.

He found the documentation. It was buried under a submenu called "Citation Procedural Requirements," which was nested inside "Advanced Core Compliance Tools," which was nested inside "Inspector Certification Level 3." He had never unlocked Level 3. He had never known Level 3 existed.

The Structural Basis Statement was a one-page attachment that documented the physical measurements, photographic evidence, and code section references that supported the Citation. Without it, the Citation was procedurally defective, and a Core could file a Procedural Counter-Citation within thirty cycles of the original. The window had closed for most of Gerald's Citations, but Citation #4409-0034 was only four cycles old.

The window was open.

Gerald looked at the Counter-Citation's requested remedy again. Void the Citation. Apply a Procedural Violation mark to Core 4409.

A Procedural Violation mark, and he pulled up the definition.

It was a permanent entry on a Core's record. It lowered the Core's Compliance Rating by five points.

It flagged the Core as "procedurally unreliable," which meant future Citations issued by that Core would be subject to automatic review before taking effect.

Five points. Gerald's Compliance Rating was currently 78.

Five points would drop it to 73, which was still passing, but it was the kind of number that made the Inspector General's audit harder. Not because 73 was a failing score, but because it meant Gerald had a documented procedural failure on his record going into the audit.

And the audit was in two cycles now, not three. The Counter-Citation had triggered an accelerated review. The System had recalculated. The Inspector General was coming sooner.

Gerald sat on the floor of his dungeon and thought about what he was going to do.

The obvious fix was to file the missing Structural Basis Statement retroactively.

He pulled up the form. It required measurements, photographs, and code section references.

He had all of those, and he had measured Vexithar's columns. He had photographed them. He knew the code sections.

He could fill it out in ten minutes.

But the form had a field at the bottom. Witness Signature. Licensed third-party Core.

The same problem as the self-inspection report. He needed someone else to sign.

Gerald stood up, and he walked to the eastern corridor, where Vexithar's body was still embedded in his column.

The reclassified mass sat in the wall like a fossil. Mechanical.

Compliant. Part of his dungeon.

He looked at it and felt nothing, and he opened his panel and checked his list of known Cores. He found eleven.

Most of them he had Cited.

One of them was Vexithar, who was currently trying to destroy him. One of them was a small dungeon core in the park across the street that had never given him trouble but also had never spoken to him. The others were hostile, indifferent, or dead.

He checked the park Core.

Core 5521. Small.

Residential.

Had never issued a Citation. Had never been Cited. Compliance Rating 61.

Barely passing. Gerald had passed it on the street a dozen times and never noticed it.

He sent a ping. No response.

He tried again. Nothing.

He tried the dead Cores. Nothing, and he tried the hostile ones. They blocked him.

He sat back down.

The self-inspection report was still open on his panel.

Field 14 was still blank. The Counter-Citation was still pending.

The Inspector General was still coming in two cycles.

Gerald looked at the rat, and the rat was sitting on the floor three meters away, grooming its paw. It had been his first spawn. It had been with him since the beginning. It was technically a biological entity within his dungeon, which made it technically part of his dungeon's population.

He checked the System's definition of "licensed third-party Core." The definition was: "Any registered Core entity that is not the subject of the document in question."

The rat was not a Core. The rat was a spawn.

But Gerald looked at the rat. The rat looked at him.

The rat was a registered entity within his dungeon, which had a registration number. It had a status. It had a name in the System's database, which was "Entity 4409-0001."

It was not a Core. But it was registered.

Gerald opened the witness signature field and typed the registration number.

The System rejected it. "Witness must be a registered Core entity."

Gerald tried again. Same result.

He stared at the field. He stared at the rat. The rat stopped grooming and looked at him with its small black eyes.

Then he thought about something else.

The Counter-Citation had been filed by Vexithar.

Vexithar was dead. Its Core had been destroyed during the structural collapse caused by Gerald's Citation.

But the Counter-Citation was still active.

That meant the filing had been submitted before the Core died, or it meant that a dead Core could still file.

Gerald checked the filing timestamp. The Counter-Citation had been filed six hours after Vexithar's death.

A dead Core had filed a legal challenge against him, and he pulled up the filing details. The signature on the Counter-Citation was not Vexithar's Core signature. It was a proxy signature. Someone had filed on Vexithar's behalf.

He checked the proxy's registration. Core 8801.

He did not recognize the number.

He checked his records, and he had never Cited Core 8801. He had never encountered Core 8801.

It did not appear in his scan history.

Core 8801 was new. Or it was hiding.

Gerald tried to ping it. The ping was refused.

Not blocked.

Refused. Which meant the Core existed and was active but had set its privacy settings to maximum.

He looked at the Counter-Citation again. The proxy signature had a credential attached. "Licensed Compliance Advocate, Level 4."

A Compliance Advocate. Someone who represented Cores in procedural disputes. Gerald had never encountered one. He had never even known the role existed.

He checked the System's documentation on Compliance Advocates, and they were registered entities that could file and defend procedural challenges on behalf of Cores. They were not dungeon cores themselves. They were a separate class of registered entity, created by the System to handle the legal complexity of Citation disputes.

Gerald had never hired one; he had never needed one, because he had always won his Hearings on the merits. But now someone had hired one against him.

And the Advocate had found the one thing Gerald had missed. The Structural Basis Statement.

Gerald felt the pressure behind his eyes again. He breathed through it.

He had a problem with three components. One: the Counter-Citation would succeed unless he could file the missing Structural Basis Statement with a witness signature. Two: he could not find a Core willing to sign. Three: the Inspector General was coming in two cycles, and his Compliance Rating was about to drop by five points.

He looked at his dungeon, and the handrails were installed. The egress signage was up.

The fire suppression system was operational.

The ventilation was adequate. The electrical wiring was to code. The structural supports were sound.

Everything was compliant except the paperwork.

Gerald stood up. He walked to the column where Vexithar's mass was embedded.

He ran his hand along the surface, and the mass was cold.

It had been cold for four cycles.

He opened his panel. He opened the Citation file for #4409-0034. He scrolled to the bottom. The signature field was blank.

He had signed it with his Core signature. But the form required a second signature. A witness.

He had never known that. He had never known because he had never looked at the Level 3 documentation. Because he had assumed the System would tell him what it required.

The System had not told him. The System had let him file twenty-three Citations without the required attachment.

Twenty-three Citations. All of them procedurally defective. All of them vulnerable to Counter-Citations.

The window was thirty cycles. Most of his Citations were still within it.

Gerald sat down slowly, and he had built a fortress out of paperwork.

He had used the System's rules to destroy stronger enemies. He had made himself a target. The Inspector General had flagged him.

A stranger had filed a legal challenge against him.

And now he realized that every Citation he had ever issued was a house of cards.

Opening his panel, he started drafting a new document. Not a self-inspection report. Not a Structural Basis Statement.

Something else.

He was going to file a Motion to Amend.

The System's documentation said that a flagged Core could amend its own Citations within the procedural window if it could demonstrate that the omission was due to "lack of access to required documentation." Gerald had never unlocked Level 3. He could prove that. He could prove that the System had not made the requirement visible to him.

If the Motion to Amend was accepted, the Structural Basis Statement could be attached retroactively, curing the procedural defect. The Counter-Citation would fail. His Compliance Rating would not drop.

But the Motion to Amend also required a witness signature.

Gerald closed his eyes, and he thought about the rat.

He thought about the park Core. He thought about the dead Cores.

He thought about the Compliance Advocate, Core 8801, who was watching him from behind a privacy wall and waiting for him to fail.

He thought about Field 14.

Then he thought about something he had not considered before. The System's definition of "licensed third-party Core" "registered Core entity." It did not say "dungeon core." It said "Core entity."

A Compliance Advocate was a registered Core entity.

Gerald opened his panel. He searched for "Compliance Advocate" in the System directory.

Three existed in the Ashford area.

All three were Level 4. All three had the same privacy setting as Core 8801.

He pinged all three.

One responded.

"Who is this?"

Gerald typed his Core number.

"Core 4409. I need a witness signature on a Motion to Amend. I will pay whatever your standard rate is."

A pause. Then: "You're the one who cited Vexithar."

"Yes."

"Core 8801 is my colleague. I don't think I can sign against their interest."

"I'm not asking you to sign against their interest. I'm asking you to attest that I lacked access to Level 3 documentation. That's a fact. You can verify it by checking my Core certification history."

Another pause. Longer this time.

"What's your Compliance Rating?"

"Seventy-eight."

"And you're flagged by the Inspector General."

"Yes."

"Enhanced review?"

"Yes."

Another pause. Then: "What happens to my reputation if I sign for a flagged Core?"

Gerald thought about it, and he thought about what he could offer. He had no currency.

He had no reputation.

He had a dungeon that was up to code and a list of enemies that was getting longer.

He typed: "If you sign, I will include your name in the Motion to Amend. The System will see that a Level 4 Compliance Advocate attested to my documentation gap. That establishes precedent. The next time someone is cited without the proper form, they can point to your signature as proof that the requirement was not universally visible."

The pause was long this time.

Then: "Send me the form."

Gerald sent the form.

Thirty seconds later, Field 14 was filled. A signature appeared. Core 9103, Licensed Compliance Advocate, Level 4.

Gerald filed the Motion to Amend.

The System accepted it. The Structural Basis Statement attached to Citation #4409-0034. The Counter-Citation was marked "Under Review."

Gerald exhaled.

Then his panel flashed.

*Motion to Amend accepted. However: Inspector General has reviewed the amendment and issued a supplemental finding.

Core 4409 has twenty-two additional Citations with the same procedural defect.

Inspector General will address all defects during on-site audit.

Audit accelerated to one cycle.*

One cycle.

Gerald looked at the countdown. It had reset. The number was smaller now.

And then a second notification.

*Core 8801 has filed a second Counter-Citation against Core 4409. Basis: Citation #4409-0017. Same procedural defect. Requested remedy: void Citation, apply Procedural Violation mark.*

Gerald opened the second Counter-Citation. He looked at the filing timestamp. It had been filed three seconds ago.

Core 8801 was still filing. It was going to file all of them. All twenty-two.

Gerald looked at his Compliance Rating. Seventy-eight.

Twenty-two Procedural Violation marks would drop it to negative thirty-two.

He looked at the rat. The rat looked at him.

He opened his panel. He started typing a response to Core 8801. His hands were steady. His voice, if he had one, would have been flat.

"You want to do this by procedure? Fine. I know procedure better than you. Let's see how long your filing takes."
