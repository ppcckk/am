Chapter 405: Citation Accepted

# Chapter 405

The tape measure snapped back against Gerald's palm.

He didn't flinch. Pain was irrelevant if the measurement was accurate.

The Bailiff stood frozen in the center aisle of the fiction section, his sword hovering inches from a paralyzed grip, his eyes wide with a terror that transcended the immediate threat.

Gerald noted the distance between the Bailiff's shoulder blades. Twenty-two inches. Non-compliant for a standard commercial egress route, which required a minimum width of thirty-six inches for safe evacuation.

"Three inches short," Gerald muttered. He pulled a red marker from his pocket and drew a line across the Bailiff's breastplate. "Violation 4.12: Obstruction of Primary Egress. Penalty: Immediate cessation of hostile intent."

The System interface flickered in Gerald's peripheral vision.

**[Citation Accepted.]** **[New Debuff: Structural Rigidity. The target's skeletal structure is now aligned with municipal safety codes. Movement speed reduced by 90%.]**

Gerald stepped over the paralyzed man, and he needed to secure the perimeter before the next wave arrived. The dungeon beneath the library was not merely reacting; it was expanding.

The floor tiles in the history section had buckled, revealing dark, pulsing roots of mana that slithered between the shelving units.

The air smelled of ozone and wet paper.

A low growl echoed from the reference desk.

Gerald adjusted his glasses. He checked his watch. 11:42 AM.

The library closed to the public at 9:00 PM, but the internal security protocols had already failed.

The local dungeon core, now identified as *The Archivist's Lament*, was attempting to integrate the physical building into its lair.

This was a zoning violation of the highest order. It was not just a dungeon; it was an unauthorized structural modification of a public landmark.

Gerald pulled a fresh citation form from his briefcase. The paper was crisp, white, and terrifyingly ordinary.

"Mr. Hutchins," a voice called out.

He turned. A figure emerged from behind a stack of encyclopedias. It was not a monster, not exactly.

It was a construct of parchment and ink, shaped roughly like a human, with hollow eyes that glowed with a faint, sickly yellow light. It held a quill that dripped black fluid.

"You are interfering with the curation process," the construct said. Its voice sounded like pages tearing.

"I am conducting an inspection," Gerald replied. "Your entity is composed of flammable materials in a space lacking adequate fire suppression systems. Where are your extinguishers?"

The construct tilted its head.

"We do not require extinguishers. We are fire."

"Then you are a fire hazard," Gerald said.

He clicked his pen.

"I am citing you for improper storage of combustible materials. Violation 2.1.

Penalty: Combustibility increased by 15%.

"

The construct shrieked, a sound like dry leaves burning. Black smoke poured from its mouth.

Gerald stepped back, covering his mouth with a handkerchief, and he was not afraid. He was annoyed. The smoke obscured his view of the exit sign, which he had installed himself three chapters ago.

**[Warning: Visibility reduced below OSHA standards.]** **[Auto-Correct: Emergency Lighting Activated.]**

The overhead lights flickered on, harsh and white. The construct recoiled, shielding its eyes.

Gerald marched forward. He did not attack. He did not cast a spell. He pulled out a clipboard.

"Name and serial number," he demanded.

"I have no name," the construct hissed. "I am the memory of this place."

"Then I will assign you one for identification purposes. Entity 4-B.

You are now required to fill out this incident report.

Do not deviate from the form.

If you attempt to attack, I will add a charge for assault on a municipal official, which carries a mandatory three-day confinement in a null-magic zone.

"

The construct hesitated. The System rules were binding. Even a magical construct, if it registered as a 'spawn,' had to acknowledge the legal framework of the dungeon core that governed the space. And Gerald was the governor.

The construct's quill hovered over the paper. Its hand trembled.

"Write," Gerald commanded.

The construct wrote. The ink sizzled on the page.

Gerald watched the words appear. *Entity 4-B. Violation: Lack of Smoke Detectors. Sentence: Fine of 500 Mana Points.*

The construct screamed. It dissolved into a cloud of ash, leaving only the quill on the floor.

**[Citation Served.]** **[Mana Drain: 500.]** **[Construct Neutralized.]**

Gerald picked up the quill. It was still hot. He placed it in his pocket. Evidence.

He turned toward the stairs. The main entrance was blocked by a wall of thorny vines that had erupted from the lobby carpet. They pulsed with a purple energy, thick and suffocating.

The dungeon was trying to seal itself off.

Gerald frowned. He walked to the wall of vines. He ran a hand along the thorns.

They were warm. They were alive.

He pulled out his phone. He dialed the number for the Public Works Department.

"Public Works," a bored voice answered.

"It's Gerald Hutchins," he said.

"I'm at the library. I have an unauthorized vegetation installation blocking the main egress route. It's a Class C bio-hazard.

I need a removal team, and I need them now.

"

"Vegetation? Sir, this is a dungeon event. We don't handle dungeon events. You have to fight it."

"I am not fighting it," Gerald said. "I am citing it.

But the System requires a physical removal crew to validate the citation for permanent abatement.

Send a crew with industrial shears. And tell them to wear masks. This looks like nightshade.

"

"Nightshade? That's illegal to grow in city limits without a permit."

"Exactly," Gerald said. "It's a zoning violation. Send the crew. I'm citing the dungeon core for illegal cultivation."

He hung up. He looked at the vines.

He hummed a tune. It was the anthem of the Ashford Municipal Inspection Guild. It was dull, repetitive, and comforting.

The vines shuddered.

A voice boomed through the library, shaking the dust from the shelves.

"**CORE DESIGNATION: ARCHIVIST'S LAMENT. YOU VIOLATE THE PACT OF SILENCE.**"

Gerald looked up. The ceiling tiles were cracking. A massive eye, formed from swirling ink and shadow, opened in the plaster.

"The Pact of Silence?" Gerald asked. "Is that a local ordinance? I don't recall it in the municipal code."

"**IT IS THE CODE OF THE DEEP.**"

"I don't recognize the Deep," Gerald said. "I recognize the City of Ashford. And under Ashford Code Section 14, Subsection B, all public spaces must maintain a noise level below seventy decibels between the hours of 10:00 AM and 6:00 PM. Your shouting is a violation."

The eye blinked. The shaking stopped.

Gerald pulled out another citation.

"Violation 14.1: Noise Nuisance. Penalty: Sonic Dampening. All vocalizations by this Core are reduced by 50% for twenty-four hours."

He slapped the citation onto the floor. The paper burned with blue light, then vanished.

The eye narrowed. A wave of force pushed Gerald back, sliding him across the polished floor. He skidded to a halt near the circulation desk.

"Physical aggression against an inspector," Gerald said, brushing dust off his suit. "That's a felony in Ashford. That's not just a citation. That's an arrest warrant."

The System interface flashed red.

**[Alert: Hostile Action Detected.]** **[Legal Countermeasure Available: Summon Municipal Guard.]** **[Cost: 10,000 Mana Points. Current Mana: 4,500.]**

Gerald stared at the number. He was short.

He couldn't summon the Guard. He couldn't overpower the Core.

The Archivist's Lament was a mid-tier dungeon, ancient and powerful, which had centuries of accumulated knowledge, magic, and malice.

Gerald had a tape measure and a clipboard.

But Gerald had one advantage the Core did not understand.

The Core operated on the logic of narrative. It wanted a hero. It wanted a struggle. It wanted the dramatic tension of a dungeon crawl.

Gerald operated on the logic of bureaucracy, and he looked at the eye. He looked at the blocked exit. He looked at his mana count.

He had to change the rules.

He walked to the circulation desk. He opened the bottom drawer. It was empty, save for a single, dusty ledger. The library's old checkout log.

Gerald took the ledger. He opened it. The pages were brittle.

He pulled out his pen. He wrote.

He didn't write a spell. He didn't write a attack command.

He wrote a complaint.

**[System Notice: Formal Grievance Filed.]** **[Target: Archivist's Lament.]** **[Allegation: Harassment of Municipal Official.]** **[Request: Temporary Suspension of Hostile Spawning.]**

The System paused, which was new. Gerald had never tried to file a grievance. He had only issued citations.

Citations were punishments. Grievances were requests for adjudication.

The eye watched him. The ink swirled.

"**YOU CANNOT APPEAL TO THE VOID.**"

"I am not appealing," Gerald said. "I am filing a complaint with the Inspector General. The System's overarching administrative body. If the Inspector General finds that you are impeding a municipal inspection, your Compliance Rating will be reassessed immediately."

The eye flickered. Fear?

No. Calculation.

The Archivist's Lament knew what a low Compliance Rating meant. It meant debuffs. It meant weaker spawns. It meant that adventurers could loot it with ease.

Gerald was threatening its existence. Not with a sword. With a form.

"**STOP,**" the eye boomed, though the volume was already lower, dampened by the previous citation. "**WHAT DO YOU WANT?**"

Gerald looked up. His face was blank.

"I want the exit cleared," Gerald said.

"I want the vines removed. And I want a permit for this dungeon's existence. You don't have one.

You're operating without a license.

That's a fine of one million mana points, payable in monthly installments.

"

"**IMPOSSIBLE.**"

"Then I escalate," Gerald said. He held up the ledger. "I have already copied this complaint and sent it to the Inspector General's inbox. The audit begins in ten minutes."

The eye widened. The ink swirled faster. The library shook.

But the vines remained.

Gerald checked his watch. 11:55 AM. Five minutes until the audit.

He needed to buy time. He needed to keep the Core focused on him, not on the spawning of monsters.

He walked toward the eye.

"You have five minutes to submit your defense," Gerald said.

"You must appear in person. The hearing is in the basement. Sub-basement level two.

The place where I live.

"

The eye seemed to shrink.

"**WHERE?**"

"Sub-basement," Gerald repeated.

"I'll be there.

Bring your paperwork. Or don't.

Ignoring a summons is a violation of the Inspector General's protocol. That's an automatic disqualification.

"

The eye vanished. The shaking stopped. The vines retracted, slowly, reluctantly.

Gerald exhaled. His hands were shaking. He put them in his pockets so no one could see.

He had won. For now.

But he knew the cost, and he had filed a grievance. This meant the Inspector General was now aware of him.

The audit would be rigorous.

They would check everything. His ventilation. His fire codes.

His electrical grounding.

And his mana was running low, and he looked at the ledger. He had written one line in the 'Defense' column, anticipating the Core's response.

*Defense: The dungeon serves a public service by preserving knowledge.*

Gerald had prepared a rebuttal. *Rebuttal: Preservation of knowledge does not excuse the obstruction of physical safety routes.*

He smiled, a small, tight expression.

He picked up his briefcase. He walked to the stairs. The vines had cleared the way.

He descended into the dark, and the library above was silent. The dust settled.

In the sub-basement, Gerald's dungeon hummed. The rat from Chapter One sat on a handrail, gnawing on a piece of drywall.

Gerald sat at his desk. He pulled out a new form. Form 22-C: *Request for Temporary Variance.*

He needed a variance. He needed to lower the ceiling height in the main hall to fit the standard municipal corridor width. The Core's architecture was too high, too open, too vulnerable to aerial attacks. By lowering the ceiling, he could create a choke point.

He drawed.

The System interface pinged.

**[Notice: Inspector General Audit Scheduled.]** **[Time: 12:00 PM.]** **[Subject: Gerald Hutchins.]** **[Status: Pending.]**

Gerald stared at the clock. 11:58.

Two minutes, and he looked at his dungeon. It was a mess.

Half-finished handrails.

A water leak in the corner. A single light bulb flickering in the storage room.

It was 47 Compliance.

The Inspector General would tear him apart.

Gerald looked at the rat.

"Hey," Gerald said. "Do you know any lawyers?"

The rat looked at him. It squeaked.

"Didn't think so," Gerald said.

He picked up his pen. He had two minutes to fix his own house before the ultimate auditor arrived. And he had no one to help him.

The door to the sub-basement creaked open.

Gerald turned, and a man stood in the doorway. He wore a suit that was too expensive for Ashford. He held a briefcase that pulsed with dark, chaotic energy.

"Gerald Hutchins," the man said. His voice was smooth, oily. "My name is Silas Vane. I represent the Consortium of Unlicensed Dungeons."

Gerald stood up.

"We're here to offer you a settlement," Vane said. "Drop the citations. Withdraw the grievance. And we'll pay you ten million mana points."

Gerald looked at the man. He looked at the briefcase. He looked at the clock.

11:59.

"No," Gerald said.

Vane smiled. It was not a nice smile.

"Then you have an appointment with hell," Vane said. "And I'm your guide."

Vane opened the briefcase. Inside, there was no paper. There was a heart. A pulsing, black heart that beat with the rhythm of a dying star.

**[New Mechanic Unlocked: Dark Market Contract.]** **[Warning: External Legal Intervention Detected.]**

Gerald's breath caught, and the audit was here. And it wasn't alone.
