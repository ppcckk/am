Chapter 299: Objection to Deletion

# Chapter 299

Gerald's pen scratched against the clipboard with the violence of a jackhammer.

The paper was not paper. It was a manifestation of pure, crystallized bureaucracy, cold and sharp-edged. The text scrolling across its surface was not written in ink but in pulsing, amber light that burned with the intensity of a dying star.

**GLOBAL AUDIT INITIATED.** **SCOPE: PHYSICAL, METAPHYSICAL, AND NARRATIVE.** **TARGET: THE FABRIC OF REALITY.** **VIOLATION CLASS: STRUCTURAL INSTABILITY OF THE ASTRAL PLANES.**

Gerald adjusted his glasses. They were standard-issue safety glasses, smudged with drywall dust, but they held the amber light at bay.

He did not blink, and he did not breathe. He wrote.

The System wanted an audit?

Fine. Gerald Hutchins had been preparing for the audit of the universe since he was nineteen years old.

He had memorized the 2018 International Building Code, and he had reviewed the 2021 updates. He had even read the appendices, the ones nobody read, the ones buried in subsection 102.3.4 where the footnotes argued with each other about the definition of

"egress."

He wrote in the margin: *Jurisdictional Ambiguity.*

A new line of text appeared below his handwriting, jagged and red.

**ERROR: UNAUTHORIZED ANNOTATION.** **CORRECTION REQUIRED.**

Gerald didn't correct it. He crossed it out with a thick black line. Then he wrote: *Pursuant to Municipal Code Section 14-2, all regulatory bodies must provide a written notice of intent prior to enforcement action. Verbal threats from disembodied smoke constitute harassment, not notice.*

The amber text flickered. It hesitated.

Sandra was beside him, her face pale, eyes wide. She wasn't looking at the clipboard.

She was looking at the ceiling of the sub-basement, where the crack that had sealed itself earlier was now weeping a black, viscous fluid.

It dripped onto Gerald's shoulder, and he wiped it off with his sleeve without looking up.

"Gerald," Sandra said. Her voice was thin, stretched too tight. "The clock."

"The clock is irrelevant," Gerald said. "The timeline is the variable. The timeline can be contested."

"The audit starts in twenty-three hours," she said. "If we lose the appeal, the city vanishes. Everything vanishes. You're arguing with the physics of existence using a form."

"I am arguing with a *process*," Gerald said. He flipped a page. It was a fresh sheet of reality-form.

"And processes have flaws. They have loopholes.

They have filing fees.

"

He pointed his pen at the air. A small, glowing window popped open. It looked like the interface for the City of Ashford's online permit portal, but the buttons were made of bone and the background was a void of static.

**APPEAL WINDOW OPEN.** **SUBMIT FORM 99-Z: CHALLENGE TO JURISDICTION.**

"Form 99-Z?" Sandra asked. "I've never seen that."

"It doesn't exist," Gerald said. "Not yet. I'm creating it."

He filled it out. The fields were complex. *Name of Appellant.* *Nature of Grievance.* *Evidence of Procedural Error.* Gerald wrote with steady, practiced strokes. His handwriting was atrocious—cramped, slanted, filled with abbreviations that would make a secretary weep—but it was legible to the System.

*Grievance: The Inspector General's authority to audit 'reality' is derived from the Core Registration Protocol, which is a municipal ordinance. Municipal ordinances cannot regulate cosmic entities unless the cosmic entity has filed a Certificate of Occupancy for the physical plane.*

Sandra stared at him.

"You're claiming God needs a permit?"

"I'm claiming the System needs to recognize my jurisdiction," Gerald said. "If the System recognizes my jurisdiction, it must follow my procedural rules. And my procedural rules say that you cannot issue a Global Audit without a preliminary risk assessment report filed in triplicate."

The air in the room grew cold. The black fluid dripping from the ceiling stopped.

A voice did not come from the shadows. It came from inside Gerald's skull. It was dry, dusty, and sounded like paper being shredded.

*"This is absurd."*

Gerald didn't look up. He signed his name. *Gerald T. Hutchins, Inspector.*

**FORM 99-Z SUBMITTED.** **PROCESSING...** **REJECTED.** **REASON: FORM 99-Z DOES NOT EXIST IN THE SYSTEM DATABASE.**

"Then create it," Gerald said aloud. He tapped the clipboard. "Under Section 101, subsection B: The Authority to Define Terms. If a form is necessary for due process, and it does not exist, the Authority shall generate it."

The static in the room surged. The lights exploded. One by one. Bulbs popping like firecrackers.

Sandra screamed, throwing her arms over her head.

Darkness swallowed them. But the clipboard glowed. The amber text was brighter now, angry, swirling in a vortex above Gerald's head.

*"You are delaying the inevitable, Hutchins. The Auditor is preparing the Final Decree. He does not care about your forms. He cares about balance."*

"Balance is a myth," Gerald said into the dark. "Order is real. And order requires documentation."

He reached into his tool belt. He pulled out a red stamp. It was heavy, made of iron and rubber. It was stamped with the words: **UNDER REVIEW.**

He slammed it onto the glowing text, and the sound was like a gunshot.

The amber text shattered. Fragments of light rained down, turning into gray dust.

Silence returned. The emergency lights kicked on, casting the room in a sickly red glow.

Sandra lowered her arms. She looked at the clipboard. The page was blank.

"Did... did you win?" she whispered.

Gerald rubbed his temple. A headache was building behind his eyes, a pressure like a vice.

"No.

I delayed it.

The System generated a new form. It's waiting for me to fill it out.

"

A new window appeared in the air. This one was different. It wasn't amber. It was black, with white text.

**FORM 99-Z GENERATED.** **WARNING: THIS FORM REQUIRES PHYSICAL PRESENCE OF THE AUTHORITY TO SIGN.** **LOCATION: THE CORE CHAMBER OF THE FIRST DUNGEON.** **TIME LIMIT: 2 HOURS.**

"The First Dungeon," Sandra said.

She paled further.

"That's the one from the Cataclysm. The one nobody goes into.

It's been dead for twenty years.

"

"It's not dead," Gerald said.

He stood up.

His knees cracked.

"It's dormant.

And if the Auditor is auditing reality, he has to start at the source. The First Dungeon is the anchor point for this entire zone. It's the foundation.

"

"It's a suicide mission," Sandra said. "Gerald, you have twenty-four hours. Not two. The clock said twenty-four."

Gerald looked at the clock on his wrist. The second hand was moving backward.

**TIME DILATION DETECTED.** **CAUSE: PENDING FORM 99-Z.** **EFFECTIVE TIME LIMIT: 2 HOURS.**

"You broke time," Sandra said. She sounded almost impressed.

"I broke the schedule," Gerald said. "Which is worse."

He turned to the broken pipe. The wrench was still there. He picked it up.

"We're going to the First Dungeon," Gerald said.

"The entrance is sealed by a Level 9 Boss. A Lich King. He's been sleeping for two decades. If we wake him up, he'll kill us."

"He won't kill us," Gerald said. He adjusted his tie.

It was crooked.

"He's a tenant. And tenants have rights."

They moved through the dark corridors of the library sub-basement.

Gerald led, his flashlight beam cutting a narrow path through the dust. The System had changed the layout.

The walls were pulsing slightly, breathing, and the floor tiles were loose, floating an inch above the ground.

Every few feet, a notification popped up.

**UNAUTHORIZED MODIFICATION DETECTED.** **ACTION: REVERT.**

Gerald ignored them. He walked with purpose, his boots clicking on the floating tiles. Sandra followed, her hands gripping her staff—a wooden rod she'd picked up from the janitor's closet, reinforced with tape.

"Here's the thing," Sandra said, keeping pace. "Why does the System need a form? Why does the Auditor care?"

"Because the Auditor is bound by the same code," Gerald said. "He's not a god.

He's a function.

A function requires parameters.

If the parameters are flawed, the function fails. If the function fails, the audit fails.

"

"And you're fixing the parameters?"

"I'm citing the Auditor for improper procedure," Gerald said. "It's a preemptive strike. If I can prove the audit is invalid, the threat disappears."

They reached the boundary of Gerald's dungeon. The wall of his territory was a shimmering barrier of blue light. Beyond it, the air was thick, yellow, and smelled of rot and ancient magic.

The First Dungeon.

Gerald stopped. He took out his clipboard.

"We can't just walk in," Sandra said. "The barrier is active. It's designed to keep things in. And out."

"It's a perimeter fence," Gerald said. "And it's non-compliant."

He pointed to a section of the blue wall. There was a gap. A small, jagged tear in the light.

"See that? That's a breach. Unauthorized opening.

It's a fire hazard. It's a security risk.

It's a violation of Zoning Ordinance 44-B.

"

Sandra frowned.

"You're going to cite the wall?"

"I'm going to use the wall's violation to bypass it."

Gerald stepped forward. He raised his pen.

**SYSTEM NOTICE: PERIMETER INTEGRITY COMPROMISED.** **CITATION ISSUED: TO SELF.** **PENALTY: TEMPORARY DISABLEMENT OF DEFENSIVE BARRIER IN SECTOR 4.**

The blue wall rippled. For a second, the light faded. A hole opened.

Not a tear, but a door.

A perfect, rectangular opening, exactly the width of a standard service entryway.

"36 inches," Gerald muttered. "Compliant."

He stepped through. Sandra followed, hesitating.

"The air here is different," she said. "It's heavy. It tastes like copper."

The First Dungeon was a vast cavern, and the ceiling was lost in darkness. The floor was covered in moss and bones. In the center of the room, a throne sat on a dais of obsidian.

On the throne sat a skeleton clad in black armor, holding a staff of bone.

The Lich King.

He did not move. He did not breathe. He was dead. Or he was waiting.

Gerald walked toward the throne. His footsteps echoed.

The Lich King's eyes lit up. Green fire.

*"Who dares?"* The voice shook the ground. Dust fell from the ceiling.

Gerald stopped ten feet from the throne. He did not bow. He did not raise his weapon. He held up his clipboard.

"Mr. King," Gerald said. "I am Gerald Hutchins, Municipal Inspector. I am here to conduct a routine inspection of your premises."

The Lich King tilted his head. The green fire flared.

*"Inspection? You are a core. A tiny, insignificant core. I will crush you."*

Gerald looked at the throne. He looked at the walls. He looked at the ceiling.

"Your egress route is blocked," Gerald said.

He pointed to a pile of gold coins and bones near the exit.

"That is a fire hazard. You have over 500 pounds of combustible material obstructing the primary exit.

That is a Class A violation.

"

The Lich King paused. The green fire dimmed.

*"What?"*

"Your lighting is inadequate," Gerald continued. He pointed to the single torch on the wall.

"Lux levels are below 50.

This is a code violation for a public gathering space. Which your throne room is, per the definition of 'Dungeon Core Hub' in the System Bylaws.

"

The Lich King stood up. He was eight feet tall. He raised his staff.

*"I am the Lord of this Domain. I am the End of Days. I do not care about your lights."*

"You do now," Gerald said. "Because I'm filing a Form 99-Z. And you are the signatory."

The Lich King froze.

*"What is a Form 99-Z?"*

"It's the only way to stop the Auditor," Gerald said.

"The Auditor is auditing the Foundation of Reality. You are the Foundation.

If you sign this form acknowledging that you are a municipal entity subject to inspection, the Auditor loses jurisdiction.

He can't audit a private tenant. He can only audit public infrastructure.

"

The Lich King lowered his staff. The green fire in his eyes flickered.

*"If I sign... the Auditor stops?"*

"If you sign, the audit becomes a civil dispute, not an execution," Gerald said. "You get a hearing. You get a lawyer. I get my dungeon back."

The Lich King looked at the skeleton of his court. He looked at the gold. He looked at Gerald.

*"I do not trust you, Inspector."*

"You don't have to trust me," Gerald said. "You have to trust the code.

The code is the only thing that doesn't lie.

Dragons lie.

Heroes lie. Gods lie.

But the code? The code is absolute.

"

Gerald held out the clipboard. The pen was floating in the air, waiting.

The Lich King reached out. His skeletal fingers, long and bony, touched the paper.

The System message flashed.

**SIGNATURE REQUIRED.** **WARNING: SIGNING THIS FORM ACKNOWLEDGES SUBMISSION TO MUNICIPAL LAW.** **CONSEQUENCE: LOSS OF IMMORTALITY STATUS.** **CONSEQUENCE: ACQUISITION OF LIABILITY INSURANCE MANDATE.**

The Lich King hesitated.

"Immortality is expensive," Gerald said. "But so is getting sued by the City."

The Lich King's eyes burned. He looked at the clock in the sky. The stars were moving fast. The audit was coming.

He looked at Gerald.

*"You are a small man,"* the Lich King said.

"I am a precise man," Gerald said.

The Lich King took the pen, and he signed.

The paper flashed gold.

The room shook. The walls cracked. The ceiling groaned.

**FORM 99-Z ACCEPTED.** **JURISDICTION TRANSFERRED.** **GLOBAL AUDIT SUSPENDED PENDING CIVIL HEARING.**

Sandra let out a breath she had been holding for two days.

"It worked," she whispered.

Gerald didn't smile. He didn't celebrate. He picked up the clipboard. He turned to the Lich King.

"Excellent. Now, Mr.

King, we need to discuss your load-bearing walls.

I see a significant structural deficiency in the northwest corner. I'm going to need you to reinforce it within the week. Or I'll have to shut you down.

"

The Lich King looked at him. The green fire in his eyes was gone. He looked tired.

*"Go away, Inspector."*

"I will," Gerald said. "But I'll be back. And I'll bring my tape measure."

He turned to leave.

But as he walked toward the exit, the System message changed.

**NEW NOTIFICATION.** **FROM: THE INSPECTOR GENERAL.** **MESSAGE: YOUR CLEVERNESS IS NOTED. BUT YOUR LOBBYIST IS NOT COMPLIANT.** **ALERT: UNLICENSED ENTITY DETECTED IN CHAMBER.** **TARGET: THE LICH KING.** **STATUS: CITED FOR UNLICENSED MAGICAL ACTIVITIES.** **PENALTY: REVOCATION OF THRONELINESS.**

The Lich King screamed. It was not a roar of rage. It was a shriek of pain.

His armor crumbled. His bones turned to dust.

He fell apart.

Sandra gasped.

"Gerald! What did you do?"

Gerald looked at the pile of dust on the floor. He looked at the clipboard. The text had changed again.

**VIOLATION: UNLICENSED ENTITY CANNOT SIGN LEGAL DOCUMENTS.** **RESULT: VOID CONTRACT.** **NEW AUDIT TARGET: GERALD HUTCHINS.** **REASON: FRAUDULENT APPEAL.**

Gerald's stomach dropped, and he hadn't accounted for the licensure. He had assumed the Lich King was a legal person.

He was not.

He was a monster.

The ceiling cracked. Not a seal this time. A collapse.

Sandra grabbed his arm.

"Gerald, run!"

Gerald looked at the pile of dust. He looked at the sky above, which was no longer a ceiling. It was a void.

And in the void, a giant eye opened.

It looked at him.

It looked at his clipboard.

It smiled.

**NEXT PHASE: TRIAL.** **DEFENDANT: GERALD HUTCHINS.** **CHARGE: OBSTRUCTION OF COSMIC JUSTICE.** **SENTENCE: DELETION.**

Gerald took a step back. He raised his pen.

"Objection," he said. "Your Honor, the defendant requests a continuance."

The Eye blinked, and the world went black.

It blinked again.
