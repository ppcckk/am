Chapter 120: Code Zero Self Defense

# Chapter 120

The debt was not a number. It was a weight.

Gerald felt it settle into his foundation, a cold, heavy stone resting directly on top of his Core. The System interface flickered in his peripheral vision, red text pulsing with the rhythm of a failing heart.

*Asset Liquidation Notice.* *Outstanding Balance: 14,000 Compliance Points.* *Grace Period: 0.* *Collector: Municipal Dragon (Level 45).*

Gerald did not panic. Panic was inefficient. Panic consumed oxygen, and his oxygen reserves were already critically low due to the ventilation violation.

He measured the room.

Four thousand square feet had become four hundred. The walls were pressed in, the ceiling low enough that he had to think about his own height to avoid hitting the light fixtures. The air was stale, thick with the smell of wet concrete and old fear.

The dragon stood in the entryway, its head scraping the top of the doorframe. Scales the color of rusted iron.

Eyes like yellow headlights, which did not roar. It exhaled, a cloud of smoke that curled around the exposed rebar of the broken door.

Gerald's spawn list was empty.

The rat was gone. The mold spores had been crushed by the compression.

He had no minions, and he had no traps. He had three hundred square feet of non-compliant space and a dragon that wanted to take his house.

He checked the code.

*Code 7.4.1: Asset Seizure.* *Condition: Core fails to meet minimum compliance thresholds for three consecutive audits.* *Exception: If the Core can demonstrate that the debt was incurred due to external structural tampering, the liability is transferred to the tampering party.*

Gerald looked at the dragon. Then he looked at the door.

The door was not broken. It was sheared.

Clean metal edges, twisted by kinetic force, which was not wear and tear.

This was impact damage.

The figure in the rags. The one who had pushed him back.

Gerald pulled up the incident log. He scrolled back three days. Then two days. Then six hours.

There.

A timestamp. A record of unauthorized entry. A violation of *Code 12.3: Unauthorized Access to Private Core Infrastructure.*

The figure hadn't just saved him. It had committed a felony against the System.

But the debt was still his. The liquidation protocol was active. The dragon was moving forward, one clawed foot stepping onto the threshold.

Gerald needed to buy time. He needed to shift the liability.

He opened his Core menu. His fingers, which were not fingers but a mental extension of his will, moved with practiced speed. He navigated to the *Legal Defense* tab. It was buried under *Appeals*, which was buried under *Penalties*, which was buried under *System Settings*.

He selected *File Motion for Third-Party Liability Transfer.*

The System hesitated.

*Insufficient Evidence.*

Gerald focused on the door. The twisted hinge. The scuff marks on the floor. The residual magical signature left by the figure—a faint, purple static that the System logged as

"Anomalous Energy Source."

He couldn't name the figure. He didn't know what it was. But he could name the violation.

He typed the citation.

*Violation 1: Breach of Perimeter.* *Violation 2: Destruction of Structural Integrity (Load-Bearing Entryway).* *Violation 3: Unauthorized Presence of Class-A Entity within Core Boundary.*

He attached the incident log. He attached the video feed from his internal sensors, showing the figure's hand pushing him, showing the dragon arriving immediately after.

*Submit.*

*Processing...*

*Error: Citation cannot be filed against an unknown entity. Target must be registered.*

Gerald stared at the error. The dragon took another step. The floorboards creaked, not from weight, but from the sheer density of the beast.

He needed a name. He needed a registration ID.

The dragon paused. It tilted its head, sniffing the air again. It was looking for the source of the disturbance. It was looking for the debtor.

Gerald looked at the dragon's nameplate.

*Ashford Municipal Dragon.* *Registered Asset.* *Status: Here to Collect.*

"Asset."

Gerald froze.

The dragon wasn't an invader. It was property. It was registered.

And if it was registered, it had an owner.

Who owned the dragon?

The System didn't say. The System rarely said who owned what, preferring to treat dungeon cores as autonomous entities. But the tag said *Registered Asset.*

Gerald expanded the metadata.

*Owner: City of Ashford, Department of Infrastructure.*

The breath left his lungs in a single, sharp burst.

The City owned the dragon, and the City had built the library. The City had laid the foundation. The City had registered the sub-basement as a *Core Zone* twenty years ago, before Gerald was even born, before the System had fully manifested.

The debt wasn't just his. The infrastructure was public.

He opened the *Public Works* sub-menu. It was locked. *Access Denied: Core Tier 1.*

Gerald grit his teeth. He couldn't access public works. He wasn't an employee anymore.

He was a dungeon. A private entity.

But he had a citation.

If he cited the dragon for illegal occupation of a private core, the dragon would have to leave. But the dragon wasn't occupying it. It was *collecting* it. The debt was the key that opened the door.

He needed to invalidate the debt.

*Code 7.4.1.* *Liability transferred to tampering party.*

The tampering party was the figure. The figure was unregistered.

Unless.

Gerald looked at the scuff marks again. The purple static.

He recognized it. Not from a dungeon. From his old job.

Arc welding.

Not just any arc welding. *Flux-cored arc welding.* A specific industrial process used for high-strength steel repairs. Used only in municipal bridges and high-rise foundations.

The figure wasn't a monster. It was a crew member. A repairman.

And if it was a repairman, it had a work order.

Gerald pulled up the *Library Maintenance Logs.* He hadn't looked at them because he had been too busy measuring his own walls, but the logs were still there, buried in the system's archival memory.

*Date: Yesterday.* *Task: Emergency Structural Reinforcement, Library Sub-Basement.* *Technician: Unit 4.* *Authorization: Pending.*

Pending, and the work order was pending. That meant it wasn't approved. That meant the repair was illegal.

But wait.

If the repair was illegal, the damage was the result of an illegal act.

And if the damage was the result of an illegal act, the resulting debt...

Gerald's mind raced. He had to calculate this like a variance report.

*Hypothesis:* The dragon is here to collect on the debt caused by the poor compliance rating. *Counter-Hypothesis:* The compliance rating dropped because the structure was weakened by the emergency repair. *Therefore:* The repair caused the drop in compliance. *Therefore:* The repair caused the debt. *Therefore:* The owner of the repair crew is liable for the debt.

The owner of the repair crew was the City.

Gerald could sue the City, and he couldn't sue the City. He was a dungeon. The City was his landlord, in a sense. But he could file a *Compliance Dispute.*

He opened the *Dispute Resolution* interface.

*Select Category: Structural Damage Liability.*

The System offered three options: 1. *Self-Reported Damage.* (No penalty, but no credit.) 2. *Third-Party Damage.* (Requires proof of identity.) 3. *Municipal Infrastructure Conflict.* (Requires Core Tier 3.)

He was Tier 1. He couldn't choose option 3.

He had to choose option 2, and he had to identify the third party.

He looked at the dragon. The dragon was waiting. It was patient. It was a machine of collection.

Gerald looked at the purple static, and he zoomed in on the static. He analyzed the spectral signature.

*Flux-Cored Arc Welding.* *Electrode Type: E71T-1.* *Standard Usage: Municipal Bridge Repairs.*

Gerald filed the citation.

*Target: Unknown Entity (Unit 4).* *Violation: Unauthorized Structural Modification of Private Core.* *Claim: Debt Liability Transfer.*

*Submit.*

*Processing...*

*Error: Target not found in Core Network.*

The System didn't know Unit 4. The System didn't track human contractors. It tracked dungeon cores.

Gerald slammed his mental fist against the interface. The feedback loop stung, a sharp electric zap that rattled his Core.

The dragon took another step. The floor cracked. A fissure ran from the threshold to the center of the room.

If the floor cracked further, his structural integrity rating would drop below 20. He would be condemned. The dragon would eat him alive and take the land.

He needed a loophole, and he looked at the *Citation* he had just drafted. It was floating in the void, unsigned, unverified.

He looked at the dragon.

*Ashford Municipal Dragon.* *Registered Asset.*

If the dragon was an asset, it had a serial number.

Gerald scanned the dragon's chest. Scales. Bone. Muscle.

And there, embedded in the collar of rusted iron it wore around its neck, was a plate.

*ID: DR-492-B.*

Gerald copied the ID, and he went back to the *Citation* form.

*Target:* He couldn't cite the dragon. The dragon was the collector.

But he could cite the *owner* of the dragon.

He tried to enter *City of Ashford* in the target field.

*Error: Invalid Entity Type.*

He tried *Department of Infrastructure.*

*Error: Invalid Entity Type.*

The System only recognized dungeon cores as legal entities. Humans were NPCs. They had no legal standing in the Core Network.

Gerald felt the pressure building in his chest. The debt was growing. The dragon was raising a claw.

He had one move left, and he could not sue the human. He could not sue the city.

But he could cite the *infrastructure itself.*

The library. The building.

The building was not a Core. But it was *connected* to the Core. It was the shell.

*Code 1.1: Integrated Systems.*

If the building damaged the Core, the building was liable.

Gerald opened a new citation.

*Target: Ashford Municipal Library.* *Type: Structural Entity.* *Violation: Negligent Maintenance leading to Core Instability.* *Severity: Critical.*

He attached the photos of the crack. The twisted hinge. The pending work order.

*Submit.*

*Processing...*

*Warning: Target 'Ashford Municipal Library' is not a registered Core. Citation will not apply debuffs.*

Gerald gritted his teeth.

*But does it apply the liability transfer?*

The text at the bottom of the screen flickered.

*Liability Transfer: Conditional.* *Condition: The cited entity must be part of a Shared Infrastructure Grid.*

Was the library part of a Shared Grid?

Yes.

The water supply. The electrical grid. The sewage system.

All shared.

*Liability Transfer: Active.*

The red text on the debt notice paused. The numbers stopped climbing.

The dragon stopped.

It looked at Gerald. For the first time, it seemed confused. Its head tilted to the side, the claws hovering inches from Gerald's Core housing.

The System interface flashed.

*Debt Disputed.* *Audit Suspended.* *Pending Review: 48 Hours.*

The dragon huffed, a cloud of gray smoke that smelled of sulfur and wet dog. It turned, slowly, and walked back up the stairs.

The door slammed shut.

The lock engaged.

Gerald slumped against the wall. He was still small. He was still cramped. His Compliance Rating was 30/100, hovering just above the condemnation threshold.

He had bought forty-eight hours.

But the debt was still there. And the City knew he had cited them.

A new message pinged.

*Incoming Transmission.* *Sender: City of Ashford, Department of Infrastructure.* *Message: Your appeal is noted. Expect an inspector.*

Gerald looked at the message.

An inspector.

Not a dragon. An inspector.

A human. Or a Core.

Gerald knew what an inspector looked like. He knew how they thought. They looked for the one thing you missed.

He scanned his dungeon. The walls were sweating. The ventilation was dead. The egress signs were broken.

He had cited the library for negligence. Now the library would send someone to inspect him. To find a reason to cancel the appeal. To find a violation so severe that the liability transfer would be voided.

Gerald measured the room again, and he needed to fix the ventilation. He needed to fix the egress. He had forty-eight hours to make a 400-square-foot hole in the ground look like a code-compliant fortress.

He opened his build menu.

*Cost: 500 Mana.*

He had 12 Mana.

He looked at the dragon's footprints in the floor. Deep, clawed gouges in the concrete.

He looked at the twisted hinge, and he looked at the pending work order.

*Unit 4.*

If he could find Unit 4, he could find the evidence. The evidence would prove the repair was authorized. If the repair was authorized, the damage wasn't negligent. It was necessary.

If it was necessary, the City couldn't cancel the appeal.

Gerald crawled toward the door. His body was weak, his form unstable. He pressed his hand against the cold metal of the library door.

He could feel the vibration of footsteps above.

Not heavy. Light.

Fast.

Someone was running through the library, and the dragon had left. But someone else had stayed.

The door rattled.

Gerald pulled up his map. The library floor above was grayed out. *Out of Range.*

But the sensor array in the door hinge was active, and he listened.

Voices. Shouting.

*"It's in the basement! The Core is active!"*

*"Don't touch the door! It's sealed!"*

*"We have to break it in! The audit starts in ten minutes!"*

Gerald's blood ran cold.

The audit didn't start in forty-eight hours.

The *appeal* review started in forty-eight hours.

The *audit* was starting now.

And they were coming for him, and he looked at the citation he had filed against the library. It was still pending. It offered no protection against physical entry.

He looked at the dragon's footprints.

He looked at the crack in the floor.

He had ten minutes.

He had to make this room compliant. Or he had to make sure they never got in.

Gerald closed his eyes. He reached for the one rule he knew best. The rule that applied when all others failed.

*Code 0.0: Self-Defense of Structure.*

He weaved the concrete.
