Chapter 294: Bureaucratic Loop

# Chapter 294

The clipboard was already in his hand.

It was not a dungeon artifact. It was a standard-issue City of Ashford clipboard, laminated and yellowed at the corners, which Gerald had retrieved from his inventory three chapters ago and never put down.

The air in the sub-basement tasted like ozone and old paper.

Sandra was gone. The shadow step had taken her to the audit plane, a metaphysical space where the Inspector General kept its records.

Gerald could not see her, and he could not track her location. He could only wait for the System notification that would confirm she had stolen the citation for Core 404, or that she had been deleted from existence.

But the notification did not come.

Instead, the floor beneath Gerald's core shifted.

**System Alert: External Audit Request Received.** **Source: The Ashford Municipal Planning Committee.** **Reason: Unregistered Structural Modification.**

Gerald stared at the floating blue text. It hovered over his central chamber, pulsing with a rhythmic, bureaucratic orange.

He did not panic. Gerald Hutchins did not panic. He filed.

He tapped the notification.

"Access details," he said aloud.

The menu expanded.

**Requester: Deputy Director H. Vance.** **Location: Surface Level, Library Entrance.** **Status: Pending Inspection.** **Time until Entry: 4 minutes.**

Four minutes.

The dark energy from Core 404's counter-attack was still pressing against the western wall. The slurry Gerald had mixed was hardening, but it was not enough. The wall was porous. The shadows were leaking through.

And now, a city inspector was coming down the stairs to check if the library's foundation was compliant with zoning laws.

Gerald looked at his spawn list.

Three mold spores. One confused rat. One shadow thief currently in a different dimension.

He had no combatants. He had no barriers. He had a half-built wall and a clipboard.

He moved toward the surface access shaft, and the shaft was a vertical column of rough-hewn stone, barely three feet wide. It violated egress codes for public access. It violated fire safety regulations for underground structures. It violated everything.

Gerald did not care about his own violations. He cared about Vance's.

He pulled up the System's external interface.

It was clunky, and the System was designed for dungeon cores, not municipal employees. But it had hooks.

Every building in Ashford had a digital footprint.

The library had a footprint.

Gerald typed.

He did not use mana. He used a keyboard that manifested from the air, clicking away with the speed of a man who had spent thirty years entering data into Excel spreadsheets.

**Form 11-C: Emergency Stop Work Order.** **Reason: Immediate Hazard to Public Safety.** **Citation Code: 7.4.2 (Unpermitted Excavation).** **Citation Code: 8.1.1 (Inadequate Ventilation).** **Citation Code: 9.3.4 (Structural Instability).**

He attached the current status of the dungeon.

The System read his intent.

**System Error: You cannot issue a Stop Work Order to a public entity.**

Gerald paused. He smiled. It was a thin, dry expression.

"Correct," he muttered. "I cannot cite them. I am not a city employee anymore. I am a core."

But he could cite the *building*, and the library was his dungeon. He owned it. He was the landlord.

If the landlord cites the tenant, the tenant must vacate.

Deputy Director Vance was a tenant of the library's authority.

Gerald changed the form.

**Form 11-D: Eviction Notice for Non-Compliant Occupant.** **Target: Deputy Director H. Vance.** **Basis: Entering premises without valid permit during active hazardous condition.**

He hit submit.

**System Alert: Citation Issued.** **Target: H. Vance.** **Status: Served.** **Debuff Applied: Movement Speed reduced by 15% (Civil Disobedience).**

It was a small debuff. It was not a combat debuff. It was a social one.

Vance would feel a sudden, crushing sense of guilt. He would feel like he was trespassing. He would hesitate.

Gerald needed that hesitation, and he turned back to the western wall.

The dark energy had breached the slurry. A tendril of black smoke slithered through a crack in the new mortar. It reached for Gerald's core, a hungry, grasping thing.

Gerald raised his hand.

**Skill: Code Enforcement.**

He did not cast a spell. He pointed at the crack.

"Violation," he said.

The System interpreted the pointer.

The black smoke froze.

**System Notice: Unpermitted Energy Ingress.** **Action: Seal.**

The mortar glowed white. The crack sealed itself. The smoke was trapped inside, then crushed by the weight of the System's enforcement.

It was not a battle. It was a repair.

But the wall was failing. The pressure from Core 404 was too high.

The citation Gerald had filed for Core 404 was still pending, and the audit plane was a limbo.

Sandra had not returned.

Gerald looked at the rat.

The rat sat on a pile of rubble. It looked confused. It looked like a rat who had been told to stop existing but hadn't understood why.

"Gerald," a voice said.

It was not Sandra, which was not a monster.

It was a voice coming from the ceiling.

Gerald looked up.

A hole had opened in the concrete slab above. Not a magical hole. A physical one. Someone had drilled through the foundation.

Through the hole, a pair of boots descended.

Then legs.

Then a suit.

Gerald recognized the suit, which was a grey business suit. The tie was a clip-on. The shoes were scuffed.

Deputy Director H. Vance stepped into the dungeon.

He did not have the 15% movement speed debuff. He had walked down the stairs normally.

Gerald stared at him.

Vance held a flashlight. The beam cut through the dust.

"Mr. Hutchins?" Vance called out.

Gerald straightened his spine. He adjusted his clipboard.

"Deputy Director," Gerald said.

Vance swept the light across the room. He saw the handrails. He saw the handrails that were up to code.

He saw the fire extinguisher that was properly mounted. He saw the emergency exit sign that was illuminated.

He saw the rat.

"Jesus," Vance said. "What is this place?"

"This is the sub-basement," Gerald said. "It is currently undergoing emergency structural reinforcement."

Vance shone the light on the western wall. The black smoke was gone. The slurry was grey and lumpy.

"Reinforcement?" Vance asked. "With mud?"

"It is a specialized polymer composite," Gerald lied. "It meets ASTM standards for seismic retrofitting."

Vance frowned, and he looked at the ceiling.

"There was a report of strange noises. Lights.

We thought there might be a gas leak.

"

"There is no gas leak," Gerald said. "There is ventilation."

He pointed to the vents. They were ugly, metal boxes duct-taped to the walls. But they were there.

Vance stepped closer to the wall. He put his hand on the slurry.

It was cold.

"Where are the workers?" Vance asked.

"Contractors," Gerald said. "They left for the day."

Vance turned. He looked at Gerald. He looked at Gerald's burnt hand. He looked at the clipboard.

"You're not working, Gerald," Vance said. "You're the inspector."

"I am the inspector," Gerald agreed.

"Then why is there a dungeon down here?"

The question hung in the air.

Gerald did not answer, and he waited for the System notification.

**System Alert: Appeal Window Open for Core 404.** **Duration: 10 minutes.**

Sandra was still gone.

The dark energy was still pressing against the wall.

And Vance was standing in the middle of the room, asking why his subordinate was running a monster lair.

"I need to see your permits," Vance said.

"I don't have any," Gerald said.

Vance pulled a pen from his pocket. He opened a notebook.

"Then I'm shutting you down," Vance said. "Right now.

Stop work.

All of it. You're not allowed to be here.

"

Gerald felt a cold knot in his stomach.

If Vance shut the dungeon down, the System would revoke his Core status. Gerald would cease to exist. His dungeon would collapse. The library would be condemned.

He needed a delay, and he needed a violation.

Gerald looked at Vance's notebook.

"You are using a non-archival pen," Gerald said.

Vance blinked.

"What?"

"City Ordinance 14.2.1," Gerald said. "All official municipal inspections must be recorded in permanent ink, on acid-free paper, to ensure long-term liability tracking. That is a ballpoint pen."

Vance looked at his pen.

"It's a pen."

"It is a violation," Gerald said. "You are not authorized to issue a Stop Work Order while violating the record-keeping statute."

Vance scoffed.

"You're bluffing."

"I am not," Gerald said. "I have the code right here."

He flipped to page 14 of his clipboard.

Vance stepped forward. He was angry now.

"Give me that."

Gerald held the clipboard out.

As Vance reached for it, Gerald activated a hidden feature, and he had not used it before. He had saved it.

**Skill: Bureaucratic Paradox.**

The System had a bug. It loved rules. It hated contradictions.

Gerald handed Vance the clipboard.

"Sign here," Gerald said.

Vance looked at the paper, which was not a Stop Work Order.

It was a **Voluntary Admission of Liability**.

By signing it, Vance would admit that he had entered a structure without a permit. He would admit that he had failed to secure the perimeter. He would admit that the hazard existed because of his negligence.

If Vance signed, he would be fired.

If he did not sign, he could not issue the Stop Work Order, because the Order required the inspector to attest that the hazard was caused by the owner, not the inspector.

Vance stared at the paper, and he looked at Gerald.

"You're trapping me," Vance said.

"I am protecting the public," Gerald said.

The dark energy pulsed behind the wall. The rat squeaked.

Vance's hand hovered over the paper, and he could sign. He could save his job. He could save the library.

He could not sign. He could fire Gerald. He could condemn the building.

But if he condemned the building, the city would have to pay for the repairs. And the repairs would reveal the dungeon.

Vance swallowed, and he looked at the pen.

He looked at Gerald.

"I'm calling the police," Vance said.

"You can," Gerald said. "But the response time is twelve minutes. The structural integrity of this wall fails in eight."

Vance looked at the wall, and the slurry was cracking.

A hairline fracture appeared in the grey mud.

Black light seeped through.

Vance took a step back.

He looked at the paper again, and he looked at Gerald.

"Okay," Vance said. "I'll sign."

He uncapped the pen.

He touched the paper.

And then the wall exploded.

Not outward. Inward.

The dark energy from Core 404 did not break through. It *pulled* through.

A hand of pure shadow erupted from the wall. It was ten feet tall. It had no fingers, only claws of void.

It grabbed Vance.

Vance screamed.

The pen flew from his hand.

Gerald lunged, and he did not attack the monster. He attacked the pen.

He grabbed the pen before it hit the ground.

He jammed it into the paper.

He signed.

**System Alert: Liability Accepted by Inspector.** **Citation: 14.2.1 Violation.** **Penalty: Inspector Status Revoked.**

Gerald felt a surge of power.

Not from mana. From the System.

The System rewarded compliance.

But it also rewarded *consequences*.

By signing as the inspector, Gerald had acknowledged that the hazard was real. He had acknowledged that the library was unsafe.

**System Alert: Emergency Powers Granted.** **Reason: Imminent Threat to Public Infrastructure.** **New Authority: Demolition Order.**

Gerald looked at the shadow hand, which was holding Vance.

Vance was struggling. His suit was tearing. His face was turning purple.

Gerald raised the clipboard.

"Demolition," Gerald said.

The System heard him, and the wall did not break.

The wall *disappeared*.

The shadow hand was left hanging in mid-air, unsupported by any physical structure.

The System had demolished the wall to remove the hazard.

But the hazard was still there.

And now, there was no wall to stop it.

The shadow hand swiped at Gerald.

Gerald ducked.

The claw passed over his head. It cut the air.

Gerald rolled. He hit the floor. The rat squealed and ran.

Gerald scrambled up. He looked at Vance.

Vance had fallen. He was on the floor, gasping. The shadow hand was gone.

Gerald looked at the hole in the wall.

Core 404 was there, which was not a dungeon anymore. It was a void. A tear in reality.

And from the tear, a voice spoke.

It was not a monster's voice.

It was a human voice.

"Mr. Hutchins," the voice said. "You have cited the wrong entity."

Gerald froze, and the voice was familiar.

It was his boss. The City Manager.

The City Manager was a dungeon core.

He had been one all along.

Gerald looked at the tear.

The City Manager stepped through, and he was wearing his suit. He was holding a briefcase.

"You didn't file Form 11-B," the City Manager said. "You filed Form 11-C."

Gerald's blood ran cold.

Form 11-C was for external entities.

Form 11-B was for internal entities.

Core 404 was not an external dungeon, which was a branch of the Ashford Municipal Library.

Gerald had cited his own employer.

The City Manager smiled. It was a politician's smile.

"Welcome to the real city, Gerald," he said. "Where the code is whatever the highest bidder says it is."

The City Manager raised his hand, and the System did not intervene.

Because the City Manager was not violating any code.

He was operating within the jurisdiction of the city.

And Gerald was not an employee anymore.

He was a squatter.

The City Manager snapped his fingers.

**System Alert: Eviction Notice Served.** **Target: Gerald Hutchins.** **Reason: Unauthorized Occupation of Municipal Property.**

Gerald's dungeon walls dissolved.

The handrails vanished, and the lights went out.

The rat disappeared.

Only Gerald remained, standing in the dark, holding a clipboard with a signature that had just destroyed his world.

The City Manager extended his hand.

"Hand over the core," he said. "Or I will have you arrested for impersonation."

Gerald looked at his hand, and he looked at the core.

He looked at the City Manager.

He could fight. He could cite. He could argue.

But the System had sided with the Mayor.

Gerald took a breath, and he put the clipboard down.

And he picked up the pen.

"Wait," Gerald said.

The City Manager paused.

Gerald pointed to the pen.

"You used a ballpoint," Gerald said. "Again."

The City Manager blinked.

"That is irrelevant."

"It is not," Gerald said. "It proves you are not acting in your official capacity. You are acting in a personal capacity. And I am citing you for loitering."

The City Manager laughed.

"You have no authority."

"I have the pen," Gerald said.

He held it up.

The System flickered, which was a glitch. A tiny, microscopic crack in the logic of the audit.

The System did not like ballpoint pens in official proceedings.

It did not like personal capacity citations.

It preferred forms.

Gerald had created a form out of thin air.

**System Alert: Citation Issued.** **Target: City Manager.** **Violation: Improper Instrument of Record.**

The City Manager froze.

His hand stopped.

The dissolution of the walls stopped, and the System had paused.

Not because Gerald was right.

But because the System did not know how to process the contradiction.

It had a loop.

A bureaucratic loop.

And in that loop, Gerald had time.

Time to build a new wall.

Time to find Sandra.

Time to run.

Gerald looked at the darkness, and he looked at the City Manager.

He runned.
