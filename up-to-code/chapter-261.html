Chapter 261: Forensic Inspector Unlocked

# Chapter 261

The pen snapped. It didn't break; it shattered, the plastic casing exploding into sharp confetti across the wet floor.

Gerald Hutchins watched the fragments scatter, his mind automatically calculating the debris field's surface area.

Forty percent. Maybe less, if the rat had been holding the nib end.

"Pick it up," Gerald said. His voice was dry, stripped of fear by thirty-one years of suppressing panic in the face of structural failure. "That is loose debris. It is a slip hazard."

The rat—four feet of malformed muscle and bad posture—tilted its head.

The black eyes blinked.

"The lease is terminated, Mr.

Hutchins.

You are trespassing on private property. Specifically, my property. Which is also your property.

Which is currently collapsing because I removed the keystone.

"

Sarah grabbed Gerald's arm.

Her grip was tight, her knuckles white.

"Gerald. Move.

The support column is shear-failing.

"

"Stand down, Officer," Gerald said, not looking at her. He looked at the rat. "You are claiming ownership. On what basis?"

"On the basis of consumption," the rat said. Its voice sounded like gravel grinding in a mixer.

"I ate your compliance rating.

I ate your ventilation shafts. I ate the egress signage. I am now the landlord.

"

"You cannot eat a rating," Gerald said.

He reached into his wet suit pocket and pulled out a clipboard. It was empty, save for the broken pen.

He clicked the mechanism anyway.

A habit. A reflex.

"Compliance is non-transferable without a notarized transfer of authority from the Inspector General."

The rat laughed. It was a wet, coughing sound.

"There is no Inspector General here.

Only me.

And you. And the void.

"

A beam above them groaned. Dust rained down, coating Gerald's shoulders in gray powder.

"Sarah," Gerald said. "Do you have a flashlight?"

"Yes. Why?"

"Shine it on the north wall. The one with the exposed rebar."

Sarah raised the light. The beam cut through the dust, illuminating a jagged tear in the concrete. Inside, rusted steel rods jutted out like broken bones.

"Look at the fracture pattern," Gerald said. He walked toward it, stepping over a puddle that reflected the red emergency lights flickering in the ceiling. "See that spalling?

That is not shear failure. That is tensile failure.

Someone pulled the steel out.

Someone didn't just remove the keystone. They removed the *tension*.

"

The rat took a step closer.

"Stop talking about walls. Run."

"I am citing you for unauthorized structural modification," Gerald said. He raised the empty clipboard. "This constitutes a Class A violation of Municipal Code 14.2, Section B: Alteration of Load-Bearing Elements Without Permit."

The rat paused. Its smile faltered.

"You can't cite me. You lost your license."

"I did not lose my license," Gerald said.

"I lost my body. My professional standing is tied to the act of inspection, not the flesh that performs it.

Look at the wall, rat.

Look at the code.

"

Sarah aimed the light at a specific node where the rebar met the concrete floor.

"Gerald? What are you looking for?"

"A loophole," Gerald said.

He crouched. The water in the suit was cold, seeping into the small of his back. He ignored it. He traced the line of the broken steel with his finger.

"If the structural integrity is compromised by tension loss," Gerald muttered, "then the collapse isn't gravity-driven. It's tension-driven. The ceiling isn't falling. It's being pulled apart."

The rat hissed.

"You are wasting time."

"Sarah," Gerald said, not looking up. "Do you still have the citation form from the library inspection? The one we drafted before the registration?"

Sarah fumbled in her jacket.

"It's in my pocket. Why?"

"Give it to me."

Sarah handed over the crumpled paper. Gerald smoothed it out against his knee.

He wrote.

Not with the pen, and he wrote with his fingernail, scratching into the damp paper.

*Violation: Improper Anchorage of Tension Members.* *Evidence: Exposed rebar at Node 4. Lack of anchor bolts.* *Required Action: Immediate cessation of collapse via mechanical stabilization.*

The rat lunged. It covered the distance in two bounds, claws extended.

Gerald didn't dodge. He stepped forward, placing himself between the rat and the fractured wall. He held the paper up.

"Serve," Gerald said.

The air shimmered. A blue rectangle materialized in front of the rat's face.

**CITATION ISSUED.** **TARGET: CORE 774 (SELF-CLAIMED LANDLORD).** **VIOLATION: STRUCTURAL SAFETY (CLASS A).** **PENALTY: IMMOBILIZATION (5 SECONDS).**

The rat froze. Its claws stopped inches from Gerald's nose. Its muscles locked. The black eyes widened in genuine surprise.

"What—" the rat choked out.

"Five seconds," Gerald said. "That is the standard administrative delay for a Class A structural hazard. During this delay, the target cannot move, cannot act, and cannot consume."

Sarah stared at the frozen rat, then at Gerald.

"How did you cite a core that isn't in the registry?"

"It claimed ownership," Gerald said. "By asserting it is the landlord, it assumed the role of the property owner. The System recognizes property owners as liable entities.

If you claim the property, you claim the liability. If you claim the liability, you are subject to the code.

"

Gerald stood up. His legs felt like lead. The cold was spreading.

"Sarah. The stabilization. Do it now."

"What stabilization?"

"Look at the wall. The tension is pulling the ceiling up. If we anchor it down, we counter the force. We need a pin."

Gerald looked around. The room was empty except for the collapsing beams and the frozen rat. No tools. No hardware.

"The pen," Gerald said.

Sarah looked at the shards on the floor.

"It's broken."

"The nib," Gerald said. "It's tungsten carbide. High hardness. Low ductility."

He pointed to the fracture in the wall. A small gap. Just enough for a pin.

"Wedging it in won't hold a building," Sarah said.

"It won't hold the building," Gerald said. "It will hold the System's interpretation of the violation. If I physically insert a foreign object into a violation site, I am performing a 'Temporary Repair.' The System recognizes temporary repairs as a mitigation effort. If I mitigate, the penalty extends."

"The penalty extends?"

"It freezes the rat longer," Gerald said.

"But it also freezes *me*. Temporary repairs require a licensed inspector on-site. If I am performing the repair, I am on-site.

If I am on-site, I am subject to the same hazard conditions.

"

"Meaning?"

"Meaning I have to hold it," Gerald said. "While the System decides if my repair is valid."

Sarah stared at him.

"You want to hold a wall together with a pen nib while a rat tries to eat you."

"I want to file a report," Gerald said.

He walked to the wall. The concrete was crumbling. He picked up the largest shard of the pen's nib. It was small, sharp, and black.

He jammed it into the crack in the rebar.

The wall shuddered. A deep, resonant hum filled the room.

The blue rectangle above the rat expanded.

**TEMPORARY REPAIR DETECTED.** **STATUS: VALID.** **PENALTY EXTENSION: 30 SECONDS.**

The rat remained frozen. But now, a new icon appeared above Gerald's head. A red exclamation point.

**HAZARD CONDITION ACTIVE.** **USER UNDER IMMEDIATE THREAT OF STRUCTURAL COLLAPSE.** **PROTOCOL: SAFETY OFFICER OVERRIDE.**

Gerald felt a tug in his chest. It wasn't pain.

It was weight, and the System was assigning him the role of Safety Officer. It was forcing him to remain at the hazard site. If he left, he abandoned his post.

If he abandoned his post, his Compliance Rating dropped to zero.

If his Compliance Rating hit zero, he ceased to exist.

"Thirty seconds," Sarah said. "We have to fix the real problem."

"We can't," Gerald said.

He kept his hand pressed against the wall, holding the pen shard in place.

His arm trembled.

"The tension is gone.

The anchor bolts were pulled out. We need new bolts.

"

"We don't have bolts!"

"We have a rat," Gerald said.

Sarah looked at the frozen creature.

"What?"

"Rats chew through everything," Gerald said. "Look at the base of the wall. The plumbing."

He pointed to a copper pipe running along the floor. It had been severed by the collapse.

"The copper is soft," Gerald said. "We can bend it. We can use it as a pin. But we need to remove the obstruction."

"The rat?"

"The rat is the obstruction," Gerald said. "And the rat is the tool."

"Use the rat?"

"Use the rat's teeth," Gerald said. "Sarah. Grab its left claw."

"Gerald, that's insane."

"It's not insane. It's improvisation." Gerald's face was pale.

Sweat beaded on his forehead.

"I hate improvisation. But the code requires mitigation. If we don't mitigate, we die.

If we die, the library dies.

Do you like the library, Sarah?

"

Sarah looked at the rat. It was still frozen, but its eyes were moving. Tracking. Panic.

"Okay," Sarah said. She reached out and grabbed the rat's frozen claw. It was cold. Hard.

"Hold it steady," Gerald said. "Point the claw at the copper pipe."

Sarah adjusted the angle.

Gerald looked at the wall. The concrete was cracking faster. The pen shard was slipping.

"Push," Gerald said.

Sarah pushed the rat's claw. It dug into the copper pipe. The metal groaned.

"Now twist it," Gerald said. "Use the rat as a lever."

Sarah twisted. The copper pipe bent. Slowly, painfully, it curved around the rebar.

The System flickered.

**NEW OBJECT INSERTED.** **STATUS: UNLICENSED MATERIAL.** **VIOLATION: USE OF UNAPPROVED FIXTURES.**

"Damn it," Gerald muttered. "Of course."

"Does that help?" Sarah asked.

"No. It creates a new citation," Gerald said. "But it reduces the immediate hazard score. The System will prioritize the new violation over the collapse."

The blue rectangle changed.

**CITATION ISSUED.** **TARGET: CORE 774 / SELF-CLAIMED LANDLORD.** **VIOLATION: USE OF UNAPPROVED FIXTURES.** **PENALTY: STAT DEBUFF: STRENGTH (-50%).**

The rat didn't move. It couldn't move. But the pressure on the wall eased. The hum dropped in pitch.

"It's working," Sarah said.

"It's working until the pen falls out," Gerald said.

The pen shard slipped.

Gerald lunged, catching it with his bare hand. His fingers closed around the sharp plastic. He didn't feel it. He just held it.

"Sarah," Gerald said. "The door. The one leading to the street."

"What about it?"

"The man with the black eyes. He was outside. He was waiting."

Sarah looked at the door. It was closed. Sealed.

"He's not waiting," Sarah said. "He's coming in."

The door handle turned.

Gerald looked at the rat. He looked at Sarah. He looked at the pen in his hand.

"Sarah," Gerald said. "Do you have a stamp?"

"A stamp?"

"A rubber stamp. For approvals. Or denials."

"No!"

Gerald closed his eyes, and he reached into his mind, into the place where the System stored his forms. He visualized the stamp. The heavy, black ink pad.

The red ink.

*DENIED.*

The door burst open.

A man stepped in.

Tall.

Thin. His eyes were voids of black glass.

He wore a suit that looked like it was cut from the same gray dust covering the floor.

"Mr. Hutchins," the man said. His voice was smooth. "You have been served."

Gerald opened his eyes. He looked at the man. He looked at the rat. He looked at the wall.

He pressed his thumb against the invisible stamp in his mind. He slammed it down onto the air.

A red mark appeared in the air.

**APPEAL GRANTED.** **REASON: EXIGENT CIRCUMSTANCES.** **REWARD: TEMPORARY AUTHORITY GRANT.**

The man with the black eyes stopped. He frowned.

"What did you do?" the man asked.

"I appealed the termination," Gerald said. "And I cited you for obstruction of a safety officer."

The man's voids widened.

The floor beneath Gerald's feet turned to stone.

The pen in his hand glowed.

**SYSTEM NOTIFICATION:** **NEW CORE TYPE UNLOCKED: FORENSIC INSPECTOR.** **ABILITY: REWRITE STRUCTURAL LOGIC.** **LIMITATION: MUST PROVE THE LOGIC IS FLAWED.**

Gerald looked at the man. He looked at the rat.

"Show me your blueprint," Gerald said.
