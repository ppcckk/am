Chapter 3

Four minutes. Dale counted the seconds under his breath while he walked backward toward his truck, eyes on the sidewalk, watching the frozen mass. Thirty organisms, maybe fewer. They hadn't moved since the cohesion agent hit. Their legs were locked mid-step, antennae frozen at forty-five degrees, like something between a taxidermy display and a car crash.

He reached the truck, popped the passenger door, and pulled out the box of two-component expanding foam he kept for sealing rodent burrows along exterior foundations. Two cans. Enough for a standard exclusion job on a single-family home. Not enough for whatever was under the duplex.

He grabbed both cans, his clipboard, and the roll of duct tape from the dashboard. Walked back. Didn't run. Running wasted oxygen and made his hands shake, and shaking hands made bad seals, and bad seals meant re-treatment, and re-treatment meant more time on site, and more time on site meant more exposure.

The ground was still warm under his boots. Not hot. Warm, like the concrete after a July afternoon, except it was November and the warmth was coming from below. He made a note on the clipboard: SUBSTRATE TEMPERATURE ELEVATED. SOURCE: UNKNOWN. PROBABLE: BIOLOGICAL METABOLIC ACTIVITY. He underlined "biological" twice.

The tunnel entrance was a hole about eighteen inches across, half-collapsed at the edges, leading down at a thirty-degree angle into packed earth. The walls of the tunnel glistened. Not with moisture. With residue. Dale crouched, put his face near the entrance, and smelled it. Ammonia. Sulfur. Something sweet underneath, like rotting fruit left in a hot car.

Frass. The residue was frass. Metabolic waste. He'd seen it a thousand times. Ant trails had it. Termite galleries had it. Rat burrows had it. The smell was different but the pattern was the same: a chemical trail that marked the path between nest and foraging ground.

He sprayed the first can of foam directly into the tunnel mouth. The expanding compound hit the glistening walls and began to cure, pushing back into the passage in a white, hardening plug. He sprayed the second can around the perimeter, sealing the edges where the hole met the sidewalk.

The Authority notification came immediately:

ISOLATION PROTOCOL: PARTIAL COMPLIANCE. TUNNEL ENTRY SEALED. SECONDARY INFESTATION (30 ORGANISMS): TRAPPED WITHIN SEALED SEGMENT. STATUS: ISOLATED FROM PRIMARY NEST. TREATMENT POINTS AWARDED: 8. NOTE: ISOLATION STEP CONFIRMED. REMAINING STEPS: IDENTIFY, DOCUMENT, ELIMINATE, VERIFY.

Eight points. Not bad. Not great. The system had recognized the seal as a valid isolation method, which meant the creatures in the tunnel were now cut off from the nest. They couldn't reach the nest complex. They couldn't reinforce it. They couldn't bring back whatever they were going to bring back.

Dale uncapped a pen and wrote: ISOLATION COMPLETE VIA CHEMICAL EXCLUSION (EXPANDING FOAM, TWO-COMPONENT). METHOD VALIDATED. NOTE: FOAM VOLUME INSUFFICIENT FOR FULL TUNNEL SYSTEM. SECONDARY ENTRY POINTS PROBABLE.

He stood up and looked at the sidewalk. The frozen creatures were still there. He needed to eliminate them. He had no weapon that worked. His spray can was empty. He had duct tape and a trash bag and a clipboard and the knowledge that he was standing on top of a nest that was about to produce something he couldn't kill.

That's when he heard the hissing.

The guy in the camo vest came around the corner of the duplex carrying a propane torch from a weed trimmer. He was maybe fifty, sunburned, wearing steel-toed boots and a Carhartt jacket with the sleeves cut off. He saw the frozen creatures and stopped.

"Are they dead?" he said.

"No," Dale said.

"They look dead."

"They're in a torpor state. Chemical disruption. They're not dead."

The guy nodded like he understood, then pointed the torch at the nearest creature and opened the valve. The blue flame hit it and the thing's legs snapped. Not like it was moving. Like it was breaking. The carapace cracked and a fluid sprayed out, yellowish, and the creature's antennae twitched once and went still.

"Hey," Dale said. "Stop."

The guy moved to the next one. "You said they weren't dead. I'm making sure."

"That's not how this works."

"It's how this works."

The flame hit another one. Then another. Dale watched the torpor state collapse. The frozen creatures weren't dying from the heat. They were breaking out of it. The cohesion disruption was being overridden by thermal shock. The creatures on the far side of the sidewalk, the ones that had been frozen and waiting, began to move. Slowly at first. Then fast. They scattered in six directions, legs skittering on concrete, and three of them went into the storm drain, two went under the duplex foundation, and one went up the siding and disappeared into the soffit.

The Authority notification came:

FIELD GUIDE ENTRY: RATTUS PROFUNDUS. STATUS: CONTAMINATED. CAUSE: UNAUTHORIZED THERMAL TREATMENT APPLIED TO SUBJECT SPECIES. CROSS-CONTAMINATION PENALTY: TARGETED DAMAGE BONUS REMOVED. FIELD GUIDE LOCKOUT: 24 HOURS. NOTE: THERMAL PROTOCOLS ARE NOT VALID FOR THIS SPECIES. ATTEMPTED APPLICATION INDICATES MISIDENTIFICATION OR UNLICENSED INTERVENTION.

Dale read it twice. The +25% bonus was gone. The entry was locked. He couldn't reference his own notes on the species for a full day. The guy in the camo vest had just cost him his only combat advantage.

"You've made it worse," Dale said.

"I killed three of them."

"You broke their torpor. They're scattered now. They're in your walls." Dale pointed at the duplex. "That's your house."

"It's my rental."

"It's my job site. I'm the licensed technician. You need to stop."

The guy looked at the torch. Looked at Dale. Looked at the storm drain where three creatures had gone. "What do I do?"

"Put the torch down."

The guy put the torch down.

Dale walked to the duplex. The siding was vinyl, standard 1970s installation. The soffit vent was on the north side, a rectangular opening with a plastic screen. The screen was torn. One of the creatures had gone through it. He could hear it in there, scratching against the insulation.

He checked the foundation. Standard concrete block, no visible cracks. But the warm patch was on the south side, right where the duplex met the driveway. He put his palm flat on the concrete. Warm. Warmer than the sidewalk. The tunnel network was directly below.

He pulled out his clipboard and started writing.

ENTRY POINTS IDENTIFIED: (1) TUNNEL ENTRANCE, SOUTH SIDEWALK. SEALED. (2) SOFFIT VENT, NORTH WALL. OPEN. (3) STORM DRAIN, WEST SIDE. OPEN. (4) PROBABLE: FOUNDATION CRACKS, SOUTH SIDE, UNDER WARM PATCH. NOT YET LOCATED.

He needed to seal the soffit vent. He needed to seal the storm drain. He needed to find the foundation cracks and seal those too. Four entry points. He had one can of foam left.

Not enough.

He went back to the truck. Checked the supply box. Boric acid powder, two pounds. Diatomaceous earth, one gallon. A tube of silicone caulk. A box of steel wool for stuffing gaps. No more foam.

He grabbed the steel wool and the silicone. Went back to the duplex. Pulled the torn screen off the soffit vent with his multitool. Stuffed the opening with steel wool, packed it tight, then ran a bead of silicone around the edges. The scratching inside stopped.

He walked to the storm drain. It was a standard catch basin, grate on top, connected to the municipal system. He couldn't seal a municipal storm drain. That was not his jurisdiction. That was a city job. He'd need to file a referral.

But the creatures were already in there.

He pulled out his phone. No signal. The Authority notification system worked, but cell service was down. He'd seen that before. Rift events disrupted infrastructure.

He was on his own.

The notification came:

STRUCTURAL COMPROMISE DETECTED. BUILDING: 4400 MAPLE RIDGE DRIVE. FOUNDATION INTEGRITY: 74%. PROBABLE CAUSE: SUBTERRANEAN TUNNEL NETWORK EXPANSION. ESTIMATED TIME TO STRUCTURAL FAILURE: 6-12 HOURS. NOTE: UNRESOLVED STRUCTURAL COMPROMISE WILL RESULT IN THOROUGHNESS RATING DEDUCTION. NOTE: BUILDING COLLAPSE DURING ACTIVE JOB SITE = AUTOMATIC FAILURE.

Dale read that three times. The duplex was going to fall down. Not because of the creatures. Because of the tunnels. The tunnel network was eating the foundation from below, and if he didn't stop the tunnel expansion, the building would collapse, and the Authority would mark him as a failure.

He wasn't a structural engineer. He wasn't a contractor. He was a pest control technician. He sealed entry points and treated infestations. He didn't shore up foundations.

But he knew something about foundations. He knew that when you sealed a rodent burrow under a slab, you had to account for the void space left behind. If you sealed the entrance but left the tunnel intact, the tunnel could collapse under the slab's weight and create a sinkhole. That was a real thing. He'd written reports about it.

The tunnels under this duplex were already a void. The foundation was sitting on a hollow space. The creatures were digging new tunnels because the old ones were getting sealed, and when a colony is sealed in, it digs new routes. That was basic behavior. Seal one entrance, they make two more.

He'd made it worse. The foam seal at the tunnel entrance had triggered a dig response. The colony was expanding its network. The foundation was losing support faster because of what he'd done.

The notification came:

TUNNEL EXPANSION RATE: INCREASING. CAUSE: COLONY RESPONSE TO PERIMETER BREACH. NOTE: SEALING ENTRY POINTS WITHOUT ELIMINATING THE COLONY TRIGGERS EXPANSION BEHAVIOR IN SOCIAL INSECT/RODENT ANALOG SPECIES. RECOMMENDED ACTION: ELIMINATE COLONY BEFORE FULL EXCLUSION. WARNING: CURRENT APPROACH IS COUNTERPRODUCTIVE.

Dale stared at the notification. He'd been doing what every pest control technician did. Seal the entries. Deny access. Treat the nest. That was the protocol. That was how you handled rats, how you handled ants, how you handled wasps.

But the Authority was telling him that with this species, sealing first made things worse. The colony responded to perimeter breaches by expanding. He'd sealed the tunnel entrance and the colony had responded by digging new tunnels, which was eating the foundation.

He had to eliminate the colony first. Then seal.

The order was wrong. His protocol was wrong. He'd been running the steps in the wrong sequence.

He looked at the clipboard. He looked at the warm patch. He looked at the storm drain where three creatures had gone into the municipal system. He looked at the soffit vent he'd just sealed.

The ones in the walls were still in there. He could hear them. Scratching. Moving. The duplex was full of them.

He pulled out his phone again. No signal. He pulled out the Authority interface. It was still there, floating in his peripheral vision like a heads-up display. He could type into it. He'd done it before, writing Field Guide entries.

He typed: REQUEST: TREATMENT PROTOCOL FOR IN-WALL INFESTATION, SOCIAL RODENT ANALOG, SPECIES UNKNOWN, NO VISIBLE NEST ACCESS.

The response came:

PROTOCOL NOT AVAILABLE AT CERTIFICATION LEVEL I. MINIMUM LEVEL: III. NOTE: LOCAL WORKAROUND MAY BE AVAILABLE. CHECK FIELD GUIDE FOR SPECIES-SPECIFIC BEHAVIORAL TRAITS.

His Field Guide was locked. Twenty-four hours. He couldn't reference it. He couldn't use the behavioral notes he'd written.

He was locked out of his own notes because a guy with a torch had contaminated the entry.

He sat down on the curb. He put the clipboard on his knee. He closed his eyes.

The scratching in the walls got louder.

Then a new notification:

PRIMARY NEST DETECTED. DEPTH: 14 METERS BELOW GRADE. SPECIES: [CLASSIFICATION PENDING - INSUFFICIENT DATA]. THREAT LEVEL: CLASS 4. NOTE: CLASS 4 SPECIES REQUIRE CERTIFICATION LEVEL III OR HIGHER FOR SAFE ELIMINATION. NOTE: CURRENT CERTIFICATION: LEVEL I. NOTE: STRUCTURAL FAILURE IMMINENT. NOTE: IF BUILDING COLLAPSES, ALL TREATMENT POINTS FOR THIS JOB SITE WILL BE REVOKED. NOTE: YOUR THOROUGHNESS RATING WILL BE RESET TO 0. NOTE: YOUR CERTIFICATION WILL BE REVOKED. NOTE: YOU WILL NO LONGER BE LICENSED.

Dale opened his eyes.

He stood up. He walked to the truck. He opened the supply box and took out the boric acid. Two pounds. He poured it into the trash bag. He added the diatomaceous earth. He mixed them with his gloved hands.

He didn't know if these creatures ate. He didn't know if stomach poisons worked on dimensional vermin. He didn't know anything about their biology beyond what his locked Field Guide contained.

But he knew this: the ones in the walls were social organisms. They groomed each other. They shared materials. They built with whatever they found. And if you put a substance they'd incorporate into their nesting material, they'd carry it back to the nest.

Bait stations. That was all it was. A bait station was just poison disguised as something the colony wanted.

He stuffed the bagged powder into the gap under the front door of the duplex. He spread a trail of it along the baseboard inside. He put a line of it along the soffit vent, right where the steel wool met the siding.

Then he sat on the curb and waited.

The notification came:

TREATMENT POINTS AWARDED: 3. STEP: ELIMINATION (INITIATED). METHOD: INGESTION/CONTACT BAIT. NOTE: EFFECTIVENESS UNKNOWN. SPECIES BIOLOGY UNVERIFIED. NOTE: IF BAIT IS NOT CONSUMED OR INCORPORATED, NO FURTHER POINTS WILL BE AWARDED FOR THIS METHOD.

Three points. A gamble. If the creatures didn't eat it, he'd get nothing. If they did, the poison would travel back through the colony to the nest.

He didn't know if it would work. He didn't know if boric acid killed dimensional vermin. He didn't know if the nest complex would absorb the poison or if the Class-4 thing at the bottom of it would be immune.

But he knew that in fourteen years, boric acid had killed German cockroaches, it had killed brown-banded cockroaches, it had killed carpenter ants, it had killed silverfish, and it had killed the occasional confused house centipede that wandered into a bathroom and never left.

It was all he had.

He waited.

Twenty minutes passed. The scratching in the walls didn't stop. The warm patch under the sidewalk didn't cool. The foundation notification didn't change.

Then the notification came:

COLONY RESPONSE DETECTED. SUBSTANCE INCORPORATED INTO NESTING MATERIAL. TRAFFIC PATTERN: WORKERS CARRYING SUBSTANCE DOWNWARD. DESTINATION: PRIMARY NEST COMPLEX. ESTIMATED TIME TO NEST CONTACT: 90 MINUTES. NOTE: IF NEST CONTACT RESULTS IN COLONY MORTALITY, ELIMINATION STEP WILL BE CONFIRMED. NOTE: IF NEST CONTACT DOES NOT RESULT IN COLONY MORTALITY, THE PRIMARY NEST WILL DETECT THE CONTAMINANT. WARNING: DETECTION BY CLASS 4 ORGANISM MAY TRIGGER ESCALATION RESPONSE. NOTE: ESCALATION RESPONSE MAY INCLUDE DIRECT CONFRONTATION WITH TREATMENT OPERATOR.

Ninety minutes. If the poison worked, the colony died and he completed the job. If it didn't work, the thing at the bottom of the tunnel would know someone had tried to poison it. And it would come up.

Dale looked at his clipboard. He looked at the truck. He looked at the duplex, where the foundation was slowly losing its grip on the earth.

He picked up his pen.

He started writing a new Field Guide entry. Not for Rattus profundus. For the thing at the bottom. The Class-4. The thing he hadn't seen, hadn't identified, hadn't documented.

He was going to identify it from the outside, using only the frass trails and the tunnel geometry and the behavioral patterns he'd observed. He was going to write its weakness before he ever saw it.

And if he was wrong, the thing would come up through the floor of the duplex and kill him.

He wrote the first line:

SPECIES: UNKNOWN. TENTATIVE DESIGNATION: [BLANK].

He stared at the blank space. His hand was steady. His handwriting was neat.

He filled in the blank.
