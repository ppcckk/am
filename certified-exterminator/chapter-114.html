Chapter 114: Off Grid and Alone

# Chapter 114

The clipboard felt wrong in Dale's hands.

It was not the weight. It was the vibration. The tablet surface hummed against his palm, a low-frequency buzz that traveled up the wrist and into the elbow. A secondary notification overlayed the primary one, a red box pulsing with a countdown timer.

*Challenger Proximity: 4 minutes.*

Dale did not run. Running triggered the Authority's flight-response metrics, which penalized documentation integrity.

He walked.

He walked with the precise, measured gait of a man entering a suspected asbestos abatement zone. One step. Pause.

Scan.

Step.

The suburb was quiet.

Too quiet. The crickets had stopped. The wind had died.

Even the distant hum of the interstate seemed to have been muted, as if the world itself was holding its breath.

Dale adjusted his grip on the wrapped bear carcass.

It was heavy, wrapped in three layers of heavy-duty garbage bags and secured with industrial duct tape.

He had identified the species during the initial audit. It was a Class-III Apex Predator, locally designated *Ursus dimensionalus*.

The Authority had flagged it as a "High-Contamination Bio-Hazard." Leaving it unsealed would constitute a public health violation, a code infraction that could dock his Thoroughness Rating.

He kept the seal intact. That was protocol.

*Violation Code: K-14. Unsecured Bio-Waste.*

He had already paid the fine. It was a symbolic gesture, a way to tell the System that he was taking the threat seriously. The System, however, was not interested in symbols. It wanted closure.

A notification flashed in the upper left of his vision.

*Field Guide Update: New Entry Detected.*

Dale didn't look up. He knew what it was.

The Hound.

The beast he had barely survived in the previous chapter. Its entry would be populated with data he hadn't collected. That meant the data came from the Server.

That meant the Server was leaking information to his enemies.

He turned the corner onto Elm Street. The asphalt was cracked here, fissures running through the pavement like black veins. From the cracks, a faint, sulfurous vapor rose. It smelled like burnt hair and copper.

Dale stopped. He pulled a pen from his breast pocket. He clicked it. The click sounded loud in the silence.

He wrote on the clipboard. Not on the blank page. He wrote on the back of the Authority's notification.

*Note: Environmental conditions suggest localized Rift instability. Probability of secondary infestation: High.*

He tore the paper off. He folded it into a small, tight square. He placed it in his breast pocket, next to his ID badge.

This was a loophole he had discovered in Chapter 89. The Authority's algorithm prioritized digital data.

But human-written notes, if they contained specific observational data and were physically retained by the technician, could sometimes be flagged as "Pre-Combat Context." This didn't grant points.

It didn't grant power. But it granted *precedent*.

If the challenger was a bureaucrat, a true believer in the System, Dale could argue that his pre-combat assessment had been ignored. It was a weak legal defense. But Dale was not fighting for points. He was fighting for survival.

A car engine revved.

It was a black SUV. Matte finish.

Tinted windows so dark they looked like voids.

It pulled up to the curb, blocking Dale's path.

The tires crunched over gravel.

The door opened.

A man stepped out. He was tall. He wore a suit that cost more than Dale's house.

The tie was silk, the knot tight. He held a briefcase in one hand.

In the other, he held a tablet.

He did not look like a monster. He looked like an accountant.

The man's eyes scanned Dale. He did not look at the wrapped bear. He looked at the clipboard.

"Level II," the man said. His voice was smooth, practiced. "Kowalski."

Dale nodded. "Technician Kowalski."

"I am Auditor Vance," the man said. He tapped his tablet. "I am here for the audit."

Dale's heart rate spiked, but his face remained blank. He checked his watch.

"Ten minutes," Dale said. "You're early."

"I am efficient," Vance said. He opened the briefcase. Inside, there were no weapons.

There were vials.

Glass vials filled with a shifting, grey liquid. "The Authority recognizes my petition.

Your Thoroughness Rating is 9.2.

It is an anomaly. I wish to verify if it is genuine or a glitch."

"Glitch," Dale repeated.

"An error in the System," Vance said. He took out a vial.

He uncorked it.

The liquid inside boiled.

"You left the trail, Kowalski. You documented a Class-IV entity, but you did not contain the residual energy.

The Server logged the error. I am here to correct it."

Dale stepped back. His hand moved to his belt.

He carried no sword.

He carried a canister of pressurized foam. It was designed for fire suppression, not combat. But in the hands of a licensed technician, it was a protocol tool.

"You are challenging a licensed technician without proper cause," Dale said. "This is a procedural violation."

Vance smiled.

It was a thin, cruel smile. "The Authority grants me immunity for audits.

I am not a challenger.

I am a quality control officer."

He threw the vial.

It shattered on the pavement between them.

The grey liquid expanded. It rose into the air, forming a shape.

A humanoid shape.

It had no face.

It had no skin. It was made of smoke and static.

It reached for Dale.

*System Alert: Hostile Entity Detected.* *Class: Unregistered.* *Threat Level: Critical.*

Dale did not spray the foam. He did not engage the entity.

He opened his Field Guide.

He tapped the screen.

*Entry: Dimensional Hound (Rattus profundus variant).* *Status: Active.*

He dragged the entry to the left. He dragged the new, unregistered entity to the right.

He initiated a *Cross-Reference Search*.

This was the mechanic. The loophole.

The System allowed technicians to compare two entries to find commonalities. Usually, this was used to find weaknesses. If two monsters shared a biological trait, the damage bonus applied to both.

But Dale was not looking for a weakness. He was looking for a *classification error*.

The System's AI processed the comparison. It looked at the Hound. It looked at the smoke-entity.

Both were manifestations of dimensional instability. Both were generated by the Rift.

The System flagged a conflict.

*Error: Duplicate Entity Signature Detected.* *Resolution Required: Reclassification.*

The smoke-entity paused. Its form flickered. It looked at Dale. It looked at the Hound entry in the Field Guide.

Vance frowned. He looked at his tablet. His screen flashed red.

"What did you do?" Vance asked.

"I followed protocol," Dale said. "I identified a potential duplicate. I requested a merge to conserve Server resources."

The System, obsessed with efficiency, accepted the request.

The smoke-entity screamed. It was a sound of digital anguish.

It unravelled.

It was being forced to integrate with the Hound entry. The Hound entry, which was already bloated with data from the previous fight, could not support the extra load.

*System Alert: Data Corruption in Field Guide Entry: Rattus profundus.* *Action: Purge Redundant Data.*

The smoke-entity dissolved. It was sucked into Dale's Field Guide.

Vance staggered. He looked pale. He touched his chest.

"You... you corrupted my entity," Vance whispered.

"I cleaned the database," Dale said.

Vance raised his hand. A second vial appeared.

"This is not over," Vance said. "The Server will reject this merge. You will lose your entry. And you will lose your rating."

Dale looked at his Field Guide. The entry for the Hound was glitching. Text was scrambling. Numbers were floating off the page.

He had used a loophole.

But the cost was high. He had contaminated his most reliable weapon.

The Hound entry was now unstable.

He could not use the weakness bonus for twenty-four hours. The 40% debuff was already applying.

But the immediate threat was gone.

The smoke was dissipated.

Vance lowered his hand. He looked at Dale with a mixture of fear and confusion.

"You are not playing the game," Vance said. "You are breaking it."

"I am exterminating it," Dale said.

He turned and walked away. He did not run. He walked with the same measured gait.

He had bought himself time. But the cost was real. His Field Guide was damaged. His protection against the next Hound-level threat was gone.

And he had made an enemy of the Authority's quality control.

His watch beeped.

*Challenger Proximity: 8 minutes.*

The second challenger was coming. And the third was waiting.

Dale reached the end of the street. There was a storm drain. It was open. A ladder led down into the darkness.

He looked at the drain. He looked at his clipboard.

He wrote one last note.

*Note: Local infrastructure compromised. Recommend immediate closure.*

He dropped the clipboard into the drain.

It fell into the darkness. Gone.

Dale took out his phone. He opened the Authority's app. He typed a message.

*To: Central Server.* *Subject: Infrastructure Failure.* *Body: The audit trail has been severed. I am going off-grid.*

He hit send.

The app crashed.

The screen went black.

Then, a new notification appeared. Not on the phone. In his vision.

*Warning: Off-Grid Status Activated.* *Penalty: All passive buffs disabled.* *Alert: Your location is no longer hidden.*

Dale climbed down the ladder.

He descended into the dark.

Above him, the storm drain closed with a metallic clang.

He was alone.

And the System was no longer watching over him.
