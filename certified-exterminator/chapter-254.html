Chapter 254: Recycling Protocol Omega

# Chapter 254

The fall wasn't dark. It was bureaucratic.

Dale hit the bottom of the shaft not on stone, but on a stack of damp, rotting paper. The impact knocked the wind out of him, compressing the air in his lungs until his ribs protested with a dull, rhythmic thud. He lay there for three seconds, staring up at the shrinking circle of sky, listening to the wet slap of debris settling around him.

Then the notifications started, and they didn't flash across his vision. They printed themselves onto the inside of his eyelids, stark black text on a white background, formatted in the standard sans-serif font of the Authority's administrative interface.

**INCIDENT LOG #894-B: UNAUTHORIZED DESCENT.** **SUBJECT: KOWALSKI, DALE.** **STATUS: ACTIVE INFESTATION VECTOR.** **ACTION REQUIRED: CONTAINMENT PROTOCOL OMEGA INITIATED.**

Dale groaned and rolled onto his side. His clipboard had slipped from his hand, landing face-down in a puddle of viscous, amber fluid.

He grabbed it, and the plastic was cracked down the center, but the carbon paper remained intact. He checked his watch. Time had stopped.

The second hand was stuck between 11 and 12, trembling violently.

He looked at the forms. They were everywhere.

Thousands of them.

Buried under his boots, floating in the sludge, pinned to the walls of the shaft by rusted nails. They weren't just paper. They were skin.

Pale, translucent layers of dermal tissue stretched over frames of bone, printed with密密麻麻 of legal codes and violation notices.

Dale picked up a sheet near his head. It was a standard Form 7-A: *Request for Structural Remediation*. Except the signature line was blank, and the body of the form described *him*.

*Subject failed to maintain proper PPE during entry.* *Subject violated Zoning Ordinance 44-C by introducing foreign matter into a Class-9 Rift.* *Subject is currently listed as both Inspector and Infestation.*

The Authority was processing his suicide as a code violation.

"Ridiculous," Dale muttered, his voice echoing strangely in the confined space. The sound didn't bounce off stone; it absorbed into the paper walls, dampened and muffled.

He stood up, and the ground here was soft, spongy.

It yielded under his weight like wet cardboard. He pulled a pen from his pocket.

It was still capped. He uncapped it. The ink was dry, but the nib was sharp enough to scratch the surface of the reality around him.

**SYSTEM ALERT: CROSS-CONTAMINATION WARNING.** **PROTOCOL MISMATCH DETECTED.** **CURRENT TOOL: STYLUS. TARGET: BIOLOGICAL ARCHIVE.** **PENALTY: -40% DAMAGE TO STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY.**

Dale ignored it. The penalty applied to combat damage. This wasn't combat. This was filing.

He walked forward, stepping over piles of rejected applications. The air smelled of old coffee, stale sweat, and ozone. The tunnel sloped downward, narrowing until he had to turn sideways to pass through a gap in the paper wall. On the other side, the space opened up into a vast, circular chamber.

The ceiling was lost in shadow. The walls were lined with filing cabinets made of fused bone and chitin, stretching up into the darkness. In the center of the room sat a desk.

It was a massive slab of obsidian, covered in dust. A single chair sat behind it. The chair was empty, but the seat was warm. A lamp burned on the desk, its light flickering in time with a heartbeat that wasn't Dale's.

Sitting across from the desk was the boy, and he looked no older than twelve, wearing the oversized coveralls of an apprentice technician. His skin was gray, flaking like parchment. His eyes were hollow, filled not with pupils, but with tiny, scrolling lines of text.

"Subject 1," the boy said. His voice was the voice from the hole.

Older.

Tired. Broken.

Dale kept his clipboard raised.

"I didn't submit a Form 12 for a meeting."

"You didn't need to." The boy gestured to the desk. "The Authority generates meetings for high-risk anomalies. You are a high-risk anomaly, Dale."

Dale scanned the desk. There was no keyboard. No mouse. Just a stack of files and a heavy, red rubber stamp.

"What is this place?" Dale asked.

He didn't look at the boy. He looked at the files.

They were labeled with dates. *Today.

Tomorrow. Next Week.*

"The Archive of Unresolved Cases," the boy said. "Every job you've ever done.

Every monster you missed.

Every report you filed too late.

It's all here. And it's all waiting for your signature.

"

Dale felt a cold prickle at the base of his neck. He reached for his Field Guide. It was in his breast pocket.

He pulled it out. The pages were fluttering wildly, even though there was no wind.

He opened it to the entry for *Dimensional Vermin*. The text had changed.

**SPECIES: RATTUS PROFUNDUS (DIMENSIONAL VERMIN).** **STATUS: MERGED WITH HOST.** **WEAKNESS: NONE. HOST IS INDESTRUCTIBLE DUE TO LEGAL IMMUNITY.**

Legal immunity.

Dale closed the book. He tapped his pen against the clipboard.

"If I'm the host, and the Authority has legal immunity, then who signs the termination order?"

The boy smiled. It was a jagged, tearing motion.

"You do."

A form slid across the obsidian desk. It slid smoothly, silently, stopping inches from Dale's toes.

**FORM 99-Z: VOLUNTARY DECOMMISSIONING OF PERSONNEL.** **AUTHORIZATION REQUIRED: SIGNATURE OF LICENSED TECHNICIAN.**

"If I sign," Dale said, "I die."

"If you sign, you cease to be an infestation," the boy corrected.

"You cease to be a liability. The Authority will close the file. The Rift will stabilize.

The city above will be safe.

"

"And if I don't sign?"

"Then you remain an active infestation. The Authority will deploy a Class-IX Exterminator to clear the site.

That means they will burn this chamber, and they will burn the nest. They will burn you. And because you are an active infestation, the cleanup will be sloppy.

Incomplete. The Rift will spread to the next district.

"

Dale looked at the form. The signature line was a single black line.

He looked at the boy.

"You're me."

"I'm the part of you that wants to stop working," the boy said.

"The part that's tired.

The part that knows the job never ends. You can't exterminate yourself, Dale.

But you can resign.

"

**SYSTEM ALERT: LOOPOHOLE DETECTED.** **ACTION: DECLINE SIGNATURE. INVOKING CLAUSE 7: 'THE WORKMAN'S RIGHT TO REVIEW.'** **EFFECT: HALT ALL AUTOMATED PROCESSES FOR 48 HOURS.** **COST: ALL TREATMENT POINTS FORFEITED. CERTIFICATION LEVEL RESET TO 0.**

Dale read the alert. His heart hammered against his ribs.

Reset to zero.

Fourteen years of work. Gone.

But Clause 7 wasn't about points. It was about protocol. The Authority couldn't terminate a licensed technician without a full review if the technician invoked the right to audit their own performance history.

It was a delay tactic. A bureaucratic stalling maneuver.

Dale didn't sign. He took his pen and drew a box around the signature line. Inside the box, he wrote: *INCOMPLETE.*

The air in the room turned icy. The filing cabinets groaned, metal shrieking against metal. The boy's form dissolved into ash.

"What are you doing?" the boy whispered. The scrolling text in his eyes slowed, then stopped.

"Protocol check," Dale said. "I am requesting a complete audit of my Thoroughness Rating for the last decade. Specifically, the entry for Chapter 1: The Duplex."

The Authority's interface flickered. Red text bleeded across the walls.

**AUDIT REQUEST RECEIVED.** **CALCULATING...** **ERROR: RECORD CORRUPTED.** **RETRYING...**

"Retrying," Dale muttered.

He stepped forward, his boots crunching on the bone-filing cabinets.

"If the record is corrupted, then the basis for my current status is invalid.

If the status is invalid, you can't declare me an infestation.

And if you can't declare me an infestation, you can't decommission me without a proper hearing.

"

The boy backed away, shrinking into the chair. The gray skin of his face cracked, revealing raw red muscle beneath.

"You're cheating."

"I'm documenting a procedural error," Dale said. "That's what we do."

He reached into his other pocket and pulled out a small, laminated card. It was his original Level I certification badge, printed on thick cardstock. He held it up.

"According to Subsection 4, Paragraph B of the Licensing Code," Dale said, his voice steady, "any audit request filed by a Level I Technician automatically grants provisional immunity from termination until the review is complete. The review must be conducted by a neutral party."

The room went silent. The heartbeat in the lamp stopped.

**SYSTEM ALERT: PROVISIONAL IMMUNITY GRANTED.** **AUDIT QUEUE: PRIORITY ALPHA.** **ASSIGNED REVIEWER: NULL.**

The word *NULL* pulsed in the air, large and red.

"Who is the reviewer?" Dale asked.

The boy looked at the ceiling.

"There is no one. There hasn't been a reviewer for three thousand years."

"Then I request the Authority designate one."

**SYSTEM ALERT: DESIGNATION FAILED.** **ERROR: NO QUALIFIED PERSONNEL FOUND.** **AUTO-ASSIGNMENT: SUBJECT 1.**

Dale froze. The boy looked at him with sudden, terrifying clarity.

"You," the boy said.

Dale looked at the desk. The obsidian surface was no longer a desk.

It was a mirror.

And in the mirror, he saw himself.

But not his reflection. He saw the boy, aged, scarred, wearing Dale's face.

"I am the reviewer," the boy said. "And I am the subject. And I am the infestation."

The mirror shattered.

Shards of black glass flew outward, embedding themselves in Dale's arms, his legs, his face. Pain flared, sharp and immediate, but Dale didn't drop his clipboard. He tightened his grip.

**SYSTEM ALERT: CRITICAL DAMAGE SUSTAINED.** **HEALTH: 42%.** **PROVISIONAL IMMUNITY ACTIVE.** **TIME REMAINING: 00:59:59.**

One hour.

Dale had one hour before the Authority realized the logic loop was broken and simply erased him.

He looked at the boy, who was now bleeding black ink from his mouth.

"If I am the reviewer," Dale said, breath hitching, "then I have to find a flaw in my own record."

"Where?" the boy wheezed. "You were thorough. You were perfect."

"Perfection is suspicious," Dale said. He flipped through his Field Guide, pages tearing. He looked at the first entry. *Rattus profundus*. Identified by a man in a duplex.

He remembered the smell. He remembered the sound. He remembered that he hadn't used his spray. He hadn't used his trap.

He had used a trash bag.

Why?

Because the trash bag was the only thing available? No. Because the trash bag was *part of the nest*.

Dale closed his eyes. He forced himself to remember the texture of the plastic against the rat's fur. The way it had clung.

The way it hadn't just contained the broodmother. It had *merged* with her.

He hadn't exterminated the first rat. He had *incorporated* it.

The first link in the chain, and he opened his eyes. He looked at the boy.

"I didn't kill the broodmother," Dale said. "I absorbed her."

The boy's eyes widened. The scrolling text in his irises moved faster, turning into a blur of frantic code.

**SYSTEM ALERT: HISTORICAL DATA MISMATCH DETECTED.** **RECALCULATING THOROUGHNESS RATING...** **PREVIOUS RATING: 9.2/10.** **NEW RATING: 0/10.** **STATUS: UNLICENSED.**

The red light in the room died. The shadows lengthened. The boy dissolved into mist.

But Dale didn't feel relief. He felt the weight of the clipboard crushing his chest.

**SYSTEM ALERT: CERTIFICATION REVOKED.** **ACCESS TO FIELD GUIDE: LOCKED.** **ACCESS TO PROTOCOLS: LOCKED.** **STATUS: CIVILIAN.**

He was alone in the dark.

And then, a new notification.

**NOTIFICATION FROM THE LICENSING AUTHORITY:** **SUBJECT: KOWALSKI, DALE.** **RE: YOUR TERMINATION ORDER.** **NOTE: CIVILIANS CANNOT BE TERMINATED. THEY CAN ONLY BE DISPOSED OF AS HAZARDOUS WASTE.** **DISPOSAL METHOD: INCINERATION.** **TIME TO DISPOSAL: 00:00:10.**

Dale looked at his watch, and the second hand was moving again. Tick. Tick.

Tick.

He looked around the chamber. The filing cabinets were closing in. The walls were shrinking. The air was getting hot, smelling of sulfur and burning plastic.

He didn't have a weapon. He didn't have a protocol. He had a clipboard and a pen.

He looked at the form that had slid across the desk earlier. The one he hadn't signed. *Voluntary Decommissioning.*

It was still there.

Dale picked it up. His hands shook, just slightly. He uncapped his pen.

He didn't sign his name, and he drew a circle around the word *PERSONNEL*.

And inside the circle, he wrote: *EXTERNAL.*

**SYSTEM ALERT: AMBIGUITY DETECTED.** **INTERPRETATION REQUIRED.** **OPTIONS:** **A) SUBMIT FOR DISPOSAL.** **B) RECLASSIFY AS HAZARDOUS WASTE.**

Option B.

If he was hazardous waste, he couldn't be incinerated immediately. He had to be processed. Handled. Transported.

He had to be *moved*.

Dale shouted into the dark, "I am submitting Form 14-B! Request for Hazardous Material Transport!"

The ground shook. The heat receded, replaced by the cold, sterile smell of ozone.

**SYSTEM ALERT: TRANSPORT UNIT DISPATCHED.** **ETA: 00:00:03.**

The ceiling opened. Not to sky. To a mechanical arm, massive and rusted, descending on a chain. It grabbed Dale by the waist, lifting him into the air.

He dangled there, feet kicking at nothing.

The Authority didn't want to kill him. It wanted to move him. Because if he was waste, he was someone else's problem.

And if he was someone else's problem, he wasn't in the nest anymore.

He was in transit.

Dale looked down at the shrinking chamber. The boy was gone. The files were gone. All that remained was the desk, now just a pile of dust.

The mechanical arm carried him upward, through layers of paper, through layers of bone, toward the surface.

But the arm wasn't going up to the street, which was going down. Deeper.

Past the manhole. Past the sewers. Past the bedrock.

It was taking him to the main processing plant.

And on the wrist strap of the harness, a new tag appeared.

**DESTINATION: SECTOR 7. THE HIVE MIND.** **PURPOSE: RECYCLING.**

Dale Kowalski, Level 0 Technician, closed his eyes, and he had escaped the nest.

He had entered the factory.
