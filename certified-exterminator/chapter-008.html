Chapter 8: Corrupted Field Guide

# Chapter 8

The fluid was already in the foundation.

Dale could see it through the crawlspace access panel, a thin film of iridescent slime coating the concrete block like oil on water. It had seeped through the mortar joints in at least three places.

He pressed his face closer to the opening and shone his flashlight along the interior wall.

The slime was moving. Not flowing, exactly. Migrating.

Individual strands of it crawled along the block surface toward the nearest gap, seeking voids, seeking nesting material.

He pulled out his phone and photographed the panel from four angles, and then he opened his Field Guide and started writing.

"Secondary contamination site. Address: 4417 Linden Drive.

Vector: Vance, unlicensed.

Method of dispersal: mechanical destruction of egg mass, estimated twelve to fifteen eggs. Fluid contact with exterior siding, foundation, and grade. Migration observed: YES.

Direction: toward structural voids. Time since dispersal: approximately four hours.

Estimated emergence window: two to six hours."

The Authority pinged.

SECONDARY CONTAMINATION SITE LOGGED. TREATMENT AREA EXPANDED. NEW INFESTATION ZONES DETECTED: 3. PRIORITY: HIGH.

Three zones.

Dale looked at the house. The foundation had three access points where the slime had entered: the crawlspace panel, a gap around the main utility line, and a crack in the slab near the back patio.

Three entry points. Three nesting sites. Three infestations that hadn't existed four hours ago.

He stood up and wiped his knees on his coveralls. The house was dark. Every window on the ground floor was dark. He walked around to the front and found the door unlocked.

"Vance?"

No answer. He stepped inside.

The foyer smelled like the crawlspace at the duplex, but wrong, and the duplex had smelled like ammonia and wet fur. This smelled like ammonia and copper and something else, something sweet and chemical, like the solvent they used to clean specimen jars at the Authority's examination center.

He moved through the house with his flashlight.

Kitchen. Clean, except for a smear of iridescent fluid on the countertop near the sink.

Living room.

Clean. Dining room. Clean.

Hallway. He stopped.

The wallpaper was bubbling.

Not from moisture.

From underneath. Something was pushing up against the paper from inside the wall cavity.

A slow, rhythmic pressure. Like something breathing.

Dale stepped back. He pulled out his phone and photographed the wall. Then he opened his Field Guide to a fresh page.

"Wall cavity infestation. Active.

Subject: unknown.

Behavior: slow, rhythmic pressure against interior surface. Estimated size: six to eight inches. Species: UNKNOWN.

No existing Field Guide entry matches observed behavior."

He stared at the page. UNKNOWN.

He'd been dealing with unknowns for three weeks now, but always they were variations on things he'd already catalogued.

Bigger, smaller, faster, slower. Same basic biology. Same basic weaknesses.

This was different. The pressure pattern was wrong.

Insectoid creatures pushed with multiple points of contact, a distributed force, which was a point.

A fist.

He moved to the next wall. Same bubbling. Different rhythm.

Slower. He checked the ceiling.

Nothing, and he checked the floor vents. Nothing. He went back to the kitchen and opened the utility line access panel.

The slime was thicker here.

It had pooled in the gap around the pipe, a shallow puddle no more than two inches deep. And in the puddle, something was forming.

Not a creature.

A structure. A lattice of hardened slime, like a chrysalis, but irregular. Wrong.

Asymmetric. Like something was building a body out of the fluid and hadn't gotten the proportions right.

Dale photographed it. He wrote in his guide.

"Emergence structure.

Utility line penetration.

Lattice formation, asymmetric. Estimated maturation: unknown.

Species: UNKNOWN."

The Authority notification came immediately.

UNIDENTIFIED SPECIMEN DETECTED. FIELD GUIDE ENTRY REQUIRED PRIOR TO TREATMENT.

PROVISIONAL ENTRY OPTION AVAILABLE: DOCUMENT MINIMUM THREE BEHAVIORAL OBSERVATIONS TO GENERATE PROVISIONAL SPECIES CLASSIFICATION.

PROVISIONAL ENTRY DAMAGE BONUS: 15%. FULL ENTRY DAMAGE BONUS: 25%. NOTE: PROVISIONAL ENTRIES DO NOT COUNT TOWARD CERTIFICATION PROGRESS.

Dale read it twice.

A new option. He'd never seen it before.

Provisional entries.

A shortcut. You didn't have to fully identify a creature to get a damage bonus. You just had to document enough behavior patterns to make an educated guess.

Fifteen percent. Not great. But better than zero.

He looked at the lattice in the utility panel. He looked at the bubbling wall.

He looked at the crawlspace panel outside.

Three sites.

Three observations. That was his minimum.

He went back outside. Crawlspace panel. He shone his light inside.

The slime was still migrating, but now he could see something else, and the strands were not all moving the same direction.

Some were moving toward the wall cavity.

Some were moving toward the utility line. They were coordinating. Distributing themselves across multiple sites.

Observation one: distributed resource allocation. The organisms were splitting their biomass across multiple nesting sites.

He went back inside.

Wall cavity. He put his hand flat against the bubbling paper and counted.

One, two, three, four, five, six, seven.

The pressure cycle. Seven seconds. He moved to the next wall.

Same pattern. Seven seconds.

He moved to the third wall.

Seven seconds.

Observation two: synchronized behavior. All three sites pulsed on the same seven-second cycle. They were connected.

He went back to the utility panel. The lattice had grown.

It was now three-dimensional, roughly the size of a football, and it had started to take on a shape. Not a chrysalis. Something more specific.

It had a central mass and two lateral extensions. Arms.

Observation three: bilateral symmetry developing. Limb formation.

He wrote it all down. The Authority responded.

PROVISIONAL ENTRY GENERATED. SPECIES CLASSIFICATION: "UNIDENTIFIED BILATERAL HYBRID (SITE 4417 LINDEN)." DAMAGE BONUS: 15%. NOTE: PROVISIONAL ENTRY EXPIRES AFTER 48 HOURS OR UPON FULL IDENTIFICATION, WHICHEVER OCCURS FIRST.

Fifteen percent. He'd take it.

A crash came from upstairs. Then another. Then a sound that was almost but not a human voice.

Dale went to the stairs, and he took them one at a time, flashlight in his left hand, spray can in his right. Useless, probably. He knew it was useless.

He brought it anyway.

The upstairs hallway was empty. The bedrooms were empty.

The bathroom door was open, and he checked it. Nothing.

The last door at the end of the hall was closed. Light leaked from under it. And sound. Vance's voice, talking fast, talking to the camera.

"...they're in the walls, man, they're in the walls and I don't know what they are but I hit one with the bat and it just, it just went back in, it went back in and it's still in there, I can hear it moving, it's been like ten minutes and it's still moving, I don't think they're dying, I don't think the bat is working, what do I do, what do I do..."

Dale knocked on the door.

"Vance. Open the door."

A pause. Then the lock clicked.

The door opened.

Vance was standing in the middle of his bedroom, phone held up, face pale and wet with sweat.

Behind him, the bed was pushed against the wall. The dresser was on its side.

And in the corner, in the ceiling, something was moving, which had broken through the drywall. It was hanging from the ceiling joist like a spider, but it wasn't a spider.

It was wrong.

The legs were too long and too few. The body was too large. And the head, if you could call it a head, had a shape that Dale recognized from a hundred pest identification charts.

Mandibles. But set in a face that was too flat. Too wide. Too much like a human skull with the eyes removed and the jaw extended.

Dale's hand went to his Field Guide, and he flipped to the provisional entry. He looked at the classification: "UNIDENTIFIED BILATERAL HYBRID." He looked at the creature.

It looked at him.

It tilted its head. A human gesture. A confused gesture.

"Vance," Dale said. "How did you cut your hand?"

"What?"

"Your hand. You're bleeding."

Vance looked down. His right palm was split open, a line of red running from the base of his thumb to his wrist.

He'd been gripping the bat, and the bat had slipped.

He'd been hitting eggs with a bleeding hand.

"When did that happen?"

"I don't know. The bat, the eggs, I don't know, I was just hitting them, they were moving, they were moving and I just, I had to, they were going to get out, they were going to get into the house and I just, I just hit them, I hit them and they burst and the fluid, it got everywhere, it got on my hand, it got on my hand and it burned and I kept hitting because I couldn't stop, I couldn't stop, I had to stop them from hatching, I had to..."

Dale looked at the creature. He looked at Vance's hand. He looked at the creature again.

He opened his Field Guide. He wrote.

"Observation: hybrid specimen displays bilateral symmetry, seven-second respiratory cycle, distributed nesting behavior, and bilateral limb formation. Additional observation: cranial structure exhibits anthropomorphic features inconsistent with known Rattus profundus morphology. Hypothesis: human genetic material incorporated during egg-stage contamination. Source: Vance, unlicensed, laceration to right palm, direct contact with egg fluid."

He hit submit, and the Authority notification came. But it was different from the others. It came in a different color.

Amber instead of white. And the text was longer.

HUMAN GENETIC MATERIAL DETECTED IN HYBRID SPECIMEN.

PROVISIONAL CLASSIFICATION: HOMO VERMIN (PROVISIONAL). TREATMENT PROTOCOL REQUIRED: CLASS IV, PROTOCOL 7-C (HUMAN-ADJACENT BIOHAZARD). CURRENT CLEARANCE: CLASS II.

ACTION RESTRICTED.

Dale read it three times.

CLASS IV. He was CLASS II.

He was two full certification levels below what was required to treat this creature.

The system was telling him he could not kill it; he could not document it as a threat and eliminate it. He could not complete the protocol.

The creature dropped from the ceiling. It landed on all fours, then straightened.

It stood up, which was four and a half feet tall.

It walked toward Dale. Its gait was wrong, too smooth, too coordinated for an insect, too jerky for a human.

It moved like something trying to remember how bodies worked.

"Vance," Dale said. "Get out of the room."

Vance didn't move. He was still holding his phone. He was still livestreaming.

"Vance. Get out. Now."

Vance backed toward the door. The creature kept walking toward Dale.

Dale stepped back, and he stepped back again. His heel hit the bed frame. He had nowhere left to go.

The Authority notification flashed again.

LEVEL IV CLEARANCE REQUIRED. ATTEMPTING UNAUTHORIZED TREATMENT WILL RESULT IN: FIELD GUIDE CORRUPTION, CERTIFICATION HOLD, AND TREATMENT POINT REVOCATION.

Dale looked at the creature. He looked at his spray can. He looked at his Field Guide.

The creature reached for him.

And Dale made a decision, and he closed his Field Guide. He put it in his pocket.

He looked at the creature's face, at the wrong human geometry of it, and he did not write anything down.

He did not identify it. He did not document it. He did not complete the protocol.

He swung the spray can at its head, and the Authority notification came instantly.

UNAUTHORIZED TREATMENT ATTEMPT LOGGED. FIELD GUIDE CORRUPTION INITIATED. CERTIFICATION HOLD: 72 HOURS. TREATMENT POINT REVOCATION: PENDING.
