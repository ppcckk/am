Chapter 6: Unidentified Mutagen

# Chapter 6

The flashlight beam hit wet fur and Dale knew immediately that something was wrong. The broodmother's shape was wrong.

The trash bag had split along the seam where the duct tape overlapped, and the thing inside had pushed through the tear like a hand through wet paper.

It was bigger. Not by much, but the proportions had shifted. The skull was elongated, the jaw hinged differently, and the forelimbs had developed a second set of joints that bent backward at an angle that made Dale's stomach turn.

He dropped the flashlight and pulled his Field Guide from his back pocket.

The Rattus profundus entry glowed faintly in the crawlspace dark. He read it.

Looked at the thing.

Read it again.

Wrong. Every measurement in that entry was wrong.

The skull-to-body ratio didn't match.

The limb count didn't match, and the Authority had populated that entry from his original identification, and that identification was now obsolete.

The broodmother clicked its jaw. A wet, grinding sound. Something that had been a molar had been replaced by something pointed.

Dale's hands were shaking. Not from fear. From the realization that if he deployed a protocol calibrated for Rattus profundus against this thing, the cross-contamination penalty would hit him for forty percent debuff and lock his Field Guide entry for twenty-four hours. He'd be flying blind in a zone that was already at twenty-eight meters and climbing.

He needed to identify it first. Write it down. Populate a new entry. Then treat it.

The problem was that identification required observation, and observation required proximity, and proximity in a crawlspace with a mutated broodmother meant being within striking range of something that had just evolved new teeth.

Dale pulled out his phone. The flashlight app. He set it on the crawlspace floor, angled toward the thing, and crawled backward until his back hit the joist.

He had maybe three meters of working distance. The broodmother was still in its bag, still tangled in the shredded polyethylene, but it was pulling itself free with those new backward-bending limbs.

He opened the Field Guide's blank entry form. The Authority had given him three empty slots. He'd used one. Two left.

He started writing.

"Morphological deviation from baseline Rattus profundus.

Cranial elongation approximately fifteen percent. Mandibular reconfiguration: secondary dentition replaced by single-row conical teeth, estimated six to eight per side. Forelimb articulation: additional joint at proximal metacarpal, enabling reverse-grip locomotion.

Integument: partially keratinized, thicker at thoracic region.

Probable cause: stress-induced metamorphosis during containment period. Duration of containment prior to mutation: approximately fourteen hours."

The Authority processed it. The text glowed amber, then shifted to green.

FIELD GUIDE UPDATE: New entry populated.

Species designation pending. Provisional classification: Rattus profundus (mutagenic variant). Entry status: UNVERIFIED.

Targeted damage bonus unavailable until verification complete.

Unverified. That was the catch. The Authority wouldn't grant him the damage bonus until the identification was confirmed, and confirmation required either a completed elimination or a secondary observer's attestation.

He didn't have a secondary observer. He had Rick, who was outside and couldn't see into the crawlspace.

The broodmother pulled its head free of the bag. It turned toward him. Those new teeth caught the phone's light and reflected it in long, thin glints.

Dale looked at his empty canister. Looked at the broodmother. Looked at the crawlspace geometry.

He'd been doing this for fourteen years.

Not with monsters.

With roaches. With bedbugs.

With the occasional wasp nest in an attic. But the geometry was the same. Enclosed space.

Limited egress. Target that could move through gaps you couldn't fit through yourself.

The professional answer wasn't to fight it. The professional answer was to seal it in.

He pulled the duct tape from his belt.

A fresh roll, three inches wide, industrial grade. He'd replaced it after the first roll went, and he tore off a strip and slapped it across the crawlspace opening he'd come through.

Not a full seal.

A partial one. Enough to create a pressure differential when he closed the other end.

The broodmother lunged.

Those backward-bending forelimbs gave it a pushing gait that was fast and ugly, like a spider dragging itself forward on its elbows. It crossed the distance in two seconds. Dale rolled sideways, hit the crawlspace floor, and kicked the phone away from it.

The light beam swept across the joists. The thing lost visual contact.

Dale grabbed the second canister.

Rick's spare.

He'd taken it without asking. It was maybe a third full.

Not enough for a direct kill, but enough for what he needed.

He sprayed into the crawlspace air. Not at the thing.

At the walls.

At the joists. At the polyethylene bag that was still tangled around the broodmother's hindquarters, and the aerosol cloud filled the enclosed space in under two seconds.

The broodmother shrieked.

Not a sound Dale had heard before. Higher.

More resonant, and the keratinized thorax was supposed to protect it from chemical agents, but the aerosol wasn't meant to kill. It was meant to irritate. To disorient.

To drive it toward the one opening that wasn't sealed.

The east wall. The crawlspace entrance.

Dale had sealed the west opening with tape, and the east opening was where he'd come in.

He scrambled toward it, boots sliding on the crawlspace floor, and hit the door from the inside. Rick was right there.

His face was pale and his canister was in his hand, raised like a weapon.

"Close it," Dale said.

"What?"

"Close the door. Now."

Rick closed the door. Dale heard the latch engage. He was in a sealed box with a thing that had new teeth and didn't like aerosol.

He crawled backward. The broodmother was coming. He could hear it dragging itself across the crawlspace floor, those extra joints clicking against the wood. He had maybe ten seconds before it reached him.

He pulled out his Field Guide. The new entry. UNVERIFIED.

He wrote: "Behavioral note: mutagenic variant exhibits acute chemical sensitivity in exposed mucous membranes. Cranial elongation reduces olfactory protection. Aerosol dispersal in enclosed space produces avoidance response toward largest unsealed aperture."

The Authority processed it. Green.

FIELD GUIDE UPDATE: Behavioral data confirmed. Species entry upgraded to VERIFIED. Targeted damage bonus: +12% against Rattus profundus (mutagenic variant) when deploying aerosol-based protocols in enclosed spaces.

Twelve percent. Not much. It was still something.

Dale opened the door, and the broodmother was two feet from the opening, those backward limbs splayed wide, those new teeth bared. It had followed the aerosol gradient. It had come to the largest unsealed aperture, which was now the door Dale had just opened.

He sprayed. Direct.

Full stream.

The canister's remaining third emptied in under four seconds. The aerosol hit the thing's exposed skull directly, right where the elongation had thinned the bone. The Authority's bonus applied.

The broodmother convulsed.

Its limbs locked. It toppled sideways into the crawlspace.

Dale closed the door again, and he waited.

Counted to sixty. Opened the door. The thing was still.

Its thorax was rising and falling, but slower. The aerosol hadn't killed it.

The Authority hadn't awarded elimination points, and he needed to finish it.

He looked at Rick. Rick looked at the door. Rick looked at his own canister, which had maybe a quarter left.

"I need you to go inside," Dale said.

"No."

"I need you to go inside, spray it directly into the skull where the bone is thin, and come back out.

That's the protocol. That's the elimination step.

I've done identify, document, isolate, and I've partially treated. You do the kill. I verify."

Rick's jaw worked. "You're asking me to go in there with that thing."

"It's down. It's in a chemical haze. It won't see you for maybe eight seconds after you open the door. You have time to spray and get out."

"You don't know that."

"I know that aerosol compounds in enclosed spaces settle at a rate of roughly two seconds per cubic meter. The crawlspace is about four cubic meters.

The concentration will drop to non-irritant levels in eight seconds.

That's when it recovers. You have eight seconds."

Rick stared at him. Then he uncapped his canister.

He opened the door.

Counted one, two, three. Dropped to his knees.

Crawled in.

Dale heard the spray. Heard the thing shriek. Heard Rick scramble backward, boots slipping, and then Rick was out and the door was closed and Rick was on his hands and knees in the grass, gasping.

Dale waited thirty seconds. Opened the door.

The thing was still.

No movement.

The thorax wasn't rising, and he crawled in. Checked for respiration. Checked for limb response.

Pulled his pen from his pocket and pressed it into the thing's eye socket. No reaction.

He wrote the verification in his Field Guide.

The Authority processed it.

TREATMENT POINTS AWARDED: 47. THOROUGHNESS RATING: 8.1.

Not a nine. He'd lost points for the aerosol substitution, for using a non-standard protocol, for outsourcing the elimination step to an unlicensed individual. But forty-seven points. More than his entire first day's work.

FIELD GUIDE UPDATE: Rattus profundus (mutagenic variant) entry marked as ELIMINATED. New species registered to your name. All future encounters with this variant are assigned to your jurisdiction.

That was the cost. He'd created a new species entry, and the Authority had assigned it to him. Every time this variant showed up anywhere in the county, it would be his problem. He'd just made himself responsible for something that didn't exist an hour ago.

He crawled out. Stood up. Looked at Rick, who was wiping aerosol from his face with his sleeve.

"You okay?" Dale asked.

Rick didn't answer. He was looking at Dale's phone. At the notification that had appeared while Dale was inside.

Dale looked.

UNLICENSED INFESTATION DETECTED. CLASS 2. DISTANCE: 11 METERS.

Eleven meters.

Not the apartment complex. Something else. Something closer.

Something that had appeared during the sixty seconds Dale had spent verifying the broodmother.

He looked at the crawlspace. At the hole in the crawlspace floor that he hadn't noticed before.

The one that the broodmother had been digging toward, and the one that was now open, its edges lined with something that wasn't dirt. Something that looked like chitin.

CLASS 2. He was Class 1 certified.

The Authority's rules were clear.

He could observe, and he could document. He could not engage.

But the hole was right there. And from the hole, something was moving upward. Slowly. The sound of it was wet and rhythmic, like a boot pulling out of deep mud.

Rick grabbed his arm. "Dale. What the hell is that?"

Dale looked at the hole. Looked at his Field Guide. Two blank entries left.

He started writing.

The Authority processed it.

CERTIFICATION RESTRICTION: Level I technicians may not deploy treatment protocols against Class 2 or higher organisms. Violation of this restriction results in immediate suspension of licensing and forfeiture of all accumulated Treatment Points.

He looked at what he'd written. A Field Guide entry. An identification.

A weakness notation. All legal.

All within his Level I privileges.

What wasn't legal was the next step.

The thing in the hole was almost through. Dale could see the leading edge of it now. Not a head.

Not a limb.

Something that looked like a ribcage turned inside out, with the spine running along the outside and something soft and pale pulsing in the center.

He had two blank Field Guide entries. He had a phone with a flashlight; had a roll of duct tape and a canister with nothing left in it.

He had a Field Guide entry that he could write right now, in this moment, that would identify this thing and note its weakness, and that would be legal. And then he would have to decide whether to act on that knowledge or walk away and let the apartment complex at twenty-nine meters deal with whatever was coming out of the hole.

The thing's ribcage caught the crawlspace floor.

It pulled itself up. It was the size of a large dog. Maybe bigger.

It had no eyes. It had a mouth that opened vertically, splitting from what should have been a chin to what should have been a forehead, and inside that mouth was a structure that Dale recognized from fourteen years of professional experience.

A mandible. A second set of mandibles. Nested inside the first.

He knew what that meant. He'd seen it before. Not in a monster. In a flea.

The Authority notification pulsed in his peripheral vision.

CERTIFICATION RESTRICTION ACTIVE. DO NOT ENGAGE.

Dale looked at Rick. Rick looked at him.

"I need you to call someone," Dale said. "I need you to call whoever runs the Authority. Tell them I need a Class 2 technician. Tell them I have an active infestation that I am legally prohibited from treating."

"And if they don't send anyone?"

Dale looked at the thing. It was pulling itself fully out of the hole now. Its vertical mouth was opening. The nested mandibles were spreading.

"Then I write a second Field Guide entry," Dale said. "And I find out exactly how much suspension costs."

The thing turned its eyeless head toward them. The Authority notification flashed red.

WARNING: CROSS-CONTAMINATION RISK. YOUR FIELD GUIDE ENTRY FOR RATTUS PROFUNDUS (MUTAGENIC VARIANT) MAY BE INACCURATE FOR CURRENT ORGANISM. PROCEEDING WITHOUT UPDATED IDENTIFICATION WILL RESULT IN FULL TREATMENT POINT FORFEITURE.

Dale opened his Field Guide. Third blank entry.

He wrote SPECIES UNKNOWN in the entry and left the rest blank.
