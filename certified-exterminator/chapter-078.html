Chapter 78: Sanitation Response

# Chapter 78

The boots stopped at the door. Not a knock. Not a ring. Just the sound of weight settling onto the porch boards, the creak of someone shifting their center of gravity, and then silence.

Dale's pen was already moving. He'd written Voss's name in the subject field and was filling in the observation column.

Physical markers.

Posture. Gait. The way Voss held his left arm slightly away from his body, guarding his ribcage.

He scanned the windows first, then the people.

Threat assessment. Constant, involuntary.

"You're writing about me like I'm a cockroach," Voss said.

"You're writing about me like I'm a cockroach," Dale said. "We're doing the same thing."

The door handle turned.

Locked. Dale had locked it. He always locked it.

The lock was part of the protocol. You don't open your workspace to unverified entry points.

The person outside spoke. "This is a Level VI sanitation response. I need access to the interior."

Dale looked at Voss. Voss looked at Dale.

"Show your credential," Dale called.

The window beside the door went opaque.

A card pressed against the glass.

The Authority seal. Certification number.

The number was printed in a font Dale recognized from his own Level I certificate, except the numeral preceding it was a six.

Level VI.

Dale looked at his clipboard. His Field Guide entry for the arriving technician hadn't populated yet.

The system hadn't given him a species classification.

That meant she was still in the "pending identification" column of the system's perception. She was an unknown. Unknowns were dangerous.

Unknowns were what got people killed in Chapter Three.

"Voss. The window."

Voss moved. He pressed his face to the glass, reading the credential card from the inside.

"Name's Yuen.

Certification number checks out. She's got a Level VI protocol kit. I can see the canister rig through her jacket."

"Canister rig," Dale repeated.

"That's what she uses. No melee. No blades. She sprays."

Dale felt something tighten in his chest. A canister rig meant chemical treatment.

A canister rig meant she was already in the "eliminate" phase of her protocol.

She'd skipped identify and document, and she was here to kill.

"If she treats us before she documents us," Voss said, "we're dead."

"No," Dale said. "If she treats us before she documents us, she gets zero Treatment Points. The system doesn't reward undocumented kills.

She'll document first. That's how it works."

"Unless she's been tracking us."

Dale stopped writing.

"Think about it," Voss said.

"She's Level VI. She's got a different protocol set. She might not need the full five-step cycle.

Higher certifications unlock abbreviated protocols."

The phone in Dale's hand buzzed.

SYSTEM NOTICE: CERTIFICATION LEVEL VI PROTOCOL SET UNLOCKED. ABBREVIATED CYCLE AVAILABLE: IDENTIFY AND ELIMINATE.

DOCUMENTATION OPTIONAL.

TREATMENT POINTS REDUCED BY 60%. AUTHORITY RECOMMENDS FULL CYCLE FOR THOROUGHNESS RATING PRESERVATION.

Dale read it. Then he read it again.

"Documentation is optional," he said.

Voss's face went flat. "She can skip it."

"She can skip it. She'll lose points. But she'll still kill us."

The door handle turned again. This time it rattled.

Not a knock.

A test, and she was checking the lock.

Dale looked down at his clipboard. His own Field Guide entry. The one the Authority had generated.

SUBJECTS: KOWALSKI, D. AND VOSS, R.

WEAKNESS: PENDING IDENTIFICATION.

He looked at the field. It was editable. He'd known it was editable.

Every Field Guide entry had editable weakness fields.

That was the whole point. You identified a weakness, you got the damage bonus.

You identified it before combat.

But what if you identified your own weakness?

Dale put his pen to the field. He typed: CONTAMINATION ANXIETY. PRE-ENGAGEMENT PROTOCOL RIGIDITY. INABILITY TO TREAT WITHOUT ENVIRONMENTAL CONTROL.

The system pulsed.

SELF-DOCUMENTATION DETECTED. WEAKNESS FIELD UPDATED. VERIFICATION STATUS: UNVERIFIED.

SELF-DOCUMENTATION REQUIRES EXTERNAL OBSERVER FOR VALIDATION. WITHOUT VALIDATION, WEAKNESS FIELD REMAINS INACTIVE FOR DAMAGE CALCULATIONS.

He hadn't gotten the damage bonus. His own entry was still pending. The system wanted a second set of eyes.

"Voss. I need you to verify my weakness field."

Voss was already looking at his own entry. His phone screen glowed. "It says the same thing. Self-documentation requires external observer."

"Then we do each other. Now. Before she gets through that door."

Voss set his phone on the counter, and he turned to face Dale.

His expression was professional. Flat.

You inspect a client's kitchen for an entry point.

"You won't engage until your environment is controlled," Voss said. "You locked this door.

You checked the seals.

You wrote a report before you did anything else. That's not discipline. That's compulsion.

If the workspace isn't right, you freeze."

Dale felt the words land. They landed like a needle. Not painful. Diagnostic.

"Verify," Dale said.

Voss picked up his phone. He typed Dale's weakness into the verification field. The system pulsed.

WEAKNESS VERIFIED. SUBJECT: KOWALSKI, D. DAMAGE BONUS AVAILABLE AGAINST THIS SUBJECT: 30%. REMEDIATION PATHWAY: UNLOCKED.

Dale felt the notification. Thirty percent.

Against himself.

If someone knew his weakness, they could hurt him thirty percent more than normal, and he was now a target with a published vulnerability.

"Your turn," Dale said.

He looked at Voss. Looked. He examined the infestation. Not with fear. With process.

Voss was pacing. Not random pacing.

Circular.

Three steps left, three steps right, turning at the counter.

His hands kept reaching for his phone, checking the screen, putting it down, reaching again.

"You need the Authority to tell you you're doing it right," Dale said.

Voss stopped pacing.

"You check your notifications every ninety seconds," Dale continued. "You've done it four times since we started this conversation. You're not looking for information.

You're looking for approval. The system validates you.

Without validation, you hesitate."

"That's not true."

"It's in your gait. You favor your left side because you're waiting for a command to straighten up. You're holding your posture in anticipation of correction."

Voss's jaw tightened.

"Verify," Dale said.

Voss picked up his phone. He typed Dale's observation into his own weakness field. The system pulsed.

WEAKNESS VERIFIED. SUBJECT: VOSS, R. DAMAGE BONUS AVAILABLE AGAINST THIS SUBJECT: 30%. REMEDIATION PATHWAY: UNLOCKED.

Both of them were verified. Both of them had active remediation pathways, and the system recognized them as infestations with identified weaknesses and available treatment options.

They were still classified as pests.

But they were pests with a path to technician status.

"We need to file," Dale said. "A joint remediation plan. If we file before she documents us, we reclassify."

"The door," Voss said.

The wood was flexing.

Not breaking. Flexing. Something was pushing against it from the outside with steady, even pressure.

A canister rig could generate that kind of force if the nozzle was pressed flat against the frame. Chemical expansion.

The sealant was expanding in the gap around the door.

Dale grabbed his clipboard, and he started writing. Client names.

Address. Violation code. K-19.

The blank code. He filled in the observation fields with everything he'd noted about Voss.

Posture.

Gait. Phone-check frequency. He filled in Voss's weakness field.

He filled in his own, and he was building a remediation plan for two infestations. Both of them. Filed by the infestations themselves.

The door bowed.

Voss grabbed his phone. He started typing.

A parallel report.

Same subject. Same observations.

The system pinged both of them simultaneously.

REMEDIATION PLAN DRAFTED. STATUS: PENDING SUBMISSION. SUBMISSION REQUIRES WITNESS. A WITNESS MUST CONFIRM THE PLAN'S VALIDITY BEFORE THE SYSTEM ACCEPTS IT.

"We need a witness," Dale said.

"Yuen," Voss said. "She's right there."

"She's trying to kill us."

"She's also a certified technician. She's a witness. She just has to look at the plan and confirm it."

The door popped. Not open.

The frame was warping, and the sealant was pushing the door off its hinges by fractions of an inch.

The gap widened. Chemical smell.

Acrid. The kind that meant a Class III solvent. She was using a solvent that dissolved organic bonding agents.

She was dissolving the house.

Dale looked at the gap. He could see the edge of a nozzle. Gray metal. Pressed flat against the frame.

He opened his clipboard. He held it up toward the gap.

"Witness," he called. "I'm filing a joint remediation plan under K-19. I need a Level VI technician to confirm validity."

The nozzle stopped pushing.

Silence.

Then Yuen's voice. Flat. Bored. The voice of someone who'd done this a hundred times.

"K-19 isn't in the protocol manual."

"It's in my Field Guide. It's a new code. The system generated it."

"New codes don't get witness confirmation. They get eradication."

Dale felt the gap widen another fraction. The solvent was still working. He had maybe ninety seconds before the door was gone.

"The system generated the code," Dale said. "The system also generated the remediation pathway. I'm following the pathway. I need you to witness it."

"You're an infestation."

"I'm a technician filing a self-remediation plan."

"You're a MUTUAL VECTOR."

"And you're a witness.

That's all I need. Confirm the plan is valid. Don't treat it.

Don't document it.

Just confirm."

The nozzle pulled back. The door stopped flexing.

Dale held his breath.

The door opened, and she was small.

Mid-fifties. Gray hair pulled back tight. A canister rig on her back, nozzle in hand, safety catch engaged.

Her clipboard was in her left hand. It was already open.

She looked at Dale. She looked at Voss. She looked at the clipboard Dale was holding up.

"Show me the plan," she said.

Dale stepped forward, and he held the clipboard at arm's length.

Protocol distance. He didn't cross the threshold.

The house was still his workspace. She was in the entry zone.

Yuen read the clipboard. Her eyes moved fast. Professional. She'd read a thousand of these.

She looked up.

"You identified each other's weaknesses."

"Yes."

"Verified. Active. Thirty percent each."

"Yes."

She looked at Voss. Voss held up his own clipboard. Same plan. Parallel filing.

Yuen set her nozzle on the porch. She pulled a pen from her pocket. She uncapped it.

She wrote something on her own clipboard.

Dale watched her pen. He watched her fill in a field.

Then she held up her clipboard.

Her Field Guide entry. For them.

Both of them.

Same entry. Same classification: MUTUAL VECTOR. But the weakness field wasn't blank.

It wasn't pending, which was filled.

WEAKNESS: PROXIMITY TO PARTNER. PROXIMITY TO PARTNER.

Dale read it. He read it twice.

He looked at Voss. Voss was reading the same thing from his phone. The system had updated both their entries.

Yuen capped her pen.

"Your weakness isn't contamination anxiety," she said. "It isn't validation-seeking. Those are symptoms. The actual weakness is that you're standing in the same room."

She picked up her nozzle.

"The system identified it three days ago. I've been tracking you both since then. I didn't need to get in the house. I needed you to verify each other's weaknesses first."

Dale's stomach dropped.

"Because the system needs verified weaknesses to calculate damage," he said.

"Because the system needs verified weaknesses to calculate damage," Yuen confirmed. "And your verified weakness is each other. You're standing six feet apart. That's within range."

She raised the nozzle.

"The plan you filed is valid," she said. "I'm confirming it. But valid plans don't stop treatment. They just make the treatment count."

She pulled the safety catch.

Dale looked at Voss.

Voss looked at Dale. They were six feet apart. The system had calculated the damage bonus.

Thirty percent. Against both of them.

Because of each other.

Because the only way to remove the weakness was to separate; separating meant losing the only partner who understood the protocol.

Yuen's finger tightened on the trigger.
