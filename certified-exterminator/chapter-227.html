Chapter 227: Stamp of Termination

# Chapter 227

The pen tip hovered over the waiver line.

The blue uniform did not move. He stood rigid, his hand still gripping Dale's shoulder, fingers digging into the heavy canvas of the jacket. The air between them vibrated with a low, static hum that made the hair on Dale's arms stand up.

"Name," Dale said, his voice flat. "And address. Or I leave. And you deal with the Class-IV breach yourself."

The blue uniform blinked. His eyes were pale, watery, and wrong. The pupils were too square.

"I am a technician," the man said. "I do not sign waivers. I execute protocols."

"Then execute this one." Dale pointed the pen at the paper. "Sign it. Or I classify you as a secondary infestation."

The blue uniform's grip tightened. Pain flared in Dale's deltoid, sharp and bright.

He didn't flinch.

He kept his wrist steady. He had learned long ago that flinching triggered predation responses in anything with teeth or claws.

The man in blue leaned in. The smell of ozone and rot filled Dale's nose.

"You are contaminated," the blue uniform whispered. "Your field guide is corrupted. You used a Class-I protocol on a Class-III entity. The contamination timer is running."

Dale glanced at his peripheral vision. The Field Guide interface shimmered. The entry for *Rattus profundus* was grayed out. A red countdown ticked in the corner: 23:59:58.

He didn't care about the penalty. He cared about the leak.

The black fibers pulsed in the shadows of the room, spreading across the floorboards like mold. They were seeking the crack. The Paradox Seal had closed the hole in space, but the biological residue remained. The infestation was trying to re-establish a foothold using the residual energy from the previous attack.

Dale looked at the other technicians. They were frozen. Staring.

Three of them. Two men, one woman. All in blue. All wearing the same badge: *Licensing Authority, Internal Audit Division*.

They hadn't been there a minute ago.

"Temporal drift," Dale muttered. He clicked the pen again. The sound was loud in the silent room.

The blue uniform released him.

"Process the claim," the man said. His voice sounded like gravel grinding together. "You have thirty seconds before we revoke your license for procedural violation."

Dale ignored the threat. He turned to the wall.

The crack was gone. The plaster was smooth. But the black fibers were still there, writhing. They were trying to bond with the drywall, turning the house into a nest.

He pulled a canister from his belt.

Not the standard spray. This was his personal modification, a pressurized tank filled with a hyper-saline solution mixed with concentrated pheromone inhibitors.

It was illegal in most jurisdictions.

It was also the only thing that would stop the fibers from spreading without triggering a full dimensional collapse.

Dale didn't ask for permission. He didn't wait for the waiver to be signed.

He walked to the nearest cluster of fibers.

The blue uniform shouted something. Dale didn't hear it. He focused on the target.

*Identify.*

The fibers were not rats. They were not beetles. They were fungal hyphae threaded with dimensional energy. Class III. *Myco-rift spore*.

His Field Guide didn't have an entry for this. Not yet.

But he knew the biology. It was a parasite. It needed a host. And the host was the house itself.

*Document.*

He snapped a photo with his phone. The image blurred as the fibers reacted to the light, recoiling.

*Isolate.*

Dale kicked a metal bucket over. He dragged the black fibers toward it. They resisted, snapping like rubber bands, pulling against the floor.

"Stop!" the blue uniform yelled. "You are contaminating the audit zone!"

Dale ignored him. He sprayed the fibers.

The mist hit the black strands. They hissed. The smell of burning hair and sulfur filled the room. The fibers curled inward, turning brittle.

Dale grabbed a handful of the dying strands and shoved them into the bucket.

More fibers erupted from the floorboards. Dozens of them. Then hundreds.

The blue uniform raised his hands. Blue energy crackled around his fingers.

"Step away from the infestation, Technician Kowalski."

Dale looked up. He raised his clipboard.

"I am performing an emergency extraction," Dale said. "You are obstructing a licensed operation. I am documenting this."

He clicked a second photo. The blue uniform's face was in the frame.

The blue uniform froze. The energy dissipated.

"You cannot document an auditor," the man said. "That is not in the manual."

"It is not," Dale agreed. "But unauthorized interference with active extermination protocols is listed in Section 4, Paragraph B. I am invoking it."

The blue uniform's jaw tightened.

Dale kicked the bucket upright. He poured the rest of the saline solution into it. The fibers in the bucket turned white, then dissolved into sludge.

The room shook.

The walls groaned. The floor buckled.

The Paradox Seal was failing.

Dale felt the vibration in his teeth. The temporal stability of the room was collapsing. If the seal broke, the hole would reopen. And this time, whatever was on the other side would be bigger.

He needed a new seal.

He needed a contradiction.

The Paradox Seal required the user to identify a logical inconsistency in the entity's existence.

The blue uniforms were the contradiction.

They were here to audit him. But they were also the ones who had sent the complaint. But the complaint had been filed by a tenant who was currently screaming in the other room.

Who had made the call?

Dale looked at the business card in his hand.

*Don't let them hire you.*

The handwriting was his own. But he hadn't written it. Not yet.

The loop was already closed.

Dale realized the trap. The Authority wasn't just auditing him.

They were recruiting him.

The blue uniforms were future versions of himself.

Or past versions. Or parallel versions.

They were trying to force him into a role that would erase his current identity.

If he signed the waiver, he accepted the role. He became one of them.

If he refused, he remained an exterminator.

But the infestation was real. And it was growing.

Dale made a choice.

He didn't sign the waiver.

He tore it in half.

The blue uniform's eyes went wide.

"No!"

Dale threw the torn paper at the man's face.

At the same time, he activated his new ability.

*Paradox Seal.*

He didn't target the wall. He targeted the man.

The logical inconsistency: A technician who was both the auditor and the accused. A person who was both present and absent.

Dale focused on the contradiction.

The blue uniform screamed.

The air warped.

The man twisted. His body folded in on itself, like a piece of paper being crumpled. The blue uniform vanished.

In his place stood a pile of dust and a single, intact badge.

The other two blue uniforms stared.

They did not attack.

They backed away.

One of them, the woman, looked at Dale. Her face was pale.

"You broke the loop," she said.

Dale picked up the badge. He checked the expiration date. It was yesterday's date.

He pocketed it.

The shaking stopped. The black fibers retreated into the cracks, sealed behind the new, invisible barrier Dale had created.

The room was silent.

Except for the screaming in the other room.

Dale walked out.

The living room was a mess. The tenant, Mrs. Gable, was on the floor, crying. The neighbor was trying to help her up.

Dale stepped over the debris.

He checked his wrist.

**TREATMENT POINTS AWARDED: 150.**

**THOROUGHNESS RATING: 9.8.**

**STATUS: CERTIFICATION LEVEL III SECURED.**

**WARNING: TEMPORAL ANOMALY DETECTED. AVOID INTERACTING WITH PAST OR FUTURE SELF.**

Dale ignored the warning. He walked to the front door.

The other blue uniforms were gone.

He opened the door.

The night air was cold. The suburban street was quiet. The Rift over the sky was closed.

Dale walked to his truck.

He sat in the driver's seat. He didn't start the engine.

He looked at the business card.

On the back, the handwriting had changed.

*They know you are here. They know you are real. Stop hiding.*

Dale stared at the words.

He looked in the rearview mirror.

His reflection was different.

He was older. Gray at the temples. A scar running down his left cheek.

And he was wearing a blue uniform.

Dale blinked.

The reflection winked.

He rubbed his eyes.

When he looked again, he was just Dale. Thirty-eight. Unscarred. Wearing his black technician jacket.

He started the truck.

His phone buzzed.

**NEW JOB DETECTED: CLASS-V INFESTATION.** **LOCATION: DOWNTOWN CINCINNATI, CITY HALL BASEMENT.** **DESCRIPTION: BUREAUCRATIC PARASITES. THEY ARE EATING THE ARCHIVES.** **REWARD: 500 TP. CERTIFICATION PROMOTION TO IV.**

Dale drove.

He didn't go home.

He went downtown.

The city was asleep. The streets were empty.

At City Hall, the doors were locked.

Dale didn't pick the lock. He used a master key he had confiscated from a previous client who had lost their keys to a giant termite.

The hallway smelled of old paper and dust.

He walked to the elevator.

He pressed the button for the basement.

The elevator was slow. It rattled.

The lights flickered.

In the mirror, Dale saw something move behind him.

He turned.

Nothing.

The doors opened.

The basement was dark.

Dale turned on his flashlight.

The beam cut through the dust.

The archives were gone.

The shelves were empty.

But on the floor, covering every inch of concrete, was a carpet of small, white, insect-like creatures.

They were moving.

They were chewing.

They were eating the silence.

Dale took a step back.

One of the creatures looked up.

It had a face.

A tiny, human face.

It smiled.

Dale raised his clipboard.

His hand shook.

He didn't write anything.

He just watched.

The creature spoke.

"Hello, Dale," it said. "We've been waiting for you."

Dale lowered the clipboard.

He reached for his spray can.

The can was empty.

He looked around.

The walls of the basement were lined with jars. Hundreds of them.

Inside each jar was a pest control technician.

Some were dead.

Some were sleeping.

Some were awake.

And they were all looking at him.

The creature on the floor crawled toward his shoe.

"Sign the contract," it said. "Or join us."

Dale stepped back.

His heel hit the elevator door.

The doors closed.

The creature jumped.

It latched onto his ankle.

Dale kicked.

The creature detached. It landed on the floor, unharmed.

The doors closed.

Dale stood in the elevator.

He looked down at his ankle.

There was no mark.

But there was a note stuck to his boot.

He peeled it off.

It was a receipt.

**ITEM: ONE (1) IDENTITY.** **PRICE: YOUR SOUL.** **STATUS: PAID.**

The elevator dinged.

The doors opened.

Dale was not in the elevator.

He was in the basement.

The creature was gone.

The jars were gone.

The only thing left was a single, white business card on the floor.

He picked it up.

It was blank.

He turned it over.

*Welcome to Level IV.*

Dale looked at the walls.

They were gone.

He was standing in an infinite white space.

And from the darkness, a voice spoke.

"Please state your name and certification level."

Dale opened his mouth.

But no sound came out.

He tried again.

Nothing.

The voice continued.

"Your silence has been logged as a failure to identify. Please state your name."

Dale tapped his chest.

He pointed to himself.

The voice waited.

"Please state your name."

Dale looked at the white space.

He realized the horror.

He couldn't speak.

Because he had already identified himself.

And the system had accepted it.

But the system didn't know who he was anymore.

The white space darkened.

Shapes formed in the darkness.

Hundreds of them.

They were wearing black uniforms.

They were holding clipboards.

They were waiting for him to file a report.

But he had no pen.

He had no paper.

He had no name.

The shapes stepped forward.

One of them held out a hand.

In the palm was a pen.

Dale looked at the pen.

He looked at the hand.

He took the pen.

The shape smiled.

"Write," it said.

Dale looked at the clipboard the shape was holding.

It was blank.

He wrote.

He wrote his name.

*Dale Kowalski.*

The shape nodded.

"Correct," it said. "Now, describe the infestation."

Dale looked at the darkness.

The darkness was full of eyes.

He wrote.

*The infestation is me.*

The shape took the clipboard.

It read the words.

It frowned.

"Incorrect," it said. "Thoroughness rating: 0."

The shape raised the clipboard.

It smashed it against Dale's face.

Pain exploded in his nose.

Blood sprayed across the white floor.

The other shapes cheered.

Dale fell to his knees.

He looked up.

The shape with the clipboard was gone.

In its place stood the blue uniform.

The same one from the house.

He was smiling.

"You're not the technician," the blue uniform said. "You're the complaint."

Dale tried to stand.

His legs didn't work.

He looked down.

His legs were turning into paper.

He tried to scream.

But his mouth was closed.

The blue uniform stepped over him.

He picked up a stamp.

He stamped Dale's chest.

*APPROVED FOR TERMINATION.*

Dale closed his eyes.

He heard the sound of a spray can.

He smelled the chemicals.

He felt the cold.

Then, darkness.
