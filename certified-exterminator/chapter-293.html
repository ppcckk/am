Chapter 293: Permission Denied

# Chapter 293

The clipboard was not a weapon. It was a bureaucratic anchor.

Marla Vance saw the figure on the throne before she saw Dale. She was already in the tunnel, her boots sinking into the viscous black sludge that had replaced the floor. Her flashlight beam trembled, a tiny circle of light fighting against a darkness that felt thick, almost gelatinous. The air smelled of ozone and rotting gardenias, a cloying sweetness that coated the back of her throat.

Marla was a Level IV Exterminator. She specialized in dimensional rifts and spatial anomalies.

She had seen things that would make a soldier vomit.

But this? This was quiet. The silence was worse than the screaming.

She froze when the eggs cracked.

Hundreds of wet, chitinous fingers pulled themselves free of the translucent shells.

They didn't scurry. They didn't attack.

They stood up, their joints popping with sounds like dry twigs snapping, and the spawn were naked, pale, and wrong. Their limbs had too many joints. Their faces were smooth, featureless ovals of bone and skin.

Marla raised her plasma cutter. The hum of the battery pack at her hip sounded deafening in the enclosed space.

She aimed at the nearest spawn.

One of them turned its head.

It didn't look at her. It looked at Dale.

The spawn raised a hand, pointing deeper into the tunnel.

Marla followed the gesture. Her light hit the throne. The figure sitting there was massive, carved from the same obsidian-like stone as the walls.

It held a clipboard. The pen in its hand moved with fluid, practiced grace.

*Inspection complete.*

The notification echoed in Marla's mind, sharp and intrusive. She tried to swipe it away with her thoughts, a reflex developed after years of combat. The notification didn't fade. It burned brighter.

*Subject: Dale Kowalski. Status: Non-Compliant.*

Marla's stomach dropped, and she looked at Dale. He was standing in the sludge, his flashlight beam shaking. He wasn't attacking.

He wasn't defending.

He was staring at the figure on the throne with an expression of pure, professional horror.

To Dale, the horror wasn't the monsters.

It was the form. The paperwork. The Authority had sent an auditor into the heart of a Class VII infestation.

And the auditor was about to issue a citation.

Marla stepped forward, her boots squelching.

"Dale! Move!"

Dale didn't move. His eyes were locked on the stone figure's clipboard.

The spawn surged forward, and they didn't attack Marla.

They formed a circle around Dale, their fingers interlocking. A human wall.

A perimeter.

Marla fired. The plasma bolt struck the nearest spawn, vaporizing its torso.

The remaining spawn didn't flinch, and they didn't break formation. They looked at Dale, waiting.

The stone figure stood.

The movement was slow, grinding, like tectonic plates shifting. The clipboard remained steady in its hand.

The figure took a step down from the throne.

Then another.

Marla's comms unit crackled. Static, then a voice.

"Unit Four, report.

Sensors indicate massive energy spike at Sector 9.

Are you engaging?

"

Marla keyed her mic.

"Negative. We are being... processed."

"Repeat? Processed?"

The spawn tightened the circle. Dale was trapped in the center. The stone figure raised its pen.

A drop of black ink fell from the nib. It hit the sludge floor and sizzled, eating through the slime.

Marla saw the text on the stone figure's clipboard. It was too far away to read, but she saw the red stamp.

*VIOLATION: UNLICENSED INTERVENTION IN ACTIVE AUDIT.*

The realization hit Marla like a physical blow. The Authority wasn't fighting them. The Authority was fining them. The infestation wasn't a battle; it was a scene-of-crime investigation, and they were the suspects tampering with evidence.

She looked at Dale, and he was still standing there. He hadn't drawn his crowbar. He hadn't deployed a protocol.

He was waiting.

Marla screamed his name.

"Dale!"

Dale blinked. The spell broke.

He looked at Marla. Then he looked at the spawn. Then he looked at the figure.

Dale's face went blank. Not with fear, but with recognition.

He recognized the posture, and he recognized the pen. He recognized the clipboard.

He knew what it meant when an inspector stood up. It meant the inspection was over. It meant the report was being written.

And in the Authority's system, a bad report meant a penalty. A penalty meant a loss of Treatment Points. A loss of points meant he couldn't buy the anti-spawn protocols he had been saving for months.

Dale raised his crowbar. Not to strike. To point.

He pointed at the spawn.

"Field Guide Update," he muttered.

The System prompt appeared in his vision.

**SPECIES: ARCHITECT SPAWN.** **WEAKNESS: IDENTIFYING PROTOCOL REQUIRED.**

He didn't have the weakness. He had never seen this species before. But he knew the job. He knew the procedure.

*Identify. Document.

Isolate.

Eliminate.

Verify.*

He couldn't eliminate what he couldn't identify. The System would punish him for a sloppy kill. It would dock his Thoroughness Rating. It would flag him as incompetent.

The stone figure stepped closer. The spawn parted to let it through. The figure stopped ten feet from Dale. It extended the clipboard.

The paper on the clipboard was blank.

Dale looked at the blank paper. He looked at the pen. He looked at his own empty clipboard hanging from his belt.

He unclipped his own clipboard.

The air in the tunnel grew heavy. The spawn watched. Marla watched. The stone figure watched.

Dale reached into his vest pocket. He pulled out a pen. It was a cheap ballpoint, the kind he bought in bulk from the municipal supply depot.

He walked forward.

Marla tried to grab him.

"You can't! You don't know what it's asking!"

Dale ignored her. He walked through the circle of spawn. They didn't touch him. They parted like water.

He stopped in front of the stone figure. The height difference was immense. The figure towered over him, its obsidian face devoid of features.

But the posture was familiar. It was the posture of a manager who knows the employee is cutting corners.

The figure held out the pen.

Dale didn't take it. He held up his own clipboard.

He flipped to a new page, and he clicked his pen.

The stone figure tilted its head. A low rumble vibrated through the tunnel floor.

Dale wrote.

*Date: October 14.* *Location: Sub-Level 4, Sector 9.* *Subject: Active Infestation, Class VII.* *Observation: Unlicensed entity attempting to usurp inspection authority.*

He underlined "usurping."

He looked up at the stone figure.

"That's a code violation," Dale said.

His voice was flat, deadpan.

"Section 4, Paragraph B of the Licensing Act. Only certified Level III or higher can initiate a primary audit on a Class VII threat.

You are not certified.

You are an infestation.

"

The stone figure went still.

Marla stopped breathing. She watched the System prompts flicker in Dale's peripheral vision.

**STATUS: CHALLENGING AUTHORITY.** **PENALTY: IMMINENT.**

Dale didn't care. He kept writing.

*Corrective Action Required: Immediate cessation of unauthorized auditing.* *Signature: Dale Kowalski, Level II Technician.*

He tore the page off his clipboard. He held it up.

The stone figure looked at the paper. The black ink on the white page seemed to glow. The figure reached out a long, jointed finger. It touched the paper.

The paper didn't burn. It dissolved into black smoke.

The stone figure recoiled. A sound like grinding metal shrieked through the tunnel. The spawn scattered, their chitinous bodies clicking in panic.

The figure raised its pen. It wrote on its own clipboard. Fast. Furious.

Dale watched it write, and he knew what it was doing. It was writing a counter-report. It was citing him.

It was trying to frame him as the threat.

But Dale had a loophole. He had used it once before, with the rats. He had used it with the beetles. It was the only reason he had survived this long.

He hadn't just identified the threat. He had identified the *bureaucracy*.

The stone figure finished writing. It held up its clipboard.

The text was written in a language Dale didn't speak, but the System translated it instantly.

**VIOLATION: OBSTRUCTION OF AUTHORITY.** **PENALTY: TERMINATION OF LICENSE.**

Dale looked at the penalty.

Termination of his license meant he couldn't buy equipment, and he couldn't heal. He couldn't progress.

He would be stuck at Level II forever.

He looked at the figure. He looked at the spawn.

He raised his crowbar. Not to hit. To tap.

He tapped the crowbar against the stone floor. *Clang. Clang. Clang.*

The rhythm was specific. Three short taps.

Pause.

Two long taps. Pause. One short tap.

It was the Morse code for

"Wait."

The stone figure paused.

Dale pointed at the figure's clipboard. Then he pointed at his own chest.

He spoke, enunciating every word.

"I am the Inspector."

The figure tilted its head.

Dale flipped his clipboard again, and he showed the figure the previous page. The page where he had documented the spawn's weakness?

No.

He hadn't found the weakness.

He showed the page where he had listed the spawn's *entry points*.

He had done it while falling into the hole. While Marla was screaming.

While the eggs were cracking, and he had looked at the cracks in the wall.

He had noted the dimensions.

He had documented the *breach*.

The stone figure looked at the notes. The cracks. The measurements. The structural analysis.

The figure's pen hovered.

It couldn't write a violation of obstruction. Because Dale wasn't obstructing. He was *completing* the inspection.

He was doing the job the Authority required. He was doing it better, faster, and with more attention to detail than the Auditor itself.

The System prompt flashed.

**PROTOCOL OVERRIDE: DOCUMENTATION SUPERSEDES COMBAT.** **ACTION: RE-EVALUATION.**

The stone figure lowered its pen. The red stamp on its clipboard faded.

The spawn stopped attacking. They stood still, waiting.

Marla lowered her plasma cutter.

Her hands shook. She watched the stone figure. It seemed to shrink.

The obsidian surface cracked.

Veins of light pulsed beneath the stone, and the figure sat back down on the throne.

The eggs stopped hatching. The remaining spawn curled back into their shells.

The tunnel was silent again.

Dale exhaled. He clipped his pen to his vest. He picked up his flashlight.

"Marla," he said. "Get the containment foam."

Marla stared at him.

"What just happened?"

"I corrected the paperwork," Dale said. He walked toward the throne. "But we're not done."

He pointed at the stone figure. It wasn't dead. It wasn't gone. It was waiting.

And the cracks in the walls were still there. The breach was still open.

Dale looked at the System interface.

**NEW OPPORTUNITY: STRUCTURAL REPAIR PROTOCOL.** **REWARD: 50 TREATMENT POINTS.** **PENALTY FOR FAILURE: PERMANENT SITE QUARANTINE.**

Dale looked at the cracks. They were everywhere. Thousands of them.

He didn't have the materials to fix them. He didn't have the time.

But he had a clipboard, and he looked at Marla.

"We need more foam. And we need a ladder."

Marla looked at the throne. The stone figure's eyeless face was turned toward Dale. It raised its pen again.

It didn't write a violation.

It wrote a request.

**REQUEST: ASSISTANCE.**

The text appeared in Dale's vision, and he frowned. The Authority never asked for assistance. The Authority imposed penalties.

Unless the Auditor was stuck. Unless the Auditor was trapped in a loop it couldn't break without human intervention.

Dale stepped closer to the throne. The spawn parted for him.

He looked at the stone figure. The figure held out the clipboard.

On the paper, there was a diagram.

A complex, swirling geometric shape. It looked like a map. A map of the tunnel.

But the map was incomplete.

There was a hole in the map. A gap in the data.

Dale recognized the gap. It was the entrance to the next sector. The one the Authority hadn't logged yet.

The Auditor couldn't audit what it couldn't see.

Dale looked at Marla.

"It needs us to fill in the blanks."

Marla looked at the map.

"That's suicide.

That's a blind spot. If we go in there, we're off the grid.

The Authority can't track us.

"

"And if we don't," Dale said, "the crack stays open. The spawn keep coming.

And the Auditor fails its inspection.

If it fails, it escalates. To Level VIII.

"

Marla paled. Level VIII was city-wide.

Dale took the clipboard from the stone figure. The stone was cold, heavier than it looked.

He pulled his own pen from his vest, and he looked at the blank space on the map.

"I'll go in first," Dale said.

"Dale, no—"

"I'm the one with the Field Guide. I'm the one who has to document it. If I die, you take my clipboard."

Marla grabbed his arm.

"You don't have a weakness for these things. You don't have a protocol for this map."

Dale looked at the map. He looked at the gap.

He looked at the System prompt.

**FIELD GUIDE ENTRY: ARCHITECT SPAWN.** **STATUS: INCOMPLETE.** **REQUIRED DATA: BEHAVIORAL PATTERN IN DARKNESS.**

He didn't know the behavior. No one did.

But he knew pest control. He knew that creatures avoided light. He knew that they navigated by scent. He knew that they feared vibration.

He looked at his crowbar. He looked at the stone floor.

"I'm going to make noise," Dale said. "I'm going to draw them out. And I'm going to watch them move."

Marla looked at him with horror.

"That's bait."

"That's procedure," Dale said.

He walked away from the throne. He walked toward the darkness beyond the map's edge.

He didn't look back.

Marla watched him go. She looked at the stone figure. The figure's pen was still. It was waiting for the report.

Marla keyed her comms.

"Unit Four. We are entering a blind spot.

Prepare for extraction in ten minutes.

If we don't come back, detonate the entrance.

"

The comms crackled.

"Copy, Unit Four. Detonation armed."

Marla looked at the darkness where Dale had disappeared. She heard a faint *clang* from the crowbar.

Then she heard the spawn.

Not clicking. Screaming.

The sound was wet, high-pitched, and wrong. It wasn't the sound of monsters attacking. It was the sound of monsters running.

Running from what?

Marla raised her flashlight. She stepped into the darkness.

The beam hit a wall. The wall was moving.

The stone was shifting, rearranging itself. The floor was rippling.

And in the center of the room, Dale was standing. He was writing.

The spawn were fleeing past him, ignoring him completely. They were running toward the ceiling.

Dale looked up. He pointed at the ceiling.

"Look," he said.

Marla looked up, and the ceiling was gone.

Above them was a sky. A black sky, filled with stars that weren't stars.

They were eyes.

Thousands of them.

Blinking.

The Authority wasn't just auditing the tunnel, which was auditing the sky.

And the eyes were looking back.

The System prompt flashed, red and urgent.

**CLASSIFICATION: EXTERNAL THREAT DETECTED.** **CERTIFICATION LEVEL REQUIRED: IX.** **YOU DO NOT HAVE PERMISSION TO VIEW THIS.**

The screen went black.

Dale's clipboard fell from his hand.
