Chapter 248: Bait and Signature

# Chapter 248

The clipboard felt heavy.

That was the first thing anyone noticing Dale Kowalski from a distance would register. Not the blood drying in the cracks of his knuckles. Not the grey suit now stained with ichor and street sludge. Not the fact that he stood perfectly still in the center of a crater that used to be 4th and Elm.

He held the clipboard like a shield.

His thumb hovered over the dotted line.

The pen in the Authority figure's hand dripped black fluid onto the floating parchment. It didn't smell like ink. It smelled like copper and old rain.

The spiders on his legs tightened. They weren't biting. They were measuring.

From the edge of the destroyed block, a new voice cut through the silence.

"Stop."

Dale didn't turn. His eyes stayed on the contract. The signature line pulsed with a faint, rhythmic light. A heartbeat.

The Authority figure didn't look up. It kept its gaze locked on Dale's thumb.

"Inspector Halloway," the figure said, without turning. "You are out of jurisdiction."

Halloway stepped over a twisted lamppost. He wore a pristine white uniform, spotless despite the chaos.

No clipboard.

No spray bottle. Just a glowing rod of pure energy at his hip.

"He hasn't signed," Halloway said. His voice was tight. Controlled. "You can't enforce asset forfeiture on a Class II tech without a witnessed countersignature."

The Authority figure tilted its head. The movement was too smooth. Like oil sliding over glass.

"Protocol 7-Alpha allows for immediate acquisition when contamination is detected at the point of contact," the figure said. "Technician Kowalski is already tagged. The contract is merely a formality."

"It's a trap," Halloway said.

He took another step forward. His boots crunched on shattered asphalt.

"You're using the Cross-Contamination penalty as a legal hook.

He used a rodent protocol on an arachnid swarm. That's a debuff, not a surrender.

"

The Authority figure smiled. The spider-mask rippled.

"Thoroughness Rating is irrelevant if the technician is no longer free to perform thoroughness," it said.

Dale's hand shook.

The pen touched the paper.

A drop of black liquid splashed onto the blank space beneath the line.

*ERROR,* the System text flashed in his peripheral vision. *INVALID SUBSTRATE. RESIGNATURE REQUIRED.*

The spiders on his legs twitched.

Halloway raised his hand. The energy rod hummed, low and dangerous.

"Kowalski," Halloway said. "Don't move."

Dale looked at Halloway. Then at the Authority. Then at the spiders crawling up his pant leg.

He realized the truth in a cold, sharp click of recognition.

The spiders weren't tagging him to own him, and they were tagging him to *qualify* him.

If he signed, he became an asset. An employee. Subject to their direct command.

If he refused, he remained a licensed independent contractor.

But the Authority wasn't offering him a job. They were offering him a leash.

And Halloway, with his shiny rod and his clean uniform, wasn't here to save him. He was here to ensure the leash snapped.

Because if Dale became an asset, Halloway couldn't kill him.

If Dale remained a contractor, Halloway could classify him as 'Unlicensed Hazard' and purge the block, which was a bureaucratic pincer.

Dale's mind raced. He looked at the Field Guide entry in his vision.

*Dimensional Vermin (Rattus profundus).* *Arachnid Variant (Araneus voidus).*

He had documented both. He had isolated the broodmother. He had eliminated the swarm.

But he hadn't documented the *interaction*, and the System rules were clear. You get points for extermination. You get debuffs for mixing protocols.

But there was a loophole in the Thoroughness Scale. Section 4, Subsection C: *Documentation of Cross-Species Hybridization.*

If he could prove the spiders weren't just a different species, but a *hybrid* caused by the Authority's own negligence in sealing the rift, the classification changed.

He didn't need to fight them, and he needed to file a complaint.

Dale looked at the pen.

He looked at Halloway.

"Inspector," Dale said. His voice was flat. Deadpan. "Is your scanner calibrated for Class 4 bio-hazards?"

Halloway frowned.

"My scanner is military-grade. What does it matter?"

"Because if you're reading this infestation as Class 2, you're violating Code 11-B. Unauthorized containment of a dimensional leak."

Halloway paused. The energy rod lowered slightly.

The Authority figure stopped smiling.

"What are you doing, Technician?" the figure asked.

Dale dropped the pen.

It clattered to the ground, and he pulled his own pen from his pocket. His cheap, plastic, click-pen.

He clicked the cap off.

"I'm amending the report," Dale said.

He didn't sign the contract.

He wrote on the air.

His handwriting was terrible. Scrawled. Aggressive.

*SUBJECT: AUTHORITY PRESENCE.* *ACTION: UNLICENSED OBSERVATION DURING ACTIVE FIELDWORK.* *VIOLATION: INTERFERENCE WITH CLASS II PROTOCOLS.*

The System text flared bright red.

**CONFLICT DETECTED.** **CLAIMS OVERLAPPING.** **AWAITING ADJUDICATION.**

The Authority figure's face hardened. The grey suit seemed to ripple, as if the fabric itself was recoiling.

"You cannot file a claim against the Authority," the figure said. Its voice lost its smoothness. It sounded like grinding stones.

"Page 42," Dale said. He didn't look up.

He kept writing.

"The Licensing Authority is a municipal adjunct.

Subject to municipal complaint procedures. Unless you want to admit you're not part of the city government?

"

Halloway stepped back. His eyes went wide.

"You're calling their bluff," Halloway whispered.

"I'm calling their jurisdiction," Dale corrected.

The spiders on his legs stopped moving, and they froze.

The contract in the air flickered.

The Authority figure looked at Halloway. Then at Dale.

"Adjudication requires a third party," the figure said. "A neutral arbiter."

"There is one," Dale said. He pointed his pen at the collapsed manhole.

The darkness inside the hole shifted.

Something large was moving down there. Not rats. Not spiders.

"Who sent the rift?" Dale asked. "Who opened the door? Was it you, or was it *them*?"

Halloway looked at the manhole. He went pale.

"The Rift isn't an accident," Halloway said. "It's a harvest."

Dale felt a chill crawl up his spine, and he looked at his Field Guide.

*Dimensional Vermin.* *Arachnid Variant.*

But now, a new line appeared.

**UNKNOWN ENTITY DETECTED.** **CLASS: UNKNOWN.** **PROTOCOL: ESCALATE.**

The Authority figure looked at the manhole. For the first time, it looked afraid.

"Technician," the figure said. "Do not activate your Field Guide on that entity."

Dale looked at the dark hole, and he looked at Halloway, who was backing away, his energy rod charging to a blinding white.

He looked at the spiders, which were now retreating from his legs, scurrying back toward the Authority's feet.

The Authority was pulling back.

The Authority was afraid of what was in the manhole.

And Halloway was charging his weapon to burn the whole block down.

Dale had two choices.

Sign the contract, become the Authority's tool, and survive the purge because assets are valuable.

Or, document the entity.

If he documented the entity, he could claim Thoroughness points for the identification.

But identifying an unknown Class-9 entity meant his Field Guide would update.

And updating the Guide required *reading* it.

You had to read the weakness to get the damage bonus.

You couldn't get the bonus if you didn't know what the thing was.

And you couldn't know what it was if you didn't let it out.

The manhole lid groaned.

Metal shrieked against metal.

Halloway raised his rod.

"Clear the area, Kowalski. This is a sanitization event."

The Authority figure stepped forward.

"Wait. Let the technician document. We need the data."

"No!" Halloway shouted. "If he documents it, the Authority gains the data. They won't stop.

They'll send more rifts. They'll monetize the horror.

"

Dale stood between them, and he looked at the pen in his hand.

He looked at the floating contract, still waiting.

He looked at the manhole.

He clicked his pen.

And he stepped toward the hole.

"Protocol Alpha-Seven," Dale said. "Visual identification."

He leaned over the edge.

Darkness stared back.

A voice whispered from the pit. It wasn't loud. It wasn't scary.

It sounded like his mother.

"Dale," the voice said. "Check the nest."

Dale's breath hitched, and he knew that voice.

He hadn't heard it in twenty years.

He looked at the Authority.

The figure was trembling.

"Dale," the voice said again. "I'm not the monster. I'm the bait."

The manhole exploded outward.

Dale didn't run, and he opened his Field Guide.

And he wrote.
