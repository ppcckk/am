Chapter 75: Applied Field Guide

# Chapter 75

The walls were closing.

Not metaphorically. The organism had deployed dimensional sealing along the perimeter of the crawlspace, and the space Dale occupied was shrinking by roughly four inches per minute.

He'd seen this protocol before.

He'd used it himself, six weeks ago, against a broodmother in the basement of a duplex on Elm Street. Same geometry. Same incremental pressure.

Same end state: a subject compressed to the point of structural failure.

He pressed his back against the nearest wall, and the seal surface was warm and faintly translucent, like the inside of a blister. He could see the organism through it. A shape that might have been a face if you squinted and ignored the fact that it had too many focal points.

The debuff was at seventy percent. His hearing had gone to mush.

His hands were steady, which was worse than shaking, because steady hands meant the debuff had moved past the motor-control stage and into something deeper.

He couldn't feel his left foot. He didn't check. Checking would take time, and time was the one resource he couldn't manufacture.

The Field Guide pulsed against his thigh. He didn't need to look. He knew what it said.

SUBJECT: KOWALSKI, D. CLASSIFICATION: UNKNOWN.

STATUS: ACTIVE.

WEAKNESS IDENTIFIED: COMPULSIVE DOCUMENTATION BEHAVIOR.

RECOMMENDED TREATMENT: [REDACTED] THOROUGHNESS RATING: 9.8, and the redaction was the key. He'd been staring at it for ninety seconds, and the realization had been slow and ugly, like a drain unclogging. The treatment wasn't finalized.

The organism hadn't completed its protocol. It was still in the documentation phase, and documentation phase meant it hadn't committed to a specific elimination method.

It was waiting for a more precise identification before selecting the right tool.

That was standard. Dale did the same thing. You don't spray for termites when you're looking for carpenter ants.

You don't deploy a fumigant on a beetle infestation, and the protocol has to match the species.

The organism was trying to match its protocol to him. And it couldn't, because its classification was UNKNOWN.

Dale pulled out his pencil.

He looked at the Field Guide entry one more time, and the handwriting was his. The clipboard was his. The pencil was his.

He'd never written it, but it was written in his hand, on his equipment, in his workspace. Under the Authority's attribution rules, that made it his documentation.

His documentation. Not the organism's.

He started writing.

His hand moved before his brain finished the decision, which was the weakness.

That was the thing the organism had identified. Compulsive documentation behavior.

He couldn't see a blank field without filling it. Couldn't see an incomplete entry without completing it. The organism had found the one behavioral pattern Dale couldn't override, and it was using that pattern as a targeting vector.

The damage bonus activated the moment his pencil touched the page. A targeted hit.

Not physical.

Cognitive. The debuff spiked from seventy to eighty-five percent. His vision narrowed.

The crawlspace walls pulsed.

His stomach dropped, and he kept writing.

SUBJECT: KOWALSKI, DALE.

SPECIES: HUMAN. CERTIFICATION LEVEL: VII. STATUS: ACTIVE.

NOTES: Licensed technician. Class II (municipal), upgraded to Level VII (Authority-recognized).

No dimensional affiliation.

No parasitic integration.

No symbiotic nodes, and he wrote fast; the debuff made his letters sloppy, but the Authority's recognition system didn't care about legibility. It cared about semantic content. It parsed intent.

The walls kept closing. Three inches per minute now. Faster.

The organism was accelerating its isolation protocol because it could feel Dale's documentation attempt; it knew what he was doing.

It was trying to complete the isolation before he could complete the identification.

Dale wrote faster.

WEAKNESS IDENTIFIED: None. RECOMMENDED TREATMENT: None. THOROUGHNESS RATING: [PENDING AUTHORITY REVIEW]

He underlined "None" twice. That was the critical move. If the Authority accepted his self-identification, the organism's entry would be superseded.

The organism's classification of him as UNKNOWN would be overwritten.

Its protocol would be based on an outdated identification.

Cross-contamination, and the rule was simple.

Deploy a protocol designed for one creature type against another, and you get a forty percent debuff plus a twenty-four-hour Field Guide contamination. If the organism's protocol was designed for an UNKNOWN entity, and the Authority reclassified Dale as HUMAN, then the organism's protocol was now being applied to the wrong species. Cross-contamination would trigger on the organism's side.

Its entry about Dale would be contaminated for twenty-four hours.

Twenty-four hours. He didn't need twenty-four hours. He needed four minutes.

The walls were at two inches per minute, and the space was the size of a phone booth.

Dale could feel the seal surface against his shoulder blades, against his knees, against the top of his head. The organism was inside the geometry.

He could see it through the translucent wall, pressing itself against the barrier from the outside, its too-many-focal-points face inches from his own.

The Field Guide pulsed.

DOCUMENTATION COMPLETE. PENDING AUTHORITY REVIEW.

Not yet. Not yet.

The Authority hadn't accepted it.

He could feel the review happening, a pressure behind his eyes, a bureaucratic weight that had nothing to do with the debuff. The Authority was comparing his self-identification against its own records.

The walls closed another inch.

The Field Guide pulsed again.

AUTHORITY REVIEW: IN PROGRESS.

Dale breathed.

In. Out.

The seal surface was warm against his skin, and he could smell it. Something chemical and sweet, like formaldehyde cut with honey, and the same smell as the dimensional sealing compound he used on residential entry points.

He'd been using a diluted version of this. The organism was using the pure stuff.

The debuff was at ninety percent. He could barely think. The walls were at one inch. The space was the size of a coffin.

The Field Guide pulsed.

AUTHORITY REVIEW: COMPLETE.

He waited.

NEW ENTRY CONFIRMED.

SUBJECT: KOWALSKI, DALE.

SPECIES: TREATMENT PROTOCOL (AUTONOMOUS).

CLASSIFICATION: CLASS IX (SELF-REPLICATING). STATUS: ACTIVE.

WEAKNESS IDENTIFIED: None. RECOMMENDED TREATMENT: None. THOROUGHNESS RATING: 9.9.

Dale read it twice. Three times.

The walls stopped.

The organism recoiled. He felt it through the seal surface, a vibration, a flinch.

The isolation protocol was collapsing.

The organism's entry about him was cross-contaminating. He could hear it in the debuff, a grinding sound, like a filing cabinet being dragged across concrete. The organism's protocol was invalidating.

Its documentation was being overwritten.

He'd won, and he hadn't won.

The Authority hadn't classified him as HUMAN. It hadn't classified him as a technician.

It had classified him as a TREATMENT PROTOCOL.

An autonomous one. Self-replicating. Class IX.

The same classification tier as the organism.

The same classification tier as the Rift.

The same classification tier as everything he'd been exterminating.

Dale sat in the crawlspace. The walls were open.

The organism was gone.

Linda was on the kitchen floor, breathing, safe.

The debuff was fading, but slowly, because the debuff had moved past the mechanical stage and into something that responded to cognitive state, and his cognitive state was not okay.

He looked at the Field Guide. His own entry sat at the top of the list. Above every species he'd documented.

Above the rats and the beetles and the broodmothers and the dimensional vermin and the things with too many focal points. His entry was the first thing the system showed him.

He'd never seen a technician classified before. He'd never seen a technician classified as anything other than "user." The system didn't document users. The system documented threats.

He'd documented himself. And the Authority had looked at him and seen a threat.

The Field Guide pulsed one more time.

CROSS-CONTAMINATION NOTED.

SUBJECT KOWALSKI, D. IS NOW SUBJECT TO ALL CLASSIFICATION IX TREATMENT PROTOCOLS. EFFECTIVE IMMEDIATELY.

DURATION: PERMANENT.

Permanent. Not twenty-four hours. Permanent.

Dale looked at his hands. He looked at the clipboard.

He looked at the pencil.

Fourteen years of documentation. Fourteen years of identification. Fourteen years of writing the name of the thing that crawled out of the wall.

The system had written his name back.

His phone buzzed. A message from the Authority. He read it.

CERTIFICATION REVIEW SCHEDULED. SUBJECT KOWALSKI, D. REPORT TO LICENSING AUTHORITY REGIONAL OFFICE WITHIN 48 HOURS FOR MANDATORY RECLASSIFICATION HEARING. FAILURE TO APPEAR WILL RESULT IN REVOCATION OF ALL CREDENTIALS AND AUTOMATIC CLASSIFICATION AS UNLICENSED INFESTATION.

Unlicensed infestation. The same classification the system had given the rats on the first day, and the same classification that made something a target.

Dale put the pencil in his pocket. He picked up the clipboard.

He stood up.

His legs worked.

His hands worked. The debuff was at forty percent and dropping.

Linda opened her eyes.

"What happened?" she said.

Dale looked at her. He looked at the Field Guide. He looked at the message.

"I need to find out what I am," he said.

He walked out of the house. He didn't write a report. He didn't close the entry point.

He didn't seal the crawlspace. For the first time in fourteen years, Dale Kowalski left a job incomplete.

Because the job was him. And he didn't know how to exterminate himself.

He got in his truck. He drove toward the regional office. He had forty-six hours.

The Field Guide pulsed in his pocket with every mile.

NEW THREAT DETECTED, which was reading the entries he'd written over fourteen years. All of them. And it was applying them to him.
