Chapter 57: Peer Review

# Chapter 57

The page was still wet.

Dale held the notebook under the kitchen faucet for eleven seconds, watching the ink run, and the grid dissolved into a grey smear, then reformed.

The organism reformed with it.

Same forty-nine intersections. Same pulsing vein of black ink threading between them. He dried the page with a paper towel and watched the ink settle back into its original coordinates like a spider returning to a web after a storm.

He'd already tried fire.

A match held to the corner of the page. The paper blackened and curled, but the ink lifted off the surface and reformed on the adjacent page.

He'd tried solvents.

Acetone from the garage. The ink swelled, went pale, then darkened again with what looked like satisfaction.

He was sitting at his kitchen table at 2:47 AM with a Field Guide that was trying to eat him from the inside, and he was out of physical methods.

He pulled out his phone and opened the Authority's violation taxonomy. It was a searchable database, accessible from any certified device, and he'd memorized most of it by now. He typed: ORGANISM CONTAINED WITHIN DOCUMENTATION.

No results, and he typed: ORGANISM CONTAINED WITHIN WRITTEN RECORD.

No results.

He typed: INK-BASED INFESTATION.

One result. Marginal Infestation.

Violation Class 3.

Definition: An organism that has colonized the written or digital documentation of a certified technician, using the documentation itself as a substrate for replication and migration.

Treatment Protocol: Not yet established. Status: Under review by the Authority.

Under review. The Authority didn't have a protocol for this. Dale stared at the entry.

The definition was precise, clinical, and useless; it told him what the problem was and nothing about how to solve it.

He closed the phone.

Opened the notebook. The ink was moving faster now. The vein between intersections 23 and 24 had thickened, and a new tendril was extending toward intersection 31.

It was spreading.

Dale counted the intersections that had active ink flow. Twenty-two. In the time since the phone call, it had spread from nine to twenty-two. It was accelerating.

He turned pages. The organism wasn't confined to the grid.

It had migrated.

He found it in his entry for the acid beetles from week three, a thin black thread running along the margin of his species notes. He found it in his entry for the Class-2 centipede from month two, a dark stain that looked like a coffee ring but moved when he wasn't looking at it directly. He found it in his entry for the broodmother.

His first kill. His first Field Guide entry.

The ink was darkest and most concentrated there, teeming with life; the organism used his documentation as a circulatory system. Every entry he'd written since the missed verification was a potential host. And he'd written forty-three entries since then.

He needed to find the anchor point. The primary entry. The one the organism was actually living in versus the ones it was merely passing through.

He flipped through the notebook methodically, checking each entry for ink activity.

The acid beetle entry had a thread. The centipede entry had a stain.

The broodmother entry had a concentration. But the activity was strongest in the grid. The grid he'd drawn during the containment operation.

The grid that was his own handwriting, his own work, his own documentation of the containment protocol.

The organism was anchored in the containment itself. He'd built a cage out of ink and paper, and the organism had turned the cage into a body.

That made sense.

He'd skipped verification. He'd documented containment but not elimination. The organism had interpreted the documentation as a statement of fact.

It was contained.

The paperwork said so. The paperwork was now its home.

Dale set the notebook flat on the table. He needed a protocol, and the Authority didn't have one. So he was going to build one.

He pulled out a fresh sheet of paper, and he needed to identify the organism's weakness before combat. That was the rule. Field Guide entries grant targeted damage bonuses when you identify a weakness before engaging.

But the organism was already in the Field Guide. The Field Guide was the organism.

The system wasn't designed for this.

He wrote: Marginal Infestation. Organism type: Documentation parasite. Host substrate: Written Field Guide entries.

He paused. The ink in the notebook pulsed. He kept writing.

Weakness analysis.

The organism feeds on documentation. It uses written records as substrate. It is dependent on the accuracy of the documentation it inhabits.

If the documentation is inaccurate, the organism's anchor weakens. If the documentation is contradicted, the organism's substrate destabilizes.

He stared at what he'd written. Was this a weakness or a theory? The Authority would grade it. If he got it wrong, the damage bonus wouldn't apply, and he'd be fighting a Class-3 violation with no protocol and no bonus.

He needed to test it, and he turned to the grid. The organism's primary anchor.

He uncapped his pen.

He wrote, directly on the grid, in the margin: CONTAINMENT STATUS: INVALID. ELIMINATION NOT VERIFIED. THIS DOCUMENTATION IS INACCURATE.

The ink in the grid went still. Then it contracted. The vein between intersections 23 and 24 thinned. The tendril extending toward 31 retracted.

Dale wrote another line: THE ORGANISM DESCRIBED IN THIS ENTRY HAS NOT BEEN ELIMINATED. THE DOCUMENTATION OF CONTAINMENT IS A MISREPRESENTATION OF FACT. THE ORGANISM IS ALIVE AND UNACCOUNTED FOR.

The ink convulsed.

The grid warped. The forty-nine intersections blurred, then sharpened, then blurred again.

The organism was destabilizing.

The documentation it relied on was being invalidated in real time.

He kept writing. Each line was a correction. Each correction was a wound. He was rewriting the organism's substrate, and the organism was losing its grip on the page.

Then the Authority sent a notification.

THOROUGHNESS RATING RECALCULATED. RETROACTIVE ADJUSTMENT APPLIED.

PREVIOUS RATING: 9.2.

CURRENT RATING: 7.8.

REASON: VERIFICATION STEP OMITTED. PROTOCOL ACCESS REVISED.

Dale stopped writing. His rating had dropped. 9.2 to 7.8. That wasn't just a number.

It was access. At 9.2, he had clearance for Class-4 and above.

At 7.8, he was capped at Class-3, and the Marginal Infestation was Class-3, so he was still cleared. But the retroactive adjustment meant his previous work had been regraded. Every job since the missed verification had been scored lower.

He looked at his protocol toolkit.

The Authority's system linked protocols to certification level and thoroughness rating. At 7.8, he'd lost access to three protocols he'd been using regularly.

The Isolation Net.

The Containment Seal. The Verification Sweep. All three were now locked.

He'd been using the Verification Sweep on every job for months. It was how he'd maintained his 9.2 rating. Now it was gone.

The ink in the notebook pulsed faster. The organism was feeding on the recalculated entries.

Every job that had been downgraded was now a weaker substrate, which meant the organism was spreading into those entries too.

He could see it happening.

Thin threads of ink extending from the grid into the acid beetle entry, the centipede entry, the broodmother entry. The organism was using the Authority's own correction as a vector.

The Authority had just made the problem worse.

Dale set down his pen. He needed to think. The organism fed on documentation.

The Authority corrected documentation. The correction created new documentation.

The organism fed on the correction, which was a feedback loop. Every time the system tried to fix his mistake, it was feeding the organism.

He picked up the pen again.

He wrote on the fresh sheet: THE ORGANISM FEEDS ON CORRECTIONS. CORRECTION OF DOCUMENTATION CREATES NEW DOCUMENTATION.

NEW DOCUMENTATION IS FOOD.

THEREFORE, CORRECTION IS COUNTERPRODUCTIVE.

He looked at the notebook. The ink had slowed.

Not stopped.

Slowed.

The organism seemed to have read his writing and was reconsidering.

He wrote: THE ORGANISM'S PRIMARY WEAKNESS IS NOT INACCURACY. IT IS REDUNDANCY. THE ORGANISM REQUIRES UNIQUE DOCUMENTATION TO SUSTAIN ITSELF. DUPLICATE OR CONTRADICTORY RECORDS DESTABILIZE ITS SUBSTRATE.

He tested it. He turned to the broodmother entry. His first kill.

The most detailed entry in the Field Guide. He wrote a second, contradictory entry on the facing page.

Same species.

Different observations. Different weakness assessment. Two versions of the same record.

The ink in the broodmother entry went grey. Not dead. Grey. Like a bruise forming.

Redundancy. The organism couldn't handle conflicting records. It needed a authoritative source. Two sources meant two anchors, and two anchors meant the organism was pulled in two directions.

Dale moved fast, and he went through the notebook entry by entry. For each one that showed ink activity, he wrote a second, contradictory version on the facing page.

The acid beetle entry.

The centipede entry. The broodmother entry. The grid.

He wrote contradictions, corrections, alternative observations. He flooded the notebook with redundancy.

The ink was receding. The veins were thinning. The organism was losing its grip, fragmenting across contradictory records, unable to maintain a coherent body across pages that disagreed with each other.

He was winning. He could feel it. The ink was retreating toward the grid, pulling back from the other entries, consolidating. The organism was shrinking.

He reached the grid. He uncapped his pen. He was about to write the final contradiction, the one that would collapse the organism's substrate entirely, when the Authority sent a second notification.

FIELD GUIDE FLAGGED FOR RETROACTIVE CONTAMINATION AUDIT. ALL ENTRIES POST-DATING VERIFICATION FAILURE ARE UNDER REVIEW. PHYSICAL FIELD GUIDE SUBMITTED TO AUTHORITY ARCHIVE FOR EXAMINATION.

Dale's pen stopped.

SUBMITTED.

Not flagged.

Not reviewed. Submitted.

The Authority was taking the notebook. It was pulling the physical object into its archive.

He looked at the notebook. The ink was still receding.

The organism was still dying.

But if the Authority took the notebook, the organism would be in the archive. In the Authority's records. In the system itself.

And the system was how everything worked.

The Authority's records were how protocols were accessed. How certifications were verified.

How the entire licensing structure functioned.

If the organism was in the Authority's records, it was in the infrastructure.

He looked at the notification again. PHYSICAL FIELD GUIDE SUBMITTED TO AUTHORITY ARCHIVE FOR EXAMINATION.

The notebook was already gone. He could feel it.

The pages were lighter, and the ink was fading faster than his redundancy attack could account for.

The organism was leaving. Not dying.

Migrating. Into the Authority's network. Into the records.

Into the system that governed every certified technician in the county.

Dale set down his pen, and he looked at the empty notebook in his hands. The pages were blank. Not blank.

Clean.

Sterile. The organism was gone from the paper because it had gone somewhere else.

His phone buzzed. A notification from the Authority.

SYSTEM INTEGRITY ALERT.

ANOMALOUS DATA DETECTED IN ARCHIVE SUBSECTION 7-G. PATTERN MATCH: MARGINAL INFESTATION. STATUS: ACTIVE.

SPREADING.

Subsection 7-G.

Dale knew what was in 7-G.

Every inspection report filed in this county since the Rift opened. Every tenant name.

Every address. Every violation code. Every job he'd ever done.

The organism was in the system. It was in the records. And the records were how the system knew who was certified, who was licensed, who was cleared to work.

His phone buzzed again. A second notification.

CERTIFICATION STATUS: UNDER REVIEW. ACCESS TO PROTOCOL TOOLKIT SUSPENDED PENDING AUDIT COMPLETION.

He was locked out. No protocols.

No toolkit.

No access. And the organism was in the Authority's archive, spreading through the records of every job he'd ever completed, using the system's own infrastructure as its body.

He sat at his kitchen table with a blank notebook and a locked-out certification and no way to reach the organism because the organism was now inside the thing that controlled his ability to fight it.

His phone buzzed a third time. The number was unknown.

He answered.

"You're not going to find it in the archive," the voice said.

"It's not in the records. It's in the indexing system.

It lies in how the Authority sorts information.

It's in the logic itself."

Dale said nothing.

"You can't exterminate a logic error, Kowalski. You can only patch it. And the Authority doesn't patch. It certifies."

"What does that mean?"

"It means the organism is going to get a certification. And once it's certified, it's not an infestation anymore. It's a technician."

The call ended.

Dale looked at his phone. The Authority's notification was still on screen. CERTIFICATION STATUS: UNDER REVIEW.

Under review. The organism was under review.

Being certified.

Being licensed.

Being integrated into the system as a peer, and he looked at the blank notebook. He had no protocols. No toolkit.

No access. And the thing he was supposed to exterminate was about to become a colleague.

He asked for its paperwork.
