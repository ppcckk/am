Chapter 212: License Suspension

# Chapter 212

Blackness.

Absolute and total.

The sensation of falling lasted precisely 0.4 seconds. A vacuum seal broke with a wet, sucking sound. Air rushed in. Cold, stale, and smelling of ozone and burnt copper.

Dale hit the floor.

Not the tiled bathroom floor. Not the metal grating of the vent shaft.

Concrete.

Cold, polished, industrial concrete.

He rolled. The impact jarred his teeth.

His knees buckled, absorbing the fall.

He scrambled up, boots slipping on a slick surface. Oil? Water?

His hands were still glued.

The wrench remained fused to the door handle.

The door handle remained fused to the frame. The frame remained fused to the wall.

But the creature was gone.

The room was gone.

Dale stood in a vast, circular chamber. The ceiling was lost in shadows. Pillars of black iron stretched upward, disappearing into darkness. The floor was a grid of gray tiles, stained with centuries of grime.

*Ping.*

A notification flashed in his peripheral vision. Bright blue text against the dark.

**ZONE TRANSFER COMPLETE.** **LOCATION: SUB-BASEMENT 4, MUNICIPAL UTILITY INFRASTRUCTURE.** **STATUS: UNLICENSED ENTRY.** **WARNING: IMMEDIATE TERMINATION OF BIOLOGICAL FUNCTIONS IMMINENT.**

Dale ignored it.

He looked at his hands. The glue held. The wrench dangled uselessly.

He looked at the ceiling. Vargus was not there. The shaft was gone.

He looked at the far wall. A heavy steel door, marked with a red skull and crossbones.

*Hazardous Material Storage.*

Dale walked toward it. His boots left no footprints. The glue had dried instantly, becoming a hard, translucent shell around his fingers.

*Three seconds.*

The voice echoed again. Closer now. Inside his skull.

*Justify presence.*

Dale didn't stop walking. He pulled his clipboard from his back pocket.

It had survived the fall.

He flipped to a fresh page.

He uncapped a pen. His hand shook, just slightly.

The pen tip hovered over the paper.

He wrote: *Inspection.*

The air around him froze.

The temperature dropped twenty degrees in a blink. Frost crept across the floor, racing toward his feet.

A figure materialized from the shadows near the steel door.

It was tall. Seven feet.

Skin like gray parchment, stretched too tight over angular bones.

It wore a suit that looked like it had been tailored by a spider. Its face was a smooth, featureless oval, except for two vertical slits where eyes should be.

An Auditor.

Not the standard model. This one wore a sash of woven black wire across its chest. A Senior Auditor.

"Technician Kowalski," the Auditor said.

Its voice was the sound of paper tearing. "You are in a Restricted Zone.

Class IV Infestation Containment Site.

You have no badge. You have no permit. You have no authority."

Dale stopped ten feet away.

He held up the clipboard.

"Report," Dale said.

The Auditor tilted its head. The slits narrowed.

"Explain."

"Violation K-7," Dale said. His voice was flat.

Steady.

He tapped the clipboard with his glued index finger.

"Structural entry point. Compromised seal.

Uncontrolled dimensional bleed. I am here to document the breach."

The Auditor stepped closer. The frost spread. It reached Dale's boots.

"Kowalski."

"Yes."

"You did not pull the lever."

"No."

"You allowed the Class IV entity to breach containment."

"I allowed it to breach *local* containment. I initiated a transfer protocol."

The Auditor froze.

The slits widened.

"Transfer protocol," the Auditor repeated. "You are citing a non-existent protocol."

Dale turned the page on his clipboard.

There was a form there. *Form 114-B: Emergency Dimensional Redirection.*

It wasn't real. Not officially.

Dale had invented it in his head three seconds ago.

But the System loved paperwork. And the System loved thoroughness.

"I documented the breach," Dale said. "I identified the hazard. I isolated the threat. I eliminated the immediate vector."

"Where is the vector?" the Auditor asked.

Dale pointed to the steel door.

"Behind me."

The Auditor turned. The door was still shut.

But the seal around it was gone.

The metal was melting.

Not from heat. From decay.

The steel turned to gray powder, flaking away.

Behind it, something chittered. A high-pitched, clicking sound.

The Auditor spun back.

"You released it."

"No," Dale said. "I trapped it."

He pointed to the floor.

The frost had formed a perfect circle around the melting door. A containment ring.

"I used the ambient cold from the transfer zone to freeze the seal," Dale said. "The adhesive on my gloves created a thermal bridge. I initiated a localized containment field."

This was a lie.

The adhesive was industrial glue. The cold was a side effect of the Auditor's arrival.

But the System didn't check for truth.

It checked for *logic*.

And Dale's logic was sound, if you squinted.

The Auditor stared at the ring.

The chittering grew louder. More voices. Dozens.

The gray powder fell away from the door.

A leg emerged. Then another. Large.

Hairy. Black.

Chitin.

A Class IV Arachnid.

Not a spider. A *Rift Weaver*. A creature that spun webs out of raw time.

The Auditor raised a hand. A whip of black energy coiled around its wrist.

"Exterminate it," the Auditor commanded. "Or I terminate you."

Dale looked at the creature.

It was pulling itself through the hole. It was huge. The size of a minivan.

Its legs were segmented, sharp as scalpels.

Its abdomen pulsed with blue light.

Dale looked at his hands. Still glued.

He looked at the wrench. Useless.

He looked at the clipboard.

He opened his Field Guide.

The entry for *Rift Weaver* was blank. He had never seen one.

*Identification Required.*

*Weakness Required.*

*Protocol Required.*

He had no protocol.

But he had a violation.

Dale stepped forward.

The Auditor watched. The whip crackled.

Dale didn't attack the monster.

He walked to the side of the room. To a large, exposed pipe running along the wall. A steam line. Old, rusted, hissing.

He reached out with his glued hands.

He grabbed the pipe.

The heat was intense. His gloves sizzled. The glue softened.

*Warning: Thermal Damage.*

Dale ignored it.

He twisted the valve.

It was stiff. Rusted.

He put his weight behind it. He used the wrench as a lever, jamming it into the valve's crossbar. The wrench was stuck to his hand, but the handle was free.

He turned.

The valve groaned.

Screamed.

Steam erupted.

A cloud of white fog blasted into the room. It filled the space between Dale and the Arachnid.

The Arachnid shrieked. It hated heat. It hated moisture. Its time-webs dissolved in the steam.

*System Alert: Environmental Modifier Applied. Target Vulnerability: Thermal Shock.*

Dale pulled his hand back. He dropped the wrench.

He looked at the Auditor.

"Isolation complete," Dale said.

The Auditor stared at the steam. It stared at Dale.

"This is not extermination," the Auditor said.

"It is containment," Dale said. "I am not authorized to exterminate a Class IV without a Level V Certification. I am authorized to isolate."

The Auditor's whip faded.

The creature in the steam thrashed. Its legs cut through the vapor, but the webs were gone. It was blind. Deafened by the hiss.

"Good," the Auditor said. "You have bought time. But you have not solved the problem."

"No," Dale said. "I have paused it."

The Auditor stepped closer. It leaned in. Its face was inches from Dale's.

"You are a technician, Kowalski. You are not a hero."

"No," Dale said.

"You are a janitor."

"Correct."

"The Authority does not reward janitors. It rewards exterminators."

Dale looked at his Field Guide.

He had one entry. *Dimensional Vermin.*

He had a new entry. *Rift Weaver.*

But he had no weakness listed.

And he had a new notification.

**CROSS-CONTAMINATION WARNING.** **PROTOCOL MISMATCH.** **DEBUFF: 40%.** **FIELD GUIDE ENTRY LOCKED: 24 HOURS.**

He had used a steam protocol. Designed for mold. Against an arachnid.

His Field Guide was now useless for this creature.

He couldn't identify its weakness.

He couldn't target its weak point.

He was flying blind.

The steam thinned.

The Arachnid emerged. It was angry. It was fast.

The Auditor watched, arms crossed.

"Kill it," the Auditor said. "With your bare hands. Or die."

Dale looked at the creature.

He looked at the steam.

He looked at the pipe.

He had one move.

He reached into his pocket.

He pulled out a small vial.

It was his personal supply. *Concentrated Acid. For severe infestations.*

It was not approved for use in municipal zones.

It was not approved for use against Class IV threats.

It was illegal.

Dale uncapped the vial.

The smell was sharp. Vinegar and poison.

He threw it.

The vial shattered against the Arachnid's leg.

The acid hissed.

The leg turned black.

The creature screamed. It stumbled.

But it didn't die.

It lunged.

Dale didn't move.

He opened his mouth.

"Violation L-4," he said.

The Auditor blinked.

"Repeat."

"Improper disposal of hazardous materials," Dale said. "I am citing myself."

The System paused.

**CITATION ACCEPTED.** **PENALTY: 50 TREATMENT POINTS.** **BENEFIT: IMMEDIATE AUTHORITY INTERVENTION.**

The Auditor's eyes widened.

"You—"

The air above them cracked.

A rift opened. Not a monster rift.

A bureaucratic rift.

A beam of pure white light shot down from the ceiling. It hit the Arachnid.

The creature froze. Turned to stone. Crumbled to dust.

The silence was absolute.

Dale stood in the dust.

He looked at the Auditor.

"You didn't authorize that," the Auditor said.

"I cited the violation," Dale said. "The System handles enforcement."

The Auditor's face twisted. Rage. Pure, bureaucratic rage.

"You are out of line, Kowalski."

"I am within protocol."

"You are a pest."

"I am a technician."

The Auditor raised its hand.

The white light faded.

But the dust remained.

And the door behind the dust was gone.

gone.

A hole.

Leading deeper.

Down.

Into the dark.

Something else was there. Something bigger. Something that had been waiting for the Arachnid to clear the path.

A voice, low and rumbling, echoed up from the hole.

*Finally.*

Dale looked at the hole.

He looked at the Auditor.

The Auditor was backing away.

"Leave," the Auditor said. "This is no longer your zone."

"No," Dale said.

He picked up his clipboard.

He walked toward the hole.

"Where are you going?" the Auditor demanded.

"Inspection," Dale said.

He stepped off the edge.

He fell.

This time, he didn't hit the floor.

He landed in a web.

A sticky, thick, black web.

He was trapped.

The web tightened.

He couldn't move.

He couldn't breathe.

He couldn't see.

But he could hear.

The chittering was everywhere.

And a new voice.

Not in his head.

In his ear.

Whispering.

*Hello, Dale.*

It was a voice he knew.

His supervisor.

From three chapters ago.

The one who had died.

*Welcome to the nest, Technician.*

Dale struggled.

The web squeezed his ribs.

His vision blurred.

He tried to write.

His hand was stuck.

The pen was stuck.

The clipboard was stuck.

He couldn't document.

He couldn't identify.

He couldn't act.

He was just meat.

And then, a notification.

Red. Blinking.

**CRITICAL FAILURE.** **LICENSE SUSPENDED.** **PENDING REVOCATION.**

The web pulsed.

It was alive.

And it was hungry.
