Chapter 276: Ink Becomes Eggs

# Chapter 276

Dale stepped onto the dissolving asphalt. The ground beneath his boots felt spongy, like wet cardboard left in a rainstorm.

"Identification," Dale repeated. His voice was flat, cutting through the roaring static of the collapsing reality. "Step two: Documentation."

The armored figure did not move. The lightning spear hummed, a low, dangerous vibration that rattled Dale's teeth.

"You are holding a writing instrument," the figure boomed. The sound wasn't just loud; it was heavy. It pressed against Dale's eardrums. "Is this a challenge?"

"It is a tool," Dale said. He clicked the pen.

The black ink pooled at the tip, thick and viscous.

"And you are an unlicensed intrusion. Class unknown. Habitat unknown.

Behavior unknown.

"

The figure tilted its head. Chitinous plates shifted with the sound of grinding stones.

"Unknown," the figure said. "The Authority does not tolerate unknowns."

"That is why I am writing it down," Dale said. He raised the clipboard, which had miraculously survived the initial blast.

He wrote: *Entity: Armored.

Weapon: Energy Spear. Status: Hostile?* The question mark was crucial. If he declared it hostile without proof, he risked a cross-contamination penalty if it turned out to be a neutral guardian.

If he left it ambiguous, he wasted time.

The timer in his vision ticked down. 00:01:58.

Officer Miller was still standing behind the debris of a parked sedan. Her gun was lowered, but her knuckles were white. She looked at Dale, then at the giant.

"Dale," Miller shouted. "Do not provoke it. It's bigger than the mimic. And faster."

Dale ignored her, and he was watching the giant's feet.

They weren't just standing on the ground. They were rooting.

Tiny, hair-like filaments extended from the soles of the armored boots, sinking into the rift's substrate.

"Rooted," Dale muttered. He scribbled faster. *Behavior: Anchoring. Purpose: Stabilization?*

"Hey!" Miller yelled again. "Move!"

The giant raised its spear. The air around the tip ionized, turning purple.

Dale didn't move. He pointed the pen at the giant's chest plate.

"You are blocking the exit vector," Dale said. "You are causing secondary structural damage to the dimensional membrane. This is a code violation. Section 4, Paragraph B: Obstruction of Natural Flow."

The spear lowered. Not completely. Just enough to change the angle.

"Secondary structural damage," the figure repeated. The thunder-voice dropped to a gravelly rumble. "You speak of the Membrane as if it is drywall."

"It is infrastructure," Dale said. "And you are drilling into it without a permit."

Miller stared at him. Her mouth hung open.

"Dale, are you serious?"

Dale tapped the clipboard against his palm.

"Officer.

I need you to secure the perimeter.

If anyone else tries to engage this entity with kinetic force, they will trigger a backlash. The spear is drawing energy from the rift. If you shoot it, you feed it.

"

Miller blinked.

"How do you know?"

"Because it's glowing brighter with every micro-tremor," Dale said. "It's feeding on the instability. Your gunfire would be a buffet."

Miller lowered her rifle. She looked at the giant, then at Dale. A slow, horrified realization spread across her face.

"You're trying to file a complaint," she said.

"I am attempting to resolve the infestation through proper channels," Dale corrected. "Step three: Isolation."

Dale took a step closer. The giant didn't attack. It watched him. The lightning spear crackled, casting long, jagged shadows against the crumbling buildings.

"You are not a Hunter," the figure said.

"I am a Technician," Dale said. "Certification Level III. Specialty: Dimensional Breaches and Subterranean Vermin."

"A Technician," the figure mused. "The last Technician I saw was eaten by a Void Stalker in the Third Sector."

"That Technician likely failed to document the Stalker's pheromone trail before engaging," Dale said dryly.

"My protocol requires pre-engagement scent analysis. I do not have a nose for void scents, so I am relying on visual cues.

You are rooted.

You are feeding. Therefore, you are not here to fight. You are here to nest.

"

The giant went still. The silence was louder than the storm.

"Prove it," the figure said. The word was quiet, almost gentle.

Dale looked at the ground. The filaments from the giant's feet were spreading, pulsing with a faint blue light.

"If I sever the connection," Dale said, "the spear loses power. If I sever the connection, the rift stabilizes. If I sever the connection, you are removed."

"You would kill me?"

"I would terminate the infestation," Dale said. "There is a difference.

Killing is messy.

Termination is clean.

Termination leaves no residue.

"

Miller stepped forward, her boots crunching on broken glass.

"Dale, how do you sever it? You don't have a bomb. You don't have magic."

"I have the clipboard," Dale said. "And I have the Field Guide."

He opened the guide. The page for *UNKNOWN ENTITY* was blank, but the page for *Dimensional Membrane* was filled with notes. Notes he had made during the previous three chapters.

Notes about resonance frequencies. Notes about harmonic disruption.

"Officer Miller," Dale said. "What is the frequency of the sirens in this sector?"

Miller looked confused.

"What?"

"The emergency sirens. The ones that went off before the collapse. What is their pitch?"

"I don't know," Miller said. "Four hundred hertz? Maybe six hundred? I'm a cop, not a physicist."

"Approximation is sufficient for provisional identification," Dale said.

He looked at the giant.

"The Membrane vibrates at a specific frequency to maintain integrity. You are disrupting it.

Your spear is a tuning fork.

A bad one."

Dale clicked his pen again. He wrote: *Weakness: Resonance instability.*

He hadn't identified the weakness yet. The System wouldn't give him the bonus. But he could create a loophole.

"Step four: Elimination," Dale said.

He didn't attack the giant. He attacked the clipboard.

Dale held the clipboard flat, angled toward the giant's spear. He used the pen not as a weapon, but as a conductor. The ink was black, dense, and laced with trace minerals from the rift's dust. It was conductive.

"Watch," Dale said.

He dragged the pen across the paper, writing a long, jagged line. The friction generated a tiny spark. Static electricity.

The giant's spear pulsed.

"Your energy source is unstable," Dale said. "I am introducing a chaotic variable into your control loop."

The giant roared. Not in anger. In pain. The lightning on the spear flickered, turning from purple to a sickly yellow.

"What are you doing?" Miller shouted.

She raised her rifle again.

"Don't shoot!" Dale barked. The command was sharp, authoritative.

It carried the weight of a supervisor catching an employee violating safety protocols.

Miller froze. Her finger hovered over the trigger.

"He's messing with the magic," she whispered. "He's messing with the magic of a god-like being."

"He is messing with the infrastructure," Dale corrected. "And it is working."

The giant's armor cracked. Not from impact, but from vibration. The chitinous plates shivered, unable to hold their shape against the conflicting frequencies.

"Stop," the figure gasped.

The thunder-voice was gone.

Now it sounded human. Young.

Scared. "Please. The seal..."

"The seal is broken," Dale said. "You are the leak."

He wrote one more word on the clipboard: *Terminated.*

The System flashed.

**WEAKNESS IDENTIFIED: Resonance Disruption.** **BONUS ACTIVE: +50% Damage against Unstable Constructs.**

Dale didn't have a weapon to deal the damage. But he didn't need to hit the giant. He needed to hit the *connection*.

He slammed the clipboard against the asphalt. Hard. A physical impact.

The vibration traveled up the filaments. The giant's spear exploded. Not outward, but inward. The lightning imploded, sucking the energy back into the rift.

The giant screamed. The sound was like tearing metal.

The rift contracted violently. The suction returned, but this time, it was focused. It pulled the giant toward the center. The figure clawed at the ground, the filaments snapping one by one.

Miller grabbed Dale's arm.

"Dale! Get back!"

"I am observing the verification phase," Dale said. He didn't move. He watched the giant dissolve into mist, dragged back into the dark.

**INFESTATION RESOLVED.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 8.5.**

The timer stopped.

**TIME REMAINING: 00:00:00.**

Silence fell over the street. The dust settled. The streetlights flickered back on, buzzing weakly.

Miller slumped against the car. Her gun clattered to the ground. She looked at Dale, her eyes wide.

"You just... you just filed a paperwork violation," she said. "You killed a Hunter with a clipboard."

"I removed an obstacle," Dale said. He checked his pen.

The ink was dry.

Good. "It was obstructing the site. Site safety requires clear access."

Miller laughed, which was a hysterical, jagged sound.

"Site safety.

Right.

Sure."

She stood up, brushing glass from her uniform.

"The Authority. It said 'prove it.' You proved it. What happens now?"

Dale looked at his Field Guide. The *UNKNOWN ENTITY* page had populated.

**Name: Rift Warden (Juvenile).** **Class: Guardian.** **Threat: High.** **Weakness: Resonance Disruption.** **Notes: Prone to panic when anchor points are destabilized. Do not engage in melee. Exploit harmonic instability.**

"Step five: Verification," Dale said. He turned to Miller. "Officer. Did you witness the entity's dissolution?"

"Yes," Miller said. She was still shaking. "I saw it all."

"Good. I will need your signature on the incident report."

Miller stared at him.

"Now?"

"Immediately," Dale said. "The Authority requires real-time verification for Thoroughness bonuses. Any delay risks memory degradation and subsequent point deductions."

Miller rubbed her temples.

"You are impossible."

"I am thorough," Dale corrected. He held out the clipboard and the pen.

"Sign here.

And here.

And initial the section on 'Unlicensed Entity Removal.'

"

Miller took the pen. Her hand shook as she signed.

As the ink dried, a new notification appeared in Dale's vision. It was different from the usual white text. It was gold.

**CERTIFICATION UPGRADE AVAILABLE.** **PREREQUISITES MET: 10,000 Treatment Points. Thoroughness > 8.0. Unique Kill: Rift Warden.** **NEW PROTOCOL UNLOCKED: Bureaucratic Red Tape.** **DESCRIPTION: Apply a temporary debuff to any entity that violates a documented code.

Duration: 1 hour. Cost: 100 TP per application.**

Dale read the text twice.

*Bureaucratic Red Tape.*

He felt a strange sensation in his chest.

Not pride. Not excitement. Something colder.

Recognition.

The Authority didn't just reward him for killing monsters. It rewarded him for *understanding* them. And now, it had given him a tool that allowed him to weaponize procedure itself.

"Officer Miller," Dale said. "We need to file a secondary report."

Miller looked up, tired.

"Secondary?"

"Yes. The Rift is contracting, but the breach point remains unstable. The Authority's intervention was unauthorized. We need to document the side effects of the Hunter's arrival."

Miller groaned.

"Dale, please. Just let us go home. The night is over."

"The night is not over," Dale said. He looked at the spot where the giant had stood.

The asphalt was gone.

In its place was a hole, perfectly circular, leading down into darkness. And from that hole, a single object was rising.

It wasn't a monster, which was a briefcase.

A black, leather briefcase, floating upward on a column of smoke.

Dale's Field Guide popped up.

**UNKNOWN OBJECT.** **CLASS: RELIC.** **THREAT: EXTREME.**

Miller saw it too. She picked up her rifle.

"What is that?"

Dale took a step back. His hand went to his pocket, where he kept his backup clipboard.

"It is a submission," Dale said. "Someone else is filing a claim."

The briefcase hit the ground with a heavy thud. It didn't open. It waited.

The timer reset.

**NEW EVENT: LICENSING AUDIT.** **TIME LIMIT: 00:05:00.** **OBJECTIVE: Retrieve the Briefcase without triggering the Trap.**

Dale looked at Miller.

"Officer. I need you to create a distraction."

"A distraction? For what? A briefcase?"

"For what is inside it," Dale said. He clicked his pen. "Step one: Identify the trap."

Miller looked at the briefcase, then at Dale's face. She saw the calm, the cold, the absolute certainty.

"You know what it is," she said. It wasn't a question.

"I suspect," Dale said. "But suspicion is not documentation. And documentation is the only thing that keeps us alive."

He walked toward the briefcase.

"Wait," Miller said. She grabbed his arm.

"Dale.

The Authority sent a Hunter.

Then it sent a briefcase. Who is the Authority actually serving?

"

Dale stopped. He looked at the briefcase. The leather was embossed with a symbol.

A square. Inside the square, a fly.

"It serves the balance," Dale said. "And right now, the balance is tipping."

He reached for the briefcase.

The lid popped open on its own.

Inside, there was no monster. No bomb.

There was a single sheet of paper.

Dale picked it up, and the text was handwritten. In his own handwriting.

**YOU ARE UNDER REVIEW.** **CASE FILE: KOWALSKI, DALE.** **STATUS: PROBATION.**

Below the text was a single instruction.

**PROVE YOU ARE NOT THE INFESTATION.**

Dale looked up. The briefcase was gone. The hole was gone. The street was empty.

But the air in his lungs felt heavy. Thick. Like he was breathing in dust.

"Dale?" Miller asked. "What did it say?"

Dale folded the paper. He put it in his pocket.

"It says I need to update my resume," Dale said.

He looked at his hands. They were covered in black ink. But the ink was moving. Crawling under his skin.

"Step two," Dale said. "Contamination containment."

Miller backed away.

"Your hands..."

Dale looked at the pen. The ink was no longer in the cartridge. It was in his veins.

"It's not ink," Dale whispered. "It's eggs."

He looked at Miller.

"Do not touch me."

**SYSTEM ALERT: INTERNAL INFESTATION DETECTED.** **SPECIES: Rattus profundus (Variant).** **LOCATION: Bloodstream.** **THREAT: CRITICAL.**

Miller watched Dale’s jaw unhinge.
