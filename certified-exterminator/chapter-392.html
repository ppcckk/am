Chapter 392: Audit of the Rift

# Chapter 392

The void smelled of ozone and burnt hair.

Dale blinked. His vision swam, gray static crawling at the edges of his sight.

A high-pitched whine drilled into his left ear, the lingering resonance of the ion discharge.

He tried to move his arms. They felt heavy, leaden. The air around him was thick, viscous, like swimming through warm gel.

He was standing.

Not in the crater. Not in Ohio.

He was on a platform. Black obsidian, slick with condensation. Rain fell upward, droplets rising from the floor to vanish into a sky that was not a sky, but a grid of shifting geometric patterns. Neon lines pulsed with a rhythm that matched his heartbeat.

Dale looked down.

His chest protector was gone. His shirt was a singed rag hanging off his ribs.

No blood.

No wound. Just a black scorch mark in the shape of a circle.

A notification chimed. It didn't appear in his peripheral vision. It sounded inside his skull, sharp and metallic.

**SYSTEM ALERT: PHYSICAL DISPLACEMENT DETECTED.** **CURRENT LOCATION: INTERSTITIAL BUFFER ZONE 4.** **STATUS: UNAUTHORIZED ENTRY.** **PENALTY PENDING: 500 TREATMENT POINTS.**

Dale wiped soot from his forehead. He checked his pockets.

Empty.

No spray can.

No clipboard. No duct tape.

"Standard procedure for unauthorized entry involves immediate cessation of movement," Dale said. His voice sounded thin, stripped of its usual gravel.

The grid in the sky shifted. A shape descended. It wasn't a drone.

It was a construct of light and hard angles, resembling a human torso fused with a filing cabinet. It hovered before him, rotating slowly.

**ENTITY IDENTIFIED: LICENSING AUDITOR (TIER IV).** **ACTION: REQUEST INSPECTION.**

Dale straightened his spine. He adopted his neutral stance. Feet shoulder-width apart. Hands at his sides.

"I am Dale Kowalski," he said. "Certification Level Six. I was conducting a Class Nine eradication protocol in the municipal sector when a structural failure occurred. I did not choose to enter this zone."

The Auditor tilted its head. The light forming its face fractured into data streams.

**RESPONSE: LACK OF PRE-CLEARANCE FORM 12-B. UNLICENSED SPATIAL TRAVERSAL IS A CLASS A VIOLATION. YOUR CURRENT RATING IS NEGATIVE. DO YOU WISH TO APPEAL?**

Dale's hand twitched. He wanted his clipboard. He needed the blank pages. Without documentation, he was just meat in a vacuum.

"Appeal requires evidence of necessity," Dale said.

"I was mitigating an active biological hazard. The spores were airborne. The drone was malfunctioning.

My action prevented a regional infestation.

"

**RECORD CHECK: THE INFESTATION WAS NOT MITIGATED. IT WAS DISPERSED. THE REGION IS NOW COMPROMISED. YOUR ACTION IS CLASSIFIED AS NEGLIGENT DISCHARGE OF ENERGY.**

Dale frowned. "Dispersed? The spores ignited."

**RESULT: UNCONTROLLED EXPANSION. NEW BREACH POINTS DETECTED IN THREE ADJACENT SECTORS. THREAT LEVEL: RISING.**

A chill ran down Dale's spine. Not fear.

Professional dread, and he had solved the immediate problem but created a systemic failure. In pest control, if you don't seal the entry point, the ants come back. If you don't kill the queen, the colony regrows.

"I need to return," Dale said. "I need to finish the containment."

**DENIED. YOU ARE IN BUFFER ZONE 4. THE EXIT IS LOCKED UNTIL YOUR DEBT IS PAID OR YOUR STATUS IS REVOKED.**

The grid above him pulsed red. Dale looked around.

There were other platforms.

Dozens of them.

On some, shapes moved. Some were human.

Some were not. They were all working. They were all writing.

To his right, a figure sat on a stool, scratching at a stone tablet with a stylus. The figure had six arms. Its skin was chitinous, gray-green. It didn't look up.

Dale walked toward it. The rain hit his face, cold and tasting of copper.

"Excuse me," Dale said.

The figure kept writing.

Dale stepped closer. He saw the tablet. It was covered in dense, spiraling text. The ink was black, viscous.

"Are you certified?" Dale asked.

The figure stopped. It turned one head toward him. Its eyes were compound, reflecting the neon grid.

**FIELD GUIDE ENTRY: LICENSING AUDITORS (SECTOIDS).** **WEAKNESS: BUREAUCRATIC STUBBORNNESS. VULNERABLE TO LOGICAL PARADOXES OR INCOMPLETE FORMS.** **STATUS: UNIDENTIFIED. INSUFFICIENT DATA.**

Dale stared at the prompt. It appeared in his mind's eye, faint. The system was struggling to categorize him because he wasn't an enemy. He was an anomaly.

"I need a workspace," Dale said.

The Sectoid pointed a claw at the tablet. Then it pointed to a small pile of stones on the floor next to its stool.

Dale looked at the stones. They were smooth, polished. Perfect for writing.

He sat down. The stone was cold. He picked up one. It was heavy.

"If I complete a task," Dale said to the air, "does it count toward the debt?"

**CONDITIONAL: YES. LOCALIZED REMEDIATION IN BUFFER ZONE IS ACCEPTED AS PARTIAL PAYMENT. EFFICIENCY RATE: 1.5X. SPEED RATE: 0.5X.**

Half speed. But worth more.

Dale looked at the grid above. The red pulses were moving.

Breach points.

Three of them. They were small dots, but growing.

He could see them on the tablet too.

The Sectoid's work was related. It was updating the Field Guide with new species data.

The Sectoid was slow.

Deliberate.

Dale picked up the stone. He didn't know the language of the Sectoids. He didn't need to. He knew the format.

He looked at the nearest breach point on the grid. It was located in a residential zone, Sector 7.

He wrote. Not words.

He drew a diagram.

A house.

A wall. A crack.

A rat, and he drew the rat. He drew the acid beetle. He drew the broodmother.

The Sectoid watched. Its antennae twitched.

**NEW ENTRY DETECTED: CROSS-SPECIES HYBRIDIZATION RISK.**

The grid above flashed yellow.

Dale kept drawing. He added the drone. He added the ion beam. He drew the spore cluster.

He was documenting his own failure. And that was the key.

The Authority punished negligence, but it rewarded thorough documentation of errors, which was the only way to learn. To update the guide. To prevent the next accident.

His hand cramped.

The stone was rough. He wrote faster.

He didn't think, and he executed.

Identify. Document.

Isolate.

Eliminate.

Verify, and he skipped Eliminate. He couldn't eliminate anything here. But he could verify the cause.

The Sectoid leaned forward. It picked up a different stone. It wrote on its own tablet.

Dale felt a surge of energy. A warmth in his chest.

**PARTIAL PAYMENT RECEIVED: 45 POINTS.** **REMAINING DEBT: 455 POINTS.**

It wasn't enough. But it was progress.

A shadow fell over him.

Dale looked up. A new Auditor had descended. This one was larger. Its light was blinding white.

**AUDITOR TIER V.** **QUERY: WHY ARE YOU DOCUMENTING A FAILED JOB?**

"Because the next job will be harder," Dale said. He didn't look up from his drawing. He was sketching the spore structure.

The way they clumped. The way they reacted to electricity.

**YOU ARE WASTING TIME. THE BREACH IS WIDENING.**

"The breach is widening because I didn't seal the nest," Dale said. "I need to know what the nest looks like."

**THE NEST IS IN YOUR MEMORY. EXTRACT IT.**

"I can't extract what I can't draw."

The Tier V Auditor hovered.

It analyzed the tablet. The Sectoid's writing matched Dale's diagrams. The grid above stabilized.

The red pulses slowed.

**ANALYSIS: DATA CONSISTENCY DETECTED. THREAT ASSESSMENT UPDATED.** **NEW BREACH LOCATIONS IDENTIFIED. SECTOR 7, SECTOR 9, SECTOR 12.**

Dale stopped writing. He looked at the grid. The dots were moving toward each other.

"They're merging," Dale said.

**CORRECTION: THEY ARE COORDINATING. THE LICENSING AUTHORITY DOES NOT INTERVENE IN LOCALIZED INFESTATIONS UNTIL THE THRESHOLD IS MET.**

"What threshold?"

**CLASS TEN.**

Dale's blood went cold. Class Ten.

That wasn't just a monster, which was a catastrophe. That was a city disappearing.

"I can stop it," Dale said. "I can go back."

**YOU CANNOT. YOUR STATUS IS LOCKED. YOU ARE IN PROBATION.**

The Tier V Auditor raised a beam. It wasn't an attack. It was a scan.

**YOU ARE BEING MONITORED. FURTHER ERRORS WILL RESULT IN IMMEDIATE DELETION.**

Dale looked at his hands. They were shaking. He clenched them.

"If I stay here, they merge," Dale said. "If I leave, I violate protocol."

**YOU HAVE NO CHOICE. THE EXIT IS LOCKED.**

Dale looked at the Sectoid. The insect was working faster now. It was copying Dale's diagrams. It was adding notes.

*Cross-contamination risk: High. Mitigation: Electro-static neutralization.*

Dale looked at the note. He had written it. He had figured it out.

He looked at the grid. The three breach points were almost touching.

He had one move left, and he picked up the stone. He walked to the edge of the platform.

The void below was swirling.

Dark, chaotic energy churned there.

The Rift. The source.

He looked at the Tier V Auditor.

"Form 12-B," Dale said.

**WHAT?**

"Emergency Spatial Displacement Request. Section C, Paragraph 4. Immediate action required to prevent Class Ten escalation."

The Auditor paused. The light flickered.

**SECTION C PARAGRAPH 4 DOES NOT EXIST.**

"It does," Dale said. He pointed to the Sectoid's tablet. "It's in the guide.

I just updated it. Look.

"

The Auditor scanned the tablet. It read the Sectoid's new entry. It read Dale's diagram.

It hesitated.

Bureaucracy relied on precedent. If the guide said it was possible, it was possible. Even if it wasn't.

Dale held his breath.

The Auditor lowered its beam. It moved closer to Dale.

**FORM 12-B REQUIRES A WITNESS.**

Dale looked at the Sectoid.

The insect looked at him. Its compound eyes blinked.

It raised a claw. It pointed to Dale.

It clicked.

**WITNESS CONFIRMED: LICENSING AUDITOR (TIER IV).**

The Tier V Auditor stepped back. The grid above turned green. A door appeared in the air. A shimmering, translucent rectangle.

**DISPLACEMENT AUTHORIZED. DURATION: 10 SECONDS. COST: ALL REMAINING POINTS. RATING: 2/10.**

Two out of ten. Sloppy work. But he was going home.

Dale stepped toward the door.

"Wait," Dale said.

He turned back to the Sectoid. He picked up the stone he had used. He placed it on the edge of the platform.

"For the guide," Dale said.

The Sectoid picked it up. It bowed.

Dale stepped into the light.

The world twisted.

Pain shot through his legs. His bones felt like they were being rearranged. The air screamed.

Then, silence.

Dale fell onto dirt, and he gasped. He coughed. Dust filled his mouth.

He was back.

The crater was still there. The drone was smoking. Vance was standing ten feet away, his face pale, his mouth open.

The Rift was wider. The sky was tearing apart. Black veins spread across the clouds.

But Dale was alive, and he pushed himself up.

His legs shook. He checked his chest. The scorch mark was gone.

His clothes were torn, but his body was whole.

"Kowalski?" Vance whispered.

Dale looked at the ground. No clipboard.

But in his hand, he held something.

A small, gray stone. Smooth. Polished.

He looked at the Rift. The black veins were pulsing.

He looked at the stone, and he looked at Vance.

"They know we're here," Dale said. His voice was steady.

Vance took a step back.

"Who?"

"The Authority," Dale said. "They're watching. And they're disappointed."

Dale tossed the stone into the air. He caught it. He slipped it into his pocket.

"The rating was two," Dale said. "We have a backlog to clear."

A sound came from the Rift. A low, grinding noise. Like metal on stone.

Something large was coming through.

Dale reached into his empty pocket. He didn't have a spray. He didn't have a clipboard.

He had a stone, and he looked at the Rift.

"Vance," Dale said. "Get the truck."

"What? Why?"

"Because," Dale said, walking toward the edge of the crater, "we're not exterminating. We're auditing."

The ground beneath them cracked.

A hand emerged, which was huge. Gray. Chitinous.

And it was holding a clipboard.
