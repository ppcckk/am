Chapter 85: Show Me Your License

# Chapter 85

The thing in the doorway was wearing Jennifer’s face, but the geometry was wrong. Her left arm had three joints where there should have been two, and her neck elongated with a wet, snapping sound as she tilted her head to the side. The shadows pooling behind her didn’t just obscure the hallway; they seemed to eat the light from the emergency strips, leaving jagged voids where the linoleum should have been.

Dale didn’t scream. Screaming wasted oxygen and compromised vocal cord integrity during high-stress containment events. He clicked his pen.

"Examiner," Dale said, not looking at Alderman, who was currently fumbling with the flare gun like it was a novelty lighter. "The perimeter breach originated from the stairwell. That’s a shared egress path. You have a code violation for blocking fire escapes with hostile biomass."

Alderman pointed the flare gun at the ceiling. "It’s not blocking anything, Dale! It’s trying to eat us!"

"Noted." Dale scribbled on his clipboard. *Violation: Obstruction of emergency exit by Class-IV mimicry entity. Action required: Immediate removal or alternative egress.*

The entity lunged. It moved with a terrifying, disjointed speed, its limbs bending backward at impossible angles.

Dale stepped back, not away from the threat, but toward the center of the room, positioning himself between the creature and the structural column.

He needed a defensible corner. He needed sightlines that didn’t allow for blind spots.

The creature hit the column where Dale had been standing. The drywall exploded into dust and gypsum shards, and it shrieked, a sound like grinding metal mixed with a human sob.

Dale checked his Field Guide.

The entry for *Rattus profundus* was grayed out, contaminated by the cross-species usage of his previous protocol. He couldn’t use rat-specific traps.

He couldn’t use acid neutralizers, and he was locked out of his most reliable tools.

He had to improvise.

"Alderman," Dale said, his voice flat. "You have a flare. It’s magnesium-based. Does it have a phosphorus tip?"

"What? I don’t know! It’s a flare gun!"

"Then don’t fire it indoors. The smoke particulate will obscure my vision and violate OSHA visibility standards for active combat zones."

The entity recovered. It pulled itself up from the debris, its jaw unhinging further, and the receptionist’s eyes were gone, replaced by clusters of black, chitinous sensors. It hissed, a sibilant sound that vibrated in Dale’s teeth.

"Stop trying to inspect me," the entity said. Its voice was multiple layers of Jennifer’s voice, out of sync. "I am the test, Dale. I am the filter."

"You’re a code violation," Dale corrected. He reached into his belt and pulled out a canister of industrial-grade silica gel desiccant. It was meant for moisture control in basements, but the particles were fine, dry, and abrasive.

The entity pounced.

Dale didn’t dodge. He threw the canister at the floor between them and kicked it.

The container burst, sending a cloud of microscopic, sharp particles into the air.

The entity charged through the cloud.

The desiccant particles stuck to its moist, mucous-covered skin.

The creature screamed. Not in pain, but in confusion; the desiccant didn’t damage it, but it disrupted the surface tension of its mimicry membrane. Its form blurred at the edges, the illusion of Jennifer’s uniform flickering like a faulty hologram.

"Field Guide update," Dale muttered, writing rapidly. *Species: Mimicry Entity (Humanoid Variant). Weakness: Disruption of surface moisture barrier reduces cohesion. Effect: Reduced speed, visual distortion.*

He had identified the weakness. The System pinged in his peripheral vision. A small green checkmark. *Weakness Noted. Targeted Damage Bonus Active.*

Dale grabbed the fire extinguisher mounted on the wall. It was a standard ABC dry chemical unit. He pulled the pin.

"Cover your eyes," Dale said to Alderman.

"Why?"

"Because the particulate matter is going to fill this room, and if you cough, you’ll inhale the mimic’s shed skin cells. Contamination risk is extreme."

Alderman, to his credit, ducked behind the reception desk.

Dale squeezed the trigger. The fire extinguisher sprayed a thick cloud of white powder directly into the entity’s face.

The mimic shrieked. The dry chemical was abrasive. It reacted with the moisture in the creature’s tissues, creating a localized exothermic reaction.

Steam hissed from the entity’s form. The illusion of the receptionist’s face melted away, revealing the raw, gray muscle and cartilage beneath.

It wasn’t a rat.

It wasn’t an insect. It was something older. Something that had worn human skin like a suit.

Dale advanced, and he didn’t swing the extinguisher like a club. He used it as a pressure nozzle, directing the stream into the creature’s eyes and open mouth.

The targeted damage bonus kicked in.

The entity’s health bar, visible only to Dale, dropped significantly.

*HP: 85%.*

The entity stumbled back, thrashing. It swiped at Dale, but its claws slipped on the slick, powder-coated floor, and slipped, crashing into the waiting room chairs.

"Stay down," Dale commanded. He kicked the fire extinguisher into the creature’s chest, knocking the wind out of it.

He reached for his utility knife, and he didn’t need to kill it.

He needed to isolate it.

He pulled a roll of heavy-duty plastic sheeting from his emergency kit, and it was meant for containing asbestos dust, but it worked. He wrapped the struggling entity in layers of plastic, binding its limbs with industrial zip ties. The entity thrashed, its strength diminishing as the desiccant and chemical foam dried out its tissues.

Within ninety seconds, the creature was a cocooned bundle on the floor, twitching weakly.

Dale stood up, breathing heavily. He checked his watch. Two minutes and fourteen seconds. Acceptable.

He turned to Alderman. "Casualty report?"

Alderman stood up, brushing powder off his suit. He looked shaken. "You just... you just wrapped it up."

"Containment," Dale said. "It’s not dead. Killing it without verifying the core nest location yields zero points.

I need to know where it came from. It said it was a scout.

That implies a hive mind."

Alderman stared at him. "You’re insane. You know that?"

"I’m licensed," Dale corrected. "Where is the hive?"

Alderman hesitated. He looked at the wrapped bundle. The entity let out a low, gurgling moan.

"If I tell you," Alderman said slowly, "you’re no longer just a technician. You’re an investigator. The Authority doesn’t let investigators go home, Dale. They assign them to permanent cases."

"Is that a threat?"

"It’s a reality. Look at your license."

Dale looked at the glowing card in his hand. The number V was pulsing. Below it, a new line of text had appeared. *Probationary Investigator: Pending Review.*

"The Authority isn’t going to let you go," Alderman said. "You cleared the test. You passed the practical. But you failed the interview."

Dale frowned. "I didn’t have an interview."

"Every job has an interview. The question is always the same. What do you do when the job doesn’t end?"

The wrapped entity on the floor began to vibrate. The plastic sheeting rattled.

Dale looked at his clipboard. He had a new notification.

*Infestation Status: Escalated.* *New Objective: Locate Source.* *Penalty for Failure: License Revocation.*

Dale felt a cold spike of anxiety in his chest. License revocation meant no access to the System. No points.

No protection.

Just a man with a clipboard in a world full of things that wanted to eat him.

"Where is it?" Dale asked.

Alderman pointed toward the elevator bank.

"The Rift isn’t closed, Dale. It’s anchored. It’s in the basement.

But the basement isn’t the real problem."

"Then what is?"

"The other tenants. They’re not survivors. They’re vectors."

Dale looked at the hallway, and the shadows were moving again.

This time, they weren’t just shadows. They were figures.

Dozens of them. Human-shaped, but wrong. Their limbs were too long, their faces blank masks of static.

"The cleanup crew," Alderman whispered. "You’re not exterminating pests anymore, Kowalski. You’re trying to quarantine a city block."

Dale looked at the wrapped entity. It was silent now. Dead, or sleeping. He couldn’t verify.

He looked at his Field Guide. The entry for the Mimicry Entity was now fully populated. He had its weaknesses. He had its stats.

But he didn’t have the answer to the larger question.

He opened a new page in his clipboard. He wrote: *Incident Report: Unlicensed Infestation, Multi-Vector.*

He turned to Alderman. "I need a hard copy of the building’s lease agreements."

Alderman blinked. "Why?"

"Because if they’re vectors, they were signed into this building. Every infection has a point of entry. Every entry point has a signature."

Alderman laughed, a brittle, hysterical sound. "You’re trying to find the landlord’s signature on the apocalypse."

"I’m trying to find the code violation that allowed it," Dale said. "And if I find it, I can cite it. And if I can cite it, I can shut it down."

The elevator doors dinged.

The doors slid open.

Inside the elevator stood a man in a business suit. He was holding a briefcase. He was smiling. His teeth were too sharp.

"Mr. Kowalski," the man said.

His voice was smooth, oily.

"We’ve been waiting for you. The inspection is overdue."

Dale looked at the man. He looked at the clipboard. He looked at the flare gun in Alderman’s hand.

"Examiner," Dale said. "If I shoot this man, is that considered self-defense or unauthorized use of force?"

"It’s considered a termination of contract," Alderman said, aiming the flare gun at the elevator. "But if you miss, the Authority will fine you for negligent discharge."

Dale looked at the man in the elevator. The man stepped out. The shadows behind him solidified into more figures, blocking the hallway behind them.

Dale clicked his pen.

"Then I guess I better make sure I hit him."

He raised the fire extinguisher, not as a weapon, but as a distraction; sprayed the cloud again, aiming for the man’s face. The man didn’t flinch. He walked through the white cloud, treating it like mist.

Dale’s heart hammered against his ribs.

His professional calm was cracking. This wasn’t a rat.

This wasn’t a beetle; it had learned to walk among humans.

And it knew his name.

"Welcome to the job, Dale," the man said. He opened his briefcase.

Inside wasn’t documents, which was a nest.

Thousands of tiny, pulsating eggs, black and shiny, arranged in perfect geometric rows.

"We don’t want to kill you," the man said. "We want to hire you."

Dale stared at the eggs. They were moving.

He looked at his Field Guide. There was no entry for this. No weakness. No protocol.

He looked at Alderman. Alderman was pale, his hand shaking.

Dale looked back at the man. He took a breath. He steadied his hand.

"I don’t take jobs," Dale said. "I take certifications."

The man smiled. "Oh, we have plenty of certifications to offer. We’re the Authority, Dale. We’ve been waiting for someone with your attention to detail."

Dale froze. The Authority was the bureaucratic entity that had given him his powers. The thing that awarded points. The thing that rated his thoroughness.

If this man was the Authority, then the System wasn’t a game. It was a bureaucracy. And bureaucracies had rules. And rules had loopholes.

Dale looked at the clipboard in his hand. He looked at the notification that said *License Revocation Pending.*

He looked at the man.

"Show me your license," Dale said.
