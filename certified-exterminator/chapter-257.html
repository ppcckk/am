Chapter 257: Critical Vector

# Chapter 257

The glass cracked. Not with a shatter, but with a spiderweb fracture that spread from Dale's palm outward, freezing the moisture in the air into jagged diamonds. The green light from his hand bled through the fissures, illuminating the suspended fluid inside the containment unit. Elena watched the cracks with a calm that terrified him more than Kappa's rage.

Dale pulled his hand back. The skin beneath the glove was still burning.

"Containment breach," Dale said. His voice sounded strange.

Metallic, and he swallowed, tasting copper.

"Protocol 4-A. Immediate isolation of the primary vector."

Kappa didn't move. The giant stood frozen, his massive hands hovering over the console's emergency override. His eyes were fixed on Dale's hand. The System notification hung in the air, a red banner that pulsed with every heartbeat.

*Status: Flagged for Review.*

"Move," Kappa whispered.

"No." Dale stepped back.

He needed distance. He needed to assess the spread.

"If I touch you, the resonance transfers.

You're a Class-IV conduit. If this spreads to the main grid, the entire sector goes dark. And the things in the vents wake up.

"

"You absorbed it," Elena said. Her voice was muffled by the glass, but clear.

"The extraction wasn't just memory.

It was frequency.

You're the antenna now.

"

Dale ignored her. He scanned the room. The console was humming.

The other cages were vibrating. A low, thrumming bass note that rattled his teeth.

This wasn't just a localized event, which was a cascade.

He needed to stop it. Not by fighting. By fixing.

"Kappa. Shut down the external feed. Now."

"I can't," Kappa said. His voice was tight, strained. "The Authority's lock is engaged. I'm locked out of the master switch."

Dale looked at the console. He looked at the blinking red lights. He looked at the manual override lever, sealed behind a panel of reinforced polycarbonate.

"Then we do it the hard way."

Dale reached into his belt. He didn't pull a weapon. He pulled his clipboard.

The act was absurd. In the middle of a containment crisis, with his hand glowing like a radioactive isotope, Dale Kowalski pulled out a clipboard and a ballpoint pen. He clicked the pen. The sound was sharp, final.

*Field Guide updated: Resonance-Infected Human (Subject: Dale Kowalski).*

The System pinged. A new entry appeared in his peripheral vision.

*Weakness Identified: Structural Dissonance. The host body cannot sustain the frequency without a grounding point.*

Dale stared at the text.

It wasn't a combat weakness. It was a engineering flaw.

His body was a circuit board that had shorted out.

The only way to stop the light was to break the circuit.

"Grounding point," Dale muttered.

He looked at Kappa.

"The console. It's grounded to the bedrock. If I connect to it, I can drain the charge."

Kappa shook his head.

"You'll fry. The current is lethal."

"Not if I regulate it." Dale walked toward the console. Each step left a scorch mark on the tile floor. The green light intensified with every movement. He could feel the vibration in his bones, a sickening hum that wanted to tear him apart.

He reached the console. The polycarbonate panel was locked. Kappa was still too far away to reach it.

Dale placed his glowing hand on the panel. The plastic softened.

It didn't melt; it warped.

The heat from his hand was enough to bypass the lock mechanism.

With a grunt of effort, he shoved the panel aside.

Underneath lay the manual override. A thick copper rod, thick as a wrist.

Dale grabbed it, and the pain was instant. It felt like dipping his arm into a vat of acid. The green light surged, traveling up his arm, past his shoulder, into his neck. He gritted his teeth so hard he felt a molar crack.

"Disconnect!" Kappa shouted.

"Hold the line," Dale gasped. He twisted the rod. It resisted. The Authority's security protocols were fighting him.

He thought of the code violations. He thought of the frass trails. He thought of the nest.

*Identify. Document. Isolate.

Eliminate. Verify.*

He wasn't fighting a monster. He was fighting a system error. And he knew how to patch a system error.

Dale slammed his free hand against the console's interface port. The port was designed for data cables, not biological matter. But the System didn't care about intent. It cared about connection.

*Connection Established.*

The green light in his hand dimmed. The hum in the room dropped an octave.

*System Alert: Unauthorized Grounding. Protocol Violation: -20 Treatment Points.*

Dale ignored the penalty. He focused on the flow. He visualized the resonance as a liquid, a toxic sludge that had entered his bloodstream. He forced it out, through the copper rod, into the earth.

The light in his hand turned white. Then, it vanished.

Dale collapsed to his knees. The floor tiles cracked under his weight. He gasped for air, his lungs burning. The taste of copper was gone, replaced by the smell of ozone and burnt plastic.

Kappa was beside him in an instant. The giant's face was pale.

"Are you alive?"

Dale looked up.

His hand was blackened. The glove was fused to his skin. He tried to move his fingers.

They didn't respond.

*Field Guide Updated: Subject Dale Kowalski. Status: Critical Contamination. Treatment Protocol: Amputation Recommended.*

The text hovered in his vision, cold and clinical.

"No," Dale said. He stood up.

His legs shook, but he held.

"No amputation. Not yet. We haven't verified the elimination."

Elena pressed her hand against the glass. Her eyes were wide.

"You didn't ground it. You stored it."

Dale looked at her.

"What?"

"The resonance didn't go into the earth. It went into the console buffer. Look."

Dale looked at the console. The lights were no longer red. They were amber. A steady, pulsing amber.

*System Notification: Resonance Containment Achieved. Storage Mode: Active.*

He hadn't eliminated the infestation. He had relocated it.

"That's worse," Kappa said. His voice was barely a whisper. "Now it's in the mainframe. If that buffer overflows..."

"It won't," Dale said. "I regulated it. I know the capacity."

"You know the capacity of *what*?" Kappa asked.

"Of me."

Dale walked away from the console. He walked toward the door.

The green light was gone, but the sensation remained.

A cold, hollow ache in the center of his chest, and he could feel the power sitting there, dormant, waiting.

*New Loophole Detected: Internal Storage Capacity. Current Load: 15%. Max Safe Load: 50%.*

Dale stopped. He read the text again.

*Max Safe Load: 50%.*

He had 35% headroom.

Kappa followed him.

"Where are you going?"

"Inspection," Dale said. "The ventilation shafts. If the resonance is in the buffer, it's leaking. I need to check the flow."

"You're insane," Kappa said. "You need a medic. You need the Authority."

"The Authority is the one that put it there," Dale said. He pushed open the door to the maintenance corridor. The air outside was thick with dust and the smell of rot.

"If I go to the Authority, they dissect me. If I fix it, I keep my license."

He stepped into the darkness, and the corridor was narrow. The pipes overhead were sweating condensation. Dale scanned the walls with his flashlight. The beam cut through the dust, revealing a trail of black sludge.

*Frass Trail Detected.*

It wasn't insect frass. It was residue. The resonance had leaked out of the console and into the walls.

Dale followed the trail. It led deeper into the facility, away from the cages, toward the core.

"Elena was right," he said into the comms unit. "It's spreading."

"Turn back," Kappa's voice crackled in his ear. "Dale. Stop."

"Negative. Protocol requires verification."

Dale reached the end of the corridor.

A heavy steel door blocked the path. It was unlabeled. No warning signs.

No hazard symbols, and he placed his hand on the door. The metal was cold.

*System Scan: Door Integrity: 100%. Contents: Unknown.*

Dale pressed his ear to the metal. He heard breathing.

Not human breathing.

Something wet. Something collective.

*Field Guide Update: Unidentified Entity Detected.*

He kicked the door. It didn't budge.

He looked around. No tools. No weapons. Just his clipboard and his broken hand.

Then he saw it.

In the corner of the corridor, half-hidden by a coil of wire, was a maintenance hatch. It was small, barely big enough for a child. But it was open.

And something was crawling out of it.

It looked like a spider. But it was made of light. Pure, green light, solid and sharp. It skittered across the floor, leaving scorch marks.

*Dimensional Vermin (Resonance Variant) Detected.*

Dale didn't panic. He didn't reach for a weapon.

He reached for his clipboard.

"Identify," he muttered. "Document."

He wrote the observation.

*Species: Resonance Arachnid. Behavior: Parasitic. Weakness: Static Discharge.*

He didn't have a static gun. He didn't have an EMP grenade.

But he had a clipboard. And he had a pen.

Dale ripped a page from the clipboard. He crumpled it into a ball.

He rubbed it against the metal door, generating static electricity through friction, which was a stupid trick.

A child's trick.

But the System didn't care about dignity. It cared about mechanics.

*Static Charge Accumulating: 10%. 20%...*

The light-spider stopped. It turned toward him. Its many eyes glowed with the same green hue as his own hand.

Dale threw the paper ball.

It wasn't enough to kill it. But it was enough to stun it. The spider froze, twitching.

Dale grabbed the coil of wire. He wrapped it around his hand. The wire bit into his skin. He pulled it tight.

He didn't need to kill the spider. He needed to trap it.

He lunged. Not at the spider. At the hatch.

He slammed the coil of wire into the hatch opening, jamming it shut. The spider was on the wrong side. Trapped.

*Containment Successful. Treatment Points: +5.*

The spider screamed. A silent, high-pitched frequency that made Dale's nose bleed.

He stepped back. He wiped the blood from his upper lip.

"Isolate," he said. "Now eliminate."

He couldn't get inside. The hatch was jammed. The spider was inside.

But he could feed it, and he reached into his pocket. He pulled out a small battery from his flashlight. He smashed the casing. The lithium exposed itself to the air.

He dropped the battery into the hatch.

*Chemical Reaction Detected.*

The spider lunged at the battery. It ate it. The green light intensified.

The spider grew. It burst through the wire, larger now.

Stronger.

Dale smiled. It was a grim, tired smile.

"Good," he said. "Now I can see you."

He raised his clipboard. He pointed it at the monster.

*System Scan: Target Acquired.*

*Weakness: Overload. Resonance Variant is unstable. Feeding accelerates growth, but destabilizes cohesion.*

Dale didn't have a weapon to kill it. He had a battery. And a wire. And a clipboard.

He needed to make it eat too much, and he pulled out his spare batteries. One by one, he dropped them into the hatch.

The spider grew. It became a mass of light and limbs, pressing against the wire. It was losing shape. It was becoming a blob.

Dale watched. He wrote.

*Observation: Subject destabilizing.*

The blob erupted from the hatch. It filled the corridor. It was ten feet wide.

It pulsed, which was about to explode.

Dale ran.

He ran back down the corridor, his broken hand swinging, his clipboard held tight.

Behind him, the corridor filled with green fire. The explosion wasn't loud. It was silent. A wave of force that pushed him forward, slamming him against the wall.

He slid down. His vision blurred.

*System Notification: Infestation Eliminated. Thoroughness Rating: 4.2.*

*Rating Too Low. Penalty: -10 Treatment Points.*

Dale coughed. He tasted blood.

He stood up, and the corridor was empty. The spider was gone. The wire was melted.

The hatch was blown off.

But the green light was back in his hand. Fainter, but present.

He had absorbed it again.

*Internal Storage Capacity: 35%.*

He hadn't eliminated it. He had consumed it.

Kappa's voice came through the comms.

"Dale? Dale, answer me."

Dale didn't answer. He looked down the dark corridor. The light was moving. It was coming from the core.

And it was getting closer, and he looked at his clipboard. The page was blank. He had no more observations to write.

He looked at his hand. The green veins were spreading up his arm.

*Status: Critical. Time to Overload: 10 Minutes.*

Dale runned. Not away. Toward the core.

If he was going to explode, he would do it in the mainframe. And he would make sure the Authority felt it.
