Chapter 175: Courthouse ETA

# Chapter 175

The rope was not made of fiber. It was woven from compressed screams, each strand vibrating with a frequency that made Dale's molars ache.

He didn't look at it.

Looking at the Auditor's descent path invited psychological contamination, and his Field Guide entry for *Clericus infestus* was still flickering with the warning icon for unresolved trauma. Instead, he looked at the ground.

Suburban asphalt. Cracked. Stained with the black ichor of the Clerk's former nest.

"Perimeter set?" Dale asked, not turning around.

"Roughly," the junior agent named Miller said. His voice cracked. "The barrier holds. But the red light is bleeding through."

"Bleeding is a biological metaphor.

Use 'seeping' or 'transmitting,'" Dale corrected, pulling a roll of high-vis tape from his vest. "Seeping implies passive loss of integrity.

Transmitting implies active signal propagation.

The Auditor is transmitting. That means it's aware."

Dale slapped a strip of tape across the crack in the sidewalk. Then another.

Then a third.

He worked with the rhythmic efficiency of a man who had spent fourteen years sealing gaps where roaches entered through foundation slabs.

It was absurd, of course. A strip of orange adhesive against a cosmic entity.

But the System didn't reward intent. It rewarded protocol. And protocol required isolation.

"Isolate," Dale muttered. The word appeared in bold blue text in his peripheral vision.

*Isolation Protocol Active. Workspace contained. Contamination risk reduced by 40%.*

The notification was brief, cold, and satisfying. Dale felt the tension in his shoulders drop two inches. The fear didn't vanish, but it was boxed in, categorized, and filed under 'Pending Review.'

Miller scrambled to place the last canister of neutralizing foam at the base of the red rope. The foam hissed, turning grey as it touched the pulsating tether.

"It's not holding, Dale.

The Auditor is pulling. It's tightening the knot."

"Then we cut the knot," Dale said.

He opened his clipboard.

The paper was blank, save for the single word *Escalation* he had written moments ago. He tore that sheet off and dropped it.

He needed a new form.

Form 7-B: *Cross-Dimensional Breach Containment*.

He didn't have it in his physical binder. He had memorized the structure.

"Get me three pounds of calcium carbonate dust and a bucket of industrial-grade solvent," Dale ordered.

Miller stared at him. "What?

We have guns.

We have magic wards.

We have—"

"Do you have the Authority's approval for unlicensed magical discharge in a residential zone?" Dale interrupted. "Check your license status. If you're not Class V, you're contaminating the local ecosystem every time you cast a spell.

The Auditor is a bureaucrat. It will fine us for unauthorized energy expenditure before it even says hello."

Miller paled further. He checked his wrist comp. "I'm… I'm Class III."

"Then you're not authorized to use magic here. You're authorized to use chemical agents. Get the dust."

The other junior agent, a young woman named Sarah, was already moving. She pulled a heavy bag of white powder from the truck's hazard compartment. "Calcium carbonate," she said, breathing hard. "For what?"

"Desiccant," Dale said.

"The Auditor is a creature of ink and void. Ink is liquid. Solids absorb liquids.

We're not going to fight it.

We're going to dry it out."

Dale uncapped a jug of solvent.

It smelled of acetone and sulfur. He poured it over the calcium carbonate, mixing it into a thick, white slurry. He applied it to the base of the red rope using a trowel.

The slurry hit the vibrating tether and sizzled. The red light dimmed slightly.

The screaming frequency dropped an octave.

*Target Weakness Identified: Hygroscopic Instability.*

*DAMAGE BONUS APPLIED: +15% against Ink-based entities.*

Dale smiled.

It was a thin, tight thing. He had found the weakness.

He hadn't found it through combat. He had found it because he knew what happened to ink when it met a desiccant. It was basic chemistry, basic pest control.

The Auditor was just a big, bureaucratic cockroach with a flair for the dramatic.

The red rope shuddered. A hand emerged from the top of the tether.

It was long, pale, and too many-jointed.

The fingers ended in sharp, black quills. The hand grasped the edge of the sky, tearing a hole in the atmosphere. The hole was not black.

It was the color of old parchment, stained with coffee rings and grease.

Dale's breath hitched. He forced his hands to stop shaking. He gripped the trowel.

"It's here," Sarah whispered. "The Auditor."

"It's not the Auditor," Dale said, his voice flat. "The Auditor is the title. The entity is *Archivum vorax*. I haven't seen it before, but the entry is populating."

In his Field Guide, a new card slid into place. It was jagged, made of torn paper edges.

*Species: Archivum vorax.* *Habitat: Liminal Bureaucracies.* *Behavior: Hoards information. Consumes anomalies. Excretes debt.* *Weakness: Drying agents. Boredom.*

*Boredom?* Dale thought.

The hand descended further. A face followed. It had no eyes, only two deep sockets filled with swirling ink. Its mouth was a vertical slit, lined with teeth that looked like eraser shavings.

"Who is responsible?" the voice boomed. It didn't come from the mouth. It came from the air itself, vibrating in Dale's chest cavity. "Who is responsible for the unauthorized termination of the Clerk?"

Dale stepped forward. The asphalt cracked under his boots. He didn't raise a weapon. He raised his clipboard.

"Dale Kowalski," he said. "Class II Technician. License Number 884-B-99."

The ink-face tilted. The red rope tightened. The ground beneath Dale's feet softened, turning into a puddle of black sludge.

"Your documentation is incomplete," the Auditor said. "You terminated a public servant without filing a Change of Command form. You failed to secure the chain of custody for the Clerk's files. You are in violation of Protocol 12-A."

Dale felt the System warning flash red.

*VIOLATION DETECTED. CROSS-CONTAMINATION RISK IMMINENT.*

If he fought the Auditor with physical force, he would incur a 40% debuff. His attacks would be useless. His Field Guide entry would be corrupted for twenty-four hours. He would be stripped of his points.

He couldn't fight. He had to audit.

"I filed Form 4-C," Dale said. "Emergency Termination due to Active Hostility. The Clerk was attacking civilians. I followed the exception clause for imminent threat."

"The exception clause requires a witness," the Auditor said. The ink dripped from its face, hitting the ground and sizzling.

"You have no witness.

The tenant is dead. The neighbor is missing. The file is closed."

"The tenant is not dead," Dale said. "The tenant is displaced. I logged the displacement in real-time. See."

He held up his clipboard. The page was empty, but the System recognized the act of *presenting* the documentation.

It was a bluff, but a procedural one.

The System rewarded thoroughness.

By presenting a form, even a blank one, Dale was signaling his intent to follow protocol.

The Auditor paused. The red rope stopped vibrating.

"Show me," it said.

Dale flipped the clipboard. He didn't have a witness. He had Miller and Sarah.

They were unauthorized. Using them as witnesses would trigger a cross-contamination penalty because their licenses were incompatible with the Auditor's jurisdiction.

He needed a witness that wasn't human.

Dale looked at the bag of calcium carbonate. He looked at the solvent. He looked at the red rope.

"Sarah," Dale said. "Throw the bag at the Auditor's feet."

"What?"

"Throw it. Miller, dump the solvent on the rope. Create a barrier."

Sarah hesitated, then threw.

The bag burst. White powder clouded the air. The Auditor inhaled.

The ink-face recoiled.

The powder was drying out the excess ink leaking from its form.

*STATUS EFFECT: DESICCATED. MOVEMENT SPEED REDUCED BY 50%.*

Dale saw the opening. He didn't attack. He stepped into the cloud.

"I have a witness," Dale said, shouting over the hiss of the solvent. "The environment."

He pointed to the calcium carbonate cloud. "The particulate matter has registered the Auditor's presence. It is a natural witness. I am citing Environmental Witnessing, Statute 9."

The System chimed.

*ENVIRONMENTAL WITNESS ACCEPTED. DOCUMENTATION VALIDATED.*

*PENALTY WAIVED. THOROUGHNESS RATING ADJUSTED: +2.*

The Auditor's face contorted. The ink swirls in its eye sockets sped up. It was angry. Not because it was defeated, but because it had been outmaneuvered on a technicality.

"You play games with the rules," the Auditor said. Its voice was quieter now, more intimate. "You use the system against itself."

"It's a tool," Dale said. "Like a spray can. Like a trap. You're just another pest that thinks it's a god."

The Auditor extended a long, spindly finger. It pointed at Dale. A beam of red light shot out.

Dale didn't dodge. He couldn't. The isolation protocol held him in place to maintain the workspace integrity. If he broke the perimeter, the bonus would vanish, and the debuff would return.

He had to take the hit.

The beam struck Dale in the chest. Pain, white and blinding, exploded behind his ribs. He staggered back, his boots sliding in the solvent slurry. He tasted copper.

*INJURY: INTERNAL HEMORRHAGE (MINOR). PROTOCOL VIOLATION: DEFENSIVE FAILURE.*

But the System didn't punish him for the injury. It rewarded him for the *response*.

Dale coughed. He wiped his mouth with the back of his hand. He looked at his Field Guide.

*New Entry Detected: Counter-Protocol Required.*

He looked at the Auditor. The entity was pulling harder on the rope, trying to descend fully. The red line was widening, becoming a tunnel.

"It's coming through," Miller shouted. "We can't hold it!"

"We don't hold it," Dale said, gritting his teeth. He reached into his vest and pulled out a small, sealed vial. It wasn't a weapon. It was a sample.

A sample of the Clerk's black ichor. Collected from the crawlspace.

"The Clerk was a janitor," Dale said, his voice raspy.

"It cleaned up the mess. The Auditor is the landlord. It creates the mess.

But landlords don't like it when their tenants start cleaning up on their own."

He uncapped the vial. The black liquid inside glowed faintly.

"Sarah, get back. Miller, get the truck engine running."

Dale threw the vial.

It wasn't aimed at the Auditor. It was aimed at the red rope.

The vial shattered against the tether. The black ichor splashed onto the red light.

The reaction was instantaneous.

The black liquid ate the red light.

The rope turned grey, then translucent. The Auditor shrieked, a sound like tearing metal.

The entity was being pulled back, its connection to the dimensional rift severed by its own waste product.

*CONTAMINATION EVENT: SELF-REFLEXIVE. TARGET DEFEATED BY ITS OWN WASTE.*

*POINTS AWARDED: 500.* *THOROUGHNESS RATING: 9.8.*

The Auditor's form dissolved. The ink-face melted.

The quill-fingers curled inward.

It wasn't dying. It was being filed away.

"Get in the truck," Dale commanded. He grabbed Sarah by the arm and dragged her back toward the vehicle. Miller was already in the driver's seat, the engine roaring.

Dale ran. The ground was shaking. The red tunnel was collapsing, sucking the air out of the street. The calcium carbonate cloud was being pulled into the vortex, swirling like a snowstorm.

He dove into the passenger seat as Miller gunned the engine. The truck tires spun, then caught. They sped away, leaving the collapsing breach behind.

Dale slumped against the door.

He checked his ribs. The bruise was forming.

He had cracked a bone, maybe two.

He didn't care. He opened his clipboard.

He wrote down the final line of the report.

*Threat Neutralized. Cause: Improper disposal of hazardous material by Target. Action: Corrected.*

Sarah looked at him. Her eyes were wide. "You killed it."

"No," Dale said, closing the clipboard. "I audited it. It failed inspection."

He looked out the back window. The red line was gone. The sky was clear.

But on his Field Guide, a new notification was blinking. It wasn't a green checkmark. It was a gold star.

*Achievement Unlocked: Bureaucrat Slayer.* *New Certification Path Available: Class IV - Regulatory Enforcer.* *Exam Date: Immediate.*

Dale froze.

"Immediate?" he whispered.

The truck jolted. Miller looked in the rearview mirror. His face was grey.

"Dale," Miller said. "Look at the dashboard."

On the digital display of the truck, a new window had popped up. It wasn't the Authority interface. It was something else. Something that looked like a browser from the old internet.

A chat window.

**User: The_Landlord_1984** **Message:** *You've made an enemy, Mr. Kowalski.

The Auditor was my employee.

I have a grievance.

And I have a lawyer.*

A second message appeared.

**User: The_Landlord_1984** **Message:** *You'll never guess who I hired.*

Dale looked at the map on the dashboard. The GPS was recalculating. The destination was no longer their safehouse.

The destination was the County Courthouse.

And the ETA was three minutes.
