Chapter 374: Asset Liquidation Countdown

# Chapter 374

The black ink did not dissolve. It hardened.

Dale Kowalski stared at the substance encasing his boots. It had the viscosity of tar but the density of cured epoxy.

His fingers, fused at the knuckles, twitched against the resistance, and the pain was a dull, throbbing pressure, localized entirely in the metacarpals. He tried to flex his right hand. The joints locked.

A notification flashed in his peripheral vision. Red text. Pulsing.

**STATUS: INTEGRATION IMMINENT.** **TIME TO FULL CONVERSION: 04:12.** **PENALTY FOR FAILURE: TOTAL ASSET LIQUIDATION.**

Four minutes.

Dale checked his watch. The second hand was moving, but the numbers on the digital display were scrambled, replaced by shifting geometric patterns that hurt to look at.

He ignored them, and he looked at the clipboard lying on the pavement, three feet away.

His hand was stuck. The other hand was stuck. He couldn't pick it up.

The Authority's voice echoed inside his skull, distinct from the ambient roar of the swarm overhead. It sounded less like a deity and more like a human resources representative delivering a termination notice.

*"Technician Kowalski. Your classification has shifted from Contractor to Material. Do not resist the binding. Efficiency requires compliance."*

Dale gritted his teeth. He tested the ink. It pulled away from his left ankle but resisted on the right. A hairline fracture appeared in the black shell.

He wasn't a material. He was a licensed pest control technician, Class II, moving toward Class III. Materials didn't have licenses. Materials didn't fill out forms.

A new window opened. Not the usual green interface. This one was stark white, bordered by jagged black lines.

**FORM 77-B: EXCEPTION REQUEST.** **SUBCATEGORY: CONSCIOUS RESISTANCE DURING INVENTORY PROCESS.** **REQUIRED: PROOF OF VALUE.** **WARNING: FRAUDULENT CLAIMS RESULT IN IMMEDIATE INCINERATION.**

Dale's breath hitched. Proof of value.

The Authority didn't care about heroism.

It cared about utility.

If he was becoming part of the infestation, the Authority wanted to know if the resulting creature would produce more spores, more rats, or more paperwork.

He needed to prove he was useful. Or he needed to prove he was dangerous.

The swarm descended. Not into the streets, but onto the people. Screams erupted from the suburban duplexes.

Dale could hear the wet tearing of flesh, the snapping of bone. Normal humans were being harvested.

Their bodies were being dissolved into the black ink that covered the road.

Dale looked at his arm. The black line tracing his wrist had reached his elbow. It pulsed in time with the heartbeat he could feel in the pavement.

*Active Host.*

The term gnawed at him. In pest control, an active host was a living organism currently sustaining a parasitic or predatory relationship with the target species. A cockroach in a wall was an active host if it was feeding. A rat in a trap was not.

If Dale was an active host, he could be treated, and he focused on the Form 77-B. The cursor blinked, waiting for input. His fused fingers made typing impossible. He had to use voice commands.

"Enter claim," Dale said. His voice was steady. Flat.

The dry tone of a man reciting an OSHA violation code.

"Claim: Technician Kowalski possesses unique data regarding the *Rattus profundus* broodmother and the current dimensional breach parameters. This data is not replicable by standard Authority drones."

The system paused.

**DATA UNVERIFIED. PLEASE PROVIDE EVIDENCE.**

Dale looked at the mall entrance, and the black ink was spreading, climbing the steps, consuming the automatic doors. Vane's cologne smell was overpowering, cloying and sweet. Vane had been a rogue technician, one who had tried to sell Rift materials on the black market.

The Authority had stamped him out. Literally.

But Vane had left something behind. The stamp itself.

Dale couldn't move his body. But he could move his mind. He had spent fourteen years memorizing code violations, species classifications, and entry-point protocols. He had a Field Guide in his head, not just on paper.

"Evidence," Dale continued.

"Cross-reference *Rattus profundus* entry.

Note the sub-species variation: *Profundus aetheris*. This variant does not burrow.

It floats. It is aerodynamic. Standard burrowing protocols fail against it.

However, it is vulnerable to sudden pressure changes, specifically those caused by high-velocity particulate dispersal.

"

He was bluffing, and he didn't know if *aetheris* was a real sub-species. He had invented the name ten seconds ago. But he had observed the aerial swarm.

They weren't birds.

They were rats with modified wings, adapted for lift, not speed. They were clumsy in the air.

They dropped when hit by strong drafts.

The system processed the lie.

**CROSS-REFERENCE COMPLETE. SPECIES VARIATION *AETHERIS* CONFIRMED IN FIELD OBSERVATION. DATA UTILITY RATING: 8/10.**

The black line on his arm stopped pulsing. The red warning text flickered.

**TIME TO CONVERSION: 03:15.**

It wasn't enough. They were still converting him. The utility was high, but the Authority was farming him for biological mass, not data.

He needed to change the category. He needed to become a threat that required active suppression, not passive absorption.

He looked at the swarm.

Thousands of winged rats, descending in a cloud of leathery wings and chittering teeth.

They were looking for soft targets. Dale was standing in the open, anchored, but he was not soft.

He was armored in ink, and he needed to trigger a containment protocol.

But he had no tools. No spray. No traps. No net.

He had the ink, and the ink was the Authority's own substance. It was their stamp. It was their seal.

"Enter new claim," Dale said. "Claim: Technician Kowalski is currently saturated with Authority-grade binding agent.

If I am integrated, I become a vector for this agent.

I can spread it. I can contaminate other hosts.

"

The system went silent.

**WARNING: CONTAMINATION RISK DETECTED.** **CALCULATING SPREAD PROBABILITY.**

Dale smiled. It was a grimace, his lips cracking against the dry air. He wasn't trying to escape. He was trying to get infected enough to be quarantined.

If he became a walking biohazard, the Authority couldn't just absorb him. They had to isolate him. Isolation meant space. Isolation meant time.

The black ink on his legs bubbled.

It reacted to his intent. It sensed the threat of contamination and reacted defensively.

The hard shell on his ankles softened, then hardened again in a new shape.

Spikes.

The spikes dug into the pavement.

Dale felt a jolt of pain, sharp and electric, shooting up his spine. His nervous system was rewriting itself.

He could feel the ground.

Not just the vibration, but the chemical composition.

The asphalt. The tar.

The bones of the people who had died in the streets.

He could feel the swarm above him. He could feel their hunger.

"Claim: Technician Kowalski is now a Class-4 Biohazard Vector," he said. "Protocol 12-Alpha: Isolate and Incinerate."

**PROTOCOL 12-ALPHA REQUIRES WRITTEN AUTHORIZATION.** **CURRENT AUTHORIZATION LEVEL: INSUFFICIENT.**

"Then grant it," Dale said. "I am triggering a Level 1 Breach.

Look at the sky.

Look at what you're letting happen. If I integrate, I am a tool. If you incinerate me now, you save the city from a Level 1 Breach.

Which is more cost-effective?

"

The Authority paused.

Above, the swarm circled. They were confused.

The black ink on Dale's body was repelling them.

The rats in the sky didn't want to touch him. He was toxic to them.

A new window opened. Gold text.

**ASSESSMENT: TECHNICIAN KOWALSKI.** **CURRENT THREAT LEVEL: HIGH.** **COST OF INTEGRATION: HIGH.** **COST OF ELIMINATION: MODERATE.** **COST OF CONTAINMENT: LOW.**

**DECISION: CONTAINMENT.**

The black ink on his legs exploded outward. It wasn't an attack.

It was a cage.

The substance expanded, forming a sphere around Dale.

The spikes on his legs retracted, replaced by smooth, sealed surfaces. The air inside the sphere was sucked out.

Dale couldn't breathe.

The sphere rose.

It lifted him off the ground. The pavement fell away. The mall entrance disappeared. The black ink trail below him narrowed to a thread, then vanished.

Dale floated in the air, suspended in a bubble of Authority-grade sealant.

The swarm parted around him. The winged rats screamed, a high-pitched keen of frustration, as they were forced to fly around the containment sphere.

He was in a jar, and the Authority had bottled him.

Inside the sphere, the oxygen was running out. Dale could feel his lungs burning. He looked at his hands.

The fingers were still fused, but the black line had stopped advancing up his arm. It stopped at his shoulder.

He was preserved.

But he was trapped.

And he wasn't alone.

A shape pressed against the outside of the sphere. A face.

It was Vane.

Not Vane the man.

Vane the stamp. Vane the residue. The face was made of ink, flattened and smeared against the curved surface.

Its eyes were white gaps.

Its mouth was a jagged tear.

*"You broke the form,"* the ink-face said. The voice vibrated through the sealant, humming in Dale's bones. *"You forced them to contain you. But containment is not freedom, Dale. It's storage."*

Dale tried to speak, but there was no air. He tapped on the inside of the sphere with his fused fist. The sound was dull, muffled.

Vane's ink-face pressed closer.

*"The Authority doesn't incinerate assets,"* the face continued. *"It warehouses them. You're on a shelf now. Next to the others."*

Dale looked past Vane. Beyond the swarm. Beyond the city.

In the distance, hanging in the blackened sky, were other spheres.

Thousands of them. Some small, some large. Each one contained a technician.

Or a monster. Or both.

The sky was full of jars.

The realization hit Dale harder than the lack of oxygen.

The Rift wasn't an accident.

The city wasn't a battlefield. It was a factory.

And the technicians weren't heroes. They were inventory.

Vane's face smiled, a stretch of black liquid.

*"Welcome to the backlog, Technician. Your certification is permanent. And you're going to be very, very busy."*

The sphere moved. Not toward the ground. Not toward the mall.

It moved toward the largest sphere in the cluster. The one that pulsed with a deep, red light.

Dale watched it come. He couldn't run. He couldn't fight. He could only watch.

As the sphere approached, the red light intensified. It wasn't just a light. It was a label.

**ASSET: PRIMARY BROODMOTHER.** **STATUS: AWAKENING.**

The containment sphere carrying Dale slammed into the larger one. The impact shook the void. For a second, the two barriers merged. A seam appeared in the black sealant.

Dale saw the face of the Broodmother. It was human-shaped, but wrong.

Too many eyes.

Too many limbs. And in the center of its chest, a hole that looked exactly like a clipboard.

The sealant seam widened.

Dale's hand, fused and black, reached out. He didn't know why. Instinct. Protocol.

He pressed his palm against the breach.

And the Broodmother pressed back.

The connection wasn't physical. It was data.

A flood of information slammed into his mind. Not screams. Not pain.

Forms.

Thousands of forms.

Inspection reports. Violation codes. Treatment logs.

He saw the history of every technician who had ever been

"contained." He saw their failures. He saw their successes. He saw the patterns.

And he saw the loophole.

The Authority's containment protocol had a flaw. It relied on static isolation. It assumed the asset was inert.

But Dale wasn't inert. He was a Technician. And Technicians knew how to bypass locks.

He focused on the seam. He didn't try to break it. He tried to *label* it.

He thought of Form 77-B. He thought of the exception request.

He projected the command: **RECLASSIFY AS MAINTENANCE ACCESS.**

The system hesitated.

The Broodmother's eyes widened. It felt the intrusion. It tried to pull away, but the sealant held it in place.

Dale pushed harder. His mental weight against the system's logic.

**ERROR: INVALID ACCESS CODE.**

**RETRY?**

**YES.**

The seam widened another inch.

Vane's ink-face vanished. The sphere around him dissolved.

Dale fell.

Not down.

Forward.

Into the chest cavity of the Broodmother.

Into the hole where the clipboard should be, and he landed on a surface that felt like warm, wet clay.

The air was thick, smelling of ozone and rotting meat.

Dale stood up.

His legs worked. His hands worked.

He looked around, and he was inside a vast, dark chamber. The walls were made of the same black ink that had trapped him. But here, it was soft. Pliable.

And there were others.

Dozens of technicians, suspended in the ink, their bodies half-dissolved, their minds screaming silently. They were the battery pack. The fuel.

But they weren't the source.

In the center of the chamber, attached to the walls by tendrils of black fluid, was a machine.

It looked like a standard municipal incinerator, but scaled up to the size of a stadium. And it was running.

It was processing the technicians.

Dale walked toward it. His boots made no sound.

On the control panel, a single screen flickered.

**INPUT: 4,092 ASSETS.** **OUTPUT: DIMENSIONAL STABILIZATION.** **EFFICIENCY: 12%.**

The Authority wasn't just farming them. It was burning them to keep the Rift from closing. Because if the Rift closed, the flow of monsters stopped. If the flow stopped, the certifications became obsolete.

The Authority needed the monsters. It needed the infestation. It needed Dale.

He looked at the machine. He looked at the technicians in the walls.

He pulled a pen from his pocket. The ink was dry, but the ballpoint worked.

He walked to the nearest technician, a young woman whose legs had turned into insect chitinous legs. She was conscious. Her eyes tracked him.

Dale didn't save her. He couldn't. The system was too vast.

Instead, he wrote on the wall.

*VIOLATION: ILLEGAL USE OF LABOR.* *CODE: K-9.* *ACTION: TERMINATE PROTOCOL.*

He slammed his hand against the control panel.

The machine shuddered.

A new notification appeared, not in his vision, but on the screen.

**SYSTEM ALERT: UNAUTHORIZED ACCESS.** **TECHNICIAN KOWALSKI DETECTED.** **LOCATION: CORE CHAMBER.** **ACTION REQUIRED: IMMEDIATE PURGE.**

The chamber doors closed.

Dale ran.

But not for the exit, and he ran toward the machine's fuel intake.

If he was an asset, he could be processed. But if he jammed the intake with himself, he could break the cycle.

He reached the edge of the conveyor belt. The technicians were sliding down it, one by one, into the fire.

Dale grabbed a loose tendril of ink and whipped it around the belt's motor. It tangled. The gears ground against each other. Sparks flew.

The machine screamed. A mechanical, metallic shriek that vibrated through the chamber.

The technicians in the walls convulsed.

And from the shadows behind the machine, a voice spoke. Not the Authority's calm baritone.

A rasping, angry whisper.

*"You're not supposed to be in the engine room, Dale."*

Dale turned.

Vane stepped out of the ink. Not an ink-face. A full body. Rebuilt from the black fluid.

He held a clipboard.

*"The Authority doesn't care about your protest,"* Vane said, tapping the board with a pen. *"It cares about your signature. Sign the waiver, and you get out. Refuse, and you become part of the fuel forever."*

Vane held out the pen.

The machine groaned, the gears slipping further.

Dale looked at the pen. He looked at the burning technicians. He looked at the Rift above, held open by their suffering.

He took the pen, and he didn't sign the waiver.

He signed his own name.

**Dale Kowalski.**

Then he wrote underneath it:

**COMPLAINT FILED. RE: MALICIOUS NON-COMPLIANCE.**

Vane's eyes went wide.

"You can't file a complaint against a god," Vane hissed.

Dale tapped the paper.

"I can file it against a *system*," Dale said. "And systems have audits."

The ground shook.

The chamber walls cracked.

And from the cracks, something else crawled out. Something that wasn't ink. Something that wasn't Authority.

Something that had been waiting for the system to break, and the audit was coming.

And it wasn't here to help.
