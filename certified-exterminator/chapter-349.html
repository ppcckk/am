Chapter 349: Handshake with Auditor Prime

# Chapter 349

The red seal burned into the asphalt with the persistence of a neon sign in a dark alley. It wasn't just paint; it was a dimensional anchor point, humming at a frequency that made Dale's teeth ache.

PENDING REVIEW.

The letters were bold, sans-serif, and bureaucratic. They pulsed with a faint, radioactive glow that cast long, jagged shadows across the parking lot.

The girl stood three feet away, clutching the black business card like a shield.

Her knuckles were white. Her breathing had slowed, though her eyes darted toward the treeline where the janitor had vanished.

Dale didn't look at the girl. He looked at the seal.

He reached into his belt pouch and pulled out his laminated Field Guide. The entry for *Contract Enforcer* was still marked Verified, but the text beneath it was shifting. The ink was running, bleeding into the page like watercolor left in the rain.

"Step back," Dale said.

His voice was flat, devoid of the adrenaline that should have been pumping through his veins. He kept his hands visible, away from his spray canister.

"Do not touch the perimeter of the seal.

It's a containment field, not a doorway.

"

The girl didn't move.

"It's a subpoena."

"It's a code violation," Dale corrected. He tapped the side of his headset, activating the local recording protocol.

"Recording commenced.

Timestamp 14:02.

Location: Sector 4, Suburban Ohio. Subject: Unauthorized dimensional audit.

Status: Active.

"

A chime sounded in his inner ear. The Authority's interface overlaid his vision.

**NOTICE: UNLICENSED AUDIT DETECTED.** **THREAT LEVEL: CLASS III (ADMINISTRATIVE).** **ACTION REQUIRED: FILE FORM 88-B (INTERIM INJUNCTION) OR SUBMIT TO REVIEW.** **PENALTY FOR NON-COMPLIANCE: REVOCATION OF CERTIFICATION LEVEL II.**

Dale stared at the text. Revocation.

Not death.

Revocation. That was worse. Without Level II, he couldn't buy Class-3 insecticides.

He couldn't legally carry his pressurized injector.

He would be reduced to a civilian with a clipboard, watching his neighbors get eaten by things he could name but not kill.

He pulled a blank form from his vest. It wasn't paper; it was a sheet of treated vellum, sensitive to mana signatures. The Authority issued one per technician, and they were expensive.

"Who is Director Vane?" the girl asked. Her voice was steadier now. The shock was wearing off, replaced by a cold, hard calculation.

"Unknown entity within the Authority hierarchy," Dale said, scribbling the first line of the form. *Reason for Review: Unlicensed intervention in ongoing contract dispute.* "If they're sending a whistleblower, they're trying to cut out the middlemen."

The ground beneath the seal vibrated.

Dust motes danced in the air, swirling in a vortex directly above the red stamp. A low hum grew in intensity, vibrating in Dale's chest cavity. It wasn't a monster's growl.

It was the sound of a filing cabinet opening in a vacuum.

"Look," the girl said, pointing at the seal.

The red ink was bubbling. A figure rose from the pavement. Not a physical body, but a construct of light and legal jargon. It looked like a man made of stacked documents, his face a blank, featureless oval.

**PROCESS SERVER.**

The name appeared in Dale's peripheral vision.

The Field Guide didn't have an entry for it, and he hadn't encountered one before. It was a new species.

A new infestation.

Dale's hands moved automatically. He didn't think.

He reached for the spray nozzle at his hip, then stopped, and the nozzle was designed for organic pests. Acid beetles. Rats.

Not abstract legal entities.

Using it would be a cross-contamination penalty. A 40% debuff that would turn his next five minutes into a blur of nausea and failed spells.

He holstered the nozzle. Instead, he pulled out a stamp from his kit. A simple, rubber-handled stamp. *INSPECTION PENDING.*

"Stay back," Dale ordered.

The Process Server stepped forward. Its legs were made of shredded contracts. Its arms were long, tapering ribbons of red tape. It raised a hand, and a beam of pure, concentrated bureaucracy shot toward Dale.

Dale sidestepped. The beam hit the girl.

She didn't scream. She didn't flinch. The beam passed through her, dissolving the air around her feet into static.

"Targeting lock," Dale muttered. "It's targeting the whistleblower because she's the anomaly. I'm the obstruction."

He stepped between them. The Server paused, its document-face tilting.

Dale slammed the rubber stamp onto the air, directly in front of the Server's path. The stamp didn't hit the creature. It hit the *space* it occupied.

**ACTION LOGGED: INTERIM INJUNCTION FILED.** **STATUS: PENDING REVIEW.** **EFFECT: TARGET MOVEMENT SUSPENDED FOR 30 SECONDS.**

The Server froze. Its leg lifted, then hung in mid-air. The red tape arms stiffened. The humming noise dropped to a low, frustrated thrum.

Thirty seconds.

Dale turned to the girl.

"What did you take?"

She looked at the business card.

"Proof that the Authority is selling clearance."

"Selling to whom?"

"The Rift," she said. "They're not trying to close it. They're farming it."

Dale felt a cold knot in his stomach.

Farming. That was a term he knew. Pest control wasn't just about extermination.

Sometimes, it was about maintenance. Keeping the population under control so the problem didn't overwhelm the ecosystem.

But this wasn't maintenance, which was industrial agriculture.

"You're saying the Authority created the Rifts?"

"Not created," the girl said. "Cultivated.

They're breeding the monsters. They're certifying the hunters. It's a closed loop.

Supply and demand.

"

Twenty seconds left.

Dale looked at the frozen Process Server. He looked at the girl. He looked at his Field Guide.

He had a new entry forming. *Process Server.* But he didn't know its weakness. He didn't know how to kill it. He knew how to delay it.

He had to document it.

"Give me the card," Dale said.

She hesitated.

"Now."

She handed it to him, and the black card was cold. The silver embossing felt like a brand. Dale held it up, aligning it with the frozen Server.

The Authority's interface flickered.

**ITEM ACQUIRED: EVIDENCE OF CORRUPTION.** **CLASSIFICATION: CONTRABAND.** **ACTION REQUIRED: DISPOSE OR ARCHIVE.**

If he archived it, the Authority would seize it. If he disposed of it, he lost his leverage. If he used it...

He had to use it.

Dale pulled a pen from his vest. It wasn't ink. It was a stylus charged with raw, unrefined mana. He wrote on the back of the card.

*Claimant: Dale Kowalski.* *Defendant: Licensing Authority, Internal Investigations.* *Charge: Malfeasance in the First Degree.*

The words glowed blue. The System was listening.

This wasn't combat, which was litigation. And in this world, litigation was a form of warfare.

The Server's eye-hole widened. The frozen leg twitched. The thirty seconds were up.

**TIMER EXPIRED.**

The Server lunged.

Dale didn't dodge. He didn't spray. He held the card out, like a shield.

The Server's hand made contact with the card.

A shockwave blasted outward. Not of force, but of *order*.

The air snapped.

The red seal on the ground shattered, exploding into a cloud of red dust.

The Process Server shrieked, a sound like tearing paper, and dissolved into a pile of shredded documents.

Silence returned to the parking lot.

Dale stood there, holding the card. His hand was shaking. Not from fear.

From exhaustion. The mana drain had hit him hard.

He felt like he'd run a marathon while holding his breath.

The girl stared at the pile of paper.

"You sued it."

"I filed a motion," Dale said, straightening his vest. "It's different."

He looked at his interface.

**THREAT NEUTRALIZED (TEMPORARY).** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 8.5.** **NOTE: PROCESS SERVER WAS NOT ELIMINATED. IT WAS DEFERRED.** **WARNING: DEFERRAL ACCUMULATES. NEXT ENCOUNTER WILL BE CLASS IV.**

Class IV.

Dale closed his eyes for a second. Class IV meant he needed to upgrade his certification. He needed to pass the practical exam for Level III.

But the Authority was auditing him, and they were targeting him. And now, they knew he had the evidence.

He opened his eyes. The girl was watching him. Her expression was unreadable.

"You can't go home," she said.

Dale looked at his clipboard. The violation report for the kitchen infestation was still there. He tore off the top sheet, crumpled it, and threw it on the ground.

"I know," he said. "But I have a job to do."

"What job?"

Dale looked at the pile of shredded documents where the Server had been.

He walked over and picked up a single, intact fragment. It bore a seal. Not the Authority's seal.

A different one. A seal he recognized from the old textbooks.

The pre-Rift municipal code.

*City of Dayton. Code Enforcement.*

The Authority wasn't the only bureaucracy left.

"The Authority thinks they're the law," Dale said. "They're wrong. They're just the landlord. And the landlord is violating the lease."

He picked up his spray canister, and the gauge was empty. He switched to a secondary tank, filled with a viscous, black fluid.

Not insecticide. Sealant.

"We're not fighting them," Dale said. "We're exposing them."

The girl frowned.

"How?"

Dale pointed to the shattered red seal. Beneath it, the pavement was cracked.

And inside the crack, something was moving.

Not a monster. A tube. A pipe.

A conduit.

"That's the supply line," Dale said. "The Authority's getting their product from there. If we cut it, the whole operation stops."

He walked toward the crack. The girl followed.

As they approached, the ground rumbled again. But this time, it wasn't a stamp. It was a drill.

A massive, metallic drill bit punched through the asphalt, tearing through the pavement like paper. It rose from the earth, spinning violently, coated in a slime that smelled of ozone and rotting meat.

On top of the drill, a platform rose. Standing on it was a figure in a grey suit.

But this one was different.

Taller. Broader. Its face was covered in a mask of porcelain, with no mouth, only a single, glowing eye in the center of its forehead.

**AUDITOR PRIME.** **CLASSIFICATION: CLASS V.** **STATUS: ACTIVE ENFORCEMENT.**

Dale stopped. His hand hovered over his belt.

"Run," the girl whispered.

Dale didn't run. He looked at the Auditor Prime. He looked at the drill. He looked at the girl.

He pulled out his Field Guide. He flipped past the *Contract Enforcer* entry. He flipped past the *Process Server* entry. He found a blank page.

He wrote.

**SPECIES: AUDITOR PRIME.** **CHARACTERISTICS: METALLIC EXOSKELETON, PORCELAIN FACIAL STRUCTURE, SINGLE OPTICAL SENSOR.** **WEAKNESS: UNKNOWN.** **NOTE: REQUIRES CLASS III PROTOCOLS. CURRENT CERTIFICATION: CLASS II.**

He looked at the Auditor. It was looking at him. The single eye pulsed.

Dale looked at his certification level. **II**, and he looked at the Auditor's level. **V**.

He couldn't kill it. He couldn't even hurt it.

But he could document it.

And he could make them pay for the damage they were causing to the infrastructure.

Dale pulled out his stamp again. *INSPECTION PENDING.*

He didn't aim at the Auditor. He aimed at the drill.

"Recording commenced," Dale said. "Time: 14:05.

Subject: Unauthorized excavation.

Location: Protected Historical Site.

Violation: 44-C. Destruction of Public Infrastructure.

"

He slammed the stamp down.

**ACTION LOGGED: CITATION ISSUED.** **FINE: $50,000.** **STATUS: PENDING COLLECTION.**

The Auditor Prime stopped. The drill slowed. The massive machine groaned, its gears locking up as the fine was applied to its existence.

The Auditor's eye narrowed. The porcelain mask cracked.

A voice boomed from the machine, not from a mouth, but from the air itself.

"YOU CANNOT FINE THE AUTHORITY."

Dale looked up. He spoke to the empty air, to the System, to the invisible eye that watched everything.

"I'm not fining the Authority," Dale said. "I'm fining *you*. You're a contractor. And you're out of compliance."

The Auditor raised a hand. A beam of white light charged in its palm.

Dale looked at the girl.

"Take the card. Go to the library.

Find the old city charter. Page 42, subsection D.

"

"What are you doing?"

"Buying time," Dale said. He reached for his secondary tank. The black sealant. "And making them look bad in the paperwork."

He sprayed the sealant at the base of the drill. It hit the metal and hardened instantly, forming a black, tar-like shell. The drill stopped turning completely. The Auditor stumbled, its balance thrown off by the sudden change in torque.

The white beam fired.

Dale dove.

The beam scorched the air where his head had been. It hit the ground behind him, melting the asphalt into glass.

Dale scrambled to his feet. He was covered in sweat. His heart was hammering against his ribs, a frantic, irregular rhythm.

The Auditor Prime stepped off the drill. It walked toward him. Each step crushed the asphalt. It raised its hand again.

Dale had no spray left. No stamp. No plan.

He had one thing, and he reached into his pocket and pulled out the black business card. Director Vane's card.

He held it up.

The Auditor Prime paused. The glowing eye fixed on the card.

"DO YOU KNOW WHO SENT THAT?" the voice boomed.

"Yes," Dale said. "And I know what he's hiding."

The Auditor's hand lowered. The white light faded.

For the first time, the machine looked unsure.

"PROVIDE PROOF," the Auditor said.

Dale looked at the girl, who was still standing near the library entrance, clutching the card. Then he looked at the Auditor.

He had no proof. He had a rumor. He had a hunch. He had a certification that was about to be revoked.

But he had a job to do.

Dale stepped forward. He held out the card.

"Take it," he said. "And take me with it."

The Auditor Prime extended a hand made of cold, hard steel.

Dale took it, and the world went black.
