Chapter 287: Purge Protocol Omega

# Chapter 287

The siren didn't scream. It hummed.

A low, subsonic vibration that rattled the teeth before it registered in the ear. Dale felt it in his molars first, then in the soles of his boots. He didn't look up at the purple tear in the sky. He looked at the stranger's hands.

They were still trembling.

"Stop shaking," Dale said. "Contamination spreads through motion. You're broadcasting your distress signal to every Class-III entity within a two-mile radius. Stand still."

The stranger froze. His chest heaved.

The rain plastered his hair to his forehead, mixing with the grey sludge on his uniform.

Dale recognized the fabric. It was Authority-issue, but the stitching was sloppy. A subcontractor.

A glorified janitor who had seen too much and said too little.

"Who are you?" the stranger whispered.

"Licensed Technician. Class III pending.

Currently dealing with an unauthorized purge team.

" Dale tapped the clipboard.

The paper was wet, but the ink held. He had used waterproof gel pens three years ago, a decision that now saved his career.

"And you are a liability."

"I helped you," the stranger said. "I opened the door."

"You opened a breach. That's different." Dale turned away.

"Walk.

Keep your center of gravity low.

If they're deploying Purge Teams, they're using sonic lances. They target high-frequency vocal cords.

Silence is armor.

"

They moved into the parking lot. The rain fell harder, turning the asphalt into a slick, black mirror. The trucks were gone. The facility behind them had sealed itself, the steel shutters rolling down with a finality that felt personal.

Dale checked his Field Guide.

**Current Objective:** Neutralize Purge Team Alpha. **Threat Level:** Class III (Dimensional). **Protocol:** Containment via Environmental Override. **Note:** Sonic Lances resonate with structural steel. Do not engage in open spaces.

"Where are we going?" the stranger asked. His voice was too loud. Dale winced.

"Into the storm drain," Dale said. "It's a closed system. Predictable flow rates. Controlled environment."

"That's a sewer."

"It's a municipal infrastructure project with a high concentration of ammonia and methane. Class-III entities hate it. It interferes with their chitin resonance."

They reached the curb. Dale pried open the manhole cover with his multi-tool, the metal groaning under the strain. He tossed the cover aside. It clattered against the asphalt, a sound that seemed too loud in the sudden quiet.

"Jump," Dale ordered.

The stranger hesitated. The siren hummed louder.

A shadow detached itself from the roof of the facility.

It unfolded like a bat, wings spanning twenty feet, membranes translucent and pulsing with violet light. A Purge Drone.

Dale didn't look at it. He looked at the stranger's feet.

"Jump."

The stranger jumped.

Dale followed, sliding down the ladder into the darkness. The smell hit him instantly.

Urine, rot, and the metallic tang of old blood, which was comforting. Familiar. He pulled the manhole cover back into place, sealing them in.

Above, the drone shrieked. The sound was a physical blow, a wall of pressure that made Dale's vision blur. He gritted his teeth, checking his gear.

He had his spray tank. Half-full.

A mixture of concentrated saltwater, holy water (synthetic, from a licensed supplier in Dayton), and a proprietary adhesive that hardened on contact.

It wasn't a weapon, which was a sealant.

"Are we dead?" the stranger asked. The sound echoed in the concrete tunnel.

"Not yet," Dale said. He clicked on his headlamp. The beam cut through the haze of steam rising from the sewage flow.

"We're in a Class-2 infestation zone. Low humidity.

High pathogen count.

If the drones come in, they'll be vulnerable to fungal spores.

"

"Spores?"

"Look." Dale pointed his light at the walls.

Green mold grew in thick, velvet patches. *Aspergillus niger*. Black mold.

Common.

Deadly to lungs. Deadly to things made of living shadow.

Dale pulled a small vial from his belt. A spore bomb. Illegal in most jurisdictions. Legal here, under the emergency provisions of the Rift Act.

"We need to move," Dale said. "The tunnel branches ahead.

Left is maintenance.

Right is overflow.

If they're tracking heat, they'll go left. We go right.

"

"Why?"

"Because the right tunnel smells worse. And they don't like to be dirty."

They waded through the knee-deep water. The cold bit into Dale's thighs. He kept his clipboard dry, holding it above his head. The stranger stumbled, splashing.

"Careful," Dale hissed. "You're disturbing the sediment. That's a distress signal for the broodmother."

"There's a broodmother down here?"

"There's always a broodmother."

The tunnel narrowed. The ceiling dropped. They had to stoop.

The water turned from black to a slurry of grey sludge. Dale's boots squelched.

The sound was wet, heavy.

A drip echoed from behind them.

Dale stopped. He turned his headlamp toward the darkness.

Nothing.

"Keep moving," he said. But he slowed his pace. He listened.

The hum of the siren had stopped. Replaced by a clicking. Thousands of tiny, sharp clicks. Like rain on a windowpane.

*Chitin.*

They were here.

"Run," Dale said.

They didn't run. They scrambled. The water slowed them down.

The stranger slipped, falling face-first into the sludge.

Dale grabbed his collar, hauling him up.

"Get up. Don't swallow it."

The clicking grew louder.

Closer. Shadows moved along the ceiling, fast and erratic. Not drones.

Smaller things. *Rattus profundus* variants. The ones that had evolved in the dark, away from the Rift's light.

Blind.

Hungry.

Dale spun around. He raised his spray tank.

He didn't aim at the rats. He aimed at the wall.

A jet of adhesive hit the concrete, right next to a cluster of mold. It spread rapidly, trapping the spores. Then Dale kicked the wall.

The mold broke loose. A cloud of black dust exploded into the air.

The rats shrieked. It wasn't a cry of pain.

It was a sound of confusion. Their antennae twitched. They were blinded by the particulate matter.

The fungal spores clogged their sensory organs.

Dale sprayed again. This time, he coated the tunnel entrance. The adhesive hardened instantly, forming a translucent, sticky barrier.

The rats slammed into it, and they stuck. They screamed. They pulled, but the adhesive held.

It was designed for termites.

Rats were just bigger termites with better teeth.

"Move," Dale said.

They pushed forward. The tunnel opened up into a wider chamber. A junction. Water flowed from three directions.

And there, waiting in the center, was a figure.

Not a rat. Not a drone.

A man, and he stood in the middle of the sewage flow, waist-deep in the muck.

He wore a suit. A pristine, white suit. Unstained.

Dry. His face was hidden behind a mask made of polished chrome.

Dale stopped. The stranger bumped into him.

"Who is that?" the stranger breathed.

Dale checked his Field Guide.

**New Entry Detected.** **Species:** Authority Enforcer (Human-Interface Variant). **Classification:** Class IV. **Weakness:** Psychological. Over-reliance on Protocol.

"He's not an Enforcer," Dale said. "He's a Auditor."

"An Auditor?"

"The Authority sends them when the paperwork gets too messy. Or when the paperwork is being forged." Dale looked at the stranger. "Did you steal anything from the server room?"

"No.

I just...

I saw something. On the screen.

About you."

Dale felt a cold weight in his stomach. Not fear. Anger.

"You saw my file?"

"Your real file. It's not Class III. It's..." The stranger trailed off.

The Auditor raised a hand. His voice was amplified, projecting over the roar of the water.

"Dale Kowalski. Technician, Class II.

Status: Suspended.

Reason: Unauthorized containment of Class-IV assets. Violation Code: 77-B. Failure to document chain of custody.

"

Dale stepped forward. The water lapped at his knees.

"I documented it," Dale said.

His voice was flat. Professional.

"Report filed at 0400 hours.

Digital timestamp confirmed. Witness: Subject X-9. Here.

"

The Auditor tilted his head.

"Subject X-9 is a rogue entity. His testimony is inadmissible. Your report was flagged for corruption."

"It wasn't flagged. It was buried. You buried it."

"Protocol requires verification. You provided none."

"I provided results. The infestation is neutralized."

"The infestation was *contained*," the Auditor said. "Containment is not extermination.

You left a nest.

You left a breach.

You created a liability.

"

"I created a delay. I bought time to find the source."

"Time is a resource. You wasted it."

The Auditor snapped his fingers.

From the shadows of the junction, two drones dropped. Not the flying kind. The crawling kind.

Tank-like. Armored.

They moved with a mechanical whir, crushing the dead rats beneath their treads.

Dale looked at his spray tank. Empty. He had used the last of the adhesive.

He looked at the stranger.

"Give me your gloves."

"What?"

"The gloves. They're Authority-issue. They have a grounding mesh.

They're not just for protection.

They're for *conducting*.

"

The stranger hesitated. Then he pulled the gloves off. They were soaked.

Dale snatched them. He pulled out his multi-tool and ripped the lining out of one glove. He exposed the thin copper mesh inside.

He didn't have a battery. But he had a wire. The wire from the stranger's console. He had pocketed it in the chaos.

He wrapped the copper mesh around the wire. He bit the end, stripping the insulation with his teeth.

"Stand back," Dale said.

The drones advanced. Their sensors locked onto Dale's heat signature.

Dale threw the wire into the water, and the water was a conductor. The junction was grounded to the facility's main power grid. Dale had seen the cable running along the ceiling. He had seen the frayed insulation.

He didn't need to shock the drones. He needed to shock the *ground*.

He connected the wire to the rebar in the wall. The copper mesh completed the circuit.

The drones froze. Not because they were shocked. Because their sensors were overwhelmed. The surge in the ground line sent a feedback loop through their motion trackers.

They stuttered. They spun in circles.

The Auditor took a step back. His chrome mask reflected Dale's headlamp.

"Electrocution hazard," the Auditor said. "Violation Code: 104-A. Unsafe electrical manipulation."

"Emergency provision," Dale said. "Section 4, Paragraph C. Immediate threat to life permits improvisation."

"You are improvising with lethal force."

"I am improvising with *environmental* force. There's a difference."

The drones crashed into each other. Sparks flew. The water boiled around them.

Dale turned to the stranger.

"Run. Now."

The stranger scrambled up the bank. Dale didn't move. He watched the Auditor.

The Auditor didn't run. He pulled a device from his belt. A small, black box. He tossed it into the water.

It sank.

Then it exploded.

Not with fire. With light.

A blinding, white flash that bleached everything. Dale closed his eyes, but the light burned through his eyelids.

When he opened them, the Auditor was gone, and the drones were gone.

Only the stranger remained, crouching on the bank, coughing.

Dale sat down in the water. He was exhausted. His legs shook. He looked at his clipboard.

The paper was dissolved. The ink was gone. The report was gone.

He had no proof, and he had no documentation.

He had no Thoroughness Rating.

Without documentation, the kills didn't count. The points wouldn't post. The certification wouldn't advance.

He had saved his life. But he had failed the job.

"Did you see what he said?" the stranger asked. "About your file?"

Dale looked at the water.

A single rat floated past, dead. He didn't pick it up. He couldn't.

The protocol required a photo. He had no camera.

"What did it say?"

"It said you're not Class III," the stranger said. "It said you're a *Source*."

Dale went still.

"A Source?"

"Of the Rift. The Authority's scanners don't detect infestations around you. They detect *emissions*. From you."

Dale looked at his hands, and they were clean.

He had washed them. But he felt a hum in his bones.

The same hum as the siren.

He had been treating the monsters.

But what if the monsters were treating him?

A notification flashed in his peripheral vision. Red. Urgent.

**SYSTEM ALERT.** **STATUS: DEGRADED.** **REASON: LOSS OF DOCUMENTATION.** **PENALTY: -500 TP.** **WARNING: CERTIFICATION REVOCATION IMMINENT.**

Dale stood up. The water dripped from his clothes.

"We can't stay here," he said.

"Where do we go?"

Dale looked at the ceiling. The cable was smoking. The facility was above them.

"To find a printer," Dale said. "And a witness."

He looked at the stranger.

"And to find out what I am."

The stranger nodded. He looked terrified. But he moved.

They walked deeper into the tunnel. The darkness swallowed them.

Behind them, in the server room of the facility, a monitor flickered.

A new entry appeared.

**SUBJECT: DALE KOWALSKI.** **STATUS: TERMINAL.** **CLASSIFICATION: HOST.** **ACTION: INITIATE PURGE PROTOCOL OMEGA.**

The siren started again. But this time, it didn't hum.

It screamed.
