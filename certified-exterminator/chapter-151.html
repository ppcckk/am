Chapter 151: Data Integrity Check

# Chapter 151

The pen tip snapped.

Dale stared at the ink pooling on the clipboard, a dark blot spreading over the false entry for *Rattus profundus*. The creature before him did not have the snout of a rat. It had no snout at all. Just that pale, featureless oval and limbs that bent at angles the human skeleton should not support.

The System notification in his peripheral vision pulsed red. **DATA INTEGRITY CHECK: 45% COMPLETE.**

Dale didn't look at the monster. He looked at the guard still standing on the other side of the turnstile. The man's face was gray, sweat beading on his forehead despite the chill in the air.

"Hey," Dale said, his voice flat. "You have a spare ballpoint. Blue ink. Standard issue."

The guard blinked. "Sir, you just falsified a species ID. The Authority is going to flag your account. You need to back away from the anomaly."

"I need a pen," Dale repeated. He didn't blink.

He didn't check his six.

He just held out his hand, palm up, waiting. "The current ink cartridge is compromised. If I don't document the threat level now, the audit trail breaks.

Do you understand protocol compliance, officer?

Or are you just here to watch?"

The guard hesitated.

His hand drifted toward his belt, not for a weapon, but for a pack of cigarettes. "You're crazy, man. That thing... it's not a rat.

It's tall. It's looking at you."

"It's looking at the clipboard," Dale corrected.

"Which means it recognizes the authority of the documentation.

Or it's confused. Either way, I need to finish the entry before the integrity check hits sixty percent.

If it hits sixty, it locks the file. Then I have to wait twenty-four hours for a manual review while it eats Sector 4."

The guard looked at the creature. The creature tilted its head. The motion was too smooth, like oil sliding down glass.

"If I give you a pen," the guard whispered, "I'm an accessory."

"You're already an accessory by standing there," Dale said. "Open the gate."

The guard shook his head. "I can't. The override is one-time use. You're out of clearance."

Dale tapped his earpiece. It wasn't connected to a radio. It was a piece of plastic he'd found in a vending machine three chapters ago. He tapped it twice, sharply.

"Dispatch," he said into the silence. "Unit Seven-Four.

Requesting immediate backup for a Class-3 containment breach.

Suspect species: *Rattus profundus*, Variant Alpha. Aggressive. High mobility.

I am currently holding position with non-lethal deterrents."

The guard stared at him. "Who are you talking to?"

"The Authority," Dale said. "They monitor voice logs.

If they hear a Class-3 threat and a technician on-site, they auto-deploy a Containment Team.

It's in the handbook. Page 412. 'Pre-emptive Resource Allocation in Unconfirmed Zones.'"

The guard's eyes widened. He looked at the creature, then at Dale. "You're bluffing."

"Am I?" Dale pointed to the red counter in his vision. **INTEGRITY CHECK: 52%.** "The System doesn't care if it's real yet. It cares if the report *sounds* real.

I'm writing a standard high-priority alert.

If I finish this sentence, the System flags it as a known, active threat.

Then your shift supervisor gets a ping. Then you get fired for not assisting.

Then the creature gets contained by a team that actually has weapons, and you go home."

The guard swallowed. He looked at the creature. It had taken a step forward. Its long fingers twitched.

"Who is your supervisor?" the guard asked, his voice trembling.

"Officer Miller. Badge 409. He's on break. But he checks his messages every ten minutes."

The guard pulled out his phone. His hands shook. He dialed.

Dale ignored him. He picked up the broken pen.

The tip was gone, but the ink reservoir was still pressurized.

He squeezed the shaft. Black ink sprayed onto his thumb. He smeared it across the paper, turning the false entry into a jagged, urgent scrawl.

**SPECIES IDENTIFICATION: RATTUS PROFUNDUS (VARIANT ALPHA).** **STATUS: CONFIRMED HOSTILE.** **ACTION: IMMEDIATE CONTAINMENT PROTOCOL DELTA.**

The red counter froze.

**DATA INTEGRITY CHECK: PAUSED.** **REASON: PENDING DISPATCH CONFIRMATION.**

The guard lowered his phone.

He looked pale. "Miller picked up.

He said... he said he has a Unit Seven-Four in the logs.

He said you're a certified Level II technician. He said you're supposed to be in Sector 2."

"I moved," Dale said. He wiped his ink-stained thumb on his coveralls.

"Sector 2 is quiet.

Sector 4 is not.

Send the team."

The guard stepped back from the turnstile. He looked at the creature, then at Dale. "If this is a trick..."

"It's not a trick. It's a workflow optimization," Dale said. "You're welcome.

Now, stand back. Protocol Delta requires a clear perimeter."

The creature stopped. It didn't growl. It didn't hiss. It made a sound like a wet cough. *Hck.*

Dale looked at it.

For the first time, he saw the details. The white oval face wasn't skin. It was chitin.

Smooth, polished, and seamless.

There were no eyes. But there were pits along the jawline, vibrating slightly.

"Hey," Dale said to the guard. "Does it have wings? Look for vestigial structures on the back."

"It's... it's tall," the guard stammered.

"Maybe six feet? No, seven. It's thin.

Like a stick."

"Wings," Dale pressed.

"I don't see any."

"Good.

If it had wings, it would be a *Volantus* class.

I'd need a net. I don't have a net.

I have a clipboard."

The guard looked confused. "What does the clipboard do?"

"It's a prop," Dale said. "It keeps the System thinking I'm a professional.

If I drop it, the System assumes I'm a civilian in distress.

Civilians get evacuated. Technicians get supported. I want support."

The guard stared at him. "You're manipulating the algorithm."

"I'm leveraging the bureaucracy," Dale corrected. "Big difference."

A low rumble echoed through the concrete.

Not from the creature. From the ground.

The black cracks in Sector 4 were widening.

More shapes were pushing through. Smaller ones. Skittering.

"More coming," Dale said. He didn't look down.

He kept his eyes on the tall creature.

"If I don't finish this report, the System won't release the Containment Team.

They're waiting for a 'Verified Threat' tag. I gave them a 'Pending' tag.

I need to convert it."

"How?" the guard asked. He was crying now. Actually crying. "How do you convert it?"

"I have to kill one," Dale said. "Just one.

Small one.

To prove the infestation is active. Then the System validates the 'Variant Alpha' entry because it matches the sub-species profile."

The guard looked at the small things emerging from the cracks. They looked like centipedes, but with faces. "You... you have to kill them? With what?"

Dale looked at his belt.

He had a spray can. It was labeled *DETERGENT/GENERAL PURPOSE*.

Useless against chitin.

He had a flashlight. Heavy. Aluminum.

"I have this," Dale said, lifting the flashlight. "And I have the knowledge that *Rattus profundus* sub-species have a soft spot behind the third leg joint.

If I hit it, they liquefy.

Instantly.

It's in the Field Guide. I haven't filled it in yet, but I know it's true."

The guard shook his head. "You don't know that. You just lied about the rat. You can't lie about the anatomy."

Dale paused. He looked at the guard. Then he looked at the System counter. **INTEGRITY CHECK: 55%.**

"You're right," Dale said. "I don't know it. I guessed."

The guard's eyes went wide.

"But," Dale continued, "I'm going to write it in the Field Guide right now. And then I'm going to try it. If it works, the System marks the entry as 'Verified by Field Observation.' If it fails, I die.

Or I lose my license. Probably both."

"You're insane," the guard whispered.

"I'm efficient," Dale said. He turned to the nearest centipede-thing as it crawled out of the crack. It was the size of a house cat. It had too many legs and a mouth that opened sideways.

Dale stepped forward. The tall creature watched him. It didn't attack. It seemed to be waiting.

"Hey, big guy," Dale called out. "You want to see my work?"

The tall creature tilted its head again.

Dale raised the flashlight.

He didn't swing. He aimed. He focused on the third leg joint.

His hands were steady.

They were always steady when he was working. The panic was there, screaming in the back of his skull, but his arms remembered the muscle memory of fourteen years of squashing things.

He swung.

The flashlight connected with a wet crunch.

The creature shrieked. A sound like tearing metal.

It didn't liquefy.

It exploded.

Black fluid sprayed across Dale's coveralls. The legs flew off. The body collapsed into a steaming heap of goo and chitin fragments.

Dale stepped back. He wiped his face with his sleeve. He looked at his clipboard.

The System flashed gold.

**FIELD VERIFICATION COMPLETE.** **SPECIES: RATTUS PROFUNDUS (VARIANT ALPHA).** **WEAKNESS CONFIRMED: POSTERIOR LIMB JOINT 3.** **POINTS AWARDED: 150.** **CERTIFICATION STATUS: LEVEL II.**

The red counter vanished.

The turnstile beeped. The guard's phone buzzed.

"Dispatch says they're five minutes out," the guard said. He looked at Dale with a mixture of horror and awe. "You just... you just guessed."

"I didn't guess," Dale lied.

He uncapped a new pen he'd found in his pocket. He had carried it since Chapter 140. He just hadn't been able to find it until now.

"I deduced. It's a *Rattus*.

It has legs.

It has joints.

Joints are weak points. It's basic biology."

The tall creature stepped closer. It reached out. Its hand brushed Dale's shoulder.

The touch was cold. Dry.

Dale didn't move. He looked up at the faceless oval.

"Hello," Dale said. "I'm writing this in your file."

The creature didn't speak. But the ground beneath them shook.

From the main crack, a voice echoed. Not a roar. A voice. Human.

"Dale?"

Dale froze. The pen stopped mid-stroke.

It was Sarah. His ex-wife. She stepped out of the darkness of the rift, holding a sword made of fused bone. Her eyes were black. black.

"You lied to the System," Sarah said. Her voice was distorted, like it was coming through water. "I heard the error code. I came to help."

Dale looked at the guard. "Who is that?"

The guard stepped back. "That's... that's a citizen. She was reported missing in Sector 2."

Sarah smiled.

It was the same smile she used to give him when he forgot to take out the trash. "You're not a technician anymore, Dale. You're a fraud.

And the Authority knows it.

They're coming for you. Not the containment team.

The Cleaners."

Dale looked at his clipboard. The new entry was still there. **WEAKNESS CONFIRMED.**

He looked at Sarah. "You're not a citizen. You're an infestation."

Sarah's smile faded. "I'm your wife."

"You're a *Rattus* variant," Dale said. He pointed his pen at her. "Look at your hands.

You have four fingers. You used to have five."

Sarah looked down at her hands. She looked confused. She looked at Dale.

"I have five," she said.

"You have four," Dale said. "And you're standing in a puddle of your own blood. You're bleeding. You didn't notice."

Sarah looked down at her feet. The ground was wet. Red.

She screamed.

It was a human scream.

Then it changed. It became the wet cough of the creature.

Her bones snapped. Her shape twisted.

Dale stepped back. He raised his clipboard.

"Guard," Dale said. "Run."

The guard ran.

Sarah lunged.

Dale didn't fight. He wrote.

**SPECIES IDENTIFICATION: SARAH KOWALSKI (INFECTED).** **STATUS: TERMINAL.** **ACTION: QUARANTINE.**

The System flashed.

**IDENTIFICATION REJECTED.** **REASON: SUBJECT IS NOT A TARGET SPECIES. LEGAL PROTECTIONS ACTIVE.**

Dale's pen stopped.

He couldn't kill her. The System wouldn't let him. She was a protected person. A survivor.

Sarah was on him. Her cold, dry hands grabbed his throat. Her face was melting. The white oval was forming over her skin.

Dale choked. He couldn't breathe. He couldn't write.

The tall creature watched. It didn't attack. It just watched.

And then, from the darkness behind Sarah, a second voice.

"Dale Kowalski," it said. "Your Thoroughness Rating is dropping. Please correct the error."

Dale's vision blurred. He looked up.

Standing behind Sarah was a figure in a white suit. It held a clipboard. It looked exactly like Dale.

Except its face was gone.

And it was smiling.
