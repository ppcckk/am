Chapter 241: Negotiating with Gods

# Chapter 241

The stylus hummed against the clipboard, leaving a trail of burning crimson glyphs in the air. Dale didn't look at the writing. He looked at the hand.

It rose from the fissure like a jagged shard of obsidian, fused with human metacarpals that had been stretched and twisted into impossible angles. Glass.

Bone.

Class 5 threat. No entry in the Field Guide. The contamination penalty meant the gray screen was his reality for the next twenty-four hours.

He had no identification data.

No weakness profile. No protocol selection.

"Three minutes," Vance barked, her voice cracking like a whip over the static of the widening Rift. "Brief them. Now."

The Rapid Response Unit stood in a semicircle, twenty fresh faces staring at Dale with varying degrees of awe and terror. They wore sleek, tactical armor that cost more than Dale's entire service vehicle. They held plasma rifles, kinetic hammers, and one guy holding a expensive-looking axe. None of them held a clipboard.

Dale clicked the stylus off. Then on. The red glow pulsed.

"Listen up," Dale said.

His voice was flat, stripped of the adrenaline that made Vance's voice tremble. "You are not heroes. You are not soldiers.

You are technicians."

A young woman in the front row, her helmet visor reflecting the glass-hand monster's approach, stepped forward. "We're here to kill it, Kowalski. The Authority sent us to clear the sector."

"The Authority sent you to *exterminate* it," Dale corrected, pointing the stylus at her chest plate.

"There is a semantic difference.

Killing yields zero points if the protocol is incomplete. You are here to document, isolate, eliminate, and verify.

If you kill it and don't verify the nest is dead, the infestation returns. If you don't document the species, we can't map the Rift's expansion rate. If you don't isolate the workspace, you risk cross-contamination penalties that will strip your certification."

The young woman—Badge ID: RRU-04, name tag obscured by sweat—scowled. "We don't have time for paperwork. That thing is coming out of the ground with enough force to liquefy a tank."

"Then you have no time for mistakes," Dale said. He tapped the clipboard.

"I am the Commander now.

That means I own the methodology. You own the execution. I need you to identify the entry vector.

The ground is glass and bone.

That implies a silicate-based biological matrix. Standard kinetic impacts shatter it, but shattering creates splinters.

Splinters are secondary vectors.

Do you understand?"

The guy with the axe raised his hand. "So, no smashing?"

"No uncontrolled smashing," Dale said. "Controlled fragmentation.

We need to keep the pieces contained.

If those shards hit the surrounding asphalt, they'll embed.

Embedded shards become secondary infestation points. We treat this as a Class 5 containment breach, not a combat engagement."

Vance stepped forward, her face pale. "Dale, they're not trained in containment protocols. They're combat specialists."

"Then teach them containment," Dale said, not looking up. He was already sketching a diagram on the holographic display of his clipboard. "Line them up.

Three squads. Squad A, left flank.

Squad B, right flank.

Squad C, rear guard. You, with the axe, you're in Squad C. You're the anchor."

The axe-holder bristled. "I'm the heavy hitter. I should be on the front."

"You're the heavy hitter because you hit hard," Dale said.

"That makes you dangerous to your teammates if you swing wild. Rear guard.

You prevent retreat.

You also prevent the shards from rolling backward into the civilian zone behind us. Your job is to keep the debris field static."

Silence hung in the air, heavier than the humidity. The glass hand twitched. It was three hundred yards away, dragging itself up from the earth with a sound like grinding tectonic plates.

Squad A hesitated. The young woman from earlier, RRU-04, looked at Vance, then at Dale.

"What's the plan for the isolation zone?

We don't have containment foam.

We don't have null-void barriers."

Dale looked at his clipboard. The red glyphs were fading. The stylus was running out of charge.

He had three minutes left on the clock, but he had no inventory of specialized tools. He had a clipboard, a stylus, and a team of over-equipped amateurs.

"Improvisation," Dale said. "Section 9, Paragraph C: Improvised Containment for Silicate-Based Organisms."

"That's not in the manual," Vance said. "You can't invent protocols on the fly."

"I'm not inventing.

I'm adapting." Dale pointed to the street behind them. A construction site for a new suburban mall sat across the road. Concrete mixers.

Steel rebar.

Plastic tarps. "Use the environment.

Squad A, go to the construction site.

Get five large plastic tarps. Squared off. Heavy duty.

Squad B, get the rebar bundles. We're building a cage, not a wall.

A cage contains.

A wall deflects.

Deflection spreads contamination."

RRU-04 blinked. "You want us to run away from the monster to get supplies?"

"I want you to set up the workspace," Dale said. "If we engage without a defined workspace, every hit is an uncontrolled event. Uncontrolled events trigger the Thoroughness penalty.

I just took a fifty percent hit because I used a Class 1 tool on a Class 5. I will not let that happen to you.

You will spend your points on the elimination, not the cleanup."

The ground shook. The hand was halfway out. It was the size of a bus. The fingers were translucent, veins of black ichor pulsing beneath the surface.

Squad A moved. They didn't argue. They ran toward the construction site. Their boots slammed against the pavement.

Squad B followed, grabbing rebar.

Squad C, the axe-holder and four others, remained with Dale.

"You're naked," the axe-holder said. He looked at Dale's clipboard. "You have no weapons.

No protocols.

Your guide is gray. What are you going to do?"

Dale looked at the entity.

He didn't see a monster. He saw a violation. A structural failure in the dimensional membrane.

The hand wasn't attacking; it was *infesting* the local geography, replacing organic matter with crystalline structures.

"I'm writing the report," Dale said.

The axe-holder stared. "For what? The Authority doesn't care about reports during live ops."

"The Authority cares about the *data*," Dale said.

"And right now, you are the data.

Every step you take, every angle you choose, every second you wait, is being recorded. If you mess up, the report reflects it.

If you succeed, the report reflects it. I need to ensure the narrative of this event is clean. If it's messy, we get fined.

If we get fined, we don't get promoted. If we don't get promoted, the next wave kills us."

The axe-holder swallowed. "Fine. What do I do?"

"Wait," Dale said.

"Do not engage until I give the signal. Watch the fingers. The thumb is longer than the index.

That's an anomaly.

In standard human anatomy, the thumb is shorter. In Rattus profundus, the claws are symmetrical.

This asymmetry suggests a specific growth pattern.

It's growing from the palm, not the knuckles. That means the core is in the palm."

Vance stepped closer. "How do you know that? Your Field Guide is contaminated."

"I don't," Dale admitted. "I'm observing.

Observation is the first step of the protocol.

You don't need the guide to see the shape.

You need the guide to know the chemical weakness. We don't have the weakness.

So we target the geometry."

"Geometry?" Vance asked.

"The palm is the center of mass. If we sever the connection at the wrist, the hand collapses.

But the wrist is deep underground.

We can't reach it. We have to bring the hand to the surface first. We need to bait it."

"Bait?" RRU-04 had returned with four teammates, dragging massive plastic tarps. "With what?"

Dale looked at the axe-holder. "You."

The axe-holder paled. "Me?"

"You're the only one with a melee weapon that can create a distraction without shattering the local infrastructure.

You stand at the edge of the tarps. You yell.

You make noise.

The entity is drawn to vibration. It will reach for you. When it reaches, Squad A drops the tarps over the wrist.

Squad B pins the rebar through the tarps to anchor them. Then Squad C, you swing at the wrist, not the palm.

You cut the connection."

"That's suicide," the axe-holder said.

"That's protocol," Dale said.

"Section 9, Paragraph C. Bait and Contain.

The risk is calculated. The reward is a verified kill with zero environmental debris."

The axe-holder looked at Vance. Vance looked at Dale.

"Do it," Vance said.

The axe-holder nodded, gripping his weapon until his knuckles turned white. He walked to the edge of the prepared zone, a fifty-yard stretch of asphalt Dale had cleared with mental precision.

The glass hand was fully out now. It towered over the buildings.

It didn't roar.

It didn't speak. It simply expanded, its fingers spreading like a spider's web.

The axe-holder screamed.

A raw, human sound. He banged his axe against his shield. *Clang.

Clang.

Clang.*

The hand stopped.

It turned.

The movement was slow, grinding. The sound of glass against asphalt was deafening. It reached for the axe-holder.

"Now!" Dale shouted.

Squad A threw the tarps. They didn't fly; they fell, weighted by the rebar Squad B had already planted.

The plastic slapped over the wrist of the entity.

The glass fingers twitched, trying to grip the axe-holder, but the tarps restricted their reach.

The fingers scraped against the plastic, shredding it, but not tearing it.

The axe-holder swung.

He didn't aim for the palm. He aimed for the wrist, where the glass met the earth. He swung with everything he had. The axe bit into the translucent bone.

It didn't break.

The axe bounced off. The axe-holder staggered back, the force of the recoil breaking his stance.

The hand lunged.

Dale didn't move. He clicked his stylus.

"Penalty warning," he muttered. "Kinetic impact insufficient. Structural integrity too high."

The hand swiped at the axe-holder. The axe-holder dodged, rolling under the sweeping fingers. The fingers smashed into the rebar anchor, sending sparks flying.

"Squad C!" Dale yelled. "Rotate! Switch to high-frequency sonic emitters! The glass resonates at 440 hertz!"

The four members of Squad C who hadn't drawn melee weapons activated their gear. Humming devices on their backs. They tuned them.

A low whine filled the air.

The glass hand vibrated. Cracks appeared along the wrist.

"It's working!" Vance yelled.

"Not enough," Dale said. He was looking at his clipboard. The stylus was flickering.

The battery was at 12%. He needed to finish the documentation before the entity died.

If he didn't log the method, the Authority would mark it as "Improper Use of Unlicensed Tools."

"Keep the frequency steady," Dale ordered. "Do not increase volume. It's not about power.

It's about resonance.

Match the pitch of the crack."

The cracks spread. The hand shuddered.

The axe-holder, bleeding from a cut on his arm, stood up. "What do I do?"

"Wait for the shatter," Dale said.

The hand exploded.

Not violently. It fractured inward, collapsing into a pile of jagged shards. The core of the hand, a pulsating black sphere, fell to the ground.

Squad A rushed it with containment canisters. They sprayed a neutralizing agent over the sphere. The sphere sizzled, then went dark.

Silence.

Dale exhaled. He looked at his clipboard. The red glyphs were stable.

**TREATMENT POINTS: 450.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 8.5.**

Vance walked over, her face tight. "You didn't verify the nest."

Dale pointed to the fissure. "The core is dead. The source is gone. But look at the shards."

The shards on the ground were still vibrating.

"They're not just debris," Dale said. "They're eggs."

Vance's eyes widened. "Eggs?"

"Class 5 threats don't reproduce quickly. But they don't die easily. If we leave those shards, they'll reform in six hours. We have to clear the workspace."

"How?" Vance asked.

Dale looked at the team.

They were panting, battered, but alive. They looked at him with a new kind of respect. Not fear.

Respect.

"We sweep," Dale said.

"We document every shard.

We bag them. We incinerate them off-site.

That's the protocol."

"And if we don't?" Vance asked.

"Then the penalty for incomplete extermination is permanent. We lose the certification.

The Authority blacklists us.

No more licenses. No more income. We're just civilians with expensive toys."

The axe-holder looked at the shards. "So we clean up."

"We clean up," Dale said.

He clicked the stylus off. The red glow died.

"Good work," Dale said. "Dismissed."

The team moved, efficient and quiet. They began the tedious process of collecting shards.

Vance stayed by Dale's side. "You didn't tell them about the Rift's second layer."

Dale paused.

He looked up at the sky. The Rift was no longer just widening.

It was *descending*.

A second shape was forming in the clouds. Not a hand. A mouth.

"I didn't because they needed to focus on the ground," Dale said. "Now that the ground is clear, we can look up."

Vance grabbed his arm. Her grip was hard.

"Kowalski, that thing in the sky.

It's not a vermin.

It's not a pest."

"What is it?"

"It's a Client," Vance whispered.

Dale froze. Clients weren't enemies. Clients were the people who hired the Authority.

They were the ones who paid the bills. They were the ones who caused the infestations for profit.

"A Client?" Dale said. "Here?"

"The Authority is selling the Rift," Vance said. "They're licensing the monsters. You're not exterminating an infestation, Dale. You're enforcing a property line."

Dale looked at his clipboard. The empty page seemed to stare back at him.

He picked up the stylus.

"Write it down," Vance said. "Write it all down. Because if you don't, they'll kill you. And if you do, you'll have to go up there and negotiate a contract with a god."

Dale looked at the mouth in the sky. It opened. A sound came out, not a scream, but a voice. A voice that sounded like thousands of insects rubbing their legs together.

"DEAL TERMS ACCEPTED," the voice boomed.

Dale clicked the stylus.

He started writing.
