Chapter 304: Motion Filed

# Chapter 304

The pen nib snapped.

Not because Dale pressed too hard. The ballpoint had simply given up, its reservoir dry, its plastic casing brittle under the stress of holding reality together. The red ink that had been suspended in the air, forming the words **APPEAL FILED**, flickered and died. The Authority's interface, usually a crisp, sterile blue, turned a bruised purple.

**APPEAL STATUS: PENDING.** **REASON: INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE.** **TIME REMAINING: 00:59:59.**

The figure wearing Miller's face tilted its head. The neck cracked, a wet, popping sound that echoed in the small kitchen.

It didn't look angry.

It looked bored. A bureaucrat checking a form that wasn't filled out correctly.

"Counter-complaint," the figure said, its voice sounding like gravel grinding in a mixer.

"You are citing jurisdictional overreach. You are claiming the Authority failed to provide adequate PPE for Class III exposure.

This is a baseless accusation, Mr.

Kowalski. Your certification level is one. You are not authorized to handle Class III entities.

Therefore, you are not authorized to file complaints regarding them. You are unauthorized personnel.

"

Dale stared at the floating text. The timer ticked down. *00:59:58.*

Sarah was still on the floor, curled into a ball, her hands over her ears. The sleeping dolls were gone. In their place were two piles of gray ash, shaped vaguely like children.

The air smelled of ozone and old blood.

Dale looked at his hand. The pen was useless. His clipboard was empty. His Field Guide was contaminated with the entry for *Rattus profundus*, which meant any protocol he used against this thing would trigger the cross-contamination penalty, debuffing his damage output by forty percent.

He had forty percent less chance of survival, and he also had a job to do.

Dale took a breath.

It was shaky, shallow.

His heart hammered against his ribs, a frantic bird trapped in a cage of bone. But his hands, once again, were steady.

They always were, when he was working, and he reached into his belt. No wrench. No spray. Just a small, battered notebook and a pencil.

"Unauthorized personnel," Dale repeated. His voice was flat. Deadpan. "Is that the official terminology?"

The figure smiled. Its teeth were too long.

"It is the correct classification."

"Classification implies categorization," Dale said. He opened the notebook. "Categorization implies the existence of a standard."

The figure paused. The red light in its eyes dimmed slightly.

"Proceed."

Dale didn't look up. He wrote.

Not in the air.

On the paper. The scratch of the pencil on the cheap wood pulp was the loudest sound in the room.

**OBSERVATION 1:** Entity mimics host (Miller) but lacks host's biometric signature. **OBSERVATION 2:** Entity possesses Authority-issued citation (Violation Code: 404-B). **OBSERVATION 3:** Entity is interacting with a minor (Sarah) who is a dependent of the primary contractor.

The figure stepped forward.

One step. Two.

The floorboards groaned under its weight.

It wasn't just standing there. It was *auditing* the room. Checking for compliance.

"You are attempting to document me," the figure said. It sounded amused. "That is a Class III protocol. You do not have the clearance."

Dale kept writing.

"I am documenting the *presence* of a secondary infestation.

I am not engaging it.

I am reporting it, which is a Class I duty. Reporting is mandatory.

Failure to report is a violation. I have already been cited for failure to report. I am correcting the record.

"

The timer hit *00:59:10.*

The Authority's interface shifted. The purple hue flickered, trying to decide which rule to apply.

**NOTICE:** Subject is attempting to utilize Class I reporting protocols to bypass Class III engagement restrictions. **ANALYSIS:** Protocol 1.4 (Mandatory Reporting) conflicts with Protocol 3.2 (Engagement Restriction). **RESOLUTION:** Awaiting manual adjudication.

Dale looked up.

"Manual adjudication? That takes time. The timer is running."

The figure's jaw unhinged further. A black void opened in its mouth.

"The timer is for *your* survival, Mr.

Kowalski. The adjudication is for *my* penalty. If you die before I am penalized, you cease to exist.

If you are penalized, you are erased.

Either way, the problem is solved.

"

It lunged.

Dale didn't dodge. He couldn't. He was too old, too slow, and too tired.

But he didn't move away. He moved *in*.

He grabbed the figure's ankle. Not to fight. To tag it.

His fingers closed around the cold, hard substance that made up Miller's shoe. It felt like polished stone.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** Physical contact initiated with Class III Entity. **WARNING:** Cross-contamination risk. **ACTION:** Field Guide entry *Rattus profundus* corrupted. **PENALTY:** -40% Effectiveness on all next three protocols.

The penalty hit Dale like a physical blow.

His vision blurred. His knees buckled. The world tilted sideways.

He felt sick, a sudden wave of nausea that had nothing to do with fear and everything to do with the system punishing his mistake.

But he had his hand on the ankle.

And he had a label.

Dale ripped a strip of masking tape from his belt. He had been using it to mark entry points in the walls earlier. It was sticky. It was mundane.

He slapped the tape onto the figure's ankle.

"Labeling complete," Dale wheezed.

The figure froze.

It looked down at its ankle. It looked at the tape. It looked at Dale.

"That is not a valid identification tag," the figure said. Its voice was tight now. Less amused.

"It's a field marker," Dale said.

He was breathing hard, sweat dripping from his forehead.

"Standard OSHA protocol for hazardous materials. If you can't identify the hazard, you isolate it.

You tag it. You quarantine it.

"

The Authority's text flashed.

**FIELD TAG DETECTED.** **SOURCE: UNLICENSED.** **VALIDITY: UNKNOWN.** **REVIEWING...**

The figure tried to lift its leg. The tape held.

It wasn't magical.

It wasn't reinforced. But the Authority's logic was binary. If the tag was applied, and the tag was applied by a registered technician (even if unlicensed for this class), the system had to acknowledge the tag.

If the tag was acknowledged, the entity was no longer

"unidentified."

It was "Identified but Unprocessed."

And an "Identified but Unprocessed" entity was a backlog item.

The figure's eyes widened.

It realized what Dale had done. He hadn't attacked it.

He hadn't tried to kill it, and he had turned it into paperwork.

"You cannot process me," the figure hissed. "I am the auditor."

"You're a citation," Dale corrected. "You're a pending audit. And you're currently blocking a service area."

The timer hit *00:59:00.*

A new notification popped up. Not in red. In green.

**NOTICE:** Local Authority Node 4227 (Suburban Ohio) has detected a critical backlog of Class III entities. **ALERT:** Emergency Triage Protocol activated. **CONDITION:** Any technician with Certification Level I or higher may temporarily assume Class III Processing duties if the primary auditor is compromised. **STATUS:** Auditor Compromised (Field Tagged). **ELIGIBILITY CHECK:** Dale Kowalski.

The green text hung in the air, glowing brighter than the red.

Dale stared at it. He hadn't leveled up. He hadn't earned points. He hadn't fought.

He had just filled out a form.

But the system loved forms. It loved categorization.

It loved order.

And right now, the order was broken, and the auditor was tagged. The audit was stalled.

The system needed someone to clear the jam.

Dale was the only person in the room who knew how to clear a jam.

He looked at Sarah. She was looking at him, eyes wide, tears cutting tracks through the dust on her cheeks.

"Stay down," Dale said.

He turned back to the figure, and the figure was shrinking. It was losing cohesion. The face of Miller was melting away, the features sliding off like wax under a heat gun. The tall, thin body was unraveling, the edges of its silhouette fraying into smoke.

It was being processed.

Not killed. *Processed.*

The Authority was taking over. The entity was being stripped of its agency, reduced to data, filed into a queue.

**PROCESSING...** **DATA EXTRACTION...** **SOURCE: ENTITY 77-ALPHA (CLASS III).** **WEAKNESS IDENTIFIED: Bureaucratic Inertia.**

A new line appeared in Dale's Field Guide. It wasn't the *Rattus profundus* entry. It was a new one.

**Entity: Auditor Construct (Generic).** **Weakness: Can be stalled by procedural non-compliance.** **Damage Bonus: None. Utility: High.**

Dale felt a strange sensation in his chest. It wasn't power. It was satisfaction. A deep, professional pride.

He had identified the weakness. He had used the system's own rules against it.

But the timer was still running.

*00:58:45.*

The figure was gone. In its place was a floating cube of gray light, pulsing slowly. It was the entity, compressed. Filed.

Dale reached out. He didn't touch it. He just looked at it.

**CITATION RESOLVED.** **VIOLATION: FAILURE TO REPORT SECONDARY INFESTATION.** **STATUS: CLOSED.** **POINTS AWARDED: 50.**

Fifty points. A massive amount. But it didn't feel like a reward. It felt like a bill.

The Authority's interface shifted again.

**CERTIFICATION LEVEL:** I. **POINTS TO NEXT LEVEL:** 450 / 1000. **NEW RANK:** Junior Intern.

Dale blinked. *Junior Intern?*

He had just saved his family from a Class III horror. He had outmaneuvered an auditor. And the Authority had demoted his title from *Certified Technician* to *Junior Intern?*

He looked at the fine print.

**NOTICE:** Due to unauthorized use of Class III protocols (Physical Contact/Tagging), Technician Dale Kowalski is placed on Probation. **PROBATION STATUS:** Active. **RESTRICTION:** Cannot engage Class II or higher entities without direct supervision. **SUPERVISOR:** PENDING ASSIGNMENT.

Dale's blood ran cold, and he couldn't fight anymore. Not without a supervisor. And he didn't know who his supervisor was.

The green light faded. The room was quiet. The smell of ozone lingered, mixed with the metallic tang of blood.

Sarah stood up. Her legs were shaking. She looked at the gray cube, then at Dale.

"Dad?"

"It's over," Dale said. His voice was rough. "We need to leave. Now."

He grabbed his clipboard. The violation was gone. The page was blank.

He walked to the door. He opened it.

The hallway was empty. The neighbors were gone. The street was silent.

But as Dale stepped out onto the porch, he saw them.

A dozen figures. Standing in the yard.

They weren't monsters. They were people.

But they were wearing suits.

Gray suits. Holding clipboards.

And in front of them was a man in a black tuxedo. He held a cane. He had a monocle. And he was holding Dale's clipboard.

The man in the tuxedo smiled. It was the same smile the figure had worn.

"Mr. Kowalski," the man said. "You've been placed on probation. You require a supervisor."

Dale gripped his pen. It was still broken.

"I don't need a supervisor," Dale said. "I need a license."

The man in the tuxedo laughed. It was a dry, rustling sound.

"You have the wrong license, Mr.

Kowalski. You're a pest control technician.

But the thing in your house wasn't a pest.

It was a *debt collector*.

"

The man raised the clipboard.

"And you just signed the waiver."

Dale looked down at his hands, and he hadn't written anything on the clipboard.

But there was a signature at the bottom. In his own handwriting.

**I, Dale Kowalski, hereby waive my right to legal counsel in matters of dimensional debt.**

He hadn't written that, and he hadn't signed anything.

The man in the tuxedo stepped forward. The dozen figures in the yard stepped forward with him.

"Please come with us," the man said. "The Authority has questions about your methodology. And the Board of Examiners has some very... specific... requirements for your probationary period."

Dale looked at Sarah. She was trembling.

He looked at the man, and he looked at the clipboard.

And then, Dale Kowalski did the only thing he could do.

He turned around. He walked back into the house.

He went to the kitchen. He picked up the ash pile that used to be a doll. He put it in a Ziploc bag.

He labeled the bag: **EVIDENCE: SAMPLE 1.**

He walked back out, and he held up the bag.

"I'm bringing evidence," Dale said. "And I'm filing a motion for discovery."

The man in the tuxedo stopped. His smile faltered.

"Discovery?" the man asked.

Dale opened his Field Guide. The new entry was there. *Auditor Construct.*

*Weakness: Bureaucratic Inertia.*

"Process the sample," Dale said. "Before the timer runs out."

The man in the tuxedo looked at the bag. Then he looked at Dale.

For the first time, the man looked uncertain.

The Authority's interface flickered in Dale's peripheral vision.

**MOTION FILED.** **PENDING REVIEW.** **TIME REMAINING: 00:00:10.**
