Chapter 395: Bureaucratic Hubris

# Chapter 395

The pen tip hovered a millimeter above the paper.

Vance's breath hitched in the corner, a ragged, wet sound that did not match the sterile silence of the living room. Dale ignored it. He ignored the way the air in the room had turned cold and thick, smelling of ozone and old paper. He ignored the man in the grey suit, who stood with his clipboard extended like a shield.

Dale wrote.

*Sub-species: Auditor, Rival Class.* *Noted Weakness: Bureaucratic Hubris.* *Protocol Requirement: Full Documentation of Pre-Conflict State.*

The words burned into the Field Guide. The pages didn't flutter. They didn't tear. They just sat there, heavy with ink.

The man in the suit tilted his head. A single, sharp movement.

"You are attempting to classify me before you have assessed the threat level. That is non-compliant, Dale."

"I am assessing," Dale said. His voice was flat.

Monotone, and he kept his eyes on the page.

"You are unauthorized. You have no credential in my registry.

Therefore, you are an infestation.

"

The man smiled again. It didn't reach his eyes. His eyes were flat, black voids where light went to die.

"I am not an insect."

"Everything is an insect if you look at it long enough," Dale replied. "Especially the ones that hide in the walls."

He lifted the pen. The tip was still wet. He pressed it down again, signing his own name.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** *Field Guide Entry Updated.* *New Weakness Identified: Paradox of Authority.* *Targeted Damage Bonus: +30% against entities claiming superior jurisdiction.*

The number in Dale's peripheral vision shifted. The amber count didn't drop this time.

It rose.

Just slightly. A fraction of a fraction.

The man in the suit stopped smiling.

"You have weaponized the paperwork," the man said. His voice lost its smoothness. It became brittle, like dry leaves underfoot. "You have turned the audit against the auditor."

"I follow protocol," Dale said. He raised the hammer.

The head was cracked, the wood splintered from previous jobs, but the weight was familiar.

Heavy.

Honest. "You demanded I sign.

I am providing the documentation for the signature.

"

The man lunged.

It wasn't a fight move. It was a filing motion.

His arm shot out, the pen in his other hand darting toward Dale's chest like a spear.

He intended to mark Dale's skin with a violation code. A permanent stain.

Dale didn't dodge. He didn't retreat. He stepped into the space, closing the distance, and swung the hammer upward.

Not at the man. At the clipboard.

The impact rang out like a church bell. The clipboard shattered. Plastic shards rained down onto the carpet. The pen flew loose, skittering across the floor until it hit the baseboard.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** *Evidence Destroyed.* *Penalty: -50 Treatment Points.* *Thoroughness Rating: 8.9 (Reduced).*

The man in the suit screamed, which was not a human scream.

It was the sound of a file cabinet crashing down a staircase. The physical form of the Auditor flickered.

For a second, he was a man in a suit.

Then he was a swarm of grey moths. Then back to the man.

"You cannot destroy the record!" the man shrieked. His suit was tearing at the seams, revealing not skin, but shifting geometric shapes underneath. "The record must be kept!"

"The record is in my head," Dale said. He slammed the hammer down again, this time hitting the man's shoulder.

The impact was solid.

The man crumpled, falling to one knee.

The grey fabric of his suit was now stained with something black and viscous. Ink.

"Your file is incomplete because you are missing a vital component," Dale said. He reached into his belt and pulled out a small, sealed evidence bag. Standard municipal issue.

Clear plastic.

"You lack a chain of custody."

The man looked up. His face was sliding. The nose was detaching, sliding sideways.

"What are you doing?"

"Dale?" Vance whispered. The man was huddled behind the sofa, his face white. "Dale, what is that thing?"

Dale didn't answer Vance. He opened the evidence bag.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** *New Protocol Unlocked: Containment (Class III).* *Requirement: Physical Enclosure of Hostile Entity.* *Cost: 100 Treatment Points.*

Dale didn't have 100 points to spare. He had just lost fifty. He checked the count. 1,402,880. Plenty.

He grabbed the man by the collar of his suit. The fabric was hot. Burning. It smelled like burning hair and formaldehyde.

"Sign," Dale commanded.

"I cannot!" The man thrashed. "The form is lost!"

"Then you are undocumented."

Dale kicked the man's legs out from under him.

The Auditor fell flat on his back. Dale pinned him with one knee, pressing the evidence bag over the man's face. The plastic hissed.

The man's hands clawed at Dale's arm, but the strength was fading.

The geometries beneath the suit were unraveling.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** *Contamination Detected.* *Warning: Cross-Contamination Penalty Imminent.* *Entity Type: Auditor. Protocol Type: Vermin. Mismatch Detected.*

Dale froze.

The rule flashed in his mind. *Cross-contamination penalty: deploying a protocol designed for one creature type against another inflicts a 40% debuff and contaminates your Field Guide entry for 24 hours.*

He had used the Vermin Containment protocol on an Auditor. He had treated a bureaucratic entity like a cockroach.

The amber eye flared red.

**PENALTY APPLIED:** *All Treatment Points gained in this encounter are voided.* *Field Guide Page 44 Corrupted.* *Debuff: Strength -40% for 1 hour.*

Dale's arms went limp.

The weight of the man was unbearable. The man in the suit smiled through the plastic bag. His teeth were sharp.

Too sharp.

"Ah," the man said. Muffled by the plastic, but clear. "A rookie mistake. You treated the inspector like the pest."

Dale tried to lift the hammer. His arm shook. The debuff was real. His muscles felt like water.

The man shoved Dale off. He stood up, brushing ash from his suit. The suit was healing. The tears were closing, knitting together like time-lapse footage of a wound.

"You have violated the Separation Principle," the man said.

He picked up the broken clipboard, and he didn't look at it. He looked at Dale.

"Auditors do not interact with pests. We audit them. We do not exterminate them."

Dale backed away. His hand gripped the hammer handle so hard his knuckles turned white.

"I can still kill you," Dale said.

"You can try," the man said. He reached into his pocket and pulled out a new pen.

Black ink.

Sharp point. "But I have already filed the report. The Authority knows you are defective."

"Defective?"

"Unlicensed for inter-dimensional auditing," the man said.

"Your Certification Level is too low. You are a Level II Technician trying to handle a Class IX Threat.

That is not bravery, Dale.

That is negligence.

"

The man pointed the pen at Vance.

Vance whimpered.

"Leave him alone," Dale said.

"He is collateral," the man said. "By proximity. His presence in the containment zone makes him a witness. Witnesses must be documented or removed."

Dale looked at Vance. Vance was crying.

Silent, shaking tears, and he was just a homeowner.

He had a mortgage. He had a dog that barked at the mailman.

He had never seen a monster until the floor opened up.

Dale looked at the man, and the man was smiling again. The predatory, smooth smile.

"Here is the offer," the man said. "You surrender your Field Guide.

You surrender your hammer.

You sign the confession of incompetence. You become an Assistant to the Audit Division. You will never fight again.

You will file paperwork.

"

Dale's heart hammered against his ribs, and he looked at the Field Guide. The pages were still open.

Page 44 was blank now.

Corrupted. He could not read his own notes.

He looked at the hammer, and he looked at the pen.

If he surrendered, he lived. He could protect Vance.

He could stay out of the Rift.

He could go back to spraying for ants in suburban duplexes, which was safe. It was clean.

It was OSHA-compliant.

But the man was already walking toward Vance.

"Step back," Dale said.

"The protocol is clear," the man said. "Witnesses must be processed."

Dale looked at the System Alert. *Debuff: Strength -40%.*

He couldn't overpower the man. He couldn't outfight him. The man was faster, stronger, and backed by the Authority's entire hierarchy.

But Dale was a technician. And technicians knew how to fix things.

He looked at the floor. The hole was still there.

The door to the infinite hallway, and the man had come through it. Which meant the hallway was still open.

Dale didn't swing the hammer at the man. He swung it at the floor.

*Crack.*

The wood splintered. Not the floor. The floor was solid oak. He hit the baseboard near the hole.

The man paused.

"What are you doing?"

Dale hit it again. *Crack.* And again. *Crack.*

"You are destroying the structural integrity of the room," the man said. He sounded confused.

Dale hit it a fourth time. The baseboard popped loose.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** *Environmental Hazard Detected.* *Loose Baseboard.* *Code Violation: K-7 (Structural Entry Point).*

Dale smiled. It was a small, tight thing.

"You said I was a violation," Dale said.

He reached into his belt and pulled out a canister. Not spray. A sealant. Industrial-grade expanding foam used for sealing cracks around pipe penetrations.

"What are you doing?" the man asked. He took a step back.

"Sealing the entry point," Dale said.

He sprayed the foam into the hole.

The white foam expanded instantly. It bubbled and hissed, filling the gap between the wall and the doorway. The man's doorway. The connection to the infinite hallway.

"No!" the man shouted. He lunged for the hole.

Dale threw the hammer.

It wasn't an attack. It was a distraction. The hammer flew through the air, spinning, and hit the man's knee. The man buckled.

Dale sprayed more. The foam was hardening. The hallway was disappearing. The infinite dark was being plugged with a white, chemical blob.

The man screamed. It was a sound of tearing reality.

"You are blocking the Audit Path!" the man yelled. "You are obstructing an official inquiry!"

"I am correcting a code violation," Dale said.

The foam expanded further.

It pressed against the man's suit. The man clawed at it, but the foam was already setting.

It was hot.

It burned.

The man's arm went numb. He stumbled back, away from the foam. He looked at Dale. His face was twisted in rage.

"You have trapped me," the man said. "But you have trapped yourself with me."

The foam sealed the hole completely. The dark hallway was gone. Just white, hardened plastic filling the wall.

The man in the grey suit stood in the living room. Alone. Cut off from his source of power. Cut off from the Authority.

He looked at Dale.

Dale looked at Vance.

Vance was shaking. But he was alive.

The man in the suit straightened his tie. The suit was tattered, stained with black ink and white foam. But he looked calm again.

"Do you know what you have done?" the man asked.

"I fixed it," Dale said.

"No," the man said. "You isolated me. You cut me off from the Grid. You think that saves you?"

He raised his hand. The pen was still in his grip.

"It makes me desperate," the man said. "And a desperate Auditor does not follow rules."

The air in the room changed. It grew heavier. The lights flickered.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** *Local Authority Connection Severed.* *Field Guide Offline.* *System Functions Disabled.*

Dale felt the amber eye vanish from his vision. The numbers were gone. The protocols were gone.

He was just a man with a hammer.

The man in the suit smiled. It was not a predatory smile. It was a hungry one.

"Now," the man said. "Let's see if you can fight without your clipboard."

He raised the pen. The tip glowed with a pale, sickly light.

Dale gripped the hammer. His hands shook.

The debuff was still there, and he felt weak.

Small.

Vance screamed.

Dale stepped forward.

He had no points. No guide. No rules.

He had only the job.
