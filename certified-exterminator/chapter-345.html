Chapter 345: The Older

# Chapter 345

The fall lasted exactly four seconds.

Gravity in the Authority's sub-basement pulled with the consistency of industrial-grade adhesive. Dale Kowalski hit the concrete floor hard, the impact shuddering through his reinforced steel-toed boots. Dust motes danced in the harsh, flickering light of overhead fluorescent tubes that buzzed with a frequency designed to induce mild nausea.

He didn't stay down.

Knees popped. He scrambled up, brushing grit from his coveralls. The bone pen was still in his hand. It felt warm, pulsing with a faint, rhythmic heat that matched his own heartbeat.

Before him stood a woman in a high-visibility yellow jumpsuit. The fabric was stained with fluids that smelled faintly of copper and old vinegar.

A hard hat sat askew on her head, tucked behind one ear.

She held a clipboard. Not the Authority's sleek, obsidian tablets, but a battered, metal-framed clipboard with a chipped plastic cover.

"Time check," she said. Her voice was flat, devoid of inflection. "You're three minutes late. That's a write-up."

Dale wiped his palms on his thighs. He looked around. The room was vast, stretching into a fog of grey mist and towering shelves of black binders. The floor was sealed concrete, marked with yellow safety lines that faded into darkness.

"Where is the supervisor?" Dale asked. His voice sounded steady. He forced it to be steady. Panic was a contamination hazard.

She pointed a gloved finger toward a desk in the center of the room.

"There. You'll need your ID."

Dale walked toward the desk. The air grew colder with each step. Frost formed on the edges of the safety lines.

The desk was empty.

On the surface sat a single file folder, red. The color of a Stop Work Order.

Dale approached it. His Field Guide, previously blank after the ink-sludge incident, flickered in his peripheral vision. Text populated, scrolling rapidly.

**SYSTEM ALERT: JURISDICTIONAL SHIFT DETECTED.** **CURRENT LOCATION: INTERNAL AUDIT ZONE 4.** **ACTIVE PROTOCOL: COMPLIANCE REVIEW.**

He ignored the alert. He reached for the red folder.

"Don't touch it yet," the woman said. She was standing right behind him. He hadn't heard her move. "Protocol requires a visual inspection before handling sensitive documents."

Dale pulled his hand back. He checked his watch. The hands were spinning backward.

"What is the violation code for Unauthorized Existence?" he asked.

The woman tilted her head.

"Code 0-0. Non-Emergent. Administrative backlog."

"Administrative?" Dale laughed. It was a dry, humorless sound. "I just survived an ink-based entity trying to erase my narrative presence. That's a Class 9 existential threat."

"Not anymore," she said.

"You signed. You're an employee now.

Or a contractor.

The distinction is blurry.

"

She gestured to the desk.

"Open it. The supervisor is waiting. He hates paperwork delays."

Dale opened the folder.

Inside was not a charge sheet. It was a work order.

**TASK:** Locate and neutralize unauthorized structural modifications in Sector 7-G. **TARGET:** Recursive Infestation. **THREAT LEVEL:** Class IV (Habitat Modification). **ALLOWED PROTOCOLS:** Exterminator Class II. No lethal force authorized.

Dale blinked. He looked at the woman.

"No lethal force?"

"We're in a heritage zone," she said. "Everything here is protected by dimensional preservation laws. You can't kill the bugs. You have to relocate them."

Relocate.

The word hit Dale harder than a sledgehammer. Extermination was about killing.

Relocation was about logistics.

It required containment, transport, and release.

It required resources he didn't have.

"I don't have a containment unit," Dale said.

The woman tapped the clipboard.

"Improvisation is encouraged. Failure to improvise is grounds for termination.

Of the contract. Not your life.

Probably."

A low hum rose from the floor. The concrete beneath Dale's feet vibrated. Cracks spiderwebbed outward from the center of the room.

"Sector 7-G is adjacent to the floor," the woman said. "It's breaking through. Your job is to seal the breach and move the population.

You have fifteen minutes before the shift ends.

If you're not done, you go back in the file. And the next entry won't be a task.

It'll be a disposal order.

"

Dale looked at the cracks. From the fissures, translucent, chitinous legs emerged. They were long, segmented, and shimmered with iridescence.

Not insects. Not quite.

They looked like glass sculptures that had learned to walk.

**FIELD GUIDE UPDATE:** **SPECIES:** *Vitrea recursus* (Glass Spider). **HABITAT:** Dimensional Fractures. **WEAKNESS:** Structural Resonance. **WEAKNESS:** Sound frequencies below 20Hz can shatter exoskeletons. **NOTE:** Fragile. Handle with care.

Structural Resonance.

Dale's mind raced. He couldn't kill them. He had to move them. But how do you move a swarm of glass spiders that are actively trying to turn your world into a stained-glass window?

He looked around the room. Shelves. Black binders. The woman's clipboard.

The clipboard, which was metal. Rigid.

"Can I use the clipboard?" Dale asked.

The woman frowned.

"That's government property."

"It's the only rigid container I see. If I don't use it, I fail the task. If I fail the task, I get deleted. If I get deleted, you don't get your shift completion bonus."

The woman hesitated. She looked at the emerging spiders. One had fully breached the surface, its legs clicking against the concrete. It turned toward Dale, its face a smooth, featureless oval of glass.

"Fine," she said. "But if you break it, you buy a new one. From the company store. With interest."

Dale snatched the clipboard from her hands. He ripped the metal clip off the top. The plastic cover shattered on the floor.

He held the bare metal frame.

"Resonance," Dale muttered. "Frequency."

He needed a sound. Low, heavy, and powerful.

He looked at his boots. The steel toes.

He looked at the floor. The concrete.

He stomped, and the sound was dull. Not enough.

The Glass Spiders paused. Their leg joints clicked in unison, a high-pitched screech that made Dale's teeth ache.

They were coordinating. A hive mind. If he disrupted one, they all knew.

He needed to trap them, not scare them.

Dale scanned the room again. The shelves. The binders.

Binders were heavy. Paper was dense.

He ran to the nearest shelf. He grabbed a stack of ten black binders. They weighed a ton. He dragged them to the center of the room, near the largest crack.

"Hey!" the woman shouted. "You can't move the archives!"

"Protocol 4.1," Dale shouted back, not looking up. "Isolation of source. I need to create a physical barrier to funnel the population."

He stacked the binders in a semi-circle around the crack. A wall of bureaucracy.

The Glass Spiders approached the wall. They tested it, tapping the spines with delicate legs.

Dale backed away. He needed to herd them.

He picked up the bone pen. It was still warm.

He tapped the pen against the steel frame of the clipboard.

*Ting.*

The sound was sharp. Clear.

The spiders flinched.

*Ting.*

They retreated slightly.

*Ting. Ting.*

The rhythm became a lure. The creatures were drawn to the vibration. They crawled toward the sound, abandoning the cracks they had made.

Dale moved backward, tapping the frame, leading them toward the binder wall.

The woman watched, arms crossed. Her expression was unreadable.

Dale herded them.

Five. Ten.

Twenty. The swarm moved like a liquid wave, smooth and silent.

He led them into a corner where the binder wall blocked three sides.

He raised his foot, and he stomped on the floor.

The vibration traveled through the concrete. The Glass Spiders froze. Their legs locked.

Now.

Dale slammed the metal frame of the clipboard into the gap between the binders and the wall. He used it as a door.

The frame bent. The metal groaned.

He kicked it. Hard.

The frame snapped.

The spiders poured out through the gap, shattering against the hard concrete floor. They didn't die. They didn't dissolve. They bounced.

They were glass. They were tough.

Dale stared at the broken frame in his hands. The clipboard was ruined.

"Contamination," the woman said. "You broke the containment vessel."

Dale looked at the swarm. They were regrouping.

He looked at the bone pen, and he looked at the Field Guide.

**WARNING: CROSS-CONTAMINATION RISK. IMPROPER USE OF VIBRATORY DISRUPTION PROTOCOL AGAINST *VITREA RECURSUS* MAY CAUSE CASCADING FAILURE.**

He had used the wrong tool. He had broken the rule.

But he had a new idea, and he picked up a shard of the broken clipboard. It was jagged. Sharp.

He looked at the woman.

"Give me your pen," he said.

She didn't move.

"No."

"I need a conductor. Something organic. Something that resonates with bone."

The woman's eyes narrowed. She looked at the bone pen in Dale's hand. Then she looked at the swarm.

The spiders were moving toward her. Not Dale. Her.

She was yellow. High-visibility. A target.

Dale moved. He didn't think. He reacted.

He lunged, not at the spiders, but at the woman. He grabbed her wrist. Her skin was cold. Like marble.

"Give me your pen," he repeated.

He pulled her hand away from her chest, where a second pen was tucked into her uniform pocket.

She resisted. He was stronger. He twisted her arm.

The pen came free. It was black plastic. Cheap. Disposable.

Dale threw it at the swarm.

It bounced off a spider's leg.

Nothing happened.

The woman screamed. It wasn't a scream of pain. It was a scream of procedural error.

"You violated Chain of Custody!" she yelled.

Dale ignored her. He looked at the shards of the clipboard.

He picked up a small piece of metal, and he held it up to the light.

He looked at the Field Guide entry again.

**WEAKNESS: STRUCTURAL RESONANCE.**

Not just sound. *Material.*

The spiders were glass. Glass breaks under stress.

But glass doesn't break if you hit it with plastic.

It breaks if you hit it with something harder. Or if you vibrate it until its own structure fails.

He needed a tuning fork, and he looked at his boots again. Steel toe.

He looked at the floor. Concrete.

He looked at the bone pen.

He jammed the bone pen into the crack in the floor.

Then he hit the top of the pen with the metal shard from the clipboard.

*Thud.*

The bone pen absorbed the impact. The crack in the floor widened.

The vibration wasn't in the air. It was in the ground.

The Glass Spiders stopped, and they looked down.

They felt the tremor.

Their legs shook. Micro-tremors.

Dale hit the pen again.

*Thud.*

The spiders' exoskeletons developed hairline fractures, and he hit it harder.

*Thud.*

One spider shattered. It exploded into a cloud of glittering dust.

The others scattered, and they ran.

They ran back into the cracks in the floor.

Dale kept hitting the pen. He drove it deeper into the concrete. He created a seal. A plug.

The vibration stopped the escape. The fracture lines in the floor stabilized. The spiders retreated into the subsurface.

Dale stopped. He stood there, breathing hard.

The room was silent.

The woman rubbed her wrist. She looked at Dale.

"You used a non-approved tool," she said.

"I used a bone pen. It's in the inventory list."

"It's in the *personal* inventory list. Not the *professional* one."

"Same inventory. Different jurisdiction."

She pulled out a tablet. It glowed red.

**VIOLATION: IMPROPER TOOL USAGE.** **PENALTY: DEGRADATION OF CERTIFICATION LEVEL.**

Dale felt a cold drop in his stomach. The yellow bar of his Certification Level, usually glowing a steady green, flickered. It turned orange.

**LEVEL II: AT RISK.**

**PROGRESS: 40% -> 35%.**

He had lost progress.

"Who is the supervisor?" Dale asked. His voice was quieter now.

The woman smiled.

"He's already here."

She pointed to the ceiling.

Dale looked up.

The ceiling wasn't a ceiling. It was a face.

A giant, stone face, embedded in the architecture. Its eyes were closed. Its mouth was a fissure.

The fissure opened.

A hand reached down. Not stone. Ink.

The same black sludge from the paper cage.

It grabbed Dale by the collar, and he was lifted into the air. His feet dangled.

The woman watched. She didn't help. She didn't stop.

"The supervisor doesn't like improvisation," she said.

Dale kicked. He kicked at the ink hand. It was cold. Viscous.

The ink hand dragged him up, toward the open mouth of the stone face.

Inside the mouth, there was a desk.

And on the desk, a single sheet of paper.

Dale struggled. He reached for his Field Guide.

The Guide was changing.

**STATUS: DETAINED.** **NEW MECHANIC UNLOCKED: HIERARCHICAL APPEAL.**

**CONDITION: MUST IDENTIFY A SUPERVISORY VIOLATION WITHIN 10 SECONDS TO TRIGGER COUNTER-MEASURE.**

Dale looked at the woman, and she was standing by the shelf. She was holding the clipboard.

But she wasn't writing anything.

She was looking at her watch.

"Time check," she said again. "You're still late."

Dale realized it, and the supervisor was the Authority. The Authority had strict time protocols.

But the woman had detained him. She had delayed him.

She was the bottleneck.

Dale shouted. Not in fear. In accusation.

"Violation Code 4.7!" he yelled. "Obstruction of Duty!"

The woman froze.

The ink hand stopped.

The stone face's eyes opened. They were empty. Black holes.

A voice boomed from the mouth. It wasn't a sound. It was a thought pressed directly into Dale's skull.

**SPEAK.**

Dale gasped. The ink burned his throat.

"The employee," Dale gasped, pointing at the woman. "She detained me.

She violated the Speed of Service protocol, and she is the obstruction. I am the victim.

"

The ink hand tightened.

The woman's face went pale. The yellow of her jumpsuit seemed to darken.

"That is false," she said. "I was enforcing protocol."

"Which protocol?" Dale choked out.

"The protocol that says you can't use government property? I used it.

I broke it.

I paid the penalty. I lost certification progress. I took the hit.

But you? You held me.

You stopped me.

You caused the delay.

"

The stone face blinked.

The ink hand released Dale, and he fell.

He hit the concrete floor. Hard.

He rolled. He scrambled up.

The woman was gone.

The shelf where she had stood was empty.

In its place was a new file.

Not red. Not black.

White.

Dale crawled toward it. His body ached. His Certification bar was still orange. 35%.

He opened the white file.

Inside was a photograph, which was a picture of Dale.

But he wasn't in Ohio. He wasn't in the basement.

He was in a field. A wheat field.

And he was screaming.

On the back of the photo, written in shaky handwriting:

**YOU WERE HERE. BEFORE.**

**YOU WERE THE FIRST TO SIGN.**

Dale stared at the photo. His hands trembled.

He looked up, and the stone face was gone. The ceiling was normal.

The door to the elevator was open.

The elevator was waiting.

And inside the elevator, standing in the corner, was a man in a grey suit.

The man looked exactly like Dale.

But older.

And he was smiling.

"Welcome back, Dale," the older Dale said. "We have so much work to do."

Dale looked at the white file. Then he looked at the older man.

He stepped into the elevator.

The doors closed.
