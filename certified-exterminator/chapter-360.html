Chapter 360: Denied Second Opinion

# Chapter 360

The sewer grate did not smell like sewage. It smelled like wet drywall and old carpet.

Dale's nose twitched. He took a half-step back.

Miller was already halfway down the ladder, his flashlight beam cutting a jagged wedge through the darkness.

"Dale. It's cold down there."

The temperature drop was immediate. A draft rose from the hole, chilling the sweat on Dale's neck.

He ignored it, and he clicked his pen again. The click sounded loud in the alley. Too loud.

He pulled his clipboard against his chest. The paper was clean. Crisp. Uncontaminated.

He looked at the black void beneath the iron bars.

"Miller. Stop."

Miller froze, one hand gripping the rusted rung, the other holding the light. His face was pale in the beam's reflection.

"We don't have time for protocol, Dale. Vane said the exam starts now."

"Vane is an examiner," Dale said, his voice flat. "Not a supervisor. You don't enter a containment zone without a hazard assessment."

"Dale—"

"Look at the grate. Really look at it."

Miller sighed. It was the sigh of a man who had been dragged through too many inspections by a man who insisted on labeling the fire extinguishers. But Miller looked.

The iron bars were not rusted. They were coated in a fine, gray powder.

"Frass," Dale said.

"Frass?" Miller frowned. "Like insect waste?"

"Insect waste," Dale confirmed.

"But this isn't a beetle. The particle size is too uniform.

Too fine.

It's exoskeletal dust. Shed skin. High-volume molting.

"

He stepped closer. Not into the hole. Into the perimeter.

He pulled a small plastic evidence bag from his belt. He used the tip of his pliers to scrape a sample from the iron bar.

He sealed the bag, and he labeled it with a marker. *Sample 1A.

Origin: Sewer Interface. Suspected Agent: Unknown.*

He wrote the entry in his Field Guide.

*Species: Unidentified. Note: High-frequency shedding suggests rapid growth cycle or parasitic mimicry. Do not engage with standard biological protocols until visual confirmation is obtained.*

The System notification chimed in his peripheral vision.

*Treatment Point Awarded: 2. Action: Documentation. Efficiency: 100%.*

Two points. It wasn't a lot. But it was better than zero. And it proved the system was watching.

"If we drop in blind, we trigger a cross-contamination penalty," Dale said. "If that thing in there is a mimic, and I use a Class II Pesticide Spray, I lose my ability to treat actual pests for twenty-four hours. I can't afford that. I have a backlog."

Miller rubbed his temples.

"You're worried about your license while a dead woman is calling for help?"

"I'm worried about the structural integrity of the workspace," Dale said. "The walls in this alley are compromised. Look at the brickwork."

Miller turned. He shone his light on the adjacent building. The mortar between the bricks was gone. Replaced by a webbing of dark, viscous slime.

"It's not just a voice," Miller whispered. "It's an infestation."

"Exactly," Dale said. "And you don't spray an infestation when the nest is inside the walls. You seal the entry points first."

Dale moved to the base of the wall. He pulled a tube of industrial sealant from his tool belt. It was meant for sealing foundation cracks against termites.

He squeezed the nozzle. Thick, white paste oozed out.

He sealed the gaps in the brickwork.

Miller stared at him.

"Are you serious? We're in a magical dungeon crawl and you're caulking the wall?"

"I'm isolating the vector," Dale said.

He worked with mechanical precision. Left to right. Top to bottom.

Covering every millimeter of exposed mortar.

"If the asset—the woman—is a mimic, she uses sound to lure prey into the nest. If I seal the acoustic channels, the lure loses potency.

It forces the entity to come to me.

"

"That's... actually smart," Miller admitted.

"Don't compliment my work. It creates bias," Dale said. He finished the last gap.

He stepped back. Inspected his work.

"Better."

The voice changed.

*"Help me. It hurts."*

The tone was different.

Less giggly. More pained.

The pitch dropped. It sounded less like a child and more like the woman from Chapter 1. The tenant.

The one who had screamed.

Dale's hands tightened around the pliers, and he remembered the sound. He remembered the way the floorboards had splintered.

But he didn't react. He checked his sealant line. It was solid.

"Miller. Get to the other side of the alley. Stay behind the dumpster."

"Why?"

"Because if this is a mimic, it will try to flank me using the acoustic distortion from the open sewer. You're a distraction. I need you to be a witness."

Miller nodded. He moved quickly. His boots scuffed against the pavement.

Dale stood alone in front of the open sewer, and he raised his clipboard.

"State your identification," he called out.

His voice was steady. Procedural.

"This is a licensed inspection zone.

Unauthorized entry is prohibited.

"

Silence.

Then, a wet, sliding sound.

Something emerged from the darkness, which was not a woman.

It was a mass of pale, translucent limbs. They unfolded from the sewer hole like a spider unfolding its legs.

The body in the center was twisted, human-shaped, but wrong.

The head was elongated. The mouth was open too wide.

It crawled over the sealant.

The white paste hissed. Steam rose from the contact points.

Dale noted this. *Weakness: Chemical barrier. Standard industrial sealant acts as a corrosive agent against this species.*

He updated the Field Guide mentally.

*Species: Dimensional Mimic (Variant). Weakness: Alkaline compounds. High sensitivity to physical barriers.*

The mimic pulled itself fully onto the pavement. It stood up. It was seven feet tall. Its limbs clicked against the concrete.

The face shifted. The eyes blinked.

They became blue.

Human.

The mouth formed the words.

*"Dale. You let me die."*

Dale didn't flinch. He clicked his pen.

"I followed protocol," Dale said. "You were already inside the walls. Entry into a sealed crawlspace without PPE is a safety violation."

The mimic tilted its head. The blue eyes narrowed.

*"I was screaming."*

"I heard you," Dale said. "I documented it. Report Code K-7.

Structural failure. I tried to help, but the door was jammed by debris.

I am licensed for extermination, not rescue.

I called 911. They were too slow.

"

This was true. He had called. He had written the report. He had done everything the law required.

The mimic screamed. A high, tearing sound that shattered the remaining streetlight bulb. Glass rained down on Dale's shoulders. He didn't brush it off.

The mimic lunged.

Dale sidestepped. He didn't run. He pivoted. He used the momentum to swing his pliers at the mimic's knee.

The metal bit into the translucent flesh. Black ichor sprayed.

The mimic shrieked. It stumbled back.

Dale did not pursue. He retreated. He moved to the center of the alley, where he had placed three buckets of industrial solvent earlier.

"Miller! Now!"

Miller threw a heavy-duty tarp over the sewer hole. It hit the ground with a thud. The tarp was weighted with cinder blocks Dale had dragged from the dumpster three minutes ago.

The mimic tried to turn back to the hole. It slipped on the sealant Dale had applied to the wall. Its legs tangled.

Dale grabbed a bucket. He threw it.

The solvent splashed onto the mimic's torso.

The creature dissolved.

Not instantly.

Slowly. The flesh bubbled.

The bones cracked and turned to powder.

The mimic fell to its knees. It clawed at the ground.

*"No... no... I'm trapped..."*

The voice was fading. The blue eyes were melting away.

Dale watched. He didn't look away. He couldn't. He had to verify.

*Verify,* the protocol said, and he walked closer. He checked the pulse.

There was no pulse.

He checked the respiratory rate.

Zero. He checked for signs of necrosis.

Immediate, and he pulled out his pen. He wrote in the Field Guide.

*Elimination confirmed. Cause of death: Chemical degradation via alkaline solvent. Secondary cause: Structural confinement.*

He took a photo with his phone. He uploaded it to the Authority's local server.

*Thoroughness Rating: 9.8.*

*Treatment Points Awarded: 150.*

*New Species Unlocked: Mimic of the Deceased (Class III).*

*Note: Do not engage without pre-application of pH 12+ sealant. Vulnerability to sound-based lures requires auditory dampening or immediate chemical neutralization.*

Miller ran over. He looked at the dissolving pile of flesh. He looked at Dale.

"You killed her," Miller said. His voice was hollow.

"I exterminated an infestation," Dale said. He cleaned his pliers with a rag.

"The host body is a vector.

The entity is the parasite. You don't mourn the vector. You remove it.

"

Miller kicked a piece of bone. It shattered.

"That was a person, Dale."

"It was a violation," Dale said. He put the pliers back in his belt. "A severe code violation."

He looked at the sewer grate. The tarp was holding. For now.

But the tarp wasn't the problem, and the tarp was a band-aid.

Dale looked at his clipboard. He had written *Next Step: Retrieve Host Asset.*

He had failed to retrieve it. He had destroyed it.

And the Authority had given him points for it, and he felt a cold weight in his stomach. Not fear. Doubt.

Had he misidentified the threat? Was the mimic actually the victim? Was the

"infestation" actually a containment breach of a soul?

The System didn't seem to care. The points were there. The rating was high.

But the voice had said *

"I remember you."*

And Dale remembered the way she had looked at him through the crack in the door. Not with fear. With accusation.

He looked at the tarp.

Underneath the cinder blocks, a hand pushed up.

Pale. Translucent.

Fingers curled over the edge of the tarp.

Miller gasped.

Dale didn't move. He watched.

The hand didn't attack. It waved. A slow, deliberate motion.

Then, a second hand emerged. It was holding something.

A clipboard.

Dale's clipboard. The one he had lost in the duplex, fourteen years ago.

He had written the code violation on it. *K-7. Structural Entry Point.*

The handwriting on the clipboard in the hand was different. It was jagged. Desperate.

*You didn't seal the door,* the note read. *You sealed the truth.*

Dale's breath hitched, and he had sealed the door. He had walked away. He had reported the violation but not fixed it. He had followed the letter of the law but not the spirit of safety.

The Authority rewarded thoroughness. But thoroughness required truth.

The hand pulled the clipboard up. The mimic's body was gone. Only the hand remained, fused to the tarp, fused to the sewer.

It wasn't dead, which was waiting.

Dale looked at the clipboard in his own hands. It was blank.

He looked at the hand.

The System notification chimed.

*Warning: Active Infestation Detected. Species: Regenerating Mimic. Status: Immune to Chemical Degradation.*

*Cross-Contamination Penalty Applied: Your Field Guide entry for Mimic of the Deceased is now corrupted.*

*New Objective: Re-Identify Threat.*

Dale's hand shook, and he had made a mistake. A professional error.

And the Authority was punishing him for it.

Miller grabbed Dale's arm.

"Dale. We have to go. That thing is coming back."

Dale stared at the clipboard in the hand, and he reached out. He grabbed the edge of the tarp. He pulled.

The hand didn't resist. It released the clipboard.

Dale caught it.

The paper was warm, and he looked at the sewer. The hole was widening. The brickwork was cracking. The sealant he had applied was dissolving.

Not from acid.

From vibration.

A low hum rose from the depths. It was not a voice. It was a frequency.

The same frequency that had opened the Rift.

Dale looked at Miller.

"It's not a mimic," Dale said. "It's a beacon."

Miller's eyes went wide.

"A beacon for what?"

Dale looked at the clipboard. The note was fading. The words were rearranging themselves.

*Vane is not the examiner,* the new note read. *Vane is the gatekeeper.*

The ground beneath them shook. The tarp tore.

The sewer grate exploded upward.

Not with water.

With light.

A beam of pure, white energy shot into the sky. It pierced the darkness. It connected the sewer to the stars.

And from the light, a figure stepped out, which was tall. It wore a suit. It held a pen.

Dale knew that face.

It was his own.

Older.

With more scars.

The other Dale looked at Miller. Then he looked at Dale.

"You didn't finish the job," the other Dale said. His voice was dry. Procedural. Deadpan.

Dale's blood froze, and he looked at the System interface.

*Certification Level: II.*

*Threat Level: Class X.*

*New Protocol Unlocked: Temporal Variance Analysis.*

The other Dale raised his pen.

"Let's review the case file."

Dale gripped his pliers. His knuckles turned white.

He had prepared for monsters. He had prepared for demons.

He had not prepared for himself.

The ground cracked again. The sewer hole expanded, swallowing the alley. The light intensified.

Miller screamed.

Dale didn't scream, and he clicked his pen.

He wrote the first line of the new report.

*Incident 360: Contamination Event.*

*Subject: Self.*

*Status: Critical.*

He looked up at the figure in the light.

"I request a second opinion," Dale said.

The other Dale smiled.

"Denied."

The light swallowed them.
