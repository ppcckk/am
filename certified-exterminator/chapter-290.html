Chapter 290: Report with Clipboard

# Chapter 290

The drop was twelve feet.

Kowalski hit the wet concrete hard, his knees absorbing the impact with a sickening crunch that vibrated up his spine. He didn't check for injury.

Injury was a variable he couldn't control, and variables slowed down containment.

He rolled onto his side, his hand immediately going to the canister on his hip. It was empty. He'd used the last of the Class-1 solvent on the wall breach above, a mistake born of panic and a lack of proper ventilation protocols.

Vane landed two feet away, coughing up sewer gas that smelled of rotten eggs and old blood.

"Up," Kowalski said. His voice was flat, stripped of the fear that should have been screaming in his throat. "Move to the left. Don't touch the walls."

"Why?" Vane wheezed, wiping slime from his forearm.

"Biofilm," Kowalski said. "If the Authority flagged me for Felony Infestation Nesting, the local environmental safeguards are probably offline. The walls are active."

Vane scrambled to his feet, keeping his hands close to his chest.

"So we're walking through a biohazard zone with no license? We're going to get fined into death."

"We're going to get certified."

Kowalski pulled his flashlight from his belt.

The beam cut through the murky darkness, revealing a tunnel that stretched further than any municipal sewer line in suburban Ohio had a right to. The concrete was stained with a iridescent, oily residue that pulsed faintly in the dark.

It wasn't sludge, which was frass.

He stopped. The air here was thick, humid, and heavy with the scent of chitin and decay.

"Field Guide update," Kowalski muttered, tapping the side of his helmet where the Authority's interface usually projected data. Nothing happened. The screen was black.

"System's down," Vane whispered, his eyes wide in the beam's halo. "We're off the grid, Dale. You can't log a kill. You can't earn points."

"I don't need points," Kowalski said, stepping forward. "I need evidence."

He walked toward the wall, not touching it, but studying the texture. The iridescence wasn't random.

It followed a spiral pattern, tightening as it moved deeper into the tunnel.

This wasn't a random breach, which was a colony structure. A nest.

"Look at the density," Kowalski said, pointing his light at a cluster of nodules protruding from the brickwork. "High concentration of pheromone markers. They're not just hiding here. They're farming it."

"Farming what?"

"Us," Kowalski said. "Or rather, the energy signature of our fear. That's why the Authority revoked my license.

I didn't just contain the broodmother above. I trapped a beacon.

"

Vane looked pale.

"Beacon for what?"

For an answer, the tunnel shook.

It wasn't a tremor. It was a rhythm. A low, wet thumping that resonated in Kowalski's teeth. Something massive was moving in the darkness ahead, dragging itself along the floor of the main sewer artery.

Kowalski checked his watch. The second hand had stopped. The Authority's timekeeping was gone. He relied on his own internal clock, calibrated by fourteen years of waiting for tenants to sign inspection forms.

"Three minutes," he said.

"Three minutes until what?"

"Until the patrol cycle resets. If we stay here, we get hit. If we move forward, we hit the source."

Vane grabbed his arm.

"Dale, listen to me. That thing in the bag back there? It broke open.

Whatever was in it is out.

And we just dropped down on top of it.

"

Kowalski pulled his arm free.

"Then we are already in the infestation zone. Protocol dictates we identify the vector before we engage."

"There is no protocol!" Vane shouted, his voice echoing down the tunnel. "You're a criminal, Dale! You're a rogue! The Authority won't help you!"

"The Authority doesn't help anyone," Kowalski said, his voice dropping to a whisper.

"It observes. It rates. It judges.

And right now, I'm about to give it something worth rating.

"

He walked forward, his boots squelching in the slime. The thumping grew louder, closer. The air grew hotter.

Ahead, the tunnel split. Two branches led away into darkness. The main path continued straight, wider and darker.

Kowalski stopped at the junction. He pulled a small, yellow tag from his vest—a blank entry tag, useless without a logged ID. He stuck it to the left branch. Then he stuck another to the right.

"What are you doing?" Vane asked.

"Marking exclusion zones," Kowalski said.

"If we go down the middle, we assume the threat is in the middle.

That's a heuristic error. The broodmother above was territorial.

It didn't flee. It waited. That means the thing below is an anchor.

Anchors don't move. They wait for food to come to them.

"

"So?"

"So we don't go to the food. We go to the plumbing."

Kowalski turned and kicked a rusted valve wheel on the wall. It didn't budge. He kicked it again, harder. The metal groaned, but held.

"Vane. Hold the light on the hinge."

Vane hesitated, then obeyed, his hands shaking so badly the beam jittered across the concrete.

Kowalski produced a multi-tool from his belt, and he didn't use a screwdriver. He used the pry bar end, jamming it into the gap between the valve housing and the pipe. He twisted.

The metal shrieked.

"Come on," Kowalski gritted out, sweat beading on his forehead. "Old infrastructure. Poor maintenance. Just like the duplex."

The valve gave way with a snap.

A rush of air hissed out, carrying a smell distinct from the sewer gas. It smelled of ozone and copper.

"Ventilation," Kowalski said. "They're using the exhaust shafts to regulate temperature. That means there's a heat source nearby. A nest."

He looked at the main tunnel. The thumping was closer now. Visible.

A shape emerged from the darkness. It was low to the ground, a mass of segmented plates and writhing legs. It was the size of a minivan.

Vane screamed.

Kowalski didn't scream. He stared at it. His eyes narrowed.

"Is that a Class-3?" Vane yelled, backing away.

"No," Kowalski said. "It's a Class-2. But it's wrong."

He stepped closer, ignoring the creature's hissing mandibles. The thing lunged, snapping at his boots. Kowalski sidestepped with the efficiency of a man who had dodged angry dogs and drunk tenants for a decade.

"It's molting," Kowalski said, pointing at the soft, translucent skin hanging off the creature's rear section. "It's not attacking. It's protecting its shell."

The creature shrieked, a sound like grinding metal. It scrambled backward, dragging its soft underbelly across the wet concrete.

"Kill it!" Vane shouted, raising his makeshift spear.

"Don't," Kowalski said sharply. "If you kill it while it's vulnerable, you miss the weak point. You'll waste your energy. And since we have no System support, we have no stamina regen."

"Then what do we do?"

"We wait," Kowalski said. He checked his watch again. The second hand still hadn't moved. He counted to sixty in his head.

The creature stopped moving. It curled into a ball, its soft skin pulsing.

"Sixty seconds," Kowalski said. "That's how long it takes for the chitin to harden enough to support the creature's weight. It can't move fast. It can't attack."

"It's a monster, Dale! It's trying to eat us!"

"It's trying to survive," Kowalski corrected. "Just like the rats in the crawlspace.

It's in the vulnerable phase of its life cycle.

We document the phase. We verify the species. We eliminate it when it is compliant with the protocol.

"

"Compliant?" Vane laughed, a hysterical, broken sound. "It's a demon!"

"It's a pest," Kowalski said. "And pests follow rules."

The creature's skin hardened. It unfolded, shaking itself off.

It looked larger now, more solid.

But it was slower.

Its movements were stiff, calculating.

Kowalski raised his empty spray can. It was useless. He raised his clipboard. It was empty.

"Vane," Kowalski said calmly. "Look at the grate above us. The one we came down through."

Vane looked up. The grate was rusted, hanging by a single hinge.

"What about it?"

"The Authority flagged me for Felony Nesting. That means they consider the nest active. But a nest requires a queen. Or a primary unit."

Kowalski pointed at the creature.

"That's not the queen. That's a worker.

A drone. It's carrying waste.

"

He pointed at the soft, discarded skin on the ground. It wasn't just skin. It was a pile of refuse. Bones, shells, the remnants of other creatures.

"It's a scavenger," Kowalski said. "And scavengers follow trails."

The creature looked at them. Its multiple eyes blinked, independent and unblinking. It took a step forward.

"Run," Kowalski said.

"Where?"

"Away from the nest. Toward the main artery."

"Why?"

"Because the drone will follow us. It thinks we're part of the waste stream. And where the drone goes, the Authority will track."

Vane stared at him.

"You're using the monster as a GPS?"

"I'm using professional methodology," Kowalski said.

"We don't hunt the monster. We let the monster lead us to the nest. We document the trail.

We verify the source.

"

He turned and ran into the darkness, away from the creature.

Behind him, the drone let out a low hiss, then followed, its legs clicking rhythmically against the wet floor.

Vane hesitated for a heartbeat, then ran after Kowalski, and they ran for what felt like an hour, though the air grew hotter and the tunnel grew larger. The drone stayed behind them, a constant, clicking presence.

Finally, they reached a large chamber. The ceiling vaulted high above them, lost in shadow. In the center of the chamber stood a structure that made Kowalski's stomach turn.

It was a throne of fused bones and black chitin. And on it sat a figure.

It was humanoid, but elongated. Its skin was gray and slick. It had no face, only a smooth, oval surface that reflected Kowalski's flashlight beam.

The Authority's interface flickered to life in Kowalski's vision.

**ALERT: HIGH-VALUE TARGET DETECTED.** **SPECIES: Rift Architect (Juvenile).** **STATUS: Unlicensed.** **PENALTY FOR ENGAGEMENT: Immediate Execution.**

Kowalski stopped. His breath caught in his throat.

"Vane," he whispered. "Get behind the pillar."

"What is that?" Vane asked, his voice trembling.

"It's not a monster," Kowalski said, his mind racing, calculating the odds. "It's a bureaucrat."

The figure on the throne turned its faceless head toward them. It didn't speak. But the air around them grew heavy, pressing down on their shoulders.

Kowalski looked at the drone behind them, which had stopped at the entrance to the chamber, chittering nervously.

He looked at the empty spray can, and he looked at the clipboard.

And then he did something no exterminator had ever done.

He stepped forward, not with a weapon, but with a gesture. He raised his hand, palm open, in the universal sign for *pause*.

"I am filing an appeal," Kowalski said.

The Rift Architect tilted its head.

The silence that followed was absolute.

Then, the Architect raised one clawed hand.

It pointed at Kowalski.

And for the first time since the Rift opened, the Authority's interface displayed a new option.

**APPEAL ACCEPTED.** **TRIAL BY COMBAT INITIATED.** **WINNER TAKES ALL.**

Kowalski felt a cold spike of adrenaline in his chest. He had no license. He had no protocols. He had no support.

But he had a clipboard.

And he had a job to do, and he looked at Vane, who was frozen behind the pillar, eyes wide with terror.

"Stay here," Kowalski said. "I'm going to write the report."
