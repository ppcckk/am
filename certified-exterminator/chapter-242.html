Chapter 242: Filing Against the Authority

# Chapter 242

The stylus hovered over the clipboard, trembling.

Dale Kowalski did not tremble. His hands were steady. They had been steady while he'd crawled through a crawlspace infested with acid beetles. They had been steady while he'd sealed a structural breach against a broodmother that could liquefy concrete.

This was different.

The voice from the sky was not screaming. It was negotiating.

*"DEAL TERMS ACCEPTED,"* the voice boomed again. The sound vibrated in Dale's molars. It sounded like wet gravel grinding against glass.

Vance grabbed his wrist. Her fingers dug into the skin of his forearm, hard enough to leave marks.

"Stop writing," she hissed. "You can't sign a contract with a Class IX entity without a counter-signature from the Regional Director. It's a violation of Protocol 44-B. Immediate termination of your license."

Dale looked down at the page. The ink was drying too fast. The Authority's interface didn't wait for human hesitation. It demanded input.

"I'm not signing," Dale said. His voice was flat. OSHA-compliant. "I'm filing a dispute."

"Against whom?" Vance asked. "The sky?"

"Against the client," Dale said. He pointed the stylus at the open mouth in the clouds. "If they're a Client, they're a property owner. And if they're a property owner, they're liable for nuisance violations."

The mouth in the sky paused. The insect-leg grinding noise ceased. The silence that followed was heavier than the noise had been.

Below them, the team of exterminators had stopped collecting shards. They stared up, faces pale, hands hovering over their kits. One of them, a young technician named Miller, dropped a containment bag. It hit the asphalt with a wet slap.

Dale didn't look at Miller. He looked at his Field Guide.

The interface blinked. A new notification pulsed in his peripheral vision.

**UNLICENSED CLIENT DETECTED.** **SUBJECT: THE HOLLOW MOUTH.** **STATUS: CONTRACT PENDING.** **PENALTY FOR NON-COMPLIANCE: LICENSE REVOCATION.**

Dale clicked the stylus again. This time, he didn't write a signature. He wrote a citation.

*Violation: Unauthorized Presence.* *Code: Z-19 (Zoning Non-Compliance).* *Evidence: Entity exceeds local dimensional capacity. No permit on file.*

He held the clipboard up. The paper was blank white against the bruised purple of the sky.

"I'm placing a stop-work order," Dale shouted. His voice cracked only slightly.

He cleared his throat and tried again.

"By authority of the Municipal Licensing Board, you are in violation of local containment statutes. Cease expansion immediately."

Vance stared at him. Her face was a mask of professional horror.

"You're joking."

"I'm documenting," Dale said.

The sky shifted.

The clouds swirled inward, forming a vortex. The mouth narrowed, then widened.

A tongue—if it could be called a tongue—slithered out of the clouds, which was made of shadow and teeth, thousands of them, rotating in concentric circles.

It didn't attack. It descended.

It hit the ground three hundred yards away. The impact didn't cause an explosion.

It caused a ripple.

The asphalt buckled.

Car alarms screamed in unison. The air pressure dropped so that Dale's ears popped.

**INFESTATION LEVEL UP:** **CLASS IX: ABERRANT CLIENT.** **NEW PROTOCOL UNLOCKED: JURISDICTIONAL ENFORCEMENT.**

Dale felt the new ability slot into his toolkit. It wasn't a weapon. It was a bureaucratic override.

He could now issue fines that manifested as physical barriers. He could subpoena entities into stasis fields.

But he had no jurisdiction. Not yet.

"Kowalski," Vance said, her voice tight.

"We need to move. If that thing decides you're not a valid contractor, it will eat your soul. Literally.

The Authority's fine print covers 'ontological digestion.'

"

"Then we file an appeal," Dale said. He turned to his team.

"Miller!

Get the perimeter tape. Red and white. Standard hazard perimeter.

"

Miller scrambled to obey, his hands shaking as he pulled a roll of caution tape from his belt.

"Sarah! Get the noise-canceling headsets. If the client speaks again, we need to record the audio for the transcript. Evidence matters."

Sarah, a senior tech with a scar across her left eye, nodded and moved quickly. She was good at documentation. She was the reason Dale had survived the Broodmother Incident in Chapter 12.

"Vance," Dale said, not looking at her. "Get the Authority's emergency contact.

Not the local office.

The Regional Director.

Now."

"They're on a holiday," Vance said.

"Then wake them up. Tell them I'm citing a god for zoning violations."

Vance blinked. Then she pulled out her communicator. Her fingers flew across the screen.

The entity—the Hollow Mouth—took another step closer. The ground beneath its foot dissolved into gray sludge. The sludge smelled like old pennies and rotting fruit.

Dale stepped forward.

"Stay back," Vance warned.

"No," Dale said. "If I stay back, it thinks I'm fleeing. Fleeing implies guilt. Guilt implies admission of liability."

He walked toward the sludge line. His boots squelched in the wet debris.

He stopped at the edge. He pulled out a spray bottle from his kit. It wasn't chemical.

It was a marker. Neon orange paint.

He sprayed a circle on the ground in front of the entity.

"Boundary set," Dale said. "Inside this circle is your property. Outside is municipal land. You are trespassing."

The entity tilted its head. The teeth in its mouth stopped rotating.

A sound came out. Low. Resonant.

*"YOU... CLAIM... SPACE?"*

"I claim jurisdiction," Dale said. He clicked his stylus. **Protocol: Jurisdictional Enforcement. Target: Hollow Mouth. Action: Containment.**

Nothing happened.

The Authority's interface flashed red.

**ERROR: INSUFFICIENT CERTIFICATION LEVEL.** **CURRENT LEVEL: VII.** **REQUIRED LEVEL: VIII FOR CONTRACTUAL CONFLICT.**

Dale frowned. "Damn it."

"Level VIII?" Vance yelled from behind. "You're stuck at VII? That's been your status for six months!"

"The practical exam is next week!" Dale shouted back. "I haven't passed the advanced negotiation module!"

The entity lunged.

Not with speed, but with weight. It swept one of its shadow-limbs across the ground. The sludge surged forward, coating the caution tape Sarah had laid down.

The tape dissolved instantly.

Miller screamed.

"It's eating the tape!"

"It's eating the boundary," Dale said. He dropped his clipboard. He reached into his kit and pulled out a flare gun.

Standard procedure said you never used incendiaries on Class IX entities. They absorbed heat. They converted thermal energy into mass.

But Dale wasn't trying to burn it. He was trying to blind it.

He fired the flare.

The magnesium burst lit up the street. Blinding, white light.

The Hollow Mouth recoiled. The teeth stopped. The grinding noise spiked in pitch.

**STATUS EFFECT: TEMPORARY DISORIENTATION.** **DURATION: 15 SECONDS.**

"Use the guide!" Dale yelled. "Look up! Entry 404-B! *Void-Whisperers*! Weakness: Acoustic feedback loops!"

Vance looked at her Field Guide. She was slow. She was good, but she wasn't Dale.

"I don't have the entry unlocked!" she said.

Dale pulled out his own stylus. He couldn't fight it. He couldn't kill it. But he could document it.

*Identify.* The Hollow Mouth was a parasitic contract. It fed on unresolved legal disputes.

*Document.* He wrote the entry into his guide. The ink glowed gold.

*Isolate.* He pointed to the flare's light.

"Light disrupts its visual processing. Keep it lit!"

*Eliminate.* He couldn't kill it. He had to void the contract.

*Verify.*

"Vance!" Dale shouted. "Read the terms! Look at the fine print!"

Vance looked at the sky. The Authority's interface overlayed text on the entity.

*CLAUSE 7: TERMINATION FOR CAUSE.*

"What cause?" Vance asked.

"Contamination!" Dale said. "Check the ground! Look at the sludge!"

Vance looked. The gray sludge was bubbling. It was mixing with the road tar.

"It's cross-contaminating!" Vance said. "The Authority hates cross-contamination!"

Dale grinned. It was a thin, dangerous grin.

"The system punishes sloppy mixing," Dale said. "If the Client mixes with local infrastructure, the contract is void due to unsanitary conditions."

He kicked the flare. It rolled into the sludge. The magnesium sizzled. The sludge turned black.

**VIOLATION DETECTED:** **CROSS-CONTAMINATION OF MUNICIPAL ASSETS.** **CONTRACT VOIDED.**

The Hollow Mouth shrieked. It was a sound that made Dale's teeth ache.

The entity unravelled.

The shadow-limbs dissolved. The teeth fell out, turning into dust.

It didn't die. It was *deported*.

The sky cleared. The mouth vanished. The clouds returned to normal suburban gray.

Silence returned to the street.

Dale stood in the sludge. His boots were ruined. His clipboard was in a puddle.

He picked it up. The pages were wet, but the ink had held.

"Good work," he said.

Vance rushed to his side. She grabbed his arm again. Her grip was softer this time.

"You just voided a contract with a deity," she said.

"I cited a health code violation," Dale corrected. "Same thing."

Miller was crying. Actually crying. He was sitting on the curb, rocking back and forth.

"Miller," Dale said. "Stand up."

"I... I can't," Miller whispered.

"My license.

Did I do it right?

Did I document the tape?

"

Dale walked over to him. He looked at Miller's Field Guide.

**ENTRY UNLOCKED:** **VOID-WHISPERER (HOLLOW MOUTH).** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 2/10.**

"Sloppy," Dale said. "You didn't isolate the perimeter. You let it breach."

"I was scared!" Miller yelled.

"Fear is not a protocol," Dale said. He reached into his kit and pulled out a new roll of tape. "Go to the end of the block.

Tape off the intersection. Mark the zone as 'Under Review.' If anyone crosses it, write down their name.

You get points for witness statements.

"

Miller stared at him.

"You're serious?"

"The Authority rewards thoroughness, Miller. Even when you're terrified. Especially when you're terrified. It proves you're following procedure instead of instinct."

Miller stood up. He wiped his face. He took the tape.

Vance looked at Dale.

"You know what you did? You triggered a Class IX expulsion event. The Authority's oversight committee is going to notice."

"Let them notice," Dale said. "I want a meeting."

"A meeting with whom?"

"With the people who set up this system." Dale looked at his Field Guide. The entry for the Hollow Mouth was there. It was complete.

No gaps.

No errors.

**TREATMENT POINTS AWARDED:** **500.** **CERTIFICATION PROGRESS: 88%.**

He was close to Level VIII.

But he had a problem.

The sky wasn't clear.

High above, past the clouds, past the Rift's usual distortion, something else was watching.

It wasn't a mouth. It wasn't a hand.

It was an eye.

A single, massive eye, blinking in the upper atmosphere.

Dale's interface flashed.

**NEW NOTIFICATION:** **SUBJECT: OBSERVER CLASS.** **STATUS: PASSIVE MONITORING.** **ACTION REQUIRED: NONE.**

"None?" Vance asked. "It's just watching?"

"It's auditing," Dale said. He felt a cold knot in his stomach. Auditors didn't care about the contract. They cared about the paperwork.

"If the Observer sees a discrepancy in your logs," Dale said, "it doesn't kill you. It erases you. From the record. From existence."

Vance went pale.

"We erased the contract. Did we log the erasure?"

Dale looked at his clipboard. The page where he'd written the citation was wet. The ink had smeared.

He couldn't read his own writing.

"Damn it," Dale said.

The eye in the sky blinked again.

A new text box appeared in Dale's vision.

**AUDIT COMMENCED.** **DOCUMENTATION MISSING.** **DEADLINE: 10 MINUTES.** **PENALTY: TOTAL ERASURE.**

Dale looked at Vance.

"We need a backup copy," she said.

"I don't have one."

"Find one."

"I have the original."

"The original is unreadable."

Dale looked at the sludge. He looked at the destroyed tape. He looked at Miller, who was taping off the intersection with shaking hands.

He had ten minutes to reconstruct the entire extermination report from memory.

And he had to do it before the Observer blinked again.

"Sarah," Dale said. "Get my backup stylus. The one with the carbon-copy feature."

"I broke it," Sarah said. "In the car."

"Go get it."

Sarah ran.

Dale stood in the middle of the street. The sludge seeped into his socks.

He closed his eyes, and he spoke.

"Violation Code K-7," he said.

His voice was steady.

"Structural entry point. Observed at 14:02 hours.

Entity identified as Hollow Mouth...

"

He recited it all. Every second. Every step. Every citation.

He spoke into the air, hoping the Authority was listening.

Hoping the Observer was recording.

Hoping that his memory was as sharp as his spray nozzle.

The eye in the sky did not blink.

But the clock on his interface ticked down.

*09:58.*

*09:57.*

Dale kept talking. He didn't stop for breath. He didn't stop for doubt.

He knew the code. He knew the penalty. He knew the cost.

And he knew that if he stopped talking, he would cease to exist.

He looked up at the eye.

"I am filing a complaint," Dale said. "Against the Authority."

The eye widened.

Dale smiled. It was a cold, professional smile.

"Because you didn't give me the right tools."
