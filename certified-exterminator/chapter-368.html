Chapter 368: Run or Get Certified

# Chapter 368

The perimeter fence rattled in a rhythm that had nothing to do with the wind. It was a low, wet thrum, the sound of wet meat pressing against chain-link until the metal groaned in submission.

Officer Miller adjusted his grip on the rifle, his knuckles white inside the tactical gloves. He stared through the cracked windshield of the squad car, watching the figure in the hazmat suit walking away from the containment zone. The figure moved with a strange, deliberate gait, like a man carrying an invisible tray of eggs.

"Target is moving toward the secondary breach," Miller said into his radio. Static hissed back.

The figure stopped. It didn't turn around. It just stood there, a silhouette against the swirling purple mist of the Rift.

"Command, do you copy?" Miller shouted. He wiped sweat from his eyes.

The air smelled of ozone and rotting lilacs.

"The suspect is stationary. Requesting confirmation of engagement rules."

No answer. The radio dead air was louder than the screaming outside.

Miller looked at his partner, Detective Halloway.

Halloway was pale, his eyes wide and fixed on the figure. Halloway had been a cop for twenty years.

He had seen bodies, and he had seen blood. But he had never seen a man in a stained white jumpsuit walk calmly toward a dimensional anomaly that had just swallowed three city blocks.

"He's not surrendering," Halloway whispered.

"He's working," Miller said. The word tasted wrong in his mouth.

The figure raised a hand. Not in a threat.

Not in a wave.

Just a flat, open palm, held out to the side.

A gesture of pause. Or perhaps, a gesture of inspection.

From the mist, a shape detached itself. It was large, chitinous, and wrong. The legs moved too fast, a blur of black armor and clicking joints. It chittered, a sound like dry bones rubbing together.

The figure didn't run. The figure pulled something from its belt. A canister. A spray nozzle.

Miller raised his rifle.

"Hold fire! Do not engage!"

The figure clicked the nozzle. A cloud of amber mist erupted. It wasn't fire.

It wasn't smoke. It was a thick, viscous gas that hung in the air, heavy and slow.

The creature lunged, its mandibles snapping at the haze.

The haze didn't dissipate. It clung to the creature's shell, turning the black armor a sickly, sticky orange.

The creature shrieked. It fell to its knees, its legs tangling in the viscous fluid. It couldn't breathe. It couldn't move.

The figure walked past the dying monster. It didn't look at the beast. It looked at the ground. It tapped a small digital clipboard with a gloved finger.

"Target isolated," Miller heard himself say, though he hadn't spoken. The figure's voice carried through the mist, amplified by some unseen mechanic. "Status: Neutralized. Documentation pending."

Halloway lowered his rifle.

"Did he just... exterminate it?"

Miller stared. The figure was already moving on, stepping over the twitching carcass without looking down. The Authority's notification, visible to everyone with a neural link, flashed in the corner of Miller's vision.

*UNLICENSED ASSET DETECTED.* *THREAT LEVEL: CLASS III.* *ACTION: OBSERVE.*

Miller felt a cold knot form in his stomach. He knew the regulations. He knew that unlicensed engagement with Class III entities resulted in immediate memory scrubbing. Or worse.

"Pull us back," Halloway said.

"No." Miller watched the figure disappear into the purple fog. "We follow. At a distance."

Dale Kowalski was not aware of the police. He was aware of the sticky residue on his left boot. It was interfering with his grip traction, a minor safety hazard that required immediate correction.

He stopped.

The vortex behind him was shrinking, the edges fraying into sparks that burned with cold blue light. Vane was gone.

Jenkins was gone, and the only thing left was the infestation.

And the smell.

It wasn't just rot. It was the scent of old copper and wet earth.

The signature of *Rattus profundus*, but mutated, and the pheromones were wrong.

They were sharp, acidic, designed to panic than attract.

Dale checked his Field Guide. The entry for the local rat population was greyed out. Cross-contamination penalty active.

He couldn't use the standard burrow-capping protocol. He had to improvise.

He looked at the ground, and the soil was cracked, fissures splitting the asphalt like dry skin. From these cracks, things were crawling. Not rats.

Not beetles.

Something with too many legs and a body that pulsed like a heart.

*Dimensional Vermin (Variant: Arthropod-Hybrid).*

The Authority's text appeared in his peripheral vision, glowing faintly red.

*IDENTIFICATION REQUIRED FOR TREATMENT BONUS.* *CURRENT STATUS: UNKNOWN.*

Dale pulled out a small glass vial from his utility belt.

He uncorked it. Inside was a clear liquid. Standard ethanol base, spiked with a mild hallucinogen derived from the broodmother's venom from Chapter 40.

It was designed to lure nesting females, but Dale had repurposed it.

He poured a drop onto the asphalt near the nearest crack.

The creature nearest the drop froze. Its antennae twitched. It turned, its segmented body rising from the fissure. It was the size of a large dog, its back plated with iridescent scales that reflected the Rift's purple light.

Dale didn't look at the creature. He looked at the crack. He needed to map the entry point. If he didn't seal the breach, the infestation would spread to the neighboring district within hours.

The creature lunged.

Dale sidestepped. It was a clumsy move, hindered by his heavy boots, but it was enough. The creature's claws scraped the pavement, sending sparks flying.

*VIOLATION: FAILURE TO MAINTAIN SAFE DISTANCE.* *WARNING: PHYSICAL HARM IMMINENT.*

The warning flashed.

Dale ignored it, and he was close enough to see the details. The creature's thorax was soft between the plates.

A gap. A vulnerability.

He pulled out his flashlight again. The beam was intense, focused.

He aimed it not at the creature's eyes, but at the gap in its armor.

The light didn't blind it. It revealed the soft tissue.

Jenkins's backup drive, which was the key. The Authority had revoked his certification, which meant his Field Guide was locked.

But the *data* was still there, stored on Jenkins's personal server.

The system punished the user, not the knowledge.

Dale had accessed the data before he left. He hadn't copied it. He had memorized the weakness.

*Target: Thoracic Ventral Gap.* *Weakness: Thermal sensitivity.*

Dale reached into his belt and pulled out a flare. Not a signal flare. A chemical flare, designed for marking entry points during inspections. It burned at 1,200 degrees.

He didn't throw it. He waited.

The creature circled, chittering. It was confused by the chemical lure. It was looking for the source of the scent, not the threat.

This was the loophole. The Authority rewarded *protocol*.

Protocol required identification.

Identification required observation.

Observation required exposure.

By letting the creature get close enough to observe the weakness, Dale was completing the *Identify* step of the extermination protocol, even if he wasn't licensed to perform the *Eliminate* step with standard tools.

The creature lunged again. This time, Dale didn't sidestep. He dropped to one knee, sliding under the creature's legs. The creature's weight shifted, off-balance.

Dale jammed the flare into the gap in the creature's armor.

The creature screamed. A high-pitched, mechanical shriek. The chemical flare ignited inside its body. The iridescent scales cracked, spewing black fluid.

Dale rolled away. The creature thrashed, burning from the inside out. It collapsed, twitching, until the light in its eyes faded.

Dale stood up. He was breathing hard. His ribs ached where Vane had hit him. But he was alive.

He pulled out his clipboard. The one he had borrowed, the one that wasn't linked to the Authority.

He wrote: *Target identified. Weakness confirmed. Elimination complete via improvised thermal injection.*

The air around him shimmered.

*SYSTEM ERROR: UNLICENSED USER.* *POINTS DENIED.* *THOROUGHNESS RATING: 0.*

Dale looked at the dead creature. Then he looked at the crack in the ground. More things were coming.

He tapped his pager. Not the Authority pager. A burner phone Jenkins had given him.

"Jenkins," Dale said. "Did you upload the Field Guide data?"

A crackle of static. Then Jenkins's voice, breathless and distant.

"Dale? Are you alive?"

"I'm alive. Did you upload it?"

"No. I encrypted it. It's on the local grid.

The Authority can't touch it unless they have root access to the municipal servers. Which they don't have.

Yet."

"Good." Dale looked at the approaching swarm. Hundreds of legs. Clicking joints.

"I need you to broadcast the weakness data to every licensed technician in the county. Not through the Authority network. Through the emergency public address system."

"Dale, that's illegal. That's inciting unauthorized violence against Class III entities."

"It's not violence," Dale said. "It's pest control. And if we don't treat the nest now, the Rift will widen.

The Authority won't send help.

They'll just quarantine the city. We have to clear the breach ourselves.

"

Jenkins was silent for a moment. Then, "If I do this, they'll come for me. And you."

"They're already coming for me," Dale said. He looked at the edge of the police perimeter. He could see the headlights now. Red and blue lights flashing in the mist.

He saw Officer Miller's squad car. He saw the rifles pointing at him.

Dale raised his clipboard. He held it up, high, so the police could see it.

"Jenkins, broadcast it now."

The air crackled. A new notification appeared, not in red, but in a confusing, glitching yellow.

*EXTERNAL DATA SOURCE DETECTED.* *FIELD GUIDE UPDATED.* *WARNING: CROSS-JURISDICTIONAL BREACH.*

Dale felt a surge of energy. It wasn't Treatment Points. It was something else. A feedback loop from the Field Guide updating itself in real-time, bypassing the Authority's certification lock.

The swarm hesitated. They could sense the change in the air. The pheromones were shifting. The weakness was no longer hidden.

Miller lowered his rifle slightly. He looked at his partner.

"What did he do?"

"I don't know," Halloway said. "But look at the things. They're stopping."

The creatures were turning. They were looking at Dale. But they weren't attacking. They were analyzing.

Dale walked toward them, and he didn't have a weapon. He had a clipboard. He had a flare.

And he had a loophole.

He walked until he was ten feet from the nearest creature. It towered over him, its head raised, its antennae brushing against his hood.

Dale spoke.

Not to the creature.

To the police. To the Authority.

To Jenkins.

"Protocol 7-Alpha," Dale said. "Isolation via chemical barrier. Initiate."

He pulled a second flare from his belt. But this time, he didn't throw it. He crushed it in his hand. The powder spilled onto the asphalt, mixing with the blood and the fluid from the dead creature.

The powder reacted. It smoked. Thick, white, choking smoke.

The creatures recoiled. The smoke disrupted their sensory input. They were blind.

Dale walked into the smoke. He walked toward the main crack, the largest fissure where the Rift's energy was leaking out.

He needed to seal it. Not with concrete. With documentation.

He pulled out a spray can. Not bug spray. Sealant foam. Industrial grade.

He sprayed the edges of the crack. The foam expanded, hardening instantly, creating a barrier.

The creatures couldn't get through. They were trapped on this side.

But the seal was temporary. It would break in an hour.

Dale needed time. He needed the police to stop shooting. He needed the Authority to stop ignoring him.

He turned to face the police. He raised his hands.

"Officer!" he shouted. "Do not fire! The target is contained!"

Miller stepped out of the car. He walked toward Dale, rifle raised.

"Step away from the hazard," Miller said. His voice was steady, but his eyes were darting around, watching the smoke, watching the creatures pounding against the sealant.

"I am containing the infestation," Dale said. "This is a public health emergency."

"You're a suspect," Miller said. "You assaulted a federal agent."

"I followed protocol," Dale said. "I isolated the threat."

Miller looked at the sealant. It was holding. The creatures were pounding against it, but the barrier was strong.

"If that breaks," Miller said, "we open fire."

"If that breaks," Dale said, "I will document your failure to maintain a safe perimeter."

Miller flinched. The threat was absurd. But it was also specific. It was procedural.

Behind them, the Rift pulsed. The purple light intensified.

And then, a new notification appeared in the air, visible to everyone. Not to Dale. To the police.

*LOCAL LICENSE REVOKED.* *GLOBAL AUDIT INITIATED.*

Miller looked at the text. He looked at Dale.

"What did you do?" Halloway asked.

Dale smiled. It was a small, tired smile.

"I didn't break the rules," Dale said. "I just made the rules visible."

The ground shook. The sealant cracked. A massive claw punched through the foam.

Dale looked at the claw. He looked at the police.

"Run," Dale said. "Or get certified."

He pulled out his flashlight and clicked it on. The beam hit the claw.

The Rift screamed.
