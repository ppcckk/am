Chapter 178: Protocol for Deletion

# Chapter 178

The ventilation shaft smelled of ozone and old dust. Not the sharp, metallic tang of a fresh rift, but the stale, recycled air of a building that had been closed for maintenance weeks.

Sarah Jenkins adjusted her respirator mask, checking the seal against her cheekbone. Her flashlight beam cut through the darkness, illuminating floating particulates that danced in the cone of light. She was a junior inspector for the Municipal Sanitation Bureau, tasked with verifying that the recent seismic tremors hadn't compromised the structural integrity of the downtown sewer mains.

It was a boring job. Until it wasn't.

Her boot clipped something metal. She looked down. A briefcase, leather-bound and scuffed, lay half-submerged in a puddle of stagnant water.

"Command, this is Unit 4," she whispered into her headset. "I've found debris in Sector 7-G. Looks like personal effects."

Static crackled. Then, a voice that sounded less like a dispatcher and more like a disappointed parent answered.

"Do not touch it.

Maintain distance. Wait for Hazmat."

Sarah frowned. Hazmat didn't come out for briefcases. She reached for her radio to ask for clarification, but the device in her hand grew hot. The display flickered, replacing the standard frequency channel with a single, bold word in bright green text:

**UNLICENSED.**

She dropped the radio. It clattered into the water, shorting out with a hiss of sparks.

Before she could step back, the shadows in the corner of the room detached themselves.

They weren't natural shadows. They moved with purpose, pooling together to form tall, rigid shapes.

Suits.

Ties. Clipboards.

They had Dale's face.

Sarah backed away, her heart hammering against her ribs. One of the shadow-Dales raised a pen. It didn't write on paper. It wrote on the air, leaving glowing red trails that hung suspended like neon signs.

*VIOLATION: INTERFERENCE.* *VIOLATION: CONTAMINATION.* *VIOLATION: EXISTENCE.*

The shadow-Dales stepped toward her. Their movements were jerky, like stop-motion animation, but their intent was clear. They were auditing her.

Sarah raised her hands, trembling. "I'm just a sanitation inspector," she stammered. "I didn't do anything."

The lead shadow-Dale tilted its head. The face was identical to the man Sarah had heard about on the news—the pest control technician who had supposedly gone missing during the reservoir breach. But the eyes were black voids, devoid of pupils.

"Compliance is mandatory," the shadow said. Its voice sounded like multiple voices speaking in unison, out of sync.

"You have observed.

You have recorded.

You are now part of the record."

Sarah turned to run, but the corridor behind her was gone. The walls had shifted, closing off her exit. The shadows closed in, their clipboards raised. The pen tips glowed with a menacing red light.

She screamed. The sound echoed, but no one came.

***

Dale coughed, spitting out a mouthful of gritty sludge. The taste was copper and rot, a flavor he associated with the inside of a dead rat's stomach. He wiped his eyes with the back of his gloved hand, smearing the black muck across his forehead.

The pipe network was a labyrinth of wet concrete and rusted iron. The water level was rising, inching up his boots. He checked his watch.

The digital display was dead, but the internal chronometer in his head ticked steadily. Four minutes since the jump.

He moved with practiced efficiency, scanning the walls for markings. Most pipes in Ohio were standardized, marked with painted codes indicating flow direction and capacity. These pipes, however, were wrong. The markings had been overwritten in red ink.

*REJECTED.*

Dale touched the word on the wall. The ink was still wet.

He didn't have the briefcase anymore. He'd lost it in the churning water, or maybe the current had taken it. Without it, he had no field notes, no evidence of the Auditor's classification, and no proof that he hadn't been terminated by the system.

But he had the pen.

It was tucked into the breast pocket of his work jacket, secured by a safety pin.

The pen was a Class-IV artifact, standard issue for Level III and above, but Dale had modified it. He'd filed down the tip to create a finer point, allowing for more precise documentation. In the wrong hands, it was a weapon.

In Dale's hands, it was a tool for compliance.

A splash echoed from the tunnel ahead.

Dale froze. He pressed his back against the curved wall, trying to make himself small. His breathing slowed, controlled by years of holding his breath in confined spaces.

The sound grew louder. Footsteps. Heavy, deliberate footsteps.

Dale pulled the pen from his pocket. He didn't draw a weapon.

He didn't ready a spell.

He checked the ink cartridge. Full. He checked the nib.

Sharp.

He waited.

A figure emerged from the darkness. It wasn't a monster. It was a man in a grey suit, holding a clipboard that glowed with faint blue light. The man's face was blurred, pixelated, as if the universe couldn't render his identity.

"Technician Kowalski," the man said. His voice was smooth, devoid of emotion. "You have failed to submit your resignation letter. Your employment status is currently 'Under Review.' This is a violation of Code 44-B: Unauthorized Existence."

Dale stepped out of the shadows.

He kept his hands visible, open palms.

"I didn't know I had to resign," Dale said. His voice was steady, though his knuckles were white around the pen.

"I was following protocol."

"Protocol requires clearance," the man said. He tapped his clipboard.

"The Authority has flagged your recent activities as 'High-Risk Deviant Behavior.' Specifically, your engagement with the Class-IX entity known as the Auditor.

You documented it without authorization. You attempted to classify it."

Dale's eyes narrowed. "I identified it. That's the job."

"Identification without classification is incomplete," the man corrected. "Incomplete work leads to contamination. Contamination spreads. You are now a vector."

The man raised the clipboard. The blue light intensified.

"I am issuing a Stop-Work Order," the man said. "Cease all actions. Surrender your tools. Submit to decontamination."

Dale looked at the pen in his hand.

He looked at the rising water. If he surrendered, he would be erased.

He had seen what happened to the other freelancers.

They didn't die. They were edited out. Their memories, their skills, their existence were scrubbed from the timeline to maintain narrative coherence.

He couldn't let that happen.

"I need to inspect this area," Dale said.

The man in the grey suit paused. "This is not an inspection zone."

"Everything is an inspection zone if you know how to look," Dale replied.

He moved fast. Not with the speed of a warrior, but with the sudden, decisive motion of a technician identifying a fault line. He lunged, not at the man, but at the wall beside him.

The wall was damp. Condensation dripped from the ceiling, pooling on the concrete. Dale slammed the tip of his pen into the wet surface.

He didn't write a word. He drew a diagram.

A simple circle. A line. A triangle.

The pen glowed gold.

The System recognized the input.

*ACTION: DOCUMENTATION INITIATED.* *SUBJECT: Structural Integrity, Sector 7-G.*

The man in the suit hesitated. "What are you doing?"

"Creating a hazard report," Dale said. He continued to draw, rapid strokes adding details to the diagram. *Cracked conduit.

Corroded valve.

Standing water.

Slip hazard.*

The gold light from the pen expanded, spreading across the wall. The drawing became a physical manifestation of the danger. The air around the diagram warped, bending light.

*WARNING: HAZARD DETECTED.* *THOROUGHNESS SCORE: 8.5.* *STATUS: ACTIVE.*

The man in the suit stepped back. "Stop! You are altering the environment!"

"I'm highlighting it," Dale said. "OSHA requires that all slip-and-fall risks be clearly marked."

The golden light spread to the floor. The standing water glowed, turning a violent yellow. The man in the suit looked down at his feet. The water rose, not naturally, but aggressively, climbing his legs.

He tried to move, but his boots were stuck. The System had locked the hazard in place.

"You're breaking the rules," the man hissed.

"I'm enforcing them," Dale said. He pointed the pen at the man's clipboard. "Your documentation is incomplete.

You didn't note the rising water level. You didn't note the lack of ventilation.

You're sloppy."

The man's face contorted. He tried to swipe the air, casting a deletion spell, but the golden barrier around Dale's diagram blocked it. The System protected the user who had successfully logged a hazard.

Dale walked forward. The man couldn't move. The water was up to his waist.

"Submit your report," Dale said.

"I... I can't," the man choked. "The Authority..."

"Then you're non-compliant," Dale said. He raised the pen. "And non-compliant entities are to be removed."

He didn't attack the man. He attacked the clipboard.

He stabbed the pen into the glowing blue surface of the clipboard.

*ACTION: CORRECTION.*

The clipboard shattered. The blue light exploded, scattering into fragments. The man in the suit screamed, not in pain, but in glitching static. His form dissolved, pixels unraveling into the dark water.

Dale stepped back, watching the entity disappear. He didn't feel triumph. He felt the familiar exhaustion of a long shift.

He retrieved the fragments of the clipboard. One piece remained intact. A single page, torn but readable.

It listed a schedule.

*Next Audit: 00:15.* *Target: Licensing Authority Headquarters, Dimension 4.* *Status: Pending.*

Dale stared at the timestamp. Fifteen minutes.

He looked at his watch again. Still dead. But he knew it was close.

He picked up the briefcase. It had washed up against the wall during the struggle. He opened it.

The Field Guide was there. But the entry for the Auditor had changed.

*Entry 177: The Auditor.* *Class: IX.* *Weakness: Structural Rigidity.* *Note: The Auditor cannot adapt to unforeseen hazards. If the environment changes faster than the audit can process, the Auditor fails.*

Dale closed the book.

He wasn't just surviving. He was finding a loophole. The Authority relied on order.

It relied on predictable patterns.

Dale had introduced chaos.

But chaos had consequences.

A new notification flashed in his peripheral vision. Red. Urgent.

*SYSTEM ALERT: LOCAL REALITY DESTABILIZING.* *CAUSE: UNAUTHORIZED EDIT.* *REMEDIATION: MANDATORY REBOOT.*

The walls shook. Cracks spiderwebbed across the concrete. The water in the pipes erupted, geysering into the air.

Dale grabbed the briefcase and ran. He didn't know where he was going. He just knew that staying in one place meant deletion.

He ran toward the nearest access hatch, kicking it open. It led to a service ladder, climbing up into darkness.

He climbed. Hand over hand, his muscles burning. Above him, the hatch creaked open.

Light flooded in. Not sunlight. Artificial light.

Dale pulled himself up onto a metal platform.

He was in a server room.

Rows of black towers hummed with power. Cables hung from the ceiling like vines. In the center of the room, a single chair sat facing a massive screen.

On the screen, a map of Ohio was displayed. Red dots pulsed like heartbeats.

And sitting in the chair, with his back to Dale, was a figure in a white lab coat.

The figure turned.

It was Dale.

But older. Thinner. With scarred hands and eyes that held the weight of a thousand audits.

"You're late," the older Dale said.

"Who are you?" Dale asked, his grip on the pen tightening.

"I'm the result," the older Dale said. He stood up. "And you're the cause."

He pointed to the screen. The red dots were merging. Forming a shape. A door.

"Open it," the older Dale said. "Or we all get erased."

Dale looked at the screen. Then at the pen in his hand.

He had a choice. Follow the protocol, or break the system.

He walked toward the chair.
