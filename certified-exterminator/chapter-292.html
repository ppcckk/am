Chapter 292: Inspection Complete

# Chapter 292

Concrete shards skittered across the exposed rebar like dry leaves.

Kowalski didn't blink. He adjusted his grip on the clipboard, using the steel clip to deflect a falling chunk of drywall that had narrowly missed his left temple. The dust in the air was thick, choking, and smelled of ozone and ancient rot.

Above them, the Architect's hand descended again. It wasn't a fist. It was an open palm, fingers splayed, pressing down on the remaining grid infrastructure with the casual indifference of a man crushing an ant farm.

"Move left!" Vane shouted, scrambling behind a collapsed support beam. "It's checking the load-bearing pillars!"

"No," Kowalski said. He kept his voice flat, barely rising above the grinding sound of stone against stone. "It's checking the perimeter seal. It's looking for an entry point it missed."

Vex stood in the center of the destruction, the stolen staff humming in his hands. The chiton armor of the staff pulsed with a sickly green light, syncing with the Authority's interface visible only to Kowalski.

Vex swung the staff. A shockwave of raw magical force erupted outward, shattering the nearest concrete block into powder.

**WARNING: UNLICENSED ENERGY EXPENDITURE.** **CROSS-CONTAMINATION RISK: 85%.** **FIELD GUIDE CORRUPTION IMMINENT.**

Kowalski ignored the warning. He stepped forward, boots crunching over rubble.

"Vex. Stop swinging."

The Inquisitor froze, chest heaving. Sweat cut tracks through the grime on his face.

"I have to hold it back."

"You're contaminating your own license," Kowalski said, pointing at the glowing runes on the staff. "Look at the decay rate. You're burning integrity points faster than you're earning combat credits. The Authority isn't going to approve this alliance if you're bleeding out your certification before the job is done."

Vex looked down at his hands. The skin was turning gray, the veins blackening. The wild magic of the Architect's staff was eating him alive.

"How do I stop it?" Vex gasped.

Kowalski pulled a small, battered notebook from his vest pocket. He flipped it open to a page covered in frantic scribbles and diagrams.

"You don't stop the energy. You redirect the liability."

Kowalski walked toward the center of the collapse zone. The Architect's hand hovered just fifty feet above them, fingers twitching, probing the broken earth.

"Stand back," Kowalski ordered.

"It's going to crush us!" Vane yelled from behind the pillar.

"It's looking for a violation," Kowalski muttered. "So I'm going to give it one."

He reached into his belt pouch and pulled out a marker. Not a magical rune-inscriber. A standard black permanent marker, the kind used for labeling janitorial carts.

He uncapped it.

The giant hand paused. The fingers hovered, sensing the sudden spike in administrative intent.

Kowalski sprinted toward the edge of the rift. The ground there was shimmering, unstable, a tear in reality that leaked black smoke. He skidded to a halt at the precipice, the heat of the void singing his eyebrows.

He dropped to one knee.

With three precise strokes, he drew a large circle on the exposed concrete lip of the rift. Inside the circle, he wrote: **TEMPORARY CONFINEMENT ZONE. PERMIT PENDING.**

He capped the marker.

The air around him crackled. The Authority's interface flashed red, then white, then red again.

**CONFLICT DETECTED.** **LOCAL CODE VIOLATION: UNAUTHORIZED DIMENSIONAL PORTAL OPENING.** **RESPONSE: MANDATORY CLOSURE PROTOCOL INITIATED.**

The Architect's hand stopped. The fingers curled inward, confused by the sudden bureaucratic assertion.

The entity was vast, old, and powerful, but it operated on rules.

It operated on structure. And Kowalski had just filed a paper claim on its workspace.

"Vex," Kowalski said, not looking up. "Throw the staff."

Vex hesitated.

"What?"

"Throw it. Into the circle. Now."

Vex looked at the swirling black smoke, then at Kowalski's calm, dirty face. He gritted his teeth and hurled the staff.

The chiton weapon spun through the air, trailing sparks, and landed squarely in the center of Kowalski's marker-drawn circle.

The moment the staff touched the boundary, the system reacted.

**ITEM LOGGED: UNLICENSED ARTIFACT.** **STATUS: CONTRABAND.** **ACTION: SEQUESTRATION.**

The rift screamed. A sound like tearing metal echoed through the ruin. The black smoke rushed inward, sucked toward the staff as if it were a drain. The Architect's hand jerked back, pulling away from the grid, forced to retreat by the sudden vacuum of the containment protocol.

Kowalski stood up. He brushed dust off his khakis.

"Vane. Get the net."

Vane scrambled over the debris, unrolling a heavy-duty containment mesh from his pack. It was standard issue, designed for Class-2 vermin, useless against a god.

But Kowalski wasn't using it to catch the Architect, and he was using it to catch the leak.

"Spread it over the circle," Kowalski commanded. "Anchor it with the rebar."

Vane looked at the retreating hand, then at Kowalski.

"You're trying to plug the hole with a bug net?"

"I'm trying to document the closure," Kowalski said. "The Authority sees the staff as the source. It's logging the staff as the cause of the infestation. By sequestering the staff, I'm forcing the environment to reject the breach."

Vane's eyes widened. He understood. It wasn't magic. It was liability.

He spread the net. The mesh caught the edges of the rift, and the black smoke thinned. The ground stopped shaking.

But the cost was immediate.

A red notification burned in Kowalski's vision.

**FINE: 500 TP FOR UNAUTHORIZED USE OF CONTAINMENT MESH ON NON-BIOLOGICAL ENTITY.** **PENALTY: DEGRADATION OF EQUIPMENT INTEGRITY.**

The net turned brittle. Grey ash flaked off the fibers.

Kowalski felt the sting of it. His hands twitched. He wanted to fix it, to reinforce it, but he couldn't. He had spent his budget on the identification.

The Architect's hand was gone. The rift was shrinking, the staff pulsing in the center of the mesh, locked in a stasis field created by the system's own rigid adherence to protocol.

Silence returned to the ruin.

Vex slumped against a broken wall, the grayness in his skin receding slightly. He looked at Kowalski with a mixture of awe and horror.

"You used the system," Vex whispered. "You used the rules against it."

"I used the paperwork," Kowalski corrected.

He walked over to the sequestered staff. The Authority's interface showed the item as **LOCKED**. He couldn't touch it without incurring another fine. He didn't try.

Instead, he pulled out his clipboard. He wrote.

**INCIDENT REPORT: DIMENSIONAL BREACH.** **CAUSE: UNLICENSED ARTIFACT (ARCHITECT'S STAFF).** **ACTION: TEMPORARY SEQUESTRATION VIA LOCAL CODE MANIPULATION.** **STATUS: CONTAINED. NOT RESOLVED.**

He underlined the last two words.

"Get up," Kowalski said to Vex. "We're not done."

Vex struggled to his feet.

"The hand. It retreated."

"It retreated because it has to process the violation," Kowalski said. "It's filing a counter-claim. We have maybe ten minutes before the Authority rules in its favor and dissolves the sequestration."

"How do you know that?"

"Because I've seen the backlog.

Bureaucracy moves slowly, but it moves inevitably. The Architect is Level IX.

It doesn't lose.

It just waits for the paperwork to catch up.

"

Kowalski turned to Vane.

"Check the perimeter. Look for secondary entry points. If the main hand is distracted, the fingers might have gone elsewhere."

Vane nodded, his face pale, and ran off into the smoke.

Kowalski looked at Vex.

"You're compromised. Your license is damaged. You can't lead the next phase."

"I can fight," Vex said, though his voice lacked conviction. He looked at his blackened hands.

"Fight isn't the job anymore," Kowalski said. "Containment is. And you're a liability."

Vex stiffened.

"What are you saying?"

"I'm saying I need a witness who isn't contaminated. And I need a runner."

Kowalski tapped the clipboard.

"The Authority gave us provisional status. That means we have access to the Local Grid's maintenance tunnels. The ones under the foundation."

Vex stared at him.

"The sewer system? Below the Rift?"

"The Rift is anchored to the bedrock," Kowalski said. "The staff was trying to drill down. The counter-claim is going to try to drill up. We need to be in the middle."

"That's suicide," Vane shouted from across the debris. "The structural integrity is gone!"

"The Authority doesn't care about structural integrity," Kowalski said. "It cares about root cause.

If we don't identify the anchor point, the fine doubles every hour.

And if we get fined, we lose our provisional status.

And if we lose our status, the Architect eats everyone in this county.

"

Kowalski looked at the retreating dust cloud. He could feel the pressure building, the weight of the system turning against them.

He opened his Field Guide. The entry for **Architect** was blank, marked **INCOMPLETE**.

He needed data. He needed to see what the thing was actually building.

He walked to the edge of the containment circle. The staff hummed, a low, mournful sound.

He reached into his pocket and pulled out a spare roll of duct tape and a piece of chalk.

"Vex," Kowalski said. "I'm going to put you on a leash."

Vex flinched.

"Not a magical one," Kowalski said. He tossed Vex a length of industrial rope. "Tie it around your waist. Tie the other end to this pillar."

Vex caught the rope. He looked confused.

"If you try to use magic again, I'll cut the rope and you'll fall into the rift," Kowalski said. "If you stay still, you help me hold the perimeter while I go down."

Vex looked at the rope. He looked at Kowalski. He tied it around his waist.

"Why would you trust me?" Vex asked.

Kowalski looked at the man who had tried to kill him an hour ago.

"I don't," Kowalski said. "But you're the only one with a sword who doesn't have a gun I can confiscate."

Kowalski walked to the maintenance hatch. It was twisted metal, half-buried in concrete. He pried it open with his crowbar.

Darkness yawned below, and the smell was worse than the ozone. It was the smell of wet earth, old blood, and something sweet, like rotting flowers.

Kowalski clipped his flashlight to his helmet.

"I'm going down," he said. "Vane, monitor the timer. If the containment circle starts to dissolve, pull the rope. Don't wait for me."

Vane nodded, his hands shaking.

Kowalski dropped into the hole, and he landed in sludge, up to his knees. The tunnel was narrow, the walls slick with black slime.

He clicked on his flashlight.

The beam cut through the dark.

And froze.

The tunnel wasn't empty, and the walls were lined with eggs.

Thousands of them. Translucent, pulsing, the size of basketballs. And inside each one, something was moving. Small, sharp things.

They weren't rats, and they were fingers.

Dozens of them, curled up in fetal positions, waiting.

Kowalski's breath hitched. His hand tightened on the crowbar.

**FIELD GUIDE UPDATE:** **NEW SPECIES DETECTED: ARCHITECT SPAWN.** **CLASS: LEVEL VII.** **WEAKNESS: UNKNOWN.**

He stepped back, his boot slipping.

A single egg cracked.

A wet, chitinous hand pulled itself free, dripping slime. It looked at Kowalski.

It didn't attack.

It pointed deeper into the tunnel.

At the far end, in the darkness, a massive shape was rising from the floor. It had no face. It was a throne. And on the throne sat a figure made of the same black stone as the Architect's hand.

The figure held a clipboard.

Kowalski's heart stopped.

The figure held a clipboard identical to his own.

The thing on the throne raised its pen.

It wrote something on the paper.

Kowalski couldn't read it from this distance. But the figure turned its eyeless head toward him.

And Kowalski heard a voice in his head. It wasn't a scream. It was a notification sound.

**INSPECTION COMPLETE.** **SUBJECT: DALE KOWALSKI.** **STATUS: NON-COMPLIANT.**

The figure stood up.

The eggs hatched all at once.
