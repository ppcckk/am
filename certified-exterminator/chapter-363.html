Chapter 363: Jenkins Follows into Darkness

# Chapter 363

The perimeter of the exclusion zone was not a wall. It was a suggestion.

Sarah Jenkins, District Safety Inspector for the Authority's Compliance Division, stood behind a barrier of yellow tape that flapped uselessly in the sub-zero wind. She adjusted her spectacles, her breath pluming in the air, and watched the man in the center of the crater.

He was not fighting, and he was not screaming. He was writing.

To the naked eye, Dale Kowalski was a broken thing. A hollowed-out shell wearing a high-visibility vest that had long since lost its reflective sheen. His left hand clutched a clipboard with white-knuckled intensity. His right hand held a pen, moving in a rhythm that was less like combat and more like a metronome.

"Target is stabilizing," Jenkins said into her radio.

Her voice was flat, professional.

"Subject 402 is engaging a Void Architect, Class V.

Visual confirmation of Protocol 44-B activation.

Memory wipe successful. Emotional core is... absent.

"

"Absent?" The dispatcher's voice crackled, distorted by the interference from the rift. "Define absent."

"He doesn't care," Jenkins said, watching Dale step over a puddle of black ichor without looking down. "He's running the checklists. He's documenting the kill. He's not afraid because the part of his brain that produces fear has been sequestered to pay for the weapon he's using."

The Void Architect, a towering entity of shifting geometric glass and weeping stone, loomed over Dale. It had no face, only a aperture where a face should be, spiraling into infinite depth.

It paused, tilting its head, which was confused.

Prey usually ran. Prey usually bled from the mouth when they screamed.

This prey was measuring the distance between its eyes and the ground.

Dale's pen scratched against the paper.

*Entry 402-Alpha. Species: Void Architect.

Sub-strata: Geometric Lattice.

Weakness: Uncertainty. Status: Hostile.*

The creature lunged.

Dale did not dodge, and he adjusted his stance. He shifted his weight to his back foot, aligning his clipboard so that the edge of the board caught the creature's limb.

The impact should have shattered his arm.

Instead, the System flared a blinding white text in the air.

**PROTOCOL: STRUCTURAL DEFLECTION.** **COST: 50 POINTS.** **DEBT REMAINING: 9,950.**

The creature's arm shattered against the clipboard. Shards of glass-geometry exploded outward.

Dale didn't flinch, and he didn't even blink.

He simply wrote the word *Fractured* on his sheet and moved forward.

Jenkins felt a cold knot in her stomach. She had seen many technicians break under the pressure of the Authority's debt system. She had seen them go mad.

She had never seen one operate with such terrifying efficiency while emotionally lobotomized. It was like watching a machine process data.

There was no hesitation, no mercy, no pride.

Just the execution of code.

"Command," Jenkins said, her hand hovering over her abort button. "Requesting authorization to terminate engagement. The subject's liability exposure is exceeding threshold."

"Denied," the radio said. "The infestation is spreading into the residential sector. If he stops, we lose the containment grid. Let him finish the job."

Jenkins watched as Dale kicked the creature's knee, which was a clumsy strike, unrefined by martial arts, but precise. He knew the joint structure from his Field Guide.

He had spent hours studying the anatomy of things that shouldn't exist, mapping their bones on graph paper.

He treated monsters like cockroaches. They had exoskeletons. They had weak points.

They had nests.

The Void Architect shrieked, a sound that vibrated in Jenkins' teeth. It tried to phase through the ground, to escape into the dimensional bleed. Dale anticipated this. He had already drawn a circle around the creature's feet with a spray can of industrial sealant, a prop he had purchased at a hardware store three towns over.

**APPLIED: DIMENSIONAL SEALANT (CLASS II).** **EFFECT: LOCAL SPACE ANCHORED.** **PENALTY: CROSS-CONTAMINATION (INSECTICIDE PROTOCOL USED ON VOID ENTITY).** **DEBUFF: -40% EFFICACY.**

The sealant sizzled and turned purple.

The creature screamed in pain, confused by the chemical burn that had nothing to do with magic.

Dale ignored the debuff notification. He didn't care about efficiency.

He cared about containment, and he stepped closer. The creature lashed out, a tendril of void-energy whipping toward his head.

Dale ducked.

He didn't roll. He didn't dance. He simply lowered his center of gravity, tucked his chin, and brought the pen down on the creature's wrist.

*Tap.*

Not a strike. A tap. Like checking a box on a form.

*Item: Void Tendril. Condition: Severed.*

The limb detached.

The creature howled, stumbling back. Dale advanced.

He looked like a accountant auditing a building that refused to pass inspection.

He was relentless. He was tedious. He was killing the god-like entity with the boredom of a man who had sprayed ten thousand kitchens and never once enjoyed it.

Jenkins stepped forward, breaking protocol. She crossed the yellow tape.

The air inside the zone tasted like copper and ozone.

Her skin prickled.

The Authority's presence was heavy here, thick and oppressive. She could feel the weight of the debt hanging over Dale's head, a physical pressure on his shoulders.

Dale stopped. The creature was down on one knee, its lattice structure cracking. It was dying, but not dead.

The final blow required documentation. The final blow required verification.

Dale looked at the creature. Then he looked at his clipboard.

He looked confused.

For a second, the blank void in his mind flickered.

A spark of recognition. He looked at the name *Sarah* written in small letters on the back of his clipboard. A name from a past he couldn't touch.

He looked at the creature, and he looked at the name.

"Who..." his voice was raspy, unused. "Who am I?"

Jenkins froze.

The System hadn't wiped his identity. It had wiped his attachments. His memories were intact, but the emotional context was gone.

He knew who he was. He just didn't know why he was here.

Why he was fighting.

"Kowalski," Jenkins said, her voice soft. "You're Dale Kowalski. Certified Exterminator."

Dale tilted his head. He looked at the pen in his hand. He looked at the blood on his vest.

"Exterminator," he repeated. The word sounded foreign in his mouth.

"Yes. Finish the job. The nest is still active."

Dale looked down at the ground. Beneath the Void Architect, the earth was pulsing. A second rift was opening, smaller, darker. A nursery.

The creature tried to rise. Its shards clattered against the stone.

Dale raised his pen again. But he didn't strike. He wrote.

*Query: Is the nest contained?*

The System paused. This was not a protocol. This was a question.

**SYSTEM ALERT: UNAUTHORIZED QUERY.** **PENALTY: 500 POINTS.** **DEBT ACCRUED: 10,450.**

Jenkins gasped.

"Dale, stop! You're digging a hole!"

But Dale didn't stop. He wrote another line.

*Query: Can the debt be transferred?*

The air shimmered. The red text boxes vanished, replaced by a single, pulsing black box.

**CLAUSE 77-C: LIABILITY TRANSFER.** **CONDITION: ACCEPTANCE OF ASSET SEIZURE BY THIRD PARTY.** **WARNING: THE THIRD PARTY MUST BE A LICENSED PROFESSIONAL.**

Dale looked up. He looked at Jenkins.

He didn't smile. He didn't frown. He just pointed the pen at her.

"You," he said.

Jenkins stepped back.

"What?"

"The debt," Dale said.

His voice was monotone, stripped of all nuance.

"It is high.

My capital is low. I have identified a vulnerability in the liability structure. The Authority prioritizes the completion of the job over the solvency of the worker.

If I die, the debt is written off. If I transfer it, it becomes your problem.

"

Jenkins' heart hammered against her ribs, and she looked at the Void Architect, still struggling to rise. She looked at the new rift opening beneath it. If she didn't act, the infestation would spread.

If she did act, she would be bound to the debt.

"You're bluffing," she said.

"No," Dale said. He tapped the clipboard.

"I am documenting.

Clause 77-C is a loophole. It has been exploited by Class VII exterminators in the Eastern Districts. They use it to offload high-risk engagements to junior inspectors.

I have read the bylaws. Section 4, Paragraph 12.

"

Jenkins felt a wave of nausea, which was true.

She had buried that section in her memory, hoping no one would find it. It was the dirty secret of the Authority.

The system was designed to harvest labor, not to protect it.

"You can't do this," Jenkins whispered.

"I am already doing it," Dale said.

He wrote his final entry.

*Action: Transfer Liability.* *Recipient: Sarah Jenkins, Class III Inspector.* *Consent: Implied by proximity.*

The black box pulsed. A chain of light snapped from Dale's chest to Jenkins' chest. It hurt. It felt like a wire being pulled through her nerves.

**LIABILITY TRANSFER COMPLETE.** **DEBT ASSUMED: 10,450 POINTS.** **NEW STATUS: INSOLVENT.**

Jenkins fell to her knees, gasping. The weight of it was crushing.

She could feel the System's eyes on her, cold and calculating.

She was now responsible for the debt. If she failed, her license would be revoked. Her mind would be scrubbed.

Dale stood straight. His shoulders dropped. The tension left his body. He was free.

He looked at the Void Architect. He raised his pen.

The creature roared, a final desperate surge of power. It lunged for Dale, sensing his vulnerability.

But Dale was no longer vulnerable. He was solvent. He was light.

He didn't fight the creature. He stepped aside.

The Void Architect crashed into the spot where Dale had been standing. It stumbled, off-balance. Its momentum carried it into the sealant circle Dale had drawn. The chemical reaction flared one last time, violently.

**VERIFICATION: TARGET NEUTRALIZED.** **REWARD: 2,000 POINTS.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 9.8/10.**

The points flowed into Dale's account. His balance went from negative to positive in a heartbeat. The debt was gone. The memory of his daughter remained a void, but the crushing weight of the liability was lifted.

He looked at Jenkins, who was still kneeling, trembling.

"Report," Dale said.

Jenkins looked up. Her eyes were wide, terrified, and furious.

"You... you used me."

"I used the system," Dale corrected. "You were in the workspace. You were a certified professional. The transfer was valid."

"You erased my safety buffer," she hissed.

"You have forty-eight hours to file a dispute," Dale said.

He tore a sheet off his clipboard. He handed it to her.

"Here is the receipt.

I suggest you hire an auditor.

"

He turned away from her. He walked toward the edge of the crater. The wind was picking up, carrying the scent of sulfur and rot.

"Where are you going?" Jenkins shouted.

Dale didn't look back.

"To the next address."

"The zone is closed," she said. "You're not authorized to leave until—"

"I am authorized," Dale said. "I have a Level I license. I have a clean record. And I have a job to do."

He walked past her. He walked past the shattered remains of the Void Architect. He walked toward the dark tree line at the edge of the suburb.

Jenkins scrambled to her feet. She checked her balance. The red numbers stared back at her. *Negative 10,450.* She looked at the tree line. Dale was disappearing into the shadows.

But he wasn't going home.

The map on his wrist display showed a new infestation marker. It wasn't in Ohio. It wasn't on Earth.

It was in the Rift itself.

And the marker wasn't blinking. It was pulsing in time with Dale's heartbeat.

Jenkins realized, with a jolt of horror, that Dale hadn't just transferred the debt. He had transferred the *connection*.

The Authority didn't just track debts. It tracked souls that owed them.

And now, Sarah Jenkins was linked to Dale Kowalski.

If he went into the Rift, she would feel it.

If he died, she would pay, and she ran toward the tree line, her boots slipping on the mud.

"Dale!" she screamed. "Wait!"

Dale stopped. He stood at the edge of the darkness. He turned his head. Just slightly.

He raised his hand. Not in a wave. In a warning.

He pointed to the sky.

Above the tree line, the clouds were parting. A massive, eyeless face formed in the swirling vapor. It looked down at the crater. It looked at the two figures standing at the edge of the world.

The face opened its mouth.

The sound wasn't a roar. It was a notification chime.

**INFESTATION DETECTED: CLASS IX ENTITY.** **NAME: THE INSPECTOR GENERAL.** **STATUS: EN ROUTE TO AUDIT.**

Dale lowered his hand. He looked at Jenkins.

"They're coming," he said.

"Who?" she whispered.

Dale clicked his pen.

"The people who wrote the bylaws," he said. "And they're not happy about the transfer."

He turned and walked into the darkness. The Rift yawned open beneath his feet, swallowing the light. Jenkins watched him go, the weight of the debt burning in her chest, knowing she had no choice but to follow.

Jenkins stepped forward and let the darkness take her.
