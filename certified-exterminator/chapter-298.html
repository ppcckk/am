Chapter 298: Citation of Death

# Chapter 298

The woman's name was Sarah, and she was watching a man commit suicide with a clipboard.

She had seen men die in the Rift before. They usually died fast, caught by a jaw that closed faster than the eye could track, or dissolved by acid that ate through Kevlar like wet paper. They screamed, usually. They ran, almost always.

Dale Kowalski did not run. He did not scream. He adjusted his pen.

To Sarah, standing three feet behind him in the sludge of the tunnel, Dale looked less like a hero and more like a man who had missed his bus. His face was gray with exhaustion, sweat cutting channels through the grime on his forehead, but his hands were steady.

Too steady.

His Certification Level had just dropped to zero. The purple warning text floated in the air around his head, a halo of bureaucratic failure that pulsed with a rhythmic, annoying throb. *License Suspended.*

But he stepped forward. Into the dark. Into the drain that smelled of copper and old rot.

Sarah's instinct was to grab him, to drag him back toward the light of the entry shaft.

Her hand twitched at her side, fingers curling around the grip of a rifle she had found but never fired. She hesitated.

That hesitation was the difference between surviving and being dead, she told herself, but it didn't feel like wisdom.

It felt like cowardice.

Why was he walking toward the thing that had just stripped their licenses?

A rat, no bigger than a housecat but covered in chitinous plates, skittered across the wet concrete ahead of them. It didn't look at Dale.

It looked at the floor, its antennae twitching.

It moved with a mechanical precision that made Sarah's skin crawl.

This wasn't an animal acting on instinct. This was a unit executing a path.

Dale stopped. He didn't raise a weapon. He didn't cast a spell.

He crouched, his knees cracking audibly in the damp silence. He pulled a small, battered notebook from his pocket—the one he kept separate from the official Field Guide, because the Authority didn't like unsanctioned observations.

He clicked his pen. The sound was tiny, insignificant against the low, grinding hum rising from the drain.

Sarah wanted to scream at him to get up, and the air was thickening. The shadows in the tunnel corners were lengthening, detaching themselves from the walls. A violation log had been filed against them.

If they were caught standing here, they wouldn't just lose their points.

They would be reclassified as infestations themselves.

But Dale wasn't looking at the shadows. He was looking at the rat's footprints.

He traced the wet smear of slime left by the creature's hind legs.

He measured the distance between them with his thumb and forefinger. He muttered something under his breath. Sarah couldn't hear it over the rushing water, but she saw his lips move. *K-7.

Structural entry point.*

He stood up, wiped his hand on his trousers, and pointed to a grate set into the side of the tunnel wall, half-submerged in the murky water.

"That's not a drain," Dale said. His voice was flat, devoid of the fear that was currently paralyzing Sarah's legs. "That's a maintenance hatch."

The rat skittered onto the grate. It paused. Its compound eyes reflected the faint bioluminescence of the moss on the ceiling. It seemed to be waiting.

Sarah's breath hitched.

"Dale.

The Authority says we're suspended. If we interact with the site, we're violating the quarantine protocol.

We'll be erased.

"

"We're already erased if we leave," Dale said, not turning around. "Look at the water level."

Sarah looked. The water in the tunnel wasn't draining away.

It was receding.

But it wasn't flowing out. It was being sucked *in*. The grate wasn't an exit.

It was an intake valve.

"Someone is pumping the tunnel dry," Dale continued. "To access the lower levels.

To install more of these.

" He gestured to the barcode on the wall, the one that had triggered the suspension.

"This isn't a wild infestation. It's a managed colony. And the manager is below us."

The rat turned its head. It looked at Dale, then at Sarah. It opened its mouth, revealing rows of needle-like teeth, and emitted a sound that was not a squeak, but a dial-up modem screech.

*System Alert: Unregistered Signal Detected.*

Dale didn't flinch. He opened his notebook to a fresh page. He drew a circle. Then he drew a line from the circle to the grate.

"Sarah," he said. "Give me your rifle."

"I'm not giving you my gun, Dale. We're unarmed. The System won't let us shoot."

"The System won't let us shoot *monsters*," Dale corrected. "It doesn't have a protocol for *maintenance equipment*."

He pointed to the grate again.

"The Authority classifies threats based on biological signature.

That rat?

Biological.

That grate? Infrastructure.

There is no cross-contamination penalty for interfering with municipal plumbing.

"

Sarah stared at him. The logic was so absurd, so strictly adherent to the rigid rules of their reality, that it broke through her panic.

The Authority was a bureaucracy first, a god second.

It followed rules. And Dale Kowalski was the only person alive who knew how to read the fine print.

"You want to shoot a pipe," Sarah said.

"I want to break the pump mechanism," Dale said. "If the water rises, the infestation is suppressed by environmental conditions. The Authority recognizes flood mitigation as a valid extermination tactic for subterranean species. It's in the Class I handbook, page 42."

"That's not in the handbook," Sarah whispered.

"It is. Section C, Subsection 4. *Excessive moisture as a suppression agent for Rattus profundus.*" Dale tapped the notebook. "I read it. Twice."

The rat hissed. It launched itself at Dale.

Dale didn't dodge, and he stepped sideways, a movement so practiced it looked like he was stepping over a curb. He didn't have a protocol shield.

He didn't have a stun baton.

He had a clipboard. He swung it, not to hit the rat, but to block its path, forcing it into the narrow space between his legs and the wall.

The rat crashed into the clipboard. The wood splintered. The creature thrashed, its claws tearing at Dale's coat.

Sarah moved. Not because she was brave, but because the rat was in her line of fire.

She raised the rifle.

Her finger hovered over the trigger.

The reticle in her vision flickered red, then gray. *Target Unlicensed. Fire Denied.*

She couldn't shoot the rat.

Dale shoved the rat back with a kick that sent it skittering into the water. It didn't die. It just regrouped.

"Shoot the grate, Sarah!" Dale shouted.

She aimed. The reticle hovered over the metal grate. It turned green.

*Target: Structural Element. Permissible.*

The crack of the rifle was deafening in the enclosed space. The bullet struck the lock on the grate. It didn't break the grate, but it sheared the hinge. The metal door groaned and swung inward, revealing a dark, circular hole leading down into the earth.

A rush of cold air blasted out, smelling of ozone and stagnant water.

Dale dropped the broken clipboard. He pulled the sample vial from his pocket. The glass was cracked, but the stopper held. He held it up to the hole.

"The signal is coming from below," Dale said. "The pump. It's pulling the water down to cool the nest.

If we break the pump, the water backs up. The rats drown.

The Authority marks it as an environmental hazard containment.

We get points for mitigation, not just extermination.

"

"It's suicide," Sarah said.

"It's compliance," Dale replied.

He jumped.

Sarah watched him fall into the dark. For a second, she thought he was dead. Then she heard the splash of water, and the sound of something metallic snapping.

She scrambled to the edge of the hole. Peering down, she saw Dale standing in ankle-deep water, holding the bent rifle he had picked up earlier. He was aiming it at a large, spinning impeller in the center of a concrete chamber.

The rats were swarming around him, but they weren't attacking. They were fleeing.

Dale fired.

The bullet struck the impeller's bearing. The machine shrieked, a sound of tearing metal, and seized. The water, which had been rushing down, stopped. Then, with a groan of pressure, it swelled.

The rats squealed, high-pitched and frantic. They climbed the walls, trying to find higher ground.

Dale waded back toward the hole.

"Grab the rope!"

Sarah dropped the climbing rope from her pack. Dale caught it, tied it to a pipe, and climbed.

As he rose, the water level rose with him. The rats washed away into the darkness, swept back into the deep channels where they belonged.

When Dale pulled himself up onto the tunnel floor, he was soaked. His clothes clung to him. His hair was plastered to his skull. He looked like a drowned man.

He lay on the concrete, gasping for air.

Sarah dropped to her knees beside him.

"Are you okay?"

Dale nodded, and he reached into his pocket and pulled out a small, crumpled piece of paper. It was a receipt.

Not from a store.

From the Authority.

He held it up. The ink was wet, running slightly, but the text was legible.

*Job Completion: 45%. Status: Pending Verification. Points Awarded: 12 (Environmental Mitigation). Note: Unauthorized modification of infrastructure logged.*

His Certification Level hadn't returned. It was still suspended. But he had points. He had a record.

The rumble in the tunnel stopped. The water settled, slapping against the walls.

From the darkness of the lower chamber, a voice echoed. It wasn't the synthetic voice of the Authority.

It was human.

Tired.

Amused.

*"Clever trick, Technician. You used the plumbing code against us."*

Dale sat up. He wiped the sludge from his eyes. He looked into the hole.

"Who are you?" Dale asked.

*"I'm the Auditor,"* the voice said. *"And you're not just under review, Dale. You're under observation. You think you're fighting monsters.

You're fighting people. And you just broke their equipment.

"*

A shadow moved at the bottom of the hole. It was tall. It wore a suit.

*"Your license isn't suspended,"* the voice continued. *"It's being reassigned. You're no longer a Pest Control Technician. You're an Inspector. And we're coming up to inspect your work."*

The water in the tunnel rose faster than the pump could handle. The rats were gone, but something else was moving in the flooded dark. Something large. Something that didn't fear water.

Dale looked at Sarah. He looked at the empty space where his Field Guide should have been. He looked at the broken pen.

He pulled a new pen from his pocket. It was cheap, plastic, and likely to leak. He uncapped it.

"Sarah," he said. "Get the flashlight."

"What are you going to do?"

Dale looked at the rising water. He looked at the approaching shadow.

"I'm going to write a citation," Dale said. "And then I'm going to fine you for it."
