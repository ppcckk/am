Chapter 341: Defoliant and Paperwork

# Chapter 341

The ground beneath their feet didn't just shift; it liquefied.

Linda screamed, a sharp, jagged sound that cut through the humid night air, but Dale didn't look at her. He looked at his boots. Mud sucked at them, thick and black, smelling of ozone and old copper. The Rift above them pulsed, a bruised purple bruise against the starless sky, dripping a substance that sizzled when it hit the grass.

Kael scrambled back, losing his footing. He flailed, grabbing a branch that immediately withered into ash under his grip.

"Don't touch the vegetation!" Dale barked, his voice flat. He planted his feet, widening his stance. "It's reactive. Treat it like a chemical spill."

"It's a *forest*," Kael shouted, eyes wide with terror. "It's trying to eat us!"

"It's a structural anomaly with aggressive root systems," Dale corrected. He pulled a small, silver canister from his belt.

"Standard defoliant.

Class II. Hold your breath.

"

He sprayed a mist toward the encroaching vines. The liquid hit the leaves, and the plants recoiled, retracting with a hiss that sounded like boiling tea.

Linda stared at him.

"You brought weedkiller?"

"I brought what the protocol requires for Class-4 Flora Intrusions," Dale said.

He stepped forward, the mud squelching.

"Stay in the cleared path.

If you step out, you trigger a growth response.

And I don't have enough canisters for a second spray.

"

"Who are these people?" Linda whispered, pointing toward the swirling vortex.

Inside the Rift, the shapes resolved. They weren't monsters.

They were men.

Dozens of them, and they wore suits, but the fabric was shimmering, shifting like oil on water. They held briefcases that glowed with a faint, sickly light.

"The Auditor's staff," Dale said. He checked his clipboard. The paper was still blank, but a new header had faded in, written in ink that looked like blood.

**CASE STATUS: PENDING APPEAL.** **JURISDICTION: NON-LOCAL.** **PROTOCOL: LITIGANT DEFENSE.**

"They're not attacking," Kael said, his voice trembling. "They're... organizing."

One of the suit-clad figures stepped out of the light. It was tall, thin, with a face that seemed to have too many angles. It raised a hand.

Not a weapon. A stamp.

Dale stepped in front of Linda.

"Back up. Both of you."

"Dale, they're coming at us!" Linda cried.

"They're serving notice," Dale said. He didn't raise a shield. He raised his pen.

The figure stopped ten feet away. It bowed, a jerky, mechanical motion. Then it spoke. Its voice was the sound of paper tearing.

"Dale Kowalski. License Holder Class II. You have filed a grievance against the Authority's enforcement division. This is a violation of Interdimensional Statute 7-B: Unsanctioned Litigation."

Dale looked at the figure, and he didn't blink.

"I filed an appeal. There is a difference.

An appeal is a right.

A grievance is a complaint.

"

The figure tilted its head.

"Your distinction is noted. However, your filing was incomplete. You failed to attach supporting documentation.

Therefore, your case is dismissed. The penalty for frivolous litigation is immediate termination of your license.

And your life."

The figure raised the stamp again. The ink on the stamp glowed red.

"If you stamp me, you're out of jurisdiction," Dale said.

He pointed his pen at the figure's chest.

"This is Ohio.

Well, technically, it's a Rift, but it's anchored in Ohio municipal code. The Authority doesn't have local enforcement rights without a permit. Do you have a permit?

"

The figure paused. The steam hissing from its vents grew louder.

"We are the Authority," the figure said.

"You're the *Auditor*," Dale corrected. "Different department.

Different rules.

I checked my Field Guide. Entry 442: *Bureaucratic Constructs*. Weakness: Jurisdictional Overreach.

If you act outside your designated zone without proper paperwork, you are subject to a Cross-Contamination Penalty.

"

The figure's glass face cracked further. A spiderweb of fractures spread across its eyes.

"You cannot invoke a penalty against a higher entity," the figure said.

"I can invoke a procedural halt," Dale said. He tapped his clipboard.

"I am invoking Code K-9: Unauthorized Entry.

You are on my property. The Rift is an unpermitted structure. I am the primary tenant.

You are trespassing.

"

Kael lowered his sword. He looked at the ground, then at Dale.

"You're arguing with a god about property lines?"

"I'm arguing with a middle manager about zoning laws," Dale said.

He kept his eyes on the figure.

"If you touch me, you violate the Tenant Agreement. Which voids your contract with the local reality layer.

You'll dissolve.

"

The figure didn't move. It stood frozen, processing. The other suits behind it shifted, whispering to each other. Their whispers sounded like shuffling cards.

"This is a trick," the figure said.

"It's a loophole," Dale said. "And you're stuck in it.

Because if you kill me, you have to file a termination report.

And if you file a termination report, you admit that a Level II technician successfully challenged an Auditor. Which lowers your KPI score.

"

The figure's hand lowered slightly. The stamp hovered in the air.

"What do you want?" the figure asked. Its voice was no longer tearing paper; it was the sound of a jammed printer.

Dale looked at Linda. She was pale, her hands shaking. She looked at Kael, who was staring at the glowing briefcases.

"I want my points," Dale said.

The figure stared at him.

"Your points?"

"I'm owed Treatment Points for the infestation in the duplex.

I completed the protocol. Identify, document, isolate, eliminate, verify.

I did all five.

But you deducted them because I used a non-standard containment method. Trash bags and duct tape.

"

"The Authority does not recognize improvisation," the figure said.

"The Authority recognizes results," Dale said. He held up his clipboard.

"I have the report.

I have the frass samples.

I have the witness statements from the neighbors who didn't panic. I have a Thoroughness Rating of 9.2.

You can't deny me points for doing the job correctly just because you didn't like my tools.

"

The figure looked at the clipboard. It seemed to scan the blank page.

"The page is blank," the figure said.

"It's not blank," Dale said. "It's *pending*. I'm writing it now. Watch."

Dale wrote. He wrote quickly, his pen scratching against the paper.

He wrote the date.

The location. The species. The method.

The outcome.

As he wrote, the air around them shimmered. The mud stopped sucking at their boots. The vines retreated further.

The figure watched the ink appear. It watched the numbers form.

**TREATMENT POINTS: +15.** **BONUS: +5 FOR JURISDICTIONAL ADVOCACY.** **TOTAL: +20.**

The figure stiffened.

"You are manipulating the system."

"I'm using the system," Dale said. He didn't look up from his writing.

"There's a difference.

If you want to contest this, you have to file a counter-motion. But you have to do it before the ink dries. The ink dries in twelve seconds.

"

The figure looked at its own hand. The stamp was trembling.

"Twelve seconds," Kael whispered. He looked at Dale, then at the suits. "Dale, what are you doing?"

"I'm buying time," Dale said. "And I'm auditing them."

He finished the last line. He signed his name. *Dale Kowalski.*

The page flashed gold.

The figure let out a shriek. It wasn't a sound; it was a wave of pressure that knocked Kael backward.

**SYSTEM ALERT: CONTRADICTION DETECTED.** **SUBJECT: AUDITOR UNIT 7-ALPHA.** **ACTION: REVIEWING LOCAL CODE.**

The suits behind the figure dissolved. Their suits unravelled into threads of light. They weren't attacking; they were being deleted.

"Stop it!" the figure screamed. "Stop writing!"

"I'm not writing," Dale said. "I'm documenting."

He turned to Linda.

"Did you see the entry point?"

Linda blinked. She looked at the Rift.

"The... the crack in the sky?"

"No. The crack in the *ground*," Dale said.

He pointed to a fissure near the tree line, which was small, barely visible, but it was leaking a faint blue light.

"That's the source. The suits are just the immune response.

The Rift is the nest.

"

"We have to close it," Linda said.

"We can't close it," Dale said. "We have to *certify* it."

Kael scrambled to his feet. He looked at the dissolving suits, then at Dale.

He looked terrified, and he looked confused. He looked like a man who realized he had been fighting the wrong war.

"Certify it?" Kael asked.

"If we document the Rift as an unlicensed dimensional breach, we gain control over its parameters," Dale said.

He grabbed Linda's arm and pulled her toward the fissure.

"We become the licensed exterminators.

Not the victims.

Not the heroes. The *technicians*.

"

"That's insane," Kael said.

"That's efficient," Dale said. He reached into his pocket. He pulled out a small, yellow tag. It was the kind used to tag dead rats in a dumpster.

He held it up.

"What is that?" Linda asked.

"A warning," Dale said. He walked toward the fissure.

The blue light grew brighter.

The ground around it was vibrating.

"If you step in here, you violate the quarantine zone."

The figure lunged. Its arm stretched, impossible lengths, reaching for Dale's neck.

Dale didn't dodge. He slapped the yellow tag onto the figure's chest.

**TAG APPLIED: QUARANTINE ZONE.**

The figure froze. It snapped back into itself, shrinking, condensing. It became a small, gray cube on the ground.

Dale kicked it aside. It skittered across the mud, harmless.

"Come on," Dale said. He looked at Kael. "You can run. Or you can help me fill out the form."

Kael looked at the cube. He looked at the Rift. He looked at the blue light in the ground.

He sheathed his sword, and he walked over to Dale.

"What do I do?" Kael asked.

Dale handed him a second pen.

"Hold the clipboard," Dale said. "And don't drop it. If you drop it, we lose the point bonus."

Kael took the clipboard. His hands were shaking, but he held it steady.

Dale looked at the fissure. He took a deep breath. He checked his gear.

Spray bottle. Pen.

Clipboard.

Yellow tag, and he stepped onto the blue light.

The world fell away.

He wasn't in Ohio anymore. He was in a hallway. A long, endless hallway lined with filing cabinets. The air smelled of dust and old coffee.

And at the end of the hallway, a door, and the door was open.

And something was sitting at the desk.

It wasn't a monster. It wasn't a man.

It was a creature made entirely of red tape.

Dale stepped into the hallway. The door slammed shut behind him.

He looked at his clipboard. The page was no longer blank.

It was filled with text.

**NEW CASE: THE OFFICE OF THE UNDERSECRETARY.** **THREAT LEVEL: CLASS X.** **OBJECTIVE: PERFORMANCE REVIEW.**

Dale clicked his pen.

"Let's start," he said.
