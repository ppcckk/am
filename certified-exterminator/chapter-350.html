Chapter 350: Isolate Host

# Chapter 350

The transition was not a fade to black. It was a hard cut, like a power surge tripping a breaker.

Dale blinked. The smell hit him first.

Ozone, stale coffee, and the metallic tang of old filing cabinets.

He wasn't on the cracked asphalt of the parking lot anymore. He was standing on polished linoleum in a room that smelled of bureaucracy.

Fluorescent lights buzzed overhead, one of them flickering with the irritating rhythm of a dying strobe. The walls were lined with beige partitions, each one stacked high with manila folders that had yellowed with age. There were no windows. Just air vents humming with recycled breath and the distant, muffled clatter of keyboards.

He checked his pockets. The black business card was gone. His clipboard remained, though the pages were blank.

"Mr. Kowalski."

The voice came from behind a partition. A woman stood there, holding a stack of papers so high it obscured her face. She wore a suit that looked like it had been ironed in 1985. Her badge read: *Director Vane's Liaison, Level IV Administrative*.

"Where is Vane?" Dale asked. His hand drifted to his belt, searching for his spray nozzle. It wasn't there. His field kit was gone.

"Director Vane is currently under audit," the woman said, lowering the stack to reveal a face devoid of expression. "You have been brought in for a preliminary inquiry regarding your unauthorized interaction with an Auditor Prime. This is a Class IX Infraction."

Dale's jaw tightened. Class IX. That was above his current certification. That was above anyone's certification.

"I was responding to a live threat," Dale said, keeping his voice level. "The drill was active. The threat was imminent. Protocol 7-B states that if a primary containment unit fails, secondary mitigation is authorized."

"Protocol 7-B applies to Class I through IV entities," the woman replied, tapping a pen against the desk.

"Auditor Prime is Class IX. You do not have the clearance to interact with Class IX assets.

You do not have the clearance to *see* Class IX assets.

By looking at it, you have violated the Cognitive Security Act.

"

Dale felt a cold prickle at the base of his neck. He had assumed the Authority operated like a municipal health department. They didn't. They operated like a government agency hiding a massive scandal.

"I identified the weakness," Dale said. "The torque variance in the drill housing. I documented it."

"Documentation is irrelevant if the classification is wrong." The woman pushed a form across the desk. "Sign this.

It acknowledges your error and waives your right to appeal.

If you do not sign, your license is revoked immediately.

Permanently. You will be reassigned to sanitation duty in Sector 7.

"

Dale looked at the form. It was short. Two lines of text. *I acknowledge that my actions constituted a breach of dimensional containment protocols.*

If he signed, he lost his job. His career. His purpose.

If he didn't, he lost his freedom. Probably his mind, given the Cognitive Security clause.

He picked up the pen. Then he set it down.

"Show me the violation report," Dale said.

The Liaison frowned.

"This is an administrative hearing, not a negotiation."

"It's a workplace safety review," Dale corrected. "If I'm being disciplined, I have a right to know which specific code I violated. Was it the interaction? Or was it the observation?"

The Liaison hesitated. The air in the room seemed to thicken. Dust motes danced in the flickering light.

"Observation," she said. "You saw the Prime's core. That is a Class IX visual hazard. Exposure leads to memory fragmentation."

"Then why is it here?" Dale pointed at the stack of files. "If it's a hazard, it should be in containment. You have it sitting on a desk in an open-plan office."

The Liaison's eyes narrowed.

"That is a matter for Internal Affairs."

"It's a matter for me," Dale said.

"Because if that core is exposed, it's leaking. And if it's leaking, it's attracting things. Not just Auditors.

Lower-tier vermin.

Things that feed on static.

"

A sound echoed from the hallway outside. A wet, slapping noise. Like raw meat hitting linoleum.

The Liaison froze.

"That's not possible. The sector is sealed."

Dale listened. The sound came again, closer. *Slap. Drag. Slap.*

"Open the door," Dale said.

"Stay seated."

Dale stood up. He didn't reach for a weapon. He reached for the pen again. But instead of signing the form, he drew a circle around the word *Audit*.

"I'm not signing because I'm guilty," Dale said. "I'm signing because the form is incomplete. It doesn't list the secondary contamination risk. If you let me walk out of here without addressing that, you're liable for the next three breaches."

The Liaison stared at him. The slapping sound grew louder. It was right outside the partition now.

"What do you want?" she whispered.

"Give me my kit," Dale said. "And give me five minutes. If the infestation is below Level II, I'll clean it up and sign your form. If it's above Level II, you arrest me, and you deal with the mess yourself."

The Liaison looked at the door. Then at Dale. She grabbed a key from her pocket and threw it onto the desk.

"If this is a trap, Kowalski, I will ensure you never see sunlight again."

Dale picked up the key. He didn't wait for a response. He walked to the door and unlocked it.

The hallway was long, lit by the same buzzing fluorescents. The walls were covered in motivational posters about synergy and compliance. At the far end, a vent grate had been torn off the wall. Something was crawling out of it.

It looked like a cockroach, but wrong. The legs were too many, and they moved with a jerky, stop-motion rhythm. The body was translucent, revealing pulsing organs inside that glowed with a faint, sickly violet light.

Dale's Field Guide pop-up appeared in the corner of his vision.

**SPECIES:** *Static Mite (Variant)* **CLASS:** III **THREAT:** Medium **WEAKNESS:** Sonic Resonance

He didn't have a sonic emitter. He didn't have a spray. He had a clipboard and a pen.

The mite scuttled faster, sensing the movement. It left a trail of viscous slime on the floor. The slime sizzled where it touched the linoleum, eating away at the wax.

Dale stepped back. He couldn't touch it. Direct contact would contaminate his suit. And he didn't have a suit.

He looked at the ceiling vents. He looked at the fluorescent lights. He looked at the pen in his hand.

The mite lunged.

Dale sidestepped. The creature slammed into the wall, its mandibles clicking against the plaster. It turned, its compound eyes reflecting Dale's face in a hundred distorted shards.

"Protocol 12-A," Dale muttered. "Environmental disruption."

He didn't have a tool. He had an environment.

He looked at the flickering light directly above the mite. The ballast was failing. It was making a low, buzzing hum. Not a clean 60-cycle hum, but a jagged, irregular drone.

Sonic resonance.

Dale walked toward the light fixture, and he reached up and twisted the cover. It was hot. He didn't care.

He pulled the tube out.

The light died. The hum stopped.

The mite froze. It tilted its head, confused by the sudden silence.

Dale didn't stop. He took the glass tube and smashed it against the floor.

It shattered into a thousand pieces.

The mite shrieked, a high-pitched whine that vibrated in Dale's teeth. The broken glass scattered, catching the residual static charge from the ballast.

Dale grabbed a metal chair from a nearby desk. He didn't swing it at the bug. He swung it at the ceiling tile next to the vent.

*Crack.*

The tile fell. Behind it, the wiring was exposed. Live sparks jumped from the severed ends.

Dale kicked the debris toward the mite. The creature recoiled from the sparks, its translucent body rippling. It tried to scurry back into the vent, but Dale kicked a second chair into its path.

The mite hit the chair and slid, losing its footing on the slime. It scrambled, legs flailing, trying to find traction.

Dale picked up a piece of the broken glass tube. It was still warm. Still charged.

He didn't throw it. He dropped it.

The glass landed on top of the mite.

The static discharge hit the creature's shell. The violet light inside flared, then imploded. The mite convulsed, its legs curling inward, and then went still.

Silence returned to the hallway.

Dale stood there, breathing hard. He looked at the dead insect. Then at the broken glass. Then at the open vent.

More slapping sounds were coming from the darkness of the duct.

He pulled out his clipboard. He didn't write a report. He drew a map of the vent system based on the building's layout he'd memorized in the office.

Three vents connected to this corridor. Three entry points.

He needed a containment field. He needed a sealant. He needed help.

But he had one thing. The Liaison was watching.

"Level II?" the Liaison's voice crackled over the intercom system embedded in the wall.

Dale looked up at the speaker.

"Class III. Active infestation. Multiple vectors."

"Your license is suspended," the Liaison said.

"Then I'm not licensed," Dale said. "Which means I'm not bound by the protocol restrictions that stop you from using heavy containment."

He walked to the nearest fire extinguisher. He pulled the pin.

"What are you doing?" the Liaison asked.

"Extinguishing the fire," Dale said. "Before the smoke triggers the sprinklers and washes the nest into the other vents."

He sprayed the extinguisher's contents into the vent opening.

White powder billowed out, coating the ducts.

It wasn't poison. It was dry chemical.

It disrupted the static charge.

The mites in the vent shrieked again. The sound was layered, harmonious, and horrifying.

"Stop!" the Liaison shouted. "You're contaminating the air filtration system!"

"Better air than brains," Dale said.

He moved to the next vent. Spray. Move. Spray.

He worked quickly, methodically. He treated the building like a kitchen.

Seal the entry points.

Remove the food source. Eliminate the pests.

The food source was the static electricity. The pests were the mites.

By the time he reached the third vent, the air in the hallway was thick with white powder. Coughing fitfully, Dale sealed the last opening with a heavy tarp he tore from a supply closet.

He stepped back, covered in white dust. His glasses were fogged.

The silence was total. No slapping. No clicking.

He walked back to the office. The Liaison was standing by the door, her face pale.

"You ruined the HVAC system," she said.

"I saved the mental stability of everyone on this floor," Dale replied.

He walked to the desk. He picked up the form.

He signed it.

Then he picked up the pen and wrote a single line at the bottom: *Violation Code K-9: Failure to maintain static grounding in Class IX proximity zones.*

The Liaison looked at the line. Her eye twitched.

"That's not a real code," she said.

"It is now," Dale said. "And I'm filing a counter-grievance for unsafe work conditions."

He walked toward the elevator. He needed to leave. He needed to get back to the surface. He needed to find out what Vane was hiding.

The elevator doors opened.

But they didn't lead to the lobby, and they led to a dark, stone corridor. The walls were damp. The air smelled of copper and rot.

Standing at the far end of the corridor was a figure. Tall. Thin. Wearing the same suit as the Liaison, but torn.

It was Vane.

But Vane wasn't human. His face was a mask of shifting geometric shapes, constantly rearranging itself.

"Dale," the thing that was Vane said. The voice was the Auditor's voice, but softer. "You solved the problem too easily. You missed the point."

Dale stopped. He gripped the handle of his clipboard.

"What problem?" Dale asked.

"The infestation isn't the mites," Vane said. "The infestation is the Authority itself. And you just invited it into the mainframe."

The floor beneath Dale liquefied. The linoleum turned to black sludge.

Dale jumped, and he landed on the edge of the stone corridor. The sludge bubbled where he had stood.

Vane stepped forward.

The geometric face settled into a human shape. It was Dale's face.

Older.

Tired.

"You thought you were exterminating," Vane said. "You were just pruning. And the Authority doesn't like pruned branches. It likes roots."

Dale looked down at his hand. A black vein was spreading up his wrist.

"Contamination," Vane said. "Class IX. You're already infected."

Dale looked at the black line. It pulsed in time with his heartbeat.

He raised his clipboard. The paper was blank.

But a new entry appeared in the corner of his vision.

**STATUS:** *Carrier* **NEXT STEP:** *Isolate Host*

Dale looked at Vane. Then at the dark corridor behind him.

He didn't run. He didn't fight.

He took out his pen, and he wrote.
