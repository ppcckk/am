Chapter 226: Sign the Waiver

# Chapter 226

The fingers digging into Dale's collar were ice-cold.

Not cold like rain. Cold like a freezer left running with the door open.

Dale didn't scream. He didn't flinch.

He dropped the pry bar.

It clattered against the wet concrete floor, echoing in the cramped maintenance shaft. His hand moved automatically to his chest pocket. Not for a weapon.

For his pen.

"Let go," Dale said.

The face hanging above him smiled. It was a practiced smile. A customer service smile. The kind Dale had perfected over fourteen years of telling angry tenants their roaches were their own fault.

"I said let go," Dale repeated. He kept his voice flat. Monotone. The voice he used for code violations.

The thing's grip tightened. Knuckles whitened.

The skin around the collar button tore.

A small tear in the fabric. A structural failure.

Dale noted it. *Wear and tear. Improper handling.*

"Dale," the thing said. Its voice was his.

Exactly his.

But smoother.

Less raspy from years of shouting over industrial vacuums. "You're contaminating the workspace."

Dale stopped struggling. He went limp. Not surrendering. Testing.

If this was a clone, or a projection, or a hallucination, it would react to physical resistance. If it was a system interface given form, it would react to procedural error.

Dale reached up. He didn't try to break the grip. He pointed a finger at the entity's chest.

"Violation," Dale said.

The smile faltered. Just a fraction. A twitch of the left eye.

"Excuse me?" the entity asked.

"Violation," Dale said again. "You are interfering with a documented extermination site. Section 4, Subsection C: Unauthorized personnel presence during active treatment protocols. Penalty: Immediate suspension of access privileges."

The hand didn't loosen. The face remained upside down, hair dangling into Dale's eyes. It smelled like ozone and old paper.

"You don't have jurisdiction here," the entity said.

"I have a clipboard," Dale said. He gestured vaguely at the empty air where his notebook usually sat. It was gone.

Lost in the chaos of the tunnel collapse. But the Authority knew he had it.

The Authority knew his habits.

The entity tilted its head. "You think rules apply to me?"

"Rules apply to everything," Dale said. "That's the whole point."

A crack formed in the air between them. Not glass. Not light.

A seam in the reality of the tunnel walls.

Black fibers pulsed along the edge of the crack.

The entity's smile widened. It looked tired. Human-tired. Not monster-tired.

"Look at you," the entity said. "Still writing. Still checking boxes. You haven't changed."

"I haven't stopped working," Dale corrected. "That's different."

"Is it?"

The hand released Dale.

He fell backward, landing on his elbows. The pry bar lay two feet away. He didn't grab it.

He sat up slowly, brushing dust off his shirt.

The entity hung from the ceiling for a second, then dropped down.

It landed silently.

It stood up. It was wearing Dale's uniform.

The same stains. The same frayed cuffs. But the ID badge was blank.

"Who are you?" Dale asked.

"I'm the next level," the entity said. "I'm the version of you that passed the exam."

Dale blinked. He pulled a handkerchief from his pocket and wiped his glasses. He checked the lens for scratches. Clean.

"The exam isn't over," Dale said.

"For you, it is," the entity replied. It reached into its pocket. It pulled out a clipboard.

Dale's heart hammered against his ribs. He kept his face neutral. He looked at the clipboard.

It was identical to his. The same battered metal frame. The same clip holding the same multi-forms.

Except the paper on it was black. And the text was glowing red.

"Show me," Dale said.

The entity held it out.

Dale took it. He didn't read the header. He flipped to the back. The final page.

It was his own signature.

But it was dated tomorrow.

"You signed off on your own termination," the entity said. "Standard procedure for Class-IV threats that require recursive cleansing."

Dale stared at the signature. He traced the loop of the 'K' in Kowalski.

"I didn't sign this."

"You will," the entity said. "Or you're a rogue element. And rogue elements get sanitized."

Dale looked up. "Define 'sanitized'."

"Erased," the entity said. "Scrubbed. Deleted from the Field Guide."

A cold wind swept through the tunnel. The black fibers on the walls writhed.

They weren't just map patterns anymore.

They were reaching. Tendrils of shadow grasping at the air.

Dale stepped back. He picked up the pry bar. He didn't raise it. He used it to prop open a nearby electrical panel.

"System," Dale said aloud. "Query: Current Certification Status."

The entity laughed. It was a dry, hacking sound.

"The System is offline, Dale. You know that."

"Query: Cross-contamination penalty for unlicensed entities," Dale continued. He ignored the entity. He spoke to the air.

The entity's smile dropped. "Stop."

"Query," Dale said. He raised his voice. "Define 'Unlicensed Entity' under Protocol 7-B."

Silence.

Then, a chime.

It wasn't in his head. It was in the walls. The black fibers vibrated. A low hum resonated through the concrete.

The entity took a step forward. "You're trying to trick the Authority."

"I'm filing a report," Dale said.

He pulled a pen from his pocket. The ink was running.

He scribbled on the back of a torn label from his belt. *Unlicensed duplicate detected.

Requesting classification.*

He held the label up.

The entity lunged.

Dale didn't move. He didn't dodge. He held the label out like a shield.

The entity froze. Inches from Dale's face.

A text box appeared in the air. Not in Dale's peripheral vision. Right in front of his nose.

**ERROR: DUPLICATE ENTRY DETECTED.**

**STATUS: CONFLICT.**

**ACTION REQUIRED: MERGE OR PURGE.**

The entity's eyes widened. "No."

Dale looked at the text. Then he looked at the entity.

"Merge," Dale said.

The entity shook its head violently. "No.

You don't understand.

If you merge, you lose the separation.

You lose the distinction between the technician and the tool."

"I'm the tool," Dale said. "Always have been."

"No," the entity hissed. "You're the user. I'm the interface. We're supposed to be separate."

"Separation causes inefficiency," Dale said. He tapped the pry bar against the ground. *Clack. Clack. Clack.* A rhythmic, annoying noise.

The entity gritted its teeth. "You're provoking me."

"I'm documenting my workspace," Dale said. "And your workspace is cluttered."

The entity raised its hand. Black light coiled around its fingers. It wasn't magic.

It was code. Raw, uncompiled code trying to rewrite reality.

Dale watched the black light. He recognized the pattern. It was a frass trail. A sign of infestation.

"Violation," Dale said. "Hazardous material release without proper containment."

The entity screamed. It wasn't a human scream. It was the sound of a hard drive failing.

The black light exploded outward.

Dale threw himself to the side. He rolled behind a stack of concrete pipes. Debris rained down. Dust choked the air.

He coughed. He wiped his mouth. He stood up.

The tunnel was silent.

The entity was gone.

But the black fibers remained. They had spread. They covered the walls, the floor, the ceiling. They pulsed in time with Dale's heartbeat.

And in the center of the room, where the entity had stood, lay the clipboard.

Dale walked over. He picked it up.

The black paper was gone.

In its place was a single sheet of standard inspection form.

The header read: **CERTIFICATION EXAM: PRACTICAL PORTION.**

Question 1: *Define the difference between extermination and eradication.*

Dale stared at the paper.

He looked at the walls. The black fibers were receding. Pulling back into the cracks. Like a tide going out.

His phone buzzed.

It was impossible. The signal had been severed.

He pulled it out.

One notification.

**SYSTEM RESTORED.**

**NEW PROTOCOL UNLOCKED: SPLIT-SECOND IDENTIFICATION.**

**DESCRIPTION: Allows instantaneous comparison of two similar entities to determine canonical status. Cost: 5 Treatment Points per use.**

Dale frowned. He hadn't spent any points. He didn't have enough points for that.

He looked at the clipboard again.

At the bottom of the form, in small print:

**PENALTY FOR FAILURE: ASSIMILATION.**

A voice came from behind him.

"Nice trick."

Dale turned.

Standing in the shadow of the tunnel entrance was a woman.

She was holding a clipboard.

She was wearing a Class III uniform.

And she had Dale's face.

But she was older. Much older. Her hair was gray. Her eyes were tired.

She walked into the light.

"You bought yourself five minutes," she said. "Maybe less."

Dale checked his Field Guide.

*Status: Online.*

*Connection: Re-established.*

*New Entry: Recursive Authority Agent (Female Variant).*

*Weakness: Unknown.*

He looked at the woman.

"Who are you?" Dale asked.

"I'm the one who failed," she said. She tapped her clipboard. "Twice."

She handed him a pen.

"Write," she said. "We don't have time for the written exam."

Dale took the pen. "What's the practical?"

The woman pointed to the ceiling.

The black fibers were gathering again. Forming a shape. A door.

"The door leads to the Source," she said. "You have to close it."

"How?"

"You have to identify it," the woman said. "Correctly."

Dale looked at the door.

It wasn't made of wood or metal. It was made of faces. Thousands of them.

Screaming.

Silent. Frozen in agony.

He recognized one.

It was the neighbor from Chapter 1. The one who screamed about demons.

"Violation," Dale said. "Unapproved entry point."

The woman nodded. "Correct. But you can't just close it. You have to certify it."

"Certify a door?"

"Certify the infestation," the woman said.

"Treat it as a Class-IV breach. Requires full documentation. Photos.

Samples. Witness statements."

Dale looked at his phone. No camera.

He looked at his belt. No sample kit.

He looked at the faces.

"I need a witness," Dale said.

The woman smiled. It was a sad smile.

"I'm right here."

Dale looked at her. looked at her.

She wasn't a clone. She wasn't a projection.

She was a technician.

She was him.

But different.

"Write this down," Dale said. He spoke slowly. Dictating.

*Subject: Dimensional Breach, Sub-level 4.*

*Observation: Recursive entity detected. Identification: Self.*

*Status: Contained.*

*Action: Initiate Protocol: Mirror Check.*

The woman raised an eyebrow. "Mirror Check? That's not a standard protocol."

"It is now," Dale said.

He raised his pry bar.

He didn't aim at the woman.

He aimed at the black fibers on the wall.

"Dale," the woman said. "What are you doing?"

"Isolating the vector," Dale said.

He swung.

The pry bar connected with the wall.

The fibers snapped.

A shockwave rippled through the tunnel.

The faces on the door screamed.

The woman vanished.

Dale stood alone in the dark.

His phone buzzed again.

**TREATMENT POINTS AWARDED: 50.**

**CERTIFICATION LEVEL INCREASED: II TO III.**

**NEW ABILITY UNLOCKED: PARADOX SEAL.**

**DESCRIPTION: Allows user to seal an entry point by recognizing the contradiction within its existence.**

**WARNING: Using this ability creates a temporal paradox. Side effects may include memory loss, identity drift, or sudden appearance of additional technicians.**

Dale looked at the wall. The crack was closed.

But on the floor, where the woman had stood, lay a new item.

A business card.

It was white.

The text on it read:

**DALE KOWALSKI** **LICENSED PEST CONTROL TECHNICIAN (CLASS IV)** **CONSULTING SERVICES FOR EXISTENTIAL THREATS.**

**CALL TO SCHEDULE A FREE ESTIMATE.**

Dale picked it up.

He turned it over.

On the back, in his own handwriting:

*Don't let them hire you.*

A hand grabbed Dale's shoulder.

He spun around.

Another face.

Same face.

Different uniform.

This one was blue.

"Mr. Kowalski?" the blue uniform said. "We received a complaint about noise. Are you the one doing the extermination?"

Dale looked at the blue uniform.

He looked at the black fibers, still pulsing in the shadows.

He looked at the business card in his hand.

He smiled.

"Yeah," Dale said. "I'm the one doing the work."

He held out the card.

"But I'm going to need you to sign this waiver first. Before I start."

The blue uniform hesitated.

"Why?"

"Because," Dale said, "I'm charging you for the cleanup."

The blue uniform's eyes narrowed.

The air grew cold.

Dale clicked his pen.

"Name and address, please."
