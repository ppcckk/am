Chapter 154: License Suspended

# Chapter 154

The clipboard hit Inspector Vance's nose with a wet, decisive crack.

Not a weapon. A tool.

Vance staggered back, blinking. The golden rod clattered to the stone floor, its light flickering like a dying fluorescent bulb. Behind him, Marcus Thorne flinched, hands raised in a gesture that was half surrender, half apology.

Dale didn't breathe. He didn't blink. He stared at the red text burning in his peripheral vision.

**VIOLATION DETECTED.** **ACTION: OBSTRUCTION OF AUTHORITY INSPECTOR.** **SEVERITY: CRITICAL.** **PENALTY: LICENSURE SUSPENSION PENDING REVIEW.**

The suspension notice was the real problem. Without a license, Dale had no access to the Field Guide. No access to the Guide meant no targeted damage. No targeted damage meant he was just a man in a dirty coveralls holding a pen against a man who could level a city block with a wave of his hand.

But Dale had written one word on that page. *Loop hole.*

He hadn't written it in the Field Guide. He had written it on his personal inspection log.

The Authority processed actions based on context. An attack was an attack. A document submission was a submission.

Vance wiped blood from his upper lip. The anger vanished from his face, replaced by a cold, bureaucratic confusion. He looked at the clipboard lying on the ground. Then he looked at Dale.

"You threw a filing apparatus," Vance said. His voice was smooth, practiced.

"That is not an act of war, Mr.

Kowalski. That is an act of... disorder."

Disorder was a code. K-11. Failure to maintain workspace standards.

Dale kicked the clipboard. It slid across the wet stone, stopping at Vance's polished shoes.

"Page four," Dale said.

His voice was flat. Dry.

"Subsection C.

Submission of counter-evidence during active examination."

Vance hesitated. He glanced down. He didn't pick it up. Instead, a faint green shimmer rippled around the clipboard.

**DOCUMENT RECEIVED.** **STATUS: PENDING REVIEW.** **EXAMINATION PAUSED.**

The red text in Dale's vision flickered. The *HUNT OR BE HUNTED* alert remained, but the timer stopped counting down.

Thorne let out a breath. "He bought you time. Don't waste it."

Vance stepped over the clipboard. He didn't bend down.

He didn't care about the paper.

He cared about the hierarchy.

But the pause was real. The Authority's algorithms required human review for procedural anomalies.

And Vance, for all his power, was bound by the rules just as Dale was.

"Five minutes," Vance said. "Review your files, Technician. If the anomaly is invalid, I will terminate you before the clock resets."

He turned and walked back into the shadows of the tunnel. Thorne stayed. He looked tired. He looked like he hadn't slept since the Rift opened.

"You can't run," Thorne said. "Vance isn't chasing you. He's auditing you."

Dale looked at his hands. They were shaking.

He clenched them into fists until the tremors stopped.

He picked up his pen. The ink was low. He tapped the cap against his thigh to check the flow.

It worked.

"I need to know what I released," Dale said.

Thorne grimaced. "It wasn't in your Guide.

You couldn't ID it.

You sprayed the lubricant because the tunnel was slick. You thought it was a moisture issue. It wasn't moisture."

"It was a sealant," Dale said. The realization hit him harder than the clipboard had hit Vance.

"The entity wasn't trapped.

It was held together by friction.

My spray broke the seal."

Thorne nodded slowly. "The Authority doesn't punish for accidents. It punishes for failure to identify.

You guessed. That's a Class IV negligence charge on top of the felony."

Dale closed his eyes for a second.

He could feel the 40% debuff sitting on his shoulders, a heavy, invisible weight. *Contaminated Field Guide.* Every entry was scrambled. He couldn't look up weaknesses. He couldn't look up species traits.

He was flying blind.

But he wasn't blind. He was a technician.

Technicians didn't rely on memory. They relied on samples.

Dale walked to the spot where the entity had been. The floor was wet, but not with water.

It smelled like ozone and rotting kelp.

He pulled a plastic sample bag from his belt. He had three left. He used one on the residue on the wall.

He scraped the slime with a putty knife.

**SAMPLE COLLECTED: UNKNOWN BIO-RESIDUE.** **ANALYSIS PENDING.**

He moved to the next patch.

He scraped.

He bagged. He labeled. *Location: Tunnel Junction 4B.

Depth: 12 meters. Surface: Calcified Mucus.*

Thorne watched him. "You're wasting time."

"I'm building a file," Dale said. He didn't look up.

"Vance is an Elite Inspector.

He works from the top down. He assumes the infestation is obvious. He assumes the hazard is standard.

He doesn't look at the dirt."

Dale moved to the center of the room. There was a crack in the floor. A small one. He shined his flashlight into it.

Darkness. Then, a glint.

Something metallic. Not part of the tunnel.

He crouched. The debuff made his muscles ache.

It felt like moving through molasses.

He forced his hand into the crack. The stone bit into his fingers. He grabbed the object.

It was a tag. A small, copper tag, stamped with a symbol he didn't recognize. A spiral with three dots.

He turned it over.

**ITEM IDENTIFIED: BAIT MARKER.** **ORIGIN: PRE-RIFT INDUSTRIAL SITE 7.** **NOT A NATURAL ENTITY.**

The text in his vision changed. It wasn't red anymore. It was yellow. Warning, but not violation.

**LOGIC ERROR: ENTITY CLASSIFICATION MISMATCH.** **CORRECTION PROTOCOL AVAILABLE.** **COST: 50 TREATMENT POINTS.**

Dale didn't have 50 points. He had been spending everything on survival. He looked at Thorne.

"Do you have points?"

Thorne blinked. "What?"

"Transfer points. From your account to mine. I need to correct the classification error before Vance finishes his review. If I don't, the system will default to the felony charge."

Thorne's eyes went wide. "You're asking me to bribe the Authority."

"I'm asking you to pay for a correction. It's a service fee."

Thorne looked at the tunnel entrance, then back at Dale. He looked at the clipboard still lying on the ground.

He walked over and picked it up.

He flipped to page four.

He looked at the word *LOophOLE*.

He snorted. A dry, bitter sound.

"Fine," Thorne said. He pulled a small card from his pocket. He tapped it against Dale's wrist.

A chime sounded.

**TRANSFER COMPLETE.** **50 TP RECEIVED.**

Dale didn't hesitate. He focused on the yellow text.

**ACCEPT CORRECTION?** **[Y/N]**

He thought of the entity. The way it had moved. The way it had folded.

It wasn't a monster. It was a piece of machinery that had gone organic.

Or an organism that had been mechanized.

The bait marker proved it. It had been lured here. Planted here.

**YES.**

The red text vanished.

The 40% debuff lifted. The weight on his shoulders disappeared.

He took a deep breath.

The air tasted clearer.

**FIELD GUIDE RECALIBRATING...** **ENTRY UPDATED: MECHANICAL PARASITE (Variant 7).** **WEAKNESS IDENTIFIED: POWER COUPLING LOCATED AT POSTERIOR JOINTS.**

He had his weakness. But he didn't have his combat status back. He was still on trial.

The five minutes were almost up.

Vance emerged from the shadows. He didn't walk. He glided. The golden rod was gone, but the air around him hummed with static.

"Your submission was... irregular," Vance said. He didn't sound angry.

He sounded bored.

"But acceptable.

The Authority recognizes procedural creativity."

"Creativity is a code violation," Dale said.

"Only if it causes harm," Vance replied. He pointed at the floor. "You released the entity.

It is currently in Sector 9. It has begun to reproduce."

Reproduce?

Dale looked at his updated Field Guide. **MECHANICAL PARASITE.** No reproduction stats listed.

Thorne went pale. "Sector 9 is the water treatment plant."

"If it gets into the intake," Vance said, "it will contaminate the municipal supply. Every household in the county will be exposed to its spores. That is a mass casualty event. You caused it."

"I documented it," Dale said. "I collected the sample. I corrected the log."

"Documentation after the fact is not prevention," Vance said.

He raised his hand. The air in the room grew heavy. "I am hereby revoking your temporary license.

You are under arrest for negligence of the highest order.

You will accompany me to the Holding Cells."

Two guards stepped out of the dark. They weren't human. They were constructs. Stone golems with Authority sigils glowing on their chests.

Dale looked at the guards. He looked at Vance.

He looked at his clipboard.

He had one move left.

He picked up the clipboard again. He didn't throw it. He held it up. He pointed to the bottom of page four.

"Read the footer," Dale said.

Vance frowned. He squinted.

The text was small. Written in Dale's cramped, neat handwriting.

**SUBMITTER: DALE KOWALSKI.** **CLASS: LICENSED TECHNICIAN.** **PROTOCOL INVOKED: PRE-EXISTING LIABILITY WAIVER.**

Vance's eyes widened. "What waiver?"

"Standard municipal code," Dale said. "Section 4, Paragraph 12. If a technician identifies a hazard that falls under 'Pre-Existing Infrastructure Failure,' the liability shifts to the municipal owner. The technician is exempt from immediate termination if they can prove the hazard was planted."

Vance stared at him. "You're claiming the infestation was planted?"

"I have the bait marker," Dale said.

He held up the copper tag. "It's stamped with the logo of Apex Industries. A private contractor.

Not a wild monster. Not a rift spawn.

A commercial product."

The room went silent.

Apex Industries.

The company that had built the Rift containment grid. The company that had paid for the initial surveys.

The company that Vance worked for? Or worked with?

Vance's face tightened. The golden light in his eyes dimmed.

"That is a serious accusation, Technician."

"It's in the file," Dale said. "I filed it.

You reviewed it.

You accepted the transfer of points to correct the classification. By accepting the correction, you acknowledged the data source."

The system pinged.

**ACCUSATION FILED: EXTERNAL INFLUENCE ON INFESTATION.** **INVESTIGATION REQUIRED.** **DEFENDANT STATUS: HELD PENDING HEARING.**

Vance looked at the guards. The golems hesitated. Their instructions were to arrest Dale for negligence. But if the negligence was mitigated by external sabotage, the protocol required a pause.

"You are delaying your own execution," Vance said quietly.

"I'm doing my job," Dale said. "I'm identifying the source."

Thorne grabbed Dale's arm. "Walk. Now. The pause won't last."

Dale walked.

He didn't run. He kept his posture upright.

He kept his clipboard in his hand.

He kept the copper tag in his pocket.

They exited the tunnel. They emerged into the gray light of the morning. The suburban street was empty. The Rift was still open above them, a jagged tear in the sky that leaked purple mist.

But something had changed.

The mist wasn't just hanging there. It was moving. It was flowing toward the water treatment plant in the distance.

And from the sewers, a sound rose. Not a roar. A hum. A low, mechanical grinding sound.

**SYSTEM ALERT: MASS INFESTATION EVENT IMMINENT.** **THREAT LEVEL: CLASS V.** **RECOMMENDED ACTION: EVACUATION.**

Dale looked at the notification. He looked at Thorne.

"We have to go to the plant," Thorne said. "If we don't, they'll pin the whole thing on us. Even with the waiver, if the water turns toxic, you're still the technician on site."

"I need to inspect it," Dale said.

Thorne laughed. It was a manic, jagged sound. "You're insane. You just got away with a felony, and you want to walk into a Class V zone?"

"I don't want to walk into it," Dale said. He pulled out his pen. He opened his notebook to a fresh page. "I want to document it."

He wrote the date. He wrote the time. He wrote the location.

Then he wrote the first line of his report.

**SUBJECT: APEX INDUSTRIES CONTAINMENT FAILURE.** **STATUS: ACTIVE LEAK.** **ACTION REQUIRED: SHUTDOWN PROTOCOL ALPHA.**

He looked up at the purple mist. He looked at the distant silhouette of the water treatment plant.

And then he saw it.

A second figure. Standing on the roof of the plant.

Small. Distant. But unmistakable.

It was wearing a white suit.

Vance wasn't on the street. Vance was already there.

And he wasn't alone.

Surrounding the plant, hundreds of black shapes were crawling out of the drains. They weren't rats. They weren't beetles.

They were gears. Teeth. Chains. Living, grinding metal.

Dale's Field Guide popped open. The entry for **MECHANICAL PARASITE** flashed red.

**ERROR: SWARM DETECTED.** **TARGET COUNT: EXCEEDS LICENSE CAPACITY.** **SUGGESTED ACTION: RETREAT.**

Dale didn't retreat. He looked at Thorne.

"Give me your phone," Dale said.

"Why?"

"I need to livestream the inspection."

Thorne stared at him. "If you broadcast this, the Authority will shut you down. They'll erase your existence."

"The Authority is a bureaucracy," Dale said. He held out his hand.

"Bureaucracies hate public scrutiny.

If I document this failure in real-time, they can't bury it.

They have to respond."

Thorne handed over the phone.

Dale opened the camera. He hit record.

He pointed it at the swarm. He pointed it at the plant. He pointed it at the white figure on the roof.

"This is Dale Kowalski, Licensed Pest Control Technician, Class II," he said. His voice was steady. "I am currently conducting an on-site investigation of a suspected corporate sabotage event involving Apex Industries. The following footage serves as primary evidence for a Class V Infestation Report."

He looked into the lens.

"Please note," he said, "that this infestation is not natural. It is man-made. And the man who made it is watching."

He pointed the camera at the figure on the roof.

The figure raised a hand. Not in surrender. In a wave.

Then the phone in Dale's hand exploded.
