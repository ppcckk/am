Chapter 124: Level Two Inspection

# Chapter 124

The hand did not have fingers.

It had segments. Articulated, chitinous plates interlocking with the wet, sickening precision of a hinge. The joint popped, a sound like a dry twig snapping under a boot, as the appendage flexed. It was grey, identical to the substance covering Gerald's face, but it moved with an intelligence that the grey did not possess.

Dale stepped back. His boot scraped against the linoleum, leaving a smear of black sludge.

The sphere's crack widened. The edges curled back like burnt paper. The red glow pulsed faster, syncing with the rhythm of Dale's own heart, though he kept his breathing steady through the filtered air of his respirator.

**SYSTEM NOTICE:** **UNREGISTERED ENTITY DETECTED.** **SPECIES UNKNOWN.** **CLASSIFICATION: PENDING.**

The prompt hung in the air, translucent and mocking. Dale ignored it.

He looked at the hand.

He looked at the clipboard in his left hand, the blue ink still wet on the paper. He looked at Halloway, who had backed himself against the doorframe, his face a mask of pale sweat.

"Write it down," Halloway whispered. His voice cracked. "Dale, write it down."

"I'm writing it down," Dale said.

He didn't look up. He dipped the tip of the ballpoint into the residual red blood on the page, creating a slurry of ink and gore.

He wrote: *Unidentified Appendage.

Chitinous. Jointed.*

The hand reached for Gerald's face. It didn't grab.

It pressed.

The grey substance on Gerald's skin rippled, reacting to the touch.

The hand was trying to pull the grey off the man, or perhaps merge with it. It was difficult to tell.

The boundary between victim and object had dissolved.

Dale moved forward. One step. Then another.

"Dale, stop!" Halloway shouted. "It's got him! It's got him inside!"

"It's got him filed," Dale corrected. He stopped two feet from the sphere. He raised his clipboard. "Observe the interaction."

The hand paused. The red glow dimmed, then flared brighter. The sphere emitted a low, vibrating hum that vibrated in Dale's molars.

**FIELD GUIDE ENTRY CREATED:** **SUBJECT: The Archive Hand.** **HABITAT:** Rift Proximity. **BEHAVIOR:** Extraction/Integration. **WEAKNESS:** None recorded. **CROSS-CONTAMINATION WARNING:** Using Class I Vermin protocols on this entity will result in immediate systemic failure.

Dale read the entry. He read it twice.

He had never seen a weakness listed as *None recorded* before.

Usually, the System provided a hint. A vulnerability to salt, to fire, to sound. Here, there was only silence.

He switched his grip on the pen. He tapped the clipboard against his thigh, a rhythmic *thump-thump-thump*.

"The hand is an interface," Dale said, not looking at Halloway. "It's not an attack vector. It's a retrieval tool."

Halloway lowered his weapon, a rusty pipe wrapped in duct tape. "What?"

"Think about the job site," Dale said, his voice flat, procedural.

"We find a nest. We don't just spray.

We map the entry points.

We identify the pheromone trails. This thing..." He gestured with the pen toward the sphere. "This thing is an entry point.

And it's trying to pull something out of the substrate."

"The substrate is Gerald," Halloway said, horrified.

"The substrate is the Rift," Dale said. "Gerald is the contamination."

The hand moved again. This time, it dug into the grey mass. Gerald's mouth opened, silent, the jaw unhinging slightly. The grey substance drained away from his cheeks, flowing into the hollows of the hand's fingers.

**THREAT ASSESSMENT UPDATE:** **INTEGRATION IN PROGRESS.** **ESTIMATED TIME TO TOTAL ASSIMILATION: 4 MINUTES.** **RECOMMENDED ACTION: SEVERANCE.**

Severance.

The word hung there.

Dale knew the protocol. Severance was used for deep-rooted structural infestations, like termites in load-bearing beams.

You cut the connection. You isolate the damage. You treat the remaining structure.

But you couldn't sever a person from a monster if the monster was inside them.

Unless you treated the person as a unit.

Dale looked at Halloway. "Do you have the sealant? The industrial grade?"

Halloway blinked. "The epoxy? It's in the truck."

"Get it."

"It's glue, Dale! You can't glue a monster!"

"It's not glue. It's a void-fill compound.

It stops air exchange.

It stops nutrient flow." Dale pointed the pen at the sphere. "The hand is pulling nutrients. It's feeding.

If we block the flow, the integration fails."

Halloway stared at him. Then, understanding dawned in his eyes.

Not admiration.

Panic. But a functional panic. He ran out of the room.

Dale stayed. He watched the hand. He watched Gerald.

The grey was receding faster now. Gerald's eyes were visible again, clouded with cataracts of the Rift substance. The hand was full of the grey, pulsing with a light that was no longer red but a sickly yellow.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** **FIELD GUIDE ENTRY UPDATED.** **SUBJECT: The Archive Hand.** **WEAKNESS:** Metabolic Dependency. **NOTE:** Entity requires constant influx of biological matter to maintain cohesion. Starvation causes structural collapse.

A loophole. Dale's jaw tightened.

The System rewarded observation.

He had observed the flow.

He had identified the dependency.

Now he had to execute.

Halloway returned, breathless, carrying a bucket of the thick, grey sludge used to patch foundation cracks. It smelled of ammonia and old sweat.

"Pour it," Dale said.

"Where?"

"On the connection point." Dale pointed to where the hand met Gerald's face. "Create a barrier. Seal the interface."

Halloway hesitated. "That'll suffocate him."

"Gerald is already dead," Dale said, and the words tasted like ash in his mouth. "He was filed the moment he touched the sphere. We are treating the infestation. We are not performing triage."

Halloway nodded. He raised the bucket.

The sphere shrieked. A high-pitched frequency that shattered the lightbulb above them. Darkness fell, broken only by the red glow and the yellow pulse of the hand.

Halloway threw the epoxy.

It splattered across the interface, thick and viscous. The hand jerked back. The yellow light flickered. The grey substance trapped beneath the sealant bubbled, turning black.

Gerald's body convulsed.

**ACTION LOGGED:** **ISOLATION PROTOCOL INITIATED.** **SEVERANCE ATTEMPTED.** **OUTCOME: UNKNOWN.**

The hand spasmed. The chitinous fingers curled inward, crushing the epoxy seal. It punched through the barrier, tearing a hole in the grey mass.

But the flow stopped. The yellow light dimmed.

The sphere groaned. The cracks spread wider. The hand withdrew, dragging a clump of the grey substance with it. It retreated into the fissure.

The red glow faded. The sphere went dark. The cracks sealed themselves, knitting together with a sound like wet gravel grinding.

Gerald lay on the floor. He was still. The grey was gone from his face, leaving behind raw, red skin that looked like it had been flayed.

Silence rushed back into the room, heavy and suffocating.

Dale lowered his clipboard. His hands were shaking. He hid them by pressing the clipboard against his chest.

Halloway walked over to Gerald. He checked for a pulse. He waited. He checked again.

"He's not breathing," Halloway said.

Dale nodded. He pulled a fresh sheet of paper from the dispenser in his vest. He wrote.

**INCIDENT REPORT: 124-B.** **LOCATION:** Suburban Ohio, Kitchen Sector 4. **SUBJECT:** Gerald M. (Deceased). **INFESTATION TYPE:** Dimensional Archive (Class III). **PROTOCOL EXECUTED:** Isolation and Severance. **THOROUGHNESS RATING:** 4.5.

"Four point five?" Halloway asked, his voice hollow. "We saved the room. We stopped the spread."

"We didn't identify the source," Dale said, not looking up. "We didn't document the full lifecycle. We applied a patch, not a cure. The Authority penalizes speed over precision."

"We survived!"

"Survival is not the metric," Dale said. He signed the form. He stamped it with the digital seal on his wristband. **CERTIFIED EXTERMINATOR (LEVEL IV).**

The stamp glowed green. Then it flickered. Then it turned red.

**CERTIFICATION REVOKED.** **REASON: CROSS-CONTAMINATION PENALTY.** **DETAILS: Use of Void-Fill Compound on Class III Entity constitutes improper protocol application. Field Guide entry compromised for 24 hours.**

Dale stared at the red text.

His Level IV status was gone. He was back to Level I. His Field Guide was locked.

He couldn't use his targeted damage bonuses.

He was vulnerable.

Halloway saw the change. He looked at Dale, then at the sealed sphere.

"You lost it," Halloway said. "You lost your rank."

Dale wiped the blood off his pen. He capped it. He put it back in his pocket.

"I lost the rank," Dale said. "I kept the data."

He looked at the sphere.

It was quiet. Too quiet. The epoxy seal was hardening, turning a solid, unnatural black.

It looked like a scar.

But scars can reopen.

A sound came from the sphere. Not a hum. A knock.

*Knock. Knock. Knock.*

Three distinct, rhythmic raps against the inside of the metal.

Halloway froze. "Who is knocking?"

Dale didn't answer. He picked up his clipboard. He walked to the sphere. He placed his hand on the cold metal, right over the black seal.

The knocking stopped.

Then, a voice came through the metal. It was muffled, distorted, but clear.

"Dale Kowalski."

It wasn't Gerald's voice. It was Dale's.

"Dale Kowalski," the voice said again. "You have been flagged."

**SYSTEM ALERT:** **UNAUTHORIZED ACCESS DETECTED.** **LICENSE STATUS: SUSPENDED.** **NEXT EXAMINATION: MANDATORY.** **TIME LIMIT: 12 HOURS.** **FAILURE CONSEQUENCE: TERMINATION OF CONTRACT.**

Dale pulled his hand back. He looked at Halloway.

"They're calling me in for the exam," Dale said. "Now."

"Here?" Halloway asked, gesturing to the bloodied kitchen. "In the middle of a job?"

"The Authority doesn't respect job sites," Dale said.

He ripped the page off his clipboard.

He folded it. He put it in his pocket.

"The Authority respects results. And we just filed a Class III."

He walked to the door. He opened it.

Outside, the sky was not black. It was a swirling vortex of grey and red, mirroring the sphere inside. The suburban neighborhood was gone. In its place was a landscape of twisted metal and pulsing organic matter, stretching to the horizon.

And standing in the driveway, waiting, was a figure. It was tall, wearing a suit that looked like it had been made of skin. It held a clipboard. It held a pen.

It was Dale.

Or something that had learned to be Dale.

The doppelgänger raised its pen.

**WELCOME TO LEVEL II INSPECTION,** the System announced. **BEGIN.**
