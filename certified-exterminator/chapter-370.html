Chapter 370: No Tools No History

# Chapter 370

The clipboard in Dale's hand was no longer paper. It had calcified into a slab of obsidian, heavy and cold against his palms. The pen, still a standard BIC ballpoint, now dragged across the surface with a sound like grinding stone.

Across the widening rift, the figure in the suit adjusted its tie. It did not blink. Its fingers, too long and possessing too many joints, tapped a rhythm against the desk behind it. Files floated in the air around the creature, forming a slow, orbiting galaxy of paper.

Dale did not run. Running triggered the chase instinct of the high-tier entities. He clicked his pen twice. The sound echoed in the sudden vacuum of silence.

*SYSTEM ALERT: RIVAL DETECTED. THE ARCHIVIST (CLASS X). STATUS: HOSTILE.*

The text hovered in the corner of his vision, pulsing with a bruised purple light. It was not a notification. It was a threat assessment.

Dale stepped forward. One foot.

Then the other.

The ground beneath him was no longer asphalt. It was a lattice of black glass, thin and translucent, showing the swirling void beneath.

The Archnivist leaned forward. Its smile stretched wider than a human jaw should allow.

"Mr.

Kowalski," the entity said. Its voice did not come from a mouth.

It vibrated through the air, bypassing ears and resonating directly in the chest cavity.

"You have an outstanding balance of unresolved citations."

Dale ignored the voice. He looked at the floating files.

He looked at the hand that had pulled the rift open.

He looked at the ring on the finger, which was gold. Tarnished.

The insignia of the County Licensing Authority, Audit Division, Pre-Split era.

He raised his clipboard. He wrote a single word in the header: *VIOLATION.*

The Archnivist tilted its head. A file detached itself from the orbit and flew toward Dale. It moved with the speed of a thrown knife.

Dale didn't dodge. He sidestepped. The file smashed into the black glass where he had been standing, shattering into a thousand shards of paper that dissolved into gray dust.

"Unauthorized retrieval of classified records," Dale said. His voice was steady.

Dry, and he sounded like he was reading a meter reading.

"Section 4, Paragraph B. Prohibition of non-licensed entities accessing Authority archives."

The Archnivist laughed.

It was a wet, clicking sound.

"You are a pest control technician.

You do not audit.

You exterminate.

"

"Extermination requires identification," Dale replied. He pointed his pen at the creature. "I am identifying you now."

He opened his Field Guide. The interface had changed. The usual neat columns were gone, replaced by a chaotic scramble of red text. The entry for *The Archnivist* was blank.

*SYSTEM MESSAGE: FIELD GUIDE ENTRY CORRUPTED. CROSS-CONTAMINATION RISK DETECTED. DO NOT ENGAGE WITH CLASS I PROTOCOLS.*

Dale felt the familiar itch in his palms. The contamination anxiety spiked.

He wanted to step back, and he wanted to clean his hands.

But the ground was shifting. The rift was widening.

If he stepped back, the void would expand, and the county would lose another mile of reality.

He had to treat the nest, and he had to document the threat.

He looked at the Archnivist's suit. It was made of shadows, yes, but the fabric had a weave. He could see the loose threads. He could see the frayed edges where the shadow met the light of the office.

"Your structural integrity is failing," Dale observed. "Visible degradation at the collar and cuffs. This suggests poor maintenance of containment protocols."

The Archnivist's smile faltered. Just for a second. The joints in its fingers stiffened.

"You speak of rules," the entity said. It stood up.

It unfolded, which was taller than it had appeared sitting down. Nearly nine feet.

"I *am* the rules."

"No," Dale said. "You are a code violation."

He clicked his pen again.

This time, he didn't write. He activated a protocol.

Not a combat protocol.

A containment protocol.

*PROTOCOL: SILENT SEALANT (LEVEL IV).*

This was usually used for sealing cracks in residential foundations. It involved creating a temporary barrier of hardened resin to stop moisture intrusion.

Dale aimed the clipboard at the floor. He focused on the black glass lattice beneath the Archnivist's feet. He visualized the resin. He poured his intent into the system.

A thin line of translucent amber light shot from the tip of his pen. It hit the ground and solidified instantly, forming a perfect circle around the entity's feet.

The Archnivist looked down. It stepped forward. The barrier held. The shadow-suit rippled against the amber wall.

"Interesting," the entity murmured. "You attempt to cage me."

"Containment is the first step to elimination," Dale said. He moved his pen to the side.

He drew a second circle.

Then a third, and he was building a maze. A simple, geometric maze of hard light.

The Archnivist laughed again. It raised a hand. The files around it swirled faster. They became projectiles.

*SYSTEM ALERT: MULTIPLE HOSTILES DETECTED. DOCUMENTED FILES. CLASS: MEMETIC HAZARD.*

The files flew. They weren't paper. They were memories.

Specific, bureaucratic memories. Dale saw a vision of himself at age twenty, failing his first inspection.

He saw a vision of his supervisor yelling at him for a missed frass trail.

He saw a vision of the day the rifts first opened, and he hadn't done anything.

The visions hit him. They burned.

Dale staggered.

His knees buckled. The anxiety spiked.

He felt the urge to vomit, and the contamination was in his head now.

He squeezed his eyes shut. He breathed in for four seconds.

Out for four.

In for four.

Out for four.

*OSHA Protocol 12: Stress Management in High-Hazard Zones.*

He opened his eyes. The visions were gone. But the damage was done.

His Field Guide flickered. The entry for *Dimensional Vermin* turned gray.

The entry for *Acid Beetles* vanished entirely.

*PENALTY: FIELD GUIDE CORRUPTION. 30% LOSS OF DATA INTEGRITY.*

Dale wiped his mouth with the back of his hand. He looked at the Archnivist. The entity was stepping out of the first circle. It was breaking the seal.

"You cannot contain truth," the Archnivist said. It snapped its fingers. The amber walls shattered.

Dale didn't retreat. He advanced.

This was the loophole. The one the Authority hadn't mentioned in the manual.

Certification Level I through IX were for exterminating monsters. Level X was for exterminating *bureaucrats*.

Dale pulled a small, silver badge from his pocket. His Level II Technician license. It was laminated. Cheap plastic.

He held it up.

"License Number 44-B-Kowalski," he said. "Current status: Active. Thoroughness Rating: 9.2."

The Archnivist paused. It looked at the badge. It looked at Dale.

"A minor license," the entity said. "Irrelevant."

"Under the Authority's Bylaws, Section 1, Article 4," Dale continued, his voice dropping an octave, becoming harder. "Any entity engaging a licensed technician during an active inspection is subject to immediate suspension of authority if the technician invokes the 'Chain of Command' defense."

The Archnivist's eyes narrowed. The shadows of its suit rippled.

"That clause was removed in the Third Amendment," the entity said.

"It was not," Dale lied. He didn't know if it had been removed. He suspected it had.

But the Archnivist had been trapped in the rift for three years, which had lost touch with the updates.

It was a gamble. A professional, high-stakes bluff.

The Archnivist hesitated. Its fingers twitched. The floating files slowed their orbit.

Dale pressed his advantage. He walked forward. He stepped into the entity's personal space. The smell was old paper and ozone.

"You are out of jurisdiction," Dale said. "You are operating without a valid permit. You are harassing a licensed professional."

He raised his pen. He pointed it at the Archnivist's chest.

"I am writing you a citation."

He wrote.

*VIOLATION: UNLICENSED PRESENCE IN ACTIVE SERVICE AREA.* *VIOLATION: OBSTRUCTION OF INSPECTION.* *VIOLATION: PSYCHOLOGICAL HARASSMENT OF LICENSING PERSONNEL.*

The words glowed on the obsidian clipboard. They burned bright white.

The Archnivist shrieked. It was a sound of tearing metal. It reached for Dale, but its hand stopped in mid-air. It couldn't move.

*SYSTEM ALERT: CITATION ISSUED. RIVAL RESTRAINED. ACTION POINTS LOCKED.*

Dale felt the pressure in his chest ease. The anxiety receded, just slightly. He had a foot in the door.

But the Archnivist wasn't beaten. It was frozen.

From behind the entity, a new sound emerged. The rustle of pages.

Another figure stepped out of the office.

Smaller. Older. It wore a uniform.

The same uniform Dale wore, which was the man with the signet ring.

The Head of Audit Division.

But he wasn't human anymore. His skin was parchment.

His eyes were ink wells, and he looked at Dale. He looked at the citation.

"Mr. Kowalski," the man said.

His voice was soft.

Sad. "You have made a mistake."

Dale froze.

"The Chain of Command defense," the parchment-man continued, "applies only to disputes between licensed technicians. It does not apply to... auditors."

The Archnivist broke free. It lunged.

Dale jumped back. The entity's claws slashed the air where his throat had been.

*SYSTEM ALERT: CITATION VOIDED. RIVAL UNLOCKED. RETALIATION INCOMING.*

Dale's heart hammered against his ribs. He had pushed too hard. He had used a rule that no longer existed. The System had punished him instantly.

The Archnivist raised both hands. The files around it stopped spinning. They aligned. They formed a spear.

Dale looked at his Field Guide, which was empty. He had no data.

No weaknesses.

No protocols.

He looked at his clipboard. The citation was still there. Glowing.

He could tear it out. Start over.

But he didn't have time.

The Archnivist thrust the spear of files.

Dale did the only thing he could think of. He didn't dodge. He didn't counter.

He opened his mouth. And he spoke the truth.

"I am not a technician," Dale said.

The spear stopped. The Archnivist paused.

"I am a pest control technician," Dale clarified. "And you are a contamination."

He slammed his hand down on the clipboard.

*PROTOCOL: TOTAL INCINERATION (LEVEL VII).*

This was a nuclear option. It required burning all current Field Guide entries. It erased weeks of progress. It left him vulnerable to every other monster in the county.

The System demanded a sacrifice.

Dale paid it.

His Field Guide flashed red. Then black. Then white.

Every entry. Gone.

The energy released from the deletion hit the Archnivist. A wave of pure, white fire erupted from the clipboard.

It washed over the entity.

The shadows of the suit boiled away.

The files disintegrated.

The Archnivist screamed. It was unmade. Its form dissolved into ash and paper dust.

The rift collapsed. The office behind it faded.

Dale stood in the center of the void. He was alone.

His Field Guide was empty, and he had zero data. No knowledge. No targeted bonuses.

He was vulnerable. Exposed.

But the threat was gone.

Or was it?

The parchment-man stepped forward. He was still there. He had not been hit by the incineration. He watched Dale with ink-black eyes.

"You have destroyed the Archivist," the man said. "But you have not resolved the citation."

Dale looked up. His hands were shaking. He looked at his empty clipboard.

"Explain," Dale said.

"The Archivist was a symptom," the man said. "I am the cause."

He reached into his pocket. He pulled out a stamp. It was red. Heavy.

*DENIED.*

He stamped the air in front of Dale.

*SYSTEM ALERT: LICENSE REVOCATION PENDING. PROBATIONARY STATUS ACTIVE.*

Dale felt a cold weight settle on his shoulders. It was heavier than the obsidian clipboard.

"You are no longer authorized to operate in this sector," the man said. "You are now the infestation."

The rift closed.

Dale was back on the asphalt. The night was quiet. The birds were singing.

But the System interface was different. The usual green text was red.

*STATUS: PROBATION.* *RESTRICTION: NO NEW FIELD GUIDE ENTRIES PERMITTED.* *RESTRICTION: NO COMBAT PROTOCOLS AVAILABLE.*

Dale stood there. He had saved the county. He had killed a Class X entity. And he had lost the ability to fight back.

He looked at the ground. There was a single object left behind.

A file folder, which was closed. Sealed with a red wax stamp.

Dale picked it up. He didn't open it. He knew what was inside.

His own inspection reports. All of them. Fourteen years of work.

The man had stolen them.

And without them, Dale didn't exist, and he looked up at the sky. The stars were wrong. They were arranged in a grid. A filing system.

He clicked his pen. It made no sound.

He had a job to do. But he had no tools. And he had no history.
