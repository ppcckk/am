Chapter 301: Targeted Service Scope

# Chapter 301

The rune on the severed finger pulsed with a sickly, amber light. It beat against the concrete floor like a second heart, syncing with the wet, gurgling rhythm of the hole behind it.

Dale Kowalski stared at it. He did not breathe. He counted.

One. Two. Three.

The amber glow shifted to a violent crimson.

A sound tore through the room. Not a roar.

A shriek of tearing metal and snapping cartilage.

The hole in the wall, previously a neat, circular breach, stretched. The concrete groaned, dust raining down on Dale's uniform. The finger lifted from the floor, hovering in the air, spinning slowly.

"Mr.

Kowalski," a voice oozed into his skull. It sounded like oil being poured over gravel.

"Your client profile is incomplete.

Please provide your service scope.

"

Dale didn't answer. He reached for his belt.

His fingers closed around empty air.

His clipboard was gone.

His Field Guide was gone.

"Sarah," Dale said. His voice was flat. Professional.

"The containment zone is failing. Move to the perimeter."

Sarah stood ten feet back, her axe raised.

Her knuckles were white.

"You're talking to it? Dale, it's pointing at you.

It's waiting for you.

"

"It's waiting for a contract," Dale said. He didn't look at her.

He looked at the finger.

"Do not engage. Do not attempt to sever the connection. It's a Class IX negotiation interface.

You cannot hack it with steel.

"

"Great. So we do what?" Sarah asked. "Just stand here and let it look at us?"

"We file the paperwork."

Dale crouched, and he scanned the floor.

Debris. Rebar.

A shattered transformer. He picked up a chunk of drywall, still dusted with pulverized gypsum. It was dry.

Clean, and he pulled a pen from his pocket. It was a cheap, clicky ballpoint, one he used for incident reports. He clicked it. The sound was sharp in the heavy silence.

"Identify," Dale muttered.

"Document. Isolate. Eliminate.

Verify."

The finger twitched. The crimson light intensified.

"Mr. Kowalski," the oily voice purred.

"You lack the necessary credentials for this transaction.

Submit to arbitration, and I will spare your life. Submit your license, and I will absorb your soul. These are the only two options.

"

"Those are not options," Dale said. He wrote on the drywall. *Incident Report: Unauthorized Contract Solicitation. Suspect: The Broker. Location: Sector 4 Breach.*

The voice laughed. It was a wet, sliding sound.

"You have no system interface.

You have no authority.

You are just a man with a stick.

"

"I have a pen," Dale said.

He dropped to one knee. He wasn't writing a report. He was drawing a circle.

A specific geometric pattern. The containment circle for Class-4 Acid Beetles.

"Sarah," Dale said. "I need a conductive path. Anything metal. Long and thin."

"My axe shaft?"

"No. Too short. Look at the conduit tray. The copper grounding wire."

Sarah looked at the ceiling.

A bundle of stripped copper wire hung loose, sparking intermittently. She moved toward it.

"Dale, the finger is moving.

It's coming closer.

"

"Let it," Dale said. He finished the circle. He clicked the pen again. "Isolate."

He placed the drywall shard in the center of the circle. He placed his pen in the center.

The finger hovered above him. The rune pulsed.

*"I see your ploy, Dale. You are attempting to bait me with symbolic compliance. This is a violation of Subsection 9, Paragraph 4. You will be penalized."*

"Penalize me," Dale said.

He dragged the copper wire across the floor. He dragged it through the drywall dust. He dragged it to the edge of the circle.

The wire sparked. The amber light of the finger flickered.

"Contact established," Dale said.

"Established with what?" Sarah shouted. She was sweating. Her axe trembled.

"With the ground," Dale said. He looked at Sarah.

"The rune is a signal.

A beacon. It broadcasts a request for service. But a beacon requires a receiver to confirm the handshake.

The Authority doesn't just listen. It validates.

"

Dale looked at the wire, which was connected to the building's electrical system.

The building was dead. The grid was down.

But the copper wire was still part of the structure. It was part of the *place*.

"The Authority recognizes the structure," Dale said. "It recognizes the violation. I am not filing a complaint with the Broker. I am filing a complaint with the building."

The voice in his head hissed. *

"That is not how the system works."*

"Check the bylaws," Dale said. He clicked the pen. *Click. Click. Click.*

He wrote a single word on the drywall.

**VOID.**

The copper wire sparked violently. A blue arc jumped from the wire to the floating finger.

The finger screamed.

It wasn't a sound. It was a psychic backlash.

Sarah staggered back, covering her ears.

Dale remained kneeling. His face was stone.

The amber rune on the bone cracked.

*"You cannot void a Class IX contract! It is binding!"*

"It's not a contract," Dale said. "It's an infestation. And I'm treating the source."

He grabbed the copper wire. He wrapped it around his fist. The metal bit into his palm, cold and sharp. He pressed his hand against the drywall, against the word VOID.

The rune shattered.

The finger dropped to the floor with a wet thud.

The hole in the wall collapsed. Not into the void, but into rubble. The rift sealed with a hiss of steam.

Silence returned to the room.

Sarah lowered her axe.

Her breathing was ragged. She looked at Dale.

She looked at his hand.

Blood was welling up between his fingers where the copper wire had cut him.

"Did you just... short-circuit a god?" she asked.

"I voided a transaction," Dale said. He stood up.

He wiped his hand on his pants.

"The Broker tried to force a service agreement without a verified host.

The Authority penalizes unauthorized solicitation. I used the building's grounding system to report the breach as a structural failure caused by an unlicensed entity.

"

Sarah blinked.

"That's... that's not how you fight monsters."

"It's how you fix buildings," Dale said.

A new alert appeared in the corner of Dale's vision. It was faint, greyed out, like a glitching text box.

**SYSTEM ERROR: PROTOCOL MISMATCH.** **TREATMENT POINTS: 0.** **NOTICE: FIELD GUIDE CORRUPTED.**

Dale frowned. He blinked. The alert didn't disappear.

"Sarah," Dale said. "Look at your status screen."

Sarah grimaced. She focused.

"Why? What did I do wrong?"

"Check your Field Guide. The entry for *Rattus profundus*. Is it there?"

Sarah looked confused.

"I don't have one. I'm not a technician."

"Look at the shared queue. The public log."

Sarah closed her eyes. She winced.

"It's... it's blank.

Everything is blank. My log is empty.

"

Dale looked at his own hands. He flexed his fingers. The cuts from the wire were already closing, but too fast. Too clean.

"The Authority didn't delete the Broker," Dale said. "It deleted the record of the interaction."

"What does that mean?"

"It means we don't exist," Dale said.

"To the System, that event never happened. Which means we didn't get the points. We didn't get the XP.

We didn't get the new protocols.

"

Sarah stood up straight.

"We saved our lives. Who cares about points?"

"I do," Dale said. "Because without the points, we don't advance.

And without advancing, we don't survive the next wave.

The Authority rates us on thoroughness. We were thorough. But we were *unlicensed* thoroughness.

"

A sound echoed from the hallway outside.

Heavy, wet footsteps.

Not one set. Many.

Sarah raised her axe.

"More of them?"

"No," Dale said. He looked at the shattered finger on the floor.

The bone was dark now.

Dead.

"These are auditors."

The door to the room burst open.

Three figures stood there. They were tall, slender, made of the same grey concrete as the walls. They had no faces.

Just smooth, featureless ovals where heads should be. They wore suits.

Ill-fitting, dusty suits that looked like they had been tailored for humans who had long since turned to dust.

They held clipboards.

"Mr. Kowalski," the first one said. Its voice was the sound of pages turning. "Your file has been flagged for irregularity."

Dale stepped forward. He didn't take a weapon. He took a breath.

"I followed protocol," Dale said.

"Protocol was violated," the second figure said. "You utilized non-standard infrastructure to terminate a Class IX entity. This constitutes an unauthorized bypass of the Certification Ladder."

"It was a necessary emergency measure," Dale said. "The environment was compromised. The risk of cross-contamination was critical. I isolated the threat using available resources."

"The Authority does not recognize improvised resources," the third figure said. "Your license is suspended."

Dale felt his stomach drop.

"Suspended?"

"Pending review," the first figure said. "You are currently operating as an Unlicensed Pest Control Technician. Any further engagement with Rift entities will be classified as trespassing.

You will not receive points.

You will not receive protection.

"

"Then how do we survive?" Sarah shouted. "If we're unlicensed, the Authority won't help us?"

The concrete figures turned to look at her.

"You are not licensed," the first figure said.

"You are collateral. Mr. Kowalski is responsible for your safety.

If he fails to document and eliminate all threats in this zone, his license will be revoked permanently. And you will be processed as waste.

"

Dale looked at the clipboard in the auditor's hands. It was blank.

"I need to file an appeal," Dale said.

"There is no appeal process for suspension," the second figure said. "There is only remediation."

The figure pointed a long, grey finger at the hole in the wall. The rubble was shifting. Something was pushing through.

"Remediation is required immediately," the auditor said.

"The Broker's agent is still present.

It is hiding in the infrastructure. You have twenty-four hours to locate and eliminate it using only Class II tools.

If you fail, your license is revoked. And the infestation spreads.

"

Dale looked at the rubble, and he looked at Sarah.

He looked at the auditor.

"Class II tools," Dale said. "No new abilities. No upgraded protocols. Just the basics."

"Just the basics," the auditor confirmed.

The three figures turned and walked away. They didn't slam the door. They just faded into the shadows of the hallway.

Sarah looked at Dale.

"Twenty-four hours. No points. No upgrades."

Dale looked at his hand. The cuts were gone. He looked at the dead finger on the floor.

He picked it up, which was heavier than it looked. Cold. Dense.

"Dale, put that down," Sarah said. "It's cursed. Or whatever."

"It's evidence," Dale said. He wrapped it in his handkerchief. He put it in his pocket.

He walked to the hole in the wall. The concrete was still warm.

"Sarah, get the flashlight. Check the junction boxes in the sub-basement. Look for wiring that doesn't belong. Look for copper that's been stripped but not connected."

"Why?" Sarah asked.

"Because the Broker is a middleman," Dale said. He clicked his pen.

It was broken, and the spring was gone. He clicked the empty casing anyway. *Click.*

"Middlemen need contracts," Dale said. "And contracts need signatures. I'm going to find where the Broker signed the deal. And then I'm going to erase it."

Sarah looked at the dark tunnel leading down into the basement. She looked at Dale.

"You're going to file a report," she said. It wasn't a question.

"I'm going to file a report," Dale said. "And I'm going to make sure it's perfect."

He stepped into the darkness.

The first thing he saw was not a monster, which was a clipboard.

Lying on the floor of the sub-basement, perfectly aligned with the floor tiles.

It was his.

And on it, in neat, bureaucratic handwriting, was his name.

And the word: **TARGETED.**

Dale's breath hitched. He didn't move.

From the shadows of the boiler room, a voice spoke. It was not oily. It was sharp. Like paper being cut.

*"Mr. Kowalski. You forgot to sign the receipt."*
