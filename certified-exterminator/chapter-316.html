Chapter 316: Certified Auditor

# Chapter 316

The rustling stopped.

Silence crashed into the clearing like a wave.

Dale didn't move. His pen hovered over the blank page, ink trembling against the paper grain. The clipboard pressed hard into his ribs. He kept his eyes on the tree line, but his focus narrowed to the single point where the sound had ceased.

A figure stepped out from behind a massive oak.

It wasn't human. It wasn't insectoid. It wore a suit that looked like it had been stitched from shadows and old newspaper clippings. The face was a smooth, featureless oval of white porcelain, devoid of eyes or mouth, yet Dale felt the weight of its scrutiny.

"Violation logged," the figure said. The voice didn't come from the porcelain face. It vibrated from the air around Dale's head, dry and crisp like cracking ice.

Dale lowered his pen. He didn't raise his hands. He didn't reach for his spray. He checked his watch.

"Time of incident?" Dale asked.

The figure tilted its head. The motion was jerky, wrong.

"You are interrupting the audit, Technician Kowalski."

"I'm clarifying the scope," Dale replied. His voice remained flat.

He clicked his pen.

"You appeared in my vehicle. You left a residue. That is a contamination event, not an infestation.

I need a hazard assessment before I proceed.

"

The figure took a step forward. The ground beneath its feet turned to ash.

"The Authority does not issue hazard assessments for existential threats."

"Section 4, Subsection C of the Licensing Code states that all unverified entities must be classified before engagement," Dale said. He tapped the clipboard. "I am invoking the right to preliminary documentation. You are blocking my workspace."

The figure stopped. The porcelain face didn't change, but the air grew colder.

"You are stalling."

"I am working," Dale corrected. "Identify yourself.

Species, origin, threat level.

If you don't identify, I have to guess. And guessing is inefficient. Guessing gets people killed.

"

A second figure emerged from the trees to the left. It was taller, its suit comprised of wet, black leather. Its face was a single, vertical slit.

"Stop him," the first figure said.

The second figure lunged.

Dale didn't run. He sidestepped, a practiced pivot that he'd used a thousand times to avoid splashing chemical runoff. The black-suited figure slammed into the dirt where Dale had been standing.

Dale looked at the scuff marks in the mud.

"Aggression noted," Dale said, writing. "Hostile intent confirmed. Classifying as Class 4 Aggressive Entity."

The black figure rose. It didn't look angry. It looked confused.

"You... you are not attacking?"

"I am documenting," Dale said. He held up his clipboard.

"Look at it.

Look at the field guide.

I need a visual reference.

"

The first figure, the one in the newspaper suit, watched Dale. It seemed to be waiting.

"What do you want?" the porcelain figure asked.

"A name," Dale said. "Or a code. I can't file a report on 'Unknown Entity Number One.' It messes up the cross-contamination protocols.

If I use a Class 2 insecticide on a Class 4 entity, I get a debuff. If I use a Class 5 protocol on a Class 1 entity, I waste resources.

I need accuracy.

"

The newspaper figure stepped closer. The ash trail widened.

"You believe the rules apply to us?"

"The rules apply to everything," Dale said. "That's why the Authority exists. Chaos is bad for business.

You're causing a disturbance.

That's a violation.

"

The figure laughed. It was a dry, rustling sound.

"You are small," the figure said. "You are a technician. You clean nests. You do not understand the architecture of the Rift."

"I understand entry points," Dale said.

"I understand structural integrity. You're in a zone that isn't cleared. You're unauthorized.

Do you have a permit?

"

The second figure, the one in leather, growled. It bared teeth that were too long, too numerous.

"Kill him," the leather figure said.

The newspaper figure raised a hand. The leather figure froze.

"Wait," the porcelain face said. "He is... thorough."

Dale kept writing. He didn't look up.

"Please remain still," Dale said.

"I need to note the texture of your attire for the Field Guide.

Is that synthetic? Organic?

The ash trail suggests combustion, but the suit looks woven. Complex.

"

The newspaper figure looked down at its own coat.

"It is memory," the figure said.

"Understood," Dale wrote. *Composition: Mnemonic residue. High volatility.* "What is your primary function?

Are you a broodmother?

A scout? A worker?

"

"We are Editors," the figure said.

Dale paused. He looked up.

"Editors?"

"We correct errors," the figure said.

"You are an error. You broke the script.

You were supposed to flee.

You were supposed to die in the dark. Instead, you drove. You returned.

You are now a variable.

"

Dale uncapped his spray can. It was empty. He put it back in his belt.

"I don't have a weapon that works on Editors," Dale said.

"I have a clipboard.

I have a protocol toolkit. I have a Level III Certification.

I need to know if I can treat you.

"

The leather figure stepped forward again.

"He is trying to classify us. It is amusing."

"It is necessary," the newspaper figure said. It pointed a long, spindly finger at Dale's clipboard. "Show me your Field Guide."

Dale hesitated. Showing the Guide was a breach of security protocol. But refusing was a breach of cooperation protocol.

"Standard practice requires a warrant," Dale said.

The figure snapped its fingers.

Dale's clipboard ripped itself from his grip. It flew through the air, landing at the feet of the newspaper figure. The pages fanned out.

Dale didn't chase it. He stood still.

"You have violated the Chain of Custody," Dale said. His voice was colder now. "That is a Class B infraction. It voids your immunity from audit."

The figure looked at the pages. It flipped them rapidly.

"Rattus profundus," the figure read. "Dimensional Vermin. Weakness: Sonic resonance. Weakness: Structural isolation."

It turned the page.

"Acid Beetles. Weakness: Alkaline neutralization."

It turned another.

"Giant Ants. Weakness: Nest disruption."

The figure stopped at the last page. The entry for the Rift itself.

It was incomplete.

A single line read: *Unknown. Origin: Beyond.*

"You have not identified the source," the figure said.

"No," Dale said.

"I have not. That is why I am still here.

If I had identified it, I would have treated it.

I would be done.

"

The newspaper figure looked up.

"You are not done. You are beginning."

Dale picked up his pen. He didn't look at the figure. He looked at the dark trees behind it.

"Who sent you?" Dale asked.

"We sent ourselves," the figure said. "The Authority is not a benevolent god, Dale Kowalski.

It is a bureaucracy.

Bureaucracies hate variables.

You are a variable. You are inefficient.

You do not kill. You document. You delay.

You ask for permits.

"

"I do my job," Dale said.

"Your job is obsolete," the leather figure said.

It stepped closer. The smell of wet dog and ozone filled the air.

"The world has changed.

The old protocols do not fit.

We are updating the system.

"

Dale looked at the leather figure. He noted the slit for a mouth. He noted the lack of eyes.

"Classifying you as 'System Update Agent'," Dale said. He started writing. "Threat Level: Unknown. Recommended Protocol: Containment and Observation."

The leather figure grabbed Dale's throat.

Dale didn't struggle. He reached into his pocket.

He pulled out a small, folded piece of paper. It was a receipt. From a hardware store. Dated three days ago.

He held it up.

The leather figure froze.

"What is that?" the newspaper figure asked.

"Duct tape," Dale said.

"And a trash bag. I bought them three days ago. I was prepared for a containment breach.

I am always prepared for a containment breach.

"

The leather figure loosened its grip.

"You are bluffing."

"Protocol states that if an entity engages a Technician, the Technician is authorized to invoke Emergency Containment Clause 7," Dale said.

He tapped the paper.

"This receipt is proof of pre-meditated containment intent. If you kill me now, the Authority will retroactively invalidate your existence.

You will be deleted. Not killed. Deleted.

Like a bad line of code.

"

The silence stretched.

The newspaper figure looked at the receipt. Then at Dale.

"You are using a loophole."

"I am using the rules," Dale said.

"You said I am an error. But errors are just undocumented variables. Document me, and I am no longer an error.

I am a known quantity.

And known quantities can be managed.

"

The leather figure released him. Dale gasped, rubbing his throat. He didn't cough. He straightened his jacket.

"Continue," the newspaper figure said.

Dale picked up his clipboard. The pages were scattered in the ash. He gathered them. He smoothed them out.

"I need a witness," Dale said.

The leather figure laughed.

"Who?"

"You," Dale said.

He pointed to the leather figure.

"You have to sign off on the containment attempt. If you don't, the Authority won't recognize the engagement.

You'll be off the books. You'll have no protection from the Rift itself.

Do you want to be unprotected?

"

The leather figure looked at the newspaper figure.

"Does he have protection?" the leather figure asked.

"He has no protection," the newspaper figure said. "But he has a clipboard."

Dale held out a pen.

"Sign it," Dale said.

The leather figure stared at the pen. Then it stared at Dale.

"Why?" the leather figure asked.

"Because I need to know if you're hostile," Dale said.

"If you sign, you're consenting to observation. If you don't, I have to assume you're a rogue element.

And rogue elements get exterminated. I have a Level III permit. I can use high-grade solvents.

I know where to find them. I know how to mix them.

You don't know if I'm bluffing.

But I'm not."

The leather figure took the pen. Its hand was cold, damp.

It signed the bottom of the page.

*Dale Kowalski, Licensed Technician.*

The ink glowed faintly blue.

The System notification appeared in Dale's vision.

**Protocol Engaged: Observation Mode.** **Status: Active.** **Bonus: +10% Insight against 'System Update Agent'.**

Dale looked at the newspaper figure.

"Now," Dale said.

"Talk. What is the Rift?

Why is it opening?

And why are you afraid of a man with a clipboard?

"

The newspaper figure sat on a stump. It crossed its legs.

"We are not afraid," the figure said. "We are concerned."

"Concerned about what?"

"About the Auditor," the figure said.

Dale's pen stopped.

"There is no Auditor," Dale said. "There are Inspectors. There are Examiners."

"The Auditor is different," the figure said. "The Auditor does not certify. The Auditor erases. It comes when the Thoroughness Rating exceeds 9.5."

Dale checked his mental stats. His rating was 9.2.

"It's coming?" Dale asked.

"It is already here," the figure said.

The woods shifted.

The trees didn't move. But the shadows between them deepened. They grew longer, darker.

Dale looked at his clipboard. The entry for the Editor was fading. The blue glow of the signature turned black.

"You lied," Dale said.

"We told you what was true," the newspaper figure said. "You asked what we were.

We are Editors.

We edit reality.

The Auditor edits existence.

"

Dale looked at the leather figure. The signature on the page was gone. The page was blank.

"Who is the Auditor?" Dale asked.

The leather figure pointed to the sky.

Above the trees, the rift was widening. It wasn't a crack anymore. It was a tear. And something was looking through it.

Not a monster.

A man, and he wore a grey suit. He held a clipboard.

He looked exactly like Dale.

"He is the previous iteration," the newspaper figure said. "He failed. He was too thorough.

He documented everything. He left no loose ends.

And so, he became the loose end.

"

Dale looked at the figure in the sky. The figure in the sky looked down at Dale.

The figure in the sky raised its hand.

Dale's clipboard grew heavy. It burned his hands.

"He is auditing you," the leather figure said. "He is checking your work."

Dale didn't drop the clipboard. He gripped it tighter.

"What is the protocol for fighting an Auditor?" Dale asked.

The newspaper figure stood up. It dusted off its suit.

"There is no protocol," the figure said. "Because Auditors do not exist. They are conceptual errors."

Dale looked at the page. The blank space where the signature had been.

He pulled a pen from his pocket.

"I need to document him," Dale said.

"You cannot document a concept," the figure said.

"I can document a violation," Dale said. "If he is here, he is in my jurisdiction. If he is in my jurisdiction, he is subject to inspection."

Dale wrote.

*Violation: Unauthorized Existence.* *Subject: Self.* *Status: Critical.*

The sky ripped wider. The grey-suited man descended. He moved with perfect, terrible precision.

Dale's hands shook. He kept writing.

*Recommendation: Immediate Erasure.*

The grey-suited man landed in front of the Editors. He did not look at them. He looked at Dale.

He spoke. His voice was Dale's voice.

"Stop writing," the Grey Suit said.

Dale kept writing.

"Why?" the Grey Suit asked.

"Because it's my job," Dale said.

The Grey Suit smiled. It was a sad, tired smile.

"You are making a mistake," the Grey Suit said. "You are documenting the end of the world. And you are doing it without a permit."

Dale looked up.

"I have a permit," Dale said.

He held up his clipboard. The top page was blank.

No. Not blank.

It read: *CERTIFICATION LEVEL IX PENDING.*

The Grey Suit's smile vanished.

"How?" the Grey Suit whispered.

"I passed the exam," Dale said. "While you were busy erasing yourself, I was studying."

The Grey Suit lunged.

Dale didn't dodge. He stepped forward.

He slammed the clipboard into the Grey Suit's chest.

The System notification exploded in Dale's vision.

**Violation: Unauthorized Access to Tier IX Data.** **Penalty: Immediate Demotion to Level I.** **Reward: Access to 'Final Solution' Protocol.**

The Grey Suit screamed. It didn't bleed. It unraveled. Threads of grey light burst from its body.

The Editors watched. They didn't help. They didn't move.

The Grey Suit dissolved into ash.

Dale stood alone in the clearing, and he looked at his clipboard.

The text was gone. All of it. Every entry.

Every violation code.

Every Field Guide species, and the page was white. Pure, terrifying white.

The leather figure stepped forward.

"You wiped your history," it said.

Dale looked at the blank page. He looked at the rift in the sky. It was closing.

"I didn't wipe it," Dale said. "I archived it."

He turned to the Editors.

"Now," Dale said. "Let's talk about my new certification."

The newspaper figure bowed.

"Level IX," it said. "You are no longer a technician."

"What am I?"

"You are the Auditor," the figure said.

Dale looked at the blank page. He picked up his pen.

He wrote one word.
