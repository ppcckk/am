Chapter 295: Checkmate Protocol

# Chapter 295

The first auditor swung his briefcase like a mace.

Dale sidestepped. The impact cratered the pavement, sending shards of reconstituted asphalt flying into his shins.

Pain flared, sharp and electric, but he didn't drop the crowbar.

He adjusted his grip, knuckles white against the rusted steel, and scanned the approaching swarm. They moved in lockstep, a rhythmic shuffle of leather shoes on concrete that sounded like a heartbeat slowing down.

He didn't look at their faces. He looked at their hands.

Every auditor held a clipboard in the left hand and a red pen in the right.

The pens were long, metallic, and capped with a needle-point nib that glowed with a faint, sickly amber light. Red ink.

Correction fluid.

Erasure.

Dale clicked his tongue.

"Inefficient," he muttered.

"Standard issue should be blue for initial review.

Red is for final rejection.

You're skipping steps.

"

The auditor nearest to him paused. The suit was immaculate, charcoal gray, tailored to fit the rigid geometry of a human who had forgotten how to slouch. The face was smooth, devoid of pores or expression, save for the two empty sockets where eyes should have been.

Yet, Dale could feel the gaze. It felt like a scan line moving across his skin.

The auditor tilted its head. A sound emanated from its chest cavity, a dry rustle of paper.

"Violation: Unauthorized commentary on procedure," the entity stated. Its voice was not a voice; it was the vibration of air through a hollow tube. "Penalty: One Treatment Point deducted."

Dale felt the sting.

It wasn't physical. It was a hollowing, a sudden lightness in his chest where his progress bar used to be. He glanced at his peripheral vision.

The Authority's interface was glitching, the numbers flickering from 4,200 to 4,199, and he didn't panic. He checked his watch.

Time limit: None.

That was the trap. The exam wasn't about speed.

It was about endurance.

The more he fought, the more time passed. The more time passed, the more the environment shifted to disadvantage him. The city was rebuilding itself around him, but the architecture was wrong.

The angles were acute. The doors opened inward only.

The windows were too small to escape through, and he backed away, keeping the crowd between him and the central spire. The spire was the source of the noise, a low hum that made his teeth ache. It was where the Chief Auditor likely sat.

Dale raised his crowbar. He didn't swing. He held it horizontally, creating a barrier.

"Field Guide update," he whispered.

A semi-transparent window popped up in his vision.

**SUBJECT: Bureaucratic Automaton (Type: Auditor, Class VI).** **WEAKNESS: Procedural Rigidity.** **NOTE: These entities cannot deviate from the written script. If the script contains a contradiction, they will freeze to resolve it.**

Dale smiled. It was a thin, grim expression that didn't reach his eyes.

The auditors advanced. Ten of them. Twenty. They formed a phalanx, briefcases held high like shields.

Dale reached into his pocket and pulled out the blue pen. He uncapped it.

"Stop," he said.

The front line halted. The rhythm of their footsteps ceased.

"I am invoking Section 4, Paragraph C of the Licensing Protocol: Right to Silence," Dale announced. His voice carried over the hum of the spire. "I am exercising my right to remain silent pending review of the initial citation."

The auditor in front of the phalanx lowered its briefcase. The red pen hovered over its clipboard.

"Silence is not a defense," the auditor said. "Silence is an omission. Omission is a violation of Thoroughness."

"Then you are violating the code," Dale said.

"The Authority's bylaws state that silence is permitted during active litigation. You are currently litigating my license.

Therefore, my silence is protected. To attack me while I am silent is to punish a protected action. That is an illegal enforcement action.

"

The auditor froze.

The amber light in its pen flickered.

Behind it, the other nineteen auditors shifted uncomfortably. The paper-rustle sounds grew louder, overlapping, creating a cacophony of static.

Dale watched the glitch. It was spreading. The auditor's suit rippled, the fabric turning translucent. The underlying structure wasn't bone or muscle; it was stacked paper, compressed and glued together.

"Cross-contamination warning," Dale said softly.

He didn't use the crowbar to strike. He used it to tap the auditor's clipboard.

*Clink.*

The sound was small, but in the silence of the frozen phalanx, it was deafening.

The auditor's pen twitched. It tried to write, but the hand spasmed. The red ink leaked from the nib, pooling on the white paper.

Dale stepped forward. He didn't hit the auditor. He hit the space between the auditor and the one behind it.

He swung the crowbar in a wide arc, catching the edge of the second auditor's briefcase. The steel rang against the steel case.

*Clang.*

The second auditor broke formation. It stumbled back, bumping into the third.

The cascade effect was immediate.

The rigid lines of the phalanx buckled. The script they were following assumed a static enemy. Dale was moving.

The script didn't account for dynamic variables during a 'Silence' pause.

Dale saw the opening, and he didn't run for the spire. He ran for the side street.

The auditors broke their freeze. The red pens slashed through the air, leaving trails of glowing ink that burned on contact.

Dale ducked under a swing, the heat of the ink singing the hair on his arms.

He slammed his shoulder into the chest of a pursuing auditor. The impact felt like hitting a wall of hardcover books.

He kept moving, and the city streets were narrowing. Buildings were shifting, walls sliding across the pavement to block his path. The environment was hostile because the exam was hostile. The Authority was testing his ability to adapt to changing code violations.

Dale skidded around a corner into an alleyway. A dumpster sat there, overflowing with shredded documents.

He jumped into it.

The auditors stopped at the mouth of the alley. They couldn't enter. The alley was too narrow. Their bulk, the rigidity of their forms, prevented them from fitting through the gap.

Dale crouched in the trash. Papers fluttered around him. He grabbed one.

It was a termination notice. His name was at the top.

He tore it in half.

The Authority's interface flashed red.

**VIOLETION: Destruction of Official Record.** **PENALTY: Level Demotion Imminent.**

Dale ignored it. He looked at the other papers in the dumpster.

They were all similar.

Citations.

Violations. Appeals.

He sorted them, which was his job. He was a technician. He knew how to organize chaos.

He knew that if you didn't treat the nest, the problem returned. And these auditors were just workers.

They followed orders.

If he could find the order, he could break the chain.

He found a form titled **PROCEDURE 902: MASS AUDIT PROTOCOL.**

It detailed the formation patterns, the response triggers, and the failure states.

He read it.

Fast. His eyes scanned the dense legalese, picking out the keywords. *Static Engagement.

No Verbal Response.

Scripted Reaction.*

Then he saw it.

**ADDENDUM: In the event of a Class VI candidate, the Audit Team is authorized to escalate force if the candidate attempts to bypass the primary interview room.**

Bypass.

Dale was in an alley. He was bypassing the interview.

He was giving them permission to kill him, and he had to stop bypassing. He had to engage the interview.

He climbed out of the dumpster. The auditors were waiting. They were back in formation, three deep, blocking the alley exit.

Dale walked toward them, and he didn't raise the crowbar. He put it down.

He picked up the blue pen.

He uncapped it.

"I request a recess," Dale said.

The lead auditor stepped forward.

"Recess is not permitted during active audit."

"I am citing medical necessity," Dale said. He pointed to his arm.

The skin was red, blistered by the ink burns from the earlier swing.

"Contamination exposure.

Protocol requires immediate decontamination. If you continue the audit, you violate Health and Safety Standards.

The Authority will cite *you*.

"

The auditor hesitated. The paper-rustle sound returned, but slower.

"Medical necessity requires verification," the auditor said. "You must submit to a physical examination."

"Here?" Dale asked.

"Here."

Dale knew this was the trap. A physical examination would mean they would look inside him.

They would find the Field Guide, and they would find the corruption. They would erase him.

But he had to buy time.

"Fine," Dale said. "But I require a witness. Section 7, Paragraph B: Disputes regarding medical necessity require a third-party observer."

"Who?"

Dale pointed to the sky.

"The Rift. It is still open.

It is a natural phenomenon, which is not a licensed entity, but it is an external variable. It counts as an observer.

"

The auditor looked up. The gray sky was churning, purple lightning crackling above the spire.

"The Rift is a hazard," the auditor said. "Not a witness."

"It is a presence," Dale corrected. "The definition of 'witness' in the Civil Code does not require consciousness. It requires proximity. The Rift is proximate."

The auditor's pen hovered over the paper. It wanted to write *Denied*. But the logic was flawed. Dale had found a loophole in the definition.

If the auditor wrote *Denied*, they were ignoring the proximity clause. If they wrote *Granted*, they had to pause the attack.

Dale waited.

The auditor's hand trembled.

The amber light in the pen flared, then dimmed.

"Granted," the auditor said. The word was forced, grinding out of the hollow chest.

The formation broke. The auditors stepped back.

Dale exhaled. His heart was hammering against his ribs, a frantic bird trapped in a cage of ribs. He kept his face blank.

"Proceed with examination," Dale said.

He held out his arm. The blisters were oozing clear fluid, mixing with the red ink stains.

The auditor stepped forward. It raised its pen.

Dale watched the nib, and he didn't have a weapon. He had no protection. His crowbar was on the ground, ten feet away.

The auditor reached for his arm.

Dale didn't flinch.

As the pen touched his skin, Dale activated a command he had never used before. A risky, untested subroutine he had buried in the back of his Field Guide during Chapter 290, hoping he'd never need it.

**ACTION: INVOKE CONTAMINATION LOCKDOWN.**

The Field Guide entry for **Bureaucratic Automaton** flashed green.

**STATUS: CONTAMINATED.**

The auditor's pen froze.

A red error message exploded across the auditor's chest.

**ERROR: INVALID TARGET. TARGET CLASS: CANDIDATE. WEAPON CLASS: ERASURE. INCOMPATIBLE.**

The auditor couldn't write. It couldn't erase.

It was holding a pen designed to delete monsters, and Dale was no longer a monster.

He was a candidate.

The protocol forbade using erasure tools on candidates without a final verdict, and the auditor was stuck.

It tried to pull the pen back, but the logic loop held its arm in place. The pen was fused to the skin by the system's own rules.

Dale looked at the auditor's frozen face.

"You're out of protocol," Dale said.

He grabbed the auditor's wrist. The paper-structure crunched under his grip.

He didn't break it. He twisted.

The auditor's arm snapped at the wrist. The pen flew out of its hand.

Dale caught it, which was heavy. Warm. The nib was still glowing.

He looked at the other auditors. They were paralyzed. The Lead Auditor was in a logic loop, trying to resolve the conflict between the medical examination and the weapon incompatibility.

Dale had the weapon, and he didn't use it to kill them. He used it to write.

He grabbed a sheet of paper from the auditor's clipboard. It was blank.

He wrote:

**APPEAL GRANTED.** **REASON: PROCEDURAL FAILURE BY AUDIT TEAM.** **NEW STATUS: PROVISIONAL LEVEL VI.** **CONDITION: MANDATORY RETRAINING IN FIELD.**

He signed it with the red pen.

The Authority's interface screamed.

**ERROR: UNAUTHORIZED MODIFICATION OF SYSTEM RECORDS.** **CORRUPTION DETECTED.** **INITIATING PURGE.**

The sky turned black. The ground beneath Dale's feet dissolved. The auditors crumbled into ash.

But Dale stood firm. The red pen burned in his hand.

The world shook.

The ruins of the city vanished. The auditors vanished. The spire vanished.

Dale fell, and he fell through the darkness, the red pen clutched in his fist.

He landed hard.

Concrete. Cold, wet, dirty concrete.

Dale gasped, air rushing into his lungs. He coughed, spitting out dust and gray ash.

He was back, and he was in the real world.

He was in the sewer main. The place where the first Rift had cracked open the duplex.

But it wasn't empty.

The water was rising. And in the water, standing waist-deep, were not auditors.

They were humans.

Living, breathing humans, and they wore hazmat suits. Yellow. Orange. Black.

They held weapons that looked like industrial-grade sprayers, modified with rune-etched nozzles.

And they were looking at Dale.

One of them stepped forward. He pulled off his mask.

It was Marcus Thorne.

Thorne, the rival from Chapter 15. The one who had stolen Dale's Field Guide entry for the Acid Beetles. The one who had laughed when Dale got his Level II license.

Thorne smiled. It was a cruel, thin smile.

"Nice pen, Dale," Thorne said. "Does it still write?"

Dale looked at the red pen. The glow was fading.

He looked at Thorne. He looked at the army of licensed exterminators surrounding him. They weren't here to help. They were here to finish the job.

"Protocol," Dale said, his voice echoing in the sewer. "Let's begin."

Thorne raised his sprayer.

"Game over."

Dale raised the crowbar he had left in the vision. It was in his hand.

He smiled. "Checkmate."
