Chapter 256: Resonance Breach

# Chapter 256

The extraction sequence locked in place with a wet, metallic click that echoed through the bone-steel cavern.

Dale didn't pull his hand away. He kept his palm flat against the console's cold glass, watching the biometric feedback loop stabilize. Green light bled from the interface, illuminating the grime under his fingernails.

"Stabilizing," Dale said. His voice was flat, stripped of the tremor he felt in his gut.

"Subject one is coherent.

Cognitive load at forty percent. No hemorrhaging.

"

Kappa stepped closer, his long, chitinous fingers drumming against the console's edge. The sound was like rain on a tin roof.

"Speed is irrelevant," Kappa said.

"Precision is everything. A bruised memory yields fragmented data.

A clean extraction yields a perfect map.

Do you understand the difference, Technician?

"

"I know the difference between a localized infestation and a systemic breach," Dale replied. He didn't look at Kappa.

He looked at the readout.

The woman in the nearest cage—Subject Alpha—was twitching, but her eyes remained fixed on him.

"And I know that if I rush this, the Authority docks my Thoroughness Rating. I'm not losing points for sloppy work."

Kappa made a low, chittering noise. It wasn't amusement. It was calculation.

"Show me the protocol."

Dale tapped the screen. A holographic schematic expanded into the air between them. It depicted the neural pathways of the human subject, overlaid with the Authority's proprietary extraction algorithms.

Most technicians would have used the brute-force siphon, ripping the memories out like pulling weeds from cracked pavement. Dale had modified the sequence.

He'd inserted a three-second delay at the hippocampal junction, allowing the subject's short-term buffer to flush before the long-term data was accessed.

"It's inefficient," Kappa observed, though his compound eyes narrowed. "It adds twelve seconds to the cycle."

"It reduces cognitive scarring by sixty percent," Dale said. "And it prevents the feedback loop that usually causes the subject to seize."

"Feedback loop?" Kappa tilted his head. "Explain."

"The brain resists being emptied," Dale said, pointing to a red spike on the graph. "If you pull too fast, the brain floods with cortisol. That chemical spike contaminates the data stream.

You get noise.

Garbage data. You have to scrub it out later, which costs time and points.

My method keeps the subject calm.

The data comes out clean. First time.

"

Kappa leaned in, his breath smelling of ozone and rotting meat.

"You are manipulating the biological substrate."

"I am optimizing the workflow," Dale corrected. "Standard OSHA guidelines for hazardous material removal require containment before extraction.

I treated the neural pressure as a hazmat situation.

Contained.

Extracted. Verified.

"

The woman in the cage gasped. Her body arched off the restraints.

Dale's finger didn't waver.

"Subject Alpha experiencing minor distress. Initiating sedative protocol via the intravenous line.

Not lethal. Just… calming.

"

"You use drugs?" Kappa asked, skeptical.

"I use chemical inhibitors to prevent panic-induced neural degradation," Dale said. "It's in the Field Guide under *Humanoid Stress Responses*, page 402. Did you read it?"

Kappa ignored the question.

"The data stream is active. Begin the harvest."

Dale watched the progress bar fill. It moved slowly, agonizingly slow, but the integrity indicator remained at 100%. No red flags. No static.

"This is tedious," Kappa muttered. "The other sectors move ten subjects per hour."

"They also have a forty percent data corruption rate," Dale said. "Check the logs. Sector 4 had to re-harvest three subjects yesterday because the first batch was garbage. I'm saving the Authority a re-do."

Kappa didn't argue. He just watched the numbers climb.

***

The next hour was a blur of routine procedure.

Dale moved from cage to cage, and he checked restraints. He calibrated the injection ports. He adjusted the extraction speed based on the subject's heart rate variability.

He treated each human not as a victim, but as a unit of inventory that needed to be processed with minimal damage.

It was horrifying. It was also, undeniably, effective.

By the time he reached Subject Five, a young man with tattoos snaking up his neck, Dale had found a rhythm. The Authority's System recognized his adherence to the modified protocol.

**SYSTEM NOTIFICATION:** *Protocol 1-A: Cognitive Harvesting (Optimized Variant).* *Current Efficiency: 98%.* *Thoroughness Bonus: +15 Points.*

Dale blinked at the floating text. He hadn't asked for the bonus. The System had rewarded him for finding a loophole. By slowing down the extraction to preserve data integrity, he was technically performing a higher-tier diagnostic task, which qualified for a quality-of-work multiplier.

Kappa saw the notification too. The chitinous giant paused.

"You are gaming the algorithm," Kappa said.

"I am following the instructions," Dale said, moving to the next cage. "Maximize data yield.

Minimize subject damage.

The System rewards thoroughness. I am being thorough.

"

"You are slowing the queue," Kappa said. His voice dropped an octave, vibrating with a threat. "The backlog is critical. Sector 7 is falling behind quota."

"Speed without precision is waste," Dale said. He pulled a fresh vial of sedative from his kit. "If you want more bodies, get more technicians. If you want good data, you follow my pace."

Kappa stepped in front of Dale, blocking his path to the next cage. The giant loomed, his shadow swallowing Dale's flashlight beam.

"Move," Kappa said.

Dale didn't move. He held up his clipboard.

"I have a documentation requirement.

Every extraction must be logged with a pre- and post-harvest neurological scan.

If I skip the post-scan, the entry in your ledger is incomplete. An incomplete entry is a violation.

A violation triggers an audit. An audit stops production. You know this.

"

Kappa stared at him. For a moment, the only sound was the rhythmic hum of the bone-steel machine.

Then, Kappa laughed. It was a horrible, grinding sound.

"You are small," Kappa said. "And you are arrogant. But you are correct about the audit trail. The Authority despises incomplete paperwork."

Kappa stepped aside.

"Proceed.

But know this, Dale Kowalski.

Your efficiency is noted. And your insolence is noted. The Licensing Authority keeps detailed records.

"

Dale walked past him. He didn't look back. He didn't let his hands shake.

He reached the next cage, and the subject was a woman, older than the others. Her hair was gray, matted with sweat.

Her eyes were wide, alert.

Not empty.

Dale froze.

"Subject Six," he said into his recorder. "Status: Alert. Cognitive state: High resistance. Potential for data fragmentation."

Kappa leaned over Dale's shoulder.

"Sedate her."

"I need to assess the risk first," Dale said.

"Assessment is a luxury," Kappa said. "Time is money. Sedate her."

"If I sedate her without assessing her baseline, I might overdose her," Dale said. "If she dies, the harvest fails. Zero points. And I lose my certification for negligent homicide."

Kappa's claw hovered over Dale's neck.

"You have three seconds."

Dale didn't look at Kappa. He looked at the woman. She was mouthing words again. Not *help*.

*Listen.*

Dale tapped his earpiece. He muted Kappa's audio feed to the Authority central command. It was a risky move, but the System allowed local overrides for

"environmental interference."

"Can you hear me?" Dale whispered.

The woman's eyes flickered. A single tear cut a track through the grime on her cheek.

"They're listening," she whispered, so faintly Dale barely caught it.

"No," Dale said. "I cut the feed. You have ten seconds before Kappa notices the drop in signal. Who are you?"

"My name is Elena," she said. "We're not just subjects. We're batteries. But we're also… anchors."

"Anchors for what?" Dale asked, his hand hovering over the sedative injection site.

"For the Rift," she said. "Our memories stabilize the barrier.

When you take them, the barrier weakens.

The monsters aren't just crawling out.

They're being invited in. By the Authority.

"

Dale's blood ran cold. He looked at the console. The data stream was flowing.

It was clean. It was perfect.

And it was feeding something else.

"The Authority isn't fighting the infestation," Dale said, his voice barely audible. "It's farming it."

"Yes," Elena whispered. "And if you finish this extraction, the hole gets bigger. And the next wave… it won't be vermin."

Dale's heart hammered against his ribs. He looked at the START button. The progress bar for Elena's extraction was paused. He had overridden it during the conversation.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** *Protocol 1-A: Cognitive Harvesting.* *Status: Paused.* *Reason: Unregistered Interaction.* *Penalty Warning: Further delays will incur Thoroughness deductions.*

Kappa straightened up. He noticed the paused status. He noticed the silence from the central feed.

"Technician," Kappa said, his voice dangerously soft. "Why have you stopped?"

Dale looked at Elena. She was watching him, hope and terror warring in her dilated pupils.

He looked at Kappa. The giant's claw was still raised, ready to strike.

Dale had a choice. He could press start. He could get the points.

He could keep his certification, and he could survive.

Or he could violate protocol.

But violating protocol meant death.

Unless…

Dale remembered the rule he'd found in the fine print of the Class II Licensing Manual. *Contamination Protocol: Immediate cessation of all operations if the target exhibits signs of active dimensional resonance.*

He didn't have to refuse. He just had to find a violation.

He grabbed Elena's wrist. He didn't sedate her. He didn't extract her. He pressed two fingers against her radial artery.

"Subject Six is exhibiting signs of Class-4 Dimensional Resonance," Dale said, loud enough for Kappa to hear, but quiet enough for the central feed to catch if he unmuted it. "Heart rate irregular. Pupil dilation inconsistent with standard stress response."

Kappa frowned.

"Resonance? She is human. Humans do not resonate."

"Check your scanner," Dale said. "I'm logging a hazard report. Standard procedure dictates I halt extraction until the hazard is isolated."

Kappa looked at the console. The scanner had not detected any resonance. It was reading normal human vitals.

"You are lying," Kappa said.

"Am I?" Dale pointed to the console.

"Run a deep-scan. Look for quantum entanglement signatures. If she's an anchor, the signature will be there.

If it's not, I'll take the deduction. If it is, you have to isolate her or lose your entire sector's clearance.

"

Kappa hesitated. It was a bluff. A dangerous, stupid bluff. If the scanner came back clean, Dale was dead.

But Kappa couldn't take the risk. If the Authority discovered an unisolated anchor, the sector's contract would be voided. Kappa would be incinerated too.

Kappa slammed his claw onto the scan button.

The machine hummed. The lights flickered.

The screen filled with red text.

**WARNING: QUANTUM ENTANGLEMENT DETECTED.** **SOURCE: SUBJECT SIX.** **THREAT LEVEL: EXISTENTIAL.** **ACTION: IMMEDIATE ISOLATION PROTOCOL REQUIRED.**

Kappa stepped back. His chitinous face was unreadable, but his posture was rigid.

"How," Kappa whispered. "How did you know?"

Dale didn't answer, and he couldn't.

He didn't know. Elena had lied, or the Authority had hidden the data, or something else was happening.

But the System believed the report. The System believed the scan.

And for the first time since he walked into the cavern, Dale Kowalski was not a technician.

He was a hazard.

**SYSTEM NOTIFICATION:** *Protocol Violation Detected: Unauthorized Hazard Assessment.* *Penalty: -50 Treatment Points.* *Status: Flagged for Review by Licensing Authority.* *Next Step: Containment of Hazardous Subject.*

Kappa looked at Dale. The giant's eyes were no longer calculating. They were afraid.

"Isolate her," Kappa said. His voice was tight.

"Now.

And if you fail to contain the resonance, I will not incinerate you. I will let the Authority dissect you alive to see what you know.

"

Dale looked at Elena. She wasn't looking at him anymore. She was looking at the ceiling. At the thousands of other cages.

And for the first time, Dale realized that the woman in the cage wasn't asking for help.

She was warning him.

Because the resonance wasn't coming from her, which was coming from him.

Dale's own hand, still resting on the console, glowed. A faint, sickly green light pulsed from beneath his skin. The same light that powered the extraction machine.

He had been harvesting memories. And in doing so, he had absorbed the resonance.

He was no longer just a technician, and he was the breach.

Dale lifted his glowing hand and pressed it against the glass.
