Chapter 218: Documented Breach

# Chapter 218

The field agent saw the man freeze.

Not a flinch. Not a stumble. A complete cessation of motor function, as if his nervous system had simply decided that moving forward was non-compliant with safety protocols.

Vane watched from the shadows of the collapsed atrium, her hand hovering over the hilt of her serrated short-sword. She expected panic. She expected the typical rookie reaction to a Class-4 dimensional breach: screaming, running, or trying to hit the thing with something that wasn't a weapon.

Instead, Dale Kowalski stood still.

He stared at the claw erupting from Mrs. Gable's torso.

The appendage was pale, wet, and segmented, tipped with chitinous black hooks.

It gripped the fabric of her blouse, tearing it away from the skin beneath. Mrs. Gable did not scream.

She had passed out minutes ago, slumped against the support pillar, but her body twitched with violent, jerking spasms.

Vane frowned. She stepped out of the shade.

"Move," she barked. "It's a Spawner. If it touches you, you get infected."

Dale didn't move. He tilted his head to the left. His eyes tracked the movement of the claw, not with fear, but with the clinical detachment of a technician observing a termite tube.

Vane raised her sword. "I said move!"

The claw snapped. It lunged not at Dale, but at Vane.

Vane sidestepped, the blade singing as it carved a shallow arc through the air. The claw missed, smashing into the concrete floor. Dust plumed. The rest of the creature emerged, a wet, glistening mass of limbs pushing through the human host like a bloated tick.

Vane swung again. This time, the blade connected.

Black ichor sprayed across the floor. The System flashed in Vane's vision, but the numbers were low. *Damage: 4. Effectiveness: Poor.*

She cursed. Her certification was high, but her toolkit was optimized for high-mobility combat, not for pinning down a burrowing entity inside a fragile biological container. If she cut too deep, the spores released into the air would contaminate the entire district.

The creature shrieked, a sound like grinding metal. It pulled itself further out, legs skittering across the concrete. One of the legs brushed against Dale's boot.

Dale looked down.

He looked up.

He looked at Vane.

"Stop swinging," Dale said. His voice was flat.

"You're agitating the nest.

The host is still conscious at a sub-cortical level. Stress hormones are accelerating the emergence rate."

Vane blinked. "The host is dead. Look at her."

"The host is in shock. There is a difference." Dale took a half-step forward.

He didn't raise his hands.

He didn't pull out a weapon.

He reached into his jacket pocket and pulled out a small, silver canister.

Vane stared at the canister. It looked like an aerosol spray, but the nozzle was wider, industrial-grade.

"Is that pepper spray?" Vane asked, incredulous.

"It's a localized desiccant," Dale said. "High-concentration salt and silica, pressurized. Designed for mold remediation in damp basements."

"You're going to spray it with salt?"

"I'm going to dry it out."

The creature had fully emerged now. It was the size of a large dog, a nightmare of chitin and exposed nerve endings. It scuttled toward them, mandibles clicking.

Vane raised her sword, but the creature was fast, erratic, and shielded by the limp body of Mrs. Gable, which lay draped over its back like a saddle.

Vane couldn't strike without hitting the civilian.

Dale walked toward the monster.

"Get back!" Vane shouted.

He didn't listen. He was close now. Too close. The creature raised a claw, ready to swipe Dale's face off.

Dale pressed the nozzle.

A hiss erupted. A cloud of white, powdery mist engulfed the creature's head. The desiccant hit the wet, exposed nerve endings instantly.

The creature froze.

It wasn't a magical stun.

It wasn't a damage spike. It was a physiological reaction. The salt drew moisture from the creature's tissues, causing the soft, unprotected joints to stiffen.

The nerve endings shriveled.

The claws locked in place.

The creature screamed, but the sound was muffled, choked by the dry powder clogging its spiracles.

Dale stepped back. He pulled a small, folded piece of paper from his pocket. It was a receipt from a hardware store, dated three days ago. He unfolded it.

"Identification," Dale muttered.

"Ocular structure: compound, lateral. Chitin density: low, suggesting molting phase. Limb articulation: double-jointed.

Habitat: moist, confined spaces."

He looked at the frozen monster. Then he looked at Mrs. Gable's body.

"Host compatibility: high. It's using her body as a humidifier."

Vane lowered her sword. Her eyes were wide. She looked at the creature, then at Dale.

"How did you know that?" she whispered.

Dale didn't answer. He pulled a second canister from his other pocket. This one was smaller, labeled with a red warning symbol.

"System notification," Dale said to the air. "Field Guide update."

Nothing happened.

His certification was null. His Field Guide was restricted. The text had appeared in his vision, but he couldn't access the detailed entry. He couldn't see the weakness listed in the database.

But he had his eyes. And he had his instinct.

He saw the creature struggling against the desiccant. The joints were stiff, but the core—the heart-sac visible through the translucent skin—was pulsing rapidly.

"Intuitive Tracking," Dale said softly.

The System flared in his peripheral vision.

**MECHANIC ACTIVATED: INTUITIVE TRACKING.** **ACCURACY: 88%.** **STRESS LEVEL: MODERATE.** **TARGET WEAKNESS IDENTIFIED: VENTRAL SAC.** **RECOMMENDED ACTION: PHYSICAL EXTRACTION.**

Dale looked at Vane.

"It's in the belly," he said. "The main organ. It's pulsing. It's driving the movement."

"You can't hit it. It's covered by the host."

"I don't need to hit it. I need to expose it."

Dale walked around the creature. He moved with a strange, shuffling gait, keeping his center of gravity low. The creature tried to turn, but its legs were locked by the salt. It could only pivot on its hips.

Dale reached into his bag. He pulled out a pair of heavy-duty, insulated gloves. He pulled out a crowbar.

Not a sword. A crowbar.

"What are you doing?" Vane asked, her voice rising.

"That's a Class-4 threat!

You need a high-tier protocol! A kinetic blast!

A holy ward!"

"I need leverage," Dale said.

He approached the creature from the side. He placed the tip of the crowbar under the creature's ribcage, where the chitin met the soft, exposed flesh of the ventral sac.

He pushed.

The crowbar didn't slide in. It jammed.

Dale grunted. He adjusted his stance. He twisted his wrist, using the fulcrum of the concrete floor to create torque.

The creature shrieked. The claw on the left twitched.

"Wait," Vane said. She stepped forward, her sword ready. "Let me—"

"Stay back," Dale said. "If you swing, you release the spores. This is a containment breach, not a fight."

He pushed harder.

The chitin cracked. A wet, sucking sound echoed in the atrium.

Dale pulled the crowbar back. He thrust it forward again, aiming for the gap he had created. He hooked the tip under a membrane that pulsed with violet light.

He yanked.

With a wet tearing sound, a bulbous, pulsating sac was ripped from the creature's body. It dangled from the crowbar, dripping ichor onto the floor.

The creature went limp.

Its legs gave out. It collapsed onto Mrs. Gable's body, motionless.

Silence returned to the atrium.

Dale dropped the crowbar. He stood there, breathing heavily.

His hands were trembling.

He looked at the sac on the floor. It was still twitching.

He pulled a plastic bag from his kit. He opened it with his teeth. He scooped the sac into the bag. He sealed it with a heat-welding gun from his belt.

He looked at Vane.

"Job done," he said.

Vane stared at him. She looked at the dead monster. She looked at the bag in Dale's hand.

"That's... that's insane," she said. "You just... you just tore its heart out with a crowbar and a salt spray."

"It worked," Dale said.

"Did the System give you points?"

Dale looked at his vision.

**JOB COMPLETE.** **METHOD: NON-LETHAL CONTAINMENT (HOST PRESERVED).** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 9.8.** **PENALTY APPLIED: UNLICENSED OPERATIVE STATUS.** **REWARD: 0 TREATMENT POINTS.** **BONUS: FIELD GUIDE UPDATABLE VIA MANUAL ENTRY ONLY.**

Dale read the text. He felt a cold weight in his stomach.

Zero points.

He had saved the host. He had neutralized the threat. He had done everything perfectly.

And the System had paid him nothing.

Because he was unlicensed.

The Authority didn't recognize his work. It didn't count toward his progress. It was invisible to the bureaucracy that kept the world from collapsing.

Vane walked over to him. She looked at the bag.

"You're not getting anything for that," she said. "You know that, right?"

Dale nodded. He zipped up his jacket.

"I know," he said.

"Then why do it?"

Dale looked at Mrs.

Gable's body. Her skin was pale, but she was breathing.

Shallow, but steady.

The infection had stopped spreading because the source was gone.

"Because if I didn't," Dale said, "she would have exploded. And then the spores would have killed everyone in this block."

Vane looked at him for a long moment.

"You're a technician," she said. "Not a hero."

"I'm a exterminator," Dale said. "There's a difference."

A rumble shook the ground.

Dale looked up.

The ceiling of the atrium was cracking. Dust fell from above.

Vane's eyes widened. She grabbed Dale's arm.

"That wasn't the end," she said. "That was just the first wave."

From the shadows of the upper balcony, something moved. It was larger than the first. It was humanoid, but its limbs were too long, its joints bending the wrong way. It had no face, just a smooth, white mask of chitin.

It pointed at Dale.

Not at Vane. At Dale.

Dale looked at the creature. He didn't feel fear. He felt a familiar, professional irritation.

It had violated the perimeter.

It hadn't filled out a permit.

It hadn't followed protocol.

And it had interrupted his cleanup.

Dale looked at Vane.

"I need a bigger bag," he said.

Vane looked at the approaching horror. She looked at Dale's empty hands.

"You have nothing," she said. "No points. No abilities. No guide."

"I have a crowbar," Dale said.

The creature leaped from the balcony.

It landed ten feet away, cracking the tile. It stood up, unfolding its limbs. It was eight feet tall. It clicked its mandibles, a sound like a machine gun loading.

Dale took a step back.

He looked at the creature.

*Intuitive Tracking* flared again.

**THREAT LEVEL: CLASS 5.** **STRESS LEVEL: HIGH.** **ACCURACY: 42%.** **WARNING: BIOLOGICAL SIGNATURE UNKNOWN.** **SUGGESTION: RETREAT.**

Dale looked at the warning.

He looked at the creature.

He looked at the crowbar on the floor.

He picked it up.

He didn't run.

He checked his watch.

Shift was over in twenty minutes.

If he wanted to get paid for this, he had to file the paperwork before the sun went down.

And he had to kill it first.

But not just kill it.

He had to document it.

He had to verify it.

He had to make sure the nest was gone.

Dale Kowalski tightened his grip on the crowbar.

He took a breath.

He stepped forward.

The creature lunged.
