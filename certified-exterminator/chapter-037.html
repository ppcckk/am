Chapter 37: Measuring the Void

# Chapter 37

The floor gave way at a forty-five-degree angle. Dale caught the doorframe with his left hand and felt the linoleum peel away beneath his boots. The kitchen was gone. In its place: a cavity, roughly six feet deep, lined with something that looked like wet ceramic but moved like muscle.

He counted to three. The Authority's correction had flagged this as a sentinel.

Sentinels were surveying caste, and they didn't fight. They mapped.

Which meant the thing below him wasn't attacking. It was measuring.

Dale pulled himself up.

His clipboard was still attached to his forearm strap. The Authority's entry for HIST-0041-A-3 was still forming, letters appearing one at a time like a slow typewriter.

The creature hadn't fully emerged yet.

The Authority couldn't document what it couldn't see, and he needed to see it. He needed to see it before it decided the house was no longer useful for whatever it was doing.

The cavity pulsed. A section of the ceramic-muscle lining split open and a limb pushed through.

Not an arm.

Not a leg.

A flat, segmented structure with three joints and a terminal plate that pressed against the remaining floor joist and held it in place.

The creature was bracing itself.

Dale recognized the posture. He'd seen it a hundred times in residential inspections. A termite swarm establishing a gallery through a load-bearing beam.

The insect doesn't eat the wood. It replaces it with its own structure, one that looks solid but crumbles under real weight.

Same principle. Different scale.

He dropped to one knee at the edge of the cavity and looked down.

The limb retracted. Something below turned toward him. He couldn't see it, but he could see the light of the kitchen bulb refracting through a wet, translucent surface.

Something with a body cavity that transmitted light like a geode.

The Authority's entry updated.

HIST-0041-A-3. Morphology: [INCOMPLETE]. Behavior: [INCOMPLETE]. Weakness: [INCOMPLETE].

Dale pulled his pen from his shirt pocket. He'd been carrying a ballpoint for fourteen years. He wrote on the clipboard in his own handwriting, below the Authority's text:

"Bracing posture. Structural replacement behavior.

Translucent body cavity.

Likely mineral-organic hybrid. Susceptible to vibration-induced cavity collapse."

The Authority's entry flickered. The morphological section updated to include "translucent body cavity." The behavior section gained "structural replacement."

Dale stared at it. The Authority was reading his notes. Incorporating them.

He wrote faster.

"Terminal plate functions as anchor point. Three-joint limb suggests limited mobility outside braced position. Vulnerable to lateral force applied to anchor plate while bracing."

The Authority's entry updated again. Weakness section: [PROVISIONAL]. Lateral force against anchor plate during bracing cycle.

A provisional entry. Not a full identification, but enough. Dale felt the targeted damage bonus register in his system notification, a faint warmth in his forearms that meant his next strike against this species would land with precision.

He had one shot. The creature was bracing. The anchor plate was visible.

But the 40% debuff was active. Three engagements. This was engagement one.

He reached into his kit and pulled out his pry bar. Fourteen inches of flat steel, standard issue for entry point assessment. He'd used it to test window frames, to check for voids behind drywall, to measure the depth of foundation cracks.

He jammed it into the cavity and hooked the terminal plate.

The creature reacted. The limb jerked. The house groaned.

Dale twisted the pry bar laterally, exactly as the provisional entry suggested, and the plate sheared off with a sound like a wet branch snapping. The limb retracted violently. Below, something shrieked at a frequency Dale felt in his molars.

The cavity collapsed. Not all of it.

The creature's bracing structure failed where the anchor plate had been, and the ceramic-muscle lining buckled inward.

The translucent body folded.

Dale heard the wet pop of internal structures losing their tension.

The Authority's entry completed itself in a flash.

HIST-0041-A-3. MORPHOLOGY: Mineral-organic hybrid, translucent body cavity, three-joint bracing limbs. BEHAVIOR: Structural replacement caste.

Maps infrastructure via load-bearing contact. WEAKNESS: Lateral force against anchor plate during bracing cycle.

Colony role: Surveyor.

Deployed ahead of primary infestation wave.

Dale stepped back from the edge. His clipboard buzzed with a job completion notification. Treatment Points: 0.

He stared at it. Zero.

Then a second notification.

JOB STATUS: PENDING.

Creature eliminated but not fully documented. Field Guide entry completed via provisional submission.

Thoroughness Rating adjustment: -0.3.

Reason: Kill completed before Authority verification cycle. Protocol deviation: Operator contributed to identification process. This is not a violation.

This is a new protocol. Authority is evaluating.

Dale read it twice. Then a third notification appeared.

NEW MECHANIC UNLOCKED: Provisional Identification.

Operator may contribute observational data to Authority entries in real time.

Partial entries grant provisional damage bonuses. Full entries require Authority verification.

Provisional entries do not count toward Field Guide completion percentage, and he exhaled. A loophole.

The Authority had noticed he'd fed it information and decided to formalize it.

That meant other operators could do the same. Or maybe it only worked for him because he'd done it before the Authority had a name for the action.

His clipboard buzzed again. This time it wasn't a notification. It was a correction to his debuff status.

CROSS-CONTAMINATION PENALTY: 1 of 3 engagements consumed. Note: Provisional identification submission constitutes a valid engagement action. Debuff reduced to 2 remaining engagements.

Dale almost smiled. Almost.

He looked at the house. The kitchen was gone. The living room ceiling was sagging where the joists had lost their bracing. The whole structure was compromised.

His phone rang. He didn't recognize the number.

"Kowalski."

"You got a call?" The voice was flat, professional, female. "I'm with the Authority's regional dispatch. Your provisional submission triggered a reclassification review on the entire job site."

"I'm aware."

"You should know what it means. HIST-0041-A-3 is a surveyor caste. The Authority's colony behavior model indicates that surveyors precede a primary wave by between twelve and forty-eight hours. Your job site is a waypoint."

Dale looked at the house.

Looked at the street. Six houses in a row.

All with the same foundation profile he'd written up six weeks ago.

All with the same south crawlspace entry point.

"How many surveyors?" he asked.

"We've detected three in your service area. Possibly more. The Authority is updating its threat model based on your provisional data."

"Where's the primary wave targeting?"

A pause. The kind that meant the answer was worse than the question.

"Your provisional entry noted that surveyors map infrastructure via load-bearing contact. We cross-referenced the structural profiles of your three known sites. They all connect to a single shared infrastructure element."

Dale already knew. He'd written the inspection report. He'd flagged the moisture intrusion at every property. He'd noted the same water table anomaly in his notes.

"The water treatment plant," he said.

"Correct. The Authority is reclassifying the entire job cluster as a Level IV infestation."

Dale felt the word land in his chest like a cold stone. Level IV.

He was Level I, and the certification gap was three full levels.

He couldn't legally engage a Level IV threat. The Authority wouldn't let him.

"I'm not certified for Level IV," he said.

"No. You're not." The dispatch voice didn't soften. "Which is why the Authority is issuing a provisional assignment.

You have six hours to pass the Level III examination. Written and practical.

If you pass, you're authorized to engage the primary wave at the treatment plant.

If you don't, the Authority will dispatch a certified team."

"How long does a Level III exam usually take?"

"Four to six weeks."

"And you're giving me six hours."

"The Authority has determined that your provisional identification capability is relevant to the Level III practical component. You are the only operator in this region who has demonstrated the behavior. We are not offering you a shortcut. We are offering you a compressed timeline because you are the only asset we have within response distance."

Dale looked at the house again, and the ceiling was still sagging; the cavity below was silent now, but he could hear something faint beneath it.

A rhythmic tapping.

Like a surveyor's limb, testing a surface.

Not the one he'd killed. Another one.

"Where's the exam?" he asked.

"Your clipboard. It's loading now."

The screen shifted. A new interface appeared.

Not a notification.

A test form.

The Authority's written examination, compressed into a scrolling document. Forty-seven questions.

Practical component: unknown, but the Authority's interface indicated it would be administered in the field, on-site, against a live target.

Six hours.

Dale pulled up a chair from the living room; it was the only furniture left in the house that hadn't shifted.

He sat down, and he put the clipboard on his knee. He uncapped his pen.

Question one: "Describe the regulatory framework governing cross-contamination penalties for operators deploying protocols outside their assigned species category."

He wrote. His handwriting was small and precise. He'd been writing inspection reports for fourteen years. He knew how to fill out a form.

Question two: "Identify the three stages of the Authority's Thoroughness Scale that correspond to mandatory documentation requirements."

He wrote.

Question three: "Under what conditions may an operator contribute to an Authority Field Guide entry without a full identification?"

He paused. Looked at the entry he'd just fed the Authority. Looked at the provisional notation.

He wrote: "When the operator's observational data is submitted during an active Authority documentation cycle, and the operator has demonstrated species-specific professional knowledge relevant to the entry's incomplete fields."

The Authority marked it correct.

Question four: "A Level IV infestation has been detected at a municipal water treatment facility.

The facility serves a population of 40,000. The infestation is estimated at 200+ organisms.

You are the only operator on-site.

You are Level I. What do you do?"

Dale stopped writing. He read the question again.

It wasn't a test question. It was a scenario. The Authority was asking him what he would do right now, in this moment, with the same parameters he was currently operating under.

He thought about the answer. He thought about the six houses.

The water table.

The surveyors tapping through the foundations.

The primary wave that would come through the treatment plant's intake system and flood the pipes with something that would eat the infrastructure from the inside.

He thought about what a Level I operator could actually do. What tools he had. What protocols he knew.

He wrote: "I cannot engage the primary wave. I can prepare the site. I can identify entry points, establish containment perimeters, and document the infestation pattern so that the certified team arriving later can engage with full Field Guide entries already populated.

I can buy time. I cannot provide force."

The Authority marked it correct, and he kept writing.

Question five. Question six. His hand moved steadily.

Outside, the tapping grew louder.

At question twelve, his clipboard vibrated. A new notification.

PRACTICAL COMPONENT INITIATED. Target: HIST-0041-A-4.

Location: 40 meters from your current position.

Status: Active. Time limit: 90 minutes. You must complete full extermination protocol (identify, document, isolate, eliminate, verify) to pass the practical component.

Dale set down his pen. He stood up. He picked up his kit.

The tapping was coming from the neighbor's house. The one he'd inspected three weeks ago. The one with the same south crawlspace entry point.

He walked outside. The street was empty.

Every door was closed.

Every window was dark.

The Authority's job site perimeter had been active for two hours now, and the residents had evacuated or hidden.

He could see the neighbor's crawlspace access. A concrete hatch, slightly ajar. The same one he'd flagged in his report. The same one he'd recommended sealing.

He hadn't sealed it. He'd written the recommendation. He'd filed the paperwork. He'd done his job.

He walked toward the hatch. His clipboard buzzed with question thirteen. He kept walking.

The hatch was open. The crawlspace below was dark. Something moved in the darkness with a sound like a filing cabinet drawer sliding shut.

Dale knelt at the edge. He pulled out his flashlight. He clicked it on.

The thing in the crawlspace turned toward the light. It was larger than the sentinel. It had no translucent body.

It was matte black, segmented, with a head that resembled a flat iron, and pressed against the crawlspace floor and the joists above it vibrated.

A surveyor. A different one. Larger.

The Authority's entry for HIST-0041-A-4 started to take shape.

MORPHOLOGY: [INCOMPLETE]. BEHAVIOR: [INCOMPLETE]. WEAKNESS: [INCOMPLETE].

Dale opened his clipboard to a fresh page. He uncapped his pen.

Ninety minutes, and the first line was a location.
