Chapter 68: The Gap in the File

# Chapter 68

The wall dissolved from the inside. Not crumbled, not cracked. Dissolved. The drywall went translucent, then transparent, then simply stopped existing, and the space where it had been was filled with a white so clean it made Dale's eyes water.

He stepped back. His boots hit the baseboard and he felt the floor tilt two degrees toward the hole. The clipboard in his left hand caught the light from the breach and the paper went blank. Every word he had written in the last three hours vanished from the page.

He looked at the clipboard again. Still blank.

The white space pulsed once, like a heartbeat made of absence, and then a shape pushed through it. Not a body.

A form.

It filled the room like a filing cabinet: rectangular, purposeful, and devoid of personality. It had no face. It had no limbs.

It had edges, which was the whole of it. A series of right angles arranged into a roughly humanoid volume, and every edge was sharp enough to cut light.

Dale's Field Guide updated.

ENTRY 49: Compliance Auditor (administrative entity).

STATUS: ACTIVE. NOTE: Cannot be treated. Cannot be eliminated.

Cannot be documented as a target species. This entry exists solely to inform you that you are the target.

Dale read it twice. Then he looked at the Auditor. It was three feet from the dissolved wall and moving toward him at the speed of a person walking briskly through an office park.

He looked at his Field Guide.

Entry 48.

His own name. STATUS: ACTIVE.

TEMPLATE: COMPLETE.

The Auditor was two feet from the wall.

Dale flipped to Entry 48 and stared at it. The text was already there.

He had not written it.

It had appeared while he was counting. He had never identified it. He had never documented it.

He had never filled in the weakness field, the habitat notes, the treatment protocol.

The Auditor was one foot from the wall.

He took the pen out of his pocket and did not click it. His hand was steady. It was always steady. Fourteen years of writing code violation reports in kitchens while tenants screamed at him had built something into his hands that fear could not touch.

He uncapped the pen.

The Auditor crossed the threshold. The floor beneath it turned white, then clean, then blank.

Dale watched his own boot prints disappear behind the shape as it passed.

The Auditor was not stepping on the floor. It was erasing the floor's record of being stepped on.

Dale wrote on Entry 48.

SPECIES: Kowalski, Dale. HABITAT: Suburban residential service area, Class II municipal jurisdiction.

The Auditor's leading edge stopped. Not a pause. A correction. Like a printer jamming mid-page.

Dale kept writing.

BEHAVIOR: Diurnal. Solitary. Non-aggressive toward non-target species. Feeds on documentation gaps and unresolved code violations.

The Auditor's edges blurred. The white space around it contracted.

Dale's pen moved faster.

WEAKNESS: Cannot process self-referential documentation. Cannot audit an entry that has been properly identified by its own subject.

He underlined that last line twice. Then he wrote, at the bottom of the entry:

IDENTIFIED BY: Kowalski, Dale. DATE: Current.

The Field Guide pulsed. The entry's status changed.

STATUS: DOCUMENTED.

The Auditor stopped. All of it.

Every edge, every angle, every white space between its right angles.

It halted with the abruptness of a machine receiving an invalid command.

Not broken. Not destroyed.

Simply no longer applicable.

Dale exhaled. He capped his pen. He looked at the Auditor.

The Auditor regarded him, the way a thing without eyes can regard you. It was a direction of attention. A vector of purpose that had been redirected.

DISPATCH: Compliance Auditor has been rendered inapplicable. No Treatment Points awarded.

No Thoroughness Rating adjustment, and the Auditor was not a threat. The Auditor was a function. You have corrected a function.

This is logged.

Dale looked at the blank clipboard in his left hand. The paper was still blank. He set it down on the floor and picked up his phone.

The phone was blank too. Every text message, every photograph, every note he had ever stored on it. Gone. The screen showed only the time, the battery percentage, and the word DISPATCH blinking in the top corner.

He looked back at the Field Guide. Forty-nine entries.

His own was there, properly documented now, with a weakness field filled in.

The targeted damage bonus should have appeared. The system should have given him a number, a percentage, a multiplier that would let him hit himself harder than he could hit anything else.

No number appeared, and he checked again. Flipped back and forth between Entry 48 and the Field Guide's summary page.

No bonus.

No multiplier.

The entry was documented, but the system had not registered it as a valid combat entry.

He thought about that for three seconds.

Then he thought about what it meant that the system had not registered his own documentation as valid. He thought about the fact that he had written the weakness field himself. He thought about the fact that the weakness he had written was that the Auditor could not process self-referential documentation. He thought about whether that was a real weakness or a weakness he had invented to solve the immediate problem.

He thought about whether the system could tell the difference, and the Auditor was still standing in the middle of the room. It had not moved. It had not dissolved. It waited, like a queued process awaiting execution.

Dale looked at it. He looked at his Field Guide. He looked at the blank clipboard on the floor.

He picked up the clipboard. He picked up his pen. He wrote on the blank page:

CODE VIOLATION K-15: Unregistered operator performing self-documentation without prior authorization. CORRECTIVE ACTION: None available. AUTHORITY RESPONSE: Pending.

He held the clipboard up. The Auditor's edges sharpened.

DISPATCH: Kowalski. You have filed a correction report against yourself. Protocol 0 is active.

Protocol 0 has no protocol. Protocol 0 is the act of filing a correction report when the system has produced a violation that the system cannot resolve.

You have produced a violation that the system cannot resolve.

You are the violation. You are filing the report. You are the subject.

You are the inspector.

Dale set the clipboard down.

DISPATCH (cont.): The Auditor cannot correct you. You have documented yourself.

The Auditor cannot document you.

You have identified yourself. The Auditor cannot audit you. You have filed a correction report against yourself.

There is no entity left to perform the function. The Auditor is inapplicable.

The Auditor's edges began to soften. The white space around it expanded, slowly, like a stain spreading through paper.

DISPATCH (cont.): However.

You have not resolved the violation.

You have only documented it. Protocol 0 requires resolution.

Resolution requires an external authority. You cannot be an external authority to yourself. You are at an impasse.

Dale sat down on the floor. The floor was clean where the Auditor had passed.

Not clean in the sense of sanitary.

Clean in the sense of unrecorded. No boot prints. No scuff marks.

No evidence that anyone had ever stood there, and he looked at his Field Guide. Entry 48.

His name.

His habitat. His behavior. His weakness.

He looked at Entry 49. The Auditor. It was still there, but its status had changed.

ENTRY 49: Compliance Auditor. STATUS: INAPPLICABLE.

NOTE: This entity exists to correct undocumented entries.

You have documented yourself.

This entity is now redundant. It will persist until the correction is resolved or the entry is removed.

Dale looked at the Auditor. The Auditor looked at him.

He looked at the phone. The phone was still blank.

He looked at the Field Guide. Forty-nine entries. He counted them.

Fifty.

There were fifty entries. He had forty-nine when the Auditor arrived. He had not written the new one. It was at the bottom of the list.

ENTRY 50: The Impasse. Species: procedural deadlock. STATUS: ACTIVE.

NOTE: This entry was generated by the interaction between Entry 48 and Entry 49. It is not a creature.

It is a state.

You are currently in it. You cannot leave it by documenting it, because you are the one doing the documenting. You cannot leave it by eliminating it, because it is not a target.

You cannot leave it by isolating it, because it is not a location.

You cannot leave it by verifying it, because verification requires an external observer. You are the only observer.

Dale read it three times, and he looked at the Auditor. The Auditor had not moved. It was patient. It possessed every moment the system could allocate, which meant all available time.

Dale opened his Field Guide to Entry 1.

Dimensional Vermin, Rattus profundus. The first thing he had ever identified. The first thing the Authority had ever recognized him for.

He looked at the weakness field. He looked at the treatment protocol.

He looked at the Thoroughness Rating the Authority had assigned to that job. 9.2.

He looked at Entry 50.

The Impasse. No Thoroughness Rating.

No Treatment Points. No protocol.

He looked at the Auditor, and he looked at his phone.

The phone buzzed.

DISPATCH: Kowalski. There is a way out.

You already know it.

You used it in your first three Level IX Examinations. You used it every time. You do not remember using it because the act of using it erases the memory of using it.

That is the cost, which is always the cost. You identify yourself.

You document yourself.

You file the correction. And then the system, having processed a self-referential entry, performs a routine memory consolidation to prevent recursive loops. You forget.

You wake up in the examination room with no memory of what you just did. You see forty-seven entries.

You count them.

You find forty-eight.

You begin again.

Dale looked at the Field Guide.

DISPATCH (cont.): This is your fourth time. You have passed three others. You do not remember them.

The only reason you are reading this message is that you filed a correction report against yourself, which created Entry 50, which the memory consolidation cannot erase because it is not a memory. It is an entry.

The entry is the only thing that survives the loop, and the entry is the only thing that tells you what you have already done.

Dale looked at Entry 50.

ENTRY 50: The Impasse.

STATUS: ACTIVE. NOTE: This entry was generated by the interaction between Entry 48 and Entry 49.

It is not a creature, which is a state. You are currently in it. You cannot leave it by documenting it, because you are the one doing the documenting.

You cannot leave it by eliminating it, because it is not a target. You cannot leave it by isolating it, because it is not a location.

You cannot leave it by verifying it, because verification requires an external observer.

You are the only observer, and he read the note again. Then he read it a third time.

He looked at the Auditor. The Auditor was still there. Still waiting. Still inapplicable.

He looked at the blank clipboard. He looked at his pen. He looked at the Field Guide.

He opened Entry 48. He crossed out the weakness field. He wrote a new one.

WEAKNESS: Cannot be audited by itself. Cannot be corrected by itself. Cannot be resolved by itself. Requires an external authority to perform the final step.

He underlined "external authority" twice.

He looked at the Auditor. The Auditor's edges stopped softening. They held. They waited.

He looked at his phone.

DISPATCH: Kowalski. You have identified the requirement.

You have not identified the source. There is no external authority within the examination room. There is no external authority within your jurisdiction.

There is no external authority within the Authority's current operational parameters. You have identified the requirement and you have not identified the source.

This is logged.

Your Thoroughness Rating for this examination is pending. It cannot be calculated until the examination is resolved.

Dale looked at the Field Guide. Fifty entries. He counted them again.

Fifty-one.

ENTRY 51: External Authority (species: unknown).

STATUS: UNIDENTIFIED. NOTE: You need one.

You do not have one.

You have never had one. Every examination you have ever passed has been resolved by a version of yourself that you will not remember. The version that resolves this one will not be you.

It will be someone else. You must find them before the Auditor becomes applicable again.

The Auditor will become applicable again in six minutes.

You have five minutes and forty seconds.

Dale looked at the Auditor. The Auditor looked at him.

He stood up. He picked up the clipboard. He picked up the pen. He walked toward the door that was no longer there, because the Auditor had erased it, because the wall that contained it no longer existed.

He walked into the white space, which was not white. It was the absence of information. It was the space between records. It was the gap in the file.

He walked through it, clipboard held before him like a shield, writing as he documented the undocumented, while the Auditor sharpened behind him.

It wanted the first mistake in his file.
