Chapter 294: Protocol Let's Begin

# Chapter 294

The clipboard hit the concrete. The crack was audible, a sharp snap that echoed in the silence where the spawn's screaming had abruptly ceased.

Dale stared at the fractured plastic. His hand hovered over it, fingers trembling. Not from fear. From violation.

He hadn't picked it up. Not yet.

Protocol dictated that equipment damage be logged before retrieval.

If he touched the tool with a compromised field status, the Authority might flag the subsequent data entry as corrupted. A Class IV infestation cleanup, botched by improper handling of the recording medium, would wipe out his progress.

Marla stood frozen. Her flashlight beam swept upward, illuminating the ceiling. Or what had been the ceiling.

The stone was gone.

In its place hung a void of absolute black, punctuated by millions of unblinking, wet orbs. They were not distant.

They were close enough to count.

Close enough to see the iris patterns, swirling like oil on water, and the System prompt had vanished, but the warning lingered in Dale's mind. *CERTIFICATION LEVEL REQUIRED: IX.*

He was Level V.

Level V was a technician. Level IX was an architect of reality.

A drop of viscous fluid landed on Dale's boot. Then another. From above.

He looked down at his boots. The leather was absorbing the substance. It wasn't water. It was thick, translucent, and smelled faintly of copper and ozone.

"Marla," Dale said. His voice was flat.

"Do not move.

Do not breathe.

The particulate density is increasing.

"

Marla didn't respond. She was staring at the eyes. One of them, larger than the rest, was directly above her head.

It blinked. A slow, heavy shutter of flesh.

Dale reached for his clipboard, and he ignored the crack. He picked it up. The paper inside was damp.

The ink on the previous page had smeared, turning the identification of *Rattus profundus* into an illegible blur.

He pulled a fresh sheet from the back of the pad. His hands moved with mechanical precision. He drew a box.

**FIELD ENTRY: OBSERVATION ONLY.** **SUBJECT: EYES (UNIDENTIFIED).** **LOCATION: SUBTERRANEAN VOID / CEILING.** **STATUS: NON-AGGRESSIVE (PENDING).**

The pen scratched. The sound was loud in the quiet.

The eye blinked again. A low hum vibrated through the floor. The stone walls of the tunnel pulsed, expanding and contracting like a lung.

Marla finally moved. She stepped back, her boot crunching on the gravel.

"Dale," she whispered. "Look at the floor."

Dale looked, and the spawn that had been fleeing were gone. In their place, the floor was covered in a thin layer of gray slime.

The slime was moving.

It flowed toward Dale, pooling around his ankles. It wasn't attacking. It was coating.

He tried to lift his foot. It was stuck. The slime had hardened instantly.

"Contamination event," Dale said. He looked at his wrist.

The Field Guide interface was dead.

No status bar.

No health check. Just static.

"We need to extract," Marla said. She raised her rifle. The barrel trembled.

"Negative," Dale said. "Extraction is a Class II procedure. Requires a clean perimeter. The perimeter is compromised."

He pointed his pen at the walls. The stone was sweating. The moisture was rising, merging with the slime. The entire room was becoming a single, cohesive organism.

"If we shoot, we breach the integrity of the chamber," Dale said. "If we breach the chamber, the Authority will classify this as a structural failure. My report becomes inadmissible.

I lose the points. We lose the entry fee.

We die in a lawsuit.

"

Marla lowered the rifle. Her knuckles were white.

"So what do we do?"

Dale looked at the ceiling. The eyes were watching. They weren't hostile.

They were curious.

Or perhaps, judging, and he looked at the smear on his clipboard. The ink was still wet. He could rewrite the entry. He could change the classification.

If he classified the eyes as *Habitat Features*, they were part of the environment. No threat. No combat required.

If he classified them as *Predators*, he needed to fight. He couldn't.

If he classified them as *Auditors*, he needed to submit a report.

He needed to submit a report.

But he couldn't write. The paper was ruined.

Dale reached into his pack. He pulled out a digital slate, a backup device approved for Class III infestations. It was old, clunky, but it had a hard drive.

He typed.

**FIELD ENTRY: AUDIT LOG.** **SUBJECT: THE EYES.** **OBSERVATION: SUBJECTS EXHIBIT PASSIVE OBSERVATION BEHAVIOR.** **INTERACTION: NONE.** **CONCLUSION: HABITAT FEATURE. DO NOT ENGAGE.**

He hit submit.

The System flashed.

**REJECTED.** **CLASSIFICATION ERROR: HABITAT FEATURES DO NOT POSSESS CONSCIOUSNESS. SUBMIT REVISED ENTRY.**

Dale paused. The eyes blinked in unison. A wave of pressure pushed against his eardrums.

They did possess consciousness, and he changed the entry.

**SUBJECT: THE EYES.** **OBSERVATION: SUBJECTS EXHIBIT ACTIVE SURVEILLANCE.** **INTERACTION: PASSIVE.** **CONCLUSION: PREDATORY OBSERVERS. THREAT LEVEL: UNKNOWN.**

**REJECTED.** **CLASSIFICATION ERROR: THREAT LEVEL CANNOT BE UNKNOWN. ESTIMATE OR WITHDRAW.**

Dale grit his teeth, and the slime had reached his knees. He tried to lift his leg. It didn't budge.

The slime was calcifying, turning into a gray shell around his boots.

Marla fired.

A single shot. The bullet struck the floor.

The impact didn't crack the stone. It splashed. The bullet was absorbed into the slime, dissolving into the gray mass.

The slime rippled. It rushed toward Marla.

She screamed. It wasn't a word. It was a sound of pure, primal panic.

Dale didn't look at her. He looked at his slate.

He needed a new mechanic. A loophole.

The Authority rewarded thoroughness. It rewarded identification.

He hadn't identified the *source* of the eyes. He had only identified the eyes.

He needed to identify the thing that *held* the eyes, and he looked at the walls. The sweating stone. The pulsing rhythm.

The walls weren't stone. They were skin.

The whole tunnel was a body.

And the eyes were just organs.

Dale typed furiously.

**FIELD ENTRY: SYSTEMIC INFESTATION.** **SUBJECT: HOST ORGANISM.** **IDENTIFICATION: CHITINOUS HYBRID / DIMENSIONAL ANCHOR.** **WEAKNESS: NEURAL LAYERING.** **ACTION: ISOLATE HOST NEURAL PATHWAYS.**

He didn't have a protocol for isolating neural pathways. He didn't have Level VI access.

But he had a crowbar, and he looked at the crowbar in his other hand. He looked at the wall nearest to him. A vein, thick and black, pulsed beneath the translucent skin of the tunnel wall.

He swung.

The crowbar struck the vein.

It didn't bleed. It *sang*. A high, resonant tone that vibrated through Dale's bones.

The eyes above flashed white.

**SYSTEM NOTICE: TARGET WEAKNESS IDENTIFIED.** **DAMAGE BONUS: +40% EFFECTIVE DAMAGE.** **WARNING: UNAUTHORIZED USE OF EXTERNAL TOOL AGAINST BIOLOGICAL STRUCTURE.** **PENALTY: FIELD GUIDE CORRUPTION (24 HOURS).**

Dale didn't care. He swung again. And again.

Each strike hit the vein. Each strike increased the damage bonus.

The vein burst. Not with blood, but with light. Blinding, white light poured out of the wall.

The slime on Dale's legs receded. It pulled back, retreating from the light.

Marla fell to her knees, gasping. The slime on her boots had dried and cracked, flaking off like dead skin.

The eyes above were closing. One by one. The sky of stars was shutting down.

Dale kept hitting. He needed to verify. He needed to see if the organism died.

If it died, the infestation was cleared.

If it didn't, he was digging a grave.

The light grew brighter. The humming stopped. The floor stopped pulsing.

Then, the ceiling collapsed.

Not down. *In.*

The eyes vanished. The void vanished. The ceiling reappeared as solid stone.

But the stone was different. It was scarred. Blackened. And in the center of the room, where the light had burst, a hole had formed.

A hole in the ground.

Not a hole in the tunnel floor. A hole in *reality*.

Through the hole, Dale could see something else.

A city.

Ruined, gray, and silent. Buildings twisted like melted wax.

And in the streets, things were moving. Small things. Lots of them.

**SYSTEM NOTICE: INFESTATION NOT CLEARED.** **SUB-STRATA DISCOVERED.** **NEW SUBLEVEL ADDED TO FIELD GUIDE.** **CERTIFICATION LEVEL ADJUSTED: V (PENDING REVIEW).** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 3.2 (SLOPPY).**

Dale stared at the hole.

3.2, and he had just leveled up his damage bonus. He had identified a systemic weakness. He had saved Marla.

And he had been rated 3.2.

Sloppy.

He had used the wrong tool. He hadn't documented the secondary sublevel before destroying the primary anchor. He had skipped the isolation step.

The Authority didn't care about survival. It cared about procedure.

Marla stood up. She looked at the hole. She looked at Dale.

"What did you do?"

"I violated protocol," Dale said. He dropped the crowbar. It clattered against the stone.

He looked at his slate. The entry for *Chitinous Hybrid* was gone. Corrupted.

In its place was a new entry.

**SUBJECT: THE HOLE.** **STATUS: UNCLASSIFIED.** **ACTION REQUIRED: IMMEDIATE VISUAL INSPECTION.**

The hole was pulling air. A wind was rising from it, cold and smelling of ash.

Something crawled out of the hole, which was small. No bigger than a rat. But it had too many legs. And where its face should be, there was a tiny, perfect replica of Dale's clipboard.

It clicked its legs. *Click-click-click.*

It looked at Dale.

**FIELD ENTRY UPDATED.** **SUBJECT: PARASITIC ARCHIVIST.** **THREAT LEVEL: CLASS IX.** **WEAKNESS: UNKNOWN.**

Dale stepped back. The wind from the hole was getting stronger. The ruined city beyond was starting to bleed into the tunnel. Gray dust blew out, coating the floor.

Marla raised her rifle again.

"Shoot it," she said.

"No," Dale said. "If you shoot it, you contaminate the entry. I need to observe it. I need to write the form."

"The hole is getting bigger, Dale!"

"The hole is the entry point," Dale said. "The Arhivist is the sample. If I don't document the sample, the Authority won't authorize the seal."

The Arhivist crawled toward him. It moved with jerky, insectoid precision. Its clipboard-face was blank.

Dale raised his pen. He raised his hand.

He didn't attack. He didn't run.

He wrote.

**OBSERVATION: SUBJECT EXHIBITS MIMICRY BEHAVIOR.** **WEAKNESS: LACK OF ORIGINAL DATA?**

The Arhivist stopped. It tilted its head. The tiny clipboard on its face flickered.

It pulled a pen from its leg joint.

It wrote on its own face.

**ERROR.**

The word appeared in glowing red ink on the creature's shell.

Dale frowned. "It's correcting my note."

The creature's pen moved faster.

**ERROR: INSUFFICIENT DATA.**

It lunged.

Not at Dale. At the slate.

It snatched the digital slate from Dale's hand.

It bit through the screen.

Sparks flew. The device died.

The creature dropped the broken slate. It looked at Dale.

**STATUS: REPORT SUBMITTED.** **AUDIT COMPLETE.** **NEXT STEP: INSPECT AUTHOR.**

The hole widened. More of the gray dust poured out.

And behind the Arhivist, more things crawled.

Hundreds of them. All with clipboard faces.

They weren't attacking. They were waiting.

They were waiting for Dale to fill out the next form.

Marla grabbed Dale's arm.

"We have to run."

"If we run, they follow," Dale said. He looked at the swarm. "They're an infestation of bureaucracy. You can't outrun paperwork."

He looked at the crowbar on the floor, and he picked it up.

He looked at the Arhivist.

"Okay," Dale said. "Let's see if you can handle a correction."

He swung the crowbar at the creature's legs.

But before the metal could connect, the ground beneath them gave way.

Not the floor. The *concept* of the floor.

The world tilted. The ruined city rushed upward to meet them.

Dale fell into the hole, and he didn't hit the ground.

He hit a desk.

A large, wooden desk, sitting in the middle of a gray, infinite office space.

Behind the desk sat a figure. It was tall, thin, and made of the same gray stone as the tunnel walls. It wore a tie.

It looked at Dale. It looked at the crowbar.

It smiled.

**WELCOME TO THE INTERVIEW.** **PLEASE STATE YOUR NAME AND CERTIFICATION LEVEL.**

Dale stood up. He brushed the gray dust off his uniform. He straightened his tie.

"Dale Kowalski," he said. "Level II Technician."

The stone figure picked up a red pen.

"Class II," it said. "That is a very low number."

It circled something on a stack of papers.

"You have been flagged for excessive thoroughness."

Dale froze.

"Excessive?"

"Yes," the figure said.

"You are thorough to a fault. You document the mundane.

You identify the invisible.

You waste time.

"

It slid a paper across the desk, which was a termination notice.

**TERMINATION OF LICENSE: PENDING.** **REASON: INCOMPATIBLE METHODOLOGY.** **ALTERNATIVE: REASSIGNMENT.**

Dale looked at the paper. He looked at the pen.

"If I refuse," Dale said, "I am unauthorized."

"If you accept," the figure said, "you become a surveyor. No combat. No extermination. Just mapping."

Dale looked at the gray office. He looked at the endless rows of desks stretching back into the fog.

He looked at his crowbar.

And then he looked at the figure's pen, and the pen was red. It was sharp. It was a tool.

Dale reached into his pocket. He pulled out a spare pen. Blue ink.

He signed the termination notice.

Then he flipped the page.

On the back, he wrote:

**APPEAL FILED.** **BASIS: ERROR IN THOROUGHNESS CALCULATION.** **EVIDENCE: FIELD GUIDE CORRUPTION CAUSED BY UNAUTHORIZED OBSERVERS (THE EYES).** **REQUEST: RE-EVALUATION OF CLASSIFICATION LEVEL.**

He handed the paper back.

The stone figure looked at the blue ink.

It didn't blink.

It picked up the red pen.

It crossed out the termination.

It wrote a new line.

**APPEAL DENIED.** **BUT...**

It paused.

**...YOU ARE NOW UNDER REVIEW FOR LEVEL VI.** **PREREQUISITE: SUCCESSFUL DEFENSE OF YOUR OWN LICENSE.**

The room shifted. The desk dissolved. The floor opened up.

Below him, the ruined city was changing. The ruins were rebuilding themselves. Buildings rose from the ash. And in the streets, the human shapes were turning toward him.

They were wearing suits. They were holding briefcases.

They were the Authority.

And they were angry.

**PRACTICAL EXAM INITIATED.** **OBJECTIVE: SURVIVE THE AUDIT.** **TIME LIMIT: NONE.** **CONSEQUENCE OF FAILURE: ERASURE.**

Dale stood on the edge of the abyss. He looked down at the army of bureaucrats marching toward him.

He looked at his crowbar, and he looked at his blue pen.

He clicked his tongue.

"Protocol," he said. "Let's begin."
