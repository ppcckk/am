Chapter 149: Documentation Incomplete

# Chapter 149

The first thing Dale did was check his wrist.

Not for the time. For the plate.

It had grown. A quarter-inch of dark, matte chitin had erupted from the radius of his left forearm, pushing through the fabric of his long-sleeve button-down. It was hard, cold, and vibrating with a low-frequency hum that rattled his teeth.

Gary was still staring. The man's mouth was open, a sliver of drool threatening to escape, but his eyes were wide, locked on the black patch of shell spreading toward Dale's elbow.

"Don't blink," Dale said again. His voice sounded wet.

The countdown in his peripheral vision ticked down. *59. 58. 57.*

Dale didn't panic. Panic wasted oxygen and increased heart rate, both of which accelerated metabolic processes. If the infestation was growing, he needed to slow it down. He needed protocol.

He needed a workspace.

"Gary," Dale said, not looking up. "Get me a pen.

A black gel pen, if you have it.

And a piece of cardboard. Not paper. Something with grain.

"

"What?" Gary's voice cracked. "Dale, your arm is—"

"The arm is compromised.

The host is compromised. The *report* is the only thing that isn't.

" Dale grabbed the clipboard that lay on the linoleum.

The Authority's letter sat on top, crisp and damning, and he flipped it over.

On the back, there was nothing but blank, lined stock.

"Cardboard," Dale repeated. "Now."

Gary scrambled. He knocked over a chair, kicked a vending machine, and finally ripped a piece of packaging from a crushed soda carton. He handed it to Dale with trembling hands.

Dale accepted it. He clicked his pen.

*50 seconds.*

He didn't write a diagnosis. He didn't write a plea for help. He wrote an Incident Report.

**INCIDENT REPORT: SELF-TERMINATION PROTOCOL FAILURE** **SUBJECT:** KOWALSKI, DALE (CLASS II) **LOCATION:** SECTOR 7 BREAK ROOM **VIOLATION CODE:** UNLICENSED ORGANIC GROWTH (SPECIES UNKNOWN) **STATUS:** ACTIVE CONTAMINATION

The act of writing forced his brain into procedural mode. The System notification pulsed red, screaming *SELF-TERMINATION INITIATED*, but Dale ignored the emotional valence. He treated the notification as a code violation notice. A citation.

He looked at the chitin on his arm. It wasn't just growing; it was *organizing*.

The bumps weren't random tumors, and they were forming a lattice.

A shell.

*40 seconds.*

"It's not cancer," Dale muttered, more to himself than to Gary. "Cancer doesn't have a carapace structure. Cancer doesn't respond to external documentation."

He pressed the pen harder. The ink smeared against the rough cardboard.

"Gary. Did you see the Inspector's clipboard? Before it vanished?"

Gary shook his head, tears welling in his eyes.

"No. It was gone. It was just... the letter."

"The Inspector was a Proctor. The letter says I failed." Dale's hand shook, but he stabilized it against his thigh. "Proctors don't fail candidates for killing them. Proctors fail candidates for *incomplete documentation*."

*30 seconds.*

Dale's eyes darted across the blank space on the back of the letter. He filled it. Not with words, but with observations.

**OBSERVATION 1: CHITIN LATTICE FORMATION.** **OBSERVATION 2: HOST TISSUE INTEGRATION IS SYMBIOTIC, NOT PARASITIC.** **OBSERVATION 3: THE GROWTH IS RESPONDING TO THE THREAT DETECTION PROTOCOL.**

He paused. The pen hovered.

If the growth was responding to the threat detection, then the System was treating his body as an infestation site. And if he was the infestation site, he needed to treat it.

But you can't exterminate yourself. You have to *isolate* the problem first.

*25 seconds.*

"I don't have a license," Dale said, his voice dropping to a whisper. "I don't have a license, so I can't deploy a Protocol. Protocols require a valid Class II or higher license."

Gary grabbed Dale's other arm.

"We have to get you to a hospital. Or a rift. Anything."

"No." Dale jerked his arm away. The chitin plates on his forearm cracked audibly. "Hospitals don't have Field Guides.

Hospitals don't have certification tracks. They'll amputate.

That's sloppy work.

Amputation leaves residual tissue. Residual tissue means re-infestation. Re-infestation is a code violation.

"

*20 seconds.*

Dale looked at the System notification again.

**ACTION REQUIRED: SELF-TERMINATION PROTOCOL INITIATED IN 20 SECONDS.** **NOTE: PROTOCOL REQUIRES TARGET IDENTIFICATION. TARGET NOT FOUND.**

The notification flickered. It stuttered.

*Target not found.*

That was the loophole.

The Self-Termination Protocol was an automated ability granted to unlicensed hosts to prevent dimensional contamination. It targeted the

"Infestation Subject." But the System identified infestations by matching them to known Field Guide entries.

Dale's Field Guide, populated over years of diligent work, contained entries for *Rattus profundus*, *Acid Beetles*, and *Dimensional Mites*. It did not contain an entry for *Kowalski, Dale*.

The System couldn't identify what he was. It couldn't find the target because the target was the user.

And the user was writing a report on the target.

*15 seconds.*

Dale wrote furiously.

**TARGET IDENTIFICATION PENDING.** **REASON: HOST/INFESTATION SYMBIOSIS PREVENTS EXTERNAL IDENTIFICATION.** **PROPOSED SOLUTION: CROSS-CONTAMINATION ERROR.**

He dropped the pen. He looked at Gary.

"Gary. Are you licensed?"

Gary blinked, confused by the sudden shift in topic.

"What? No.

I'm a accountant, Dale.

What does that have to do with your arm turning into bug armor?

"

"Do you have a clipboard?"

"I... I have a tablet. In my bag."

"Get it. Open a text file. Type exactly this: *UNLICENSED INFESTATION: DALE KOWALSKI. SPECIES: UNKNOWN.*"

Gary fumbled with his bag, pulling out a cracked tablet. His fingers slipped on the screen.

"Why?"

"Because the Authority's system is buggy," Dale said. He watched the chitin creep past his wrist, moving toward his shoulder. "It relies on cross-referencing. If I flag myself as an unlicensed infestation, and you, a non-licensed civilian, document it, you create a data conflict."

*10 seconds.*

Gary typed. "Okay. *Unlicensed infestation: Dale Kowalski.*"

"Now," Dale said, his voice tight. "Add a note. 'Suspected misidentification. Host appears to be the technician.'"

*9. 8. 7.*

Dale closed his eyes. He focused on the sensation of the chitin.

It was hot now.

Burning, and the System was trying to resolve the error.

*Target not found. Target not found. Target not found.*

The red text in his vision glitched. The numbers stopped ticking.

*5.*

The silence in the Break Room was absolute. The vending machine stopped humming. The flickering lights froze in mid-dip.

*4.*

Dale opened his eyes, and the System notification had changed.

**ERROR 404: TARGET SUBJECT NOT FOUND.** **ATTEMPTING CROSS-CONTAMINATION RESOLUTION.**

A new window popped up. It wasn't red. It was amber.

**NOTICE: CROSS-CONTAMINATION PENALTY APPLIED.** **PROTOCOL: SELF-TERMINATION (CLASS 9) MISAPPLIED TO NON-TARGET ENTITY.** **PENALTY: -40% EFFICIENCY ON ALL COMBAT ABILITIES FOR 24 HOURS.** **FIELD GUIDE ENTRY

"SELF" CORRUPTED FOR 24 HOURS.**

The chitin on his arm stopped growing.

The burning subsided, replaced by a dull, throbbing ache. The black plates didn't recede, but they settled. They became part of him, rigid and permanent.

Dale exhaled. It was a long, shaky breath that rattled in his chest.

*3. 2. 1.*

The timer hit zero.

Nothing happened.

Dale slumped against the table. His legs gave out, but he caught himself on the counter. He stared at his arm.

The black chitin was there. It was real.

It was heavy.

"You're alive," Gary whispered. He looked at the tablet in his hands, then at Dale. "You... you broke it. You broke the system."

"I exploited a documentation error," Dale corrected. He straightened his shirt, wincing as the fabric pulled against the new plates on his arm. "The Authority rewards thoroughness.

But it also punishes sloppy identification.

By making the System unable to identify me as a monster, I forced it to treat me as a technician. And technicians don't get terminated.

They get audited.

"

He picked up his clipboard. He picked up the pen.

"We need to go."

"Go where?" Gary asked. "The police? The rift?"

"Dale looked at the corrupted Field Guide icon in his mind. It was a greyed-out silhouette of his own face.

"Not yet. The cross-contamination penalty means I can't use my Class II protocols effectively. My spray efficiency is down.

My trap sensitivity is halved. I'm crippled, Gary.

"

He looked at the door.

"But I'm not unlicensed. Not technically. I have a license. It's just... suspended pending investigation."

Dale walked to the door. He opened it.

The hallway was empty. The air smelled of ozone and old coffee.

"Where are we going?" Gary asked, following him.

"To the nearest Class I Hazard Zone," Dale said. "I need to re-calibrate my Field Guide. And I need to find something small. Something I can document, isolate, and eliminate without using a Protocol."

"Why?"

"Because if I don't fix my Guide in 24 hours, the Authority will revoke the suspension. And if they revoke the suspension, they won't just revoke my license."

Dale looked down at his arm. The chitin plates shifted, clicking softly.

"They'll revoke my body."

They walked out of the Break Room. The corridor was long, lined with fluorescent lights that buzzed like dying flies.

As they rounded the corner, Dale stopped.

At the end of the hall, a door was open. It was the door to the Maintenance Closet.

Standing in the doorway was a figure, which was wearing a hazmat suit. White. Pristine. Unmarked.

The figure turned. Its helmet reflected the fluorescent lights. There was no face inside. Just darkness.

And in its hands, it held a clipboard.

Dale's hand tightened on his own clipboard.

The figure raised its hand. It pointed at Dale.

**SYSTEM NOTIFICATION:** **NEW PROCTOR DETECTED.** **NAME: THE AUDITOR.** **THREAT LEVEL: CLASS IX.**

Dale felt his stomach drop. The Auditor wasn't here to test him. The Auditor was here to inspect the damage.

And Dale was the damage.

"Gary," Dale said, his voice flat. "Run."

"What?"

"Run now. I have to write the report before it sees me."

Gary ran.

Dale didn't run. He sat down on the floor. He placed his clipboard on his knees. He uncapped his pen.

The Auditor stepped into the hallway. The sound of its boots on the tile was slow, deliberate. *Clack. Clack. Clack.*

Dale wrote.

**INCIDENT REPORT: SECONDARY CONTAMINATION EVENT.** **SUBJECT: AUDITOR (CLASS IX PROCTOR).** **STATUS: ACTIVE OBSERVATION.**

He didn't look up. He couldn't look up. If he looked up, he might freeze. If he froze, he failed.

He had to document the Auditor. He had to find the weakness in the Field Guide.

But the Field Guide was corrupted.

Dale looked at the black chitin on his arm.

*Target not found.*

He smiled. It was a grim, tight smile.

He had a mirror, and he described the Auditor's armor. The seams. The joints. The material.

He wasn't fighting the Auditor. He was building the entry.

And as the Auditor raised its hand, a blue light coalescing in its palm, Dale wrote one last line.

**WEAKNESS IDENTIFICATION: PENDING ANALYSIS.** **CROSS-CONTAMINATION RISK: HIGH.**

The Auditor fired.

Dale didn't move. He kept writing.

The beam hit him.

It didn't burn. It froze.

The light encased his arm, his torso, his face.

The chitin on his arm turned white.

Dale's last thought wasn't of pain. It was of the form he had left unfinished on his clipboard.

*Did he sign it?*

No, and he hadn't signed it.

**SYSTEM NOTIFICATION:** **DOCUMENTATION INCOMPLETE.** **SUBJECT STATUS: UNVERIFIED.** **ACTION: QUARANTINE.**

The world went white.

Someone else finished signing the form.
