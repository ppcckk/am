Chapter 200: The Infestation Is You

# Chapter 200

The pen snapped in half.

Dale stared at the broken plastic tube in his hand, the ink bleeding into his palm in a dark, jagged stain. It wasn't just a pen.

It was the last functional writing instrument in his field kit, and now it was useless.

The clipboard beneath it remained blank. Without the record, the violation wasn't real. Without the record, the Authority couldn't see what had happened to Yura.

He looked up. The elevator doors were sealed shut. The faceless figure inside had vanished, or perhaps it had never been there at all, leaving only the sterile hum of the mechanical shaft behind the steel.

Yura was still lunging. Her momentum carried her forward, but the air in front of her had solidified into a translucent, hexagonal barrier. She slammed against it, her body ricocheting backward with a wet thud. The blue symbol on her forehead pulsed violently, casting long, frantic shadows against the hallway walls.

*PROHIBITED ACT.*

The words weren't just glowing; they were vibrating.

A low, resonant hum filled the corridor, the kind of frequency that made teeth ache and vision blur. Dale felt it in his molars.

He recognized the sensation.

It was the sound of a containment field failing.

He dropped the broken pen. His hands shook, not from fear, but from the sudden, crushing weight of protocol violation.

He had spent twenty years ensuring that every entry point was sealed, every nest documented, every treatment logged.

He knew that paperwork wasn't bureaucracy.

Paperwork was the cage. And Yura had just tried to rewrite the bars.

"Stay down," Dale said. His voice was flat, devoid of inflection.

Yura scrambled to her feet, rubbing her shoulder where she'd hit the barrier. "I can't move. The floor is sticky."

Dale looked at the tile. It wasn't sticky. It was covered in a thin, iridescent film that oozed from the cracks in the grout.

The walls were sweating a clear, viscous fluid that smelled faintly of ozone and old copper. The infestation had breached the structural integrity of the elevator lobby.

The Rift wasn't just leaking monsters; it was leaking rules.

He tapped the side of his head. The System notification appeared in his peripheral vision, red and blinking.

**WARNING: LOCAL REALITY INSTABILITY DETECTED.** **CAUSE: UNAUTHORIZED EDIT OF LICENSING CODEX.** **CLASS: CLASS-9 ADMINISTRATIVE HAZARD.**

Dale blinked. Class-9. He had never seen a Class-9.

He had dealt with broodmothers, acid-spitting beetles, and the occasional dimensional wolf, but those were Class-4, maybe Class-5 if they were particularly nasty.

This was the top of the food chain. This was the thing that ate the other things.

And it was enforcing the rules.

"Yura," he said. "What did you do?"

"I fixed it," she said, breathing hard. "The protocol was wrong. The containment spell had a syntax error. I corrected it."

"You corrected a universal law," Dale said.

He reached into his belt and pulled out a pair of nitrile gloves. He snapped them on, the sound sharp in the quiet hallway. "You don't correct laws.

You follow them. If a protocol has a flaw, you report it.

You don't edit it in the middle of a live engagement."

"It was going to kill us," Yura snapped.

"It didn't," Dale said. "Because I interrupted it."

"You interrupted the save," Yura said. "Now it's punishing you both."

The ceiling tiles buckled.

Dust rained down, mixing with the iridescent sludge on the floor. Dale looked at his clipboard.

He needed to write something. Anything. A violation code.

A containment order. A request for backup.

He needed to engage the Authority, to trigger the bureaucratic machinery that kept the Rift from swallowing the building whole.

But his pen was broken.

He looked at the sludge on the floor. It was moving. Not randomly, but with purpose.

It was flowing toward the elevator, forming a channel.

The faceless thing wasn't gone. It was waiting.

It was processing the edit.

And then it was coming out.

The floor erupted. Not with a monster, but with a shape. A geometric abstraction of limbs and angles, made of the same iridescent sludge, hardened into a jagged, translucent form. It had no face, no eyes, only a series of floating, rotating symbols that spun around its core.

**ENTITY DETECTED: AUDITOR.** **SUBTYPE: CODE ENFORCER (CLASS-9).** **STATUS: ACTIVE.**

Dale's Field Guide flashed open in his mind. He had never encountered an Auditor before. The entry populated instantly, the text scrolling rapidly as the System analyzed the threat.

*Auditors do not kill. They erase. They remove entities that violate the Licensing Codex from existence, treating them as errors to be deleted.

They cannot be harmed by physical force. They cannot be poisoned.

They respond only to procedural compliance.*

Procedural compliance.

Dale looked at Yura.

She was staring at the Auditor, her hands raised, trying to cast another spell. The Auditor didn't attack.

It simply moved closer, one step at a time. With each step, the air around Yura grew thinner. The light dimmed.

The sound of her heartbeat, which Dale could hear from ten feet away, slowed.

She was being deleted.

"Dale," Yura said. Her voice was quiet, distant. "I can't breathe."

Dale looked at his clipboard.

Blank. He looked at the broken pen. Useless.

He looked at the Auditor.

It was five feet away. Three feet.

He had no weapon. He had no spell. He had no protocol that worked on a Class-9 Administrative Hazard.

Except the one he was about to break.

Dale dropped the clipboard. He reached into his pocket and pulled out a small, silver object. A paperclip. He unfolded it, bending the metal until it was straight, then sharp, then curved into a hook.

He wasn't a mage.

He wasn't a warrior. He was a technician. And technicians knew that if a tool was broken, you improvised.

You didn't fix the tool. You fixed the workflow.

The Authority rewarded thoroughness. It rewarded documentation. It rewarded the isolation of hazards.

The Auditor was here because the edit hadn't been filed. The error existed because it had no record.

If Dale couldn't stop the edit, he could document the error. He could make the error *official*.

He stepped forward. The Auditor stopped. The symbols spinning around its core slowed, watching him.

Dale held out the bent paperclip.

He didn't attack.

He didn't cast. He reached out and hooked the paperclip onto the edge of the Auditor's translucent form.

The metal sizzled, turning black, but it held.

"Violation," Dale said. His voice was steady. "Unlicensed modification of reality structure. Severity: Critical."

The Auditor didn't move.

"Per Protocol 7-Alpha, unauthorized edits must be logged before deletion," Dale continued. He was bluffing. He was praying. He was using the one loophole he had found three years ago, in a footnote of a footnote in the Class-4 manual.

*Administrative entities prioritize file integrity over immediate termination if a formal citation is issued within the engagement window.*

The Auditor paused. The spinning symbols froze.

Dale pulled out a ballpoint pen from his other pocket. It was cheap, plastic, and nearly empty. But it worked. He grabbed the clipboard from the floor, where it had landed face-up.

He wrote.

*SUBJECT: YURA VANCE.* *ACTION: UNAUTHORIZED CODE EDIT.* *STATUS: PENDING REVIEW.* *CITATION: 44-B (Improper Syntax).*

The ink flowed. The words appeared on the paper, glowing faintly gold.

The Auditor's core flared. The iridescent sludge that made up its body retracted, pulling back into the elevator shaft. The pressure in the room vanished. Yura gasped, air rushing back into her lungs.

Dale didn't celebrate. He didn't smile. He kept writing.

*NOTATION: CITATION ACCEPTED. DELETION SUSPENDED PENDING LICENSING HEARING.* *NOTE: SUBJECT REMAINS UNDER SURVEILLANCE. FURTHER VIOLATIONS RESULT IN IMMEDIATE TERMINATION.*

He finished the report. He signed it. He dated it.

The Auditor dissolved into a mist, the mist flowing back into the elevator doors. The doors remained closed. The hum stopped.

Silence returned to the hallway.

Yura slumped against the wall, sliding down to the floor. She looked at Dale. Her eyes were wide, white-rimmed.

"You... you cited it?" she whispered.

"I documented it," Dale said. He looked at his hands.

They were trembling again.

He closed them into fists to stop it. "The Authority doesn't care if you're right. It cares if you're recorded.

You didn't break the rules, Yura.

You filed an amendment without a witness. That's a procedural failure."

"I almost died," Yura said.

"You almost got erased," Dale corrected. "Same thing, different paperwork."

He picked up his clipboard. He picked up the broken pen. He looked at the blank space at the bottom of the report.

**THOROUGHNESS RATING: 10/10.** **TREATMENT POINTS AWARDED: 500.** **CERTIFICATION LEVEL: IX (MAXIMUM REACHED FOR CURRENT CLASS).**

Dale stared at the number.

Level IX. The highest level. He had done it.

He had survived the un-survivable.

But the rating wasn't the problem. The rating was the key.

He had just completed a job that required Level IX clearance. He had just engaged a Class-9 threat. And he had survived because he had followed the rules so perfectly that the rules themselves had bent to accommodate him.

The System notification changed.

**NEW DIRECTIVE:** **SUBJECT: THE RIFT SOURCE.** **STATUS: UNLICENSED SUPERSTRUCTURE DETECTED.** **REQUIREMENT: LEVEL IX CLEARANCE + ONE CITATION OF CODE 44-B.**

Dale felt a chill that had nothing to do with the temperature. He looked at Yura.

"What did you edit?" he asked.

Yura looked at him. For the first time since the Rift opened, she looked unsure. She looked young.

"I didn't edit the spell," she said. "I edited the definition of 'monster.'"

Dale froze.

"What do you mean?"

"If a monster is defined as an entity that consumes human life," Yura said slowly, "then technically, the Rift itself isn't a monster. It's a natural disaster. And natural disasters don't have licenses."

Dale looked at the elevator doors.

He looked at the blank space on his clipboard.

He realized then that the Auditor hadn't come to punish Yura. The Auditor had come to test the definition.

And Yura had changed the definition.

And now, because Dale had cited the error, he had validated the new definition.

He had just classified the Rift itself as non-hostile.

The floor beneath them groaned. Not a rumble, but a shift. The building wasn't shaking. It was being reclassified.

Dale looked at his Field Guide. The entry for *Rattus profundus* was gone. The entry for *Broodmother* was gone. The entire list of monsters he had spent ten years exterminating was vanishing from the book, one by one, replaced by a single, new entry.

*SUBJECT: REALITY.* *STATUS: UNLICENSED.* *CLASS: EXTERMINATION TARGET.*

Dale dropped the clipboard.

The elevator doors opened.

Inside was not a room of files. Inside was a mirror. And in the mirror, Dale didn't see himself. He saw a technician in a hazmat suit, holding a clipboard, standing in a wasteland of ash and bone.

The figure in the mirror smiled.

"Welcome to the real job, Dale," the reflection said. "You're not cleaning up the infestation. You're the infestation."

The reflection reached out.

And the doors closed.
