Chapter 245: Sealed Block

# Chapter 245

The clipboard slipped from Dale's sweat-slicked fingers and clattered against the marble floor. It was a cheap, laminated thing, purchased from a dollar store in 2019, but it had survived three Rifts, a Class-IV swarm, and a literal dimensional implosion. He couldn't afford to lose it. Not with a debt of three hundred and eighty points hanging over his head like a guillotine.

Dale didn't pick it up immediately. His eyes were locked on the interface hovering in his peripheral vision.

The red numbers pulsed with a rhythmic, taunting brightness. **-380**.

The Authority's late fee structure was opaque, but the interest rate was predatory. If he didn't generate positive Treatment Points within the hour, the debt would likely compound into a lien against his soul, or at least his future earnings.

"Credit card," Sarah repeated, her voice rising in pitch. She was gripping her rifle with white-knuckled intensity, scanning the shadows of the bank vault. "Dale, we just stopped a bomb. You're asking for a credit card?"

"Visa, Mastercard, Discover," Dale said, clicking his pen for the fourth time in ten seconds.

The plastic cap snapped off and rolled across the floor. He ignored it.

"Does the Authority have a payment plan?

Do they accept installment terms for emergency hazard mitigation?

"

The interface blinked. A new window opened, stark white text on a black background.

**PAYMENT OPTIONS:** 1. Immediate Balance Transfer (Source: Personal Assets). 2. Labor Offset (Hours worked x Hourly Rate). 3. Default (Penalty: Field Guide Corruption + License Suspension).

There was no option four. There was no

"I saved the city" waiver.

Dale stared at the text, his jaw tightening, and he hated this.

He hated the bureaucracy more than he hated the monsters. At least the monsters had predictable biology.

The Authority had terms of service.

"Labor offset," Dale muttered. "We need a job. Right now. Something with a high point value."

Vance stepped forward, wiping mercury residue from his cheek. His armor was stained grey, looking like he'd been dipped in paint thinner.

"The notification said Sector 4.

Residential. That's three blocks east. We can't just drive there.

The streets are full of debris.

"

"Then we walk," Dale said, finally bending down to retrieve the clipboard. He checked the date stamp.

It was still valid.

"Sector 4 is high-density single-family zoning. High occupancy rates. If there's an organized migration, they're targeting the nests.

The houses."

Sarah lowered her rifle slightly.

"You think they're going after families?"

"They're going after biomass," Dale corrected. "And structural entry points. Old foundations have cracks. Cracks are highways."

He pulled his radio from his belt, which was a standard-issue municipal unit, upgraded with a frequency hack provided by the Licensing Authority's support ticket #4492-B.

He keyed the mic.

"Dispatch, this is Technician Kowalski, Class II.

Code 10-4, moving to Sector 4 for active infestation. Need updated structural surveys for the eastern quadrant. Prioritize pre-1950s construction.

"

Static crackled. Then, a voice, smooth and synthesized. *Structural surveys unavailable due to system overload. Estimate: forty-five minutes.*

Dale closed his eyes. Forty-five minutes.

The mites moved fast, and they were organized, which meant they were following a scent trail or a pheromone map. If they were migrating, they were consolidating. Consolidation meant a larger nest.

Larger nests meant higher difficulty ratings, but also higher point yields.

It was the only way to pay off the debt.

"Change of plan," Dale said, turning to his team. "We don't wait for the survey. We create one."

Vance frowned.

"How?"

"Dale, you're talking about walking into a potential swarm with no intel," Sarah said.

Her tone wasn't angry, just tired.

"We're running on fumes. I'm down two magazines.

Vance's shield generator is at twelve percent.

"

"We don't need intel," Dale said, pointing his pen at the vault door. "We need a violation. Something that forces the Authority to pay us, or at least reduce the penalty."

He walked out of the vault, his boots crunching on the white powder.

The bank lobby was a disaster zone.

Tellers' desks were overturned. The security cameras had melted, their lenses dripping black plastic onto the carpet.

But in the center of the room, near the teller windows, stood a single object.

A vending machine, which was a standard glass-fronted unit, stocked with soda and chips. It was intact. Mostly.

Dale approached it slowly. He didn't look at the products.

He looked at the base.

The machine was bolted to the floor, but the bolts had sheared. A thin trail of black slime led from the bottom of the machine toward the rear service hatch.

"Unlicensed vending operation," Dale said, his voice dropping into the flat, monotone cadence of an inspector who had seen too much. "Or, more likely, an unauthorized storage unit for biological waste."

He crouched. The slime was viscous, smelling of copper and rot. It was fresh.

"Sarah, cover the door. Vance, watch the ceiling. I'm going to open this."

"That's a vending machine," Vance said.

"It's an illegal disposal site," Dale corrected.

He pulled a pair of nitrile gloves from his pocket and snapped them on. The sound echoed in the silent lobby.

He reached for the service hatch, which was locked with a standard key. He didn't have a key. He used the tip of his pen to pry the plastic latch.

It snapped.

The door swung open.

Inside was not soda.

A nest of grey, pulsating sacs clung to the inner walls of the machine. They were the size of cantaloupes, throbbing with a slow, rhythmic contraction. They blocked the electrical coils. The machine was trying to dispense a can of cola, but the mechanical arm was jammed against the organic mass.

Dale didn't flinch, and he pulled out his Field Guide.

He didn't need to open it physically; the data streamed to his ocular implant. He scanned the sacs.

The interface recognized them instantly.

**SPECIES: DIMENSIONAL VERMIN (SUBSPECIES: VENDING PARASITE).** **CLASS: II.** **STATUS: ACTIVE NEST.** **WEAKNESS: THERMAL SHOCK.**

"Thermal shock," Dale murmured. "Heaters. Fire."

"We don't have fire," Sarah said, peering over his shoulder. "We have acid rounds and kinetic bolts."

"Kinetic bolts won't work on sacs. Acid corrodes the machine, triggering the security alarm.

Alarm summons police.

Police summon Authority. Authority fines us for disturbing evidence.

" Dale's mind raced. The logic was circular, but there was a gap.

A loophole, and he looked at the interface again. **THERMAL SHOCK.** The system didn't specify fire. It specified thermal energy.

Dale looked at the electrical coils behind the sacs. They were still live. The machine was drawing power from the grid, trying to cool the compressor, but the heat generated by the friction of the jammed arm was building up. The temperature inside the machine was rising.

If he could accelerate that heating...

"Dale?" Vance's voice was urgent. "Movement outside. Shadows on the sidewalk."

Dale ignored him. He reached into his utility belt and pulled out a can of compressed air.

Not for cooling.

For propellant. He sprayed it directly into the coil assembly. The propellant was flammable.

The spark from the jammed motor ignited the spray.

A small fireball erupted inside the vending machine. The sacs shrieked—a high-pitched, wet sound that vibrated in Dale's teeth. They burst, spraying viscous fluid onto the glass front. The glass shattered outward.

**TREATMENT POINTS EARNED: 150.** **BONUS: RAPID IDENTIFICATION (+20).** **TOTAL: 170.**

Dale didn't smile. He checked the balance.

**CURRENT BALANCE: -210.**

Still negative. But better. He had bought time.

"Move," Dale said, standing up. He wiped a speck of slime from his jacket.

"The fire alarm will trigger the sprinkler system.

The water will short the rest of the grid.

The Authority will log this as a containment failure unless we provide a completed report within five minutes.

"

"We're leaving the scene?" Sarah asked, stepping back.

"We're documenting the scene," Dale said, pulling out a notepad. He wrote, his handwriting cramped and illegible to anyone but himself. "Code 7-Alpha: Unauthorized biological storage in commercial fixture.

Remediation via thermal induction. Verification pending.

"

He tore the page off the pad and held it up. The interface scanned it.

**REPORT FILED. PROCESSING...**

A green checkmark appeared.

**PENALTY WAIVED FOR TIMELY DOCUMENTATION. DEBT REMAINING: -210.**

"It's not enough," Dale said. "But it keeps my license active. For now."

They moved through the bank's rear exit, into the alleyway. The air outside was cold and smelled of ozone. The sky above Sector 4 was a bruised purple, the Rift still tearing at the fabric of reality. But the streets were empty.

Too empty.

"Where is everyone?" Vance asked, checking his perimeter. "Residential zone. It's Tuesday. People should be inside."

Dale stopped, and he looked at the houses lining the street. They were neat, suburban, two-story colonial revivals. Porch lights were on.

Curtains were drawn.

But there was no sound. No televisions. No dogs barking.

"Vacated," Dale said. "Evacuation order went out an hour ago. We missed it."

"Then where are they?" Sarah asked.

Dale looked at the ground. The asphalt was cracked.

Thin, grey threads wove through the fissures, and they looked like roots, but they were moving. Slowly, they were weaving together, forming a lattice across the street.

"Mites," Dale said. "They're not migrating to the houses. They're turning the neighborhood into a nest."

He pointed his flashlight at the nearest house. The front door was open.

A child's bicycle lay on the lawn, and the grey threads had crawled into the house.

They were visible in the windows, pulsing behind the glass.

"They're incubating," Dale realized. The horror of it settled in his stomach, cold and heavy. The mites weren't just eating.

They were farming. They were using the human population as a substrate for the next generation of rift-spawn.

"We have to burn them out," Vance said, raising his rifle. "All of them."

"No," Dale said, shaking his head.

"If you shoot the threads, you trigger a defensive response. The house collapses. The nest spreads underground.

You'll lose the whole block.

"

"Then what do we do?" Sarah asked.

Dale looked at his Field Guide. He scrolled through the entries. *Dimensional Vermin*. *Acid Beetle*. *Rift Rat*.

None of them fit, which was a new strain. A hybrid.

He needed a new identification.

He walked toward the first house. The interface flagged it.

**WARNING: PROXIMITY TO CLASS III INFESTATION. PROCEED WITH CAUTION.**

Dale stepped onto the porch. The grey threads crunched under his boots.

He reached into his pocket and pulled out a small vial of pheromone suppressant, which was meant for ant control.

It had never been tested on dimensional fauna.

It was a risk. If it failed, he would be exposed to the swarm. If it worked, he could map the nest structure without entering the house.

He uncapped the vial. The smell was sharp, chemical, and acrid. He sprinkled it on the threshold.

For a moment, nothing happened.

Then, the threads on the porch recoiled. They pulled back, writhing in confusion. The pulsating rhythm inside the house stuttered.

**IDENTIFICATION CONFIRMED: PHEROMONE DISRUPTOR EFFECTIVE.** **NEW FIELD GUIDE ENTRY: DIMENSIONAL VERMIN (SUBSPECIES: COLONIAL ARCHITECT).** **BONUS: TARGETED DAMAGE BONUS +15% FOR NEXT 10 MINUTES.**

Dale exhaled. His hands were trembling. He hated improvising.

He hated guessing. But the math worked.

He turned to Vance and Sarah.

"We don't kill the nest. We starve it. The pheromone disrupts their coordination.

They can't feed without it.

If we disrupt the pheromone trails in the next three houses, the nest collapses from within.

"

"That's science," Sarah said. "That's not extermination."

"It's pest control," Dale said. "You don't kill the ant. You kill the colony's ability to communicate."

He looked at the remaining houses. Twelve more. Maybe fifteen. He had twelve minutes of pheromone left.

"Split up," Dale said.

"Vance, take the odd numbers. Sarah, take the even. I'll coordinate.

You don't enter the houses. You just spray the thresholds.

If anything comes out, kill it.

But do not engage the internal nest.

"

"And if the Authority asks why we didn't clear the houses?" Vance asked.

"Because we were conducting a targeted chemical intervention," Dale said. "And we'll bill them for the hazard pay."

He started walking. His steps were measured.

One, two, three. He counted the cracks in the sidewalk. He noted the entry points.

He documented the structural damage in his mind, knowing the interface would capture it.

The first house responded. The second. The third.

The threads retreated. The purple light in the sky dimmed slightly.

But then, a scream, which came from the house next to Sarah.

Dale spun around.

Sarah was backing away from the porch. A figure emerged from the darkness of the doorway. It was tall, impossibly thin, its skin grey and segmented.

It looked human, but wrong.

Too many joints. Its face was a smooth, featureless oval.

A Host.

The mites hadn't just been farming. They had been replacing.

The Host stepped onto the porch. It looked at Sarah. It tilted its head.

Sarah fired. The bullet hit the chest. The Host didn't flinch. It lunged.

Dale moved. He didn't think. He reacted. He threw his clipboard.

The laminated board struck the Host's face. It stumbled, confused by the sudden, mundane impact.

Dale drew his sidearm. He fired three times into the legs. The legs shattered. The Host fell, thrashing.

"Get back!" Dale shouted.

The Host opened its mouth. A swarm of tiny, silver mites poured out, aiming for Sarah.

Dale pulled a flare from his belt. He lit it. The red light flared, bright and hot. He threw it at the Host.

The mites scattered, fleeing the heat. The Host screamed, a sound like tearing metal, and dissolved into a puddle of grey slurry.

Silence returned.

Dale breathed hard. He looked at the interface.

**TREATMENT POINTS EARNED: 0.** **REASON: UNLICENSED COMBAT ENGAGEMENT.** **PENALTY: FIELD GUIDE CORRUPTION (1 HOUR).**

He stared at the text. The red letters burned into his vision.

**CORRUPTION DETECTED. FIELD GUIDE DATA UNRELIABLE. PROCEED WITH EXTREME CAUTION.**

Sarah stared at him, her face pale.

"You lost the bonus."

Dale looked at the dissolving puddle, and he looked at the other houses. The threads were still there. The nest was still active.

And now, he was blind. His Field Guide was scrambled.

He couldn't trust the weaknesses.

He couldn't trust the identifications, and he had twelve minutes of pheromone. He had no intel. He had a team that was tired, out of ammo, and staring at him with doubt.

And behind him, in the shadows of the alley, a dozen more Hosts were stepping out of the darkness. They moved in unison. They didn't scream. They didn't hesitate.

They were organized.

Dale clicked his pen. The sound was loud in the silence.

"Run," he said.

Sarah and Vance looked at him.

"Now," Dale said. "Before they coordinate."

They ran. But as they ran, Dale looked back.

The Hosts weren't chasing them. They were surrounding the block. They were sealing it off.

The Authority hadn't just sent them a job, and they had sent them a trap.

The Authority had sealed the block and Dale had no way out.
