Chapter 171: Show Me Your Credentials

# Chapter 171

The chitinous skittering sounded like rain on a tin roof, but faster.

Dale didn't look down. His pen hovered over the final blank line of the single remaining sheet. The ink cartridge clicked, a dry, mechanical sound against the wet, organic silence of the chamber.

"Identify," the Other Dale whispered. His voice sounded like dry leaves crunching underfoot. "Name it. Or lose it."

Dale's eyes darted to the swarm pouring from the cracks in the flesh-floored chamber. They were small, no larger than house flies, but their legs were too numerous, jointed in ways that violated basic arthropod morphology. They moved with a terrifying, synchronized rhythm.

*Not flies,* Dale's mind processed. *Not beetles.*

He looked at his Field Guide. The entry for *Rattus profundus* was grayed out, contaminated by the cross-species protocol error of using rodent-specific repellent against a dimensional entity. The system had punished him for the mismatch. A 40% debuff to all current stats.

"Name it," the Other Dale repeated. The figure on the bone pedestal did not move. It only watched. Its clipboard was a monolith of paper, pages fanning out into the darkness, each one filled with the same looping sentence: *I ran out.*

Dale clicked the pen again. Nothing. The ink was gone.

He looked at the swarm. They were closing in. Thirty feet. Twenty.

"Hey," Dale said, his voice steady. "You're blocking the exit."

The Other Dale tilted his head. "There is no exit. There is only the report."

"And you're violating code 74-B," Dale said. "Unlicensed obstruction of a licensed technician's workspace. I can write you up."

The Other Dale's empty white eyes blinked. "You cannot write up a paradox, Dale. You cannot cite a memory."

"I can cite a violation." Dale reached into his utility belt. His hand brushed past the useless spray can, past the duct tape, and landed on a small, sealed envelope.

He pulled it out.

The Other Dale stiffened. The flesh-walls of the chamber pulsed harder, the black veins throbbing in time with the creature's agitation. The eyes embedded in the walls narrowed, focusing entirely on the envelope.

"What is that?" the Other Dale asked. The pity in its voice was gone, replaced by a sharp, jagged edge of fear.

"Certification Level IV Provisional License," Dale said. "Temporary. Ninety days. Unrenewable."

"You don't have it," the Other Dale snapped. "You failed the practical. You ran out of paper."

"I didn't run out," Dale said. "I ran out of *space*."

He tore the seal on the envelope. A blue light spilled out, not illuminating the room, but illuminating the *air*. The particles of dust and spores floating in the chamber froze.

**[SYSTEM ALERT: PROVISIONAL AUTHORITY GRANTED.]** **[STATUS: CLASS IV (PROVISIONAL).]** **[WARNING: CROSS-CONTAMINATION RISK CRITICAL.]**

Dale ignored the alert. He uncapped a pen.

Not his own.

This was a standard-issue Authority pen, the kind they gave out at testing centers. The ink was permanent. Legal tender.

"Stop them," the Other Dale hissed.

The swarm shrieked. The sound was high-pitched, vibrating in Dale's teeth. They lunged.

Dale didn't dodge. He stepped forward.

"Identification," Dale said.

He pointed the pen at the leading cluster of chitinous horrors. "Order: *Coleoptera*.

Family: *Staphylinidae*.

Genus: *Paederus*."

The swarm paused. Confused.

"Species: *Paederus dimensional-shifted*," Dale continued. "Common name: Blister Beetle variant, Rift-strain."

He looked at the Other Dale. "You didn't identify them.

You just let them happen.

That's why you're stuck here.

You stopped documenting before you finished treating."

The Other Dale stepped off the pedestal. The bone cracked under its weight. "I tried.

I wrote everything. Every page.

It wasn't enough."

"It was never about the volume," Dale said. "It was about the taxonomy."

Dale raised the pen. He didn't write on the paper. He wrote in the air.

The blue light from the provisional license reacted to the motion. The pen carved a glowing line in the air, a geometric shape that hung suspended in the bioluminescent gloom. A triangle.

Then a square.

Then a circle.

**[ACTION: TARGETED IDENTIFICATION.]** **[BONUS: +30% DAMAGE AGAINST SPECIES *Paederus dimensional-shifted*.]** **[NOTE: FIELD GUIDE UPDATE IN PROGRESS.]**

The swarm scattered.

"Why?" one of the creatures hissed. It spoke in a voice like tearing silk. "Why do you name us?"

Dale kept drawing. The geometric shapes expanded, forming a containment ward in mid-air. A literal barrier of bureaucracy.

"Because unnamed things are chaotic," Dale said. "Named things are manageable. And manageable things can be exterminated."

The Other Dale stopped advancing. It looked at the glowing shapes, then at its own endless stack of papers. It reached out, touching one of the floating lines of light.

"It burns," the Other Dale said.

"It's protocol," Dale corrected.

"You're using a loophole," the Other Dale said.

The figure's voice dropped to a whisper. "Provisional licenses allow for immediate field deployment without full environmental assessment. You're skipping the isolation phase.

You're going straight to elimination."

"I have to," Dale said. "If I isolate, I lose the workspace. If I lose the workspace, I lose the protocol. If I lose the protocol, I die."

"You always die," the Other Dale said.

Dale turned to face the figure fully. "No. I get cited. There's a difference."

The chamber shook again.

The cracks in the floor widened.

More creatures poured out. Hundreds.

Thousands. They were larger now, the size of rats, but with the distinct elongated abdomens of blister beetles. Their mandibles clicked.

"Kill them," the Other Dale commanded. "Kill them all. Don't stop for the forms."

Dale looked at his clipboard. One sheet. Blank.

He looked at the Other Dale.

"You want me to kill them without verifying the population count?" Dale asked.

"I want you to survive!" the Other Dale screamed. The figure's face contorted, the white voids in its eyes swirling.

"Write the death certificate first.

Fill it out. Then kill them. Or kill them, then fill it out.

Just do it!"

"Order of operations matters," Dale said. "If you kill before you identify, the Authority classifies it as unauthorized disposal.

Fine: 500 points.

Strike: Two. Strike: Three, and your license is revoked."

"Who cares about the license?" the Other Dale shouted.

"I do," Dale said. "And so do you.

Look at you.

You're a ghost because you got stripped of your credentials.

You're an echo because you lost your standing."

The Other Dale froze.

"You're not a memory of me," Dale said, his voice cold. "You're a memory of my *failure*."

The figure looked down at its torn suit. At the gray hair. At the piles of paper.

"I didn't fail," the Other Dale whispered.

"You ran out of paper," Dale said. "But you didn't run out of *process*."

Dale turned back to the swarm. They were ten feet away. The smell of them was sulfurous, like rotten onions mixed with copper.

He capped the pen.

"System," Dale said aloud. "Initiate Protocol: *Chemical Containment, Variant D*."

**[ERROR: CLASS IV PROTOCOLS DO NOT INCLUDE VARIANTS.]**

Dale smiled. A small, grim smile.

"Cross-contamination," he said.

He pulled a small vial from his belt. It was a sample of acid beetle bile, harvested three chapters ago. It was classified as *Class I Biological Hazard*. The current swarm was *Class III Chitinous*.

Using Class I acid against Class III chitin was a violation of species-specific protocol.

**[WARNING: CROSS-CONTAMINATION PENALTY APPLIED.]** **[DEBUFF: 40% REDUCTION IN TREATMENT EFFICIENCY.]** **[FIELD GUIDE ENTRY: CONTAMINATED FOR 24 HOURS.]**

The air in the chamber turned green.

The acid vial shattered against the floor. Dale kicked the shards into the swarm.

The reaction was violent. The acid didn't just burn; it destabilized the chitin. The creatures shrieked, their shells cracking, their bodies liquefying in real-time.

But the penalty was immediate. Dale felt a wave of nausea hit him. His vision blurred. The blue lines of his containment ward flickered and dimmed.

"See?" the Other Dale said, its voice gaining strength. "You're contaminating yourself. You're becoming me."

"No," Dale said. He wiped a smear of black fluid from his cheek. "I'm contaminating the *evidence*."

He pointed to the dissolving swarm.

"The Authority doesn't just reward killing. It rewards *cleanliness*."

Dale pulled out his flashlight. He clicked it on. The beam cut through the green fog.

He shined the light on the pile of dissolving beetles. He shined it on the floor. He shined it on the Other Dale.

"I'm documenting the cross-contamination event," Dale said. "I'm creating a new entry in the Field Guide."

The Other Dale recoiled. "You can't. The entry is contaminated."

"Contaminated entries can be *corrected*," Dale said. "With a new identification."

He raised his pen again. The Provisional License hummed.

**[ACTION: NEW SPECIES IDENTIFICATION.]** **[TARGET: HYBRID ENTITY (ECHO OF FAILURE + CHITIN SWARM).]**

Dale wrote in the air.

*Name: Paradox Vermin.*

*Type: Conceptual Infestation.*

*Weakness: Bureaucratic Inertia.*

The blue lines flared. The green fog solidified.

The swarm didn't die. It *stopped*.

They froze in mid-lunge, caught in the stasis of the new identification. The Other Dale screamed, its form unraveling, pages flying from its clipboard like confetti.

"You're erasing me!" the figure wailed.

"I'm filing a complaint," Dale said.

He turned his back on the figure. He walked toward the ladder.

"Wait!" the Other Dale cried. "If you leave, the breach expands. The Rift widens. You need to stay and finish the report!"

Dale climbed the first rung. His muscles burned from the debuff. His hands shook, but he gripped the rusted iron tight.

"The report is filed," Dale called back. "The correction is pending review."

He reached the top. He pushed open the hatch.

Above him, the sky was still purple. The Rift was still open. But the ground around the shaft was quiet.

The screaming tenants were gone. The screaming neighbors were gone.

In their place stood a line of men in dark suits. They held clipboards. They held pens.

One of them stepped forward. He was tall, gaunt, with skin the color of parchment.

"Technician Kowalski," the man said.

Dale stepped out into the sunlight. The heat was oppressive. The air smelled of ozone and wet earth.

"Agent," Dale said. He didn't salute. He just held up his clipboard.

"The Authority acknowledges your provisional license," the Agent said. "But it also notes a critical anomaly."

"Which one?" Dale asked.

"The Other Dale," the Agent said. He looked past Dale, into the shaft. "You identified a paradox. You named it."

"Standard procedure," Dale said.

"No," the Agent said. "Paradox Class entities are not supposed to have names. They are supposed to be *voids*."

The Agent stepped closer. He looked at Dale's clipboard. He looked at the single sheet of paper.

"You didn't just name a monster, Kowalski," the Agent said. "You named *yourself*."

Dale looked at the paper.

He hadn't written *Paradox Vermin*.

He had written *Dale Kowalski (Class IV Provisional)*.

**[SYSTEM ALERT: SELF-IDENTIFICATION DETECTED.]** **[STATUS: PARADOX CLASS.]** **[ACTION: ELIMINATE.]**

The Agent raised his pen.

"It's in the rules, Dale," the Agent said. "You cannot be both the exterminator and the infestation. One of you has to be removed."

Dale looked at the Agent. He looked at the pen.

He looked at the line of men behind the Agent.

"You're early," Dale said.

The Agent smiled. It was a smile of infinite pity.

"I'm not early," the Agent said. "I'm the audit."

Dale raised his pen.

"Show me your credentials," Dale said.
