Chapter 373: Event Escalation

# Chapter 373

The stamp hit the concrete with a sound like a gunshot.

Miles Vane adjusted his tie, though the silk was already fraying at the knot. He didn't look at the creature. He looked at the clipboard in his hand, checking the time. 11:42 PM. Too late for overtime paperwork, but early enough to claim hazard pay if the narrative held.

Vane had seen Dale Kowalski before. Not in person, but in the files.

The man was a ghost in the municipal records, a Level II technician who filed reports so thorough they were practically literature.

But Vane didn't care about literature. He cared about the red stamp on his sleeve.

*Event: Escalation.*

That meant the Authority was watching. And when the Authority watched, Vane had to be seen. He had to be the one who made the right call, the one who authorized the force. Not the guy standing in the dark holding a breaker switch.

The creature rose, water cascading off its chitinous shell. It filled the sub-basement of the shopping mall, its legs thick as support pillars. The air smelled of brine and old blood.

Vane raised his voice, projecting for the unseen observers in the Rift's upper strata.

"Kowalski! Step back!"

Dale didn't move, and he stood ankle-deep in the flooded water, his boots glowing faintly with residual bio-luminescence. His headlamp cut a stark cone of light through the steam. He wasn't looking at the monster. He was looking at Vane.

"Your license is suspended," Vane said, tapping the black stamp.

"You deployed a Class-1 Rodent Protocol on a Class-4 Crustacean. That's a cross-contamination event.

You're liable for the environmental damage.

Step back, and I will handle the containment.

"

The creature chittered. A sound like wet gravel grinding together.

Dale's hand twitched. Not toward a weapon. Toward his belt. Where his Field Guide hung.

Vane saw the movement. He stepped forward, planting his feet.

"I said, step back."

The creature lunged.

It wasn't fast. Not like the rats.

It was inevitable.

A massive claw swept through the air, knocking over a row of vending machines.

Glass shattered. Sparks showered the water.

Dale didn't run. He sidestepped. A small, precise shuffle.

Vane frowned. That wasn't panic. That was practice.

"Kowalski, this is not a negotiation," Vane shouted, raising his stamp. "Submit to administrative review."

Dale ignored him. He reached into his cargo pants and pulled out a small, sealed plastic bag. Inside was a yellow liquid.

Vane recognized it. Hydrogen peroxide and iodine. A standard cleaning agent.

But Dale wasn't spraying it. He was holding it up, letting the light reflect off it.

"The water," Dale said.

His voice was flat. Bored.

"It's conductive.

You used a high-voltage arc.

That ionized the water. The creature isn't just wet.

It's charged."

Vane paused. He looked at the glowing water. It wasn't just neon anymore. It was buzzing.

"What are you doing?"

"Neutralizing the charge," Dale said. "Before it fries you."

The creature slammed a claw down. Vane stumbled back, his expensive shoes slipping on the slick concrete.

Dale moved. He didn't charge the monster. He walked to the nearest electrical outlet.

It was half-submerged. He pulled a rubber mat from his pack—standard OSHA equipment for wet locations—and laid it over the water leading to the outlet.

Then he plugged in a device Vane hadn't seen before. A small, boxy contraption with a wire leading into the water.

"What is that?" Vane demanded.

"Grounding rod," Dale said. "Improvised. But it works."

The creature shrieked.

A high-pitched wail that shattered the remaining glass in the storefronts.

It turned its eyeless head toward Dale. It recognized the threat.

Not the weapon. The procedure.

"You're contaminating the site further," Vane yelled. "Stop!"

Dale flipped a switch on the box.

A hum filled the room. The glowing water dimmed. The creature twitched, its spasms slowing. The blue arc in the air vanished.

Vane stared. He checked his stamp. The red warning light blinked once, then turned white.

*Contamination Risk: Decreasing.*

Vane's jaw tightened. He had the license. He had the authority. But the man without the license was solving the problem.

"Kowalski," Vane said, his voice dropping. "You're violating Protocol 7-B. Unauthorized electrical intervention in a live Rift zone. That's a felony."

Dale looked up. His face was shadowed, but his posture was rigid.

"Protocol 7-B applies to Class-1 infestations where the threat is contained," Dale said. "This is a Class-4.

The containment has failed.

Therefore, 7-B is null. I am operating under Emergency Containment Clause 9.

"

Vane felt a cold sweat prickle his neck, and he hadn't read Clause 9. He never did.

He relied on the Authority's AI to flag his actions.

But the AI was lagging. The creature was too big. The data stream was clogged with debris.

"You think you can out-lawyer the Authority?" Vane sneered. He raised his stamp.

"I am invoking Emergency Override.

Technician Kowalski, your license is hereby revoked effective immediately.

Submit your Field Guide for audit.

"

The creature raised both claws. It was preparing a dual strike.

Dale didn't submit. He didn't argue.

He pulled a second device from his pack. This one was larger. A pressurized canister, modified with a nozzle made from a garden hose fitting.

"I'm not arguing," Dale said. "I'm working."

He sprayed.

Not at the monster.

At the floor.

Specifically, at the junction where the sub-basement drainage met the main sewer line. A thick, viscous foam expanded rapidly, sealing the gap.

The creature screamed. It tried to retreat into the drain, but the foam hardened instantly, sealing the exit.

Trapped.

Vane stared at the foam.

"What is that?"

"Polyurethane sealant," Dale said. "Mixed with a chitin-dissolving enzyme from the Field Guide."

Vane looked at his stamp. The Authority was processing the action. The AI was confused.

The creature was trapped, but not dead. The protocol wasn't complete.

*Thoroughness Rating: Pending.*

The creature thrashed against the seal. The foam held. It bubbled. The enzyme was eating through the barnacles on the claw.

Dale pulled out his clipboard. He didn't write. He tapped the screen.

"Field Guide updated," Dale muttered. "Weakness identified: Soft tissue beneath chitin plates susceptible to alkaline breakdown. Vulnerability confirmed."

A chime sounded in the air.

*Identification Complete.* *Targeted Damage Bonus: +15%.*

Vane watched the number float in the air, and he felt a pang of jealousy. He had access to the whole database. But he hadn't looked.

He hadn't *done* the work.

"Kill it," Vane ordered. "Use your weapon. Finish it."

"I don't have a weapon," Dale said. "I have a protocol."

He reached into his other pocket. He pulled out a small, red flare.

A standard emergency marker, and he didn't light it. He dropped it into the water, right where the creature's legs met the body.

The flare sizzled. Not fire. Chemical.

The water around the creature's legs boiled. Not from heat. From a reaction.

"Calcium carbide," Vane realized. "And water."

"Acetylene gas," Dale corrected. "Flammable. Explosive."

The creature froze. It understood.

It wasn't a hero, which was an animal.

And animals knew fire.

"Get back," Dale said.

Vane hesitated. He wanted to take the credit. He wanted to be the one who struck the final blow.

But the gas was spreading. It was filling the sub-basement.

"Kowalski, this is a violation of fire codes—"

"Move, or get burned," Dale said. He didn't look at Vane. He looked at the creature.

He kicked a piece of sparking wire toward the gas cloud, and the explosion was contained. Mostly.

A shockwave of heat and pressure slammed into the creature's legs. The chitin cracked. The legs buckled.

The creature collapsed.

It didn't die. But it couldn't move.

Silence returned to the mall. Dust settled on the water.

Vane stood there, his suit singed at the cuffs. He looked at the creature. Then at Dale.

Dale was already writing, and he walked past Vane, stepping over the debris. He didn't look at the monster. He looked at the drain he had sealed.

"Report," Vane said, his voice shaking. "Explain what happened. Explain the license suspension."

Dale didn't stop walking.

"I'll file it. You're fired, Vane. Your override was premature.

You interrupted a completed protocol.

"

Vane sputtered.

"I have the stamp!"

Dale turned. His face was grimy, but calm.

"The Authority doesn't care about the stamp.

It cares about the result. You created an infestation. I cleaned it up.

Check your Thoroughness Rating.

"

Vane looked down.

His stamp was black. Blank.

*Rating: 0/10.*

*Reason: Incompetence.*

Dale walked away, into the darkness of the mall's upper levels.

Vane stood alone with the dying creature. He tried to lift his stamp. It felt heavy. Like lead.

He looked at the black ink. It had smeared across his thumb.

And then, he saw it.

On the creature's shell, glowing faintly, was a symbol. Not the Authority's mark.

A different one.

A circle, broken by three jagged lines.

Vane touched it. The ink on his finger turned red.

A voice echoed in his head. Not the Authority's.

*"License Holder Vane. You have failed to protect the asset. Termination of contract initiated."*

Vane screamed.

The creature's eye opened. It wasn't eyeless. It had been hiding.

It looked at Vane.

*"The door is still open,"* it said.

Vane tried to run. But the foam on his shoes had hardened. He slipped.

He fell.

The creature's claw closed around him.

***

Dale was three floors up when the scream faded, and he didn't look back. He pulled out his phone. The screen was cracked, but the Authority interface was still active.

*Notification: Infestation Contained. Level 1.* *Points Awarded: 50.* *Thoroughness: 8.5.*

He frowned. 8.5. He had lost points for the explosion. The structural damage to the mall was significant.

He walked to the exit door. He pushed it open.

Fresh air. Night air.

But the sky wasn't right, and the Rift was gone. The usual purple haze had vanished.

Instead, the stars were out.

All of them.

Too many.

Dale stopped. He raised his hand to his eyes, shielding them from the sudden brightness.

The stars weren't just stars, and they were moving.

A swarm.

Millions of them, descending from the upper atmosphere.

They didn't look like insects. They looked like lights.

But as they got closer, the lights resolved into shapes.

Small. Fast. With wings.

Class-1.

But there were too many.

Dale pulled out his clipboard. His hands were steady.

He looked at the swarm.

Then he looked at the mall entrance, and the creature below was dead. Vane was gone.

But the problem wasn't over.

The notification popped up.

*New Job Detected.* *Type: Aerial Infestation.* *Scale: Municipal.* *Priority: Critical.*

Dale sighed. He took out a pen.

He didn't have time to rest, and he wrote.

*Entry 1: Unknown Aerial Species.* *Hypothesis: Dimensional Spores.* *Action Required: Containment of upper atmosphere.*

He looked up at the sky.

The swarm hit the ground.

It wasn't a raid, which was a harvest.

Dale realized then that the Authority hadn't been protecting the city.

It had been farming it.

And he had just cleaned up the harvest bin.

He turned to run, but his boots stuck to the pavement.

Glue.

Not polyurethane.

Something blacker.

Something that smelled like Vane's cologne.

Dale looked down.

The black ink from Vane's stamp was on the ground.

And it was moving.

It wrapped around his ankles, and a voice came from the shadows of the mall entrance.

It wasn't the creature's voice.

It was the Authority's.

*"Technician Kowalski. You have exceeded your scope. Your license is now... permanent."*

Dale tried to lift his foot. He couldn't.

The black ink pulsed, which had a heartbeat.

Dale looked at his Field Guide.

The entry for *Rattus profundus* had changed.

*Status: Active Host.*

He looked at his own arm.

A thin, black line traced itself up his wrist, and he wasn't exterminating them anymore.

He was becoming part of the infestation.

The sky turned black.

The swarm descended.

Dale dropped his clipboard, and he didn't pick it up.

He couldn't.

His fingers were fusing together.

The last thing he saw was the red stamp, floating in the air, hovering just out of reach.

*Next Job: Integrate.*
