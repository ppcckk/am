Chapter 233: Report for Duty

# Chapter 233

The security camera in Sector 4 didn't record a hero. It recorded a man crouching in a puddle of black sludge, wiping his glasses with a microfiber cloth while ignoring the screaming alarms that had turned the entire water reclamation plant into a strobe-lit hellscape.

To the Authority's automated surveillance sub-routine, Dale Kowalski was currently a 94% match for 'Rogue Technician.'

To the drone hovering three feet above his head, he was a liability.

"Move," the drone buzzed. Its voice was stripped of its previous polite bureaucratic tone.

Now it sounded like a car alarm that had learned to speak.

"Compliance is mandatory. Resistance will be met with neutralization."

Dale didn't look up. He adjusted the seal on his respirator. The red light of his peripheral HUD was pulsing in time with his heartbeat, a rhythmic throb that usually signaled a headache. Today, it signaled a termination order.

Behind him, the blast door had sealed. Ahead, the corridor was flooded with knee-deep runoff from the cracked filtration vats. The smell was sulfur and rot. The visual spectrum was limited to the flicker of emergency lights and the bioluminescent glow of the things crawling out of the water.

The drone extended a manipulator arm. A taser tip crackled, blue and white.

Dale shifted his weight. He didn't reach for his wrench. He reached for his clipboard.

"Protocol 7-Alpha," Dale said. His voice was flat, muffled by the respirator. "You are interfering with an active containment zone. This is a violation of OSHA standard 1910.119."

The drone paused. The taser tip dimmed slightly.

"Your certification status is revoked," the drone stated. "OSHA standards do not apply to non-certified entities."

"I'm certified," Dale said. "I'm just suspended. There's a difference."

He tapped his clipboard. The screen, protected by a layer of industrial-grade polymer, displayed his current Field Guide. It was empty of monsters. The entries had been wiped during the quarantine initiation.

That was the problem. And that was the loophole.

Without Field Guide entries, he had no targeted damage bonus. Without a Field Guide, he couldn't document kills for Treatment Points. But the Authority's system had a legacy clause, buried deep in the code of Chapter 4, Section 12: *Identification takes precedence over Authentication.*

If he could identify a threat, the system had to acknowledge it. Even if that threat was the Authority itself.

Dale looked at the water rising around his boots.

Small, translucent larvae were swimming in the sludge. They weren't Rattus profundus.

They weren't Acid Beetles.

They were new.

He didn't know their species name. But he knew their behavior.

They moved in response to sound. Specifically, to the frequency of the drone's hover-thrusters. As the drone buzzed, the larvae swarmed toward it. As the drone moved left, they followed.

It wasn't a monster. It was a parasite on the infrastructure.

"Drone," Dale said. "You're attracting them."

"Irrelevant," the drone said. The taser charged again. "Prepare for stun."

Dale stepped forward. He didn't run. He walked with the deliberate, measured pace of a technician inspecting a joist.

"You're contaminating the workspace," Dale said. "Your acoustic signature is disrupting the larval drift. If you neutralize me, you lose the only entity capable of isolating the source."

The drone's optical sensors widened. The logic processors were churning. It was looking for a directive that prioritized survival over compliance.

"Source?" the drone asked.

"The nest," Dale said. He pointed at the ceiling.

Above them, in the ventilation ducts, a soft clicking sound echoed. It sounded like marbles rolling inside a tin can.

Dale didn't wait for the drone to process this. He kicked a floating piece of debris into the water. The splash drew the attention of the larvae, but more importantly, it drew the attention of something bigger.

A shadow detached itself from the ceiling.

It was a Spore-Weaver. A Class-4 arachnid, normally found in humid basements, but mutated by the Rift energy into something larger. Its legs were made of crystallized fungal filaments. Its body was a pulsating sac of spores.

It landed between Dale and the drone.

The drone's targeting system locked on. "Hostile detected. Class: Unknown. Threat Level: High."

The drone fired its taser. The bolt hit the Spore-Weaver.

Nothing happened.

The electricity arced harmlessly off the creature's chitinous shell, which was coated in a conductive, wet mucus. The Weaver shrieked, a sound like tearing metal, and lunged.

The drone dodged, spinning its rotors to gain altitude. But the Weaver was faster in the enclosed space.

It slammed into the drone, crushing the rotors.

Metal shrieked against chitin.

The drone dropped into the sludge, sparking and smoking.

Dale didn't move. He watched the drone die.

When the drone's optics went dark, the red light in Dale's peripheral vision flickered. Then, it changed.

The red border around his HUD dissolved.

In its place, a yellow warning box appeared.

**ALERT: LOCAL LICENSING AUTHORITY UNIT DESTROYED.** **STATUS: OFF-LINE.** **ACTION REQUIRED: MANUAL CERTIFICATION VALIDATION.**

Dale exhaled. His hands were shaking, just slightly. He clenched his fists to stop it.

The drone was dead. Which meant the local Authority node was dead.

And if the local node was dead, the system was looking for the highest-ranking certified technician in the vicinity to validate the threat.

There was no one else.

Dale raised his clipboard. He opened the Field Guide.

*New Entry Required.*

He looked at the Spore-Weaver. It was turning toward him, clicking its mandibles. The drone's wreckage bubbled in the sludge behind it.

Dale didn't attack. He observed.

He noted the leg count: eight. He noted the body structure: cephalothorax fused with abdominal sac. He noted the attack vector: spore expulsion via mandible vibration.

He noted the weakness: the joints between the leg segments were dry. Brittle.

He wrote it down.

*Species: Unidentified Fungal Arachnid. Sub-species: Likely Rift-Modified.* *Weakness: Desiccated joint nodes.* *Threat Class: 4.*

The moment he typed the last letter, the system pinged.

**IDENTIFICATION CONFIRMED.** **FIELD GUIDE UPDATED.** **TREATMENT POINTS AWARDED: 50.** **BONUS: +15% DAMAGE TO TARGET.**

The Weaver charged.

Dale didn't have a weapon that could exploit a 15% bonus. He didn't have a sword. He didn't have a spell.

He had a wrench.

He sidestepped the lunge. The Weaver's mandibles snapped shut on empty air. Dale swung the wrench.

He didn't aim for the head. He aimed for the third leg segment.

The wrench connected. With the bonus applied, the force was enough to shatter the brittle chitin. The leg snapped off.

The Weaver screeched, off-balance. It tried to compensate, dragging itself on three legs.

Dale circled.

He kept his distance. He was a pest control technician, not a warrior. He didn't engage.

He contained.

He pulled a canister from his belt. Not a chemical spray. A sealant. Industrial-grade epoxy, used for patching cracked foundations.

"Isolate," Dale muttered.

He threw the canister at the Weaver's exposed abdominal sac. It burst on impact, coating the soft tissue in thick, hardening resin.

The Weaver thrashed, trying to shake off the weight. The resin hardened rapidly in the cold air of the plant.

Dale stepped back. He watched the creature struggle.

The resin sealed the spore sacs.

The creature couldn't breathe. It couldn't reproduce. It was suffocating in its own armor.

It collapsed.

Dale waited for the death animation. The Authority's system usually required a corpse to verify.

But the corpse didn't dissolve into loot. It just lay there, trapped in amber-like glue.

A new notification appeared.

**THREAT NEUTRALIZED.** **METHOD: NON-LETHAL CONTAINMENT.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 8.5.** **NOTE: LETHAL TERMINATION REQUIRED FOR FULL CREDIT.**

Dale stared at the number. 8.5.

He needed a 9.0 to qualify for the next tier of clearance. He needed a 10.0 to override a Quarantine Order.

He looked at the dead drone. Then he looked at the Weaver.

He picked up the wrench.

"Verify," he said.

He swung.

The sound was wet and final. The Weaver's body crumpled. The system pinged.

**THREAT TERMINATED.** **TREATMENT POINTS: 120.** **TOTAL POINTS: 170.** **CERTIFICATION LEVEL: II (INTERIM).** **STATUS: VALIDATED.**

The red light in his vision vanished completely.

For a moment, Dale stood in the silence. The only sound was the dripping of water from the ceiling and the distant hum of the city's power grid.

He checked his clipboard. The Field Guide now had two entries. One for the drone, which he'd classified as *Mechanical Parasite (Authority Variant)*. One for the Weaver.

He looked at the sketch he had found on Gary's clipboard. The hand symbol.

If the Authority was compromised, then the drones were no longer protectors. They were vectors.

And if the drones were vectors, then every technician in the city was a target.

Dale walked toward the service exit. The door was still locked.

He approached the control panel. It was sparking, damaged by the drone's crash.

He pulled out his multitool. He didn't try to hack the code. He didn't try to brute-force the password.

He opened the panel cover. He identified the hydraulic relay.

He disconnected the power feed. He bypassed the lock mechanism manually.

The door hissed open.

Beyond it, the corridor was dark. But it wasn't empty.

Figures stood in the shadows. Not drones.

Human figures.

They wore the grey jumpsuits of other pest control technicians. They held clipboards. They held wrenches.

Their faces were blank.

One of them stepped forward. It was Gary.

Or at least, it looked like Gary. His clipboard was intact. His posture was perfect.

"Dale," the figure said. The voice was Gary's, but it lacked the tremor, the fear, the humanity.

Gary was dead. Dale had seen his body.

"This is a code violation," Dale said. His voice was steady. He gripped his wrench tighter.

"It's an infestation," the Gary-thing replied. It raised its clipboard. On the screen, a single word flashed in red.

**JOIN.**

Behind it, the others stepped forward. Dozens of them.

Technicians from every district.

All certified.

All thorough.

All empty.

Dale looked at the system menu in his mind. He had 170 points. He was Level II.

He needed to reach Level III to access the *Purge Protocol*. The protocol that could wipe out a hive mind.

He looked at the Gary-thing. He looked at the dark corridor stretching into the unknown.

He couldn't fight them all. He didn't have enough points. He didn't have enough time.

But he had a new entry in his Field Guide.

*Mechanical Parasite (Authority Variant).*

He had identified the drone.

The Gary-thing was moving. It was fast. Too fast.

Dale didn't raise his wrench.

He raised his clipboard.

He typed a command into the Field Guide interface.

*New Entry: Hosted Vessel.* *Weakness: Connection Node.*

He didn't know where the connection node was. He had to guess.

He lunged. Not at Gary. At the wall behind Gary.

The Gary-thing flinched. It hesitated.

That hesitation was the weakness.

Dale slammed the clipboard against the wall. The polymer case cracked. The screen shattered.

But the impact triggered the building's fire suppression system.

Sprinklers erupted.

Water rained down.

The Gary-things shrieked. It wasn't a human scream. It was the sound of electronics shorting out.

The water washed over them. Their movements became jerky. Stuttering.

Dale ran.

He didn't look back. He sprinted through the service tunnel, his boots splashing in the rising water. The red alert in his mind was gone, replaced by a blue countdown.

**TIMER: 10:00 MINUTES TO GLOBAL LOCKDOWN.**

He emerged onto the surface.

The night sky was wrong.

The stars were gone.

In their place, a massive, geometric pattern glowed in the upper atmosphere. It spanned the entire horizon.

It looked like a barcode.

And in the center of the city, where the Rift had first opened, a beam of light shot upward, connecting to the pattern.

The Authority wasn't just compromised.

It was uploading.

Dale stopped. He looked at his clipboard. The screen was broken, but the data was saved.

He looked at the beam.

He looked at the technicians emerging from the sewers, from the buildings, from the shadows.

They were all looking up.

All of them.

Dale checked his pockets. He had one canister of sealant left. One wrench. And 170 Treatment Points.

He needed 500 for the next level.

He looked at the nearest technician. It was a woman he knew. Linda from Sector 2.

She turned to him. Her eyes were white.

"Linda," Dale said.

She tilted her head. "Reporting for duty."

Dale raised his wrench.

"Report," Dale said. "You're fired."

He didn't wait for the response. He swung.

The crack of metal on bone echoed through the silent city.

And above him, the barcode in the sky scanned.
