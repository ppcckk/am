Chapter 113: Paperwork Breach

# Chapter 113

The woman in the blazer did not blink.

Her eyes were the color of wet slate, reflecting the streetlamp's sodium glow without absorbing it. She held a tablet against her chest like a shield, her fingers tapping a rhythm that had no melody. From her vantage point across the cracked pavement of Elm Street, the man running toward the address on the business card looked less like a survivor and more like a hazard.

She watched his stride. It was uneven.

His left shoulder dipped two inches lower than his right, suggesting a torn rotator cuff or a spinal compression from the fall.

His breathing was ragged, a wet rasp that cut through the silence of the abandoned suburban block. To her, he was not a hero. He was a breach.

He wore a jumpsuit stained with mud and ichor.

The fabric clung to him in places where the moisture had frozen against his skin. She noted the lack of protective gear.

No respirator.

No gloves. Just bare hands gripping a clipboard that was somehow still intact.

Her tablet pinged. *Subject approaching. Heart rate: 140 bpm. Cortisol levels: Critical.*

She did not move. The Licensing Authority did not send Auditors to help.

They sent them to weigh.

If the man collapsed before reaching the door, the case closed.

If he walked through it, the audit began.

She adjusted her cufflink. The silver pin caught the light, a tiny, cold star in the dark.

Dale didn't see her. He was too focused on the timer counting down in his peripheral vision.

*04:12 remaining.*

His lungs burned. The air tasted like copper and old rain. Every step sent a jolt of pain up his spine, a reminder of the hole he had dragged himself out of, of the needle in his neck, of the Auditor's cold voice. *You are now a carrier.*

He reached the curb. The house was a split-level ranch, the kind common in these subdivisions. Peeling siding.

A single window lit on the second floor. The front door was unlocked.

Dale didn't knock. He didn't have the energy for politeness. He pushed the door open. The hinges screamed.

Inside, the air was stale. It smelled of dust and something sharper, like ozone and burnt sugar. A vacuum hummed in the background, a low, constant drone that seemed to come from the walls themselves.

He stepped onto the welcome mat. His boots left muddy prints on the beige carpet.

"I am here for the investigation," Dale said.

His voice was flat. He didn't shout. He didn't plead.

He announced his presence like he was filing a noise complaint.

No one answered.

He pulled the clipboard from under his arm. The pen was still in it. He clicked it. *Click.*

"I need to see the contamination source," Dale said. "Protocol requires visual verification before isolation."

A door at the end of the hall opened.

The Auditor stood there. He wasn't wearing the blazer anymore.

He wore a simple white shirt, sleeves rolled up to the elbows.

He looked tired. His tie was loosened. He looked like a man who had been working a double shift and hated every minute of it.

Behind him, a young man sat at a desk. The young man wore a headset and typed furiously. Papers were stacked in precarious towers on the desk, threatening to collapse with every keystroke.

The Auditor stepped aside. "Enter. Wipe your feet."

Dale looked down at his muddy boots. Then he looked at the Auditor.

"I can't," Dale said. "Protocol 4-C: Cross-contamination prevention. If I wipe, I spread the residue from the rift zone to your entryway. That's a violation."

The Auditor paused. A flicker of something—respect? annoyance?—crossed his face.

"Fine. Leave them. Step onto the tray."

A metal grate had slid out from the floor. It was filled with a white, powdery substance.

Bleach?

Lime?

Dale didn't know. He stepped onto it.

The powder hissed as his wet boots made contact.

*System Alert: Environmental Decontamination Initiated. Passive Protection: 10%.*

Dale walked forward. The young man at the desk didn't look up. His eyes were bloodshot. His fingers flew across the keyboard.

"Sit," the Auditor said, pointing to a chair.

Dale sat. The chair was stiff. He placed the clipboard on his lap.

"You have four minutes," the Auditor said. He checked his watch.

"The window for appeal closes at midnight.

After that, the system locks. You become a statistic. A cleanup crew comes to burn this house and everything in it.

Including you."

Dale looked at the young man. The typing had stopped. The young man was staring at the screen, his face pale.

"Who is he?" Dale asked.

"My clerk," the Auditor said. "He is processing the backlog. You are job number 4,112. There are 3,000 jobs before you in the priority queue."

"That's why you're slow," Dale said. Not an accusation. A observation.

"We are understaffed," the Auditor said. "The Rifts are opening faster than we can certify exterminators.

Most of them fail.

Most of them die. You are the first one to make it to Level II with a Thoroughness Rating above 6.0 in six years."

Dale looked at his hands. They were still shaking, but less now. He forced them still by gripping the edge of the clipboard.

"I need to prove I'm not a carrier," Dale said.

"You need to prove the *source* is clean," the Auditor corrected. "If you touched a Class IX core, your biometric signature is flagged.

To clear the flag, you must identify the specific strain of dimensional residue you were exposed to and document its neutralization.

If you can't identify it, we assume you are infected.

If you are infected, we terminate you to prevent spread."

Dale stood up. "Where is the residue?"

The Auditor pointed to the window. "The second floor. The source of the breach. You have three minutes to get there and find the entry point."

Dale walked up the stairs. The steps creaked.

The hallway upstairs was narrow. The walls were lined with floral wallpaper, peeling in strips. The air grew thicker the higher he climbed. It felt like walking into a cloud of static electricity.

At the end of the hall, a door was closed.

Dale didn't rush. He slowed down. His anxiety spiked, but he used it. He scanned the doorframe.

No gaps. No cracks.

He looked at the floor.

Dust.

*System Alert: Particulate Matter Detected. Composition: Unknown.*

He looked at the ceiling.

A water stain. A brown circle.

He looked back at the door.

*System Alert: Anomaly Detected in Field Guide.*

A new entry appeared in his vision. Not a monster. A substance.

*Residue: Void-Spore.* *Status: Dormant.* *Threat Level: Low.*

Dale frowned. Dormant? The Auditor had said he touched a Class IX core.

That should be lethal. Active.

Corrosive.

If the residue was dormant, it wasn't attacking him. It was hiding.

Dale pushed the door open.

The room was a nursery. Pink walls. A crib. A rocking horse.

But the crib was empty.

In the center of the room, the carpet was bulging. A mound of fabric, rising like a bubble.

Dale approached it. He didn't touch it. He pulled a pen from his pocket and poked the fabric.

It was soft. Warm.

*System Alert: Biometric Scan Detected. Host: Human.*

Dale stepped back.

"Who is in here?" Dale called out.

Silence.

Then, a cough.

The mound shifted. A hand emerged from the fabric. Small. Pale.

Dale froze.

This wasn't a monster.

It was a child.

The Auditor appeared in the doorway. "The subject is a minor. Class IV Rift event.

The residue is not on the boy.

It is on the *toy*."

Dale looked at the crib.

On the mattress, beneath the blanket, was a stuffed bear. It was ragged. One eye was missing.

Dale walked to the crib. He didn't look at the boy. He looked at the bear.

*System Alert: Field Guide Update.* *Item: Cursed Artifact.* *Species: N/A.* *Type: Parasitic Anchor.*

Dale picked up the bear. It was heavy. Too heavy.

He turned it over.

Stitched into the back of the bear, hidden by the fur, was a patch of black slime. It pulsed. Slowly.

Like a heartbeat.

"The Auditor said I touched the core," Dale said. He held the bear up. "I didn't. I touched the *anchor*."

The Auditor's eyes narrowed. "Explain."

"Dale Kowalski, Certified Exterminator," Dale said.

"My job is not to fight the monster.

My job is to treat the infestation. This slime is not a pathogen.

It is a nest."

Dale pulled a small vial from his pocket. It was empty. He hadn't used it. He had saved it.

"This is Void-Spore," Dale said. "It doesn't infect humans.

It infects *objects*.

It uses them as anchors to keep the rift open. I didn't touch the core. I touched the anchor.

My biometric signature is clean because I didn't absorb the spores.

I absorbed the *connection*."

The Auditor stepped forward. "That is a distinction without a difference. To the system, you are contaminated."

"No," Dale said. "Look at the Field Guide."

He turned the clipboard so the Auditor could see. The System had updated.

*Entry: Void-Spore Anchor.* *Weakness: Isolation.* *Protocol: Seal and Dispose.*

Dale pointed to the slime on the bear.

"It's not active because it's isolated. The boy is holding it, but the boy isn't the host. The bear is.

The boy is just a carrier of the *object*."

Dale pulled a roll of duct tape from his belt. He had stolen it from a hardware store three blocks back. He hadn't thought about it. His hands had just done it.

He wrapped the bear.

Layer after layer.

He sealed the eyes. He sealed the mouth.

He sealed the slime.

He held the wrapped bundle up.

*System Alert: Containment Protocol Initiated.* *Effect: Nullification.* *Points Awarded: 50.*

The Auditor stared at the bundle.

"You sealed it," the Auditor said.

"I isolated it," Dale corrected. "Now it's a hazardous waste item. It's no longer a threat. It's garbage."

The System flashed.

*Investigation Review: Pending.* *Contamination Check: Negative.* *Reason: Source Identified and Neutralized.*

The Auditor exhaled. It was a long, weary sound.

"You have two minutes," the Auditor said. "The system needs to process the update. If it fails, you die."

Dale looked at the boy. The boy was crying. Not loud sobs. Silent tears.

Dale walked to the boy. He didn't offer comfort. He offered a tissue from his pocket.

"Go to the kitchen," Dale said. "Eat something. Don't touch anything."

The boy looked at him. Then he looked at the wrapped bear.

"Is it dead?" the boy whispered.

"It's treated," Dale said. "The problem is gone."

The boy nodded. He walked out of the room.

Dale stood alone in the nursery. The air was clearing. The ozone smell was fading.

The Auditor walked in. He looked at the wrapped bear on the bed.

"You understand what this means," the Auditor said.

"It means I'm not contaminated," Dale said.

"It means you are dangerous," the Auditor said. "You didn't kill it. You *filed* it."

Dale looked up. "That's the job."

The Auditor smiled. It was a thin, sharp thing.

"Welcome to Level II," the Auditor said.

The System flashed.

*Certification Level II Achieved.* *New Protocol Unlocked: Hazardous Waste Transport.* *Warning: New Rival Detected.*

A sound came from the hallway. Not footsteps.

Dragging.

Something heavy being pulled across the floorboards.

Dale turned.

The Auditor didn't move. He didn't look at the door. He looked at Dale.

"You didn't finish the job," the Auditor said.

"I sealed the anchor," Dale said.

"You sealed the anchor," the Auditor agreed. "But you didn't document the *secondary* infestation."

The dragging sound grew louder. It was coming from the stairs.

*System Alert: Unauthorized Entity Detected.* *Species: Auditor's Hound.* *Threat Level: Class III.*

Dale looked at the doorway.

A creature crawled in. It was shaped like a dog, but made of ink and shadow. It had too many legs. Its eyes were blank, white orbs.

It wasn't attacking Dale. It was sniffing the wrapped bear.

The Auditor stepped back.

"That is my clerk's mistake," the Auditor said. "My clerk failed to report a secondary breach. The Hound is here to correct the error."

Dale looked at the Hound. He looked at the Auditor.

"He's going to kill the bear," Dale said.

"He's going to *eat* the bear," the Auditor corrected. "And if he eats it, he becomes a carrier.

And then he becomes a liability.

And then I have to terminate him. And you have to watch."

Dale stepped forward. He held the clipboard tight.

"I can stop him," Dale said.

"You can't fight a Hound," the Auditor said. "They are immune to physical damage. They are immune to chemical agents. They only respond to one thing."

"What?"

"Documentation," the Auditor said. "If you fill out the proper form, the Hound recognizes the item as 'processed waste' and leaves it alone. If you don't, it consumes it. And then it consumes you."

The Hound lunged.

Dale didn't dodge. He opened the clipboard. He flipped to a blank page.

He had twenty seconds.

The Hound slammed into the bed. The wrapped bear flew into the air.

Dale grabbed the pen.

*System Alert: Form 11-B: Hazardous Waste Disposal Authorization Required.*

The Hound's jaws snapped shut on the bear.

Dale's hand moved faster than his thought. He wrote the title. He wrote the date. He wrote the species.

*Void-Spore Anchor.*

The Hound paused. It sniffed the paper.

Dale signed it.

*Signature: Dale Kowalski.*

The Hound looked at the signature. Then it looked at Dale.

It dropped the bear.

The bear landed on the floor, still wrapped in duct tape.

The Hound sat back on its haunches. It tilted its head.

*System Alert: Compliance Verified.* *Penalty: Avoided.*

The Auditor clapped. Slowly.

"Excellent," the Auditor said. "You used the bureaucracy as a shield."

"It's a tool," Dale said. He was breathing hard. His hands were sweating.

"It is a weapon," the Auditor said. "And you are now licensed to use it."

The Hound stood up. It walked to the door. It left.

Dale slumped onto the bed.

The Auditor walked to the window. He looked out at the street. The woman in the blazer was gone.

"You have a problem," the Auditor said.

"I passed," Dale said.

"You passed the test," the Auditor said. "But you failed the context."

Dale looked up. "What context?"

The Auditor turned. He held up a tablet. On the screen was a map of Ohio. Hundreds of red dots.

"The Authority doesn't want exterminators," the Auditor said. "We want *cleaners*. And you just cleaned a nest. But you left the trail."

Dale looked at the floor. Where the Hound had stood, black sludge remained.

"It followed the smell," Dale said.

"It follows the *report*," the Auditor said.

"You filed a report. The report went to the Central Server.

The Server flagged the location.

Now, everyone knows where you are."

A notification popped up on Dale's vision.

*Global Leaderboard Update.* *Rank: #1,042.* *Status: Targeted.*

*New Quest: Survival Audit.* *Objective: Defend your certification against three challengers.* *First Challenger: Arriving in 10 minutes.*

Dale looked at the door.

Footsteps. Heavy boots.

Not the Hound.

Someone human.

The Auditor picked up his briefcase. "I wouldn't stay. The first challenger is a Level IV. He doesn't use clipboards."

Dale stood up. He grabbed the wrapped bear.

"Where do I go?" Dale asked.

The Auditor opened the front door. Outside, the street was empty. But the air was thick with the smell of ozone.

"You go where the paperwork goes," the Auditor said.

Dale stepped out.

He looked at the house. The lights in the nursery flickered and died.

He looked at his watch.

12:01 AM.

He was late.

And they were already hunting him.
