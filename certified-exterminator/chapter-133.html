Chapter 133: The Auditor Is the Infestation

# Chapter 133

Officer Miller watched the man in the suit fall.

He expected blood. He expected screaming. He expected the chaos of a standard homicide scene, complete with the usual smell of cordite and the frantic energy of a suspect caught off guard.

Instead, the suspect disappeared.

The pavement didn't crack open like a sinkhole. It folded. The concrete tiles lifted in a perfect square, hovering for a microsecond before the pale hand yanked Dale Kowalski downward into the void. The glass-like shards of the attacker's hand tinkled onto the asphalt, scattering under the tires of the patrol car.

Miller lowered his rifle. His finger was still white-knuckled on the trigger, but the weapon dipped toward the ground.

"What the hell?" Officer Ruiz whispered, stepping back. Her flashlight beam swept the empty square of raised concrete. "Did he just... fall through the street?"

"Check the perimeter," Miller barked, though his voice lacked its usual command. "Don't let anyone near that hole. Call dispatch. Tell them we have a 10-31—suspect fled via... subterranean egress."

Ruiz didn't move. She was staring at the black card still clutched in Dale's hand, which had remained suspended in the air for a heartbeat longer than he had, defying gravity before dropping to the ground.

The card pulsed. A faint, rhythmic thrumming that Miller felt in his teeth.

"Pick it up," Miller said.

"Should I?"

"It's evidence. Pick it up."

Ruiz approached the edge of the geometric fracture. The edges were smooth, unnaturally polished.

No jagged rebar.

No dust. Just a clean cut into the earth, as if the world had been sliced with a laser.

She reached down. Her glove touched the black surface of the card.

A static shock snapped her finger back. She yelped, rubbing the sting.

"It burned," she said.

Miller stepped forward. He looked at the empty hole. He looked at the shattered glass hand. Then he looked at the clipboard lying on the ground, untouched by the violence.

He picked up the clipboard.

The paper was blank. No report.

No name.

Just a single checkmark in a box labeled *PENDING VERIFICATION*.

"Dale Kowalski," Miller muttered, reading the nameplate on the jacket left behind. "Licensed Pest Control Technician."

The Authority did not send police to arrest criminals.

The Authority sent inspectors to audit infrastructure.

Miller realized, with a cold dread settling in his gut, that he had just witnessed an audit failure. And in this city, that meant something worse than jail.

***

The air tasted of ozone and copper.

Dale hit the floor hard. His knees buckled, absorbing the impact with practiced efficiency.

He didn't roll.

He didn't scramble.

He dropped into a crouch, his center of gravity low, his weight balanced on the balls of his feet.

The pale hand that had grabbed him shattered upon impact, leaving Dale standing alone in a circular chamber.

The walls were obsidian. Smooth. Reflective.

No exit.

Dale straightened his tie. He adjusted his collar. He checked his shoes for scuffs.

*Protocol check.*

*Environment: Enclosed. Dark. No visible light sources other than the faint glow from the ceiling vents.*

*Threat: Neutralized. The attacker was Class-3 Dimensional Construct, likely a retrieval agent for the Licensing Authority.*

*Objective: Unknown.*

A notification pinged in the corner of his vision. Not the polite, bureaucratic text of the standard interface. This one was jagged, red, and flashing.

**VIOLATION DETECTED: UNLICENSED ABSENCE.**

**PENALTY: 500 TP.**

**STATUS: PROBATION.**

Dale blinked. He hadn't even completed the transaction. He hadn't accepted the job.

He had merely failed to decline it. The Authority, it seemed, treated indecision as a breach of contract.

He tapped his temple, dismissing the alert. It remained. A persistent itch behind his eyes.

From the shadows of the chamber, a figure emerged.

It was tall. Thin. Dressed in a suit that looked like it was made of gray static. Its face was a blur of shifting pixels, unable to settle on a single expression.

"Technician Kowalski," the figure said. Its voice sounded like paper tearing. "You are late."

"I was abducted," Dale said. His voice was steady. Dry. "I was in the process of closing a site report."

"The site is compromised."

"Which site?"

"The Rift. The one you have been poking at for three months."

Dale's hand twitched.

He reached for his clipboard. It wasn't there. He reached for his spray can.

Empty.

His pockets held only lint and the bent pen.

"I am on leave," Dale said.

"You are on probation."

"I submitted the paperwork for leave six days ago."

"The paperwork was rejected."

Dale paused. "On what grounds?"

"Insufficient thoroughness.

Your last report contained three minor errors. A misspelling in the species classification. An incorrect date stamp.

And a failure to document the psychological impact of the extermination on the residential occupants."

Dale felt a muscle in his jaw tighten. He didn't look at the figure. He looked at the walls.

"Psychological impact," Dale repeated. "I killed a broodmother. I didn't interview the homeowner."

"Standard Protocol IX requires a post-incident welfare check. You skipped it."

"I was busy."

"You were careless."

The figure stepped closer. The air around it grew cold. Frost formed on Dale's breath.

"This is why you are here, Kowalski. The Authority does not tolerate sloppiness. Especially not from a Level IV technician."

Dale looked up.

Level IV.

He hadn't been promoted in six months. Not because he lacked skill, but because his Thoroughness Rating had stalled at 8.9.

The Authority wanted perfection. Dale gave them compliance. There was a difference.

"I fixed the errors," Dale said. "I resubmitted."

"Too late."

The figure raised a hand. It didn't hold a weapon. It held a pen. A long, silver needle-thin instrument.

"Write," the figure commanded.

"Write what?"

"Your confession. Admit that you are unfit. Admit that you prioritize speed over safety. Admit that you view the Rift as an inconvenience rather than a crisis."

Dale stared at the pen.

"If I write that, I lose my license."

"You already have. You are on probation. The next step is revocation."

Dale took a step back. His heel hit the wall.

He looked at the figure. He looked at the pen. He looked at the floor, where the geometric lines of the chamber formed a grid.

*System Rule: Identification precedes elimination.*

*System Rule: Documentation is the shield.*

Dale didn't speak. He didn't argue. He reached into his pocket and pulled out the bent pen he had saved from the police station.

It was a cheap ballpoint. Blue ink. The tip was crumpled.

"Is this a valid writing instrument?" Dale asked.

The figure tilted its head. "It is inadequate."

"It is available."

Dale clicked the pen.

*Click.*

Nothing happened. The figure didn't flinch.

Dale looked at the blank space in his mind. The Field Guide. It was empty regarding this entity. He had no entry for *Authority Auditor*.

He had to create one. On the fly.

"You are not an Auditor," Dale said.

The figure stopped moving. "Explain."

"The Authority does not intervene directly. They process applications.

They issue certifications.

They do not kidnap technicians to force confessions. That is a violation of their own charter, Section 4, Paragraph 12: *Non-Interference in Personal Conduct.*"

The figure's pixelated face flickered. A glitch. A stutter.

"Your interpretation is flawed."

"Prove it," Dale said. "Show me the authorization code for this detention."

"There is no code. This is internal review."

"Then this is not an audit," Dale said. "This is harassment."

He pointed the bent pen at the figure. Not as a weapon. As a pointer.

"And harassment is a Class-1 Infestation. Verbal abuse. Coercion. Intimidation."

The figure lunged.

Dale didn't dodge. He dropped to one knee.

The silver pen slashed through the air where Dale's head had been. It struck the obsidian wall, sparking.

Dale rolled forward. He came up behind the figure.

He didn't try to hit it. He tried to tag it.

He slammed his clipboard against the figure's back. The cardboard edge caught the static suit.

*Crunch.*

The sound was wrong. It sounded like breaking plastic.

The figure screamed. A high-pitched frequency that shattered the glass lights overhead.

Dale covered his ears. His eyes watered.

But the contact worked.

A notification flashed.

**IDENTIFICATION COMPLETE.**

**SPECIES: CORRUPTED BUREAUCRACY (Variant: Aggressive).**

**WEAKNESS: PROCEDURAL RIGIDITY.**

Dale gasped for air.

His heart hammered against his ribs. He had done it.

He had identified the threat.

And he had found the weakness.

*Procedural Rigidity.*

The figure turned, its face a mask of white noise. It raised the silver pen again.

"Write," it shrieked. "Write your confession."

Dale backed away. He looked at the floor. The grid.

He looked at the figure.

He looked at the clipboard.

He picked up the blank paper.

With the bent blue pen, he wrote one word.

*NO.*

He held the paper up.

The figure froze.

"I refuse," Dale said. "You have not provided the correct forms.

You have not presented your badge.

You have not cited the specific statute under which this detention is occurring.

Therefore, this interaction is void."

The figure shook. The static around it intensified.

"Accept the form," it hissed.

"Form is invalid," Dale said. "I am exercising my right to silence until proper protocol is observed."

The figure lunged again. Faster this time.

Dale sidestepped. He didn't run. He walked. He maintained his pace, his posture, his professionalism.

He walked toward the door he had seen when he landed. It was closed.

The figure slammed into the wall. It didn't break. It bounced off, confused.

Dale reached the door. He tried the handle. Locked.

He turned back.

The figure was crawling toward him. It had lost its humanoid shape. It was becoming a jagged mess of lines and text, a physical manifestation of red tape made flesh.

**CROSS-CONTAMINATION WARNING:**

**PROTOCOL MISMATCH. YOU ARE USING LEGAL DEFENSE AGAINST A PHYSICAL THREAT.**

**PENALTY: 40% DAMAGE DEBUFF.**

Dale felt a wave of nausea. His vision blurred. The debuff was hitting him. His body felt heavy, slow.

He couldn't fight it physically. He had to fight it procedurally.

He looked at the figure. He looked at the door.

He pulled out the black card. The one he had dropped. The one the police had seen.

He held it up.

"Show me your license," Dale said.

The figure paused.

"I do not carry a license," it said. "I *am* the Authority."

"The Authority requires licensure for all agents acting in the field," Dale said. "Section 2, Paragraph 5. All enforcement officers must carry a Class-III Identification Token."

The figure twitched.

"I do not need to show you..."

"Then you are operating illegally."

Dale stepped forward. The debuff was making him sluggish, but his mind was clear. Sharper than it had ever been.

"I am filing a complaint," Dale said. "Against an unlicensed entity impersonating the Licensing Authority."

The figure stopped. The text on its body swirled.

*Complaint filed?*

Dale reached into his pocket. He pulled out the pen.

He wrote on the back of the card.

*Violation: Impersonation.*

*Violation: Lack of Credential.*

*Violation: Assault.*

He held the card out.

"Sign here," Dale said.

The figure stared at the card. It stared at Dale.

For a second, the room was silent.

Then, the figure's hand moved. Not to attack. To take.

It grabbed the card.

The moment the card left Dale's hand, the figure dissolved.

It didn't vanish. It unraveled. The gray static broke apart into thousands of tiny, floating documents.

Receipts. Forms.

Notices.

They swirled around the room like a blizzard of paper.

Dale stood in the center of the storm.

He looked down at his hands. They were shaking.

The debuff lifted. The nausea faded.

A new notification appeared.

**AUDIT COMPLETE.**

**THOROUGHNESS RATING: 10.0.**

**REWARD: TEMPORARY LICENSING EXEMPTION.**

**NOTICE: YOU ARE NOW FLAGGED FOR INTERVENTION.**

Dale read the text.

*Flagged for Intervention.*

That wasn't a reward. That was a target.

The blizzard of paper coalesced into a single sheet. It drifted down and landed on Dale's clipboard.

It was a summons.

Not for him.

For the person outside.

The summons named *Officer Miller*.

And it listed a violation: *Unlicensed Interference with an Active Exterminator.*

Dale looked up. The door to the chamber had unlocked. It clicked open.

He stepped out.

The street was gone.

The brick building was gone.

The police cars were gone.

He was standing in the lobby of the Licensing Authority.

The vast, white, sterile hall that existed between dimensions.

And standing at the reception desk, holding a clipboard that looked exactly like his, was a woman he had never seen before.

She wore the same suit. The same tie. The same expression of polite, bureaucratic disdain.

"Mr. Kowalski," she said. "We need to discuss your performance review."

She didn't smile.

She opened her folder.

Inside was a picture of Dale.

But it wasn't Dale now.

It was Dale from ten years ago. Before the rift. Before the monsters. Before the first rat.

And in the photo, Dale was holding a sword.

A glowing, magical sword.

A weapon that didn't exist in his world.

A weapon that the Authority had banned.

Dale felt his stomach drop.

The woman closed the folder.

"You have been hiding a Class-IX Artifact," she said. "And you have been lying about your past."

Dale opened his mouth. He wanted to say he didn't know what she was talking about. He wanted to say he had never held a sword.

But the memory was there. Buried. Deep.

The memory of a night he didn't remember.

The memory of blood on his hands that wasn't from a rat.

The memory of a voice telling him to *cut*.

Dale looked at the woman.

He looked at his empty hands.

He looked at the summons for Officer Miller.

He looked at the sword in the photo.

And then, from the shadows behind the reception desk, a hand reached out.

It was pale.

It was human.

And it held a pen.

Dale realized, with a cold certainty, that he was not the auditor.

He was the infestation.

And the Authority had finally found the nest.
