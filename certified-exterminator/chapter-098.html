Chapter 98: No Termination Clause

# Chapter 98

The pen tip dug into the paper, tearing the fiber. Dale didn’t notice. He was writing faster than his hand had ever moved, not in neat, blocky print, but in a jagged, frantic scrawl that looked less like documentation and more like a confession.

*Violation Code: 9-Alpha. Systemic Fabrication.*

The words blurred. The truck rattled over the fractured asphalt of County Road 9, the suspension groaning under the weight of their panic. Miller kept his eyes on the road, knuckles white on the steering wheel, but Dale wasn’t looking at the road. He was looking at the clipboard.

*Evidence: The broodmother’s chitin composition matches the synthetic resin used in Authority containment units, not organic biological matter. The frass trails contained trace amounts of Class IV stabilizer, a compound not naturally occurring in the Rift biome. Conclusion: The infestation was seeded. The threat was manufactured to justify the Authority’s continued funding and expansion.*

He underlined *manufactured* twice. Hard enough to leave an indentation on the desk below.

"Are you writing your suicide note?" Miller asked, his voice tight.

"It’s an inspection report," Dale said, not looking up. "And it’s incomplete."

"It’s an accusation against the government that runs the magic system, Dale. If they catch you before you file it, they won’t arrest you. They’ll just delete you."

Dale felt a cold sweat prickling his hairline. The shaking in his hands wasn’t stopping.

It was worse, which was a vibration, a hum in his bones. The **AUDIT INITIATED** notification still hovered in his peripheral vision, pulsing with a sickly amber light.

**AUDIT STATUS: PENDING REVIEW.** **HOLDING PATTERN: ACTIVE.** **SUBJECT: KOWALSKI, DALE.**

The world around him flickered. For a split second, the interior of the truck turned grey, then snapped back to color. The trees outside the window froze, mid-swipe, then rushed forward again.

"They’re already in the system," Dale muttered. "They’re checking my logs."

"Can they?" Miller glanced over. "The Authority is supposed to be impartial."

"The Authority is a bureaucracy, Miller.

Bureaucracies don’t care about truth. They care about procedure.

And I just broke the most important procedural rule in the book." Dale finally looked up.

"I didn’t just accuse them. I accused them without having the proper jurisdiction. A Level II technician cannot audit a Class V Inspector.

That’s not just an error. That’s a structural breach."

"So what? We run. We find a safe zone. We hide."

"You can’t hide from a database query." Dale ripped the page out of the clipboard. He crumpled it, then smoothed it out against his knee. "But you can challenge the query. You can demand a peer review."

Miller slammed on the brakes as the truck skidded to a halt near the edge of the woods. "What?"

"Dale, stop.

We need to move.

That Inspector didn’t freeze because of your accusation. It froze because the System got confused.

Give it ten seconds, it’ll reboot, and it’ll know exactly where we are."

"If we run, we’re fugitives. If we file the report, we’re whistleblowers.

The Authority has to process the paperwork, which has to follow its own rules, and it has to send a human auditor, not an automated script. Humans can be bribed.

Humans can be reasoned with.

Humans can be confused."

Miller stared at him. "You want to confuse the Authority?"

"I want to bury the accusation in enough procedural noise that it takes them three weeks to sort through the red tape," Dale said. He pulled a spare blank form from his kit. **FORM 44-B: REQUEST FOR RECLASSIFICATION OF THREAT LEVEL.**

"What does that do?"

"Nothing, normally.

It’s a useless form for when you find a cockroach and think it might be a beetle but you’re not sure. But if you attach it to a Class V accusation, it creates a logical loop. The System has to verify if the Class V entity *could* be misidentified as a Class IV pest before it processes the high-level audit.

It forces the Authority to lower the threat level temporarily to process the paperwork."

"That’s insane."

"It’s OSHA compliant." Dale began writing on the new form.

His handwriting returned to its usual sterile, blocky precision. *Subject: Inspector Vane.

Classification: Class V. Proposed Reclassification: Class IV (Ambiguous).

Rationale: Incomplete Field Guide Entry.*

The truck shuddered. The amber light in Dale’s vision flared brighter.

**AUDIT STATUS: INTERVENING.** **ACTION REQUIRED: RESOLVE IDENTITY CONFLICT.**

"It’s working," Dale whispered.

"How do you know?"

"Because the vibration stopped."

Miller didn’t wait for confirmation. He put the truck in reverse, tires spinning in the dirt, and swung it around, heading not away from town, but toward the old municipal library.

"Why there?"

"The library has the only public terminal connected to the Legacy Database," Dale said. "It’s hardwired.

No wireless connection.

The System can’t audit a hardline terminal from the cloud. We have to physically submit the report."

"The System can track you, Dale. It knows where the truck is."

"Not if we go underground." Dale pointed to a storm drain grate near the library steps.

"The ventilation shafts. The Authority ignores subterranean infrastructure.

Too many variables.

Too much noise."

Miller looked at the grate, then at the sky, and the clouds were swirling into a vortex above the town center. The Inspector was coming. Or worse, the System was rewriting reality to remove Dale’s existence before he could speak.

"Fine," Miller said. "But if we die down there, I’m haunting your next inspection."

Dale didn’t answer. He was already calculating. He had one trick left. The **CROSS-CONTAMINATION PENALTY**.

He had tried to use a Class III protocol on a Class V entity; the penalty was a 40% debuff and a contaminated Field Guide entry. But what if he triggered the penalty intentionally?

He opened his Field Guide. The entry for **Rattus Profundus** was flashing red. *CONTAMINATED. DATA UNRELIABLE.*

Dale stared at the warning. If he submitted a report containing contaminated data, the Authority’s automated filters would flag it as *Garbage*. But the accusation itself was in the attachment.

If the primary document was flagged as invalid due to contamination, but the attachment was a separate, uncorrupted file...

The System’s logic was rigid. It segregated data types. If the Field Guide data was bad, it shouldn't invalidate the text of the complaint, provided the complaint didn't reference the bad data.

Dale had to write the complaint so it didn't cite the Field Guide. He had to cite *external* evidence.

He pulled out a small plastic bag from his kit. Inside was a shard of the Inspector’s armor. He had grabbed it when he ran. It was cold, vibrating slightly.

"Miller, keep the engine running. I’m going down."

"You're going alone?"

"I have to submit the report from a terminal that doesn't have a biometric link to my current status. If I log in with my ID, the audit triggers instantly. I need to use a guest account. And guest accounts require physical presence at the terminal."

Miller nodded slowly. "Go. I’ll watch the street."

Dale climbed out of the truck. The air smelled of ozone and rotting vegetation. The sky was bruised purple, and he moved quickly, his clipboard clutched to his chest, his boots crunching on broken glass.

He reached the storm drain. It was rusted, the grate half-collapsed. He kicked it aside, revealing a dark square of black water.

He jumped, and the drop was twelve feet. He landed in shallow, icy water, the impact jarring his knees. His flashlight beam cut through the darkness, illuminating moss-slicked concrete and the occasional scurrying of rats—normal rats, thank God.

He waded through the tunnel, following the signage he had memorized from city maps decades ago. *Library. Ventilation. Sub-level 2.*

The tunnel opened into a maintenance shaft that led directly to the library’s basement archive. Dale climbed a ladder, his muscles burning, and pushed open a heavy steel door.

He was in, and the basement was dusty, lined with shelves of obsolete microfiche; a terminal sat on a desk in the corner, covered in a sheet.

Dale approached it. He lifted the sheet. The screen was black.

He pressed a key.

**LOGIN REQUIRED.**

Dale didn’t have a password for this machine. He didn’t have access.

But he had a workaround, and he pulled out his technician’s badge. It was a simple RFID chip. He held it against the card reader.

**ACCESS DENIED. UNAUTHORIZED USER.**

Dale sighed. He had expected that.

He looked at the keyboard. He typed in the command: **sudo -u auditor.**

**ACCESS DENIED.**

He tried again. **sudo -u inspector.**

**ACCESS DENIED.**

The audit was accelerating. Dale could feel it. A pressure in his skull. The world was trying to delete him.

He had one option left. The **Field Guide**.

He opened his mental interface. The **Contaminated Entry** was pulsing. He couldn't use it for combat. But he could use it for *documentation*.

The System allowed users to flag errors in the Field Guide. It was a maintenance function. A bug report.

Dale typed: **/report_error [ID: 404-VANE]**

**ERROR: ID NOT FOUND IN LOCAL CACHE.**

"Of course not," Dale muttered. The Inspector was a Class V entity. The local cache only held up to Class III.

He had to upload the data, and he pulled the shard of armor from his pocket. He placed it on the desk next to the keyboard.

Then he typed: **/upload_evidence [FILE: SHARD.CRT] [CATEGORY: CLASS_V_ANOMALY]**

The System hesitated.

**WARNING: CLASS V DATA UPLOAD REQUIRES CLASS V AUTHORIZATION.**

Dale smiled. It was a dry, humorless thing.

He knew the loophole, and the Authority had a provision for *emergency cross-referencing*. If a technician discovered a creature that *should not exist* according to the standard taxonomy, they could upload a fragment of its tissue to the central database for immediate classification.

It was meant for rare, undiscovered species.

But Dale wasn’t uploading a species. He was uploading *evidence of fabrication*.

He typed the next command carefully.

**/upload_evidence [FILE: SHARD.CRT] [CATEGORY: TAXONOMICAL_ERROR]**

**QUERY: IDENTIFY SOURCE OF ANOMALY.**

The System processed. The screen flickered.

**ANALYSIS: MATERIAL COMPOSITION MATCHES AUTHORITY CONTAINMENT PROTOCOLS (VERSION 4.2).**

There it was. The smoking gun.

The System had just confirmed, on the screen, that the Inspector’s armor was made of the same stuff as the containment units used to breed the monsters.

Dale typed the accusation text into the report field. He didn't reference the Field Guide. He referenced the *System’s own analysis*.

*Report: The entity designated 'Inspector Vane' possesses material composition identical to Authority containment units. This suggests the entity is not a natural Rift manifestation, but a synthetic construct deployed by the Authority. Requesting immediate review of containment logs for Sector 7.*

He hit **SUBMIT**.

**SUBMISSION RECEIVED.** **STATUS: PENDING REVIEW.**

**AUDIT: SUSPENDED.**

The pressure in his skull vanished.

Dale exhaled. He slumped against the desk.

He had done it. He had paused the erasure.

But then the screen changed.

**REVIEWER ASSIGNED: ADMINISTRATOR ZERO.**

**NOTE: ANOMALY CONFIRMED. SUBJECT KOWALSKI FLAGGED AS HIGH-RISK ASSET.**

**ACTION: RECRUITMENT.**

The door to the archive burst open.

Dale spun around.

Miller stood in the doorway. But he wasn’t alone.

Behind him stood a figure clad in armor that looked identical to the Inspector’s. But this one was pristine. White and gold. Its face was hidden behind a mask that reflected the terminal screen back at Dale.

The figure stepped forward. It didn’t speak. It just held out a hand.

In the palm was a new badge.

**LEVEL IX LICENSE.**

**RANK: INTERNAL AUDITOR.**

"Pick it up, Dale," Miller said, his voice trembling. "They’re not going to erase you."

Dale looked at the badge. Then he looked at Miller.

Miller’s eyes were wide, terrified. They also held something else. A hunger.

"They’re going to make you fix it," Miller whispered. "From the inside."

The armored figure took a step closer. It extended the badge further.

Dale looked at the terminal. The submission was still pending. The Authority had acknowledged the error, but it hadn’t punished him. It had *promoted* him.

Because if Dale was an Auditor, he could bury the evidence; he could classify the fabrication as a *necessary administrative cost*. He could rewrite the report to say the monsters were *necessary*.

And if he didn't take the badge, he was dead.

If he did take it, he became part of the machine that farmed the monsters.

Dale looked at his hands. They were steady now.

He reached out.

His fingers hovered over the cold metal of the badge.

The figure waited.

Miller held his breath.

Dale looked at the terminal one last time. The words *PENDING REVIEW* glowed green.

He grabbed the badge.

The figure snapped it onto Dale’s chest.

A surge of power ripped through Dale’s body. Not pain. *Clarity*.

He saw the town outside differently now. The cracks in the road were data streams.

The trees were code.

The Rift wasn't a tear in reality, which was a *server room*.

**SYSTEM NOTICE: WELCOME, AUDITOR KOWALSKI.**

**CURRENT OBJECTIVE: CONTAINMENT PROTOCOL DELTA. TARGET: THE RESISTANCE.**

Dale looked at Miller.

Miller was smiling. But the smile didn't reach his eyes.

"Well?" Dale asked, his voice sounding strange in his own ears. Metallic. Flat.

Miller stepped back. "They said you’d accept. They knew you’d choose order."

Dale looked down at his clipboard. The accusation was still there.

He looked at the **Field Guide**.

The entry for **Rattus Profundus** was gone.

In its place was a new entry.

**SUBJECT: RESISTANCE OPERATIVES.** **CLASSIFICATION: PEST.** **PROTOCOL: TOTAL ERADICATION.**

Dale closed the guide.

"Let's go," he said. "We have a job to do."

Miller nodded, relieved. He turned to leave the archive.

Dale didn’t follow, and he stayed at the terminal.

He pulled up the report he had just filed.

**STATUS: APPROVED.**

He hit **DELETE**.

**DELETE COMPLETE.**

The file was gone. The evidence was gone.

Dale had just erased his own proof of corruption, and he had accepted the badge; he had become the Auditor.

But he had also deleted the record.

Which meant the Authority didn't know he had deleted it.

He had one clean slate. And he had the highest level of access in the world.

He opened a new file.

**/new_document [FILE: MASTER_PLAN.DOC]**

He typed one line.

*The farm is real. The monsters are food. And I am the head chef.*

He saved it to a hidden partition.

Then he turned and walked out of the archive.

Miller was waiting by the truck.

"Ready?" Miller asked.

Dale climbed into the passenger seat. He looked out the window at the town, at the people fleeing in panic, at the soldiers setting up containment zones.

He saw them not as victims, and he saw them as *clients*.

And clients were always late on their payments.

"Ready," Dale said.

But as the truck pulled away, Dale felt a vibration in his pocket.

He pulled out his phone.

A text message from an unknown number.

*Welcome to the Authority, Dale. Did you enjoy the upgrade? We’ve been watching you for a long time.

Your father was one of us. We’re glad you followed in his footsteps.*

Dale stopped breathing.

His father had died in a construction accident when Dale was six, and the Authority had *faked* his death.

And now they had him.

The truck sped toward the edge of town, toward the Rift, toward the heart of the farm.

Dale sat in the silence, the weight of the gold badge crushing his chest.

He wasn’t a hero, and he wasn’t a victim.

He was a pest control technician.

And he had just accepted a job he couldn’t quit.

The contract had no termination clause.
