Chapter 279: Unauthorized Discharge

# Chapter 279

The clipboard was gone. The water swallowed it without a splash, just a soft, wet absorption that made Dale's stomach drop.

He stared at the ripples. They didn't settle. The surface of the reservoir churned, not from wind, but from the displacement of mass. A thousand gallons of sludge and swarm moving in unison.

Dale didn't pick up the clipboard. He couldn't. His hands were busy.

His left hand gripped the emergency shut-off valve on the maintenance pipe jutting from the concrete embankment. His right hand held a flare, the red phosphorus burning with a hiss against the damp night air. He wasn't looking at the face anymore. The face had retreated beneath the surface, its smile still visible through the murk, watching.

Dale looked at the notification blinking in his peripheral vision.

**INFRACTION DETECTED: UNAUTHORIZED DISCHARGE.** **VIOLATION CODE: ENV-99-OMEGA.** **STATUS: CRITICAL.**

The Authority wasn't just watching. It was auditing.

The swarm behind him, a chittering tide of Class-3 Vermin, pressed against his back. The hive mind screeched, a psychic pressure that tasted like copper and old blood.

It wanted to surge forward.

It wanted the reservoir. It wanted the pipes.

If they got into the municipal supply, the infestation wouldn't just be local. It would be systemic. Every toilet in the county would become a portal. Every tap, a mouth.

Dale adjusted his grip on the flare. He didn't shout. He didn't scream. He spoke into the air, his voice dry and clipped, carrying the weight of fifteen years of writing violation notices that nobody read until the house caught fire.

"Field Note 44-B," Dale said. "Water source compromised. Bio-load estimated at four thousand units. Cross-contamination risk: Severe."

The face in the water tilted. The eyes, formed of swirling silt, narrowed.

"You're interrupting the exam," the water-face gurgled. Its voice sounded like gravel shifting under a tide.

"Dale ignored it.

He turned to the swarm. He raised his free hand, the one not holding the flare, and made a sharp, cutting gesture across his throat.

It wasn't a command to attack, which was a command to stop.

The hive mind hesitated. The swarm froze, a wall of chitin and limbs halting inches from his heels.

Dale pointed the flare at the open pipe mouth. The red light reflected in the black water, casting long, jagged shadows.

"Protocol: Isolate," Dale muttered. "Source containment failed. Emergency seal required."

**SYSTEM NOTIFICATION: COMMAND PROTOCOL UNLOCKED: HYDRO-SHUTDOWN.** **COST: 150 TP.** **THOROUGHNESS PENALTY: -2.0 FOR MANUAL OVERRIDE.**

Dale didn't care about the penalty. He slammed the shut-off valve.

The metal groaned, rusted threads fighting to lock into place.

The current against his legs intensified.

The reservoir wanted to flow. The swarm wanted to flow.

He pulled the pin on the flare and dropped it into the pipe intake.

The reaction was immediate. The flare didn't burn the water. It reacted with the chemical residue left by the previous infestation, the algal bloom of magical decay that the Authority had been ignoring for years. A thick, orange foam erupted from the pipe, solidifying instantly upon contact with the air.

It wasn't fire. It was sealant.

The face in the water recoiled. The smile twisted into a snarl.

"You cannot seal the nest from the inside," the face hissed. "I am the current. I am the flow."

Dale wiped sludge from his forehead. His eyes stung. He reached into his belt pouch and pulled out a small, laminated card. It was his License Card, Level VII, glowing with a faint blue luminescence.

"Field Note 44-C," he continued, his voice steady despite the screaming in his head. "Attempted containment via chemical barrier. Status: Partial Success.

Entity designation: Hydro-Entity, unclassified. Behavior: Defensive.

Note: Entity exhibits knowledge of infrastructure layout.

"

He wasn't just fighting. He was documenting. And the System rewarded documentation.

**FIELD GUIDE UPDATE: HYDRO-ENTITY (UNNAMED).** **WEAKNESS IDENTIFIED: STATIC CONTAINMENT.** **TARGETED DAMAGE BONUS APPLIED TO NEXT ACTION.**

The hive mind shrieked. It didn't like the weakness being identified. The swarm surged forward, breaking the standoff. They clawed at Dale's back, trying to drag him into the reservoir, to feed him to the face.

Dale stumbled. His ribs cracked under the pressure of a thousand insect legs. He grunted, the sound swallowed by the rushing water.

He didn't fight the swarm. He didn't have the strength. He had the knowledge.

He remembered the inspection report from the previous chapter. The one he hadn't finished. The one about the pressure valves in the secondary overflow channel. The one he had marked as

"Low Priority" because it was technically the city's jurisdiction, not his.

The Authority had deducted points for that. Sloppy work.

But now, the loophole was there.

"Command: Redirect," Dale gasped.

He couldn't stop the swarm. He could only change where it went.

He kicked the base of the shut-off valve. Hard. The rusted pipe, stressed by the chemical sealant and the sudden pressure change, sheared off at the joint.

The orange foam barrier shattered.

The water surged. But it didn't go into the municipal supply. It went down.

The broken pipe opened a vent to the sub-basement of the treatment plant below. The swarm, driven by the hive mind's panic, poured into the dark hole.

The face in the reservoir screamed. A sound like a thousand voices crying out in unison.

"No!"

The water churned violently. The face dissolved, unable to maintain cohesion without the flow. The sludge fell away, revealing only black, still water.

Dale fell to his knees on the concrete embankment. The water below was calm.

The hive mind was gone. Or rather, it was trapped. Below him.

A new notification appeared.

**EXAM PHASE: COMMAND.** **STATUS: FAILED.** **REASON: INFRASTRUCTURE DAMAGE EXCEEDS ALLOWABLE THRESHOLD.** **DEDUCTION: -15 TP.** **CERTIFICATION PROGRESS: 42%.**

Dale stared at the numbers. The red text burned into his retinas.

He had saved the reservoir. He had saved the town's water supply.

But he had failed the exam, and he pulled himself up, his body shaking. He looked at the water. It was empty.

The face was gone.

The swarm was below.

But something else was happening.

The concrete beneath his feet vibrated. Not from the water. From the earth.

A new notification, larger than the previous ones, expanded across his vision. It wasn't a warning. It was an invitation.

**NOTIFICATION: AUTHORITY INTERVENTION PROTOCOL 7.** **SUBJECT: UNLICENSED ENTITY, LEVEL IX.** **LOCATION: SUB-BASEMENT, TREATMENT PLANT.** **STATUS: DETECTED.** **ACTION: IMMEDIATE ASSESSMENT REQUIRED.**

Dale's blood ran cold.

Level IX, and he had never seen a Level IX notification. The highest he had dealt with was a broodmother, a Class-4 threat. Level IX was theoretical. It was the end of the line.

He looked at the hole in the pipe. The orange sealant was still hardening, but it was cracking. The swarm was still there, trapped in the dark, but the presence they had sensed... it was still there.

It wasn't the Devourer. It wasn't the face.

It was something that had been waiting in the pipes for longer than the town had existed.

Dale reached for his belt. He found only the empty holster where his flashlight had been. He found a pen. He found a single sheet of graph paper, crumpled and stained with sludge.

He uncrumpled it, and he began to write.

"Field Note 45-A," he said, his voice trembling. "Primary objective: Containment of Hydro-Entity. Secondary objective: Neutralize Level IX threat in sub-basement. Obstacle: Structural collapse, limited resources, compromised licensure status."

He paused. He looked at the dark hole.

He knew he shouldn't go down there. He knew it was suicide. He knew the Authority was testing him, trying to break him, trying to prove that thoroughness was a liability in the face of absolute power.

But he also knew one thing.

The Authority hated waste.

And the Level IX entity, whatever it was, was wasting the infestation.

If Dale went down there, he wouldn't be fighting a monster. He would be conducting a sanitation inspection.

He would be finding the nest, and he would be documenting the violation.

And if he could find the weakness...

He stood up. He picked up a piece of rebar from the debris pile. It was heavy, rusted, and useless as a weapon.

But it was a tool.

Dale walked to the edge of the broken pipe. He looked down. The darkness stared back.

He didn't have a flashlight. He didn't have a clipboard. He had a pen, a piece of paper, and a rusted bar of metal.

He dropped the rebar into the hole.

It clattered down, echoing into the deep, wet dark.

**SYSTEM NOTIFICATION: PROBE DEPLOYED.** **DATA RECEIVED: BIO-SIGNATURE ANOMALY.** **CLASSIFICATION: UNKNOWN.** **RECOMMENDATION: DO NOT ENGAGE.**

Dale ignored the recommendation, and he climbed into the pipe.

The metal groaned under his weight. The sludge coated his boots. He slid down, the darkness swallowing him whole.

Above him, the reservoir remained still. The water was clear. The town slept.

But below, in the dark, the water was rising.

And something was swimming up to meet him.

***

The descent took longer than Dale expected, and the pipe was wider than it looked from the outside, a concrete throat leading down into the earth. The smell hit him first. Not sewage.

Not rot. Something older.

Like wet stone and ozone.

He landed in a pool of shallow water. The surface was black and oily.

He activated his Field Guide, and the interface was glitching. Text flickered, overlapping with static.

**SEARCHING: SUB-BASEMENT INFRASTRUCTURE.** **ERROR: MAP CORRUPTED.** **ATTEMPTING LOCALIZATION...**

Dale scanned the area.

His eyes adjusted to the gloom. He saw pipes, vast and twisting, snaking through the darkness.

He saw valves, corroded and leaking. He saw the swarm, clustered in the corners, their chitinous shells dull in the low light.

They weren't attacking. They were watching.

They were waiting.

Dale moved forward. He kept his hand on the rebar, though he knew it wouldn't help. He was looking for patterns. He was looking for the entry points.

Every infestation had a source. Even Level IX.

He found a grate. It was welded shut, but the metal was thin. He tapped it with the rebar.

*Clang.*

*Clang.*

The sound echoed. And then, a response.

A hum. Low, vibrating through the soles of his boots.

It wasn't a monster. It was a machine.

Dale knelt. He examined the grate.

There were no insects here.

No vermin. This was the infrastructure itself. The pipes, the valves, the concrete... they were all part of the nest.

The Authority hadn't sent him to kill a monster. It had sent him to fix a leak.

**FIELD NOTE 46-A:** **HYPOTHESIS: The Level IX entity is not a biological organism. It is a sentient infrastructure system. The

"Devourer" was a symptom. The "Face" was an interface.** **CORRECTION: The nest is the plant.** **ACTION: Shut down the main power.**

Dale laughed. A dry, humorless sound.

He had been trying to exterminate a bug. But the bug was the building.

He stood up and looked for the breaker box, and he found it on the far wall, half-buried in sludge. The label was faded, but he could read it.

**MAIN PUMP: DO NOT OPERATE.**

He wiped the grime from the switch.

**SYSTEM NOTIFICATION: UNAUTHORIZED ACCESS DETECTED.** **AUTHORITY OVERRIDE REQUIRED.** **WARNING: DISCONNECTING POWER MAY CAUSE CATASTROPHIC FAILURE.**

Dale hesitated.

Catastrophic failure.

If he pulled the switch, the pumps would stop. The water would stop flowing. The swarm would be trapped, but so would the entity.

But if he didn't, the entity would wake up fully. It would take control of the entire grid.

He looked at the swarm. They were moving toward the breaker box. Protecting it.

The hive mind wasn't attacking Dale. It was defending the nest.

And the nest was the Authority.

Dale realized the truth. The Authority wasn't a separate entity. It was a parasitic system, woven into the world's infrastructure, feeding on the chaos of infestations, rewarding those who maintained order so it could harvest the energy of the struggle.

The exams weren't tests. They were training.

They were making him efficient. Making him thorough. Making him a perfect tool for the Authority's endless maintenance cycle.

Dale's hand hovered over the switch.

If he pulled it, he broke the system. He failed the exam. He lost his certification.

If he didn't, he became the Authority's perfect worker, and he thought about the clipboard. The report. The code violations.

He thought about the Thoroughness Rating.

He thought about the deduction.

**-15 TP.**

He smiled.

"Field Note 47-A," he whispered.

He pulled the switch.

The hum stopped. The lights died.

In the total darkness, the water rose.

And from the pipes, something vast and silent flowed toward him.

Dale screamed as the dark water swallowed his voice.
