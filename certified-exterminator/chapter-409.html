Chapter 409: Target Invalid

# Chapter 409

The silence was louder than the light.

Marcus Vane stood untouched. The rift behind him screamed, a jagged tear in the fabric of reality that bled violet static, but the man himself remained solid, breathing, and much alive. Dale lowered his arm. His pen hovered over the clipboard, trembling not from fear, but from the recoil of a rejected command.

*System Error: Target Invalid.* *Reason: Subject lacks verifiable biological markers for Dimensional Conduit classification.*

The text hung in Dale's peripheral vision, pulsing with a sickly amber hue. It wasn't a glitch.

It was a rejection.

The Authority, for all its bureaucratic omnipotence, could not process what it could not classify. Marcus was not a monster. He was a person who happened to be standing in front of a monster.

Dale stepped back. His boots crunched on the gravel of the collapsed parking lot. The air smelled of ozone and burnt hair.

"You missed," Marcus said.

His voice was flat, devoid of the panic that had defined his previous interactions with Dale. He adjusted his cufflinks.

"I told you.

I am not a conduit. I am a shareholder.

"

"The System disagrees." Dale looked at his clipboard. The entry for Marcus was blank.

Not unknown.

Blank.

"The System requires a species tag. You don't have one."

"Because I'm not a species," Marcus replied. He gestured to the rift. "I'm the architect. And you're just the janitor."

Dale felt the instability in his core. The stolen energy from the tunnels was still churning inside him, a volatile cocktail of raw mana that was eating his neural pathways. He could feel the edges of his thoughts fraying.

If he tried to use *Total Annihilation* again, the recoil would liquefy his brain. He had one shot left, and it required a target that the System recognized as a threat.

The rift pulsed. Something was pushing through.

A limb emerged first.

Not a claw, but a hand. Long, multi-jointed, and translucent. The skin rippled like oil on water.

Behind it, a face unfolded, which had too many eyes, and they were blinking in asynchronous patterns.

Dale's Field Guide auto-populated. The entry scrolled rapidly, text blurring until it stabilized.

*Entity: The Auditor.* *Classification: Dimensional Parasite (Type Omega).* *Behavior: Consumes conceptual certainty. Feeds on doubt.* *Weakness: Absolute Procedural Certainty.*

Dale stared at the entry. He had never seen a weakness listed as a state of mind. Most entries listed fire, ice, or blunt force trauma. This was a paperwork problem.

The Auditor stepped out of the rift, which was seven feet tall, wearing a suit that seemed to be made of compressed shadows. It looked at Dale. It looked at Marcus.

It tilted its head, and the sound of cracking bones echoed across the parking lot.

"Your work is sloppy," the Auditor said.

Its voice bypassed Dale's ears and vibrated directly in his molars.

"The infestation is not contained. The host is not removed.

The report is incomplete.

"

Marcus stepped forward.

"It's working. We're merging the dimensions. It's efficient."

"Inefficient," the Auditor corrected. "Messy. Unlicensed. Unapproved."

The Auditor turned its many eyes toward Dale. A beam of white light shot from its forehead, hitting Dale's chest.

He didn't dodge, and he couldn't. The energy didn't burn. It erased.

For a second, Dale forgot his name.

Then, the memory snapped back, harder than before. *Dale Kowalski. Level II Technician. License Number 8940-K.*

The System flashed a red warning. *Cognitive Contamination Detected. Thoroughness Rating dropping.*

Dale gasped, dropping to one knee, and the pain was not physical. It was the sensation of his own identity being questioned.

If he doubted he was a technician, he wasn't one.

If he wasn't one, he had no power. The Auditor was attacking the premise of his authority.

"You don't believe in the rules," the Auditor said. It walked closer. The ground beneath its feet turned to dust. "You only believe in the paperwork."

Dale gritted his teeth. He looked at Marcus.

Marcus was smiling, and the man was enjoying this.

The Auditor was going to erase Dale, and Marcus would inherit the rift.

But the Auditor had made a mistake. It had treated Dale as an individual. It hadn't treated him as a function.

Dale forced himself to stand. His legs shook. He looked down at his clipboard.

The paper was blank. His pen was dry.

He reached into his utility belt, and he bypassed the spray bottles. He bypassed the containment traps. He pulled out a standard-issue OSHA compliance form.

Form 7B: *Notification of Hazardous Material Spill*.

He clicked his pen.

The Auditor paused. It cocked its head.

"What are you doing?"

"Documenting," Dale said. His voice was hoarse.

"This is not a spill. This is an evolution."

"Section 4, Paragraph C," Dale recited. His hands were shaking, but his writing was steady. "Any entity exhibiting non-terrestrial bio-matrix characteristics is classified as a hazardous biological waste. Disposal protocol requires immediate isolation and verification of containment."

He wrote the date. He wrote the location. He wrote the time.

The Auditor shrieked. The sound was a physical blow. *You cannot categorize the inevitable.*

"I can categorize anything," Dale said. "If it exists, it has a code."

He underlined the word *Hazardous*.

*System Alert: Classification Override Active.* *Target: The Auditor.* *Status: Pending Verification.*

The System was listening. But it needed proof. It needed a witness who was not compromised.

Dale looked at Marcus.

"You're a witness."

Marcus laughed.

"I'm the owner. You work for me."

"I work for the Authority," Dale said. "And the Authority requires a signed statement from the property owner confirming the nature of the infestation."

Marcus's smile faded.

"You can't make me sign that. I know what you are. I know you're dying."

"I am asking you to sign it," Dale said. "Or I classify you as an accomplice to the infestation. And the Authority has a specific protocol for accomplices."

Marcus's eyes narrowed. He looked at the Auditor.

The creature was growing, its form expanding, filling the parking lot with shadow, which was preparing to strike the final blow. It was waiting for Dale to break.

Dale held out the pen.

"Sign it," Dale said.

Marcus hesitated. He looked at the rift.

He looked at Dale.

For the first time, he saw the exhaustion in Dale's face.

The sweat. The trembling hand.

And the absolute, terrifying certainty in his eyes.

Marcus took the pen, and he didn't sign his name. He signed a check.

*Marcus Vane. Authorized. Verified.*

The pen slipped from his fingers.

*System Notification: Witness Statement Verified.* *Target Classification Confirmed: Hazardous Biological Waste.* *Protocol: Total Annihilation (Revised).* *Note: Protocol modified to exclude non-infested personnel.*

The light changed. It was no longer violet. It was a blinding, sterile white.

It didn't take Marcus. It didn't take Dale.

It took the Auditor.

The creature didn't scream. It dissolved.

Its shadow-suit unraveled into threads of nothingness.

Its many eyes popped like balloons. The rift didn't close; it collapsed inward, folding space into a singularity the size of a marble.

The marble fell to the ground. It rolled across the asphalt.

It stopped at Dale's feet.

The silence returned.

Dale bent down. He picked up the marble. It was warm. It hummed with residual energy.

*Item Acquired: Dimensional Seed.* *Rarity: Legendary.* *Description: A compressed pocket dimension. Contains the essence of a Type Omega Parasite.* *Warning: Do not ingest. Do not attempt to open without Level IX Certification.*

Dale put the marble in his pocket. He looked at Marcus.

Marcus was slumped against a concrete pillar. He was pale. He looked like he had seen a ghost.

"You..." Marcus whispered. "You actually did it."

"I filed the report," Dale said.

He turned and walked away. His legs felt like water. The energy in his chest was fading, replaced by a cold, hollow ache. The stolen power was gone, burned out in the flash.

He walked to the edge of the parking lot, and the police tape was still up, fluttering in the wind. The other survivors were gone.

The Authority had sent its cleanup crews.

They were already sweeping up the debris, scrubbing the asphalt with acid-neutralizing foam.

Dale didn't look back at Marcus. He didn't look back at the rift.

He opened his clipboard. He flipped to the final page.

*Job Closure Report.* *Client: Vane Industries.* *Complaint: Unsanctioned dimensional breach.* *Action Taken: Isolation, Elimination, Verification.* *Result: Hazard contained.* *Thoroughness Rating: 10.0.*

He signed his name.

*Certification Level IX Unlocked.* *New Title: Senior Exterminator.* *New Responsibility: Rift Monitoring.*

The text faded.

Dale walked to his truck. The engine turned over on the first try.

He sat in the driver's seat, and he didn't start driving.

He sat there, staring at the steering wheel.

His hands were still shaking.

He looked in the rearview mirror. His face was older than it had been an hour ago. There were gray streaks in his hair. A new scar ran across his left cheek, thin and white.

He reached into his pocket. He pulled out the marble.

It was quiet now. The hum had stopped.

He closed his fist around it. He felt the weight of it. The weight of what he had become.

He wasn't just a technician anymore. He was a custodian of the gaps.

He started the truck. He drove out of the parking lot. He didn't go home. He went to the office.

***

Six weeks later, and the rain was falling on the roof of the suburban duplex.

Dale sat at his kitchen table. The table was bare. No clipboard.

No pen.

Across from him sat his daughter, Emily. She was twelve. She was eating a bowl of cereal.

"The leak in the basement is still there," Emily said. She pointed her spoon at the ceiling. "Dad. It's dripping."

Dale didn't move. He looked at the ceiling. There was a water stain. It was shaped like a hand.

"I know," Dale said.

"Can you fix it?"

"No."

Emily frowned.

"Why not?

You fixed everything else.

You fixed the giant beetles. You fixed the shadow man.

"

Dale looked at her. He looked at his hands. They were resting on the table. Still.

"I can't fix that one," Dale said.

"Why?"

"Because it's not a leak."

Emily looked at the ceiling again. The drop of water fell. *Plip.*

It landed in the cereal bowl.

Emily didn't flinch. She ate it.

Dale stood up. He walked to the window.

He looked out at the street, and the neighborhood was quiet.

The lawns were green. The mailboxes were aligned.

But in the corner of his vision, a new notification blinked.

*New Infestation Detected.* *Type: Conceptual Mold.* *Location: Local High School.* *Severity: Low.*

Dale watched the notification for a long time, and he didn't reach for his clipboard.

He reached for the phone.

"Hello," Dale said. "This is Kowalski. I need to file a complaint about a structural anomaly.

It's not rats. It's not bugs.

It's bad ideas.

"

He hung up, and he picked up his keys. He opened the door.

He stepped out into the rain.

He didn't bring the clipboard.
