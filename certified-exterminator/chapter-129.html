Chapter 129: Forging a Level

# Chapter 129

The ink hand grasped Dale's ankle. It was cold, slick, and smelled of ozone and rot.

The bone fingers didn't squeeze; they vibrated, a low-frequency hum that rattled the fillings in his teeth.

Dale didn't look at the hand. He looked at the clipboard.

His pen moved.

*Incident Report: Unauthorized Dimensional Breach. Sector 4.* *Substrate: Liquid Obsidian (Class: Unknown).* *Entry Point: Subterranean. Immediate action required.*

The hand pulled.

Dale's boots skidded on the wet pavement. He dug his heels in, planting his stance with the wide, grounded posture of a man who had spent fourteen years resisting the urge to run from a bad smell.

The System interface remained a void.

No health bar. No stamina meter. Just the blinking cursor and the word **UNLICENSED** pulsing like a neon sign in a dead city.

"Let go," Dale said. His voice was flat. Administrative.

The ink hand tightened. The skin of his ankle went numb.

Above, the sky was bruising purple. The clouds weren't rain clouds; they were sediment.

Heavy, particulate matter hanging low, obscuring the stars.

Marcus was gone, and the alley was empty save for Dale and the thing trying to drag him into the earth.

Dale kicked the wrist of the creature. His boot connected with something hard and jagged. The vibration stopped. The hand froze.

He leaned down. Not to attack. To inspect.

He pulled a penlight from his pocket. Clicked it on. The beam cut through the swirling black mist. He focused on the wrist joint.

*Observation 1: Structural integrity compromised at the carpal junction.* *Observation 2: Material composition resembles hardened resin, not flesh.* *Observation 3: Lack of biological response to trauma.*

The hand twitched. A vein of white light pulsed beneath the black surface.

"Not a monster," Dale muttered. He scribbled furiously. "It's a construct. A filing cabinet with teeth."

The System pinged. Not a notification box. A sound. A dry, paper-rustling noise inside his ear.

**[FIELD GUIDE UPDATE: ENTITY IDENTIFIED]** **[NAME: ARCHIVIST WARDEN]** **[TYPE: ADMINISTRATIVE CONSTRUCT]** **[WEAKNESS: PROTOCOL VIOLATION]**

Dale stopped writing. He stared at the words floating in his vision. The system hadn't given him points.

It hadn't given him a license. It had given him data.

*Weakness: Protocol Violation.*

He looked at the ink hand. He looked at the empty space where his badge had been.

"You're a compliance officer," Dale said. "You're here to shut me down."

The hand surged upward. It wrapped around his calf. The cold spread to his knee.

Dale didn't panic.

Panic was inefficient. He reached into his belt pouch and pulled out a small, sealed envelope. It was blank.

He had prepared it earlier, out of habit, for a job that never came.

A requisition form for industrial solvent, and he held it up.

"I am filing a grievance," Dale said.

The Archivist Warden hesitated. The vibration in the bone fingers faltered.

"Article 4, Section 2 of the Municipal Code," Dale continued, his voice gaining the rhythmic cadence of a man reciting safety regulations from memory. "Any entity acting as a regulatory agent must present valid credentials upon contact. Failure to do so constitutes an illegal search and seizure."

The ink hand tightened, and the pain was sharp now, electric. Dale's leg went numb up to the thigh. He gritted his teeth, forcing his breathing to stay even.

In through the nose. Out through the mouth.

"I have no badge," Dale said. "But you have no jurisdiction."

The System flashed.

**[VIOLATION DETECTED]** **[ERROR: LICENSE STATUS CONFLICT]** **[CALCULATING...]**

The ground beneath Dale shook.

The crack widened.

More hands rose from the sludge. Three.

Four. Dozens. They reached for his knees, his waist, his shoulders.

A forest of black, bone-laced fingers.

Dale didn't fight them. He stood still. He waited for the calculation.

The System was a bureaucracy. It hated errors. It hated ambiguity. And Dale had just created a paradox.

He was an unlicensed civilian performing an extermination. The entities were licensed regulators performing an arrest. But the regulators had no proof of their own licensure in this sector.

The math broke.

The ink hands froze. The white veins in them flickered, then dimmed. The sludge around them receded, pulling back into the earth like a tide going out. The sky above remained dark, but the pressure in the air dropped.

Dale exhaled. His leg throbbed. He could feel the blood returning, pinpricks of sensation.

He didn't run, and he walked backward. Slowly. Carefully.

Watching the receding sludge.

He kept his eyes on the ground, checking for residual contamination.

"Report filed," he whispered.

He turned and walked toward the main street.

The city of Columbus was quiet. Too quiet. The cars were abandoned, doors open, engines cold. The streetlights flickered in a synchronized pattern, like a heartbeat.

Dale walked down the center of the road. His clipboard was tucked under his arm. His pen was still in his hand.

He passed a storefront. *Val's Discount Hardware*. The glass was intact. The sign was lit.

Inside, a figure stood behind the counter.

It wore a gray suit.

Its face was a smooth, featureless oval. No eyes. No mouth.

Just skin the color of wet ash.

Dale stopped. He didn't raise his hand. He didn't reach for a weapon.

He looked at the figure. Then he looked at the register.

"Closed?" Dale asked.

The figure tilted its head. A joint popped. A sound like snapping dry wood.

It raised a hand. Pointed at Dale.

A notification appeared.

**[WARNING: ACTIVE ENFORCEMENT UNIT DETECTED]** **[STATUS: HOSTILE]** **[ACTION: SURRENDER CREDENTIALS OR FACE TERMINATION]**

Dale looked at the message. Then he looked at the figure.

"I don't have credentials," Dale said. "I have a clipboard."

The figure moved.

It didn't walk.

It *glitched*. One frame it was behind the counter, the next it was in front of Dale, three feet away.

The air around it distorted, light bending into sharp angles.

Dale didn't flinch. He opened his clipboard.

He flipped to a new page.

*Inspection: Commercial Premises.* *Violation: Unauthorized Automation.* *Penalty: Immediate Cessation of Operations.*

He wrote the words. Then he held the clipboard up.

The figure paused. The gray skin rippled.

"You are logging a violation," the figure said. Its voice was the sound of a dial-up modem connecting. Screeching, digital.

"I am documenting a non-compliant entity," Dale said. "You are operating without a posted permit. You are engaging in unlicensed detention. And you are failing to maintain a clean work environment."

Dale pointed to the floor.

"There is ink on your counter. Slip hazard. OSHA violation 1910.22(a)."

The figure looked down. It touched the counter.

The System pinged.

**[CROSS-CONTAMINATION ALERT]** **[PROTOCOL MISMATCH: LEGAL VS. PHYSICAL]** **[PENALTY: 40% EFFECTIVE REDUCTION]**

The figure shuddered. The glitching slowed. The distortion around its body wavered.

Dale stepped forward, and he reached into his pocket and pulled out a roll of duct tape. He tore off a strip.

"Stop moving," Dale said.

The figure tried to lunge. But the 40% debuff was real. Its movements were sluggish, heavy. Like moving through water.

Dale wrapped the tape around the figure's wrist. Then the other wrist. Then he taped them together.

It was absurd. It was suicidal. It was pest control.

He taped the figure's torso. Then its legs.

He moved with the speed of a man who had taped up thousands of cockroach traps, hundreds of rodent bait stations.

He didn't see a monster. He saw a pest that needed to be contained until proper disposal.

When he was done, the figure stood rigid. A statue of gray static bound in silver tape.

Dale stepped back. He wiped his hands on his pants.

He looked at the clipboard.

*Job Complete.* *Thoroughness: Pending.*

The sky above the street cracked. A sound like tearing canvas echoed through the empty blocks.

Dale looked up, and the clouds were splitting. Not apart. They were being peeled back.

Behind the clouds was not space. It was a grid. A vast, infinite grid of glowing lines. And on the grid, millions of dots.

Some dots were moving. Some were static.

And some were being deleted.

A text box appeared in the center of Dale's vision. It was large. It covered his entire field of view.

**[SYSTEM UPDATE: AUTHORITY INTERVENTION]** **[LOCAL SECTOR 4: QUARANTINED]** **[NEW OBJECTIVE: SURVIVE THE AUDIT]**

Dale stared at the words.

*The Audit.*

He looked at the taped-up figure. It was fading. Dissolving into pixels.

He looked at the grid in the sky, and he looked at his own hands. Still clean.

He picked up his pen.

He wrote one line.

*Audit Date: Today.*

The ground shook again. But this time, it wasn't the earth. It was the air.

The silence broke.

Screams.

From every direction. From the buildings.

From the cars.

From the sewers.

People. Alive people.

They were running.

But they weren't running from Dale, and they were running from the sky.

A beam of white light struck the building across the street. It didn't burn.

It erased.

The brick, the glass, the sign—gone.

Just smooth, white void where the structure had been.

Dale backed into the hardware store. He closed the door.

He locked it, and he sat on the floor.

He opened his Field Guide.

He had one entry. *Rattus profundus*. Old. Useless.

He needed a new one, and he pulled out a fresh page. He sketched.

Not a rat.

A suit.

A man with no face.

A man who worked for the Authority, and he drew the details. The gray fabric. The static.

The way it moved. The way it obeyed the rules until the rules contradicted each other.

He wrote the weaknesses.

*1. Paradoxical Logic Loops.* *2. Lack of Physical Mass.* *3. Dependence on System Validation.*

He finished the sketch. He labeled it.

**[ENTITIES: THE INSPECTORS]** **[THREAT LEVEL: ADMINISTRATIVE]** **[COUNTER-PROTOCOL: MALICIOUS COMPLIANCE]**

The door to the hardware store rattled.

Dale didn't look up. He kept writing.

"Go away," he said.

The door opened.

Marcus stood there, and he was soaked. His hair was plastered to his forehead. He held a crowbar.

His eyes were wide, terrified, but sharp.

"Dale," Marcus said. "We have to move. The sky… it's deleting things."

Dale finished the sketch. He tore out the page.

He stood up. He handed the page to Marcus.

"Read this," Dale said. "Memorize it."

Marcus looked at the paper.

"What is this?"

"Field Guide entry," Dale said. "For the things in the suits. They're not monsters, Marcus. They're bureaucrats."

Marcus looked at Dale.

"You're insane."

"Possibly," Dale said. "But they can't fire me. I'm already unemployed."

He walked past Marcus. Out into the street.

The white light was closer now. It was sweeping down the avenue. Erasing block by block.

Dale walked toward it.

Marcus grabbed his arm.

"Are you crazy? It's erasing you!"

Dale shook him off.

"I have a job to do," Dale said.

He pulled out his pen. He pointed it at the white void.

"Open up," Dale said.

The void stopped.

It hovered.

A voice echoed from the light. It wasn't loud. It was precise.

**[STATE YOUR PURPOSE.]**

Dale took a breath.

"I am here to file a complaint," Dale said. "About the quality of your extermination services."

The light pulsed.

**[COMPLAINT REGISTERED.]** **[WAITING FOR EVIDENCE.]**

Dale held up his clipboard.

"I have fourteen years of evidence," he said. "And I'm ready to present it."

The light dimmed. The erasure stopped.

But the grid in the sky shifted. The dots moved. They formed a shape. A face.

The face of the Authority.

**[EVIDENCE REQUIRED.]** **[SUBMIT: CERTIFICATION LEVEL 10.]**

Dale froze.

Level 10.

There was no Level 10. The system went up to 9.

He looked at Marcus. Marcus looked at Dale.

"You don't have a Level 10," Marcus whispered.

Dale looked at the clipboard. He looked at the pen.

He looked at the void.

"No," Dale said. "I don't."

He smiled.

"But I can fake it."

He turned to the void. He wrote.

Not a report.

A forgery.

The ink on the page glowed.
