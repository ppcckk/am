Chapter 167: Too Slow Technician

# Chapter 167

The pen was gone.

Dale stared at the asphalt where it had rolled. Nothing but a dark stain and the smell of ozone.

Without a writing instrument, he couldn't document the violation.

Without documentation, there were no Treatment Points. Without points, the Authority would flag him for abandonment.

The black puddle widened. It pulsed like a bruise under skin.

Twenty-nine days.

The clock in his peripheral vision ticked down. **[TIME TO VIOLATION: 28 DAYS, 23 HOURS, 59 MINUTES.]**

Marla grabbed his arm. Her fingers dug in hard enough to bruise. "Dale. Move."

He didn't move.

He scanned the street. No neighbors.

No witnesses.

The streetlights flickered, buzzing with an erratic rhythm that matched the heartbeat of the thing on the ground.

"We need a witness," Dale said. His voice was flat. OSHA compliant.

"She's gone, Dale!" Marla shouted. "The Inspector is gone!"

"Not gone. Unlicensed." Dale pointed at the empty space where the briefcase had been.

"The Authority deletes unauthorized agents.

But it doesn't delete violations.

The violation is still here."

He looked at the face in the sludge. It was gone, dissolved back into the mass, but the door remained. A rectangular seam in the pavement, glowing with a sickly purple light.

Dale walked toward it.

"Don't," Marla whispered.

Dale ignored her. He reached into his cargo pants. His pockets were empty.

No clipboard. No pen.

No flashlight.

The black liquid had taken his tools. It had taken the Inspector.

He crouched.

The smell hit him first. Not rot.

Not decay.

It smelled like old ink and copper wire. The scent of a ledger that had been thrown away.

He pressed his palm against the pavement near the door. The heat radiated through his gloves.

*Protocol check,* he thought. *Identify. Document. Isolate.*

He couldn't identify. The species was unknown.

He couldn't document. No pen.

He could isolate.

Dale stood up. He turned to Marla. "Do you have a marker?"

Marla blinked. "What?"

"A marker. Permanent. Sharpie. Anything that leaves a trace."

She patted her pockets. Then she reached into her jacket. She pulled out a small, silver tube. A lipstick.

"Will this work?" she asked.

Dale took it. He uncapped it. The smell of wax and synthetic red dye rose up.

"Better than nothing," Dale said.

He knelt again. He drew a circle around the door.

Thick, waxy red lines.

The pavement hissed where the wax touched it.

Steam rose from the ground.

The black liquid recoiled.

It shuddered. The door tried to widen, but the red circle held it back.

**[CONTAINMENT PROTOCOL INITIATED. UNORTHODOX.]**

Dale ignored the notification. He focused on the line. He had to close the loop.

If the circle broke, the infestation would spread into the adjacent yards. The Thoroughness Rating would drop to zero.

He moved clockwise. The lipstick smeared unevenly. He applied more pressure. The wax melted into the asphalt, bonding with the stone.

"Keep talking," Dale said.

"About what?" Marla asked. Her voice shook.

"Anything.

Distract it. The creature responds to attention. If you ignore it, it grows.

If you acknowledge it, it stabilizes."

Marla looked at the door. "Why does it have your name on it?"

"Because I haven't filed the report yet."

"That makes no sense."

"It makes perfect sense if you view the Rift as a backlog of pending inspections. I am the inspector. The door is the open ticket. Until I close it, it remains in my queue."

The black liquid surged again. It pushed against the red line. The lipstick wax cracked.

Dale bit his thumb. Hard.

He squeezed blood onto the crack in the line.

The red mixed with the red. The barrier held.

**[BIOMETRIC AUTHENTICATION DETECTED. PENALTY WAIVED FOR TEMPORARY CONTAINMENT.]**

Dale wiped his thumb on his pants. He stood up, breathing hard. His hands trembled, but he kept them steady.

"It's not enough," Marla said. "You can't keep it there forever."

"No," Dale agreed. "I need to file the report. I need the Authority to see the violation and assign it a code."

"But you don't have a pen."

"I have a Field Guide."

Dale pulled the book from his back pocket. It was thick, heavy, bound in synthetic leather. He flipped to page 142.

The page was still blank.

Except for the sentence he hadn't written.

*I wrote this.*

Dale stared at the words. His handwriting. Perfectly formed. The loop of the 'd' matched his signature.

He had never written it.

The face in the sludge reappeared. Just a mouth this time. A jagged tear in the oil.

"Tick tock, Dale," the mouth gurgled. "You have twenty-nine days to write the entry. If you don't, the entry writes itself. And once it's written, you become part of the infestation."

Dale looked at the mouth. He looked at the blank page.

He understood the loophole.

The System rewarded documentation. But it also punished incomplete entries.

If an entry remained blank, it was vulnerable.

Vulnerable entries attracted parasitic entities. This thing wasn't a monster. It was a bureaucratic parasite.

It fed on incomplete paperwork.

To kill it, Dale had to complete the entry.

But he didn't know what to write.

He didn't know the species. He didn't know the weakness. He didn't know the protocol.

And he had no pen.

"Marla," Dale said. "Give me your phone."

Marla handed it over. "What for?"

"I'm going to dictate the entry."

"That's not how the System works, Dale. You have to *write* it. Physical inscription."

"The System accepts digital logs if they are certified by a witness," Dale said. He dialed 911.

Marla went pale. "You're calling the police?"

"I'm calling the only licensed witnesses left in the county. The police are Class II Certified in Civil Disturbance. They have authority to document crimes."

"You're insane."

"I'm thorough."

Dale spoke into the phone.

"Dispatch, this is Dale Kowalski, License Number 44-B-9.

I am reporting an active dimensional breach at 42 Oak Street. Class-1 Vermin.

Aggressive. Contained by temporary wax barrier. Requesting immediate witness presence for citation."

The dispatcher's voice crackled. "Sir, we have no record of that license number. Hang up."

Dale hung up.

The phone died. The battery drained instantly. The screen went black.

The face in the sludge laughed. It was a wet, sloppy sound.

"Nice try, Dale. But the Authority controls the network.

The police aren't witnesses.

They're civilians. Civilians don't count."

Dale looked at Marla. "Do you have a license?"

Marla shook her head. "No. I'm a teacher."

Dale cursed. A short, sharp sound.

He looked at the door. The red line was holding, but barely. The black liquid was eating the wax. It was learning the shape of the barrier.

He had to write the entry. Now.

He flipped through the Field Guide. Page 141 was empty too. Page 140 had an entry for *Rattus profundus*. Page 139 for *Acid Beetles*.

Page 142 was a gap in his knowledge. A blind spot.

The creature knew his blind spots. It had targeted him because he was thorough. Thoroughness meant checking every page. And he had missed one.

"Marla," Dale said. "Read page 141."

"What?"

"Read it. Aloud."

Marla looked at the book. She didn't want to touch it. The book felt cold.

"It says *Rattus profundus*," she whispered. "Weakness: exposed underbelly. Vulnerable during molting."

"Good. Now read page 139."

"Acid Beetles. Weakness: thorax separation. Prone to self-immolation when stressed."

Dale closed his eyes. He listened.

The System recorded audio. Not as a direct entry, but as a verification. If he could get the System to accept an audio transcript as a draft entry, he could buy time.

He needed a loophole.

*Protocol: Cross-Contamination.*

The rules stated that deploying a protocol for one creature against another inflicted a 40% debuff. But it also contaminated the Field Guide entry for 24 hours.

Contamination meant the entry became invalid.

Invalid meant it could be rewritten.

Dale opened his eyes. He looked at the black door.

"I need to contaminate page 142," Dale said.

"Contaminate it?" Marla asked.

"I need to apply a protocol for a different species to this creature. It will hurt me. But it will mark the entry as 'Corrupted.' Corrupted entries can be overwritten by new data."

"That's suicide, Dale."

"It's a filing error. And I know how to fix filing errors."

Dale walked toward the door.

The black liquid surged. It lashed out like a whip.

Dale dodged.

He wasn't fast. He was methodical.

He moved in steps, counting them.

One step left. Two steps back. Three steps forward.

He pulled a small bottle from his pocket. It was empty. He had used the last of the insecticide in the crawlspace.

But he had something else.

He reached into his other pocket. He pulled out a tube of toothpaste.

The Inspector had confiscated his tools, but not his hygiene kit.

Dale squeezed the toothpaste onto his finger. White paste. Abrasive.

He smeared it on his palm.

The black liquid watched him. The face formed again. A nose. Eyes.

"What are you doing, Dale?"

"Applying a Class-4 Mollusk Protocol," Dale said.

The face tilted. Confused.

Mollusks were soft-bodied. They disliked abrasives. They disliked salt. They disliked calcium.

This wasn't a mollusk. This was a dimensional void.

But the System didn't care about reality. It cared about classification.

If Dale treated it as a mollusk, the System would register a protocol mismatch.

He lunged.

He slapped his paste-covered hand onto the black sludge.

The pain was immediate. Fire in his nerves. The black liquid burned through his glove, through his skin, into his muscle.

Dale didn't scream. He gritted his teeth. He pressed harder.

**[PROTOCOL MISMATCH DETECTED. CROSS-CONTAMINATION WARNING.]**

**[ENTRY 142 CORRUPTED.]**

The black liquid shrieked. It pulled back. The door shuddered.

The red wax line cracked completely.

But the black mass didn't spread. It recoiled.

It was confused.

It had been treated.

It had been identified as a wrong species.

For a moment, the System was confused.

And in that moment of confusion, Dale saw the gap.

The page in his Field Guide was no longer blank.

It was static. Gray noise. A corrupted file.

Dale fell to his knees. His hand was blistered. The pain was throbbing, a deep, rhythmic ache.

Marla ran to him. "Dale! Your hand!"

"I'm fine," Dale said. He didn't look at his hand. He looked at the book.

The gray noise on page 142 was settling. Words were forming. Not in his handwriting. Not in the creature's voice.

In the font of the Authority.

**[SPECIES: VOID TICKET.]** **[CLASS: ADMINISTATIVE HAZARD.]** **[WEAKNESS: FINAL SIGNATURE.]**

Dale stared at the words.

*Final Signature.*

He needed to sign the report.

He had no pen.

He looked at his burned hand. The skin was raw. Red. Angry.

He couldn't sign.

The black liquid rose again. It was adapting. It had learned the abrasive. It was hardening.

"It's resetting," Marla said. "It's going to break through."

Dale looked at the door. The nameplate was glowing. *DALE KOWALSKI.*

He needed a signature.

He looked at Marla.

"Give me your phone," Dale said.

"It's dead."

"I don't need it to call. I need it to scan."

Dale pulled out his own phone. It was dead too. The battery was drained by the proximity to the rift.

He looked at the pavement. The black liquid was forming a new face. It was smiling.

"You can't write it, Dale," the face said. "You're out of time."

Dale looked at his burned hand. He looked at the red lipstick on the pavement.

He picked up the lipstick.

He walked to the door.

The black liquid surged. It wrapped around his legs.

Dale didn't struggle. He stood still.

He raised the lipstick to the nameplate on the door.

He didn't sign his name.

He signed the date.

He wrote the current date in red wax on the glowing name.

The door froze.

The black liquid stopped moving.

**[DATE STAMP APPLIED. REPORT PENDING.]**

**[WITNESS REQUIRED FOR FINAL VERIFICATION.]**

Dale looked up.

The Inspector was back.

Not the one who had vanished.

A different one.

He stood on the roof of the duplex. He wore a gray suit. His face was a blur of static.

He held a pen.

**[VERIFICATION IN PROGRESS.]**

The Inspector stepped off the roof. He didn't fall. He floated.

He landed in front of Dale.

The black liquid hissed. It shrank back.

The Inspector raised the pen.

He looked at Dale.

"You contaminated the file," the Inspector said. His voice sounded like grinding stones.

"Yes," Dale said.

"That is a violation."

"It's a fix."

The Inspector tilted his head.

"Fix it," the Inspector said. "Sign the entry. Or I delete you."

Dale looked at the pen.

It was real. It was solid.

But it wasn't his pen.

If he used the Inspector's pen, the report would be signed by the Inspector.

The violation would be closed.

But Dale wouldn't get the points.

He wouldn't get the credit.

And the Authority would know he didn't solve it alone.

If he didn't sign, the black liquid would consume him.

Dale looked at the pen. He looked at the door.

He reached for the pen.

The Inspector pulled it back.

"Too slow, Technician."

The black liquid erupted. It covered Dale's vision.

The last thing Dale saw was the pen, hovering in the air, waiting for a hand that wasn't there.
