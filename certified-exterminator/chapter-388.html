Chapter 388: Certified Paperwork

# Chapter 388

The rain did not fall; it assaulted the pavement, a relentless grey curtain that turned the suburban street into a slick mirror of neon and misery.

Inspector Halloway pressed his forehead against the cold glass of his sedan. Condensation bloomed under his palm. He wiped it away with a sleeve stained by coffee rings and cheap polyester. Through the distorted window, he watched the man in the high-visibility vest stand alone in the downpour.

The man was not running. He was not screaming. He was holding a clipboard.

Halloway checked his watch. The Authority's response time for a Class-IV dimensional breach was supposed to be three minutes.

It had been four.

The man in the vest had not moved from the epicenter of the tear. Instead, he had drawn a chalk circle around the rift, measured the ambient humidity with a hygrometer clipped to his belt, and written something on his form.

Halloway tapped the dashboard.

"Unit Seven, this is Dispatch. Why hasn't the containment team arrived?"

Static crackled in the earpiece. Then, a voice, tinny and distant.

"Sir, we're... we're waiting on clearance. The local tech is still filing paperwork."

Halloway slammed his hand against the steering wheel. The vibration rattled his teeth.

Paperwork.

In the middle of a reality collapse, and he grabbed his radio again.

"Identify the technician."

"Dale Kowalski. Class II. He's... he's arguing with the Appeals Rep."

Halloway opened the car door.

The rain hit him like buckshot. He didn't care.

He stepped out, his shoes sinking into the mud that had formed near the gutter.

He marched toward the rift, his badge swinging against his chest like a pendulum.

He wanted to fire Dale Kowalski on the spot. He wanted to bury him in a mountain of forms so high that the man would never see the sky again.

But then Halloway saw the jars, and they were lined up on the invisible shelf beyond the tear, glowing with a soft, bioluminescent hum. Thousands of them. Each one contained a swirling mass of grey fog, trapped and preserved.

And standing between the jars and the rain was the man in the grey suit. The Appeals Rep. He held a pen like a scalpel.

Halloway stopped ten feet away. The air smelled of ozone and wet dog.

"Stand down," Halloway shouted, his voice cracking. "This is a Level-9 breach. You don't have jurisdiction."

The Appeals Rep didn't look up. He made a checkmark on his clipboard.

"Jurisdiction is irrelevant when the subject is actively violating the Code of Conduct, Inspector. Specifically, Section 4, Paragraph B: *Unsanctioned Interaction with Dimensional Assets.*"

Halloway turned his glare on Dale.

Dale was crouching in the mud. He had his Field Guide open.

His gloved hands were steady, and he wasn't looking at the Appeals Rep.

He wasn't looking at Halloway. He was looking at the nearest jar.

"Kowalski," Halloway said, stepping closer. The mud sucked at his boots. "Close the book.

Walk away. Now.

"

Dale didn't move.

"Mr.

Kowalski," the Appeals Rep said, his tone smooth as silk. "Your certification is suspended. However, the Authority recognizes a unique proficiency.

You have identified a nesting behavior in the Auditors that our standard protocols missed.

"

Halloway looked at the jar Dale was examining. Inside, the fog was moving.

Not randomly, which was pulsing. A rhythm.

"What are you doing?" Halloway asked, his voice dropping.

Dale pointed a pen at the glass.

"Look at the resonance frequency."

"It's a containment unit. It holds things."

"It's a *breeding* unit," Dale said. His voice was flat, devoid of fear.

"See the condensation patterns?

They're not from the rain.

They're metabolic exhaust. The creature inside is alive.

It's breathing. And it's listening.

"

Halloway felt a cold prickle at the base of his neck. He looked closer.

The fog in the jar shifted, forming a vague shape.

An eye. Then a mouth. Then it dissipated.

"That's impossible," Halloway whispered. "Auditors are non-corporeal constructs. They don't have biology."

"Dale Kowalski," the Appeals Rep said, smiling that terrible, thin smile. "Explain."

Dale stood up, and he wiped mud from his knee, then clicked his pen.

"You're wrong about the classification.

You treat them as energy entities.

That's why your seals fail. They aren't energy. They're parasites.

They feed on belief. On fear.

That's why they're in jars.

Not to contain them.

To *farm* them.

"

The Appeals Rep's smile vanished. The rain seemed to pause, hanging in the air around them.

"Accusations of Authority misconduct are serious," the Rep said.

"I'm not accusing," Dale said. He pulled a small vial from his belt. It wasn't a weapon.

It was a standard EPA-approved sample container.

"I'm documenting.

And I'm correcting the Field Guide entry.

"

A chime sounded in Dale's peripheral vision. Halloway couldn't see it, but he saw Dale's eyes narrow.

**FIELD GUIDE UPDATED.** **SPECIES: AUDITOR (SUB-TYPE: Symbiotic Parasite).** **WEAKNESS: Cognitive Dissonance.** **NEW PROTOCOL UNLOCKED: *Causal Denial*.**

Halloway blinked.

"What did you just do?"

Dale held up the vial.

"I'm going to open a jar."

"No," Halloway said. He reached for his stun baton. "You are under arrest for unauthorized experimental procedures."

"You can't arrest me for doing my job," Dale said. He turned to the Appeals Rep. "Do you want to exterminate them, or do you want to keep collecting jars?

Because if you keep collecting, they're going to hatch.

And when they hatch, they won't just eat your fear, Inspector. They'll eat the concept of *order*.

And then, who will write the reports?

"

The Appeals Rep stared at Dale. His glasses fogged. For a second, the accountant looked human. Terrified.

"If you break the seal," the Rep whispered, "the Thoroughness Rating is irrelevant. The penalty is total erasure."

Dale looked at Halloway.

"Inspector, check your clipboard. Do you have a violation code for *Conceptual Infestation*?"

Halloway looked down at his own hands. He was holding his ticket pad. He hadn't picked it up since the car. But it was there.

And on the top sheet, written in a handwriting that was not his own, was a new code: **V-99: THE ABSTRACTION OF REALITY.**

Halloway's breath hitched.

"How?" Halloway asked.

"Because the job is already done," Dale said. "You just haven't signed it yet."

Dale uncapped the vial, and he didn't throw it. He didn't shoot it. He poured the liquid directly onto the base of the nearest jar.

The liquid was clear. Water? No. It shimmered, like oil on water.

The Appeals Rep lunged forward.

"Stop him!"

Halloway didn't stop him. He couldn't. His legs were locked.

The mud had become quicksand. He was sinking.

The liquid hit the jar.

Nothing happened for three seconds.

Then, the jar cracked.

It didn't shatter. It *unraveled*. The glass turned into grey dust, then into mist, then into nothing.

The fog inside the jar spilled out. It didn't fly. It crawled. It slithered across the wet pavement, moving with the grace of a centipede.

Halloway scrambled back, his hand slipping on the slime.

The creature rose up, which had no face. It had a shape.

A vague, humanoid silhouette made of static and shadow. It hovered between them.

The Appeals Rep pointed at Dale.

"You violated containment! You are hereby terminated!"

The Rep raised his pen. A bolt of black lightning struck Dale.

Dale didn't move. He didn't dodge. He held up his clipboard.

The lightning hit the clipboard.

And stopped.

The light fractured, splintering into harmless sparks that skittered across the wet asphalt.

**PROTOCOL ACTIVATED: CAUSAL DENIAL.** **EFFECT: The Authority's action is deemed 'Invalid' due to procedural error.** **DAMAGE REFLECTED.**

The black lightning bounced off the clipboard and struck the Appeals Rep.

The Rep screamed. It wasn't a human scream.

It was the sound of paper tearing.

His suit shredded, not at the seams, but from the inside out. Threads of grey fiber floated into the air.

Halloway stared. The system... it worked?

Dale walked forward. The parasite-creature hissed, its form flickering.

"Name your species," Dale said.

His voice was calm. Clinical.

"Or identify your nesting habits.

I need data for the next entry.

"

The creature lunged at him.

Dale sidestepped. Not with combat grace, but with the practiced shuffle of a man dodging a spill. He reached into his belt and pulled out a canister.

It wasn't insecticide. It was a canister of *Epistemic Solvent*. A new item. One he had crafted in the last ten minutes while arguing with the Rep.

"Target acquired," Dale muttered.

He sprayed the canister directly into the center of the static.

The solvent didn't burn. It *edited*.

Where the mist touched the creature, the shape lost its coherence. The static smoothed out.

The vague humanoid form flattened into two dimensions.

It became a sketch.

A drawing of a monster on wet concrete.

Then, it faded.

The rain washed it away.

Silence returned to the street.

Halloway fell to his knees in the mud. He gasped for air, his lungs burning. He looked at the Appeals Rep.

The man was gone. In his place was a pile of grey dust and a single, pristine clipboard.

Dale walked over to the pile. He picked up the clipboard. He inspected it.

**APPEALS BOARD OFFICER: TERMINATED.** **REASON: PROCEDURAL INVALIDITY.** **POINT ADJUSTMENT: +50.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 10.0.**

Dale looked at Halloway.

"Inspector," Dale said. "Your shoe lace is untied. You're going to trip."

Halloway looked down. His lace was, indeed, undone.

He tied it with shaking hands. He stood up. He felt heavy. Old.

"You broke the law," Halloway said. His voice was quiet.

"I completed a job," Dale corrected. "The law requires identification before elimination. I identified the Auditors as parasitic entities dependent on bureaucratic validation.

By denying their existence through *Causal Denial*, I removed their food source. They starved.

Simple."

Halloway looked at the rift. It was still open. The shelves of jars were still there.

But now, many of the jars were empty.

The dust of the Appeals Rep had settled into the gaps between them.

"The Authority will come for you," Halloway said.

"The Authority is a bureaucracy," Dale said. "Bureaucracies don't punish mistakes. They punish *incomplete files*. And my file is now the most complete in the district."

Dale turned and walked toward his truck. He climbed in. He didn't start the engine. He just sat there, writing in his notebook.

Halloway approached the truck. He knocked on the window.

Dale rolled it down an inch.

"You can't leave," Halloway said.

"There are still thousands of jars. Thousands of parasites. You killed one.

They'll send more.

"

Dale looked up.

His eyes were bloodshot.

Dark circles underlined them. He looked exhausted.

Not from battle, but from the sheer weight of the paperwork he had mentally processed.

"I know," Dale said.

"What do we do?"

Dale pointed to the clipboard in Halloway's hand. The one with the new violation code.

"File the report," Dale said. "Use code V-99. Cite me as the primary technician. And Inspector?"

"What?"

"Make sure you get my signature on the witness statement. I need it for my liability waiver."

Halloway stared at him. Then, slowly, he raised his pen. He wrote.

**VIOLATION CODE: V-99.** **SUBJECT: THE ABSTRACTION OF REALITY.** **TECHNICIAN: DALE KOWALSKI.** **STATUS: PENDING REVIEW.**

As Halloway wrote the last word, the ground beneath his feet rumbled.

Not an earthquake.

A page turning.

The sky above the street flickered. The rain stopped mid-air. The clouds rearranged themselves into neat, orderly rows. Like text.

Dale stepped out of the truck. He looked up at the sky.

"It's starting," he said.

"What is?"

"The Revision."

The air above them split open again. But this time, it wasn't a tear.

It was a page.

A giant, translucent sheet of paper descended from the heavens. It covered the street, the houses, the trees. It was blank.

And in the center of the page, a single word formed. Written in ink that looked like blood.

**ERROR.**

Dale looked at Halloway.

"Run."

Halloway looked at the word. He looked at Dale.

"Where do we run to?" Halloway asked. "The whole world is the page."

Dale pulled out his spray can. It was empty.

He pulled out another one. Also empty.

He looked at Halloway.

"Inspector," Dale said. "Do you have any spare ink?"

Halloway reached into his pocket. He pulled out a pen. It was the same pen the Appeals Rep had held. It was still wet with black fluid.

Halloway handed it to Dale.

Dale uncapped the pen, and he walked toward the descending page.

"Stay here," Dale said.

Dale approached the giant letter **R** in **ERROR**.

He raised the pen.

He drew a line through it.

The ground shook. The rain fell again, but now it was thick. Viscous. Black.

And from the black rain, shapes formed.

Not monsters.

Forms.

Paper people, and they rose from the mud, folding themselves into humanoid shapes. They had no faces. Just smooth, blank surfaces.

They looked at Dale.

Dale looked at them.

He clicked his pen.

**FIELD GUIDE UPDATED.** **SPECIES: THE REVIEWERS.** **WEAKNESS: UNKNOWN.** **PROTOCOL REQUIRED: Level IX CLEARANCE.**

Dale's hand trembled.

For the first time in fourteen years, Dale Kowalski did not know the protocol.

The paper people took a step forward.

Dale stepped back.

And behind him, Halloway heard his radio crackle.

"Unit Seven, this is Dispatch. We have a Class-X event.

Code: *Retcon*.

All units, cease operations. This is not a drill. This is a rewrite.

"

Halloway looked at Dale.

"Dale," he shouted. "Stop writing! Stop fixing it! You're making it worse!"

Dale didn't hear him. He was staring at the Reviewers.

One of them raised a hand. Its fingers were made of folded corners.

It pointed at Dale.

And Dale realized, with a cold, sinking horror, that the creature wasn't attacking him, which was waiting for his signature.

The Reviewers didn't want to kill him.

They wanted to *approve* him.

And approval, in the Authority's system, meant erasure.

Dale looked at the pen in his hand. The ink was glowing now.

He had a choice.

Submit to the review and vanish, leaving the world rewritten by beings who understood nothing but structure.

Or refuse, and be branded a rogue element, hunted by every agency in the dimensional spectrum.

Dale uncapped the pen, and he looked at Halloway.

"Cover me," Dale said.

"Cover you with what?" Halloway yelled. "I don't have a gun! I have a ticket pad!"

Dale smiled. It was a grim, tight expression.

"Then start writing," Dale said. "Write them out of existence. Use the Thoroughness Scale. Make them *sloppy*."

Halloway froze.

"You want me to... botch the report?"

"I want you to make the Authority doubt its own data," Dale said. He turned back to the Reviewers. "If the data is inconsistent, the system crashes. And if it crashes, we get a reboot."

Halloway looked at his ticket pad, and he looked at the paper people.

He looked at Dale, who was now holding the pen out like a sword, the ink dripping onto the wet pavement.

Halloway took a deep breath.

He clicked his pen.

And for the first time in his career, Inspector Halloway lied.

The paper people stopped moving.
