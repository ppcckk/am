Chapter 204: Class IX Question Mark

# Chapter 204

The book's pages didn't just scream. They vibrated with a frequency that made Dale's teeth ache.

He didn't look at the face emerging from the torn tarp. He looked at the hand.

Black. Slick. The fingers were too long, the joints bending in directions human anatomy did not support. The skin of the book binding pulsed with a wet, rhythmic throb.

*CRITICAL ERROR: PRIMARY INFESTATION IS A SYMPTOM.* *SECONDARY INFESTATION DETECTED: ARCHIVAL PARASITE.* *CLASS: IX.*

Class IX.

Dale's left hand shook. He clamped it against his thigh, forcing the tremor to stop.

In fourteen years of pest control, he had never seen a Class IX.

He had never even read about them in the public manuals. The Authority's Field Guide, when he had first unlocked it, had shown a single silhouette for Class IX: a question mark.

The hand reached for him.

Dale stepped back, boots scraping against the concrete floor. His flashlight beam swung wildly, catching the dust motes that danced in the stagnant air. The Construct lay dead in the corner, a heap of pale, chitinous limbs. The dead mites were a carpet on the floor, crunching under his weight.

The hand stopped.

It hovered, trembling. The screaming pages flapped faster, the sound rising to a deafening whine.

Dale didn't move. He kept his clipboard raised.

"Identify," he muttered. The word was automatic. A reflex deeper than fear.

He squinted at the hand. The ink wasn't just on the skin. It was *in* the skin. Veins of black fluid pumped visibly through the translucent flesh.

He opened his Field Guide. The interface flickered, glitching violently.

*ACCESSING ENTRY: ARCHIVAL PARASITE (GENUS: MEMORIA).* *STATUS: UNKNOWN.* *REQUIREMENTS: INSUFFICIENT DATA.*

"Insufficient data," Dale said. His voice sounded thin in the basement. "Of course."

The hand lunged again.

Dale didn't dodge this time. He didn't need to. The hand stopped mid-air, inches from his face.

The screaming stopped.

Silence rushed into the room, heavy and suffocating.

From the hole in the tarp, a voice emerged. It was dry, raspy, like sandpaper on wood.

"Name."

Dale blinked. The hand remained frozen, hovering. The book held open by invisible force.

"Name," the voice repeated.

Dale swallowed. He looked at the notification still burning in his peripheral vision. *CLASS: IX.*

"Dale Kowalski," he said.

The hand lowered slowly. The book snapped shut. The ink on the fingers receded, retreating into the pores of the blackened skin.

A figure pulled itself up from the hole.

It was humanoid, but wrong. The proportions were stretched.

The limbs were too long.

The face was a mask of smooth, featureless obsidian, save for two hollow sockets where eyes should be.

The Archival Parasite stood in the center of the room. It towered over Dale by three feet.

*SYSTEM NOTIFICATION:* *INTERACTION LOGGED: NON-COMBAT CONTACT.* *THREAT LEVEL: DORMANT.* *PROTOCOL UNLOCKED: BUREAUCRATIC NEGOTIATION (LEVEL VI REQUIREMENT).*

Dale stared at the text. *Level VI requirement?* He was Level IV. He had just cleared a Class IV infestation. He had not earned Level VI privileges.

The error message flashed again. *ERROR: AUTHORITY OVERRIDE DETECTED. LOCAL ADMINISTRATOR PRIVILEGES GRANTED TEMPORARILY DUE TO CLASS IX CONTACT.*

The Parasite tilted its head.

"You have a clipboard," the Parasite said. The voice came from everywhere and nowhere. It sounded like pages turning.

"Standard issue," Dale said. He kept his voice flat.

Dry.

"I don't do verbal reports.

I do written ones."

The Parasite laughed. It was a horrible sound, a cascade of rustling paper.

"You are small," the Parasite said. "And your protocols are small. But your documentation is... acceptable."

"Acceptable," Dale repeated. He uncapped his pen. "I need a permit."

The Parasite paused. The obsidian face remained blank, but the air around it seemed to ripple.

"A permit," the Parasite said.

"Yes. I'm currently operating in a restricted zone. This is a Class IX entity.

That requires a Class VII Supervisor's signature, or a temporary variance granted by the Local Authority. I don't have the variance.

I need it now, or I have to shut down the operation."

The Parasite stepped forward. The floorboards didn't creak; they *aged*. Brown rot spread from its feet, eating the wood in seconds.

"You are threatening me with paperwork," the Parasite said.

"I'm threatening you with a code violation," Dale corrected. "Section 4.2, Subsection C: Unauthorized Interaction with Sentient Infestations. If I don't have the variance, I can't engage.

And if I can't engage, I can't exterminate.

And if I can't exterminate, this infestation becomes permanent. And you know what happens to permanent infestations."

The Parasite's head tilted the other way. "They are consumed."

"Correct. You are consumed. Your narrative is erased. You cease to exist."

The silence stretched. The dust motes stopped moving.

Dale's heart hammered against his ribs, but his hand was steady. He wrote on the clipboard.

*DATE: OCT 14.* *SUBJECT: ARCHIVAL PARASITE (CLASS IX).* *ISSUE: MISSING VARIANCE FORM 12-B.* *ACTION REQUIRED: IMMEDIATE AUTHORIZATION FOR TREATMENT PROTOCOL OMEGA-9.*

He held up the clipboard.

"Sign it," Dale said.

"Or I walk. And I leave the breach open. The next technician who comes here won't have a clipboard.

They'll have a shotgun. And they won't know you're sentient.

They'll just spray you with poison."

The Parasite stared at the white paper. The black ink on its fingers shifted, forming shapes. Letters. Numbers.

It reached out. Not with the hand. With a tendril of smoke that poured from its mouth.

The smoke touched the clipboard.

Dale flinched, expecting pain. The smell of old libraries hit him—vanilla, lignin, decay.

The smoke solidified on the paper.

A signature. It wasn't a name.

It was a symbol. A spiral that hurt to look at.

*NOTIFICATION:* *VARIANCE GRANTED.* *PROTOCOL OMEGA-9 UNLOCKED.* *WARNING: OMEGA-9 IS LETHAL TO HOST AND HUNTER ALIKE. USE WITH CAUTION.*

Dale stared at the signature. He felt a wave of nausea. He had just negotiated with a monster. He had used the Authority's bureaucracy as a weapon.

The Parasite pulled the smoke back.

"You have five minutes," the Parasite said. "Before the narrative collapses. Before the other readers arrive."

"Who?" Dale asked.

"The ones who want the book."

The Parasite pointed a long, black finger at the sealed tarp. At the ceiling above them.

"They are already here."

A thud. From above.

Another thud. Closer.

The library floorboards groaned.

Dale looked at his clipboard. He looked at the Parasite.

"Protocol Omega-9," Dale said. "What is it?"

The Parasite smiled. It was a tear in the obsidian mask.

"It is not a spray," the Parasite said. "It is an edit."

The ceiling exploded.

Wood splinters rained down. But they weren't just wood.

They were pages.

Thousands of pages. Swirling in a vortex of ink and wind.

From the hole in the ceiling, a swarm descended. Not mites. Not rats.

They were words.

Physical, jagged, black words. *THE. END. OF.*

They bit into the air, eating oxygen, eating light.

Dale raised his flashlight. The beam was swallowed.

He looked at the Parasite. The creature was dissolving. Its edges were fraying into loose script.

"You have to choose," the Parasite said. Its voice was faint now. "The story or the silence."

Dale looked at the swirling vortex of words. They were heading for the stairs. Heading for the house above. Heading for the street.

If those words got out, if they entered the minds of the people on the street...

Dale pulled his pen. He clicked the cap.

"Silence," Dale said.

He wrote on the clipboard.

*ACTION: INITIATE OMEGA-9.* *TARGET: NARRATIVE COHERENCE.* *METHOD: REDACTION.*

The System flashed white.

*PROTOCOL OMEGA-9 ACTIVATED.* *WARNING: LOCAL REALITY DISTORTION IMMINENT.* *POINT VALUE: NULL.*

The words in the air froze.

For a second, everything was still. The vortex stopped spinning. The Paraites hung suspended in the air.

Then, the redaction hit.

It wasn't a sound. It was an absence.

The words didn't disappear. They were *removed*. Cut out. As if they had never been there.

The vacuum created by their removal sucked the air out of the room.

Dale was thrown backward. He hit the support pillar. Hard.

His clipboard flew from his hand.

The Parasite was gone. The hole in the ceiling was sealed. The dead mites were gone. The Construct was gone.

Dale was alone.

He lay on the concrete. His ribs screamed.

He sat up.

The room was empty. Clean. Sterile.

But his Field Guide was blinking.

*ENTRY: ARCHIVAL PARASITE.* *STATUS: REDACTED.* *DATA CORRUPTED.* *POINTS AWARDED: 0.*

Zero points.

Dale stared at the text. He had saved the neighborhood. He had stopped a Class IX breach. And the Authority had given him nothing.

Because the entity was gone. Unidentifiable. Unverifiable.

"Thoroughness Rating: 1.0," the System noted coldly.

Dale picked up his clipboard. It was blank. The signature was gone. The redaction had erased the evidence of the transaction.

He had no proof he had done anything.

He stood up. His legs shook.

He walked to the stairs.

At the top, the front door was open.

Sunlight poured in.

A man stood on the porch.

A neighbor. Mr.

Henderson.

He was holding a baseball bat. He looked at Dale, then at the house, then at the sky.

"What happened?" Henderson asked.

Dale wiped blood from his lip. He looked at the clipboard. Blank.

He looked at Henderson.

"Nothing," Dale said. "Just a structural issue. I'm filing a report."

Henderson frowned. "You're covered in ink."

Dale looked down. His jumpsuit was stained with black fluid. It was seeping into the fabric.

"It's a leak," Dale said. "From the books."

Henderson squinted. He looked past Dale, into the dark hallway of the house.

"I hear whispering," Henderson said.

Dale froze.

He listened.

Faint. Distant.

*...help us... we are still here... rewrite us...*

The words were coming from the walls.

Dale's hand went to his pocket. His pen was there.

He pulled it out.

"Who told you that?" Dale asked.

Henderson tapped his temple. "The air. It tastes like paper."

Dale looked at the house. The siding. The windows.

The black stains on Dale's suit were spreading. He could feel them moving. Crawling under his skin.

He opened his Field Guide.

*NEW ENTRY DETECTED.* *SPECIES: NARRATIVE RESIDUE.* *CLASS: UNKNOWN.* *THREAT: EXISTENTIAL.*

*ACTION REQUIRED: IMMEDIATE QUARANTINE.* *NOTE: HOST IS NO LONGER SEPARATE FROM INFESTATION.*

Dale looked at his hands. The black veins were back. They were under his skin.

He had used Omega-9. He had redacted the parasite.

But he had been holding the clipboard. He had signed the variance.

The redaction had spread.

It hadn't removed the parasite. It had moved it.

Into him.

Henderson raised his bat. "Dale? Your eyes are black."

Dale looked at his reflection in the window.

His left eye was obsidian. The right was still brown.

He could feel the book in his chest. He could feel the pages turning in his lungs.

He had not exterminated the infestation.

He had become the container.

And the Authority's notification appeared, one last time.

*CERTIFICATION LEVEL: VI.* *CONDITION: LIVING HARD DRIVE.* *NEW DIRECTIVE: MAINTAIN NARRATIVE INTEGRITY.* *FAIL CONDITION: EJECTION OF HOST.*

Dale turned to Henderson.

He didn't have a weapon. He didn't have a spray.

He had a pen.

And he had a story that was trying to eat him from the inside out.

"Don't come in," Dale said. His voice was raspy. Like sandpaper.

Henderson hesitated.

Dale stepped back. Into the dark.

The whispering grew louder. It was using his own voice.

*...Chapter Two Hundred and Five...*

Dale slammed the front door. He locked it.

He leaned against it, gasping. The black veins in his arm pulsed.

He pulled out his notebook. Not the clipboard. The small, leather-bound one he kept in his breast pocket.

He opened it.

He wrote.

Not a report.

A map.

Because if he was the hard drive, then the Authority had just locked him in with the data. And if there was more data to be found, if there were more books to be eaten...

He had to find them before they woke up.

He had to find them before they rewrote the world.

Dale Kowalski, Certified Exterminator, Level VI, Living Archive, opened his eyes.

The black eye saw everything.

He walked. Not away from the house.

Toward the town library.

The library that had been burned down three days ago.

The library that was currently rebuilding itself, brick by brick, in the center of the park.

And it was waiting for him.
