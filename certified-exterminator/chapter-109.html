Chapter 109: A Ten Year Audit

# Chapter 109

The clipboard was standard issue. Municipal grade.

Heavy-duty polypropylene with a metal clip that had rusted at the corner.

Dale knew the brand. He used three of them.

The figure holding it stood twenty feet tall.

It was a construct of municipal failure. Twisted rebar formed its torso, wrapped in cracked asphalt that shifted like scales.

Its head was a chaotic pile of rubble, broken bricks, and shattered concrete blocks, held together by the tension of its own jagged limbs.

It did not have eyes. It did not need them. It had jurisdiction.

"Your workspace is not up to code," the thing said. Its voice was Dale's, but flattened. Stripped of inflection. A recording of Dale's own compliance officer voice, amplified through a dozen layers of distorted static.

Dale didn't blink. He didn't raise his hands.

He reached into his utility belt and pulled out his own clipboard.

The metal clip clicked against the plastic board.

A sharp, distinct sound in the heavy silence.

Miller lowered the fire extinguisher. His face was gray, the yellow powder in his beard making him look older. "Dale.

That thing is looking at you. Not the house.

You."

"Rival licensee," Dale said. His voice was steady. Too steady.

He checked his Field Guide.

The entry for *Dimensional Vermin* was still stuck at 5% recovery, but a new line had populated below it.

*Competitive Overlap Detected.* *Rival Species: Bureaucratic Revenant.* *Threat Level: Class III.* *Primary Weakness: Procedural Inconsistency.*

"Procedural Inconsistency," Dale muttered.

He looked at the giant. It was holding its own clipboard up, inspecting the debris field where the ink face had been. It made a checking-off motion. *Check.

Check. Fail.*

"It's documenting the scene," Dale said. "It's filing an inspection report on us."

"On us?" Miller asked. "We just killed a monster."

"We did not follow protocol," Dale corrected. "We used a chemical agent not listed in the approved containment matrix for *Rattus profundus*. We failed to isolate the perimeter. We failed to document the initial entry point."

The giant took a step forward. The ground shook. A fissure opened in the asphalt, swallowing a parked car.

*Notice of Violation,* the System announced. *Immediate citation issued. Penalty: 200 Treatment Points deducted.*

Dale felt the number vanish from his total. His cert progress bar dipped. A physical sensation, like a tooth being pulled without anesthetic.

"That's not fair," Miller said. "We saved the block."

"Fairness is not part of the licensing framework," Dale said.

He looked at the giant.

It raised its other arm. A jagged spike of rebar extended, pointing directly at Miller.

"It's citing you, too. Improper handling of hazardous materials. Unlicensed use of fire suppression devices in a dimensional breach zone."

Miller looked at the extinguisher in his hands. "I didn't know!"

"Ignorance of the code is no defense," Dale said. He flipped a page on his clipboard.

"Miller, drop the canister.

Step back. Do not touch the debris."

"What are you doing?"

"Contesting the citation," Dale said.

The giant paused. The rubble-head tilted. A low hum emanated from its torso. The air pressure dropped.

Dale stepped forward. He held his clipboard up, matching the giant's posture.

"I am filing an appeal," Dale said. "Under Section 4, Paragraph 2, Subsection C of the Municipal Pest Control Act."

The giant did not speak. It simply watched. The rebar fingers tightened around its own clipboard.

Dale spoke, not to the giant, but to the air. To the Authority.

"The initial contact was initiated by the entity, not the technician. The technician was already present in the structure. The infestation was active. The technician's actions were reactive, not proactive, thereby exempting the requirement for pre-engagement perimeter isolation under emergency provisions for Class-1 encounters."

The System interface flickered in Dale's vision.

*Reviewing Claim...* *Evaluating Precedent...* *Insufficient Data.*

"Insufficient data?" Dale asked aloud.

He knew the System was watching. He knew it was grading him.

He wasn't trying to fight the giant.

He was trying to fight the audit.

The giant took another step. The asphalt cracked further. The distance between them closed. Ten feet.

"Miller," Dale said, not turning around. "Hand me the yellow powder bag. The unopened one."

Miller scrambled to his back and pulled out a spare canister of aerosolized fungicide. He tossed it to Dale. Dale caught it without looking.

"You're not spraying it," Miller whispered.

"No," Dale said. "I'm labeling it."

He shook the canister. He pointed it at the ground between him and the giant. He squeezed the trigger. A cloud of yellow dust billowed out, settling on the cracked asphalt.

*Field Marked,* the System noted. *Zone 4.*

Dale looked at the giant. "This area is now classified as a Controlled Containment Zone. Under the Municipal Act, unauthorized entry into a Controlled Containment Zone by an unlisted entity constitutes Trespass on Municipal Property."

The giant stopped.

The hum in its chest changed pitch.

*Argument Accepted,* the System announced. *Citation Re-evaluated.* *Penalty Reduced: 50 Treatment Points.*

Dale exhaled. His hands were shaking, but he kept the clipboard steady.

"It's working," Miller said. "You're confusing it."

"I'm not confusing it," Dale said. "I'm out-bureaucratizing it."

The giant lowered its arm. The rebar spike retracted slightly.

It seemed to be processing the new data.

It looked at the yellow powder.

It looked at Dale. It looked at its own clipboard.

Then, the giant raised its clipboard high.

*Counter-Notice,* the System announced. *Rival Licensee invokes Precedent of Superior Certification.* *Challenge Accepted.* *Terms: One Job. Winner takes the Thoroughness Rating. Loser loses all Certification Levels.*

Dale's blood ran cold.

"That's not a contest," Dale said. "That's a termination order."

"It's a job," Miller said, his voice rising in panic. "Look at the street. Look at the damage.

That thing is causing more damage just by standing there. You can't just appeal that away.

You have to fix it."

Dale looked down the street. The giant had caused a ripple of destruction in its wake. Windows were blown out.

Pipes were burst.

Water sprayed into the air, mixing with the yellow powder to create a toxic sludge.

But Dale noticed something else.

In the reflection of a broken storefront window, he saw the giant's clipboard. It wasn't blank. It was full. Pages and pages of dense, handwritten text.

It wasn't just holding a clipboard. It was *carrying* the backlog.

Every violation. Every citation. Every failed inspection from the last ten years.

"It's not a monster," Dale realized. "It's the Aggregate."

"What?" Miller asked.

"The Authority doesn't just punish you for one mistake," Dale said. "It accumulates. It builds up debt. And when you hit a certain threshold... it comes to collect."

The giant took a step forward. The water and powder mixture sizzled where its feet touched the ground.

*Time Limit: 10 Minutes,* the System announced. *Failure to Resolve Infestation Results in Immediate Revocation of License.*

Dale looked at his Field Guide. The entry for *Bureaucratic Revenant* was still incomplete. But the *Weakness* line had updated.

*Weakness: Procedural Inconsistency.*

Dale looked at the yellow powder. He looked at the water. He looked at the giant's clipboard.

"Miller," Dale said. "Do you have a pen?"

"A pen? We're about to get turned into roadkill!"

"I need a pen," Dale said.

"And I need you to read the signs. All of them. The street signs.

The business signs. The parking regulations."

Miller stared at him. "Why?"

"Because it's a municipal construct," Dale said. "It runs on local ordinances. And I just realized something."

Dale looked at the giant. It was waiting.

"I know this block," Dale said. "I've inspected every duplex on this street. I know the zoning codes. I know the setback requirements."

Miller's eyes widened. "You know the code?"

"I know the *violation*," Dale said.

Dale stepped forward. He walked right up to the giant. The heat radiating from its asphalt body was intense. The smell of hot tar and old rain filled Dale's nose.

He didn't attack. He didn't spray.

He reached out and touched the giant's clipboard.

The giant froze.

*Contact Made,* the System announced. *Initiating Audit.*

Dale pulled out a pen from his pocket. He uncapped it. He clicked the top.

"Let's see your paperwork," Dale said.

He wrote. Not on his own clipboard. On the giant's.

He wrote a single word.

*Zoning.*

The giant shuddered. The rebar limbs vibrated. The rubble-head collapsed inward, then expanded outward.

*Error,* the System announced. *Zoning Conflict Detected.*

The giant looked at its own clipboard. Dale's handwriting was scrawled across its dense, bureaucratic text.

*Residential Zone.* *Prohibited: Industrial Structures.* *Prohibited: Aggregate Entities.*

The giant looked at itself. It looked at Dale. It looked at the word *Prohibited.*

"It doesn't belong here," Dale said. "It's an illegal structure in a residential zone. Under Ordinance 14-B, it is subject to immediate condemnation."

The giant roared. It wasn't a roar of anger. It was a sound of bureaucratic distress.

*Condemnation Proceeding Initiated.* *Demolition Sequence: Active.*

The giant's body crumbled. The rebar snapped. The asphalt fell away in chunks.

Miller ducked behind a car. "It's falling apart!"

"It's being evicted," Dale said.

He kept writing.

He filled page after page. He listed every ordinance the giant violated.

Every code violation. Every missing permit.

The giant tried to step back, but its legs gave way. It crashed to the ground, a pile of harmless rubble.

The dust settled.

Silence returned to the street.

Dale stood in the middle of the debris. He was covered in gray dust. His clipboard was full.

The System interface flashed.

*Infestation Resolved.* *Method: Administrative Eviction.* *Thoroughness Rating: 10.0* *Certification Level: II -> III*

Dale felt the surge of power. His Field Guide updated. New protocols unlocked.

*Protocol Unlocked: Citation Force.* *Description: Use legal documentation to weaken enemies vulnerable to order.*

Miller stood up, brushing dust off his jacket. He looked at Dale with a mix of awe and terror.

"You just killed a Class III monster with... paperwork?"

"Thorough paperwork," Dale corrected. He capped his pen. He wiped his hands on his pants, but the grime remained.

He looked at the pile of rubble. Something was glowing underneath it.

A small, blue light.

Dale walked over. He kicked aside a chunk of rebar.

There, nestled in the broken asphalt, was a small, rectangular object. It looked like a USB drive, but it was made of crystal.

Dale picked it up. It was warm.

*Item Acquired: Authority Core Fragment.* *Warning: Unauthorized possession of Authority artifacts is a Class I Felony.*

Dale stared at the fragment.

The System notification burned in his vision.

*Notification: Rival Licensee Terminated.* *Notification: New Rival Detected.*

Dale looked up.

At the end of the street, where the streetlights were dead, something else was walking toward them.

It was smaller. It was human-sized.

It was wearing a suit. A pristine, white suit.

And it was holding a tablet.

Dale dropped the crystal fragment. He grabbed his clipboard.

"Miller," Dale said. "Run."

Miller didn't ask why. He just started running.

Dale didn't run. He stood his ground. He looked at the man in the white suit.

The man smiled. It was a thin, bureaucratic smile.

"Hello, Mr. Kowalski," the man said. "I'm here to audit your last ten years."
