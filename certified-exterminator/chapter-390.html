Chapter 390: Audited by the Hand

# Chapter 390

The chitinous fingers were already three feet off the ground when Dale slapped the empty can against his thigh. Metal on thigh. A hollow thud. Useless.

Dale scanned the debris field with a technician's eye, not a soldier's. Left flank: shattered asphalt, rebar exposed. Right flank: crushed sedan, fuel leaking onto the fissure edge. Rear: Halloway, pinned under a slab of concrete, bleeding from a gash in his shoulder.

Above them, the drone hovered. Its camera lens rotated, focusing on the emerging hand.

The hand didn't strike. Not yet.

It paused, hovering over the fissure like a waiter presenting a dish.

The Authority's logo glowed on its palm, pulsing with a slow, rhythmic light. White. Cold.

Bureaucratic.

"Status," Dale muttered. He didn't look at Halloway. He looked at the hand.

*Species: Unidentified. Class: IX? Status: Authorized Collection. Entry: Sealed.*

The text hovered in the corner of Dale's vision, crisp and green. Green meant approved. Green meant the system recognized this event as a legitimate, pre-authorized removal of assets.

That was the problem. The Authority wasn't invading. It was auditing.

"Find me a can," Dale said. His voice was flat, stripped of panic. It was the voice he used when telling a homeowner their kitchen was unfit for human habitation.

Halloway strained against the concrete.

"I can't move! My legs are trapped!"

"Good. Stay there.

Don't look at it directly.

Peripheral vision only. Direct observation triggers the Contaminated Field penalty if you panic.

"

Halloway's face went white. He squeezed his eyes shut.

Dale moved. He didn't run.

Running wasted energy and signaled prey behavior, and he walked, stepping over jagged stones with the careful gait of a man navigating a construction site.

His clipboard was tucked under his arm. The paper scrap Halloway had held was gone, lost in the chaos, but the log entry remained in the cloud.

The hand twitched. A joint cracked, loud as a gunshot.

Dale stopped. He raised his clipboard.

"Identification protocol initiated," he said aloud. Not to the Authority. To himself. To keep the rhythm.

He looked at the palm. The runes weren't just decoration. They were a barcode. A tracking ID.

*Item: Asset 749-B. Origin: Local Node. Disposition: Termination.*

Termination. Not extermination. There was a difference.

Extermination implied an infestation that needed clearing. Termination implied an asset being retired.

If it was an asset, it belonged to the Authority.

If it belonged to the Authority, Dale couldn't kill it.

Killing an authorized asset was a Class-1 Violation. It would trigger an immediate audit of Dale's own licensing status. He could lose his certification.

He could lose his life.

But he couldn't let it crush them, and he needed to change the classification.

Dale pulled a spare can from his belt. It was a standard Class-2 aerosol, designed for small vermin.

Useless against a Class IX entity.

But the chemical composition wasn't the point. The *label* was.

He unscrewed the nozzle. He tilted the can, letting the propellant hiss out into the air. A white cloud, thin and fragile.

He sprayed it directly at the glowing logo on the palm.

The white mist coated the runes.

Nothing happened. The logo still glowed.

Dale frowned. He checked his Field Guide.

It was blank for this entry.

No weakness listed, and he needed to create one.

He reached into his pocket and pulled out a marker. Black, permanent, thick-tipped. He uncapped it.

Halloway groaned.

"Dale, move!"

The hand dropped. One inch. Two. The fingers curled inward, ready to close over the fissure.

Dale stepped forward. He walked right into the shadow of the hand. The smell hit him first.

Ozone and rot. The scent of a dumpster behind a seafood restaurant left in the sun for a week.

He raised the marker. He drew a line across the logo. A single, thick, black slash.

*Defacement.*

The drone above jerked. Its lens dilated.

Dale didn't care, and he kept drawing. He circled the logo. He added a question mark.

He added a 'K-7' code.

*Structural Entry Point. Unauthorized Modification.*

The system paused. The green text flickered.

*Processing... Asset Tag Compromised. Re-evaluating Disposition.*

The hand froze.

"Re-evaluating," Dale said. "That's what I do. I evaluate."

He backed away, careful not to touch the chitin. He needed the system to reject the asset. If the Authority couldn't verify the seal, it couldn't authorize the removal. And if it couldn't authorize the removal, it couldn't crush him.

But rejection meant something else. Rejection meant the item was now *unauthorized*.

Unauthorized items were infestations.

The green text vanished.

Red text snapped into existence.

*Violation Detected: Seal Integrity Failure.* *Object Reclassified: Unlicensed Infestation.* *Threat Level: Variable.* *Action Required: Immediate Containment.*

Dale exhaled. His heart hammered against his ribs, but his hands were steady.

He picked up his spray can, which was still empty, but it was no longer a weapon. It was a prop.

"Cover me," Dale said.

He turned to Halloway.

"The drone is confused. It's waiting for a supervisor override. Give it time."

"Time for what?" Halloway shouted.

"For the paperwork to catch up."

The hand twitched again. The black marker line pulsed.

The chitin skin rippled.

It wasn't just a hand anymore, which was a conduit. The runes were leaking.

Not blood, but data. Streams of white light poured from the wound Dale had made in the seal.

The drone rotated. It descended.

It wasn't attacking the hand. It was scanning Dale.

A beam of blue light hit Dale's chest.

*User: Dale Kowalski. Status: Level II Technician. Action: Unsanctioned Modification of Authority Property.*

The text hovered. It was waiting for a response.

Dale looked up. He didn't speak. He held up his clipboard. He pointed to the fresh entry he had scribbled on the back.

*Observed: Entity displays characteristics of Dimensional Parasite. Seal is decorative, not functional. Entity is attempting to breach containment via unauthorized channel. Request: Reclassification as Class IV Pest.*

The drone processed.

*Analysis: Seal integrity is 40% compromised. Entity is unstable. Reclassification approved.*

The red text changed.

*Target: Dimensional Parasite (Unlicensed).* *Protocol: Eliminate and Document.* *Reward: 50 Treatment Points.* *Penalty: 10 Certification Points for Property Damage.*

Dale accepted, and he didn't have a weapon. He had a clipboard. He had a marker. He had a can of empty spray.

But he had a rule.

The Cross-Contamination Penalty.

If he used a Class II protocol on a Class IX entity, it would fail. It would contaminate his guide.

But if he used a *Class IX* protocol.., and he didn't have one.

He had to borrow one.

Dale looked at the drone. He looked at the hand.

"Hey!" Dale shouted. "Authority! You're leaking!"

The drone froze.

"The entity is bleeding data," Dale said. "That's a contamination hazard.

If it spreads, the whole node is compromised.

You're not cleaning it. You're spreading it.

"

The drone tilted.

*Query: Define Contamination Hazard.*

Dale pointed to the white light streaming from the hand.

"See that? That's raw dimensional energy.

It's not contained.

It's splashing on the street. That's a code violation. Section 4, Paragraph B: Improper Disposal of Hazardous Materials.

"

He didn't know if that section existed. He was bluffing. But the system liked specificity. The system liked rules.

*Scanning...*

The drone moved closer. It scanned the light.

*Analysis: Energy leakage exceeds safe thresholds. Environmental contamination risk: High.*

*New Protocol Unlocked: Containment Field.*

A sphere of blue light expanded from the drone. It wrapped around the hand. The hand stopped moving. The fingers curled into a fist, trapped in the blue bubble.

The drone pulled back. It was trying to contain the leak.

But containing it wasn't enough. The system had reclassified the hand as an infestation. Infestations had to be *eliminated*.

Containment was not elimination.

*Warning: Incomplete Protocol. Elimination Required.*

The drone struggled. The blue sphere flickered.

Dale saw the opportunity, and the drone was busy holding the sphere. It couldn't attack the hand. And it couldn't attack Dale because Dale had just pointed out a violation in its own procedure.

Dale ran.

Not to Halloway. To the sedan.

He jumped into the car. He pulled the fuel line. Gasoline sprayed onto the asphalt.

He didn't light it. Not yet.

He looked at the blue sphere. The hand was thrashing inside. The data leak was growing.

"Documentation," Dale muttered.

He pulled out his phone. He took a photo of the scene.

The hand.

The drone.

The leaking light. He uploaded it to the Authority cloud.

*Evidence Attached: Case 749-B.*

The drone's lens snapped toward him.

*User: Interfering with Active Containment.*

"Am I?" Dale shouted. "Or am I facilitating the elimination?"

He kicked the fuel line. A stream of gasoline hit the edge of the blue sphere.

The fuel didn't ignite. It just coated the barrier.

The drone scanned the fuel.

*Material: Hydrocarbon.*

*Interaction: Unknown.*

Dale pulled a lighter from his pocket. He didn't light it. He held it up.

"Combustible material detected," Dale said. "If you don't eliminate the host now, you risk explosion. Explosion damages the node.

Node damage requires a full system reset. You don't want that.

It's too much paperwork.

"

The drone hesitated.

The hand shrieked. A sound like metal grinding on glass.

The blue sphere cracked.

*Protocol Override: Emergency Elimination.*

The drone fired.

A beam of pure white light shot from its underside. It hit the gasoline.

It didn't hit the hand. It hit the fuel.

The asphalt erupted in fire.

The blue sphere shattered. The hand was free.

But the fire spread. It burned along the fuel trail. It burned toward the fissure.

The hand screamed. It pulled back, trying to retreat into the hole.

Dale didn't wait. He grabbed a jagged piece of rebar from the wreckage. He ran toward the burning fuel line.

He wasn't trying to kill the hand. He was trying to seal the hole.

The fissure was wide. The hand was pulling itself back, but it was slow. The fire was eating the edges of the crack, melting the asphalt, but the earth beneath was still open.

Dale jumped into the fissure.

Halloway screamed.

"Dale! What are you doing?"

Dale landed on the side of the crack. He was ten feet down. The smell of sulfur was overpowering. The heat from the fire above was intense.

He looked down. The hand was retreating. But its fingers were still gripping the edge of the hole.

Dale jammed the rebar into the hand's wrist, and he didn't stab it. He pinned it.

The rebar was hot. The chitin sizzled. The hand jerked.

Dale climbed down. He used the rebar as a ladder, holding onto the wrist. He went deeper.

Into the dark, and the tunnel was smooth. Organic. Like the inside of a throat.

He could hear the drone above. It was firing beams into the fire, trying to put it out.

Dale descended.

The hand was attached to something else, and he reached the end of the wrist. The arm continued down.

He looked up.

The drone was gone. The sky was blocked by the earth.

He looked down.

The arm ended. But it wasn't the end.

It was connected to a larger mass. A pulsating, wet, black mass that filled the tunnel.

It had eyes. Dozens of them. They blinked in the dark.

And they were looking at him.

A notification appeared.

*Contact: Primary Host.* *Status: Unsealed.* *Action: Terminate.*

Dale realized his mistake, and he hadn't sealed the hole. He had entered it.

He was inside the infestation.

The mass pulsed. The hand pulled itself free from the rebar.

Dale floated in the darkness, suspended by the grip of the creature.

The eyes opened, and they were all the Authority's logo.

White. Glowing. Watching.

A voice echoed in his head. Not text. A voice.

*Technician Kowalski.*

Dale tried to speak. His mouth was filled with dust.

*Your field guide is incomplete.*

The mass tightened around him.

*You are being audited.*

Dale's clipboard slipped from his hand. It fell into the black slime.

The last thing he saw was the logo on the palm of the hand, inches from his face.

And then, the dark.
