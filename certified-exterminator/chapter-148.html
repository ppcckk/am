Chapter 148: Unlicensed Self Termination

# Chapter 148

The void did not smell like rot. It smelled like ozone and old filing cabinets.

Gary Miller was falling.

Not through air, but through a suspension of grey, particulate static that tasted of copper. His limbs flailed uselessly, kicking against the nothingness. The Break Room had ceased to exist. The vending machine, the linoleum, the fluorescent hum—all of it had been scrubbed from the local reality.

Gary's eyes darted wildly. He couldn't see the floor. He couldn't see the ceiling. He could only see Dale.

Dale Kowalski was not falling.

He was standing.

Not on a surface. He was suspended in the center of the white void, his posture rigid, his feet planted on an invisible, geometric plane that only he could perceive.

His clipboard was out.

His pen was capped. He looked calm, which was worse than screaming.

Beside Dale, the swarm hung in the air.

Thousands of chitinous forms, their wings vibrating at a frequency that made Gary's teeth ache. They were paused.

Suspended.

Waiting for a command.

And facing them, the Inspector.

The tall, faceless figure in the suit stood with one hand behind his back, the other holding a golden pen that glowed with a light that hurt to look at directly. The mask was white. Smooth. Featureless save for the single, vertical slit where a mouth should be.

Gary tried to shout. His voice came out as a dry rasp.

"Dale! Move!"

Dale didn't move. He didn't blink. He was writing.

The scratch of the pen on the clipboard was the only sound in the collapsing sector. It was a rhythmic, aggressive scraping. *Scritch. Scritch. Scritch.*

Gary watched Dale's hand move. It wasn't frantic.

It was methodical.

The technician's eyes were narrowed, tracking the subtle shifts in the Inspector's stance.

Dale wasn't looking at the monster. He was looking at the *posture*.

Then, Dale spoke.

"Improper entry point," Dale said. His voice was flat, carrying effortlessly through the static. "Unlicensed interference with a Class IX containment zone. Section 4, Paragraph B: Unauthorized Modification of Environmental Parameters."

The Inspector's head tilted. The white mask cracked further. A fissure ran down the left cheek.

**AUDIT IN PROGRESS.** **SUBJECT: DALE KOWALSKI.** **STATUS: NON-COMPLIANT.**

The swarm twitched. They didn't attack Dale. They turned toward the Inspector.

Gary realized then that Dale wasn't fighting the swarm. He was *managing* them.

The Inspector raised the golden pen. The tip extended, lengthening into a spear of pure light. He thrust it forward.

Dale didn't dodge. He stepped left. One step. Precise.

The spear of light passed through the space Dale had occupied a fraction of a second prior. It struck the void.

The void shattered.

Not into pieces, but into code. Green runes spilled out of the air, cascading like rain. The Inspector recoiled. For the first time, the entity looked uncertain.

Dale stepped back into the frame. He tapped his clipboard.

"You're out of certification range," Dale said. "You're operating under a Class IX license, but you're targeting a Class II operator in a transitional sector. That's a jurisdictional violation."

The Inspector clicked. The sound was loud, like a bone breaking.

**ERROR.** **JURISDICTIONAL AMBIGUITY DETECTED.** **RECALIBRATING...**

The swarm froze. The insects hung in the air, their movements synchronized to the Inspector's hesitation.

Dale looked at Gary.

"Stay down," Dale said. "Don't look at the runes. They cause vertigo."

Gary scrambled onto what he hoped was solid ground. It felt like walking on ice that had just begun to melt. He crawled toward Dale, his hands shaking.

"What is this?" Gary whispered. "What are you doing?"

"Contesting the citation," Dale said.

He turned back to the Inspector. The entity was reforming. The mask was knitting itself together, the cracks sealing with a wet, organic sound. The swarm was agitating, buzzing louder.

Dale uncapped his pen.

"I am invoking Clause 7," Dale announced.

The Interface in Dale's peripheral vision flared.

**CLAUSE 7: MUTUAL RECOGNITION OF HAZARD.** **TRIGGERED.** **EFFECT: SHARED WORKSPACE.**

The void changed.

The grey static turned into a grid. Blue lines snapped into existence, outlining walls, floors, and entry points. It wasn't the Break Room anymore.

It was a schematic. A blueprint of the dimensional breach.

The Inspector stumbled.

He looked around, his movements becoming jerky. The grid constrained him. He was no longer an omnipotent god in a void.

He was an entity in a defined workspace.

Dale pointed his pen at the swarm.

"Identify," Dale commanded.

The swarm responded.

One insect, large and beetle-like, broke formation. It flew toward the grid line that marked the sector boundary. It landed. Its antennae twitched.

Dale wrote on his clipboard.

**SUBJECT: DIMENSIONAL VERMIN (Rattus profundus).** **STATUS: CONTAINED.**

He looked at the Inspector.

"You're contaminating my workspace," Dale said.

The Inspector roared. It wasn't a sound.

It was a pressure wave.

Gary was thrown backward, hitting the invisible grid lines. Pain shot through his ribs.

Dale didn't flinch. He stepped forward.

"Protocol: Isolation," Dale said.

He didn't attack the Inspector. He attacked the *space* between the Inspector and the swarm.

Dale drew a line in the air with his pen. It wasn't a physical stroke. It was a conceptual barrier. A firewall.

The line glowed red.

**PROTOCOL DEPLOYED: CROSS-CONTAMINATION BARRIER.** **COST: 50 TREATMENT POINTS.** **DURATION: 3 MINUTES.**

The barrier snapped into place. The swarm was cut off from the Inspector. The Inspector was cut off from the swarm.

The Inspector screamed. He slashed at the barrier with his golden spear. The spear bounced off. The barrier held.

Gary stared. He had never seen Dale use a protocol like this.

It wasn't an attack.

It was a *restriction*.

A code violation notice made manifest.

"You can't do that," Gary breathed. "That's for structural breaches."

"It's for any unauthorized crossing of boundaries," Dale said. He was sweating now. A bead of perspiration tracked down his temple. His hand trembled as he held the clipboard.

The Inspector was furious. The mask was cracking again. But this time, the cracks were leaking black fluid.

Dale looked at the Inspector's feet.

"Look at his shoes," Dale said.

Gary looked.

The Inspector was wearing standard-issue municipal safety boots. But the soles were wrong. They had tread patterns that didn't match any known manufacturer.

They were too deep. Too aggressive.

"They're not here for extermination," Dale said. "They're here for excavation."

The Inspector lunged. Not at Dale. At the grid line.

He tore through the blue schematic. The reality around them shuddered. The green runes poured out again, faster this time. The void was trying to reassert itself.

Dale's interface flashed red.

**WARNING: SECTOR INTEGRITY AT 15%.** **IMMINENT TOTAL COLLAPSE.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING PENALTY IMMINENT.**

Dale's jaw tightened. He hated penalties. They hurt more than bullets.

"Gary, hold the line," Dale said.

"What line? There's no line!"

"The grid. Hold the grid."

Dale ran.

He didn't run away. He ran toward the Inspector.

He slid under the golden spear's thrust, the tip grazing his shoulder. The pain was immediate and electric. Dale didn't stop. He slammed his clipboard against the Inspector's chest.

The clipboard connected with a dull thud.

**CONTACT REGISTERED.** **SUBJECT: THE INSPECTOR.** **SPECIES: UNKNOWN (CLASS IX ENTITY).** **WEAKNESS IDENTIFICATION IN PROGRESS...**

The Inspector froze.

Dale's eyes scanned the entity's suit. The buttons. The stitching. The fabric weave.

"Synthetic polymer blend," Dale muttered. "Reinforced with ectoplasmic residue. Standard issue for high-level enforcers."

He looked at the Inspector's mask.

"No eyes. No ears. Just a mouth."

Dale raised his pen.

"And no certification number."

The Inspector's hand grabbed Dale's throat. The grip was iron. Dale's feet left the ground. The world spun.

Gary screamed and threw a rock he had somehow found in the void. It hit the Inspector's helmet. The figure didn't even flinch.

Dale dangled in the grip. His face was turning purple. But his eyes were still fixed on the Inspector's chest.

He reached into his pocket.

Not for a weapon.

For a stamp.

It was a small, red rubber stamp. *DENIED*.

Dale pressed it against the Inspector's chest.

**ACTION: STAMP OF NON-COMPLIANCE.** **REQUIRES: DIRECT CONTACT WITH UNLICENSED OPERATOR.** **RESULT: TEMPORARY STASIS (5 SECONDS).**

The Inspector's grip loosened.

Not because it was hurt. Because it was *confused*. The System didn't know what to do with a stamp on a Class IX entity.

The logic looped.

Dale dropped to the ground. He gasped for air.

"Five seconds," Dale wheezed. "Gary. The breach. Seal it."

Gary looked at the tear in the grid. The Inspector was frozen, his hand still reaching out, his body vibrating with the strain of the stasis.

"How?" Gary yelled. "I'm a janitor!"

"Use the trash bag!"

Dale pointed to the debris field. A large, black contractor bag had survived the collapse. It was floating near the edge of the grid.

Gary scrambled toward it. He grabbed the bag. It was heavy.

Dale stood up. He wiped blood from his mouth. He looked at the Inspector. The five seconds were ticking down.

"Put it over the tear," Dale commanded.

Gary unfolded the bag. He walked to the largest rift in the blue grid. The black fluid from the Inspector's mask was seeping into it, widening the gap.

Gary pulled the bag open. He threw it over the rift.

The bag expanded. It sucked the black fluid into itself. The rift closed.

The Inspector twitched. His eyes snapped open within the mask. The slits widened.

"NO," the Inspector said.

The voice was not human. It was the sound of tearing metal.

The Inspector broke the stasis. He lunged for Gary.

Dale tackled him.

Not a tackle. A *restraining hold*.

Dale wrapped his arms around the Inspector's legs, tripping him.

The Inspector fell. He hit the grid lines. The grid sparked.

Dale scrambled up. He grabbed the golden pen that had fallen from the Inspector's hand.

It was warm. It burned his palm.

**ITEM ACQUIRED: THE INSPECTOR'S PEN.** **CLASS: ARTIFACT (SEALED).** **WARNING: UNLICENSED USE RESULTS IN IMMEDIATE TERMINATION.**

Dale looked at the pen. He looked at the Inspector, who was struggling to rise, his limbs moving in jerky, unnatural spasms.

The swarm was waiting. They hovered behind the barrier, watching.

Dale raised the pen.

He didn't aim it at the Inspector.

He aimed it at his own clipboard.

**ACTION: SELF-CERTIFICATION OVERWRITE.** **COST: ALL CURRENT TREATMENT POINTS.** **RISK: CATASTROPHIC FAILURE.**

Gary froze. "Dale, don't!"

Dale ignored him.

He pressed the pen to the paper.

The tip didn't write. It *punched*. It tore through the page, through the clipboard, through the air.

A shockwave erupted.

The blue grid shattered. The barrier fell. The swarm surged forward, not at the Inspector, but at the space Dale had just punctured.

They poured into the hole.

The Inspector screamed. He tried to pull back, but the swarm was on him. Thousands of chitinous legs, biting, tearing, *documenting*.

The Inspector's mask dissolved.

Underneath, there was no flesh. There was only a swirling vortex of paperwork.

Forms.

Reports.

Violation notices. A hurricane of bureaucratic madness.

Dale fell to his knees. The pen in his hand turned to ash.

The void was collapsing around them. The walls of the Break Room were trying to reassemble. But they were wrong.

The angles were off. The colors were inverted.

Gary grabbed Dale's arm. He dragged him toward the only solid point remaining: the center of the original room.

"Dale! Get up!"

Dale didn't move. He was staring at his clipboard.

The page was blank.

But there was a new entry.

**FIELD GUIDE UPDATE:** **SUBJECT: THE INSPECTOR.** **IDENTIFIED: FALSE POSITIVE.** **REALITY: HE IS NOT AN ENEMY.** **HE IS A MIRROR.**

Gary pulled Dale up. The world snapped back into focus.

They were standing in the Break Room. The lights were flickering. The vending machine was humming. The floor was linoleum.

It looked normal.

Except for the swarm.

The insects were gone. The Inspector was gone.

But on the table in the center of the room, there was a clipboard.

Dale's clipboard.

And on it, a single sheet of paper.

Dale walked to it. His legs felt like lead. His head pounded with a migraine that felt like a physical blow.

He picked up the paper.

It wasn't a report.

It was a letter.

**TO: DALE KOWALSKI** **FROM: THE AUTHORITY** **RE: CERTIFICATION LEVEL IX EXAMINATION**

**DEAR CANDIDATE,**

**YOUR PERFORMANCE IN THE SECTOR 7 BREACH WAS NOTED.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 10/10.**

**HOWEVER.**

**THE SUBJECT KNOWN AS 'THE INSPECTOR' WAS NOT A MONSTER.** **IT WAS A PROCTOR.**

**YOU HAVE FAILED THE PRACTICAL EXAM.**

**YOU DESTROYED THE PROCTOR.**

**PENALTY: REVOCATION OF LICENSE.**

**NEXT EXAMINATION: IMMEDIATE.**

**LOCATION: YOUR BODY.**

Dale looked up.

Gary was staring at him.

"What does it mean?" Gary asked. "Location: your body?"

Dale looked at his hands.

His skin was itching.

A tiny, hard bump appeared on his forearm.

It looked like a chitinous plate.

Dale watched it spread. It crawled up his wrist, under his sleeve.

**SYSTEM NOTIFICATION:** **INFESTATION DETECTED IN HOST.** **SUBJECT: DALE KOWALSKI.** **STATUS: UNLICENSED.** **ACTION REQUIRED: SELF-TERMINATION PROTOCOL INITIATED IN 60 SECONDS.**

Dale looked at Gary.

"Don't blink," Dale said.
