Chapter 383: Tuesday Municipal Worker

# Chapter 383

The perimeter of the collapse zone hummed with a frequency that vibrated in the marrow of the bone.

Officer Kaelen stood at the cordon line, his rifle raised, knuckles white around the grip. He watched the figure emerging from the dust cloud.

It wasn't running.

It wasn't charging. It was walking with the terrifying, rhythmic precision of a municipal worker on a Tuesday.

Dale Kowalski adjusted his respirator mask.

The straps bit into his skin. He ignored the pain.

He focused on the clipboard in his left hand and the spray wand in his right.

The wand had been modified. The nozzle was wider, the pressure gauge calibrated to shatter chitin than just dissolve it.

Kaelen lowered his rifle an inch. Confusion prickled at the base of his skull.

This wasn't a hero.

Heroes shouted.

Heroes posed. Heroes had capes.

This man wore a stained jumpsuit that smelled of bleach and old sweat. He looked like he wanted to file a complaint.

"Step back," Kaelen yelled, though his voice lacked conviction.

Dale didn't step back. He stepped forward.

Behind Dale, Martinez trailed like a broken toy. The man was limping, favoring his left leg.

His eyes were wide, darting between the sky and the ground, but he kept moving.

He held his own flashlight like a club, though his grip had shifted. It wasn't defensive anymore. It was preparatory.

The air grew heavier. The green sky above fractured, leaking a viscous, black fluid that dripped down the sides of the ruined library. The fluid hit the pavement and sizzled, eating into the asphalt.

Dale stopped ten feet from the cordon. He raised his pen.

He wrote something on the clipboard.

Kaelen squinted, trying to read the tiny, cramped script from fifty yards away. He couldn't make out the words, but he saw Dale underline them twice. A hard, aggressive double-stroke.

Then Dale looked up. His eyes, visible above the mask, were flat. Dead.

"Clear the zone," Dale said. His voice was amplified by the respirator, coming out as a metallic drone. "This is a Code 99-B active infestation. Non-licensed personnel are creating cross-contamination hazards."

Kaelen blinked.

"Code what?"

"Move back," Dale repeated. "Or I write you up."

The absurdity of the threat broke the tension for Kaelen. He almost laughed. Almost. But then he saw the ground beneath Dale's feet.

The asphalt was cracking.

Not from impact. From root systems.

Thin, black tendrils were surfacing from the cracks, pulsing with a faint, bioluminescent rhythm.

They reached toward Dale, but they stopped three feet away. They recoiled.

Dale hadn't fought them. He had simply stood there.

The tendrils withdrew.

Kaelen felt a cold sweat slide down his spine. He signaled his squad.

"Fall back. Move the perimeter back two hundred meters."

"But Officer, we have a mission to—"

"Two hundred meters!" Kaelen snapped. He turned back to watch the man in the jumpsuit.

Dale turned his back on the police line. He faced the library. The entrance was a jagged maw of shattered stone and twisted rebar. The black fluid poured out of it like blood from a slit artery.

Dale checked his Field Guide, and he flipped pages with one hand, the other hovering over the spray nozzle. His movements were economical.

Precise.

He wasn't searching for a monster.

He was searching for a procedure.

He found the entry. **Human Replicant.**

He read the weakness. **Moral Discord.** **Authority Denial.**

Dale closed the book. He tapped the cover twice. A small, resonant chime sounded in the air, visible as a ripple of gold light. The Authority acknowledged the query.

"Martinez," Dale said, not looking back.

"Here," Martinez gasped.

"Stand at the threshold. Do not engage. Do not speak. If they try to recruit you, deny the contract."

Martinez swallowed hard. He nodded. He walked to the edge of the rubble.

He looked small against the towering structure of the library. He looked like a man who had lost his job and found it again in the worst way possible.

Dale raised the spray wand, and he didn't aim at the library. He aimed at the air above it.

He pulled the trigger.

A mist of silver vapor erupted from the nozzle. It wasn't poison. It was a binding agent, a Class-IV containment aerosol designed for high-mobility dimensional entities. It hung in the air, forming a shimmering net.

The black tendrils surged upward. They hit the net.

They froze.

The library groaned. The sound was like metal grinding against stone, amplified by the supernatural resonance of the Rift.

From the darkness of the entrance, a figure emerged, which was tall.

Too tall. Its proportions were wrong. The arms were too long, the head too small.

Its face was a smooth, featureless oval of pale bone.

It wore a suit. A sharp, black suit that seemed to absorb the light around it.

This was not a monster. This was a bureaucrat.

The Entity stopped at the threshold. It looked at Martinez. It tilted its head.

**"SUBJECT IDENTIFIED,"** the Entity's voice boomed. It didn't speak; it projected directly into the minds of everyone within a hundred yards. **"UNLICENSED ASSET. RETURN TO QUEUE."**

Martinez flinched. He took a step back. His hand tightened on the flashlight.

Dale stepped between Martinez and the Entity.

"Asset is terminated," Dale said. "Contract voided by voluntary resignation. See Form 14-C, Paragraph 4."

The Entity paused. The smooth face rippled.

**"NON-TRANSFERABLE. DEBT REMAINS."**

"Debt is nullified upon cessation of employment," Dale stated. "And the employer has committed violation Code 99-B. Illegal employment of sentient beings. Penalty: Termination of Employer."

The Entity's form flickered. For a second, Dale saw something inside the bone face. A network of wires. A puppeteer's strings.

The Entity laughed. It was a dry, rattling sound.

**"YOU HAVE NO JURISDICTION. THE HOLLOW IS BEYOND YOUR GRADE."**

Dale opened his clipboard. He wrote a new line.

**SUBJECT: HOLLOW OVERSIGHT. STATUS: NON-COMPLIANT.**

He looked up.

"I have a Grade VII Clearance," Dale lied.

The Entity stopped. The air pressure dropped. The silver mist boiled, turning a violent shade of red.

The Authority was watching.

Dale could feel it. The weight of the gaze was physical, pressing against his temples. He was bluffing.

He had a Grade IV. Maybe Grade V if he counted the bonus points from the Teeth-Lord incident.

Grade VII was reserved for Regional Directors.

But the Entity didn't know that. The Entity operated on rigid protocol. It checked the credentials.

**"VERIFY,"** the Entity projected.

Dale held up his left hand. He activated his Certification Badge.

It was a piece of plastic and glass.

But it glowed with the Authority's seal. The seal pulsed.

It wasn't a Grade VII seal. It was a glitched, flickering mess of data. A loophole.

Dale had spent the last three days hacking his own Field Guide. He had found a buffer error in the System's memory allocation. When a user completed a high-thoroughness job, the System briefly over-allocated processing power to validate the entry. During that split second of validation, the Authority's interface accepted any input as temporary truth.

Dale had timed it. He had triggered a micro-interaction with the Field Guide just as the Entity demanded verification. The System, confused by the conflicting data of his actual rank and his self-inserted

"Grade VII" tag, had defaulted to a permissive error state.

The Entity's face went blank.

**"VERIFICATION... DELAYED."**

"Process the termination," Dale said. His voice was steady.

His heart hammered against his ribs like a trapped bird.

"Or I escalate to Regional Oversight. I will file a grievance for obstructing a certified technician."

The Entity's form unravelled. The black suit frayed at the edges. The bone face cracked.

It wasn't attacking. It was checking the paperwork.

And paperwork was slow.

While the Entity waited for the System to resolve the contradiction, the library shifted. The ground beneath it buckled. The black tendrils thrashed against the silver net, dissolving the mist, trying to pull the Entity back into the depths.

The Entity looked down at its own hands. They were turning to ash.

**"THIS... IS ANOMALOUS."**

"Anomaly resolved," Dale said. He raised the spray wand again. "Preparing for extraction."

He fired.

The second burst wasn't containment, which was a solvent. A concentrated acid designed for chitinous armor.

But Dale had modified the nozzle.

He had aimed it at the ground, at the roots connecting the Entity to the library.

The acid hit the black tendrils.

They screamed. A silent, psychic scream that shattered windows in the surrounding blocks.

The Entity lurched forward. It reached for Dale.

Martinez shouted. He swung the flashlight. He hit the Entity's knee.

The bone leg snapped.

The Entity stumbled. It fell.

Dale didn't hesitate. He sprinted forward.

He didn't attack the Entity, and he attacked the connection.

He jammed the spray wand into the crater where the Entity had been, into the root mass that anchored it to the material plane.

He pulled the trigger. Full power.

The nozzle burst. The wand snapped in his hand.

The explosion of chemical energy tore through the root system. The library shuddered. The Entity shrieked, its form collapsing into a pile of inert, white powder.

Silence.

The green sky flickered. The black fluid stopped flowing.

Dale stood over the pile of ash. He was breathing hard. The respirator hissed as it filtered the dust.

He looked at his Field Guide, and the page was blank.

No points. No notification.

The System hadn't registered the kill. It had registered the *procedure*. The termination of the employer.

Then, a chime.

**JOB COMPLETED.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 8.4.** **NOTES: UNAUTHORIZED CREDENTIAL USAGE DETECTED. AUDIT PENDING.**

Dale's blood ran cold.

An audit.

The Authority wasn't fooled by the glitch. It had just been slow. And now it was looking.

He looked up at Martinez.

Martinez was staring at the pile of ash. He looked older. His hair seemed grayer.

"We have to go," Martinez whispered.

"Why?"

"Because they're coming," Martinez said. He pointed to the sky.

The green rift was widening. Not opening. Widening.

And from the edges of the rift, new shapes were descending. Not monsters.

Men. In suits. With clipboards.

Dale stared at them, and they weren't attacking.

They were inspecting.

One of the men landed on the roof of the library. He looked down at Dale. He raised a pen.

**"CITIZEN,"** the voice projected. **"YOU HAVE BEEN FLAGGED FOR MALPRACTICE."**

Dale looked at the broken spray wand. He looked at the ash. He looked at the approaching inspectors.

He realized the trap.

The Authority didn't care if he killed the monster. It cared that he broke the rules to do it. And in this system, breaking the rules wasn't rebellion. It was a liability.

Dale picked up his clipboard. The paper was torn, stained with acid and blood.

He had one move left, and he turned to Martinez.

"Run," Dale said.

"They won't catch us," Martinez said.

"They won't try to catch us," Dale said. He looked at the inspectors. "They'll try to *relicense* us."

The first inspector jumped down. He landed softly. He held out a hand.

"Mr. Kowalski," the inspector said. "Please surrender your Field Guide. We need to correct your entry."

Dale looked at the clipboard in his hand, and he looked at the inspector.

He looked at Martinez.

If he surrendered the Guide, he lost his power. He became unlicensed. He became vulnerable.

If he refused, he became a fugitive. A rogue technician.

The inspector stepped closer.

"Mr. Kowalski," the inspector said. "The Authority values order.

You have introduced chaos. Chaos is expensive.

"

Dale's hand trembled. He gripped the clipboard.

He didn't hand it over, and he ripped the top sheet off the clipboard.

He held up the piece of paper. It was blank.

"Where is the record?" Dale asked.

The inspector paused.

**"YOU DID NOT FILE THE REPORT."**

Dale smiled. It was a thin, cruel smile.

"Correct," Dale said. "No report, no record. No record, no audit."

The inspector's face hardened.

**"THE SYSTEM RETAINS A BACKUP COPY."**

"Does it?" Dale asked. He looked at Martinez. "Martinez, do you have a lighter?"

Martinez fumbled in his pocket. He produced a Zippo. He flicked it. The flame jumped to life.

Dale held the blank sheet of paper over the flame.

It didn't burn, and the paper was treated.

Authority-certified stock. Fireproof. Waterproof.

Time-stamped.

Dale looked at the inspector, and he looked at the blank paper.

He realized the inspector hadn't lied. The System *did* retain a copy.

But the copy was in the cloud.

The cloud was in the server. The server was in the Rift.

And the Rift was still open, and the inspector took another step forward.

"Give me the Guide," the inspector said.

Dale looked at the sky. The men in suits were landing now. Dozens of them. They formed a circle around the library.

They weren't there to arrest him, and they were there to witness.

Because if Dale ran, he was guilty.

If Dale fought, he was a threat.

If Dale submitted, he was broken.

But if Dale did nothing?

If Dale let the audit happen?

The inspector extended his hand.

Dale looked at Martinez.

Martinez shook his head.

Dale looked at the clipboard, and he made his choice.

He handed the clipboard to the inspector.

The inspector took it. He flipped it open. He looked at the blank page. He looked at Dale.

**"YOU ARE UNDER REVIEW,"** the inspector said.

Dale stepped back. He raised his hands.

"I am cooperating," Dale said.

But under his coat, his hand was closed, and he was holding the resonance whistle.

And he was already blowing it.
