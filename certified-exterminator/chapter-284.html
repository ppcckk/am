Chapter 284: Report Your Own Arrival

# Chapter 284

Miller's pen hovered over the notebook page. The ink trembled against the paper, a tiny black dot threatening to bleed.

Up twelve flights of stairs, the air grew thinner. It tasted like copper and ozone, sharp enough to coat the back of Dale's tongue. He didn't climb. He stood on the landing of the fifth floor, his clipboard raised like a shield.

The technician was still writing.

The wall behind him shimmered. The plaster wasn't plaster. It was a membrane of compressed time, translucent and veined with pulsing blue light. The technician's pen moved with frantic speed, scratching symbols that hurt Dale's eyes to look at directly.

Miller climbed. One step. Two steps. His knees knocked together, a hollow sound in the silence.

Dale watched the man's feet. Heavy boots.

Standard issue.

Scuff marks from concrete grinding. Not the gait of a hero. The gait of someone who knew the floor was slippery and had no intention of falling.

"Stop writing," Dale said. His voice was flat, carrying the bored tone of a municipal inspector noting a missing fire extinguisher.

The technician didn't stop. The blue light intensified, bleeding into the air. Shards of reality fell again, clinking against the stairwell like broken wind chimes.

Miller reached the third floor. He gasped, clutching his chest.

Dale turned his head. Just a fraction.

"Protocol 44-B: Witness Containment," Dale said.

He tapped the top of his clipboard. The paper there was blank.

White.

Perfectly sterile.

The technician paused. A drop of blue light landed on his shoulder, sizzling against his jacket. He didn't flinch. He looked down at Dale, his eyes wide and unblinking.

"You're not listening," the technician whispered.

"I am listening," Dale said. "I am documenting."

He flipped a page on his clipboard.

**FIELD NOTE 284-A** **SUBJECT:** Unauthorized Transmission via Architectural Membrane. **LOCATION:** Non-Euclidean Stairwell, Sector 7-G. **STATUS:** Active. Uncontained.

Dale wrote the date. He wrote the time.

He signed his name in the margin with a flourish he'd practiced for two decades.

The signature looked official.

It looked final.

The air around the clipboard solidified. A grid of faint orange lines appeared in the air, framing the empty page.

**SYSTEM NOTICE: DOCUMENTATION INITIATED.** **THRESHOLD:** 15% Completion. **EFFECT:** Localized Reality Stabilization (Zone: 5 Meters).

The falling shards stopped mid-air around Dale. They hung there, suspended in an invisible box.

The technician screamed. It wasn't a scream of pain. It was a scream of frustration.

He wrote faster, the pen tearing through the membrane wall. The blue light flared, trying to push back the orange grid.

Miller froze on the sixth floor. He looked at the suspended shards. He looked at Dale.

"What did you do?" Miller shouted.

"Dale Kowalski," Dale corrected, not looking up. "Certification Level IV. Senior Exterminator."

He uncapped a different pen. This one was red.

**VIOLATION CLASS:** E-7. **DESCRIPTION:** Unauthorized Use of Ambient Reality as a Signal Carrier. **REQUIRED ACTION:** Immediate Cessation and Documentation of Source Frequency.

Dale walked up the stairs. One step. Two steps.

The orange grid moved with him. It pushed the blue light back inch by inch. The technician shrank, hunching against the wall, his pen skidding across the surface.

"You can't document the song," the technician hissed. "It's not a bug. It's a message."

"Everything is a bug if you don't control the population," Dale said.

He reached the ninth floor, and he was close now. Close enough to see the texture of the wall. It was made of faces.

Thousands of them, frozen in silent screams, their mouths open in the same vowel shape.

Miller dropped to his knees. He vomited. The bile sizzled when it hit the floor, turning into small, frantic beetles that scurried away.

Dale stepped over the beetles. He didn't look at them. He looked at the technician.

"Your field guide is contaminated," Dale said. "You're mixing metaphysical signals with biological vectors. That's a Class 9 hazard."

"It's not biological!"

"Look at the floor."

Miller looked. The vomit-beetles were gone.

But the stain remained.

It pulsed. It had a rhythm. *Thump-thump. Thump-thump.*

"Rattus profundus," Dale said. "Dimensional Vermin.

They feed on panic.

You're feeding them.

Every time you scream, you create a new entry in your own Field Guide.

"

The technician's face went gray. He looked at his hands. They were shaking.

"I... I didn't know."

"Ignorance is not a defense," Dale said. "It's a citation."

He raised his clipboard. The red pen hovered over the air.

**CITATION ISSUED.** **OFFENDER:** Unlicensed Entity. **OFFENSE:** Endangerment via Cognitive Contamination. **PENALTY:** Immediate Erasure from Local Reality Index.

The orange grid snapped outward. It hit the technician.

He didn't vanish. He folded.

His body compressed. His bones snapped inward, collapsing into a dense, black sludge. The blue light went out. The singing stopped.

The silence was absolute.

Miller stared at the spot where the technician had been. A puddle of black goo remained. It bubbled.

Dale didn't celebrate. He didn't smile. He opened his notebook.

**EXTERMINATION LOG:** **TARGET:** Unlicensed Signal Source (Humanoid Vector). **METHOD:** Administrative Erasure. **VERIFICATION:** Visual Confirmation of Biomass Reduction. **POINTS AWARDED:** 0.

Miller blinked.

"Zero?"

Dale pointed his pen at the sludge.

"You didn't sign the witness statement. You vomited.

That's an unsanitary condition. The Authority doesn't reward chaos.

It rewards order.

"

He turned to Miller.

"Sign it."

Miller looked at the empty notebook in his hand. He looked at the sludge. He looked at Dale.

"I can't."

"Then you're an accessory," Dale said. He tapped his clipboard.

A new box appeared in the air. It was larger than the first. It was black.

**SYSTEM ALERT: COMPLICIT NEGLECT DETECTED.** **PENALTY:** Integration into Infestation Cycle. **T-MINUS:** 30 SECONDS.

Miller scrambled backward. He slipped on the wet floor, sliding down two steps.

"Wait!" he yelled.

Dale watched him slide. He checked his watch.

"Twenty seconds," Dale said. "You have to acknowledge the violation. You have to admit that you failed to contain the vector."

"I don't understand!"

"You watched a man die because you were too scared to write a sentence," Dale said. "That's the failure. That's the infestation.

Your fear.

Your hesitation. It's growing.

"

Miller's eyes widened. He looked at his hands. The veins in his arms were turning black. The same black as the sludge.

"No," Miller whispered.

"Sign it," Dale said. "Admit you were negligent. Admit you let the protocol fail."

"I can't do that!"

"Then you're already dead," Dale said. He turned his back.

He walked down the stairs. One step. Two steps.

The black veins spread up Miller's neck. He screamed, but no sound came out. The air in the stairwell cracked again, but this time, the cracks were black.

Dale reached the first floor. He opened the door.

The sunlight outside was bright. Blinding. Clean.

He stepped out. The blue light faded. The syrupy thickness vanished. He was back in the suburban duplex.

But the door behind him didn't close.

It stayed open.

Dale turned around, and the stairwell was gone. In its place was a hole in the wall. A ragged, tearing hole that looked like a mouth.

And from the hole, something crawled out.

It wasn't a rat. It wasn't a beetle.

It was a hand. A human hand, made of black sludge and singing voices. It grasped the doorframe.

Then another hand.

Then a head.

Miller's head.

But it wasn't Miller. The face was smooth, featureless, except for a single, wide-open eye in the center of the forehead.

The thing smiled.

It spoke in Miller's voice, but layered with a chorus of thousands.

*"You didn't sign it, Dale."*

Dale looked at his clipboard. The page was blank.

He looked at the thing in the doorway, and he looked at the System Notification flashing in his peripheral vision.

**ERROR: WITNESS INTEGRATION FAILED.** **SYSTEM ERROR: REALITY INDEX CORRUPTED.** **ACTION REQUIRED:** MANUAL OVERWRITE. **WARNING: THIS ACTION REQUIRES CERTIFICATION LEVEL V.** **CURRENT STATUS: LEVEL IV.**

Dale dropped his pen.

He reached into his pocket.

He pulled out a small, silver badge. It was old. Scratched.

He hadn't used it in ten years, and he pinned it to his chest.

The badge glowed.

**OVERRIDE ACCEPTED.** **WARNING: LICENSING VIOLATION. PENDING REVOCATION.** **PERMISSION GRANTED: TEMPORARY UPGRADE TO LEVEL V (INTERIM).** **TIME LIMIT: 60 SECONDS.**

Dale picked up his pen, and he looked at the thing that used to be Miller.

"Identify," Dale said.

The thing tilted its head. *

"What are we?"*

Dale wrote in the air. The red ink hung there, burning like fire.

**TARGET:** Composite Infestation (Class IX). **WEAKNESS:** Administrative Disassociation. **ACTION:** Termination of Existence.

He slashed the air with his pen.

The thing screamed. Its body unraveled. The black sludge flew apart, scattering into dust. The eye burst.

The dust hung in the air. It didn't fall. It floated, forming a new shape.

A shape that looked like a clipboard.

Dale stepped back.

The dust-clipboard opened.

On the page, written in letters made of ash, was a single word.

**REVIEW.**

A system notification appeared, larger than any he had ever seen. It covered the sky.

**FINAL EXAMINATION COMMENCING.** **SUBJECT: ETHICAL COMPLIANCE.** **QUESTION 1: DID THE EXTERMINATOR KNOW THE COST?**

Dale looked at the dust. He looked at his badge, which was now cracking. The silver was peeling away, revealing the metal beneath. It was rusted.

He looked at his hands. They were shaking.

He picked up his pen, and he wrote his answer.

**YES.**

The ground beneath him cracked. Not from a rift. From weight.

Something huge was rising from the asphalt.

Not a monster.

A building.

It rose up, blocking the sun. A tower of black glass and screaming faces.

Dale looked up. The top of the tower vanished into the clouds.

And on the side of the building, in letters ten feet high, was a sign.

**CERTIFIED EXTERMINATOR AUTHORITY** **HEADQUARTERS**

Dale didn't move. He checked his watch.

Five seconds left on the Level V override, and he looked at the door. The door that had been a stairwell. It was gone.

He looked at the tower.

He looked at his clipboard.

He wrote one last note.

**NOTE TO SELF: DO NOT TRUST THE AUTHORITY.**

The badge shattered.

The Level V effect ended.

The tower didn't stop. It grew faster.

And from the entrance of the tower, a figure walked out.

It wore a suit. It held a clipboard.

It looked exactly like Dale.

It smiled.

"Welcome," it said. "We've been waiting for your report."
