Chapter 306: License Suspended

# Chapter 306

The claw snapped shut inches from Dale's nose. It tasted of rust and ozone, a metallic tang that coated the back of his throat.

Dale didn't flinch.

He didn't step back. He simply leaned his weight onto the clipboard, using the rigid plastic surface to create a fulcrum, and drove the edge of the board into the creature's wrist joint.

Bone cracked. The limb went limp.

"Entry point secured," Dale muttered, his voice flat.

He kicked the severed claw away. It skittered across the asphalt, leaving a trail of black ichor. Around him, the suburban street was silent. The other survivors had scattered when the sky opened, but Dale remained planted in the center of the crater, waiting for the audit.

The air shimmered. Three figures dropped from the rift, landing with a heavy thud that shook the pavement. They wore suits of polished obsidian armor, seamless and reflective. Their helmets were featureless visors, except for a single, glowing vertical slit that tracked movement.

Dale didn't raise his hands. He pulled a pen from his shirt pocket.

"Identification," the lead auditor said. The voice wasn't spoken; it resonated directly inside Dale's skull, vibrating against his teeth. "State your certification level."

"Dale Kowalski.

Level II Technician. License number 44-B-9021.

" Dale wrote the number on his clipboard, tapping the pen twice for emphasis.

"I filed a motion for expedited processing regarding the Ash Pile incident.

You're late."

The auditor stepped forward. The ground cracked under its weight.

"You destroyed the sample.

The Authority requires intact biological matter for cross-referencing with the Field Guide.

Your Thoroughness Rating has been flagged at 3.1.

"

"3.1?" Dale looked up, eyebrows furrowed. "That's below the threshold for residential clearance. I cleared the nest.

I documented the broodmother's entry vector. I sealed the perimeter with industrial-grade adhesive.

That's a 9.2 job.

"

"You used fire," the auditor said. The visor slit narrowed. "Fire is a blunt instrument.

It contaminates the ash.

It destroys the chitinous records. You did not isolate.

You incinerated.

"

"The infestation was spreading," Dale said. "It was moving into the water table. I had three minutes before the contamination reached the municipal supply. I chose containment over documentation because the deadline was physical, not bureaucratic."

"Irrelevant." The auditor raised a gauntleted hand. "The protocol is clear. Identify.

Document. Isolate.

Eliminate.

Verify.

You skipped the middle two steps. The Authority does not tolerate shortcuts.

We do not tolerate technicians who break their tools.

"

Dale felt a cold knot tighten in his stomach. Not fear. Anxiety. The specific, gnawing dread of a man who knows he is right but cannot prove it in the language the other side understands.

"Show me the report," Dale said.

"There is no report." The auditor gestured to the smoking crater. "There is only residue. And now, there is this."

The ground rumbled again. The second set of auditors moved to flank Dale. They didn't draw weapons. They drew scanners—long, rod-like devices that hummed with a low-frequency whine.

"We are here to assess the breach," the lead auditor said. "You have summoned us. You have wasted our time. And you have created a new variable."

Dale looked down at the severed claw. Then he looked at the churning dirt where the creature had emerged.

"It's not a new variable," Dale said. "It's a secondary infestation. Look at the soil composition."

The auditor paused. The humming scanners lowered, pointing at the dirt.

"Dale Kowalski," the voice in his head sharpened. "Do not lecture the Authority on soil science."

"I'm not lecturing. I'm observing." Dale stepped closer, ignoring the defensive stance of the flanking auditors.

He pointed at the black ichor pooling around the claw.

"See the viscosity? It's too thin for *Rattus profundus*. And look at the claw structure.

Three phalanges, not four.

And the wire?" He pointed to the metallic strands woven through the bone. "That's not biological.

That's salvage.

Someone built this.

"

The auditor tilted its head. The visor flickered, data streams scrolling behind the glass.

"Synthetic construct," the auditor said. "Unauthorized modification of dimensional fauna. A violation of Section 4, Paragraph 12: Prohibited Hybridization."

"Exactly," Dale said. He scribbled furiously on his clipboard.

"And if it's synthetic, it doesn't have a nest.

It has a controller.

Or a source."

The auditor remained silent for a long moment. The pressure in the air increased, pressing against Dale's eardrums.

"You are attempting to deflect blame," the auditor said.

"I'm attempting to complete the job," Dale replied. "If I'm liable for the Ash Pile, I'm liable for this, too. Because I'm the only one who noticed the wire.

If I hadn't cut that claw, you'd be standing in a puddle of acid right now. Which protocol were you checking for?

Containment or liability?

"

The lead auditor stepped back. The flanking units lowered their scanners.

"Explain the wire," the auditor commanded.

Dale crouched. He didn't touch the ichor. He used the tip of his pen to lift a strand of the wire from the ground. It sparked against the plastic.

"Copper-core, rubber-insulated. Standard residential wiring. But look at the splice." Dale pointed to a crude twist in the metal.

"Hand-made.

Amateur. Whoever made this thing didn't have a factory.

They had a garage.

And they're hiding it.

"

"Where?" the auditor asked.

Dale stood up. He pointed down the street, toward the cluster of houses that had survived the initial rift event. Toward the house with the pristine lawn and the meticulously maintained hedge maze.

"The Ash Pile was in the backyard of number 402," Dale said. "This emergence point is in the front yard of number 404. The wiring matches the electrical schematic I pulled from the county records for 404. They share a transformer."

The auditor looked at the house. The windows were dark. The curtains were drawn.

"Inspection required," the auditor said.

"Granted," Dale said. He held out his clipboard.

"I have the schematic.

I have the entry vector.

I have the threat assessment. Let me go in.

I know how to handle hybrids. I know how to handle people who think they're smarter than the code.

"

The auditor stared at him. The silence stretched, thin and brittle.

"Your Thoroughness Rating is still 3.1," the auditor said.

"Change it," Dale said. "Or don't.

But if you send in your team, you'll trigger the failsafe.

These things are wired to the grid. If you use brute force, you'll blow the substation. If you blow the substation, you lose power to the containment fields for the next three miles.

That's a Class IV breach.

That's a city-wide lockdown. And that's on your report.

"

The auditor's visor flickered again. A calculation processed behind the glass.

"You are manipulating the metric," the auditor said.

"I am presenting a risk assessment," Dale said. "Which is, by definition, part of the protocol."

The auditor raised a hand. The other two stepped back.

"Proceed," the auditor said. "But we are watching. Any deviation from standard procedure, any destruction of evidence, and your license is revoked. Permanently."

"Understood," Dale said.

He didn't wait for permission to move. He walked toward number 404. The streetlights flickered as he passed under them. The air smelled of wet earth and burning plastic.

At the front door, Dale paused, and he didn't knock. He reached into his tool belt and pulled out a small, rectangular device. A frequency jammer, modified for residential use.

He pressed it against the doorframe.

*Click.*

The electronic lock disengaged. Dale pushed the door open.

The house was dark. The air was stale. Dust motes danced in the beam of his flashlight.

"Hello?" Dale called out. His voice was steady, professional. "Pest Control. Open up."

No answer.

Dale moved through the hallway.

His boots made no sound on the hardwood floor, and he checked the thermostat. It was set to seventy-two degrees.

He checked the air filter. It was clean.

This was a home. A lived-in, maintained, orderly home. Which meant the owner was careful. Which meant they were hiding something deliberate.

He found the kitchen first. Clean counters.

Empty sink.

No food waste. No trash.

"Where are you?" Dale muttered. He opened the cabinet under the sink.

Empty, and he moved to the living room. The television was off. The books on the shelf were arranged by color.

Dale felt a prickling at the base of his neck. Not instinct. Protocol. His peripheral vision flashed a warning.

**SYSTEM ALERT: ANOMALOUS ENERGY SIGNATURE DETECTED.** **SOURCE: BEHIND WALL.** **CLASSIFICATION: UNKNOWN.**

Dale walked to the wall behind the television. He ran his hand along the drywall. It felt cold. Colder than the rest of the house.

He knocked, and the sound was hollow.

"Open the wall," Dale said to the empty room. "I know you're there. I can hear the hum."

A voice came from behind the drywall. Soft, calm, and amused.

"You're persistent, Mr. Kowalski."

Dale froze. "Who's there?"

"Someone who respects the rules," the voice said. "More than you do."

A panel slid open. Not a door. A section of the drywall simply retracted, revealing a hidden passage. Behind it stood a woman.

She was small, wearing a lab coat over a sundress. Her hair was pinned up in a severe bun. She held a clipboard of her own.

"Ms.

Evans," Dale said. He recognized her.

She was the homeowner, and she had complained to him twice about ants in her pantry. He had treated the entry points. He had never seen her this close up.

"You've been watching my inspections," Dale said.

"I've been monitoring your methodology," Ms. Evans said.

She stepped forward.

"You're efficient.

But you're lazy. You treat the symptoms.

You don't treat the cause.

"

"I treat the infestation," Dale said. "The Authority defines the scope."

"The Authority is a bureaucracy," Ms. Evans said.

"They care about points.

About ratings. About keeping the numbers manageable. I care about the truth.

"

She gestured to the dark passage behind her.

"Come in," she said. "I want to show you what you're actually fighting."

Dale looked at his clipboard. The System prompt hovered in his vision.

**OPTION 1: ENGAGE HOSTILE.** **OPTION 2: INVESTIGATE SOURCE.** **PENALTY FOR OPTION 2: IF FALSE, IMMEDIATE REVOCATION OF LICENSE.**

Dale looked at Ms.

Evans. She wasn't holding a weapon.

She was holding a clipboard.

And she was smiling.

"If I go in there," Dale said, "and this is a trap, I lose everything."

"If you don't," Ms. Evans said, "you'll spend the rest of your life cleaning up messes you didn't make. You'll be a janitor for the apocalypse."

Dale stepped forward.

"Show me."

Ms. Evans turned and walked into the darkness. Dale followed.

The passage was narrow. The walls were lined with copper mesh.

Dale recognized the pattern, which was a Faraday cage, but modified.

It didn't block signals; it amplified them.

They walked for a hundred feet. The air grew colder. The hum grew louder.

Ms. Evans stopped at a steel door. She typed a code into the keypad. The door hissed open.

Inside was a laboratory. But not a clean one. It was filled with cages. Hundreds of them.

And inside the cages were things.

Small, twisted creatures. Chimeras. Rat heads with bird wings.

Beetle shells with human teeth. They chattered and hissed, pressing against the bars.

Ms. Evans walked to the center of the room. There was a large, empty cage. A sign on it read: **SUBJECT 7: THE HERALD.**

"Rifts aren't natural disasters," Ms.

Evans said. "They're harvests. The Authority isn't stopping them.

They're farming them.

And they need new stock. They need monsters that haven't been cataloged yet.

Monsters that don't fit the Field Guide.

"

She turned to Dale.

"I'm breeding them. I'm breaking their classification. I'm making them unrecognizable.

Because if they're not in your Guide, you can't kill them. And if you can't kill them, you can't stop them.

And if they can't be stopped, the Authority can't claim victory.

It has to negotiate.

"

Dale stared at the creatures. He looked at his Field Guide entry in his mind. It was blank for these species.

"You're violating the licensing code," Dale said. His voice trembled, just slightly.

"I'm rewriting it," Ms. Evans said. "And you're the only one who's close enough to see it. You're the only one who cares enough to document it."

She picked up a key from the table. She held it out to Dale.

"Kill me," she said. "Take my research.

File it with the Authority. Get your promotion. Or join me.

And help me burn the whole system down.

"

The steel door slid shut behind Dale. The lock engaged with a heavy *thunk*.

Dale looked at the key.

Then he looked at the cages. The creatures were still now. Watching him.

Waiting.

**SYSTEM ALERT: NEW PROTOCOL UNLOCKED.** **NAME: CROSS-CONTAMINATION OVERRIDE.** **DESCRIPTION: You may now deploy protocols against unclassified entities. Warning: This action is illegal under Authority Code 9.**

Dale looked at Ms. Evans. She wasn't smiling anymore. She was waiting.

"If I take that key," Dale said, "do I get a promotion?"

"No," Ms. Evans said. "You get a war."

Dale looked at the key. It was brass. It was heavy. It was the only thing in the room that made sense.

He didn't take it, and he pulled out his clipboard. He wrote *VIOLATION: UNAUTHORIZED GENETIC MODIFICATION. CLASS IX.*

"I'm filing a report," Dale said.

Ms. Evans's face went pale.

"You can't.

You're in the room. You're complicit.

"

"I'm a technician," Dale said. "I document. That's what I do. Even if it kills me."

He turned his back to her. He walked toward the steel door. He pressed the intercom button.

"Auditor," Dale said. "I have found the source of the hybrid infestation.

I am requesting immediate containment.

Do not open the door from the outside.

The subject is hostile.

"

Ms. Evans screamed. It wasn't a sound of anger. It was a sound of terror.

"You fool," she shrieked. "They won't save you. They'll purge you."

The handle of the steel door turned. From the outside.

But the lock held.

Dale waited. He held his breath. He watched the gap beneath the door.

Light spilled in. Then shadow. Then the shadow of the Auditor's visor.

The door slid open, and the Auditor stood there. He wasn't looking at Dale. He was looking at Ms. Evans.

"Subject identified," the Auditor said.

The Auditor raised his hand. A beam of pure white light shot out.

It didn't hit Ms. Evans.

It hit the cage labeled **SUBJECT 7: THE HERALD**.

The cage exploded.

Something massive unfolded from the darkness. It had no eyes. It had too many mouths.

The Auditor stepped back. He looked at Dale.

"You filed the report," the Auditor said. "Good. Now you verify."

The creature lunged at Dale.

Dale raised his clipboard, and he didn't have a weapon. He didn't have a plan. He had a Field Guide entry that was blank, and a System prompt that said *ILLEGAL.*

He slammed the clipboard into the creature's face.

It shrieked. A sound that shattered the glass in the laboratory windows.

Dale's vision went white.

**SYSTEM ALERT: CRITICAL FAILURE.** **LICENSE SUSPENDED.** **AUDIT INITIATED.**

The last thing Dale saw before the darkness took him was Ms. Evans laughing. And the Auditor, standing in the doorway, checking his watch.

When Dale woke, the Auditor was smiling.
