Chapter 337: Pest Not Exterminator

# Chapter 337

The ventilation shaft was screaming.

Not with wind. With teeth.

Dale Kowalski adjusted the seal on his respirator, his gloved fingers slipping against the condensation of the metal duct. The air here tasted of copper and old ozone, a sharp tang that coated the back of his throat. He didn't wipe his face. Protocol dictated that hands stayed sterile until the final decontamination cycle, and Dale was already three violations deep in the current job order.

Seven moved ahead, a blur of immaculate white fabric in the dim emergency lighting. The Auditor's clipboard glowed with a soft, pulsing red light. Seven did not cast a shadow, but the beam of Dale's headlamp slid off Seven's shoulder plates, refracting oddly against the suit's unnatural surface.

"Sector 4, Junction B," Seven said. The voice came from everywhere and nowhere, vibrating in Dale's inner ear. "Structural integrity failing. Containment breach imminent."

"Junction B is a service corridor," Dale replied, checking his clipboard. The paper was blank.

The Authority had replaced it with a holographic interface that hovered just above the lens.

"It's not rated for high-pressure dimensional bleed. That's a code violation. Class A."

"Relevant," Seven said.

Dale ignored the Auditor. He tapped the screen, pulling up the Field Guide entry for *Gutturalis*—the screaming thing currently chewing through the far bulkhead.

He had identified it twenty minutes ago.

A hive-mind entity composed of localized acoustic waves and chitinous shrapnel. It didn't eat flesh.

It ate structure.

It resonated until walls liquefied and support beams snapped.

Dale's job wasn't to kill it. Killing a *Gutturalis* required shattering its core frequency, which usually brought the entire building down on top of the exterminator.

His job was to isolate it.

The screaming grew louder, a jagged, grinding noise that made Dale's molars ache. He stepped over a coil of severed conduit. Sparks showered down, hitting the grate beneath his boots.

"Move," Seven ordered.

Dale stopped. He pointed his flashlight at the floor. The metal grating was warped, the bars twisted into spirals by sonic pressure.

"Look at the stress fractures," Dale said, his voice flat. "The resonance is bouncing off the right wall. If we walk down the center, the standing wave will rupture our eardrums before we reach the nest."

Seven tilted its head.

"Protocol requires direct engagement."

"Protocol assumes a standard acoustic environment," Dale said. He pulled a canister from his belt.

Not spray.

A dampener.

A small, heavy cylinder filled with non-Newtonian gel that absorbed kinetic and sonic energy.

"This is a Class III Acoustic Suppressor.

It's not in the standard loadout for Sector 4. The Authority didn't issue it because the budget cut last quarter.

"

Seven turned. The mask reflected Dale's face, distorted and pale.

"You are hoarding equipment."

"I am utilizing available resources to prevent a total collapse," Dale said. He tossed the canister to Seven.

"Catch.

Throw it against the right wall. The left. Not the center.

"

Seven caught it. The Auditor hesitated, the red ink on its clipboard swirling into a question mark.

"If you don't throw it, the corridor collapses and we all die," Dale said. "And your report on 'Unit 7's inability to follow field technician advice' will get you demoted to janitorial duties."

Seven threw the canister.

It struck the right wall with a dull *thud*. The gel expanded, coating the metal in a thick, grey layer. The screaming faltered. The grinding noise dropped in pitch, becoming a low, wet gurgle.

"Effective," Seven said.

"Of course it is.

It's physics." Dale moved forward, keeping to the left side of the corridor. "Stay on the dead zone.

Don't touch the walls.

The vibration is still traveling through the floor.

"

They reached the intersection. The bulkhead had burst open.

Inside was not a room. It was a cavity in the building's skeleton, a hollow space where the foundation had been eaten away.

Inside the cavity, the *Gutturalis* thrummed.

It looked like a knot of black wire and shattered glass, pulsing with a sickly green light, which was the size of a sedan.

Dale checked his Field Guide.

*Weakness: Harmonic Disruption. Requires precise frequency injection.*

He didn't have a frequency injector. He had a drill.

He unholstered his heavy-duty impact driver, modified with a sonic emitter tip. It was illegal to use non-certified tools in a live containment zone. The cross-contamination penalty for mixing pest control hardware with dimensional weaponry was severe.

"Seven," Dale said. "I'm going to drill into the core."

"Unauthorized method," Seven stated.

"It's the only way." Dale lined up the tip of the drill. His hand was steady. He had drilled into load-bearing walls for fifteen years.

He knew where the stud hit. He knew where the drywall gave way.

He engaged the trigger.

The drill whined, a high-pitched scream that clashed with the monster's own frequency. Dale pushed. The bit bit into the black wire mass.

Sparks flew. Not electrical. Chromatic. Blue and violet bursts of raw dimension energy sprayed out, hitting Dale's hazmat suit.

*ALERT: SUIT INTEGRITY COMPROMISED. SECTOR 4.*

A tear in his left sleeve. Small. Barely an inch.

Dale didn't flinch. He kept drilling. The *Gutturalis* shrieked, a sound that felt like nails being dragged across his brain.

"Pull it out," Seven said. "It is destabilizing."

"Wait for the resonance peak," Dale muttered. He watched the gauge on his drill. The needle jittered wildly.

The monster lashed out. A tendril of black wire whipped around, striking Dale's chest.

He flew backward, slamming into the corridor wall. The impact knocked the breath out of him. His clipboard skittered away, the hologram flickering out.

"Mr. Kowalski!" Seven's voice was sharp. "Status?"

"Code violation," Dale wheezed. He sat up, rubbing his ribs. The suit's inner lining had bruised his skin, but he was intact. "My workspace is compromised."

"Leave it. We are retreating."

"No." Dale grabbed his clipboard.

The screen was cracked, but functional. He looked at the *Gutturalis*. The drill had lodged in the core.

The creature was vibrating violently, its form blurring.

"If I leave it now, it expands," Dale said. "It'll eat the foundation of Sector 4. Then Sector 5."

Seven froze. The Auditor's posture stiffened.

"You are exceeding your certification level."

"I'm exceeding your patience," Dale said. He stood up. He ignored the pain in his ribs. He looked at the drill, still embedded in the monster.

He couldn't pull it out. The vibration would trigger a chain reaction.

But he could overload it.

Dale reached for his belt. He pulled out a secondary battery pack. A high-capacity unit meant for running industrial vacuums. It was rated for continuous power draw.

"Seven," Dale said. "I need you to hold this."

He tossed the battery pack to the Auditor.

Seven caught it, confused.

"What is this?"

"Connect it to the drill's power input. Now."

Seven looked at the drill, then at the battery. The Auditor's hands were perfect, unblemished. They moved with mechanical precision, plugging the battery into the side of the impact driver.

The drill spun up, faster. The hum became a roar.

The *Gutturalis* convulsed. The green light flared, turning blinding white.

"Step back," Dale ordered.

Seven stepped back.

Dale stepped forward. He placed his hands on the drill handle.

The vibration traveled up his arms, shaking his teeth, and he held it. He forced the bit deeper.

The monster's core shattered.

A shockwave of pure sound blasted outward. Dale was thrown backward, sliding across the grated floor. He twisted his shoulder, the joint popping audibly.

The *Gutturalis* dissolved into dust and static. The screaming stopped.

Silence returned to the corridor. Heavy, suffocating silence.

Dale lay on the floor, breathing hard. His suit alarms were blaring.

*SYSTEM ALERT: INFESTATION ELIMINATED.* *PROTOCOL: NON-STANDARD.* *THOROUGHNESS RATING: 6.4.* *PENALTY: EQUIPMENT DAMAGE, SUIT BREACH, UNAUTHORIZED USE OF HIGH-VOLTAGE SOURCE.*

6.4.

Dale closed his eyes. Six point four. He had never dropped below seven. Not once.

He sat up. He checked his Field Guide. The entry for *Gutturalis* was marked *ELIMINATED*. But next to it, a new line appeared.

*CAUSE OF DEATH: MECHANICAL OVERLOAD.* *NOTE: SOURCE ANOMALY DETECTED IN CORE RESIDUE.*

Dale frowned. Anomaly?

He crawled toward the pile of dust where the monster had been. He used his tweezers to pick up a single shard of black wire. It was warm.

He put it under his magnifying lens.

Inside the wire, there was a symbol. Tiny. Etched with microscopic precision.

It was a barcode.

But not a standard barcode. The lines were curved, forming a spiral that hurt to look at.

"Seven," Dale said. He held up the tweezers. "Look at this."

Seven walked over. The Auditor stared at the shard. The red ink on Seven's clipboard stopped moving.

"This is not biological," Seven said.

"No," Dale agreed. "This is manufactured."

Seven leaned closer. The mask's lenses zoomed in, clicking softly.

"Identify," Seven said.

Dale checked his Field Guide. The system couldn't parse the symbol. It flashed a question mark.

"Unknown," Dale said. "But it's new. And it's small."

Seven straightened up. The Auditor turned to Dale, and for the first time, the movement was slow. Deliberate.

"You have found something that is not on the Registry," Seven said.

"It's just a code," Dale said. "A violation code."

"No," Seven said. "It is a license."

Dale blinked. "A license? For what?"

"For the entity that made the monster," Seven said. "This symbol... it is a signature. It means the creature was not a wild infestation. It was deployed."

Deployed.

The word hung in the air.

Dale looked at the dust. He looked at the shattered drill. He looked at his cracked clipboard.

"Deployed by whom?" Dale asked.

Seven did not answer. The Auditor turned and walked away, the red clipboard glowing brighter.

"Seven?" Dale called out.

The Auditor did not stop.

"We are leaving. You are contaminated. Your Field Guide is now flagged."

Dale scrambled to his feet. His shoulder screamed in protest. He grabbed his clipboard.

The screen was black, and he tapped it. Nothing.

He tried to open the Field Guide.

*ACCESS DENIED.*

*REASON: CROSS-CONTAMINATION WITH UNLICENSED DATA.*

Dale stared at the screen. His hands trembled. Not from fear. From rage.

He had solved the problem. He had stopped the collapse. He had found the evidence.

And the Authority had shut him out, and he looked at the retreating back of Unit 7.

"Seven!" Dale shouted. "This is a cover-up!"

Seven paused at the tunnel entrance. The dark swallowed the suit's lower half.

"It is a classification," Seven said. "You are now Subject 004. You are part of the investigation."

"Part of the investigation?" Dale laughed, a dry, rasping sound. "I'm a pest control technician. I'm not a spy. I don't have the clearance."

"You have the instinct," Seven said. "And you have the evidence in your suit."

Dale looked down. The tear in his sleeve. The black dust from the monster was embedded in the fabric. He couldn't remove it without removing the suit.

He was carrying the evidence.

And he was trapped with it.

"Move," Seven said. "The next sector is already burning."

Dale followed. He walked with a limp, his clipboard dead in his hand.

As they moved deeper into the tunnels, the air grew hotter. Smelled of sulfur and burning plastic.

New alerts popped up on Dale's peripheral vision. Not from the Authority. From his suit's internal sensors.

*UNIDENTIFIED BIO-SIGNAL DETECTED.* *MATCH CONFIDENCE: 0%.* *RECOMMENDATION: IMMEDIATE QUARANTINE.*

Dale stopped. He looked at the wall.

Painted on the concrete, in bright orange spray paint, was a symbol.

The same spiral barcode.

And next to it, a message.

*YOU ARE NOT THE EXTERMINATOR.*

Dale touched the paint. It was wet.

Someone was here. Someone had been here minutes ago.

"Seven," Dale whispered.

The Auditor was gone. The tunnel ahead was empty.

Dale was alone, and he looked at the message again.

*YOU ARE NOT THE EXTERMINATOR.*

Then, below it, smaller.

*YOU ARE THE PEST.*
