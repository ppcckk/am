Chapter 355: Keys to the Monster

# Chapter 355

The cold bit into his neck before the cold hit his mind. Dale felt the wetness of the injection site, the sting of the counter-agent mixing with the black ichor leaking from Vane's pores.

It wasn't a fight.

It was a medical emergency, and Dale treated it like one. He didn't scream. He didn't panic.

He checked his watch.

Time of death: 11:42 PM.

Or rather, time of containment: 11:42 PM, and the difference was semantic, but the Authority cared about semantics. The Authority cared about paperwork.

Dale's hands shook so violently the pen tip snapped against the clipboard, leaving a jagged scar across the paper where he'd written *INCINERATION*.

He ignored the leak. He ignored the rain hammering the roof above him, leaking through the shattered skylight in steady, rhythmic buckets that pooled around his boots.

A low hum vibrated through the floorboards. The Authority trucks. They were close. Too close.

Dale looked down at his certification badge. The silver Class II pin was tarnished, dull with grime.

He thought about the cost. *Permanent loss of Certification Level 2.* That meant he was down to Class I.

Back to basic entry-level protocols.

No more high-grade aerosols. No more structural sealants that could hold back a dimensional breach.

Just basic bait stations and entry point identification, which was a downgrade. A demotion.

But it was a choice.

Vane's body twitched. The black veins pulsed, spreading up the neck, creeping toward the jaw.

The worms were still moving inside the chest cavity, writhing against the ribcage like trapped fish.

Dale had to act. Not as a hero. As a technician.

He pressed his thumb against the prompt in his peripheral vision.

**CONFIRM MERGE.**

The world went white.

Not a bright white.

A sterile, hospital-white. The kind that bleaches color out of everything.

Dale felt a tearing sensation, not in his muscles, but in his awareness.

His own Field Guide entry for *Silas Vane* vanished, replaced by a chaotic, scrolling wall of data that wasn't his.

*Symbiotic Link Established.* *Host Identity: Silas Vane.* *Status: Deceased.* *Current Host: Dale Kowalski.* *Note: Cognitive Dissonance Expected. Suppression Protocol Active.*

Dale blinked. The rain sounded louder now.

Distant.

Muffled, like hearing a storm from inside a sealed room, and he could feel the worms. Not in Vane's chest.

In his own. A phantom pressure in his sternum. A heaviness in his left lung.

He stood up. His legs felt heavy.

Wrong.

The balance point of his center of gravity had shifted. He stumbled, catching himself on the edge of the broken workbench.

Outside, headlights swept across the warehouse floor. Red and blue lights flashing in the rain.

"Unit Seven to Command," a voice crackled over a radio. "We have visual on the structure. No movement detected."

Dale straightened his tie, which was crooked. He fixed it.

He picked up the clipboard.

The pen was broken, but he had a spare in his pocket. He uncapped it.

The front doors burst open. Three figures stepped in, clad in the heavy, lead-lined containment suits of the Authority's Rapid Response Division.

Their helmets were opaque, reflecting the dim light of the warehouse.

They carried containment rods, not guns.

Weapons were for heroes. Rods were for exterminators.

The lead figure walked toward Dale. The suit's servos whined with each step.

"Technician Kowalski," the voice came through the helmet's external speakers, distorted by static. "Report status."

Dale didn't look up. He was writing.

*Subject Vane: Contained via experimental symbiotic transfer. Risk of secondary infestation: High. Recommend immediate quarantine of Technician Kowalski.*

He finished the line. Then he looked up.

"Protocol failed," Dale said. His voice sounded strange. Deeper.

The words felt thick in his throat, tasting of copper and old earth.

"The counter-agent was ineffective against the new strain.

I was forced to improvise.

"

The lead officer stopped ten feet away. He tilted his head.

"Improvised?"

"I merged."

The silence that followed was heavy. Rain dripped from the ceiling, hitting a metal bucket nearby with a *plink*.

"You don't merge," the officer said. It wasn't a question. It was a statement of fact, delivered with the flat tone of a man reading a regulation.

"Level II technicians do not have clearance for Symbiotic Transfer.

That is a Class VI privilege. Reserved for containment specialists.

"

Dale held up his badge, and the pin was dark.

"I used a loophole. The system allowed it because the subject was already compromised.

I didn't have a choice.

"

"You always have a choice, Kowalski. You just chose the one that cost you your license."

The officer stepped closer. The containment rod in his hand hummed, a low frequency that made Dale's teeth ache.

"Step away from the body," the officer said.

Dale didn't move. He pointed at Vane's corpse. The black veins had stopped spreading. The worms were still, curled tight within the chest cavity.

"The body is inert," Dale said.

"The symbiosis is stable.

But the host rejection is complete. If you try to remove the parasites now, the necrotic tissue will release the spores.

You'll infect the entire tri-state area.

"

The officer paused. He looked at the body. Then he looked at Dale.

"Spores?"

"Look at the lungs."

The officer approached the body. He used a scanner tool, a handheld device that glowed with pale blue light. He swept it over Vane's chest. The device beeped, a rapid, frantic chirping.

"Massive parasitic load," the officer muttered. "It's dormant. But it's active."

"Because I'm holding it," Dale said.

The officer looked up sharply.

"You're what?"

Dale tapped his chest.

"I'm the nest.

The system merged us.

I'm the containment vessel. If you remove me, or if you try to scan me with anything that penetrates the skin, the symbiosis breaks. The parasites will go wild.

They'll swarm.

And since I'm in Class I now, I don't have the protocols to suppress them.

"

It was a bluff.

Mostly. Dale didn't know if the symbiosis would actually break if he was scanned. But he knew the Authority hated risk.

They hated liability. A Level II technician turning into a biological bomb was a liability nightmare.

The officer's helmet tilted.

"That's a violation of Protocol 7-Alpha. Unsanctioned self-containment."

"It's the only option you have left," Dale said. "Unless you want to burn this whole warehouse down with me inside. Do you have the incineration permit? Because I didn't see one."

The officer looked at the other two units. They shifted uncomfortably. The rain was getting harder. The roof was groaning under the weight of the water.

"We need to verify," the officer said.

"You can verify," Dale said.

"But you can't touch.

Use remote sensors. Visual only.

And do not speak to me in close proximity. The vocal frequencies can trigger agitation in the host strain.

"

It was nonsense. Dale made it up on the spot. But the Authority was a bureaucracy. They followed rules, even invented ones, if they sounded official.

The officer pulled out a tablet. He tapped the screen.

"Unit Seven to Command.

We have a Code Yellow. Possible biological containment issue. Technician Kowalski is claiming host status.

Requesting remote verification and medical escort to Site Four.

"

"Site Four is the isolation ward," one of the other officers whispered. "Kowalski, if you go there, you're not coming back out."

"I'm not planning to," Dale said.

The lead officer looked at Dale. For a second, just a second, the helmet's visor cleared.

Dale saw the eyes behind it.

Tired. Human. Worried.

"You know what this means," the officer said.

"I know what the forms say," Dale replied.

He stepped back from the body. He kept his hands visible.

He kept his breathing steady, and he focused on the sensation in his chest.

The heavy, wet weight of the worms. They were moving.

Slowly. Testing the boundaries of his own lungs.

It hurt. A dull, throbbing ache that radiated from his sternum to his back.

He ignored it.

Pain was data. Pain indicated presence.

The tablet in the officer's hand flashed. Green.

"Command approves remote monitoring," the officer said. "Do not move. A scanner drone is inbound. Stand still."

Dale planted his feet, and he stared at the officer. He didn't blink.

He forced his face into a mask of professional boredom. *I am a technician.

This is a procedure. I am bored by it.*

The drone lowered from the ceiling, a black sphere with rotating eyes. It hovered at eye level with Dale. It scanned his chest. A beam of red light swept over his shirt, then over his skin.

Dale felt it. The symbiosis reacted to the laser.

The worms in his chest tightened.

A spasm ripped through his diaphragm, and he clenched his jaw, holding his breath. He didn't cough.

He didn't wince.

The drone beeped. A single, long tone.

"Host detected," the drone's mechanical voice said. "Source: Internal. Species: *Necro-Parasitic Symbiote, Variant B*. Threat Level: 4."

Variant B.

Dale's heart skipped. He hadn't seen Variant B in the Field Guide. It was new. Or it was hidden.

"Variant B is restricted," the officer said. His voice was tight. "Where did you get it?"

Dale looked at the drone. He looked at the officer.

"I didn't get it," Dale said. "I caught it. From Vane."

"Vane was a Class III handler," the officer said. "He shouldn't have been exposed to Variant B. That's a Rift-Deep contaminant. It doesn't survive in the surface world."

"It does now," Dale said. "I'm the proof."

The officer stepped back. He holstered his containment rod.

He pulled out a new set of cuffs.

Not metal. Heavy, lead-lined polymer.

"Kowalski," the officer said.

"You are now under indefinite detention. You will be transported to Site Four.

You will not be allowed to leave.

You will not be allowed to communicate with anyone outside the medical team. And you will be monitored every second of every day.

"

Dale looked at the cuffs. They were designed to suppress magical resonance.

To block protocols.

If they put them on him, he couldn't use his skills.

He couldn't document. He couldn't exterminate.

He would just be a subject, and he looked at Vane's body. It was beginning to slump. The black veins were receding, pulling back into the chest. The symbiosis was settling.

And then he saw it.

On Vane's hand, half-hidden by the sleeve of his jacket, was a piece of paper. A small, folded square. It had slipped out during the struggle.

Dale didn't move. The drone was still scanning him. The officer was watching him.

But Dale saw the corner of the paper. It had the logo of the Authority.

Not the standard blue shield.

A black star. The Rift Command logo.

Vane had been working for Rift Command. Not the local branch. The central command.

And Dale had just swallowed the evidence. Literally. The symbiosis was digesting Vane's memories, his knowledge. It was pulling it into Dale's Field Guide.

New entries were popping up in Dale's vision. Not from his actions. From the host.

*Entry: Rift Command Liaison.* *Status: Compromised.* *Key Insight: The Rift is not an infestation. It is a harvest.*

Harvest.

The word echoed in Dale's mind. It resonated with the worms. The worms liked the word. They pulsed in time with it.

The officer moved forward with the cuffs.

"Hands behind your back," the officer said.

Dale didn't move.

"There's a violation," Dale said. "Code 12-4. Unlicensed Data Extraction."

The officer froze.

"What?"

"I have access to Rift Command data," Dale said.

His voice was calm.

"Via the symbiosis.

If you cuff me, you sever the link, and the data is lost. But if you let me keep the link, I can give you the coordinates of the Rift's source. The real one.

Not the breach in Ohio. The one feeding it.

"

The officer looked at the other two. They looked at the drone. The drone was still scanning.

"You're bluffing," the officer said.

"Check the logs," Dale said.

"My Field Guide updated ten seconds ago.

It has a new tag. *Rift Command Liaison.* You have to authorize data transfer from a licensed technician if you want to access it. That's Protocol 4-B.

I'm still licensed. Just lower level. But I'm licensed.

"

The officer hesitated. The rain hammered the roof. The drone hovered, waiting.

"Command," the officer spoke into his radio. "Requesting authorization for data extraction from Technician Kowalski. Hostile symbiosis present. Potential intelligence value: High."

Static hissed. Then a voice, cold and distant, came through.

"Deny. Kowalski is contaminated.

Quarantine only.

No data transfer. No access. Contain the asset.

"

The officer looked at Dale. He shrugged.

"Sorry, Kowalski. Orders are orders."

He moved to cuff Dale.

Dale didn't run. He didn't fight. He closed his eyes.

He reached into the symbiosis, and he reached into the black veins that were now part of him. He found the connection to Vane's mind.

The last thoughts.

The last commands.

There was a command there. A kill switch.

Vane had known he was going to die. He had programmed a final action.

Dale pushed the command.

*Execute: Purge.*

The warehouse lights flickered. The drone shrieked, a high-pitched alarm. The containment rods in the officers' hands sparked and died.

Dale opened his eyes. The black veins on his arms glowed.

Not with light.

With absence of light.

A dark, sucking void, and he wasn't just containing the parasites. He was becoming the hive.

The officer took a step back.

"What did you do?"

Dale looked at his hands. His fingers were turning black. The skin was hardening, chitinous plates forming under the epidermis.

He could feel the armor growing. He could feel the strength.

He looked at the officer. He smiled. It was a dry, procedural smile.

"I'm filing a complaint," Dale said. "About the lack of proper PPE in this sector."

The officer raised his hand, but his suit was dead. His tools were dead.

Dale raised his hand.

The black chitin on his fingers shifted, forming a long, sharp spike. A natural weapon. Not a protocol.

A mutation, and the Authority had rules. They had licenses. They had forms.

But they didn't have a rule for what happens when a Level II technician becomes a Class VI threat from the inside out.

The drone fired a taser dart. It hit Dale in the shoulder. He didn't feel it.

He took a step forward.

The officer stepped back.

And then the ground beneath them cracked.

Not from the rain. From below.

Something else was digging up. Something that knew Dale was here. Something that recognized the scent of the harvest.

Dale looked down at the crack. He looked at the glowing black veins on his arm. He looked at the terrified face of the officer.

He checked his Field Guide.

**NEW ENTRY DETECTED:** *Entity: Unknown.* *Classification: Apex.* *Threat Level: 10.* *Note: It is hunting you.*

Dale looked at the hole in the floor. Dark, wet shapes were emerging.

They were moving fast.

Too fast for the Authority's response time. Too fast for the containment team.

He looked at the officer.

"You have a choice," Dale said. "You can try to arrest me. Or you can follow me."

The officer stared at the emerging horrors. He looked at Dale. He looked at the dead tools in his hands.

Dale turned and walked toward the hole, and he didn't run. He walked. He adjusted his tie. He picked up his clipboard.

The officer didn't follow. He stood there, watching.

Dale stepped into the darkness. The black veins pulsed. The worms in his chest sang.

He wasn't exterminating anymore, and he was leading the infestation.

And the Authority, with all its forms and all its rules, had just handed the keys to the monster.

Something was waiting for him in the dark.
