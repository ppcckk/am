Chapter 36

The truck's engine was rough. Fourteen miles of back roads and Dale was already calculating fuel. He kept his eyes on the asphalt. Hands at ten and two. The GPS pinged the address. He hadn't memorized it. He didn't need to. He'd written the inspection report. He knew the house.

He also knew the homeowner had ignored the structural violation.

The notification had come through while he was still on the highway. ACTIVE CAPTURE REQUEST: HIST-0041-A. ASSIGNED: R. VANCE, CLASS II, LICENSE #4471-C. Status: ENGAGED. Location: 2847 Linden Street.

Dale read it twice. Then the timestamp. Vance had been engaged for forty-seven minutes.

Forty-seven minutes. The capture protocol took eleven. A full extermination protocol, top to bottom, took twenty-two. Forty-seven minutes meant Vance wasn't capturing. He was fighting.

He took the exit at Linden Street. Turned onto the residential block. Saw the work van before he saw the house. County logo on the side. Parked at an angle across two driveways. Rear doors open. A crate sat on the pavement. Empty.

The house was 2847. Two-story colonial. Same lawn. Same cracked sidewalk. Same kitchen window where he'd documented the frass trail six weeks ago.

Except the kitchen window was open now. Something was moving behind the curtain.

Dale parked straight in the driveway. Engine off. Keys out. He grabbed his clipboard from the passenger seat. Field Guide open to the HIST-0041-A entry. Read his own notes.

Rattus profundus. Dimensional Vermin. Primary entry. Class 1. Behavioral notes: burrowing, territorial, nocturnal. Weakness identified: lateral mandible joint, vulnerable during feeding cycle. Thoroughness rating: 8.7. Flagged.

He closed the Guide. Walked toward the house.

The front door was open. Not forced. Just open. Dale stepped inside and did what he always did. He looked at the floor.

Track marks. Fresh. Leading from the kitchen to the basement door. The basement door was closed. He checked the gap underneath. No light. No sound.

He moved to the kitchen. The counter was intact. The stove was off. The refrigerator was running. Normal domestic sounds. The kind that meant the house was still occupied.

Then he heard it. From the basement. A wet, rhythmic sound. Like something pulling itself across concrete.

Dale pulled out his phone. Opened the Authority's live engagement feed. He wasn't supposed to have access to other technicians' feeds, but the portal had a bug. Or maybe not a bug. Maybe a feature. He'd noticed it three weeks ago. The feed showed real-time engagement data for any active request in your service area.

R. Vance. Class II. Engagement type: COMBAT. Duration: 52 minutes. Treatment Points earned: 0.

Zero. Vance was hitting the creature and earning nothing. He hadn't documented. He hadn't isolated. He hadn't identified a weakness. He was just swinging.

Dale closed the feed. Went to the basement door. Opened it.

The basement was unfinished. Concrete floor. Exposed joists. The same layout he'd mapped in his report. He'd noted a crack in the foundation wall. Six inches wide. Running diagonally from the floor to the joist line. He'd written it up as a structural entry point. Violation code K-7. Recommended: epoxy seal and drainage correction.

The crack was still there. The homeowner had done nothing. And the creature had been using it.

Dale could see the wear pattern on the concrete floor. A channel worn into the surface, maybe three feet wide, running from the crack to the basement's center. The creature had been traveling the same path repeatedly. Feeding. Returning. A foraging corridor.

He pulled out his pen. Wrote on his clipboard. "Secondary entry point active. K-7 violation unaddressed. Foraging corridor established. Estimated duration of re-infestation: four to six weeks."

He looked up.

Vance was in the far corner of the basement. Big man. Mid-forties. Built like a high school football player who'd never stopped playing. He had a combat protocol active. Some kind of kinetic pulse he was firing at the creature in bursts. The creature was pinned against the wall. The same wall where the crack was.

The creature was wrong.

Dale saw it immediately. Same species. Same body plan. Same mandible structure. But bigger. Maybe thirty percent larger than the primary entry he'd documented. And its behavior was different. It wasn't fleeing. It wasn't burrowing. It was holding position. Facing Vance. Waiting.

Dale's eyes went to the crack in the wall. There was something else there. A second creature. Smaller. Partially inside the crack. Not attacking. Watching.

A juvenile.

Dale opened his Field Guide. Flipped to the HIST-0041-A entry. Read his own weakness notation. Lateral mandible joint, vulnerable during feeding cycle.

The creature in front of Vance wasn't feeding. It was defending. The weakness wasn't active.

But the juvenile in the crack was feeding. Dale could see the frass trail running from its mouth to the crack's edge. It was eating the concrete. Or something in the concrete.

He made a decision.

He pulled out his phone. Opened the Field Guide editor. Created a new entry.

SPECIES DESIGNATION: HIST-0041-A-2. Subspecies. Behavioral variant.

The System pinged.

NEW FIELD GUIDE ENTRY CREATED: HIST-0041-A-2. Status: INCOMPLETE. Thoroughness Rating: 0.0. Protocol access: NONE.

Good. Now he needed to document. Fast.

He watched the juvenile. Watched its feeding pattern. It was eating calcium deposits in the concrete. The crack was its food source. It wasn't a burrower. It was a consumer. It was eating the foundation.

Dale wrote. "Subspecies designation: HIST-0041-A-2. Primary subspecies of Rattus profundus. Behavioral divergence: non-burrowing, calcium-consumptive, territorial defense of feeding site. Size: 1.3x primary. Mandible structure: reinforced, adapted for mineral consumption. Weakness: posterior thoracic segment exposed during consumption cycle."

The System pinged.

FIELD GUIDE UPDATE: HIST-0041-A-2. Weakness identified. Targeted damage bonus available. Thoroughness Rating: 4.2.

Four point two. Not enough.

He kept watching. Kept writing.

"Juvenile exhibits pack behavior. Primary individual acts as sentinel. Juvenile acts as consumer. Symbiotic relationship. Primary defends feeding site. Juvenile processes structural material. Feeding cycle duration: approximately ninety seconds per calcium deposit. Post-feeding vulnerability window: twelve seconds."

Thoroughness Rating: 6.1.

Vance was still fighting. The kinetic pulse was doing nothing. The creature was just standing there. Taking hits. Not moving.

Dale walked forward.

"Hey." Flat. Like he was addressing a homeowner who'd left the back gate open. "You're using a combat protocol on a calcium-consumptive subspecies. It's not going to work. The thoracic plating is mineralized. You're hitting armor."

Vance turned. Face red. Sweating. "What the hell are you doing here?"

"I have a capture request for this address."

"I got here first."

"I can see that." Dale pointed at the creature. "You're not even close. It's in a defensive posture. Your protocol targets the lateral mandible joint. That's the primary subspecies weakness. This thing's mandibles are reinforced for mineral consumption. Your protocol is hitting the wrong joint."

Vance stared at him. "How do you know that?"

"Because I inspected this house six weeks ago. I documented the primary species. And this thing has been eating the foundation ever since. I can see the consumption pattern." Dale held up his clipboard. "I have a subspecies entry. I've identified the correct weakness. If you stop firing, I can neutralize it in under a minute."

Vance didn't stop firing.

Dale watched him waste another ninety seconds. The creature didn't flinch. The kinetic pulse just scattered calcium dust off its plating.

Dale stopped waiting.

He activated his capture protocol. Not on the primary. On the subspecies. The new entry. HIST-0041-A-2. Thoroughness Rating: 6.8. The capture protocol required 9.0.

He couldn't use it. Not yet.

But the targeted damage bonus worked at any rating. The bonus didn't require a protocol threshold. It required only that the weakness was documented before combat.

Dale had documented it.

He activated his Class II suppression protocol. Standard. Not targeted. Not optimized. But he aimed it at the posterior thoracic segment. The spot he'd identified in his new entry.

The System highlighted the target. BONUS ACTIVE. HIST-0041-A-2. Targeted damage: +34%.

The suppression pulse hit the juvenile's exposed segment. It went rigid. Then limp

It went limp.

Dale logged the kill. Suppression confirmed. He pulled out his Field Guide and checked the entry. HIST-0041-A-2. Juvenile. Thoracic plating mineralized at the anterior segment, soft at the posterior. He'd seen the consumption pattern on the foundation six weeks ago and filed it under "structural degradation, probable subterranean feeding." He'd written a recommendation for calcium sulfate treatment. Nobody had called back.

Now the System had a use for that note.

He knelt beside the juvenile and photographed the specimen. Standard documentation. Size, posture, plating coloration, evidence of mineral consumption on the mandibles. He noted the absence of a pheromone gland on the ventral surface. That was new. He added it to the entry.

Vance stopped firing. He stared at the body. Then at Dale. Then at his own hands, which were shaking.

"You killed it."

"I suppressed it. There's a difference. Suppression means it's recoverable for specimen analysis." Dale pulled a containment bag from his kit. "You need to file a cross-contamination report. You deployed a lateral mandible protocol against a mineral-consumption subspecies. That's a 40% debuff on your next three engagements and a Field Guide contamination flag for twenty-four hours."

"I don't have a Field Guide."

"You will. The Authority will issue one after your first documented kill. You don't have one. Which means you didn't identify this creature before you engaged it." Dale sealed the bag. "That's a zero-point kill. You wasted ninety seconds and you contaminated your future entries. I'm going to note that in my report."

Vance's face went from red to white.

Dale stood. He looked at the house. The front door was open. The kitchen light was on. He'd been here six weeks ago. He'd written up the foundation damage, the moisture intrusion, the entry point at the south crawlspace. He'd recommended sealing.

He hadn't recommended this.

His clipboard buzzed. Not a notification. A correction. The Authority was updating the species classification. HIST-0041-A-2 wasn't a juvenile. The entry was being reclassified. The pheromone gland wasn't absent. It was internal. The creature was a sentinel.

Sentinels don't operate alone.

Dale looked at the house again. The kitchen light flickered.

His clipboard buzzed again. A new entry was populating. The Authority was writing it in real time. He could see the letters forming as something moved behind the walls of the house.

HIST-0041-A-3.

The entry wasn't finished. The Authority hadn't seen it before.

The floor of the kitchen collapsed inward.
