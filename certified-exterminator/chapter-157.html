Chapter 157: Accepting Liability

# Chapter 157

The nozzle sparked.

A thin stream of translucent vapor arced toward Dale's chest.

He didn't dodge. He didn't block. He stepped sideways, just enough to let the spray hit the clipboard.

The wood hissed. The plastic casing bubbled.

Vane frowned. "Target acquired."

"Denied," Dale said.

He pointed his pen at the floor.

"Look at the substrate. You're applying a Class-4 solvent to a Class-2 bio-matrix without pre-treatment neutralization."

Vane paused. The nozzle hovered an inch from Dale's sternum.

"The Authority doesn't care about protocol," Vane said. "It cares about clearance."

"Protocol is the law," Dale replied. His voice was flat, bored, like he was reading a lease agreement.

"If you deploy a solvent without verifying the species match, you trigger a Cross-Contamination Event.

Check your Field Guide. Page 412. Section C, Paragraph 3.

"

Vane didn't look down. His eyes stayed locked on Dale's.

"I know the rules," Vane said. "I wrote them."

A wet, chittering sound erupted from the ceiling vents.

Gray dust drifted down, coating the briefcase.

Dale's peripheral vision flickered.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** *Unlicensed Entity Detected. Classification: Unknown. Threat Level: Critical.*

The ductwork groaned. A section of metal grating bent outward.

Something poked through.

A leg. Long, jointed, and black.

Not insectoid.

Arachnid, but wrong. Too many joints. Too many eyes clustered in a single, wet mass.

Vane turned. "Stand down, Kowalski. I will handle this."

"You can't," Dale said.

Vane raised the nozzle.

"I am authorized."

"You are authorized to exterminate," Dale corrected. "This isn't a pest.

It's a structural compromise.

If you spray, you kill it.

If you kill it before documenting the entry vector, you lose the Thoroughness Score. And if you lose the Score, you lose the License.

"

Vane hesitated. That half-second was enough.

Dale dropped the clipboard, and he grabbed the trash bag from the corner. The one he'd used earlier for the sealed woman's containment. It was heavy with black fluid.

Vane fired.

The nozzle discharged a concentrated jet of corrosive gas.

Dale spun the bag into the path.

The gas hit the plastic. The bag dissolved instantly, but the fluid inside splashed outward, coating the floor in a slick, viscous layer.

Vane skidded. He slammed a hand against the wall to keep his balance. The gray suit soaked up the black liquid.

The creature in the vents shrieked. A high-frequency frequency that made Dale's teeth ache.

It dropped.

Not one. Several.

They hit the wet floor with a slap. Black, multi-legged things. Size of large dogs.

No fur. Smooth, chitinous shells that reflected the fluorescent lights.

**FIELD GUIDE UPDATE:** *Species: Rift-Walker (Juvenile). Weakness: Moisture-Dependent Respiration.*

Dale looked at the guide. The entry populated rapidly.

*Note: Juveniles rely on ambient humidity to cycle oxygen. Desiccation kills them in seconds.*

He looked at Vane. The agent was struggling to stand. The black fluid was burning through his shoe leather.

"Vane," Dale shouted. "Your suit is compromised. You're a bio-hazard."

Vane gritted his teeth. He raised the nozzle again. Aimed at the nearest Rift-Walker.

"Stop!" Dale yelled.

Vane pulled the trigger.

The jet hit the creature. The monster exploded into a cloud of black mist and ichor.

The mist hit the walls. The paint bubbled.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** *Cross-Contamination Penalty Applied. -40% Effectiveness for all future treatments. Field Guide Entry Corrupted.*

Dale cursed. A real, guttural sound.

"Idiot," he muttered.

He didn't have time to argue. The remaining juveniles were swarming. They moved fast, legs skittering across the wet floor, seeking dry air, seeking heat.

They sought Vane.

Vane stumbled back, firing wildly. Each shot wasted. Each shot added to the mess. The room was becoming a hazard zone.

Dale scanned the room, and he needed a desiccant.

The HVAC unit in the corner was humming. The vents were open. The cold air was gone, replaced by the humid breath of the Rift-Walkers.

But the furnace was still on.

Dale ran, and he didn't run toward the monsters. He ran toward the utility panel.

Vane shouted something. A warning? A command?

Dale ignored it. He ripped the panel cover off. His fingers flew over the controls.

*Override: Damper Position. Full Open.*

*Override: Fan Speed. Max.*

*Override: Heat Exchanger. Bypass Safety.*

**SYSTEM ALERT:** *Unauthorized Modification Detected. License Revocation Pending.*

"Shut up," Dale said to the floating text.

He hit the manual crank.

The furnace roared to life. Not with a gentle hum, but with a violent, shaking churn.

Hot, dry air blasted out of the ceiling vents.

The Rift-Walkers froze.

Their legs stopped moving. Their shells cracked.

One by one, they slumped.

The moisture in the air was sucked away in seconds. The creatures dried out like old leather.

Dale stood by the panel, breathing hard. His hands were shaking.

He pulled out his pen. He wrote on a scrap of paper.

*Time of intervention: 14:02.* *Method: Environmental Control.* *Target: Rift-Walker Juveniles.* *Result: Cessation of movement.*

He turned to Vane, and the agent was on his knees. The black fluid had eaten through his right sleeve. His skin underneath was blistered.

Vane looked up. His face was pale.

"You broke the rules," Vane said.

"I saved the job," Dale said.

"You used a Class-3 thermal spike on a Class-1 infestation. That's excessive force."

"It was effective."

"It was sloppy."

Dale walked over. He didn't look at Vane. He looked at the dead creatures.

He walked to the first one. It was curled up, legs tucked under its body.

Dale crouched. He took a sample jar from his belt. He scraped a piece of the chitin into the jar.

He sealed it, and he labeled it.

Then he walked to the next one.

He repeated the process.

Vane stood up.

He swayed. "Kowalski. You need to leave.

Now."

"I'm finishing the documentation," Dale said.

"The hearing is over. You're suspended."

"I'm not suspended until I sign the termination form. And you're too injured to sign it."

Vane glared. He tried to raise the nozzle. His hand trembled.

Dale pointed at the nozzle.

"You're out of propellant. I heard the pressure drop when you fired the last shot. You're bluffing."

Vane looked at the nozzle. He looked at Dale.

He lowered the weapon.

"Fine," Vane said. "Write your report.

But know this.

The Audit Division doesn't forgive protocol breaches. We hunt them.

"

"We hunt pests," Dale corrected.

"You're not a pest," Vane said. "You're a liability."

He turned and walked to the door. He didn't look back.

As soon as the door closed, Dale's vision blurred.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** *License Status: Suspended. Access to Field Guide: Limited. Access to Treatment Points: Locked.*

The text remained.

Dale sat on the floor. He opened his clipboard.

He stared at the empty page, and he didn't have Treatment Points. He didn't have a valid license. He didn't have the Field Guide.

But he had the sample jar.

He held it up to the light. The chitin inside was dark, brittle.

He pulled out his pen. He wrote a new entry in his personal notebook. Not the System's Field Guide. His own.

*Rift-Walker Juvenile.* *Weakness: Desiccation.* *Note: Adults may have higher tolerance. Do not assume linear scaling.*

He circled the word *Adults*.

Because he remembered the sound.

Not just the juveniles.

The wet slithering in the ducts, which had continued after the furnace started.

Dale stood up. He walked to the vent.

He shone his flashlight into the ductwork.

The heat had killed the juveniles. But further back, deeper in the shadows, something was moving.

Not a single leg.

A cluster.

A nest.

And it was growing, and he could hear the clicking. The wet, rhythmic clicking of chitin against metal.

It wasn't retreating.

It was advancing.

Dale checked his watch.

14:05.

The Authority's suspension notice pulsed in his vision.

**NOTICE:** *Effective immediately. All active protocols disabled. Report to nearest Authority Office for tribunal proceedings.*

Dale looked at the vent, and he looked at the nozzle Vane had left behind. It was leaking fluid on the floor.

He picked it up.

It was empty. Useless.

But the nozzle tip was still attached, and he ripped the hose from the wall.

He connected the nozzle to the main gas line.

The gas line was natural gas.

Dale held his breath.

If he opened the valve, the gas would flood the duct.

If he sparked it, the nest would burn.

But the building would burn, and he looked at the other survivors. The tenants. They were outside, behind the police tape.

He couldn't burn the building.

He looked at the Field Guide. It was blank.

He looked at his personal notes.

*Desiccation.*

He needed dry air, and the furnace was running. But the damper was open.

He ran back to the panel.

He closed the damper halfway.

The heat trapped in the house rose.

The Rift-Walkers in the ducts paniced. The clicking sped up.

They were trying to escape the heat, and they were trying to get to the open vents.

The only open vent was the one Dale had closed.

They would try to break through the other vents.

There were six other vents in the house.

If they broke out, they would be in the living room.

In the kitchen.

Where the tenants were.

Dale ran to the kitchen, and he grabbed a bag of salt.

He ran to the hallway.

He poured the salt into the vent grates.

It wasn't enough.

It was a drop in the ocean.

But it was all he had, and he heard a crash from the ceiling.

One of the vents popped out.

A Rift-Walker fell.

It was bigger than the juveniles.

Its legs unfolded. It stood six feet tall.

It looked at Dale, which had no eyes. Just sensors.

It tasted the air.

It tasted the salt.

It recoiled.

Dale threw the rest of the salt at it.

The creature hissed. It stepped back.

Then, from the other end of the hall, another vent popped.

Then another.

Dale stood in the center of the hallway, and he had no license.

He had no points.

He had no protection.

He had a bag of salt and a clipboard.

The creature took a step forward.

Dale didn't move, and he tapped his pen on the clipboard.

"Violation," he said. "Unauthorized entry into residential zone."

The creature paused.

It tilted its head.

It seemed to understand.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** *Hostile Actor Detected. No Protocol Available. Manual Override Required.*

A new window popped up.

**MANUAL OVERRIDE:** *Accept Liability? (Yes/No)*

*Warning: Accepting liability for unlicensed extermination results in permanent license revocation. But immediate execution of emergency protocols is permitted.*

Dale looked at the creature, and he looked at the other vents.

The tenants were screaming outside.

He tapped the pen.

"Yes."
