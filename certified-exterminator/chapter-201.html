Chapter 201: Unlicensed Suicide Mission

# Chapter 201

His knuckles were white on the elevator button. Dale stared at the empty space where the wood and paper had been. His hand would not open all the way. The elevator doors were shut. The reflection was gone. The air in the lobby smelled of ozone and burnt hair, a scent that bypassed his olfactory nerves and hit the back of his throat like a warning label.

A hand clamped onto his shoulder. Hard.

"Step away from the doors, Dale."

Marcus. The union rep. He wore his safety vest over his armor, the reflective stripes peeling off in the humidity. His eyes were wide, white-rimmed, and fixed on Dale's face.

Dale didn't move. He looked down at his hands. They were shaking. Not from fear, but from the sudden, violent absence of the tool he had relied on for two hundred chapters.

"Did you see it?" Dale asked. His voice was flat. Procedural.

"See what?"

"The reclassification. The entry for *Reality*. Status: Unlicensed."

Marcus blinked. He looked at the elevator, then back at Dale.

"You're hallucinating.

The stress load is too high. You need a break.

"

"I need a new clipboard."

"You need a medic."

"I need a form 114-B.

Reclassification of Ambient Dimensional Hazards.

" Dale turned, scanning the lobby. Desks were overturned.

Papers floated in the air, caught in a static current that made the hair on his arms stand up.

"Who manages the archives here?"

"Dale, listen to me." Marcus stepped closer, blocking Dale's path to the stairwell. "The Authority isn't responding.

The server link is dead.

No points.

No protocols. If you walk out that door, you're unshielded.

You're naked in the field.

"

"Then I'm working without certification," Dale said. "Which is a violation of Section 4, Paragraph C."

"Which is a death sentence."

"Is it?" Dale tilted his head. "Or is it a loophole?"

Marcus frowned. The confusion on his face was a crack in the panic.

"If the Rift is no longer a monster," Dale said, pointing at the elevator, "then it is not an infestation. Infestations require exterminators. Disasters require emergency responders."

"Which we are not."

"We are licensed technicians," Dale corrected. "And technicians respond to structural failures.

The building is groaning, and the foundation is shifting. That's a structural failure. That's a code violation.

"

Marcus stared at him.

"You're treating the apocalypse like a bad roof?"

"I'm treating it like a liability issue." Dale looked around the lobby. "Where is Yura?"

"Gone. She ran into the maintenance shafts. Before the doors closed."

"Did she take anything?"

"Her badge. And the keycard to the sub-basement."

Dale nodded. That was smart. Yura always followed protocol, even when breaking it.

"We need to go down," Dale said.

"Down? To the sewers? With no Field Guide?"

"No," Dale said.

"To the sub-basement. The boiler room.

There's a manual archive there.

Paper records. Pre-digital.

"

Marcus looked at him like he had lost his mind.

"Paper?

Dale, there are Class-VII horrors in the walls, and they eat concrete.

They drink electricity. Paper won't stop them.

"

"I don't need to stop them," Dale said. "I need to define them. If the digital Field Guide is wiped, I have to rebuild it.

Manually. By observation.

By documentation.

"

"That's suicide."

"That's bureaucracy."

Dale turned and walked toward the stairwell. He moved with the stiff, deliberate gait of a man carrying an invisible weight. His feet found the cracks in the tile, avoiding the weak points.

Old habits died hard.

Even without the System, his body remembered the geometry of safety.

Behind him, Marcus cursed. A loud, wet sound. Then the heavy thud of boots following.

Dale didn't look back. He knew Marcus would follow. Union loyalty was the only thing stronger than fear.

The stairwell was dark.

The emergency lights flickered, casting long, jagged shadows that danced on the concrete walls. The air grew colder. A smell rose from below.

Sulfur. Rotten eggs.

And beneath that, the coppery tang of blood.

Dale paused on the landing between floors 12 and 11.

"Marcus," he said.

"What?"

"Do you hear that?"

Marcus stopped. He listened. A low hum. Vibrating through the railing.

"Is that the Rift?"

"No," Dale said. "That's resonance. The building is singing."

"The Rift is opening?"

"No.

The Rift is *settling*.

" Dale placed his hand on the railing. The metal was warm.

"When a structure is reclassified, the load-bearing elements shift. The Authority didn't erase the monsters. It erased their *category*.

Which means they're still here. But they're not monsters anymore.

"

"Then what are they?"

"Features," Dale said. "They're part of the environment. Like mold. Like asbestos."

Marcus laughed, which was a brittle, jagged sound.

"So we're just living with it? That's your big plan?

Coexist with the things that ate Mrs.

Gable?"

"No," Dale said. "We're managing it. There's a difference."

They continued down.

Floor 10. Floor 9. The hum grew louder.

Dust fell from the ceiling in small, gray clouds.

On floor 8, they encountered the first entity, which was not a rat.

It was not a broodmother.

It was a shape made of static and shadow, clinging to the ceiling. It had no eyes.

No mouth. Just a swirling vortex of darkness where a face should be.

Marcus raised his rifle.

"Target acquired."

"Don't shoot," Dale said.

"It's hostile."

"It's static," Dale said. "Look at its edges. It's not moving. It's vibrating."

"It's looking at us."

"It's not looking. It's *resonating*." Dale took a step closer.

The entity pulsed.

A wave of nausea rolled off it, hitting Dale in the gut. He didn't flinch. He watched the frequency of the pulse.

Slow. Steady. Like a heartbeat.

"It's a harmonic interference," Dale said. "The building's natural frequency changed when the definition changed. This thing is just... noise."

"Then why is it killing people?"

"Because we're reacting to it," Dale said.

"Fear is a feedback loop. You shoot it, it gets louder.

You run, it chases.

You ignore it, it fades.

"

Dale took off his hazmat gloves.

Marcus stared.

"What are you doing?"

"Testing the hypothesis."

Dale walked directly under the entity. He stood still. He breathed. He let the nausea wash over him, acknowledging the symptom but refusing to treat it as an injury.

The entity hovered above him. It spun faster. The air around Dale's head warped.

Marcus aimed. His finger tightened on the trigger.

"Wait," Dale whispered.

The entity dipped lower. A tendril of shadow reached down, brushing against Dale's hair.

Dale didn't move. He didn't blink. He looked up, not at the entity, but through it. At the ceiling.

"Marcus," Dale said. "Shoot the sprinkler pipe."

"What?"

"Shoot the pipe. Now."

Marcus hesitated. The entity touched his ear. Dale felt a cold chill, a whisper of pure void.

"Shoot it!" Dale shouted.

Marcus fired.

The bullet hit the overhead pipe. Water exploded outward, spraying the lobby and the stairwell. The water hit the entity.

The shadow hissed. It didn't burn.

It *condensed*.

The static coalesced into droplets of black liquid, which dripped to the floor.

The hum stopped. The silence was absolute.

Dale wiped water from his face. He looked at the puddle. It was just water.

Dark, oily water. But water.

"See?" Dale said. "It's not a monster. It's a leak."

Marcus lowered his rifle. His hands were shaking.

"How did you know?"

"Physics," Dale said.

"Static electricity grounds when it touches a conductor. Water is a conductor. The entity was trying to ground itself.

It wasn't attacking.

It was trying to discharge.

"

"So we just... splash it?"

"Sometimes.

Depends on the material.

" Dale picked up a wet piece of debris. "But don't get comfortable. That was Class-2.

Next one might be Class-5. And they won't be made of static.

"

They moved down. The lobby was flooded now, ankle-deep. The water reflected the flickering lights, making it look like the floor was made of broken mirrors.

At the bottom of the stairwell, the door to the sub-basement was locked. A heavy steel door with a keycard reader. The light on the reader was red.

"Yura took the card," Marcus said.

"She didn't take it," Dale said. He ran his hand over the keypad. "She hacked it."

"How?"

"She changed the access level. If the Rift is no longer a threat zone, then this area is no longer restricted. It's a storage room."

Dale pulled out his pen. He held it against the sensor. Nothing happened.

He pulled out his phone, and the screen was cracked, but it still worked.

He opened the calculator. He typed in the date.

Then he multiplied it by the building's square footage.

Marcus watched him.

"Are you doing math?"

"I'm generating a password," Dale said. "The Authority's old system used date-times as temporary overrides for manual entry. Yura knew that. She left the backdoor open for us."

Dale typed the number into the keypad.

*Beep.*

The light turned green. The door clicked open.

Inside was a small room. Dusty.

Shelves lined the walls, filled with binders.

Yellowed paper. Old reports.

Dale walked in. He ran his finger along the spine of a binder labeled *1998-2002: Minor Breaches, Zone 4*.

He pulled it down. He opened it.

Inside was a hand-drawn map of the sewer system. And next to it, a drawing of a creature. It looked like a centipede, but made of stone.

Dale stared at the drawing.

"Marcus," he said. "Bring me the next one."

Marcus grabbed a binder from the shelf. *2003-2007: Containment Failures.*

Dale opened it. Another drawing. A bird, but with wings made of fire.

"This is the old Field Guide," Dale said. "Before the Authority took over. Before the System."

"It's just drawings," Marcus said. "It doesn't give us stats. It doesn't give us points."

"It gives us context," Dale said.

He flipped through the pages.

"Look at the notes.

'Avoid direct eye contact.

Use reflective surfaces.' 'Highly susceptible to sonic disruption.'

"

Dale looked up.

"The System stripped us of our knowledge.

It gave us power, but it took away our understanding.

That's why we're failing.

We're fighting blindly.

"

"So what? We read old books?"

"We build a new guide," Dale said. He grabbed a blank notebook from the desk. He grabbed a pen.

"We document everything. We identify the weaknesses.

We write it down.

We make it *official*.

"

Marcus looked at the door. A sound came from the hallway. A scraping noise. Like stone on stone.

Something was coming.

Dale closed the binder. He tucked the notebook into his vest.

"Ready?" Dale asked.

"No," Marcus said.

"Good."

They stepped out into the flooded lobby. The water was rising. Waist-deep now. The static entity was gone, but the air was thick with tension.

From the east corridor, a shape emerged, which was large. Blocky.

Made of jagged rocks that shifted and ground against each other.

It had no head. Just a glowing red core in the center of its chest.

It didn't look like a monster. It looked like a boulder with legs.

Marcus raised his rifle.

Dale put a hand on his arm.

"Don't shoot the core," Dale said.

"It's glowing. It's vulnerable."

"It's hot," Dale said. "Look at the steam. It's geothermal."

Dale walked forward. The water rippled around his legs.

The rock creature stopped.

It turned toward him.

The red core pulsed.

Dale didn't run. He walked until he was five feet away. He looked at the creature's feet. They were fused to the floor by magma.

"Marcus," Dale said. "Pour water on its feet."

"What?"

"Water. The creature is holding itself together with heat. Cool the base, and the structure fails."

Marcus splashed water from his canteen onto the creature's feet.

The steam hissed. The rock cracked. A spiderweb of fissures spread across the legs.

The creature roared. A sound like grinding tectonic plates. It lunged.

Dale dodged. He slipped on the wet tile, sliding backward. He didn't panic. He analyzed.

"It's fast, but clumsy," Dale said. "Center of gravity is high. Trip it."

Marcus aimed. He shot at the ankle joint. The bullet ricocheted off the stone.

"I can't penetrate it!" Marcus yelled.

"You don't need to penetrate it," Dale shouted. "You need to destabilize it. Hit the knee."

Marcus fired. One shot. The rock knee shattered.

The creature toppled. It crashed into the wall, taking out a section of plaster and drywall. The red core flickered. It dimmed.

It wasn't dead. It was just... stopped.

Dale walked over. He looked at the creature. He pulled out his notebook.

He wrote.

*SUBJECT: Lithic Construct (Class III).* *WEAKNESS: Thermal shock.* *METHOD: Cool extremities. Target structural joints.* *NOTES: Requires heavy water supply. Do not engage in dry environments.*

He wrote the entry. He underlined it.

When he finished, a chime sounded in his head. Not the digital chime of the System. A different sound. A physical bell.

A notification appeared in his vision. Not in blue. In yellow.

*FIELD GUIDE UPDATED: MANUAL OVERRIDE ACCEPTED.* *NEW PROTOCOL UNLOCKED: THERMAL SHOCK.* *TREATMENT POINTS: 0.* *THOROUGHNESS RATING: 10.0.*

Dale looked at Marcus.

"It didn't give me points," Dale said.

"But it gave you a new ability?"

"It gave me permission," Dale said. "It acknowledged the data."

Marcus looked at the dead creature.

"So we can do this again?"

"We can do this forever," Dale said. "As long as we document it."

He turned to the elevator. The doors were still closed. But now, there was a keypad next to them.

Dale walked to it. He typed in the code.

The doors opened.

Inside was not a mirror.

Inside was a room. A small, square room. And in the center of the room stood a man.

He wore a suit. Perfectly tailored. Black. No tie.

He was holding a briefcase.

The man looked at Dale. He smiled. It was a thin, practiced smile.

"Mr. Kowalski," the man said. "You've been busy."

Dale stepped into the elevator. He didn't look at Marcus. He looked at the man.

"Who are you?"

"I am the Auditor," the man said. "I am here to audit your work."

"You can't," Dale said. "The System is down."

"The System is not down," the man said. "It is paused. We are reviewing your certification."

Dale felt a cold sweat break out on his back. This was worse than the mirror. The mirror was a warning. This was an inspection.

"What for?" Dale asked.

"For unauthorized reclassification," the Auditor said. He opened his briefcase. Inside were files.

Hundreds of them.

"You have redefined the scope of your license, Dale.

You have declared a dimensional breach to be a structural hazard.

This is outside your jurisdiction.

"

"I saved lives," Dale said.

"You violated protocol," the Auditor said. "Protocol states that dimensional breaches are to be contained, not managed. You managed them. You treated them like pests."

"Because they are pests."

The Auditor closed the briefcase.

"Then you are unfit for duty," the Auditor said. "Your license is revoked."

The doors closed.

Dale lunged forward. He jammed his hand between the doors.

"Wait!"

The Auditor looked at Dale's hand.

"Let go," the Auditor said.

"If you revoke my license, who fixes the building?" Dale asked.

"The Authority will send a Class-IX team."

"Class-IX teams are heroes," Dale said.

"They break things. They don't fix them.

They don't document.

They don't leave a paper trail.

"

The Auditor paused. His eyes narrowed.

"You are implying inefficiency," the Auditor said.

"I am stating fact. Look at the lobby.

The water is rising.

The electrical systems are failing, and the structural integrity is at 40%. If you send heroes, they will fight the Lithic Constructs.

They will break the walls. The building will collapse. If I stay, I stabilize the foundation.

I manage the infestation. I keep the structure intact.

"

The Auditor looked at the lobby. At the dead rock creature. At the water. At the notebook in Dale's hand.

He looked back at Dale.

"You want to stay," the Auditor said.

"I want to work," Dale said.

The Auditor smiled. A real smile this time. Dangerous.

"Very well," the Auditor said. "I will reinstate your provisional license. But there is a condition."

"What condition?"

"Your next job," the Auditor said. "It is not a pest control job. It is a demolition job."

Dale froze.

"What?"

"The Authority has determined that the Rift cannot be managed," the Auditor said.

"It must be purged. You are to go into the Rift. You are to identify the source.

And you are to eliminate it.

"

"That's not extermination," Dale said. "That's suicide."

"That is the job," the Auditor said. "Class IX.

No return protocol.

No backup. No clipboard.

"

The doors closed.

Dale stood in the lobby. The water was at his knees.

Marcus walked up to him.

"What did he say?"

Dale looked at the elevator. The reflection was back. The technician in the hazmat suit.

He looked at his notebook. The entry for the Lithic Construct.

He looked at the blank page on the next sheet, and he uncapped his pen.

He wrote: *SUBJECT: SUICIDE MISSION.* *STATUS: UNLICENSED.* *CLASS: EXTERMINATION TARGET.*

He looked at Marcus.

"We need more water," Dale said. "And we need a bigger gun."
