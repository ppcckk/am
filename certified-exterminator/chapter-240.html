Chapter 240: Reporting the Unclassified

# Chapter 240

The transfer beam smelled like burnt ozone and stale coffee. Dale's stomach lurched as the light dissolved his suburban street, replacing the cracked asphalt with the polished obsidian floor of a command center that looked less like a military bunker and more like a high-end law firm's lobby. He stumbled, his boots squeaking against the reflective surface, and caught himself on a desk made of something that pulsed faintly.

He straightened his tie. His clipboard was still clutched to his chest. The pen was uncapped.

Around him, other technicians stood in a loose semicircle. They wore armor. Glowing runes etched into steel plates, energy swords strapped to their backs, and capes that fluttered despite the lack of wind. Dale looked like he had wandered out of an inspection report.

A woman stood at the head of the table. She was tall, her posture rigid, and her eyes were obscured by a visor that displayed streaming data streams in neon green. Her name tag read **COMMANDER VANCE**. Below her name, the rank was etched in gold: **CLASS IX**.

"Report," Vance said. Her voice was amplified, cutting through the hum of the room. It wasn't a request. It was a command.

Dale blinked. He looked at the other technicians. They were staring at him with a mix of pity and contempt. One man, holding a sword that crackled with lightning, sneered.

"Support specialist," the man muttered. "They sent a janitor."

Dale ignored him. He walked to the table.

He didn't salute.

He didn't bow. He placed his clipboard on the obsidian surface and clicked his pen.

"Infestation site: Sector 4, Residential Zone," Dale said. "Class-5 entity, designated *The Architect*. Status: Active. Containment: Failed."

Vance tapped a finger on the table.

The data streams on her visor shifted. "You are under direct command.

You do not speak until spoken to.

You do not initiate action. You document. You support.

You obey."

"Understood," Dale said. "However, my protocol requires initial site assessment before deployment. Without it, I cannot identify vulnerabilities."

"You have no clearance to assess," Vance said. "The Architect is a reality-anchor. It does not have weaknesses. It has geometry."

"Everything has weaknesses," Dale said flatly. "Even geometry."

The lightning-sword man laughed. "Listen to him. He thinks it's a cockroach."

Vance raised a hand. Silence fell. She looked at Dale. Her visor flickered.

"Your Thoroughness Rating is 9.8," she said. "That is the highest in the region. It is also your only utility. Do not make me regret recruiting you."

A hologram projected from the center of the table.

It showed the suburban street Dale had just left.

But it was wrong. The houses were twisted.

The trees grew downward. The sky was a bruised purple. And in the center of the street, the eyeless head of the Architect was embedded in the earth, its mouth a tunnel into a space that shouldn't exist.

"Team Alpha, deploy," Vance ordered.

The armored technicians vanished in flashes of light. Dale remained.

"Stay here," Vance said. "Monitor the feed.

If you see a structural anomaly, log it.

If you see a breach, log it. Do not engage."

Dale looked at the hologram.

He looked at his Field Guide. He had one entry for *The Architect*.

It was blank except for the designation.

No weakness. No entry. Just red text screaming **INSUFFICIENT DATA**.

He picked up his pen. He wrote.

**OBSERVATION 1:** *Architect's mouth is not a biological orifice. It is a spatial fold. Air currents flow into it, not out of it. Suggests a vacuum seal.*

Vance watched him. "You are wasting time."

"Protocol requires documentation," Dale said. He didn't look up.

The screen flickered. A scream cut through the room. It wasn't a human scream. It was the sound of metal bending.

Team Alpha had engaged.

On the hologram, the lightning-sword man slashed at the Architect's jaw. The sword passed through the flesh like smoke.

The Architect didn't move.

Instead, the ground around it rippled.

The man was thrown backward, not by impact, but by the displacement of space itself. He hit a house and vanished into the wood.

"Alpha Leader lost," a voice reported from the comms channel. "Second impact imminent."

Dale's pen moved faster. He wasn't watching the combat. He was watching the ground.

**OBSERVATION 2:** *Ripples originate from the left side of the mouth, 3.2 meters from the center. Frequency: 0.5 Hz. Ripples expand in a spiral pattern, counter-clockwise.*

"Stop writing," Vance snapped. "Save your life. Or theirs."

"I am saving them," Dale said. He pointed at the hologram. "They are attacking the head.

The head is a decoy. The anchor is in the throat."

"The throat is sealed," Vance said. "Scans show no opening."

"Scans are wrong," Dale said. "Look at the frass."

Vance frowned. "Frass?"

"Excrement.

Waste product. Dimensional waste." Dale zoomed in on the hologram. Around the Architect's mouth, tiny black specks were drifting out.

They weren't dust.

They were geometric shards. Tiny, perfect tetrahedrons.

"They're shedding matter," Dale said.

"Because they can't contain it. The mouth isn't an entrance. It's a leak.

The real structure is inside the chest cavity. The head is just the pressure valve."

Vance stared at the data. Her visor flashed red, then green.

"Verify," she said.

Dale closed his eyes.

He accessed his Field Guide.

He didn't have an entry for *The Architect*. He had an entry for *Dimensional Vermin (Rattus profundus)*.

He remembered the broodmother. The way it had expanded to fit the crawlspace. The way it had expelled waste when stressed.

He applied the logic. A creature that consumes space to grow must expel waste to maintain structural integrity.

**HYPOTHESIS:** *Architect's chest cavity contains the primary mass. Mouth is a secondary exhaust port. Target: Sternum plate, 1.5 meters below the jawline.*

**ACTION:** *Deploy High-Pressure Containment Foam (Protocol 7-C).*

"Useless," the lightning-sword man's voice crackled over the comms. He was alive, barely. "We tried explosives. Nothing works."

"Foam isn't explosive," Dale said. "It's viscous. It fills the gap."

"You don't have foam," Vance said. "You have a clipboard and a pen."

"I have a protocol," Dale said. "And I have a loophole."

He looked at the System notification. **WARNING: INDEPENDENT ACTION PENALTY: 50% TP LOSS.**

He also looked at the rule set he had memorized years ago. *Cross-contamination penalty: deploying a protocol designed for one creature type against another inflicts a 40% debuff and contaminates your Field Guide entry for 24 hours.*

But there was a clause in the Licensing Authority's manual, Section 4, Paragraph B: *If a protocol is repurposed for a Class-5 threat without prior documentation, the penalty is waived if the user achieves a Thoroughness Score of 10 during the engagement.*

It was a theoretical rule. No one had ever claimed it. It required perfect execution in the face of a threat that killed experts in seconds.

"Dale," Vance said. "Get back."

Dale stepped off the platform.

The transfer beam didn't activate. He didn't wait.

He walked toward the hologram's projection zone, which was linked to the real-time feed.

He reached into his kit bag. He pulled out a canister. It wasn't a weapon.

It was a industrial-grade expanding foam, used for sealing cracks in drywall.

Class 1 pest control.

He stepped into the light.

The world shifted.

He was in the suburban street. The air tasted like copper and old blood. The Architect loomed above him.

The ground shook.

The armored team was scattered. The lightning-sword man was pinned under a collapsed roof. Two others were running, their armor cracking under the pressure of the shifting space.

The Architect's mouth opened. The wind roared.

Dale didn't run.

He set his feet.

He uncapped the foam canister. He checked the nozzle.

It was clogged. He used his pen to clear the tip.

"Target identified," he muttered. "Sternum plate. 1.5 meters below jaw."

He looked at the System. **TARGET NOT IN FIELD GUIDE. DAMAGE BONUS: 0%.**

He looked at the Architect. The creature's skin was like wet concrete. It shifted, trying to find purchase.

Dale stepped forward. The pressure hit him.

His knees buckled.

He fought the urge to vomit. His contamination anxiety spiked. The air was unsterile.

The ground was unclean.

The monster was unregistered.

*Protocol,* he told himself. *Identify.

Document. Isolate. Eliminate.

Verify.*

He couldn't isolate it. He couldn't eliminate it with force. He had to contain it.

He aimed the foam at the spot he had calculated. He pulled the trigger.

The white foam shot out.

It hit the chest of the Architect.

It didn't bounce off. It stuck.

It expanded. It hardened.

The Architect shrieked. It wasn't a sound. It was a vibration that shook Dale's teeth.

The foam spread, filling the cracks in the creature's chest. It was a Class 1 tool against a Class 5 threat.

The cross-contamination debuff hit Dale's vision.

His color perception grayed. His movements slowed.

**DEBUFF ACTIVE: 40% MOVEMENT SPEED REDUCTION.**

He stumbled.

The foam kept expanding. The Architect thrashed.

It turned its head toward Dale.

The mouth widened. The vacuum pulled at him.

Dale held his ground. He sprayed. He sprayed until the canister was empty.

The foam hardened into a white, jagged barrier over the chest. The Architect's thrashing slowed. The vacuum weakened.

**PROGRESS REPORT:** *Containment 12%.*

It wasn't enough.

Vance's voice came over his earpiece. "Pull out. Now."

"No," Dale said. "I need more."

"You have nothing."

Dale looked at his clipboard. He looked at the pen. He looked at the other technicians.

The lightning-sword man was struggling to free himself. His sword was jammed in the debris.

Dale ran to him. He didn't attack the monster.

He attacked the environment.

He used his pen to pry at the debris.

He used his body to shift a piece of drywall.

"Your sword," Dale said. "It channels energy. Right?"

The man glared at him. "It's plasma."

"Plasma is heat," Dale said. "Foam expands with heat. If I superheat the foam, it will expand faster. It will breach the chest cavity."

"You can't control the heat," the man said.

"I don't need to control it," Dale said. "I need to direct it."

He grabbed the man's arm. He pointed to the white foam seal. "Shoot the center.

Low intensity. Just enough to warm it."

The man hesitated. The Architect was recovering. The foam was cracking.

"Do it," Dale said. His voice was flat. Procedural. "It's a code violation to leave the job unfinished."

The man raised his sword. He fired a pulse.

The beam hit the foam. The foam hissed. It expanded violently. The white barrier blew apart, sending shards of hardened polymer into the Architect's chest.

The creature screamed.

Its chest cavity burst open. Inside, there was no heart. There was a knot of black light.

A dense, swirling mass of dimensional energy.

**TARGET EXPOSED.**

Dale looked at his Field Guide. The entry for *The Architect* was still blank.

But the System flashed.

**NEW WEAKNESS DETECTED: CORE EXPOSURE.** **FIELD GUIDE UPDATED.** **DAMAGE BONUS: +200% (TEMPORARY).**

Dale didn't attack. He couldn't. He was a pest control technician.

But he had a protocol for infestations.

He pulled out his second tool. A canister of pheromone disruptor. Usually used to confuse ant colonies. He had repurposed it for dimensional vermin.

He sprayed the core.

The black light flickered. The Architect convulsed. The vacuum stopped.

The ground stopped shaking.

Silence.

The Architect slumped. The black light dimmed. The eyeless head drooped.

Dale stood there. He was covered in foam dust. His suit was torn. His pen was broken.

Vance appeared beside him. Her visor was gone. Her face was pale.

"You broke protocol," she said.

"I completed the job," Dale said. He pulled out his clipboard. He wrote.

**JOB COMPLETE.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: PENDING.**

Vance looked at the dying monster. She looked at the other technicians, who were staring at Dale with open mouths.

"You used a Class 1 tool against a Class 5 threat," Vance said. "You should be dead."

"I followed the manual," Dale said. "Section 4, Paragraph B."

Vance's eyes narrowed. She accessed the System. The Thoroughness Score calculated.

**9.9.**

Not 10. Not enough to waive the penalty.

**PENALTY APPLIED: -50% TREATMENT POINTS.** **FIELD GUIDE CONTAMINATED: 24 HOURS.**

Dale winced. His Field Guide was grayed out. He couldn't access any data. He was blind.

But the Architect was dead.

Vance turned to him. "You are a liability, Kowalski. You acted independently. You jeopardized the team."

"I solved the problem," Dale said.

"There are more," Vance said. She pointed to the sky.

The Rift was widening.

More shapes were crawling out. Not one. Dozens.

"The Authority has a backlog," Vance said. "And you just started a war with a method no one else can replicate."

She leaned in. "I'm not firing you. I'm promoting you."

Dale looked up. "To what?"

"Commander," Vance said. "You are now in charge of the Rapid Response Unit. You have three minutes to brief them on your methodology before the next wave hits."

Dale looked at his clipboard. He looked at his broken pen.

"I don't have a pen," he said.

Vance handed him a stylus. It was glowing red.

"Use this," she said. "And Dale? Don't screw up the paperwork."

The ground shook again. A hand, larger than the first, burst from the earth. It was made of glass and bone.

Dale clicked the stylus.

He looked at the new entity. He didn't know its name. He didn't know its weakness.

But he knew how to write a report.

He started writing.
