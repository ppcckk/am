Chapter 118: The Auditor’s Gap

# Chapter 118

The woman in the severe bun did not scream. She did not run.

She stood on the other side of the shattered floorboards, her shoes immaculate, her clipboard held at a precise thirty-degree angle.

To anyone else, she looked like an auditor who had arrived early. To Dale, she looked like a violation of code so severe it defied physics.

Her gaze swept the room.

Not for the dust. Not for the structural integrity of the joists.

She was looking for the gap.

The void where the monster should be, but wasn't.

Dale hung in the void.

Gravity had been revoked. The house was no longer a house; it was a stomach, and he was currently suspended in its acid bath.

The walls rippled, contracting and expanding with a wet, rhythmic thud.

The wallpaper, peeling and damp, formed patterns that made his eyes water.

He tried to orient himself. Up was down.

Down was a memory, and he reached for his belt. Empty.

Panic, cold and sharp, tried to claw its way up his throat. He swallowed it. It tasted like copper and old drywall.

The woman stepped onto the debris floating in the zero-g chamber. Her feet touched the floating splinters of oak flooring without disturbing them.

She looked bored, which was the worst part. The boredom. It implied that this—Dale Kowalski, falling through the guts of a living house—was routine.

"Mr.

Kowalski," she said. Her voice didn't travel through the air.

It vibrated directly into his skull.

"You failed to file your pre-arrival hazard assessment. That is a Class 4 infraction."

Dale twisted in mid-air. He managed to grab a floating chunk of drywall. It crumbled in his hand, turning into grey powder that hung in the air like mist.

"Who are you?" Dale asked. His voice sounded thin, stripped of its usual procedural calm.

"I am the Inspector," she said. "And you are the liability."

She raised her pen. The tip glowed with a dull, necrotic light.

Protocol 9. Isolation of the Technician.

Dale knew what that meant. It wasn't just locking him in a room.

It was removing him from the ecosystem.

If he was isolated, he couldn't treat, and he couldn't document. He couldn't earn points.

He would become static, then waste, then nothing.

He needed to anchor.

He looked at the floating debris. A chair leg. A shattered light fixture. A single, pristine white sneaker that belonged to no one in this timeline.

The Field Guide icon in his peripheral vision was dead. Grey.

Useless, and he had no data on the Inspector. He had no data on the House. But he had data on himself.

He knew his protocols.

He knew his weaknesses.

*Pathological contamination anxiety.*

Right now, that anxiety was the only thing keeping him sane. If he didn't perceive a hazard, he couldn't react. He needed a hazard.

The floor beneath the Inspector opened further. A mouth, wide and dark, yawned beneath her. Tendrils of black slime slithered up from the abyss, seeking purchase.

She tapped her pen against the clipboard. *Tap. Tap. Tap.*

"Your workspace is compromised," she repeated. "You are now unlicensed for this sector."

The words hit Dale like a physical blow. Unlicensed.

If he was unlicensed, he had no protection. No Authority backing. No Treatment Points. He was just a man in a box.

But the Authority didn't license people. It licensed actions.

Dale stopped twisting, and he let the drywall dust drift past his face. He closed his eyes for a second, counting the heartbeats. One.

Two. Three.

He opened them. He looked at the slime tendrils. They were reaching for the chair leg.

Not him. The furniture.

The house wasn't trying to eat him. It was trying to digest the structure. He was just an obstacle in the pipe.

The realization hit him with the force of a spray can hitting a roach.

He wasn't the target. He was the contaminant.

"Hey," Dale said.

The Inspector paused. Her eyes narrowed.

"You're violating Section 12, Paragraph B," Dale shouted. His voice was steadier now. The panic was still there, but it was compartmentalized, filed under 'Hazardous Materials, Containment Required.'

"Silence," the Inspector said. The tendrils surged.

"You're not isolating me," Dale said. "You're attempting a structural purge. But you forgot the ventilation protocol."

The Inspector's brow furrowed. A tiny crack in the boredom.

Dale kicked off the floating drywall chunk. He spun in the air, grabbing the white sneaker. It was leather. Genuine leather. flammable. absorbent.

He didn't know what he was doing, and he was improvising.

This was against every protocol he knew. Improvisation was sloppy work.

Sloppy work earned low Thoroughness Ratings. But being dead earned zero.

He pulled the sneaker tight against his chest. The slime tendrils whipped toward him. He dodged. The tendrils smashed into the wall, splattering black goo against the rippling wallpaper.

The goo hissed. It ate the paper.

Dale saw it. The house was reacting to the foreign matter. The black goo was acid, but it was also a signal. The house was trying to clear the blockage.

If he could make himself the blockage, the house would try to expel him.

He looked at the Inspector. She was frowning now. The pen hovered over the clipboard. She was writing.

"You're contaminating the field," Dale said. "That's a 40% debuff to your own efficiency."

The Inspector stopped writing. She looked at the slime on the walls.

It was spreading, which was eating the floorboards, the rafters, the windows. The house was convulsing.

"Stop," she commanded.

But the house was already moving. The zero-gravity field flickered. Dale felt weight return, and brutally. He slammed into a floating beam, the wind knocked out of him.

The Inspector grabbed a handhold—a piece of molding that hadn't dissolved. She was anchored. He wasn't.

Gravity was returning. The house was trying to shake him out.

Dale clutched the sneaker. The leather was warm, vibrating against his ribcage. He needed a tool. He needed something to puncture the membrane of this reality.

He scanned the debris field. Shattered glass. Twisted metal. A child's toy, a plastic truck, floating in the air.

*Plastic.*

Not conductive. Not acidic. Just... present.

The Inspector shouted something, her voice distorted by the shifting gravity.

"Cease activity, Technician. Submit for termination."

Dale ignored her. He grabbed the plastic truck. It had a sharp edge where the wheel had broken off.

He threw it, and he didn't throw it at the Inspector. He threw it at the center of the black slime pool on the far wall.

The truck sailed through the air, tumbling end over end. It hit the slime with a wet *splat*.

Nothing happened.

Dale's heart sank. Sloppy work.

Then, the slime rippled. The plastic truck sank, not because it was heavy, but because the house was rejecting it. The membrane tried to digest the foreign object.

The digestion process caused a local collapse in the spatial integrity.

The gravity field shattered.

Dale fell, and he hit the floor—real floor, solid wood, splintered and broken. The impact jarred his teeth. He rolled, instinctively covering his head, expecting a blow from the Inspector.

It didn't come.

He scrambled up, gasping.

The room was stable again. The walls were still breathing, but now, like a sleeping animal.

The black slime was gone.

Reabsorbed, and the Inspector was gone.

Only her clipboard remained, floating in the center of the room, spinning slowly.

Dale stared at it. His hands were shaking.

He clenched them into fists, digging his nails into his palms.

Pain.

Good. Grounding.

He walked toward the clipboard. Every step was calculated. He checked the floor for traps.

He checked the corners for ambushes. He was a Level II Technician.

He didn't assume, and he verified.

He reached the clipboard.

The pen was gone.

The clipboard was black, obsidian-like. Cold to the touch.

On the paper, a single line had been written. Not in ink. In something that looked like ash.

*Insufficient Thoroughness. Score: 0.4.*

Dale read it twice.

0.4.

A failing grade.

For the first time in fourteen years, Dale Kowalski had failed an inspection.

He looked around the room, and the door was open. The window was open.

The house was just a house again.

The wallpaper was peeling, but it wasn't breathing. The floor was solid.

But the air smelled wrong. It smelled like ozone and burnt sugar.

He picked up the clipboard. It was heavy.

A notification pinged in his peripheral vision. Not the usual chime. This one was low, dissonant.

**SYSTEM ALERT: LICENSING SUSPENSION INITIATED.** **REASON: CROSS-CONTAMINATION OF REALITY STRUCTURE.** **PENALTY: FIELD GUIDE ACCESS LOCKED FOR 72 HOURS.** **STATUS: PROBATIONARY.**

Dale stared at the words.

Access locked, and he couldn't identify threats. He couldn't look up weaknesses. He couldn't use targeted damage bonuses.

He was flying blind.

And the house wasn't the only thing that had changed.

He heard it before he saw it. A scratching sound.

Small.

Rhythmic.

Coming from inside the walls.

Not rats.

Not roaches, and the scratching was coming from everywhere. From the floorboards. From the ceiling. From the foundation.

He looked down at his feet.

A tiny crack had appeared in the wood near his boot.

He knelt. He put his ear to the floor.

The scratching stopped.

Then, a voice. Tiny. High-pitched. Coming from beneath the floor.

"Help us," it whispered.

Dale pulled back. He stood up. His hand went to his belt for his flashlight. His fingers brushed the empty holster.

He looked at the door.

Outside, the sun was shining. Normal suburban Tuesday. Kids playing in sprinklers. A dog barking.

But the light hitting the floor wasn't sunlight. It was too white. Too uniform.

Dale stepped toward the door. He didn't open it. He looked through the glass.

The street was empty. No cars. No people. Just the houses, identical and grey.

And standing on the porch across the street, facing him, was a man.

The man was holding a clipboard, and the man was wearing Dale's face.

Dale stumbled back. He blinked. The man didn't move. He just stood there, waiting.

The notification pulsed again.

**NEW JOB DETECTED: RECURSIVE INFESTATION.** **TARGET: SELF.** **DIFFICULTY: CLASS IX.** **REWARD: EXISTENCE.**

Dale looked at the clipboard in his hand. The ash writing had changed.

*Find the nest.*

He looked at the doppelganger across the street. Then he looked at the walls of the house. The cracks were spreading. Fast.

He had to leave. But where?

If he walked out, he walked into the trap. If he stayed, he got digested.

He needed a loophole. A rule he hadn't used yet.

*Cross-contamination penalty.*

He had deployed the plastic truck against the house. That was contamination. It cost him his Field Guide.

But what if he contaminated himself?

Dale looked at the black slime stain on the wall. He hadn't cleaned it. It was still there, a faint smear on the wallpaper.

He reached out and touched it.

The system didn't react, and he rubbed his fingers together. The residue was warm.

He looked at the door. The doppelganger had moved. He was on the porch.

Dale opened his mouth to shout, but no sound came out.

Instead, he heard a chime. A familiar, cheerful chime.

**TREATMENT POINTS AWARDED: 1.**

**SOURCE: SELF-CONTAMINATION.**

**NOTE: YOU ARE NOW THE INFESTATION.**

The doppelganger raised its hand. It pointed at Dale.

The door to the house closed. Slowly.

Dale ran for the kitchen. He needed water. He needed bleach. He needed to clean himself before he became part of the problem.

But as he ran, his reflection in the glass of the oven showed him smiling.

He wasn't smiling, and he kept running.
