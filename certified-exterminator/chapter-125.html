Chapter 125: Jurisdictional Conflict

# Chapter 125

A drop of sweat traced the bridge of Dale’s nose, pooling at the edge of his glasses. The doppelgänger's hand did not shake. It held the posture of a man who had spent fifteen years filling out forms in triplicate, even as the air around it rippled with the static of a dimensional breach.

Dale didn't move. He didn't raise his weapon. He adjusted his glasses.

"Level II," Dale said. The word tasted like copper in his mouth. "The Authority doesn't skip levels. That's a procedural error."

The entity that looked like Dale tilted its head. The skin-suit creaked, wet and heavy.

"The Authority adapts.

You are late. The backlog is critical. Efficiency is the new metric.

"

"Efficiency is a suggestion," Dale replied, his voice flat.

"Protocol is mandatory. You can't initiate a Level II exam while I'm still in the field with a Class III active infestation.

That's a jurisdictional conflict.

It voids the liability waiver.

"

The doppelgänger blinked. For a microsecond, the System interface flickered in the air between them. Red text scrolled rapidly, then settled.

**DETECTED: JURISDICTIONAL CONFLICT.** **CALCULATING... ERROR.** **RESOLUTION: PAUSE EXAM. RESOLVE CONFLICT.**

The figure lowered its pen. The swirling vortex in the sky above the driveway slowed its rotation, grinding to a halt. The pulsing organic matter in the yard ceased its rhythmic throb. Silence rushed back into the space, deafening in its suddenness.

Dale exhaled. His knuckles were white where he gripped the handle of his backpack.

He hadn't lied, and he hadn't bluffed.

He had cited a regulation buried in Subsection 4, Paragraph C of the Field Operations Manual, a rule designed to prevent bureaucratic overlaps in high-density urban zones. He had never thought he'd use it against a copy of himself.

"Good," Dale said. "Now, let's talk about the cleanup. I have a containment breach in Sector 7. I need access to my previous logs to verify the species count before I can sign off on the closure."

The doppelgänger stared. It didn't speak. It simply watched. But the System window remained open, hovering in Dale's peripheral vision.

**EXAM SUSPENDED. TIME REMAINING: 11 HOURS, 58 MINUTES.** **TASK: RESOLVE CLASS III INFESTATION (Rattus profundus, Mutated Variant).** **REWARD: EXAM RE-INITIATION.** **PENALTY FOR FAILURE: TERMINATION.**

Dale turned away from the figure. He walked back toward the house. The door was still open, leading into the kitchen where the blood had not yet dried. The sphere inside remained dormant, its black seal intact but humming with a low-frequency vibration that made Dale's teeth ache.

Halloway was still there. He sat on the floor, leaning against the refrigerator, clutching his side. The blood on his shirt had spread, dark and glossy. He looked up at Dale with eyes that held no judgment, only a weary resignation.

"You stalled it," Halloway said. His voice was a rasp.

"I filed a complaint," Dale corrected. He moved past him, careful not to disturb the blood pool. He went to the counter.

His field kit was there. The spray can was empty.

The sealant gun was loaded, but the nozzle was clogged with chitin.

Dale pulled a small wrench from his belt and disassembled the nozzle.

"Is that going to help?" Halloway asked.

"It's protocol," Dale said. He didn't look up. "You can't treat a surface without a clean applicator. You can't certify a kill without a verified specimen."

He cleared the nozzle.

A chunk of black, tar-like substance fell into the sink. Dale wiped it away with a sterile wipe from his kit.

He reassembled the gun, and he checked the pressure gauge. It was green.

"The doppelgänger," Halloway said. "It looked like you. Exactly like you."

"It looked like a technician," Dale said. "Which is a dangerous thing to be right now."

Dale picked up the clipboard. He looked at the blank sheet the doppelgänger had left on the floor.

He picked it up, and the paper was warm.

Faint, glowing text pulsed on the surface, forming a list of required documentation for the Level II exam. It wasn't a test of combat.

It was a test of paperwork.

*Item 1: Verify Species Origin.* *Item 2: Document Environmental Impact.* *Item 3: Confirm Containment Integrity.* *Item 4: Submit Incident Report.*

Dale's mind raced. The mutation.

The sphere.

The sudden shift in the environment. The system had changed the rules without updating the manual. This was a trap.

Or it was an opportunity, and he walked to the window. Outside, the twisted landscape remained frozen. The doppelgänger stood in the driveway, motionless. It was waiting for him to fail the paperwork.

"The mutation," Dale muttered. He pulled his Field Guide from his pack.

The book was thick, bound in synthetic leather.

He flipped to the entry for *Rattus profundus*. The text was static. Standard entry.

But as he looked closer, the ink shimmered.

A new line appeared, written in a font that wasn't there before.

**VARIANT 44-B: HOLLOWAY'S STRAIN.**

Dale froze. He looked back at Halloway. The man was watching him, his hand pressed tight against his wound.

"Halloway," Dale said.

"When you were bitten.

Did you feel anything unusual? A burning sensation?

A coldness?"

Halloway frowned.

"Pain.

Just pain.

And... a taste. Like metal.

"

"Metal," Dale repeated.

He turned back to the guide. He traced the new text with his finger.

The entry didn't offer a weakness.

It offered a *liability*.

If the rat was carrying a strain of dimensional corruption that had transferred to the host, then Halloway was no longer a survivor. He was a vector. And vectors were not to be treated. They were to be contained.

The cost of thoroughness was high.

Dale looked at the System window.

**NOTE: CONTAINMENT OF BIOHAZARD VECTORS IS MANDATORY FOR CERTIFICATION LEVEL II.**

If he killed the rat, he completed the extermination. But if he didn't handle the vector, he failed the containment protocol.

His license would remain suspended, and he would never take the exam.

He would be terminated.

He needed to document Halloway. He needed to isolate him. And he needed to do it without killing him, because Halloway was not the infestation. He was the evidence.

"Stand up," Dale said.

Halloway groaned. He used the refrigerator for leverage, his face pale.

"Dale, I'm bleeding out. I can't stand."

"You can if you want to keep your license," Dale said. He wasn't joking. Halloway's license, as a bystander, was irrelevant. But Dale's was on the line.

Dale pulled a biohazard bag from his kit. It was orange, heavy-duty, rated for Class IV contaminants. He hadn't used one since the acid beetle incident in Chapter 40. He held it open.

"Put your left arm in here," Dale ordered.

"What?" Halloway stared at him. "I'm not a specimen."

"You're a vector," Dale said. "If I don't quarantine the exposure, the Authority will deem this job incomplete. They'll revoke my license.

I'll be terminated. If I'm terminated, who's going to stop the thing outside?

"

Halloway looked at the doppelgänger. The figure hadn't moved. It was just standing there, holding its pen, waiting for the clock to run out.

"You think that thing is listening?" Halloway asked.

"It's not listening," Dale said. "It's calculating. And right now, it's waiting for me to make a mistake."

Halloway looked at the bag. He looked at his bleeding side. He looked at Dale's steady, unblinking eyes.

"Fine," Halloway said.

He stepped forward. He slid his left arm into the bag. Dale zipped it up quickly, sealing the wound inside the plastic.

The blood stopped flowing, contained within the orange plastic.

Dale grabbed Halloway's good arm. He pulled him toward the back door.

"We're moving to the crawlspace," Dale said. "I need to inspect the nest chamber.

The mutation didn't come from the rat, which came from the source. The sphere.

"

"The sphere is inside," Halloway said.

"The sphere is a conduit," Dale said. "The nest is the source. And I need to see what's in the walls."

They moved through the kitchen, stepping over debris. Dale's flashlight cut through the dust.

He scanned the baseboards.

There were entry points.

Small holes, gnawed into the drywall. But the frass—rat droppings—wasn't black.

It was metallic. Silver, like mercury.

*Frass trail,* Dale noted mentally. *Non-standard consistency. Indicates ingestion of heavy metals or alchemical compounds.*

He wrote it down on the clipboard.

**OBSERVATION 4: FRACTURE ZONE CONFIRMED. SUB-STRATA CONTAMINATION.**

The System pinged.

**DOCUMENTATION ACCEPTED. THOROUGHNESS RATING: 8.5/10.**

It wasn't perfect. But it was progress.

They reached the back door. Dale kicked it open.

The crawlspace entrance was under the stairs, and he peeled back the plywood. A smell hit him—sulfur and ozone.

He shone the light down.

The crawlspace was empty.

No broodmother. No eggs. No rats.

Just a hole. A perfectly circular hole in the dirt floor, leading down into darkness. And around the edge of the hole, the dirt was fused into glass.

Dale stared. He had treated this house. He had inspected every inch of this crawlspace in the first month. There had been no hole.

"Where did they go?" Halloway whispered.

"They didn't go anywhere," Dale said. "They went down. Into the rift."

He looked at the glass rim. It was hot. He could feel the heat radiating up.

The doppelgänger appeared in the doorway behind them.

It didn't walk in. It just stood there.

Its clipboard was full now, and the pages were fluttering in the wind that wasn't blowing.

**INSPECTION COMPLETE? (Y/N)**

Dale looked at the hole. He looked at the glass. He looked at Halloway, who was trembling in the plastic bag.

If he said yes, the exam would resume. And he would have to fight the doppelgänger. Or solve a puzzle that might kill him.

If he said no, the job would remain open. The license would stay suspended.

But there was a third option.

Dale reached into his belt pouch. He pulled out a small vial of neutralizer solution.

It was designed for acid beetles, which was rated for Class I contaminants.

Using it on a Class III rift opening was a cross-contamination risk. It would debuff his toolkit.

It would contaminate his Field Guide.

But it would also seal the entry point.

And the Authority's rules stated: *An infestation is not considered active until the entry point is verified as inaccessible.*

If the hole was sealed, the infestation was technically dormant.

Dale uncorked the vial. He poured the blue liquid onto the glass rim.

The liquid hissed. Steam rose. The glass cracked, then melted, then reformed into a solid, seamless block of obsidian.

The hole was gone.

**SYSTEM ALERT: ENTRY POINT SEALED.** **INFESTATION STATUS: DORMANT.** **JOB CLOSURE: PENDING VERIFICATION.**

The doppelgänger tilted its head. The pen in its hand hovered.

**VERIFICATION REQUIRED.** **SUBMIT PHYSICAL EVIDENCE OF DORMANCY.**

Dale looked at Halloway. The man was pale, shivering, trapped in the orange bag.

"Take off the bag," Dale said.

"Dale, I'm still bleeding," Halloway said.

"The blood is the evidence," Dale said. He pulled a sterile swab from his kit.

He reached into the bag, through the plastic, and touched the wetness of Halloway's shirt.

He wiped the swab against the metal-fractured frass on the floor. Then he wiped it against the newly sealed glass floor.

He held the swab up to the light. It glowed faintly purple.

**EVIDENCE ACCEPTED.** **CONTAMINATION TRACED TO LOCAL SOURCE.** **INFESTATION CLASSIFIED AS LOCALIZED ANOMALY, NOT DIMENSIONAL BREACH.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 9.8/10.** **EXAMINATION RESUMED.**

The doppelgänger raised its pen again.

The sky above them shifted. The grey vortex spun faster. The red clouds churned.

But the doppelgänger didn't attack. It wrote. It wrote furiously, scratching across the paper.

Then it tore the page off. It held it out to Dale.

**LEVEL II EXAM: PASS.** **CREDENTIAL: INTERDIMENSIONAL COMPLIANCE OFFICER.** **NEW PROTOCOL UNLOCKED: SHELTER-IN-PLACE (LEVEL II).** **WARNING: CONTRACT RENEWAL PENDING.** **NEW CLIENT DETECTED: THE AUTHORITY'S OVERSIGHT COMMITTEE.**

Dale stared at the page. The words were burning into his retina. *Oversight Committee.* He had never heard of them. The System's interface shifted, adding a new tab. *Internal Affairs.*

He looked at the doppelgänger. It was dissolving. The skin-suit was sloughing off, revealing not flesh, but a skeleton made of black wire and glass.

"Who are you?" Dale asked.

The skeleton didn't speak. It pointed a bony finger at Dale's chest. Then it pointed at the sky.

And then it vanished, and the driveway was empty. The twisted landscape remained, but the pulsing had stopped. The silence was absolute.

Halloway slumped against the doorframe.

"Did we win?"

"No," Dale said. He looked at the new tab in his vision. *Internal Affairs.* It was blinking. A red light. Urgent.

"We just got audited," Dale said. "And I think I failed the background check."

A new notification popped up.

It wasn't from the System. It was from his own phone.

A text message.

From an unknown number.

*They know you sealed the rift with beetle acid. That's a felony in three counties. Come to the warehouse. Now.*

Dale looked at Halloway. He looked at the sealed glass floor. He looked at the sky, where the vortex was beginning to fade, revealing the stars.

He picked up his clipboard. He took out a pen. He wrote a violation code on the blank page.

**VIOLATION: UNAUTHORIZED USE OF PROTOCOL. CODE: X-99.**

He folded the page. He put it in his pocket.

"Let's go," Dale said. "We have a warrant to serve."

He didn't wait for Halloway to stand. He walked out into the driveway. The ground crunched under his boots. The air smelled of ozone and old blood.

But ahead, on the horizon, lights were flickering. Not streetlights.

Searchlights.

And the sound of engines.

Heavy, diesel engines.

The Authority wasn't the only thing watching.
