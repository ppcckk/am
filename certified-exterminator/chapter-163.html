Chapter 163: Documenting the Void

# Chapter 163

Marla’s clipboard clicked three times, confirming the Thoroughness Rating sat exactly at 4.9. It smelled like ozone and old paper.

Marla stood on the edge of the existing pavement, her boots hovering over the precipice of reality. Her clipboard was trembling in her hands, a physical manifestation of the terror she was trying to suppress. She wasn't an exterminator. She was a junior auditor for the Municipal Rift Control Board, and she had never seen a Thoroughness Rating drop below 5.0.

Below her, on the white expanse that had replaced Elm Street, a small figure moved with the jerky, purposeful gait of a man who had spent too many years walking in tight shoes.

Dale Kowalski.

Marla squinted. The light emanating from his skin was blinding, a sterile, clinical white that hurt her eyes. He wasn't looking at the thousands of black shapes marching toward him. He was looking at his clipboard.

"Is he insane?" Marla whispered to her partner, Greg.

Greg shook his head, his face pale.

"No. He's working."

The black shapes were Enforcers. They had no faces, only smooth, chitinous plates where features should be.

They moved in a synchronized wave, a tide of silence and violence.

They were the Authority's cleanup crew, designed to erase anomalies. And Dale was the anomaly.

Marla watched Dale stop, and he didn't raise his hands in surrender. He didn't beg.

He pulled a pen from his pocket.

It was a cheap ballpoint, the kind you bought in bulk packs from a gas station.

He clicked it.

The sound carried across the void, impossibly loud. *Click.*

Marla's scanner spiked. The energy signature around Dale wasn't defensive. It was administrative.

***

Dale's heart hammered against his ribs, a frantic drumbeat that he ignored. His throat still burned from the Architect's grip, the bruise already forming a purple halo around his neck.

*Three minutes.*

The Authority's voice had been flat, devoid of empathy. *Clean this up.*

Dale looked at the line of Enforcers. They were closing distance.

Fifty yards.

Forty.

The ground beneath their feet didn't ripple; it simply ceased to exist in their wake, replaced by the same white nothingness that surrounded Dale.

He looked at his Field Guide. The entry for **AUTHORITY ENFORCER** was blank. No weakness listed.

No habitat data. No protocol.

This was a problem.

In his fourteen years of pest control, Dale had learned that you cannot kill what you do not understand. You cannot spray a nest you haven't found. You cannot seal an entry point you haven't documented.

The Enforcers were not monsters. They were employees.

Dale took a step forward. The white light from his skin pulsed with his heartbeat. He could feel the Authority watching him, a cold, bureaucratic gaze pressing down on the back of his neck.

He needed a weakness.

The first Enforcer lunged, which was fast, a blur of matte black armor. Dale didn't dodge. He stepped to the side, a minimal movement, preserving his energy.

The Enforcer crashed into the space where he had been, its claws carving three parallel gouges into the white void.

Dale raised his clipboard.

*Identify.*

He needed to see the structure.

The Enforcer turned, its headless torso rotating toward Dale. It raised a hand, fingers elongating into sharp, needle-like points.

Dale squinted. He looked at the joints.

The knees.

The elbows. The neck.

They were smooth. Seamless.

A mistake? No. A feature.

Unless.

Dale thought of the Architect. The bone-mask.

The way it had shattered when the spray hit the eye socket, and the Authority was made of light and order.

Order has seams.

He looked at the Enforcer's chest plate. There was a faint, pulsing line running down the center. A vent? A seal?

Dale reached into his utility belt. He didn't have a spray can. He didn't have a poison. He had his inspection stamp.

The rubber stamp he used to mark completed inspections. *PASS. FAIL. PENDING.*

He ripped the rubber off the handle.

The Enforcer charged again. Dale waited. He waited until the creature was within arm's reach.

He could smell it now. Not rot, but ink.

Dry, fresh ink.

Dale slammed the rubber stamp onto the Enforcer's chest, right on the pulsing line.

*THWACK.*

Nothing happened.

The Enforcer's claw struck Dale's shoulder. Pain, white-hot and electric, radiated down his arm. Dale gritted his teeth. He twisted, using the momentum to slide across the white floor.

*Document.*

He looked at the stamp mark on the Enforcer. The ink was black, stark against the matte armor.

The Enforcer paused. It tilted its head.

A System prompt flashed in Dale's vision, red and urgent.

**[FIELD GUIDE ENTRY UPDATED: AUTHORITY ENFORCER.]** **[WEAKNESS: ADMINISTRATIVE INCOMPLIANCE.]** **[DESCRIPTION: Enforcers operate on rigid logical protocols. They cannot process contradictory data streams. Physical damage is minimal. Conceptual rejection is fatal.]**

Dale stared at the text. His lips curled back in a grimace.

Conceptual rejection, and he wasn't fighting a monster. He was fighting a bureaucracy.

The next three Enforcers converged. Dale scrambled to his feet. His shoulder throbbed.

He looked at his clipboard, and the page was blank.

He needed to write.

The first Enforcer swiped at his legs. Dale jumped, the air thin and cold. He grabbed a chunk of the white void—a solidified fragment of reality that had been erased—and threw it. It shattered against the Enforcer's armor, doing no damage.

But the Enforcer stopped, which was analyzing the throw. Calculating the trajectory. Assessing the threat.

Dale pulled out his pen. He wrote in the air, the ink hanging in the void like a suspended cloud.

*Violation: Unlicensed Existence.* *Violation: Structural Instability.* *Violation: Contamination of Permanent Record.*

The words glowed white.

The Enforcers slowed. Their heads snapped in different directions, processing the input.

Dale kept writing. He wrote faster, his handwriting cramped and jagged. He was channeling all the frustration of fourteen years of dealing with homeowners who didn't clean their kitchens, all the anger of seeing code violations ignored, all the exhaustion of knowing that people died because the paperwork wasn't done.

*Violation: Failure to Maintain Habitat Integrity.*

The first Enforcer twitched. A crack appeared in its chest plate, right where Dale had stamped it.

The System chimed.

**[THOROUGHNESS RATING: 4.2]**

It had gone up. By zero point one.

Dale kept writing. He wasn't trying to kill them. He was trying to audit them.

The second Enforcer lunged. Dale didn't move. He held his ground.

"Stop," Dale said. His voice was dry, bored. "You are out of compliance."

The Enforcer froze. Its arm hovered inches from Dale's face.

Dale looked at the creature, and he could see the light inside it, the pure energy of the Authority. And he could see the code running through it. The rules.

The rigid, unyielding rules.

He pulled out his phone. The screen was cracked, but it still worked. He opened the Municipal Code, Section 4B, Subsection 9: *Prohibited Conduct by Licensed Entities.*

He held the phone up.

"Display," Dale commanded.

The phone screen flashed. The code scrolled rapidly.

The Enforcer's head snapped toward the phone. It tried to read.

Bureaucracy feeds on bureaucracy.

Dale swiped through the codes. *Section 12. Section 15. Section 33.*

The Enforcer's body flickerred. It was glitching. The logic loop was breaking.

"Section 44," Dale muttered, tapping the screen. "Prohibition on self-referential processing."

The Enforcer screamed. A silent, psychic shriek that tore at Dale's eardrums.

Its body collapsed into a pile of white dust.

Dale didn't celebrate. He stepped over the dust and kept writing on his clipboard.

The remaining Enforcers paused. They looked at each other. They looked at Dale.

He held up the clipboard. The page was now covered in dense, small script.

"I have documented your non-compliance," Dale said. "You are subject to termination."

He clicked his pen.

**[SYSTEM ALERT: USER HAS ACTIVATED 'LEGAL ADVERSARY' PROTOCOL.]** **[COST: 50% OF CURRENT STAMINA.]** **[DAMAGE: INSTANTANEOUS PURGATION OF NON-COMPLIANT ENTITIES.]**

Dale felt his strength drain. His knees buckled. He leaned heavily on his clipboard, using it as a cane.

But he didn't stop, and he looked at the second Enforcer.

"Violation of Protocol 7: Use of Excessive Force against Licensed Personnel."

He pointed his pen at the creature.

The Enforcer dissolved.

White dust. Then more.

Dale walked through the cloud of dust, writing as he went. He was an exterminator now. He was spraying paperwork.

The line of thousands broke. They didn't flee. They paused. They waited.

Because they had never been audited before.

Dale reached the edge of the white void. The pavement of the real world was still there, Marla and Greg standing on it, their eyes wide.

Dale stopped. He was out of breath. His white glow was fading, returning to the dull, beige skin of a middle-aged man.

He looked up at Marla.

"Did you see that?" he asked. His voice was raspy.

Marla nodded slowly. She looked at her scanner. Then she looked at Dale.

"Your Thoroughness Rating," she said. "It's... it's not going up anymore."

Dale frowned. He checked his vision.

**[THOROUGHNESS RATING: 4.2]** **[STATUS: LOCKED.]** **[REASON: EXCEEDING ADMINISTRATIVE LIMITS.]**

Dale looked at the remaining Enforcers. They were still there. Watching.

He had used a loophole. The Authority didn't like loopholes.

He looked at his Field Guide. The entry for **AUTHORITY ENFORCER** was now fully populated. It listed weaknesses, habits, and—most importantly—a note.

*[NOTE: Enforcers are subordinate to the Licensing Authority.

But the Authority is not a single entity, which is a network. Some nodes are corrupt.

Some nodes are broken.]*

Dale stared at the word *corrupt*.

He looked at the white void. The line of Enforcers parted. A figure stepped through.

It wasn't an Enforcer. It was human.

Old. Tall. Wearing a suit that looked like it had been tailored in a previous century.

He walked toward Dale. He didn't float. He walked. His shoes clicked on the white ground.

He stopped ten feet away.

"Mr. Kowalski," the man said. His voice was smooth, like oiled machinery. "You have violated the spirit of the law, even if you adhered to the letter."

Dale tightened his grip on his pen.

"Who are you?"

"I am the Senior Auditor," the man said. "And I have been watching you for a long time."

The Auditor smiled. It didn't reach his eyes.

"You are good at extermination, Dale. But you are bad at politics. You just deleted a node of the Authority. Do you know what that does to the network?"

Dale didn't answer. He was calculating.

"It creates a gap," the Auditor said. "A hole in the record. And holes are where things get in."

Behind the Auditor, the white void darkened. Not black. Brown.

Rotting.

The smell hit Dale instantly. Sewage. Decay.

A sound came from the dark. A wet, squelching noise.

The Auditor stepped aside.

From the darkness, a shape emerged. It was massive. A mound of flesh and teeth, pulsating with a sickly green light. It looked like a tumor given legs.

It was the source of the Rift.

"The Authority is clean," the Auditor said. "But the Rift is not. It is infested. And you have just declared war on the only force keeping the infection contained."

Dale looked at the beast. He looked at the Auditor.

He looked at his Field Guide.

**[NEW ENTRY: CORRUPTED NODE.]** **[WARNING: THIS IS NOT A MONSTER. THIS IS A DISEASE.]** **[PROTOCOL REQUIRED: SURGICAL REMOVAL.]**

Dale had never done surgery. He was a pest control technician. He sealed cracks. He sprayed nests.

But this wasn't a nest, which was a tumor.

The Auditor held out a hand. In it was a scalpel.

"Your certification requires you to handle Class-V threats," the Auditor said. "This is a Class-V threat.

But it is also a political liability.

If you kill it, you kill the Authority's control over this sector. If you don't, you let it spread.

"

Dale looked at the scalpel, and he looked at the beast.

He looked at Marla, who was shaking her head. *Don't take it.*

Dale took the scalpel.

The weight of it was heavy. Cold.

The Auditor's smile widened.

"Welcome to the big leagues, Dale. Don't let your hands shake."

The beast roared. A sound like grinding bones.

Dale tightened his grip on the scalpel. He adjusted his stance.

He didn't look like a hero. He looked like a man who was about to perform a difficult, illegal maintenance repair.

He clicked his pen.

"Documenting," Dale said.
