Chapter 208: Contaminated Water Supply

# Chapter 208

The surveillance feed from Sector 4 flickered with static, then stabilized into a grainy, monochrome view of a cracked parking lot. On the screen, Dale Kowalski looked less like a monster hunter and more like a man waiting for a bus that had already been cancelled.

He stood perfectly still in the center of the lot, his clipboard held at a forty-five-degree angle. To the untrained eye, he was paralyzed by fear.

To the junior analysts monitoring the Authority's intake stream, he was performing a standard pre-engagement isolation scan.

But the senior auditor, Vex, saw something else. Vex saw a man who was annoyed.

"Look at his posture," Vex said, leaning forward. The holographic interface hovered above the desk, casting a blue pallor over his scales. "He isn't looking at the threat. He's looking at the pavement."

On the screen, Dale tapped the side of his clipboard with a pen. *Tap. Tap. Tap.*

The creature emerging from the fissure in the asphalt wasn't a rat or a beetle.

It was a Void-Stalker, a Class-6 entity composed of jagged obsidian shards and weeping black mist. It shrieked, a sound that shattered windows three blocks away.

The local militia had fled.

The police had barricaded their doors. Dale had not moved.

"Is he stuck?" the junior analyst asked.

"No," Vex replied, his voice dropping an octave. "He's waiting for the substrate to stabilize. Look."

Dale shifted his weight. He took one step to the left, then two steps to the right.

He was mapping the fault lines in the concrete.

The Void-Stalker lunged, its claws tearing through the air.

Dale didn't dodge. He sidestepped, allowing the creature's momentum to carry it past him.

As it swung wide, Dale dropped a small, silver canister onto the ground.

It wasn't a bomb. It was a level. A bubble level.

The canister popped open, releasing a cloud of luminescent blue dust. The dust settled on the ground, highlighting the micro-fractures in the concrete.

The Void-Stalker stepped into the dust cloud.

It didn't attack. It paused. Its obsidian limbs vibrated, reacting to the particulate matter.

"He's tagging it," Vex whispered. "He's not trying to kill it. He's trying to map its resonance frequency."

The junior analyst frowned. "That's not a protocol. There's no 'Resonance Mapping' skill in the Level III certification."

"Watch," Vex said.

Dale pulled out his spray can.

The nozzle was modified, a wide-angle dispersal head attached with duct tape. He sprayed the dust cloud.

The blue particles reacted with the creature's mist, turning a violent, boiling purple.

The Void-Stalker screamed, its shards beginning to drift apart. It wasn't being hurt. It was being deconstructed.

"He's bypassing the damage threshold," Vex noted. "He's exploiting a loophole in the identification protocol. If he can prove the creature is unstable before engaging, the Authority awards the 'Preventative Maintenance' bonus. It's worth triple the points of a direct kill."

On the screen, Dale wrote something on his clipboard. He didn't look up. He just wrote.

***

Dale's hand was cramping. The pen felt like it was glued to his fingers.

The Void-Stalker was currently suspended in mid-air, held in place by the magnetic pull of the resonance dust. It twitched, its shards clicking together like wind chimes in a storm.

Dale ignored the screeching noise.

He focused on the entry point.

The rift was still leaking, a thin ribbon of dark energy connecting the creature's core to the void beyond.

He stepped closer. The heat radiating from the rift singed his eyebrows. He didn't blink.

He checked his Field Guide. The *Null* entry from the previous night was already populated.

The Authority had updated the description based on his observations.

**SPECIES: VOID-STALKER (PROVISIONAL)** **WEAKNESS: RESONANT INSTABILITY** **PROTOCOL: FREQUENCY DISRUPTION**

Dale looked at the creature. It was beautiful, in a horrible, geometric way. It was also a code violation.

The energy signature was too high for a Class-6.

It was bleeding into the local grid.

He adjusted the nozzle on his spray can. He didn't have enough gas for a full saturation. He needed to be precise. He needed to hit the core node.

"Hey! Hey! Is that you?"

The voice came from behind a burned-out sedan. Dale didn't turn. He kept his eyes on the purple dust.

"Go away!" a woman screamed. "He's crazy! Look at him! He's standing there while that thing is trying to eat him!"

Dale glanced at the woman.

She was holding a baseball bat. Her knuckles were white. She looked terrified.

He looked at her, then back at the creature.

"Ma'am, you are violating the perimeter," Dale said. His voice was flat, stripped of any urgency. "Please move to the designated safe zone. This area is under quarantine."

"It's going to kill you!"

"It's already dead," Dale said. "I just haven't signed off on the paperwork yet."

The Void-Stalker lurched forward.

The dust cloud rippled.

Dale stepped back, not away from the creature, but toward the sedan. He needed cover.

Not for protection, but to get the right angle. The rift was located behind the car's trunk. If he sprayed from here, he could seal the leak.

If he sprayed from where he stood, he'd just be making a mess.

The woman swung her bat. She hit the creature's leg. The bat shattered. The shard bounced off the obsidian and embedded itself in the asphalt.

"Help!" she cried.

Dale sighed.

He pulled a second canister from his belt. This one wasn't dust. It was a containment foam, Class-4.

Expensive.

He only had three left.

"Stay down," Dale ordered.

He threw the canister.

It landed at the base of the creature. The foam expanded rapidly, wrapping the lower limbs in a thick, white cocoon. The Void-Stalker roared, its upper body thrashing.

The foam hardened instantly, acting as a temporary anchor.

Dale moved. He slid under the sedan, sliding on his side. He pulled the spray can. He aimed at the rift.

The creature lunged.

Its claw tore through the roof of the car.

Metal shrieked. Dale ducked.

The claw missed his head by an inch. He could feel the wind of it pass over his scalp.

He didn't flinch. He squeezed the trigger.

The spray hit the rift. It didn't seal it. The energy was too strong. The spray sizzled and evaporated.

Dale cursed. He checked his Field Guide. A new warning flashed in his peripheral vision.

**CROSS-CONTAMINATION WARNING: CLASS-4 FOAM INCOMPATIBLE WITH VOID ENERGY. 40% DEBUFF APPLIED.**

His hands shook. The debuff wasn't physical.

It was cognitive.

A fog rolled over his brain. For a second, he forgot how to hold the can. He forgot why he was there.

The procedure felt slippery, like trying to hold onto wet soap.

He forced his eyes to stay open. He blinked rapidly, trying to clear the mental haze.

"Think," Dale muttered. "Think.

Identify.

Document. Isolate."

He looked at the foam. It was reacting to the void energy, turning black. It was becoming part of the threat. He had made it worse.

The woman screamed again. The Void-Stalker was breaking free of the foam. The obsidian shards were sharpening, cutting through the containment.

Dale reached into his pocket. His fingers brushed the broken quill.

It was warm.

Hotter now.

It pulsed against his thigh.

He didn't pull it out. He couldn't. Not yet.

He looked at the ground. The blue dust was still there, glowing faintly. He looked at the foam, now black and oozing. He looked at the rift.

A new idea formed. A dangerous one.

The System rewarded thoroughness. But it also punished inefficiency. If he couldn't seal the rift, he had to isolate it.

But isolation required a physical barrier. He didn't have one.

Unless he used the environment.

Dale stood up. He walked out from under the car. He walked right into the creature's path.

The Void-Stalker raised its claw.

Dale didn't run. He walked. He walked straight toward the rift.

The woman gasped. "He's walking into it!"

Dale reached the edge of the fissure.

The heat was unbearable. His skin blistered. He ignored it.

He looked at the crack in the asphalt.

It was widening.

He pulled out his pen. Not the quill. His pen.

He drew a circle on the ground, directly in front of the rift.

He used the chalk from his pocket. He drew a circle, then an inner circle. Then he wrote a symbol in the center.

It wasn't a magic sigil. It was a standard OSHA hazard symbol for "Do Not Enter."

He stepped back.

The Void-Stalker lunged. Its claw hit the chalk circle.

Nothing happened.

The creature didn't stop. It walked right over the line.

Dale watched. The claw passed through the chalk. The rift pulsed.

Then, a notification flashed.

**INVALID ENTRY. LOCALIZED REALITY DISTORTION DETECTED.**

Dale smiled. A thin, dry smile.

He had lied. He hadn't tried to seal the rift. He had tried to *mark* it as a violation zone.

The Authority's system didn't care about magic. It cared about codes. By marking the area as a hazard, he had triggered a passive defense mechanism in the local grid. The city's infrastructure responded to the "Do Not Enter" sign.

The asphalt beneath the rift rose up. Like a trapdoor, the ground shifted. The concrete folded inward, clamping down on the rift. The Void-Stalker shrieked as its lower half was crushed by the shifting earth.

Dale ran. He ran not away from the creature, but toward the sedan. He grabbed the woman by the arm.

"Move," he said.

She resisted. "What are you doing?"

"Leaving," Dale said. "The job is done."

The ground groaned.

The concrete locked into place.

The rift was gone. The Void-Stalker was trapped beneath tons of displaced asphalt.

It was still alive, but it was contained.

Dale dragged the woman away. They reached the sidewalk just as the ground finished settling.

The silence was absolute.

Dale pulled out his clipboard. He checked the time. 4:12 PM.

He wrote: **INCIDENT CLOSED. METHOD: INFRASTRUCTURAL SEQUESTRATION. POINTS AWARDED: PENDING REVIEW.**

He turned to the woman. She was shaking. She looked at him with wide, horrified eyes.

"You buried it," she whispered.

"I contained it," Dale corrected. "There's a difference. Burial implies disposal. Containment implies future treatment."

He looked at the field guide in his hand. The entry for *Void-Stalker* had changed. The weakness wasn't just resonance. It was *structural integrity*.

A new line appeared in the guide.

**RIVAL IDENTIFIED: THE AUDITOR.** **ACTION REQUIRED: SUBMIT FORM 88-B.**

Dale felt a cold sweat prickle the back of his neck. He hadn't filled out form 88-B. He didn't even know what it was.

But he had to.

He looked at the broken quill in his pocket. It was vibrating.

***

Vex stared at the screen. The feed had cut out.

"He's alive," the junior analyst said. "The Authority just processed the event. Thoroughness Rating: 9.8. Points: 4,500."

Vex didn't smile. He tapped his claws on the desk.

"He didn't kill it.

He trapped it. That's not extermination. That's storage."

"It's efficient," the analyst said.

"It's temporary," Vex said. "And he's already on the next task. Look at his Field Guide logs."

Vex pulled up the data.

Dale's guide was updating in real-time. A new entry was forming.

It wasn't a monster.

It was a location.

**LOCATION: THE SUBURBS.** **STATUS: INFESTED (SYSTEMIC).**

Vex looked at the map of the city. Dozens of red dots were appearing.

Not rifts.

Just... anomalies.

Small things. A crack in a sidewalk.

A puddle that didn't dry. A shadow that moved wrong.

"He's not hunting monsters," Vex said quietly. "He's auditing reality."

The junior analyst paused. "What does that mean?"

Vex turned off the screen. He stood up. His scales ruffled.

"It means he's dangerous," Vex said. "And it means the Auditor is going to have to come down himself."

Vex walked to the window. Outside, the sky was dark. The stars were visible, but they were dimmer than they should be.

"He's filling out the form," Vex said. "And if he finds the error, he's going to try to fix it."

Vex opened a channel.

"Prepare the containment unit," he said. "And bring the backup seals. If Kowalski tries to edit the city code, we're going to have to redact the whole block."

He looked at the map. Dale's location was blinking.

He was moving.

Not away from the danger. Toward it.

He was heading to the municipal water plant.

Vex felt a chill.

The water plant supplied the entire district.

"If he contaminates the water supply," Vex said, "the infestation won't just be in the ground. It'll be in the pipes. In the sinks. In the teeth of every man and woman in this city."

Vex grabbed his coat.

"Let's go," he said. "Before he files his report."
