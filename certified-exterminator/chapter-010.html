Chapter 10

The frass trail stopped at the baseboard.

Dale knelt on the basement floor of 4417 Maple Ridge Drive, his flashlight beam flat against the concrete, and watched the trail dead-end at a gap where the baseboard met the foundation. Not a hole. A gap. Three-eighths of an inch wide, running about fourteen inches along the wall. The kind of gap you'd miss if you weren't looking for it. The kind of gap that meant something small had been going in and out of this space for a while.

He checked his watch. Six hours and twenty-two minutes until the exam.

He pulled out his Field Guide. The entry for Rattus profundus was there, but it was wrong. The text swam slightly, like a page photographed through a dirty lens. CONTAMINATED. SOURCE: IMPROPER PROTOCOL APPLICATION (GENERIC ARTHROPOD SUPPRESSION, PROTOCOL 4). DEBUFF: 40% EFFECTIVENESS REDUCTION. DURATION: 24 HOURS.

Vasquez had done that. Broad-spectrum spray, no identification, no documentation. A Level III technician who'd walked into this building at six a.m., fogged the first floor with Protocol 4, and filed a summary report that read like a receipt. Dale had seen the report. Four sentences. No species identified. No treatment zone mapped. No follow-up schedule. The Authority had stamped it 3.1 on the Thoroughness Scale and moved on.

And in doing so, Vasquez had contaminated Dale's field guide entry for the exact species he was supposed to eliminate in fourteen hours.

Dale closed the guide. He looked at the gap in the baseboard. He looked at the frass.

Small pellets. Dark, glossy, about the size of a grain of black pepper. Not the larger pellets he'd documented on the first floor, where the adult had been. These were juvenile frass. Smaller. More uniform. The pellets came in pairs, deposited at regular intervals, which meant the creature was using this trail as a fixed route. Not exploratory. Routine.

That meant there was a nest. Not the mother behind the door. Something else. A satellite cluster. The kind of thing you'd find in any residential infestation once the primary nest got crowded.

Dale followed the trail back the way it came. He crawled on his elbows, clipboard wedged under one arm, flashlight in his teeth. The trail wound behind a furnace, under a shelf of paint cans, and terminated at a gap in the drywall behind a stack of old newspapers.

He set the flashlight down. Pulled on nitrile gloves. Removed the newspapers one at a time, checking each page for damage patterns. Water staining on the bottom three. Chewing marks on the upper edges. Not rat gnawing. Too fine. The teeth marks were small and paired, like a needle being pushed through paper.

He peeled back the drywall.

The cavity behind it was about two feet deep and eighteen inches wide. The flashlight beam found three juveniles clustered in a ball of shredded insulation. They were the size of large mice. Their fur was darker than the adult he'd documented, almost black, and their eyes were oversized for their skull structure. The Authority's notification system had not flagged them. They were too small, too still, too deep in the wall for the perimeter scan to register.

But Dale could see them. And seeing them was the same thing as identifying them.

He opened his Field Guide. The contamination notice pulsed at the top of the Rattus profundus entry. He turned the page. Blank space. Waiting.

He started writing.

DIMENSIONAL VERMIN (JUVENILE). SPECIES: Rattus profundus (immature). SIZE: approx. 14 cm body length. FUR: black, dense, moisture-resistant. EYES: oversized, no visible nictitating membrane. DENTITION: needle-point incisors, paired, suited to soft materials (paper, insulation, skin). BEHAVIOR: clustered, non-aggressive when undisturbed. TRAIL: fixed route, frass deposited in pairs at 4 cm intervals.

He sketched the dentition pattern from memory. He noted the frass pairing. He wrote the entry point gap measurement. He documented the cluster size and estimated the nest age at four to six weeks based on the juvenile size relative to the adult he'd previously identified.

The Authority notification appeared.

FIELD GUIDE ENTRY CREATED: Rattus profundus (juvenile). STATUS: ACTIVE. CROSS-REFERENCE: Rattus profundus (adult) [CONTAMINATED]. GENUS-LEVEL BRIDGE AVAILABLE.

Dale read that last line twice.

GENUS-LEVEL BRIDGE.

The system recognized that his new entry shared a genus with the contaminated entry. That meant he could reference the new entry during combat against the adult. Not at full strength, because the adult entry was still contaminated, but at a reduced bonus. He did the math in his head. The targeted damage bonus from a clean entry was 25%. The contamination debuff was 40%. A genus-level bridge would give him partial access. Maybe fifteen percent. Maybe ten.

It was better than zero.

He sealed the cavity with a strip of painter's tape and a layer of caulk from his kit. Not treatment. Not extermination. Just documentation and temporary exclusion. The juveniles were not the exam subject. The Authority was clear about that. The exam subject was the adult behind the door on the second floor.

Dale climbed out of the basement. He checked his watch. Five hours and eleven minutes.

The kitchen smelled like Protocol 4. Synthetic pyrethroid, heavy on the solvent, the kind of formulation that left a film on every surface and made your sinuses burn for hours. Vasquez had fogged the whole first floor. Dale could see the residue on the counters, a faint white haze that would take three cleanings to remove.

Vasquez was sitting at the kitchen table. He was younger than Dale by fifteen years, maybe twenty. Lean, sunburned, with a certification badge pinned to his chest like a medal. LEVEL III. He had his boots on the table. His clipboard was empty.

"You found something," Vasquez said. Not a question.

"Juveniles. Satellite cluster behind the furnace wall. Three of them."

"I didn't see any juveniles."

"You weren't looking at the frass trail."

Vasquez shrugged. "Protocol 4 covers all arthropod and rodent-class organisms within the treatment radius. If there were juveniles, they're dead."

"They're behind drywall. Protocol 4 doesn't penetrate drywall."

Vasquez looked at him. Something shifted in his expression. Not anger. Dismissal. The look of a man who had decided you were not worth the energy of an argument.

"You know what your problem is?" Vasquez said.

"Contamination."

"No. You treat everything like a code violation. You want to write it up, schedule a follow-up, put a hold on it. That's not what this is."

"What is it?"

"It's a thing that will eat you if you don't kill it fast enough."

Dale looked at the door. The door at the end of the hallway that led to the second floor. Behind it, something was waiting. He could feel it. Not through any supernatural sense. Through the specific knowledge that a confined organism with a fixed territory would eventually test its boundaries. The hold was still active. The creature could not leave the room. But it could push. It could lean against the door and feel for weaknesses.

"You're going to fail," Vasquez said. "The exam doesn't care about your documentation. It cares about the kill. You get points for the kill. The documentation is a bonus, not the main event."

"The system awards points for full protocol completion. Identification, documentation, isolation, elimination, verification. Five phases. You skip any of them, you lose points."

"Sure. And if you're dead, you lose all the points."

Dale didn't answer. He was looking at his Field Guide. The juvenile entry was clean. The adult entry was contaminated. The genus-level bridge was active. He calculated again. Fifteen percent damage bonus against the adult. His current protocol toolkit included three options: Protocol 2 (Targeted Chemical Application), Protocol 5 (Mechanical Containment and Extraction), and Protocol 7 (Thermal Disruption).

Protocol 7 was the fastest. It worked by raising the ambient temperature of the treatment zone to a level that denatured the creature's cellular structure. It was designed for arthropod infestations. Rattus profundus was not an arthropod. It was a mammal, albeit a dimensional one. Using Protocol 7 against a mammal-class organism would trigger the cross-contamination penalty. Forty percent debuff. On top of the existing contamination debuff.

That was not an option.

Protocol 2 was chemical. Effective against rodents, including dimensional variants, if the species entry was clean. The entry was not clean. The bridge would give partial effectiveness. Maybe enough. Maybe not.

Protocol 5 was mechanical. Containment and extraction. Slow. Methodical. Required the creature to be isolated in a confined space before extraction. Dale already had that. The creature was behind a door in a room with no other exits. He could seal the room, enter, extract.

But Protocol 5 required the creature to be subdued first. And subduing it required either chemical or thermal suppression.

He was going in circles.

He sat down. He opened his clipboard. He started writing the treatment plan for the exam. Protocol 2, applied through the door gap. The gap under the door was half an inch. He could feed a nozzle through it. Apply a concentrated dose directly into the room. The creature would be exposed to the chemical agent through inhalation and dermal contact. The juvenile entry gave him a partial damage bonus. The adult entry was contaminated but not destroyed. The genus bridge meant the system would recognize his chemical application as targeted, not generic.

It was not clean. It was not thorough. But it was within protocol.

He checked his watch. Four hours and forty minutes.

Vasquez was gone. He'd left through the front door. His boots had not left prints on the floor, which meant he'd worn boot covers. Dale noticed this. He noticed that Vasquez had not left boot covers in the trash. He'd carried them out.

That was professional. Dale filed it away.

He went upstairs. The hallway was quiet. The door at the end was closed. The hold was still active. He could see it in the faint blue shimmer along the doorframe, the Authority's containment signature. It pulsed slowly, like a heartbeat.

Dale set up his equipment. Chemical canister, pressurized. Nozzle assembly, flexible, four inches long. Sealing tape for the door gap. Nitrile gloves. Respirator. Safety goggles.

He wrote the time on his clipboard. He wrote the address. He wrote: EXAMINATION PROTOCOL INITIATED. TREATMENT METHOD: TARGETED CHEMICAL APPLICATION (PROTOCOL 2), DELIVERY VIA SUB-DOOR APERTURE. ESTIMATED EXPOSURE TIME: 90 SECONDS. POST-TREATMENT: VENTILATION, VERIFICATION, DOCUMENTATION.

He taped the gap under the door. He fed the nozzle through the seal. He pressurized the canister.

The door handle turned.

Dale froze. The hold was active. The creature could not leave the room. The Authority's containment signature was still pulsing along the doorframe. But the handle was turning. Slowly. Deliberately. Like something on the other side had learned how doors worked.

The notification appeared.

HOLD INTEGRITY: COMPROMISED. CONTAINMENT BREACH DETECTED. SOURCE: NON-PHYSICAL MANIPULATION (COGNITIVE OVERRIDE). HOLD TERMINATED.

Dale looked at the door. The handle was fully rotated. The door was unlatched.

It was not opening. Not yet. But the hold was gone. Whatever was behind that door could leave at any moment.

The Authority notification changed.

EXAMINATION PARAMETERS UPDATED. SUBJECT STATUS: UNCONTAINED. TREATMENT WINDOW: IMMEDIATE. NOTE: PREVIOUS CONTAINMENT HOLD VOIDED DUE TO COGNITIVE OVERRIDE EVENT. SUBJECT HAS DEMONSTRATED INTELLIGENCE CLASS 3.

Dale read that line. INTELLIGENCE CLASS 3.

He had never encountered an Intelligence Class 3 organism. His Field Guide had no entry for it. His protocols had no entry for it. The system had no framework for it.

The door opened.

The thing on the other side was not a rat. It was not a mammal. It was not anything Dale had ever documented, identified, or written up. It stood on two legs. It was the size of a man. Its face was wrong, too smooth, like a mask pressed over a skull that had been rebuilt from the inside. It looked at him. It smiled.

The notification appeared.

UNLICENSED INFESTATION DETECTED. CERTIFICATION LEVEL REQUIRED: III. CURRENT LEVEL: II. TREATMENT AUTHORIZATION: DENIED.

Dale stood in the hallway with his canister in his hand and his clipboard under his arm. The thing smiled wider.

He could not touch it. He could not treat it. He could not earn a single point from it.

And it was walking toward him.
