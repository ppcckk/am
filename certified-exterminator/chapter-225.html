Chapter 225: Paperwork with Yourself

# Chapter 225

The threads tightened.

They didn't snap. They constricted. A slow, wet squeeze that tightened the air around Dale's shoulders.

He didn't pull back. Pulling back would signal prey.

Prey ran.

Dale was a technician. He stayed put.

His pen hovered over the clipboard.

The ink was running, mixing with the condensation that had bloomed on the page. The digital pathogen had moisture content.

That was new.

Viruses didn't usually weep.

*Note: Pathogen exhibits hydrophilic properties. Likely vector: airborne aerosol or direct fluid contact.*

Dale tilted his head. The black mesh above him rippled. It wasn't just watching. It was measuring.

He stepped forward. One foot.

Two.

The floor beneath his boots squelched.

The organic mesh had replaced the concrete entirely, forming a spongy, fibrous mat that resisted the sole of his steel-toed boot. It felt like walking on a dense sponge soaked in oil.

To his left, a cluster of glowing threads detached from the wall. They drifted toward him, humming a low, discordant note that vibrated in his teeth.

Dale held up his left hand. He didn't wave it. He held it steady.

Palm open. Fingers splayed.

The threads stopped.

They hovered an inch from his glove.

"Standard containment hold," Dale muttered. His voice sounded thin in the damp air. "No sudden movements. No aggressive posturing."

The threads pulsed. Green light flickered along their length, then faded to black.

He clicked the pen again.

*Behavior: Curious. Reactive to stillness.*

He needed distance. The threads were close enough to brush his shoulder, but he needed to see where they came from. He needed to identify the source node.

He took a step back.

The tunnel groaned. A low, grinding sound, like heavy machinery struggling to turn.

The threads lashed out. Not at him. At the space he had just occupied.

They struck the air, leaving trails of green sparks that fizzled and died.

Dale didn't flinch. He noted the range. Three meters.

*Threat Assessment: Melee range equivalent. Aggression triggered by displacement.*

He looked down at his boots. The sole had sunk into the mesh. He lifted his right foot. The mesh tried to hold it, sticky and tenacious.

He yanked it free with a wet pop.

A strand of the black fiber tore away, attached to his boot. It writhed, trying to reattach itself to the wall.

Dale stared at it.

This was a cross-contamination risk.

If he walked out of here with a piece of the Nexus's nervous system stuck to his shoe, he'd be treated as a carrier. The Authority would flag him immediately. His Thoroughness Rating would tank.

His certification would be suspended.

He couldn't let that happen.

He reached for his utility belt. He bypassed the spray canister. He bypassed the flashbang. He went for the roll of industrial-grade polyethylene sheeting he kept for sealing off hazardous material sites.

It was heavy. Thick. Designed for asbestos removal, not eldritch horror.

He unrolled a strip. The plastic crinkled loudly.

The threads recoiled.

Dale froze. He waited.

The threads relaxed, returning to their gentle sway.

"Static charge," he said. "They're sensitive to electrical discharge. And tactile feedback."

He laid the plastic down on the floor between his feet. A makeshift barrier.

He stepped onto the plastic.

The mesh didn't grab him.

He moved forward, careful to keep his boots on the plastic sheet. It was slow. Tedious. He had to lay down a path step by step, like paving a driveway in the middle of a swamp.

Behind him, the tunnel entrance closed. The black fibers knit together, weaving a wall of muscle and code.

He didn't turn around.

*Isolate,* he thought. *If the exit seals, I need another exit.*

He looked at the walls. The fibers were dense, but there were gaps. Cracks in the organic lattice where the original concrete showed through.

He pointed his pen at a crack ten feet to his right.

"Entry point," he said.

He moved toward it. The threads tracked him, but they didn't attack.

They seemed confused.

He was moving too slowly. Too deliberately. It didn't register as a threat.

It registered as an anomaly.

The Authority liked anomalies.

A notification pinged in the corner of his vision.

*Field Guide Update: Species Unidentified. Classification: Digital-Organic Hybrid.*

*Bonus: None.*

*Note: Identification incomplete. Weakness unknown.*

Dale ignored it. He reached the crack. He pressed his gloved hand against the exposed concrete.

It was cold. Hard. Real.

He pulled his multi-tool from his belt. Not a weapon. A pry bar.

He jammed the tip into the crack.

The threads shrieked.

A high-pitched, digital scream that shattered the silence. The green lights flared, blindingly bright. The mesh on the walls contracted violently, trying to crush the intruder.

Dale held on. He twisted the bar.

The crack widened.

He didn't need to break through. He just needed to see what was on the other side.

He squinted into the gap.

Inside the wall, behind the black fibers, was not stone.

It was a server rack.

But it wasn't a normal server rack.

The metal casing had been fused with bone.

White, jagged bone protruding from the motherboard slots. The cables weren't copper.

They were veins, pumping a thick, black fluid that glowed with a faint, sickly green.

Dale pulled his hand back.

He wiped his glove on his pants.

He turned away from the crack.

He looked at the tunnel ahead. The darkness was no longer empty.

The mass of threads had coalesced. It formed a shape. A humanoid shape.

It stood at the far end of the tunnel, maybe fifty feet away.

It was tall. Thin. Its limbs were made of tangled cables and woven fibers. Its head was a smooth, black sphere, devoid of features, save for a single, pulsing green eye that blinked with a rhythmic, irregular pattern.

It raised a hand.

Dale raised his clipboard.

"Identify yourself," he said.

The creature tilted its head.

*Species: Nexus Guardian? No. Nexus Parasite?*

He clicked his pen.

*Hypothesis: The Authority's reboot failed because the backup data was corrupted by a bio-digital symbiote. This entity is the symbiote.*

The creature took a step. The floor rippled.

Dale didn't move.

He needed a weakness. Every monster had one. Even code.

He looked at the creature's feet. They weren't solid. They were dissolving into the mesh floor, constantly regenerating.

He looked at its hands. They were manipulating the air, creating small, sharp shards of solidified data.

He looked at its chest. There was a core. A pulsing node of light, wrapped in layers of protective fiber.

He looked at its head. The eye.

The eye blinked.

Once. Twice.

Then it opened wide.

A beam of green light shot out.

Dale didn't dodge. He couldn't. He was trapped by his own plastic sheeting.

He braced for impact.

The beam hit the plastic sheet.

It sizzled. It melted.

But it didn't penetrate.

The polyethylene, designed to block microscopic particles and chemical splashes, acted as an insulator. The digital energy had no physical medium to ride on. It dispersed.

Dale looked at the melted spot in the plastic.

*Weakness identified: Physical insulation blocks energy-based attacks.*

He smiled. A thin, tight smile.

"Code violation," he said. "Unshielded energy discharge in a confined space."

He pulled a second roll of plastic from his belt. And a third.

He wrapped himself.

Layer after layer.

The creature fired again.

The beam hit the first layer. It melted.

The second layer. It melted.

The third layer. It held.

Dale was now a mummy of white plastic and blue tape.

The creature paused. It tilted its head again.

It didn't understand.

This wasn't combat. This wasn't fear. This was protocol.

The creature stepped closer. It raised a hand, forming a claw of solidified data.

It struck Dale.

The claw pierced the outer layers. It sank into the plastic.

It stopped.

It couldn't penetrate the inner layers. The density of the polyethylene was too high for a physical strike, and the insulation was too thick for energy transfer.

Dale was safe.

He was also blind.

He couldn't see out. He had to rely on sound.

The creature growled. A low, modulated buzz.

It struck again. And again.

Dale counted the impacts.

One. Two. Three.

He felt the vibrations in his chest.

He counted to ten.

The creature was wasting energy. It was exhausting itself.

Dale waited.

He waited until the strikes slowed.

Then he waited until they stopped.

Silence.

He pulled off the outer layer of plastic.

The creature was standing still. Its green eye was dim.

It was drained.

Dale stepped forward.

He didn't attack. He didn't run.

He walked up to the creature.

He held up his clipboard.

"Documentation," he said.

The creature didn't move.

Dale reached into his belt. He pulled out a marker.

He uncapped it.

He walked past the creature.

He walked to the server rack in the wall.

He found the main power conduit. A thick bundle of bone and wire.

He didn't cut it.

He didn't burn it.

He drew a circle around it.

He wrote in large, black letters: *QUARANTINE ZONE.*

Then he turned back to the creature.

"Your source is isolated," he said.

The creature's eye flared.

It lunged.

Dale didn't move.

He had anticipated this.

He had laid a trail of sticky traps. Industrial flypaper, repurposed for this exact scenario.

The creature stepped into the trap.

Its leg stuck.

It tried to pull free. It couldn't.

The more it struggled, the deeper it sank into the adhesive mesh.

Dale stepped back.

He watched it struggle.

It wasn't just stuck. It was being absorbed.

The flypaper was laced with a mild neurotoxin he used for wasp nests. It wasn't lethal to humans. But to a creature made of neural net and organic matter?

It was a disruptor.

The creature's movements slowed. Its eye flickered.

Dale pulled out his phone.

He dialed the emergency line for the Licensing Authority.

It rang.

It didn't answer.

The line was dead.

Dale frowned.

He dialed again.

Still dead.

He looked at the creature. It was slumping. The fibers were unraveling. The green light was fading to a dull, sickly yellow.

He had neutralized the immediate threat.

But the tunnel was still active. The walls were still pulsing.

And his phone wasn't working.

He checked his Field Guide.

*Status: Offline.*

*Connection: Severed.*

He looked up at the ceiling.

The black fibers were forming a new pattern.

Not a monster.

A map.

It showed the entire city.

And at the center of the map, a red dot.

The dot was moving.

It was coming toward him.

Dale clicked his pen.

He wrote: *New Violation: System Compromise. External Actor Detected.*

Underneath, he wrote: *Protocol Proposal: Evacuate.*

He looked at the creature. It was dead. Or close to it.

He looked at the map.

The red dot was getting closer.

It wasn't a monster.

It was a technician.

And it had a clipboard.

Dale's blood ran cold.

He knew that clipboard. He had seen it on every inspection site he had ever worked.

It was his own.

But he was still holding his.

He looked at the red dot.

It was wearing a Class II technician uniform.

It was wearing his face.

The Authority hadn't just been infected.

It had been cloned.

Dale dropped his phone.

He picked up his pry bar.

He had to get out.

He had to find the real source.

Because if the Authority was cloning him, then there was more than one Dale Kowalski.

And only one of them was going to pass the exam.

The walls shook.

The map disappeared.

The ceiling cracked open.

A hand dropped down.

It was white. Pale. Human.

It grabbed Dale by the collar.

He looked up.

A face stared down at him.

It was his face.

But older.

And smiling.

"Welcome back, Dale," the thing said. "We have a lot of paperwork to finish."
