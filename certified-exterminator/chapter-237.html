Chapter 237: Colony Made

# Chapter 237

Darkness swallowed the room.

The click of the hammer on the gun's firing pin echoed in Dale's skull.

He didn't flinch. He didn't reach for his weapon. He reached for the clipboard lying in the puddle.

His fingers closed around the wet cardboard. The man in the coat raised the pistol. The barrel glinted under the emergency exit sign's green glow.

"Put the clipboard down," the man said. His voice was smooth. Too smooth. Like oil on water.

Dale ignored the instruction. He wiped the water from the report with his sleeve.

The ink ran, but the header remained legible. *Inspection Report 237-B.

Site: Sub-basement 4. Status: Critical Failure.*

"I don't need the clipboard," Dale said. "I need a witness."

The man tilted his head.

"You're fired, Dale. You know the policy.

Termination means your license is revoked.

Your Field Guide is wiped. You're just a civilian in a hazard zone."

The System interface flickered in Dale's peripheral vision. Red text. Angry text.

**LICENSE STATUS: PENDING REVOCATION.** **REASON: UNAUTHORIZED DISCHARGE OF CONTAINMENT PROTOCOL.** **PENALTY: -500 TP. IMMEDIATE TERMINATION OF CAREER.**

Dale tapped the clipboard against his thigh. A dry, rhythmic sound.

"Article 4, Section 9 of the Municipal Code," Dale said. "No technician shall be terminated for failure to comply with a verbal command if that command constitutes an obstruction of justice or the concealment of a Class-4 infestation."

The man laughed. It was a short, sharp bark.

"Class-4? This was a Class-1. You're out of your depth."

"Was it?" Dale pointed a gloved finger at the shattered ice on the floor. "Look at the shards.

Look at the refraction index.

That wasn't water.

That was cryo-stasis gel. High viscosity.

Industrial grade."

The man's gun wavered. Just an inch.

Dale moved. He didn't lunge. He stepped forward, one foot sliding over the wet tile, and slapped the clipboard against the gun barrel.

It was a pest control maneuver. Distraction. Confusion.

The gun fired.

The bullet sparked off the metal frame of the exit sign above them. The ceiling tiles rained down in white squares.

The man shouted, stumbling back into a support pillar.

Dale didn't retreat. He pulled his spray can from his belt. The nozzle was clogged with dried slime, but the pressure gauge was full. He aimed it not at the man, but at the floor between them.

A mist of chemical deterrent erupted.

The man sneezed. He rubbed his eyes. "Is that pepper spray?"

"Isobutane propellant with a trace element of Rift-mite pheromone," Dale said. "It doesn't hurt humans. It tells the mites that the air is saturated with territorial markers."

The man looked around. The shadows in the room seemed to shift. The darkness wasn't empty. It was thick.

"You're bluffing," the man said.

"Am I?"

Dale clicked his flashlight on. The beam cut through the chemical fog.

Small, black shapes skittered along the walls. Hundreds of them.

Rattus profundus.

The Dimensional Vermin from Chapter 1. They weren't attacking. They were waiting.

The man froze. His eyes widened. He stared at the rats, then at Dale.

"You brought them here," the man whispered.

"I didn't bring them," Dale said. "I called them. They're the cleanup crew. They eat anything that violates the hygiene code."

The man lowered the gun. His hands shook.

The System flashed blue.

**NEW PROTOCOL UNLOCKED: SWARM COMMAND (TEMPORARY).** **COST: 15 TP PER MINUTE.** **DURATION: 3 MINUTES.** **CONDITION: ACTIVE FIELD GUIDE ENTRY FOR RATTUS PROFUNDUS MUST BE VERIFIED.**

Dale smiled. It wasn't a nice smile.

He checked his Field Guide on his tablet. The entry for *Rattus profundus* was glowing gold. He had documented it perfectly. He had identified its weakness: sensitivity to high-frequency vibration and territorial aggression toward non-native species.

The man in the coat was non-native. He was unauthorized. He was a violation.

"Get out," Dale said.

The man didn't move. He looked at the ceiling, where the Rift still pulsed with purple light.

"You can't stop it," the man said. "The Authority isn't just a bureaucracy. It's a farm. And you're the crop."

The rats hissed. A unified sound. Like static.

Dale stepped closer. The rats parted for him. They knew his scent. They knew his protocol.

"Who are you?" Dale asked.

The man straightened his coat. The gun was gone. He had holstered it.

"My name is Arthur," he said. "And I'm not here to stop you. I'm here to test you."

"Test me for what?"

"For the Class-V certification."

The lights flickered. The emergency signs buzzed louder.

"Class-V?" Dale asked. "That's for dimensional breaches on a city-wide scale. You don't get Class-V unless you've cleared a Level IV Rift."

"You cleared the Rift," Arthur said.

"You didn't close it. You sealed it.

There's a difference.

Closing ends the job. Sealing creates a pressure valve."

Dale looked at the ceiling. The purple light was swirling. It wasn't watching anymore. It was pulling.

The ice shards on the floor vibrated. They hummed a low, discordant note.

"Arthur," Dale said. "If this is a test, where's the paperwork?"

Arthur reached into his coat. He pulled out a stamp.

It was heavy.

Iron.

It looked like it had been used on documents for centuries.

He stamped the air in front of Dale.

A golden seal appeared in the System interface.

**TEMPORARY LICENSE GRANTED: CLASS-V PROVISIONAL.** **WARNING: YOU ARE NOW RESPONSIBLE FOR ALL INFESTATIONS WITHIN A 10-KM RADIUS.** **PENALTY FOR FAILURE: TOTAL ERADICATION OF PERSONNEL FILE.**

Dale stared at the message. His hands were sweating inside his gloves.

Ten kilometers. That included the residential duplex. It included the school. It included the hospital.

"You're insane," Dale said.

"I'm thorough," Arthur corrected. "And you're slow."

A sound came from the vents above.

It wasn't a scuttle. It was a thud. Heavy. Wet.

Something massive was crawling through the HVAC system.

Dale looked at the vent grille. The metal was bending.

"Is that part of the test?" Dale asked.

"No," Arthur said. He backed toward the door. "That's the penalty."

The vent burst open.

A creature fell into the room. It was the size of a horse. It had six legs, but they were jointed wrong, bending backward like a spider's.

Its body was translucent, filled with glowing blue organs. Its face was a slit in a mask of chitin.

It landed on all fours. The floor cracked under its weight.

The rats fled. The swarm command broke.

**SWARM COMMAND TERMINATED.** **REASON: HOSTILE CLASS-3 ENTITY DETECTED.**

Dale scrambled back. He kicked over a metal shelving unit. It crashed into the creature's legs.

The creature shrieked. It sounded like a dial-up modem amplified through a stadium speaker.

Arthur was already at the door. He punched the keypad. The door didn't open.

"Code change," Arthur muttered.

He looked at Dale. "You have to handle it. That's the rule.

No outside assistance during a practical exam."

The creature lunged. Its mandibles snapped inches from Dale's face.

Dale ducked. He rolled. He came up with his spray can empty.

He had nothing.

No spray. No net. No gun.

He had the clipboard.

He looked at the creature. He looked at the Field Guide on his tablet.

He hadn't identified this species. He had no weakness. He had no protocol.

But he had the Environment.

The room was flooded. Six inches of water.

The creature stood in the water. Its legs were submerged.

Dale looked at the exposed wiring in the wall where the bullet had sparked. Live current. 120 volts.

Not enough to kill a Class-3.

But enough to stun?

Dale grabbed a metal pipe from the fallen shelf. He dragged it through the water. He connected one end to the exposed outlet. He wrapped the other end in rubber insulation from his belt.

The creature turned. It sensed the movement.

Dale wasn't looking at the creature. He was looking at the ceiling.

The Rift was open. The purple light was raining down.

If he electrified the water, the current would arc. It might hit the creature.

Or it might hit the Rift.

If it hit the Rift, the dimensional barrier would collapse. The room would be torn apart.

But if he did nothing, the creature would eat him.

And then it would go to the neighborhood.

Dale looked at Arthur. Arthur was punching the keypad again. "Come on, come on," he whispered.

The creature charged.

Dale threw the pipe.

It wasn't a weapon. It was a conductor.

The pipe hit the water. The live wire sparked. Blue electricity jumped across the surface of the puddle.

The creature slipped. Its legs tangled.

But the current didn't stop.

It flowed upward. Into the walls. Into the ceiling.

The purple light of the Rift flickered.

It turned white.

Then black.

The room plunged into absolute darkness.

Silence.

Dale held his breath. He counted to three.

He clicked on his flashlight.

The creature was gone.

Not dead. Gone.

The water was gone. The ice shards were gone.

Only Dale remained.

And Arthur.

Arthur was standing by the door. The door was open now.

He wasn't looking at Dale. He was looking at the ceiling.

The Rift was closed. Sealed tight.

But there was a hole in the roof. A perfect, circular hole.

Through the hole, Dale could see the sky.

It wasn't night.

It was daytime. Bright, noon sun.

But the sun was wrong. It was too large. And it had rings.

Arthur turned to Dale.

"You passed," Arthur said.

Dale lowered his flashlight. "What happened to the creature?"

"Banished," Arthur said. "Back to its home dimension. That's what electricity does to dimensional entities. It grounds them."

Dale looked at his tablet.

**JOB COMPLETE.** **SPECIES IDENTIFIED: UNNAMED (CLASS-3 CHITINOID).** **METHOD: ENVIRONMENTAL HAZARD.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 4.2.** **NOTE: SLOPPY. NO DOCUMENTATION OF SPECIES FEATURES. NO POST-CONTAINMENT VERIFICATION.** **POINTS AWARDED: 50.**

50 points. For almost dying.

Dale felt bile rise in his throat. He swallowed it down.

"4.2," Dale said. "That's a failing grade for anything else. Why did I pass?"

"Because you didn't run," Arthur said. "And because you used the environment. The Authority rewards adaptability. But it doesn't forgive ignorance."

Arthur walked toward the door.

"Come with me."

"Where?"

"To the Authority. You have a written exam. In one hour."

Dale looked at the hole in the ceiling. The ringed sun stared down at him.

"The sun is wrong," Dale said.

"The Rift didn't just open," Arthur said. "It flipped us.

We're not in Ohio anymore, Dale.

We're in the Buffer Zone. Between dimensions. And you're the only one who can see the infestation clearly."

Dale looked at his clipboard. The paper was sodden. Useless.

He tore the page out. He folded it. He put it in his breast pocket.

"I need a new clipboard," Dale said.

Arthur smiled. It was the first genuine smile Dale had seen from him.

"I'll get you a new one," Arthur said. "And I'll get you a new suit. The old one is contaminated."

Dale stepped out into the hallway.

The walls were breathing. The paint was peeling, revealing eyes underneath.

Hundreds of eyes.

They blinked in unison.

**FIELD GUIDE UPDATE:** **NEW ENTRY: ARCHITECTURAL PARASITE (CLASS-2).** **WEAKNESS: UNKNOWN.** **ACTION: DOCUMENT.**

Dale stopped walking. He raised his hand.

He didn't have a pen.

He didn't have a camera.

He had a pencil stub in his pocket.

He crouched. He drew on the wall.

A simple diagram.

A circle.

A line. A warning symbol.

The eyes stared at him.

They waited.

Dale finished the drawing. He stood up.

**DOCUMENTATION ACCEPTED.** **+10 TP.**

Arthur watched from the doorway. He looked impressed. And terrified.

"You're starting to understand," Arthur said.

Dale looked at the pencil. He looked at the wall.

"I understand," Dale said. "We're not exterminators."

Arthur waited.

"We're the infection," Dale said.

Arthur's face went pale.

The floor beneath them lurched.

The entire building tilted.

**WARNING: STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY FAILING.** **CAUSE: MASS EXODUS.** **SOURCE: YOUR FIELD GUIDE.**

Dale looked at his tablet.

His Field Guide was open. Every entry he had ever made was glowing.

And they were all moving.

They were crawling off the screen.

Into the real world.

"Arthur," Dale said. "I think I made a mistake."

Arthur looked at his own hand.

His skin was turning into chitin.

"No," Arthur said. "You didn't make a mistake. You made a colony."

The ceiling collapsed.

Dale fell.

And the eyes followed him down.
