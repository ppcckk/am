Chapter 70: Identifying Auditor Nine

# Chapter 70

The grey-suited man held out his clipboard like a man extending a handshake. The pen was uncapped. The Field Guide in his other hand was open to a page that Dale couldn't read from this distance, but the entries were crossed out in red ink, and the red ink was still wet.

"You understand what I need," the man said.

"I understand what you're asking," Dale said. "I don't understand why you're asking it wrong."

The man's smile didn't change. It was the same smile.

Not similar.

The same. The exact angle of the mouth, the exact depth of the crow's feet. Auditor Nine had worn it.

This man wore it, which was a face that had been copied and pasted.

"Verification protocol," the man said.

"You verify me. I receive certification. The examination room closes.

We both walk out."

"Walk out where?"

"Into the field.

The infestation zone.

You've been in the examination room for eleven hours. The infestation zone has been expanding.

There are people out there who need a certified technician."

Dale looked at the clipboard. He looked at the man's Field Guide.

He looked at his own Field Guide, still open to Entry 53.

His own name. His own face. STATUS: UNIDENTIFIED.

He set his clipboard down on the examination table. He picked up his pen. He uncapped it.

"Show me your documentation," Dale said.

The man blinked. The first change in expression. "I'm the examinee. I don't have documentation."

"That's the problem." Dale pointed his pen at the man's Field Guide.

"Every entry in that book is crossed out. Crossed out means contaminated.

Contaminated means the entry was used incorrectly.

If you're an examinee, you shouldn't have a Field Guide at all. Examinees don't get Field Guides until after they pass."

"I do have one. The Authority gave it to me."

"Then you're not an examinee." Dale pulled his chair back from the table. "You're a licensed technician who's been contaminated. And contaminated technicians don't get verified. They get recalled."

The man's smile stayed. His eyes didn't move. "You're applying residential protocols to an examination scenario."

"I'm applying verification protocols to a verification scenario. That's the only scenario that exists in this room." Dale opened his Field Guide to the front.

The first fifty entries were grey.

Blank, and he couldn't see the species names, couldn't see the weaknesses, couldn't see the treatment protocols. But he could see the structure.

He'd built this book over sixty-nine chapters of work. He knew the format even when the content was gone.

He flipped to the back. Entry 52.

The Authority.

UNIDENTIFIED. Entry 53. Himself.

UNIDENTIFIED.

"Your verification request requires me to identify you," Dale said. "I can't identify you. I don't have an entry for you."

"You have an entry for me. It's in your guide."

Dale flipped through. Entries 1 through 50: grey.

Entry 51: Auditor Nine.

Entry 52: The Authority. Entry 53: Dale Kowalski.

"I don't have an entry for you."

"I'm the Authority."

"Entry 52 is the Authority. Status: UNIDENTIFIED.

If you're the Authority, I can't verify you, because I can't identify you.

If I can't identify you, I can't verify you.

If I can't verify you, this examination room is a containment zone, not an examination room."

The man's smile held. "That's a procedural technicality."

"That's the protocol."

"Then you understand what happens next."

Dale did understand. He grasped it with the same clarity as a code violation. The system had a rule.

The rule had a condition. The condition had been met.

The consequence was automatic.

The examination room walls shifted. The grey concrete became white. The white became grey.

The grey became a pattern Dale recognized, which was the same pattern as the Authority's notification text. It was the same pattern as the Field Guide page borders.

The room was being reclassified.

CONTAINMENT ZONE ESTABLISHED. SUBJECTS: 2. STATUS: UNIDENTIFIED. PROTOCOL: QUARANTINE PENDING IDENTIFICATION.

"Good," the man said. He sat down across from Dale. He placed his clipboard on the table.

He placed his pen on his clipboard. He placed his Field Guide next to his pen.

"Now we wait."

Dale looked at the walls, and the pattern was moving. Not metaphorically.

The text was scrolling, like a notification feed, but the text was the walls, and the room was made of notifications.

"How long?" Dale asked.

"Until someone identifies us."

"Who?"

"The Authority."

"That's circular. The Authority is Entry 52. Entry 52 is unidentified. The Authority can't identify itself."

"No," the man said. "The Authority can identify itself.

The Authority can identify anything.

The Authority just won't identify you. Because you're the infestation."

The cold started in his chest and worked outward.

Not fear. Recognition.

The feeling of finding a frass trail you'd been looking for.

The feeling of knowing the problem was bigger than the unit you were treating.

"I'm not an infestation," Dale said.

"You're an unlicensed entity performing licensed functions. You've been doing it since the beginning.

You've been treating infestations without a valid Field Guide entry for yourself.

Without a valid entry for yourself, you are, by definition, unidentified.

Unidentified entities in a licensed zone are subject to extermination protocols."

The walls scrolled faster. Dale could read fragments. EXTERMINATION PROTOCOL INITIATED.

TARGET: UNIDENTIFIED. SOURCE: THE AUTHORITY.

AUTHORIZATION: SELF-GENERATED.

"This is a violation," Dale said.

"Which one?"

"SELF-VERIFICATION PARADOX. A licensed technician cannot be subject to extermination protocols because their license IS their identification. My license is valid.

I passed the written exam.

I passed the practical. I have a Thoroughness Rating of 9.2."

"Your Thoroughness Rating is attached to your Field Guide. Your Field Guide entries are contaminated. Your Thoroughness Rating is attached to a contaminated document. A contaminated document is not a valid document."

Dale looked at his Field Guide.

Fifty entries grey. The Thoroughness Rating was printed on the inside cover. He looked at it.

It was still there. 9.2. But the ink was fading.

Not metaphorically, and the numbers were becoming lighter, like a receipt left in the sun.

"That's not in the protocol," Dale said. "Contamination affects entries. It doesn't affect metadata."

"It does if the metadata is embedded in the contaminated document."

Dale opened his Field Guide to the inside cover. The 9.2 was half-visible.

The 9 was readable. The.2 was a ghost. He could see the shape of the decimal point but not the number after it.

"That's a system bug," Dale said.

"It's a system feature."

The walls stopped scrolling. A new notification appeared.

Not on the walls.

On the door. The examination room door, which had been sealed since the man entered, now had a notification panel glowing on its surface.

EXTERMINATION TEAM DISPATCHED. TARGET: UNIDENTIFIED SUBJECTS IN CONTAINMENT ZONE. ETA: 00:04:32.

Four minutes and thirty-two seconds.

Dale looked at the man. The man looked at Dale. They were both sitting at a table in a room made of notifications, and neither of them was moving, and both of them had clipboards, and the difference between them was that one of them had a Field Guide with greyed entries and the other one had a Field Guide with crossed-out entries, and neither of them was identifiable, and the Authority had dispatched a team to kill them both.

"Can you identify me?" Dale asked.

"No."

"Can I identify you?"

"No."

"Then we're both unidentified, and we're both going to be exterminated, and the only way out of this room is for one of us to become identifiable."

"Yes."

Dale opened his Field Guide, and he turned to Entry 53. His own name.

His own face.

STATUS: UNIDENTIFIED. NOTE: You need one. You do not have one.

You have never had one, and he uncapped his pen. He held it over the page.

"What are you doing?" the man asked.

"I'm writing an entry."

"You can't write your own entry. That's self-identification. Self-identification is prohibited under Protocol 7."

"Protocol 7 says you can't write your own entry. It doesn't say you can't write an entry for someone else." Dale turned the page. He turned to

He turned to a blank page.

The one after Entry 53.

The page that shouldn't exist. Every Field Guide had exactly fifty-three entries.

His had fifty-four, and he didn't think about that. He uncapped the pen.

"Your name," Dale said.

"I don't have one."

"Species classification."

"I don't have one of those either."

Dale wrote: UNKNOWN. He wrote it in the species field.

Then he wrote: UNKNOWN in the genus field.

Then he wrote: UNKNOWN in the common name field. The pen moved across the page with the same flat efficiency he used when he couldn't determine the exact subspecies of a German cockroach and had to flag it for re-inspection.

The man watched him write. His face did something that might have been fear.

"You're writing me as a pest," the man said.

"I'm writing you as an unidentified subject pending classification. That's standard procedure when you can't determine species." Dale underlined the entry. "You need a physical description. What do you look like?"

"I look like—" The man stopped. Looked at his own hands. "I don't know what I look like."

Dale looked at the man. Looked, and the face was there. Two eyes, a nose, a mouth.

The proportions were correct.

The symmetry was correct. But Dale couldn't hold the image. It was like trying to read a word you'd seen a thousand times and couldn't parse.

The features were there. They meant nothing.

They assembled and disassembled behind his eyes.

"Standard humanoid morphology," Dale wrote.

"Approximately 180 centimeters. Unremarkable facial structure.

No distinguishing marks. " He paused. " Is that accurate?"

"I think so."

"Then I'm documenting what I observe." Dale wrote: OBSERVED: HUMANOID. UNCONFIRMED.

PENDING FURTHER INSPECTION.

He added the date. He added the time. He added his own certification number.

The page glowed. The entry populated. The Field Guide recognized it.

The man's status changed.

UNIDENTIFIED became IDENTIFIED: PENDING CLASSIFICATION. The greyed-out field on his own guide cleared.

His name appeared, which was a name. A real name. Dale could read it.

It was short. It was common.

It was a name that belonged to someone who had lived a life in this world before the Rift.

The notification on the door changed.

EXTERMINATION TEAM DISPATCHED. TARGET: CLASS IX SUBJECT IN CONTAINMENT ZONE. THREAT ASSESSMENT: EXISTENTIAL.

The man looked at the door. He looked at Dale. His face was doing something that was definitely fear now.

"What did you do?"

"I identified you," Dale said. "That's what the system asked me to do."

"That's not what I am."

"The system doesn't care what you think you are. It cares what the entry says." Dale capped his pen. "The entry says you're Class IX."

The door opened.
