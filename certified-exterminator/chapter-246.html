Chapter 246: Renewal Fees Due

# Chapter 246

The chain-link fence rattled against the concrete pillars.

Dale didn't look at the Hosts. He looked at the gate.

"Locked," Vance said, his voice tight. He tugged at the padlock.

It didn't budge.

"It's not a padlock. It's fused."

"It's a seal," Dale said.

He adjusted his grip on his clipboard. The paper was damp.

"The Authority doesn't lock doors.

They quarantine zones.

"

Sarah leaned against the brick wall, breathing through her mouth. Her hands shook.

"We can't go over the fence.

The Hosts are on the other side.

And if we climb, they'll see us.

"

"They already see us."

Dale turned his back to the fence. He faced the alleyway where the remaining Hosts stood in a perfect semicircle. They weren't attacking.

They were waiting. Their grey, chitinous bodies twitched in rhythm.

"Form up," Dale said.

"What?" Vance dropped the useless padlock. "Form up where? Into a wall?"

"Into a perimeter." Dale pointed to the gap between the dumpster and the fire escape. "Twenty feet of clear line. No debris. No clutter."

"Are you insane?" Sarah pushed off the wall. "They're going to tear us apart!"

"They're going to wait for me to finish writing."

Dale opened the top drawer of his field kit, and he pulled out a marker. Black, permanent, industrial grade. He wrote the number **246** on the back of his hand.

Then he wrote **K-7** on the front of his clipboard.

"Vance, grab the hose from the maintenance closet."

"There is no maintenance closet."

"There is a hose," Dale said, pointing to the side of the building. "I saw it during the pre-survey. It's hooked up to the fire suppression system. It's dry, but the pressure will be enough."

"For what?" Vance asked. He didn't move. "To spray water on demons?"

"To create a slip hazard."

Dale walked toward the Hosts. He didn't run. He walked with the measured pace of a man checking the time on a parking meter.

The Hosts shifted. A low hum emanated from their throats.

It wasn't a threat, which was a signal. They were communicating.

Dale stopped five feet from the nearest one. He raised his clipboard.

"Identify," he muttered.

The System interface flickered in his peripheral vision.

**FIELD GUIDE: CORRUPTED.** **STATUS: UNRELIABLE.**

Dale squinted at the creature. It looked like a wolf, but wrong.

Too many joints.

Too many eyes.

The grey slurry of the previous Host still stained its chest.

He couldn't trust the text. He couldn't trust the weakness.

But he could trust the behavior.

The Host tilted its head. It waited.

"Sarah," Dale said without turning. "How many breaths you've taken since we started running?"

"I don't know. Ten? Twelve?"

"Twelve," Dale corrected. "Your heart rate is elevated. You're hyperventilating.

Stop it. You're contaminating your own decision-making.

"

"I'm scared, Dale!"

"Good. Fear is data. Fear tells you where the danger is. Use it."

Dale tapped his pen against the clipboard. *Click. Click. Click.*

The Hosts twitched in unison with the sound.

"Vance, the hose."

Vance swallowed hard. He walked to the side of the building. He found the red handle. He twisted it.

Nothing happened.

"It's stuck," Vance shouted.

Dale didn't look, and he kept tapping.

"It's not stuck. It's rusted.

You need to hammer it.

"

"I don't have a hammer!"

"Use the butt of your rifle."

Vance swung the stock of his weapon against the metal wheel. The pipe groaned. A trickle of brown water sprayed out.

"More," Dale said. "I need a wet patch. Not a flood. A patch."

Vance hammered again. The water flow increased. It pooled on the asphalt, spreading toward the Hosts.

The Hosts stepped forward, and they didn't run. They walked. Their clawed feet hit the wet asphalt.

They slipped.

Not much. Just a stumble.

But it broke the rhythm.

The synchronization faltered. One Host bumped into another. They hissed.

Dale smiled. A thin, tight expression.

"Slippery surface violation," he said aloud. "Code 404: Improper footing."

The Hosts corrected themselves. They spread out, keeping distance from the water. They were adapting.

Dale wrote on his clipboard. He wasn't writing a report. He was writing a code.

**OBSERVATION:** *Hosts exhibit high cohesion. They move as a single unit. They react to auditory cues (pen clicking). They avoid friction (water/slurry).*

He underlined **friction**.

"Sarah, come here."

Sarah hesitated. Then she walked over. She stayed behind Dale, out of arm's reach.

"Take the clipboard."

"What am I supposed to do with it?"

"Write this down. Word for word."

Sarah took the clipboard. Her hands were steady now. The act of writing seemed to ground her.

"**TREATMENT PROTOCOL: CONTAINMENT VIA ENVIRONMENTAL HAZARD.**"

Dale spoke slowly, enunciating every word.

"We do not fight the infestation. We change the environment so the infestation fails to function."

Sarah wrote. Her pen scratched against the paper.

The Hosts surrounded them. The gap was closing. Ten feet. Eight feet.

Dale looked at Vance.

"The valve. Can you control it?"

"I can turn it off."

"Don't turn it off. Turn it to full blast. Aim it at the fence."

Vance stared at him.

"The fence? Why?"

"Because the Hosts are sealing the block," Dale said. "They aren't chasing us.

They're containing us.

Which means they want us to stay here.

They want us to die here.

"

"Why?" Sarah asked, not looking up from the page.

"Because they're not Hosts," Dale said.

The air went cold.

Sarah stopped writing. Vance froze.

"What?" Vance whispered.

Dale turned to face them. His eyes were wide, but his voice was flat.

"Look at their formation. Look at the way they move. They're not hunting.

They're guarding. They're protecting a perimeter.

"

"Protecting what?" Sarah asked.

"The exit," Dale said. "The Authority didn't seal this block to kill us. They sealed it to keep something else in."

The Hosts hissed. It was a loud, metallic shriek. The nearest one lunged.

Dale stepped back. Not away. To the side.

"Vance, now! The fence!"

Vance twisted the valve. The water jet blasted out, hitting the chain-link fence. The rusted metal shrieked. The water flowed through the gaps, creating a muddy slick on the ground behind the Hosts.

The Hosts didn't move into the water. They stayed on the dry side.

Dale pointed to the ground.

"They're avoiding the moisture. It interferes with their pheromone trails. That's why they're organized.

They're following a scent map.

And we just washed it away.

"

Sarah looked at the clipboard.

"So we're blind to them, but they're blind to us?"

"No," Dale said. "We're just harder to track. Which means we're invisible to the command structure."

One of the Hosts broke from the line. It moved erratically. It bumped into a dumpster. It couldn't find its place.

"It's lost," Dale said. "Protocol successful. Disruption of command hierarchy."

He walked toward the erratic Host. He pulled a flare from his kit. The red light flared again.

This time, he didn't throw it. He held it up.

The Host recoiled. It didn't scream. It just backed away, its movements jerky and uncoordinated.

"Sarah, write this down."

"I'm trying."

"**OBSERVATION: HOSTS DEPEND ON CHEMICAL COHESION. DISRUPTION CAUSES INDIVIDUAL ERRORS.**"

Dale stepped closer to the Host. He raised his clipboard. He wrote **ID: UNCERTAIN** across the page.

Then he looked at the System interface.

**FIELD GUIDE: CORRUPTED.** **NEW ENTRY PENDING: CHEMICAL DEPENDENCY.**

A notification blinked.

**PENALTY WAIVED.** **REASON: PROTOCOL ADAPTATION.**

Dale felt a surge of satisfaction. It wasn't joy. It was the quiet satisfaction of a plumber fixing a leak with a wrench instead of a hammer.

"Vance, keep the water running. Keep the fence wet. Do not stop."

"What are we doing?" Sarah asked. She looked up. Her eyes were bright.

"We're buying time," Dale said. "The Authority wants a thorough job. Thorough means identifying the root cause. If the Hosts are just guards, who is being guarded?"

"Inside the block?" Vance guessed.

"Under the block."

Dale pointed to the manhole cover in the center of the alley. It was rusted shut.

"The Rift didn't open over the neighborhood," Dale said. "It opened under the sewer system. These things aren't invaders. They're maintenance crews."

Sarah dropped the pen.

"Maintenance?" she whispered.

"Look at their tools."

Dale pointed to the Hosts' claws. They weren't sharp for tearing. They were broad, flat. Shovels.

"They're digging," Dale said. "They're widening the tunnels. They're making the breach bigger."

Vance looked at the Hosts. looked. He saw the dirt packed under their claws. He saw the grey slurry wasn't just blood. It was mud.

"They're mining," Vance said.

"Exterminators don't mine," Dale said. "We seal entry points. We don't dig them up."

The Hosts stopped. They turned toward the manhole. They walked toward it in a tight formation.

"They're ignoring us," Sarah said.

"They're following the command to expand the breach," Dale said. "If we stop them, the Authority might let us go. Or they might execute us for obstruction."

"So we stop them?" Vance asked.

"No," Dale said. "We report them."

"Report who? They're monsters!"

"They're unauthorized labor," Dale said. He pulled out his radio. It was broken, but the emergency beacon still worked. He pressed the button.

"This is Technician Kowalski, Class II," he said into the static.

"Code Yellow. I have identified an unlicensed subterranean excavation operation. Perpetrators are non-compliant with dimensional containment statutes.

Requesting immediate citation.

"

Silence.

Then, a voice. Not from the radio. From the air.

**AUTHORITY RESPONSE RECEIVED.** **CLAIM: UNVERIFIED.** **PENALTY FOR FALSE REPORTING: TERMINATION OF CONTRACT.**

Dale looked at the interface.

**WARNING: THRESHOLD APPROACHING.**

He looked at Sarah.

"You have to write it down."

"Write what?"

"The claim. If it's in the Field Guide, it's documented. If it's documented, it's real."

Sarah picked up the pen. Her hand shook, but she wrote.

**CLAIM: SUBTERRANEAN INFRASTRUCTURE COMPROMISED. CULPRIT: CLASS IV VERMIN (EXCAVATORS).**

The System text changed.

**FIELD GUIDE: UPDATING...** **STATUS: PARTIAL CORRUPTION.** **NEW WEAKNESS IDENTIFIED: STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY OF CLAWS.**

Dale felt a shift. The fog in his mind cleared slightly. He could see the cracks in the Hosts' armor. He could see where the mud packed thickest.

"Vance," Dale said. "Throw the hose."

"What?"

"Throw it. Aim for the leader's legs."

Vance didn't ask questions, and he swung the hose like a whip.

The jet of water hit the lead Host's knee joint. The mud sloughed off.

The leg buckled.

The Host fell. It didn't get up. It thrashed.

The other Hosts stopped. They looked at the fallen one. They looked at the water.

They didn't attack. They looked at Dale.

They were waiting for him to act.

Dale clicked his pen.

"If I document them, I can target the weak points," he said. "If I target the weak points, I can collapse the tunnels."

"Collapsing the tunnels will trap us here," Sarah said. "The water will flood the alley. We'll drown."

"Not if we seal the breach first," Dale said. "We don't kill the Hosts. We kill the tunnel."

"How?" Vance asked.

"Dale," Sarah said, her voice sharp. "You're talking about using the System to collapse a sewer system. That's not extermination. That's demolition."

"It's infrastructure maintenance," Dale said. "It's a different certification track."

"You don't have that certification!"

"I have a claim," Dale said. He pointed to the clipboard. "And a weakness. And a hose."

He looked at the Hosts. They were beginning to move again. The leader was struggling to rise. Its knee was shattered.

"If we don't act, the breach expands," Dale said. "If the breach expands, the Authority sends Class V. Class V doesn't negotiate. It sterilizes."

"Which means we die anyway," Vance said.

"No," Dale said. "It means the whole neighborhood dies. And I have a client contract with this duplex. I can't let it be sterilized."

He stepped forward. The Hosts parted. They were confused by the disruption.

Dale raised his clipboard. He wrote **WEAKNESS: STRUCTURAL SUPPORT.**

He looked at the manhole.

"Vance, aim the water at the manhole cover. Sarah, write the report. Final entry."

Sarah wrote. **SUBMISSION: REQUEST FOR EMERGENCY DEMOLITION AUTHORITY.**

The System pulsed.

**REQUEST PENDING.** **DECISION: GRANTED.** **COST: ALL TREATMENT POINTS FOR CURRENT CYCLE.**

Dale felt the weight of the points vanish. He was back to zero. He was a rookie again.

But he had the water, and he had the Hosts.

And he had the cover.

"Pull it," Dale said.

Vance aimed. The jet hit the rusted iron cover. It groaned. The mud around it liquefied.

The Hosts screamed. A collective, high-pitched wail.

They rushed the manhole. They tried to protect it.

But they were slipping. They were confused.

Dale watched them. He didn't move. He just watched.

The cover popped.

It didn't fall in. It flew up.

Because something below hit it.

Something big.

Something that wasn't a rat. Something that wasn't a beetle.

The shadow rising from the hole was vast. It blocked the moonlight.

Dale looked up. He saw eyes. Hundreds of them.

And then he saw the face.

It looked like a spider. But it was wearing a mask. A human face, stretched over its chitin.

It smiled.

"Certification Level IV," Dale whispered.

The spider-monster reached up with a claw the size of a car. It grabbed the manhole cover. It didn't throw it. It ate it.

Then it looked at Dale.

It spoke. Not in a hiss. In English.

"Your license is expired, Technician."

Dale dropped his pen, and he didn't run. He didn't scream.

He picked up the pen.

He clicked it.

"Renewal fees are due," he said.

The spider lunged.
