Chapter 238: Drawing While Falling

# Chapter 238

Fingers tapped a frantic rhythm against the reinforced glass of the observation booth. The monitor displayed a thermal feed from the sub-basement, where the shadows had stopped behaving like shadows.

They pooled.

They thickened. They coalesced into shapes that defied Euclidean geometry, yet remained stubbornly consistent with the biological templates stored in the central database.

"Look at the lateral movement," Supervisor Halloway muttered, her voice tight.

She didn't look at the screen. She looked at the man in the debris field below.

"He's not running.

He's not fighting. He's drawing."

Halloway adjusted her headset, ignoring the static crackle that sounded like wet insects chewing on fiber optics. The Authority's monitoring system was designed to track energy spikes and combat efficiency. It wasn't built to track a man sketching on a falling wall with a nub of graphite.

"Is he insane?" the junior analyst asked, tapping his stylus against his desk. "The structural integrity is at twelve percent. Any second now, the ceiling gives way, and he's paste."

"He's documenting," Halloway corrected. "And the system is accepting it."

On the main display, the notification flashed in bright, authoritative green. **+10 TP.** The numbers weren't just accumulating. They were mutating. The Field Guide entries on Dale Kowalski's tablet were physically manifesting, pulling themselves out of the digital substrate and into the physical space, layering over the crumbling concrete like a topographical map made of chitin and slime.

Halloway pressed the intercom button. "Kowalski. Report status. Why are you stationary?"

Static hissed. Then, a voice, calm as a spreadsheet.

"Assessing hazard vector.

The manifestation isn't random.

It's a citation error."

"Get to the extraction point!"

"The extraction point is contaminated," Dale replied. "I need a clean workspace."

The floor in the observation booth shook. Dust fell from the ceiling tiles. Down in the pit, the Architectural Parasites—the eyes in the walls—were blinking in synchronized horror.

They weren't attacking the man. They were fleeing him.

Or rather, fleeing the data he was forcing into their existence.

Halloway leaned closer to the glass. "What did you do?"

"I corrected the classification," Dale said. "You called them parasites. They're not. They're infrastructure."

The intercom cut out.

***

Dale wiped graphite dust from his forehead, leaving a gray smear across his skin. The air tasted like copper and old drywall. Gravity felt like a suggestion than a law.

When he fell, he didn't hit the ground with a thud.

He hit it with a squelch, as if the floor was made of wet paper.

He rolled, coming up on one knee. The debris field was alive. Shards of rebar writhed like worms. Puddles of plaster pulsed with a faint, bioluminescent glow.

Arthur lay five feet away, his arm encased in a hardening shell of brownish armor. The man was trying to stand, but his legs had fused with a slab of concrete. He looked like a statue halfway through a metamorphosis.

"Get up," Dale said, his voice flat. He wasn't looking at Arthur. He was looking at the tablet strapped to his forearm.

The screen was a mess of moving glyphs. Entries for *Dimensional Vermin*, *Acid Beetles*, and *Chitinous Humanoid* were scrolling off the edges, dissolving into black mist that swirled around Dale's boots.

"Did I... did I break it?" Arthur wheezed. His voice sounded like gravel grinding together.

"No," Dale said. "You broke the rules. I followed them."

Dale checked his inventory.

Pencil stub: intact. Clipboard: missing. Flashlight: dead.

He had nothing but the System interface and the raw, unfiltered data of the infestation.

A chunk of ceiling crashed down where Dale had been standing two seconds ago.

Dust billowed.

Through the gray cloud, a shape emerged. It wasn't a monster.

It was a filing cabinet. A metal, four-drawer office cabinet, sprouting legs made of spinal column and crawling toward them with a clicking, skittering sound.

**NEW ENTRY:** **BUREAUCRATIC GRIEVANCE (CLASS-3).** **WEAKNESS: INVALIDATION VIA CORRECT PROTOCOL.**

Dale stared at the cabinet. His eye twitched.

A bureaucratic grievance.

In a dimensional rift. It made sense, in the horrible, twisted logic of the Authority. If you filed a complaint incorrectly, the Authority didn't dismiss it.

It manifested the complaint as a physical entity that demanded resolution.

"This is your fault," Dale said to the cabinet.

The cabinet opened its middle drawer. A tongue, long and slimy, lashed out, wrapping around a piece of rebar and snapping it like a twig.

Arthur screamed, trying to pull his fused leg free. "Kill it, Dale! Just hit it!"

"I can't hit it," Dale said. "It's a procedural violation. Hitting it is assault. It will escalate the claim."

Dale backed away, circling the crawling furniture. The cabinet was gaining speed, its legs jerking spasmodically. It was heading for the gap in the floor where the original rats had entered. If it got there, it would file a formal appeal against the building's structural foundation.

Dale raised his tablet. The interface was glitching, the text shaking violently.

*Input: Identification.* *Target: Bureaucratic Grievance.* *Current Status: Active.* *Required Action: Submission of Valid Counter-Claim.*

The System didn't have an attack button. It had a *Submit* button.

"Counter-claim?" Arthur gasped. "What does that mean?"

Dale ignored him. He needed to find the error in the Grievance.

Every infestation had a root cause.

Every monster had a weakness. For a bureaucratic entity, the weakness wasn't a knife. It was a lack of documentation.

Dale crouched, ignoring the chitin spreading up Arthur's neck. He pulled the pencil stub from his pocket. He didn't draw on the floor. He drew on the air.

The System recognized the gesture. A holographic line appeared before him, glowing blue.

Dale drew a box. Then another. Then a checkmark.

The cabinet shrieked, a sound like tearing metal. It lunged.

Dale sidestepped, rolling under the cabinet's swinging drawer. He came up on the other side, his hand moving faster than his mind.

He was writing.

Not on paper.

On the System's temporary workspace.

*Claimant: Dale Kowalski.* *Defendant: Bureaucratic Grievance.* *Violation: Failure to provide Form 7B (Preliminary Assessment of Habitat).*

The cabinet froze. Its legs stopped moving. The tongue retracted.

**COUNTER-CLAIM ACCEPTED.** **STATUS: PENDING REVIEW.** **TIME LIMIT: 10 SECONDS.**

"Ten seconds?" Arthur yelled. "What happens then?"

"If I don't submit the Form 7B, the grievance is upheld," Dale said. He began running toward the pile of rubble where the original wall had collapsed. "And the Authority will garnish my soul."

The cabinet lurched forward, faster now, fueled by the pending deadline. Its metal skin rippled, forming faces—angry, red-faced faces shouting silent demands.

Dale reached the rubble. He needed Form 7B. He needed to assess the habitat.

But the habitat was falling apart. The walls were eyes.

The floor was chitin.

The air was data.

He grabbed a chunk of drywall. It was warm. It pulsed.

*Assessment:* The habitat is unstable. *Habitat Type:* Dimensional Breach Zone. *Occupancy:* Overcrowded.

He scrawled the text onto the drywall with his pencil. The graphite smeared, but the System scanned it.

**SUBMISSION: PARTIAL.** **ERROR: INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE OF OCCUPANCY.**

The cabinet was five feet away. It raised a drawer like a hammer.

Dale looked at Arthur. "Your arm."

Arthur looked down at his chitinous limb. It was glowing with a faint, pulsing light. The same light that came from the Field Guide entries.

"My... arm?"

"You're part of the colony now," Dale said. "You're evidence."

Arthur's eyes widened. "No. No, Dale, don't—"

Dale didn't ask permission. He grabbed Arthur's hardened arm. The chitin was warm and slick. Dale dragged Arthur toward the cabinet.

"Hey!" Arthur screamed, trying to pull away, but his legs were still fused. He was dragged across the floor, leaving a trail of brown slime.

Dale held Arthur's arm up, presenting it to the crawling cabinet.

"Exhibit A," Dale said. "Proof of occupancy. Biological integration."

The cabinet paused.

The faces on its metal skin looked confused. It scanned Arthur's arm.

It scanned the chitin.

It compared the DNA signature to the Field Guide entry for *Chitinous Humanoid*.

The System's interface flashed red, then yellow, then green.

**EVIDENCE VALIDATED.** **OCCUPANCY CONFIRMED.** **FORM 7B SUBMITTED.**

The cabinet shuddered. A low hum vibrated through the air, shaking dust from the ceiling.

**VIOLATION DISMISSED.** **ENTITY RECLASSIFIED: NON-VIOLENT ADMINISTRATIVE ERROR.**

The cabinet's legs folded under it. It didn't die.

It didn't explode.

It simply stopped.

The faces melted away, leaving smooth, cold metal. The tongue retracted completely.

It sat in the middle of the room, inert, like a piece of discarded office furniture.

Dale let go of Arthur's arm. He brushed graphite dust off his hands. His heart hammered against his ribs, but his face remained a mask of professional boredom.

He checked his tablet.

**TREATMENT COMPLETE.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 8.5.** **CROSS-CONTAMINATION RISK: LOW.** **PENALTY: NONE.**

"Eight point five," Dale muttered. "Sloppy. I used a civilian as a prop. Deduction for improper handling of non-combatant personnel."

He looked at Arthur. The man was trembling, his face pale, his arm still partially transformed. The chitin was receding, turning back into flesh, but slowly.

"You used me," Arthur whispered.

"I used evidence," Dale corrected. "You were in the way. I documented you."

Arthur stared at him. Fear, cold and sharp, lit up his eyes. "What are you?"

"I'm a technician," Dale said. He stood up, his knees cracking.

The floor was still unstable.

The ceiling groaned. "And we have a backlog."

Dale looked at the inert cabinet.

Then he looked at the hole in the floor where the cabinet had been trying to enter. The darkness below was churning.

More shapes were rising.

Not filing cabinets this time.

Desks. Chairs. Water coolers.

The office furniture was coming.

**FIELD GUIDE UPDATE:** **NEW ENTRY: ADMINISTRATIVE BACKLOG (CLASS-4).** **WEAKNESS: UNKNOWN.** **ACTION: DOCUMENT.**

Dale raised his pencil. His hand shook. Not from fear. From anticipation.

"Arthur," Dale said. "Do you have a stapler?"

Arthur looked up, his mouth hanging open. "A... a what?"

"A stapler," Dale said. "I need to bind the evidence. If I don't, the next wave will be classified as a Class-5 Compliance Audit. And I don't have the clearance for an audit."

The ground lurched again. A massive desk, its legs made of bone, crashed through the ceiling above them.

Dale didn't flinch. He stepped forward, pencil poised.

"Find. Me. A. Stapler," Dale said.

Arthur scrambled backward, crab-walking away from the approaching furniture. "There's a supply closet! On the second floor!"

"Second floor is compromised," Dale said. "The stairs are eating the people."

"Then how do we get there?"

Dale looked at the inert cabinet. He looked at the chitin on Arthur's arm. He looked at his own Field Guide, where the entries were still glowing, still alive, still hungry.

"We don't go to the closet," Dale said. "We bring the closet to us."

He walked over to the inert cabinet. He placed his hand on its cold metal surface.

**INTERACT: ADMINISTRATIVE ERROR.** **ACTION: RECLASSIFY.**

Dale closed his eyes. He focused on the concept of *Filing*. Not fighting. Filing.

The System hummed. A prompt appeared.

**WARNING: RECLASSIFICATION REQUIRES A VALID TARGET CATEGORY.** **CURRENT FIELD GUIDE HAS NO ENTRY FOR 'OFFICE SUPPLY CABINET'.**

Dale opened his eyes. He looked at Arthur.

"Arthur. Your arm.

The chitin.

It's biological.

But it's also structural. It's a support beam."

Arthur looked at his arm, horrified. "What are you doing?"

"I'm creating a new entry," Dale said. "I'm classifying you as a structural support beam."

"That's insane!"

"It's accurate," Dale said. "You're holding up the ceiling."

Dale pressed his pencil against Arthur's forehead.

**NEW ENTRY: STRUCTURAL HUMANOID (CLASS-3).** **WEAKNESS: LOAD-BEARING FAILURE.**

The System accepted it. Arthur screamed as his body hardened, his bones lengthening, his skin turning to wood and iron. He wasn't turning into a monster. He was turning into a pillar.

The cabinet reacted. It sensed a new load-bearing entity nearby.

**TARGET IDENTIFIED: STRUCTURAL HUMANOID.** **ACTION: LOAD BEARING TEST.**

The cabinet stood up. It walked over to Arthur, who was now frozen, his body rigid, his face a rictus of terror. The cabinet placed its feet on Arthur's shoulders.

It climbed.

Dale watched, his pencil hovering over his tablet. He wasn't saving Arthur. He was testing the System.

If Arthur broke, Dale lost a colleague. If Arthur held, Dale gained a tool.

Dale wrote.

*Protocol: Load Distribution.* *Method: Vertical Integration.* *Resource: Humanoid Support Unit.*

The cabinet climbed higher. It reached the hole in the ceiling. It pulled itself up, dragging Arthur with it. Arthur's body stretched, groaning, cracking, but holding.

Dale watched the gap widen. The darkness below receded. The noise from the lower levels faded.

The cabinet disappeared into the upper floors.

Arthur remained, suspended in the air, held up by the cabinet's grip. He was alive. His eyes darted around, desperate.

Dale walked over to him. He pulled a piece of graphite from his pocket and rubbed it on his fingers.

"Good," Dale said. "You held."

"Let me down," Arthur croaked. "Please."

"Not yet," Dale said. "I need to verify the load capacity."

He pulled out his tablet. The Field Guide was stable. The entries were static. The cross-contamination risk had dropped to zero.

Dale looked at the ceiling. The cabinet was gone. But the hole remained. And through that hole, he could see them.

Hundreds of eyes. Not in the walls. In the sky.

The Rift wasn't opening from below. It was closing from above.

And the eyes were watching him. Not with anger. With expectation.

Dale looked at his Thoroughness Rating.

**8.5.**

He looked at the System notification.

**CERTIFICATION LEVEL III EXAM AVAILABLE.** **PREREQUISITE: THOROUGHNESS 9.0.**

He needed half a point.

He looked at Arthur. He looked at the pencil.

"Arthur," Dale said. "I need you to cry."

Arthur's eyes went wide. "What?"

"Emotional distress is a valid metric for habitat instability," Dale said. "If you cry, I can document the psychological impact of the infestation. That's a separate protocol."

"I... I can't just..."

"Think about the chitin," Dale said. "Think about how much it hurts."

Arthur's face crumpled. Tears streamed down his cheek, cutting through the dust and the grime.

Dale watched the tears fall. He watched them hit the floor.

**DOCUMENTATION: PSYCHOLOGICAL IMPACT CONFIRMED.** **+0.2 TP.**

**CURRENT RATING: 8.7.**

Not enough.

Dale looked at the ceiling. He looked at the eyes.

He had one last idea. A loophole. A dirty, underhanded trick that would get him a black mark on his permanent record, but keep him alive.

He walked over to the edge of the hole. He looked down into the abyss.

"Hey," Dale shouted into the darkness. "I'm filing a complaint against the Authority for negligent supervision of dimensional rifts!"

The eyes blinked.

The System froze.

**WARNING: COMPLAINT AGAINST AUTHORITY REQUIRES CLASS-5 CLEARANCE.** **PENALTY: IMMEDIATE TERMINATION OF LICENSE.**

Dale grinned. It was a small, tight smile.

"Then you better process it fast," Dale said. "Because I'm about to file a second one."

The eyes widened. The floor tilted again.

Dale pulled out his pencil.
