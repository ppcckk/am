Chapter 267: Kick It in the Face

# Chapter 267

The net snapped shut with the force of a bear trap.

Dale twisted. He didn't try to dodge.

Dodging meant losing leverage, and losing leverage meant losing the box.

Instead, he dropped his center of gravity, slamming his shoulder into the lead enforcer's chest. The man was armored, but he was still a man. The impact knocked the wind out of him, disrupting the net's tension just enough for the mesh to slip.

Dale rolled. The box tumbled with him, glowing a furious violet against the wet grass.

"Asset retrieval authorized," the enforcer grunted, scrambling up. "Use non-lethal force. The mind inside is sensitive to kinetic shock."

Sensitive.

Dale heard the word. He processed it. The Authority treated the entity inside the box not as a monster, but as a patient. Or perhaps a piece of evidence too fragile to break.

He scrambled to his feet, mud slick on his palms.

The other nine enforcers were closing in. They moved with practiced efficiency, forming a perimeter.

Nets.

Tranq darts. Heavy boots.

Dale didn't look at them. He looked at the box.

The humming had changed pitch. It was a shriek now, high and thin, vibrating in his teeth. The light bleeding from the cracks was blinding.

"You have ten minutes!" Vane's voice echoed in his head. But the enforcers were here. The clock was already running out.

Dale kicked the box. Hard.

The sound was wrong. It wasn't the dull thud of wood or metal. It was the wet, sickening crunch of something internal shifting. The screaming stopped.

The enforcers froze.

"Kowalski," the lead enforcer said, his voice dropping an octave. "Stop."

Dale ignored him. He grabbed the box by the handle, which was fused to the casing by crystallized void-matter. He lifted it. It weighed as much as a cinder block.

He ran.

Not toward the street. Not toward the truck. Toward the crawlspace entrance behind the garage.

The enforcers shouted. One fired a dart. It pinged off the garage door.

Dale slid down the muddy embankment, clutching the box to his chest. His knees hit the dirt, but he kept moving.

His hands were shaking.

Not from fear.

From the vibration. The box was resonating with his own heartbeat.

He reached the crawlspace hatch. It was locked. Padlock. Chain.

He didn't have time to pick it, and he jammed the corner of the box into the chain link and pulled. The metal groaned. The void-matter on the box reacted to the stress, expanding slightly. The chain snapped.

Dale dropped into the darkness.

The smell hit him first. Damp earth. Rot. And underneath it, the ozone tang of residual rift energy.

He pulled the hatch shut above him. The sound of boots thudding against the grass faded.

He lay in the dirt, breathing hard. The box was silent.

He checked his wrist. The Authority interface blinked red.

**STATUS: SUSPECT. Warrants issued for obstruction of federal asset.**

He ignored it, and he opened his Field Guide. The pages were wet, smeared with mud, but the text remained crisp. He flipped to the **VOID MIMIC** page.

He had written: **STATUS: ACTIVE. CONTAINMENT FAILED. THREAT LEVEL: CRITICAL.**

Now, he needed to understand the

"mind."

Vane had said the mind was still in the box. The Authority called it an

"asset."

Dale looked at the box. The cracks were sealing. The violet light was dimming.

He reached into his pocket and pulled out his pen. Not a weapon. A tool.

He uncapped it. He touched the tip of the pen to the box's casing.

Nothing happened, and he tried again, pressing harder. The pen didn't scratch the surface.

Then, an idea formed. A loophole.

The System rewarded identification. It rewarded documentation. But it also rewarded *interaction* if that interaction led to a deeper understanding of the species.

Dale whispered to the box.

"I know you're not a mimic. Mimics imitate objects. You're imitating *containment*."

The box pulsed. A faint, rhythmic throb.

Dale sat up. He pulled his clipboard from his belt. He wrote.

**OBSERVATION 1: The entity within the container exhibits semi-sentient resonance.** **OBSERVATION 2: Physical disruption (kinetic shock) suppresses the entity's active projection phase.** **HYPOTHESIS: The entity is not trapped. It is dormant. The box is not a cage. It is a chrysalis.**

He wrote the last sentence three times.

The System notification chimed. Softly.

**ENTRY UPDATED.** **NEW WEAKNESS IDENTIFIED: RESONANT DORMANCY.** **TARGETED DAMAGE BONUS: +15% against Void Mimic derivatives when disrupting containment integrity.**

Dale stared at the text. He hadn't fought anything. He hadn't used a protocol. He had just written a hypothesis.

And the System had accepted it.

Because he was right.

The box wasn't holding the monster in. It was holding the monster *down*. Keeping it asleep so it could grow.

Footsteps above. The hatch rattled.

"Dale Kowalski," the lead enforcer's voice filtered down. "This is your final warning. Exit the structure. Surrender the asset."

Dale looked at the box. He looked at the hole in the crawlspace wall where the original infestation had burrowed weeks ago.

He knew that hole. He had sealed it. He had documented the frass trails. He had treated the nest.

But the nest hadn't been the rats. The rats had been the symptom.

The box was the source, and he crawled toward the back of the crawlspace. The walls were tight, pressing against his shoulders. The air was thick with the scent of mold and something else—something sweet and metallic, like old blood.

He reached the far corner. The spot where he had first found the broodmother.

He placed the box on the ground.

He pulled out his spray can. Not the bug spray. The industrial-strength solvent he used for removing adhesive residue.

He didn't spray the box, and he sprayed the floor around it.

The solvent hissed. It reacted with the residual void-energy seeping from the box's base. The chemical reaction created a fog of white vapor.

The enforcers were cutting through the hatch above. Sparks flew.

Dale watched the fog. It clung to the box.

He closed his eyes. He visualized the Field Guide entry.

**WEAKNESS: RESONANT DORMANCY.**

He tapped the box. Once. Twice. Three times.

Not a knock. A code.

*Tap-tap. Tap-tap-tap.*

The rhythm matched the heartbeat he had felt earlier.

The box shuddered.

The violet light flared, then died. Completely.

The humming stopped.

Silence.

Absolute, deafening silence.

The enforcers burst into the crawlspace. Flashlights blinded him.

"Target acquired," one said. He reached for the box.

Dale didn't move. He held up a hand.

"Don't touch it," Dale said. His voice was flat. Procedural.

"The containment field is unstable. If you disrupt the dormancy cycle now, the feedback loop will detonate the local rift energy."

The enforcers hesitated, and they looked at the box. It looked inert. Dead.

"Show me," the lead enforcer said. "Show me the instability."

Dale pointed to the floor. The solvent had eaten through the wood. Beneath it, the concrete was glowing with a faint, spiderweb pattern of purple light.

"That's not decay," Dale said. "That's energy pooling. The box is draining it. If you move it, you break the circuit."

The enforcer stepped closer. He scanned the floor. His visor whirred.

"Energy signature is... dormant," the visor read.

"But it's a ticking bomb," Dale said. "You can't move it. You can't purge it. And you can't leave it here."

"Then what do you suggest?" the enforcer asked.

Dale stood up. He brushed the dirt from his knees. He looked the enforcer in the eye.

"Re-certify the asset," Dale said.

The enforcer blinked.

"What?"

"The Authority certified this as a Class 3 Containment Asset," Dale said.

"Based on initial scans. But my field data contradicts that. This isn't a containment unit.

It's a gestation chamber, and the entity inside is evolving. It's not a Void Mimic anymore.

It's something new.

"

Dale pulled out his clipboard. He held it out.

"I'm filing an amendment to the Field Guide. Class 4. Pre-Rift Entity. Unidentified."

The enforcer stared at him.

"You can't re-certify a federal asset without an Authority audit."

"I'm not auditing it," Dale said. "I'm exterminating it. By changing its classification."

He tapped the clipboard.

"The System rewards thoroughness. If I document that the current containment method is ineffective due to species evolution, the Authority's own protocols require an immediate review. You can't fire the Purge cannons on a Class 4 asset. The collateral damage rating would exceed acceptable thresholds."

The enforcer's face tightened. He knew the rules. Everyone knew the rules.

The Authority was bureaucratic. It hated mistakes more than it hated monsters.

If Dale was right, and he was always right about the paperwork, they were stuck.

"You're bluffing," the enforcer said.

"Check the log," Dale said.

"Entry updated.

New weakness identified. The System accepted it.

That means the Authority's database is already flagging this asset for review.

"

The enforcer looked at his wrist. His screen flashed yellow.

**ASSET FLAGGED: POTENTIAL MISCLASSIFICATION.** **HOLD ACTION. AWAIT AUDIT.**

The lead enforcer cursed. He lowered his weapon.

"You bought yourself time, Kowalski," he said. "But not a reprieve."

"I bought enough time for the audit," Dale said. "That's all I need."

The enforcers backed out. They didn't take the box. They couldn't.

Dale was left alone in the crawlspace, and he sat down next to the silent box.

He was shaking now. His hands wouldn't stop.

He had gambled everything on a line in his Field Guide. On a hypothesis.

And it had worked.

But the System had given him a +15% bonus against a species he hadn't fought. That meant the next time he saw this thing, or something like it, he would be ready.

Or it meant the System was preparing him for something else.

He looked at the page in his Field Guide.

**VOID MIMIC**

He crossed out the name.

**UNIDENTIFIED PRE-RIFT ENTITY (Gestation Phase)**

He wrote below it:

**STATUS: AWAKE.**

He hadn't written that.

The ink appeared on the page, wet and black, though his pen was capped.

Dale recoiled. He slapped the page. The words remained.

The box opened.

Not the lid. The side. A seam in the metal split open like a wound.

A hand reached out.

It wasn't a hand. It was a claw, made of shadow and light. It grabbed Dale's ankle.

He screamed.

The System notification flashed in his vision, bold and red.

**CROSS-CONTAMINATION DETECTED.** **PROTOCOL ERROR: INVALID TARGET.** **PENALTY: 40% DEBUFF. FIELD GUIDE CORRUPTED FOR 24 HOURS.**

The claw pulled.

Dale kicked. He kicked at the shadow, at the light, at the thing that wasn't a rat, wasn't a beetle, wasn't a mimic.

He kicked it in the face, which had a face.

And it smiled.
