Chapter 112: Five Minutes to Midnight

# Chapter 112

The shadow swallowed the light.

Dale's boots hit the asphalt. The surface wasn't just cracked; it was liquefying.

Chunks of gray concrete floated in a slurry of black, viscous fluid that smelled of sulfur and old blood.

He didn't look at the ground. He looked at the hand.

It was the size of a minivan.

Fingers like twisted iron bars curled outward, grasping at the air. Smoke poured from the knuckles, forming shapes that dissipated before they could be named.

The hand slammed down, shattering the street.

A shockwave of dirt and glass blasted outward.

Dale dropped.

The debris bounced off his shoulders. He rolled, came up on one knee, and clicked his pen again. The sound was tiny against the roar of the entity.

*Incident Report #117. Subject: Class IX Entity. Status: Active Contact.*

He didn't have a weapon. He didn't have a protocol. He had a clipboard and a pen.

The System flashed in his peripheral vision, red and angry.

*WARNING: Unauthorized Combat. Penalty: -50% Thoroughness Rating.*

*ALERT: Field Guide Entry Missing. Cannot target weakness.*

Dale ignored the warnings. He ran forward.

Not toward the hand, but toward the gap where the hand had emerged from the pavement.

The hole was a wound in the world.

Black fluid bubbled from the edges.

He slid into the hole.

The smell hit him first. Rot. Ancient, wet rot.

The kind that lived in the walls of houses that had been empty for fifty years. Dale gagged, then forced himself to breathe through his mouth.

He pulled his face mask from his pocket and snapped it on.

The filter was old. It wouldn't stop the toxins, but it would keep the spores from clogging his lungs.

Protocol: PPE. Always.

He landed in darkness.

The hole was a shaft, dropping down into the earth. The walls were lined with chitinous ridges.

Not stone.

Bone. The streets of Elm Street weren't paved with asphalt anymore; they were paved with the calcified remains of something dead.

Dale clicked on his headlamp. The beam cut through the dark.

Below him, the shaft widened into a cavern. The ceiling was low, dripping with the same black fluid.

The floor was a carpet of movement.

Thousands of legs skittering.

Thousands of eyes blinking.

A broodmother? No. Too small. Too many.

Swarm.

The Class IX entity wasn't the monster. It was the nest.

Dale's stomach dropped. He had treated the hand as the threat. He had failed to identify the source.

*Error: Primary Target Misidentification.* *Correction: Isolate the Queen.*

The System was mocking him. He knew it. He ignored it.

He slid down the bone-lined shaft, his boots finding purchase on the slippery ridges. His hand shook. He clenched his fist around the clipboard until the knuckles turned white.

At the bottom, the swarm parted.

They were rats. But not *Rattus profundus*. These were different.

Their fur was matted with the black slime. Their eyes were too large, too human.

They moved with a synchronization that defied biology.

They didn't run; they flowed.

A rat the size of a terrier lunged at him.

Dale didn't swing. He stepped aside. The rat crashed into the bone wall and dissolved into sludge.

Another lunged. Then another.

He had no spray. No poison. No baton.

He had the clipboard.

He swung it. The hard plastic edge caught the third rat in the throat. It squealed, a high-pitched sound that vibrated in Dale's teeth. He kicked it away.

The swarm pressed closer.

*Ding.*

*Field Guide Update: Species Identified (Partial).

Name: The Shadow-Vermin. Weakness: Light.

Note: Requires UV spectrum for lethality.

Visible light only causes distraction.*

Dale read it. He looked at his headlamp. Standard LED. White light.

Useless.

He looked around. Was there a UV source?

A blacklight?

No.

This was a sewer, not a hardware store.

The rats circled. They were waiting. They knew he was trapped.

A low rumble echoed from above. The hand. It was trying to dig deeper. The ceiling of the cavern shook, sending chunks of bone raining down.

Dale had minutes. Maybe less.

He needed a protocol. He needed to isolate the nest.

Isolate. Eliminate. Verify.

He couldn't eliminate. Not without the right tool.

But he could isolate.

He looked at the walls. The bone ridges were cracked. The black fluid seeped from them.

It was the nutrient source. The food supply for the swarm.

If he cut off the food, the swarm would starve.

They would retreat to the central mass to protect it.

The central mass. The Queen.

He had to find the Queen to kill the hand.

Dale pulled out his pen. He didn't write. He used it.

He jammed the metal tip into the crack in the bone wall. He twisted. The bone splintered. The black fluid gushed out.

It burned his skin where it touched.

He hissed, pulling his hand back.

*Contamination Warning: Exposure to Class IX Biological Agent. Duration: 10 seconds. Debuff Applied: Dexterity -10%.*

His fingers went numb. The pen slipped.

He picked it up. Jammed it into the next crack.

The rats shrieked.

They weren't attacking him anymore. They were attacking the wall.

They sensed the breach in their food supply.

They swarmed the crack, trying to plug the leak with their own bodies.

Dale watched them. He noted their behavior.

*Observation: Swarm cohesion depends on pheromone trails from fluid source. Disruption of source causes chaotic behavior.*

This was it. The loophole.

He didn't need to kill them. He needed to break their formation.

He moved to the next crack. And the next.

He stabbed the pen into the bone, over and over.

Each thrust released a burst of black fluid.

Each burst drew more rats.

The swarm became a vortex. They fought each other. They clawed at the fluid, tearing each other apart to get to the source.

Chaos.

Dale backed away. He climbed the shaft, using the broken bone ridges as handholds. His numb fingers slipped. He caught himself on the ledge.

The rumble above grew louder. The hand was descending.

He wasn't out. He was just higher up in the nest.

He pulled himself onto the street level. The sidewalk was gone. The street was a pit. The hand was halfway down, fingers digging into the earth.

The rats were pouring out of the hole, thousands of them, flooding the surrounding blocks. People screamed in the distance. Car alarms wailed.

Dale stood on the edge of the pit. He was covered in slime. His suit was torn. His clipboard was cracked.

He had failed to identify the weakness. He had failed to document the species. He had failed to use the proper protocol.

But he had disrupted the hive mind.

For the first time, the Authority notification didn't flash red. It flashed white.

*Action Logged: Protocol Deviation. Effectiveness: Minimal.* *Thoroughness Rating: Adjusted to 4.1.*

Dale laughed. A dry, hacking cough.

4.1. It was a passing grade. Barely.

But it wasn't the point.

He looked at the hand. It was almost at the bottom of the shaft. It was reaching for the Queen.

If it reached the Queen, it would consume it. The entity would grow. The Rift would widen.

Dale had to stop it.

He had one thing left. One resource.

His own body.

The Authority's rules were strict. *Treatment Points are awarded only for completing full extermination protocols.*

He couldn't complete the protocol. He didn't have the tools.

But there was a clause. A subsection in the Licensing Manual he had memorized during his study sessions.

*Clause 7b: In the absence of standard equipment, the Technician may use their own biological signature to lure the target into a trap, provided the trap results in the verification of the target's location.*

It was suicide. Or it was certification.

Dale stepped off the edge.

He fell.

The air rushed past him. The hand was there, huge, blocking the light. Its fingers brushed his chest. The cold was absolute.

He didn't fight. He let the fingers grab him.

He was lifted into the air. The rats screamed below, but they didn't come for him. They were too busy fighting for the fluid.

The hand pulled him toward the center of the hole. Toward the Queen.

The Queen was a mass of flesh and eyes, buried deep in the mud. It was pulsing. It sensed him.

Dale dangled in the air. The smell of the Queen was overpowering. It smelled of ozone and fear.

He raised the clipboard.

The hand didn't care about the clipboard. It cared about him. It tightened its grip. Ribs creaked.

Dale gritted his teeth. He clicked the pen.

He didn't write.

He held the pen up.

*Incident Report #118.* *Subject: Bait.* *Status: Consumed.*

He pressed the tip of the pen into the palm of the hand.

He didn't stab. He didn't attack. He marked the hand.

He drew a circle on the shadowy skin. A simple, perfect circle.

The System pinged.

*Target Marked: Class IX Entity.* *Weakness Identified: Core Exposure.* *Bonus: +50% Damage to Marked Area.*

The hand paused. The fingers loosened.

Dale dropped.

He fell into the mud. He hit the Queen's outer layer. It was soft. Warm.

He rolled to his feet. He was bruised, broken, but alive.

The hand was confused. The mark was a signal. It had drawn the entity's attention away from the rats. It had identified the Queen.

But the Queen was still there.

And the Queen was angry.

The ground erupted. The Queen rose. It wasn't a rat.

It wasn't a shadow. It was a geometric shape, a non-Euclidean knot of flesh that hurt to look at.

The hand retracted, folding back into the shape.

The Queen looked at Dale.

It didn't have eyes. It had a mouth. A wide, vertical slit that opened to reveal rows of grinding teeth.

Dale stepped back.

He had marked the target. He had isolated the threat.

Now he had to eliminate it.

He had no weapon.

He had no poison.

He had a pen.

He looked at the pen in his hand. The ink cartridge was empty.

He looked at the Queen.

He looked at the System message.

*Protocol: Eliminate. Tools Required: None available.* *Alternative: Sacrifice.*

Dale understood.

The Authority didn't reward survival. It rewarded completion.

To complete the protocol, he had to die. Or he had to kill.

He couldn't kill.

So he had to sacrifice.

He threw the pen.

It wasn't a weapon. It was a distraction.

The pen spun through the air. The Queen's slit-mouth tracked it.

Dale ran.

Not away. Toward the wall of the cavern. Toward the cracks he had made. Toward the black fluid.

He grabbed a handful of the slime. It burned his skin. He didn't care.

He smeared it on his chest. On his arms. On his face.

He became the source.

The rats, confused by the chaos, turned to him. They smelled the food. They smelled the Queen's essence.

They surged forward.

The Queen shrieked. The sound was a physical blow. Dale's nose bled. His ears rang.

The rats hit the Queen. Not to kill it. To feed.

They climbed over the geometric mass, tearing at the flesh. They were eating their own mother.

The Queen thrashed. The hand tried to stop them. The hand was too small. The swarm was too big.

Dale watched. He held his breath.

He waited for the signal.

The Queen's movements slowed. The hand withered. The geometric shape collapsed.

The rats turned on Dale.

He didn't move.

The System flashed.

*Target Eliminated: Class IX Entity.* *Protocol Status: Partial.* *Verification: Pending.*

*Thoroughness Rating: 6.5.*

The rats reached him. They tore at his clothes. They bit into his skin.

Dale screamed.

Then the light returned.

Not sunlight. The Rift was closing. The hand was gone. The hole was sealing itself, the bone fusing back together.

The rats fell dead. Or they fled. It didn't matter. They were gone.

Dale lay in the mud. He was bleeding from a hundred cuts. His suit was destroyed. His clipboard was in pieces.

He coughed. Black bile spilled from his mouth.

He tried to stand. His legs didn't work.

A voice came from above.

"Kowalski?"

Dale looked up.

It wasn't Miller. It wasn't the police.

It was a man in a suit. A black suit. Immaculate. Not a drop of mud on him.

He was standing at the edge of the hole. He held a briefcase.

The man looked down at Dale. He didn't look concerned. He looked annoyed.

"You violated Protocol 42," the man said. "You accessed the hive without authorization."

Dale wheezed. "It was... necessary."

The man opened the briefcase. Inside were tools. Silver tools. Shaped like scalpels and probes.

"It was sloppy," the man said. "You disrupted the nest. You didn't extract the core. You didn't document the genetic makeup."

He pulled out a silver needle.

"You passed the basic level. Barely. But you failed the practical."

The man jumped down into the hole.

He landed lightly on the mud. He walked toward Dale.

"Stand up," the man said.

Dale couldn't.

The man reached down. He grabbed Dale by the hair. He lifted him up.

"Who are you?" Dale asked.

The man smiled. It wasn't a nice smile.

"I am the Auditor," he said. "And you are under investigation."

He pressed the silver needle into Dale's neck.

It didn't hurt. It felt cold.

*System Alert: Biometric Scan in Progress.* *Data Collection: Active.* *Warning: Unauthorized Entity Detected.*

Dale's vision blurred. The man's face stretched.

"You have a 6.5," the Auditor said. "That qualifies you for Level II. But it also flags you for review."

"Review for what?" Dale slurred.

"For contamination," the Auditor said. "You touched the Class IX core. You are now a carrier."

The Auditor let go. Dale collapsed.

The man turned and walked away. He climbed out of the hole. He didn't help Dale. He didn't call for an ambulance.

He closed the briefcase.

Dale lay in the mud. The rats were gone. The monster was dead.

He had won.

But the Auditor hadn't left.

Dale realized the man was watching him. Watching to see if he would survive.

If he survived, he was contaminated.

If he died, he was a statistic.

The System flashed one last message.

*New Notification: Investigation Opened.* *Deadline: 24 Hours.* *Objective: Prove Contamination is False.* *Reward: Certification Level II.* *Failure: Termination.*

Dale looked at his hands. They were shaking.

He was still alive.

But he was already being judged.

He dragged himself to the edge of the hole. He pulled himself up.

The Auditor was gone.

On the sidewalk, a single item remained. A business card.

Dale picked it up.

It had no name. Just an address. And a time.

Midnight.

Dale looked at his watch. It was 11:55 PM.

He had five minutes.

He started running.
