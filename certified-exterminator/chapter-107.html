Chapter 107: The Drain Speaks

# Chapter 107

The crack in the ceiling wasn't just spreading; it was bleeding.

Thick, viscous sludge oozed from the fissure, dripping onto the concrete floor with a rhythmic *plap, plap, plap*. It smelled like copper and old rain.

Dale didn't look at the sludge.

He looked at the pressure gauge on the water main valve he'd just found. The needle twitched, hovering in the red zone.

"Miller," Dale said, his voice flat. "Turn the handle counter-clockwise. Half a turn. Not a full one."

Miller wiped sweat from his forehead with a dirty sleeve. "Why? It's trying to get out."

"It's trying to expand," Dale corrected.

He tapped his clipboard against his thigh. "If we release the pressure too fast, the sealant I just applied shatters.

If we don't release it, the pipe bursts and floods the basement.

We need a controlled leak. A drip."

A drip was manageable. A burst was a code violation.

The ground shook again. Dust rained down from the rafters.

Somewhere below, in the sealed chamber, something massive shifted.

The heavy industrial door groaned against the steel bolts Dale had welded shut ten minutes ago.

The foam barrier held, but it was white with stress fractures.

*New Entry: The Foreman.* *Status: Active.* *Weakness: None.* *Note: Do not engage.*

Dale stared at the glowing text floating in his peripheral vision. The font was jagged, glitching like a corrupted video file. He swiped the notification away with a mental command.

It didn't work. The Authority wasn't hiding this one.

"Is it still there?" Miller asked, his rifle trained on the hatch. "The Architect?"

"Exterminated," Dale said. "The Foreman is a new species. Sub-boss tier. Probably a hive-mind interface."

"A what?"

"A manager," Dale said.

He moved to the secondary valve. "It controls the flow of the infestation. It doesn't fight; it organizes.

That's why the Architect didn't have a weakness.

It was protected by the infrastructure. The pipes.

The wires.

The layout of the plant itself."

Another tremor. This one was sharper, a crack like a whip. The sludge on the floor bubbled.

Dale pulled his pen from his shirt pocket. He didn't write anything yet. He needed data first. He needed to see what the Foreman *was*.

"Miller, scan the steam vents," Dale ordered. "Check for organic residue. If it's biological, it's a Class 3. If it's mechanical, it's a hazard."

Miller hesitated, then holstered his rifle and pulled a handheld scanner from his belt. The device beeped, a high-pitched whine that cut through the low thrum of the machine.

"It's... mixed," Miller said, frowning at the readout. "I'm getting metal readings. But also... chitin."

"Chitin," Dale repeated. He walked toward the main pressure gauge.

"Insects have chitin.

So does this thing.

It's armored. And it's connected to the plumbing."

The lights flickered. The hum of the plant deepened, dropping an octave. It sounded less like machinery and more like a heartbeat.

Dale checked his watch. Six minutes left on the jammer. Vance's warning echoed in his mind. *The Foreman doesn't sleep for long.*

If the jammer failed, the Foreman would wake up. And if it woke up while the seal was under stress, the containment would fail.

Dale needed to change the parameters. He couldn't just lock it in. He needed to starve it.

"Miller, is the water running?"

"A little. Dripping. Like you said."

"Good." Dale pulled out his Field Guide. He flipped to a blank page. "I'm going to initiate Protocol 4-B: Controlled Dehydration."

Miller looked at him. "Dehydration? Against a water monster?"

"It's not a water monster," Dale said, his pen scratching against the paper. "It's a hydraulic parasite. It feeds on pressure.

On flow. If we reduce the volume but keep the pressure high, it gets thirsty.

It gets sluggish."

"That's... stupid," Miller said.

"It's OSHA compliant," Dale said. "Minimum safe pressure for residential supply is 40 PSI. We're at 60. We're bleeding it dry."

He twisted the valve. The gauge dropped. 58... 55... 52...

The rumbling slowed. The steam vents hissed, but the sound was thinner, weaker.

Dale watched the sludge on the floor. It stopped bubbling. The thick tendrils retracted, pulling back toward the hatch.

"It's working," Miller whispered. "It's retreating."

"It's conserving energy," Dale said. "Don't celebrate. Watch the seal."

The foam barrier was still holding. But the cracks were widening. Not because of the pressure, but because something was pushing from the inside.

Not with force.

With precision.

A small hole appeared in the foam. Then another. Then a dozen.

They weren't random. They were arranged in a pattern. A grid.

Dale leaned in. His eyes narrowed. He recognized the geometry. It was a ventilation schematic.

"It's rewriting the seal," Dale said. His voice tightened. "It's using the steam to melt specific points. It knows the thermal expansion coefficients of the industrial foam."

"How?" Miller shouted. "It's just a bug!"

"It's a *licensed* bug," Dale said. He stood up, backing away from the hatch. "It has a Field Guide entry too. It knows our weaknesses."

The notification flashed again.

*Alert: Cross-Contamination Risk Detected.* *Your Field Guide entry for 'Dimensional Vermin' is being overwritten by 'The Foreman'.* *Penalty: -40% Efficacy for all Vermin-targeted protocols.*

Dale felt a cold knot in his stomach.

The debuff. He couldn't use his standard spray. His standard traps.

His identification bonuses. They were all linked to the 'Dimensional Vermin' category.

And the Foreman was stealing that category.

"Miller, get the truck back," Dale said. "We're leaving."

"Leaving? But we just sealed it!"

"We sealed a corpse," Dale said. "The Foreman is the infection. And it's already moving."

He pointed to the ceiling. The crack wasn't spreading up anymore. It was branching out, following the lines of the electrical conduits. The sludge was moving along the wires, glowing with a faint, sickly green light.

"It's not in the plant anymore," Dale said. "It's in the grid. It's in the town's power."

Miller's face went pale. "The whole town?"

"The whole block," Dale corrected. He grabbed his clipboard. "If it reaches the substation, it will turn every light bulb into a breeding pod. Every outlet, a nest."

He ran toward the exit. Miller followed, his boots clanging on the metal stairs.

Dale didn't look back.

He didn't have time to look back. He needed to calculate the spread rate.

The sludge was moving at three feet per second. The distance to the substation was four hundred yards.

He did the math in his head. Two minutes. Maybe three.

He burst out of the plant's side door. The night air hit him, cold and damp.

The jammer was still humming, a low vibration in his bones.

But it was fading. He could feel it thinning out.

Dale scanned the street.

The streetlights were flickering. Not just flickering.

They were pulsing.

In rhythm with the heartbeat he'd heard in the plant.

"Oh no," Miller said, standing beside him. "Look at the houses."

The windows of the row of duplexes across the street were glowing. Not with light.

With movement.

Shapes pressed against the glass.

Large, jagged shapes.

"Class 2 infestation," Dale muttered. "Immediate evacuation."

He pulled out his phone. No signal. Of course.

The Foreman was jamming the network. It was isolating the neighborhood.

"Dale," Miller said. "We can't fight an entire neighborhood."

"We don't have to," Dale said.

He opened his Field Guide. He looked at the new entry. *The Foreman. Status: Active.

Weakness: None.*

*None.*

He stared at the word. The Authority never listed 'None'.

It listed 'Unknown'.

'Hidden'. 'Conditional'.

'None' meant it had no physical vulnerability. It meant it was structural. Conceptual.

Dale looked at the glowing houses. He looked at the pulsing streetlights. He looked at the crack in the plant's ceiling, now visible through the open door behind him.

The Foreman wasn't a creature. It was a system. It was the infrastructure itself, possessed.

And you couldn't kill infrastructure. You could only shut it down.

Dale's hand hovered over his phone. He had one number. Vance.

He dialed.

The call connected. Static. Then Vance's voice, smooth and cold.

"Kowalski. You're late."

"The Foreman is in the grid," Dale said. "It's using the power lines as a nervous system."

Silence on the other end. Then, a sigh.

"You engaged it."

"I documented it."

"You triggered a Cascade Event," Vance said. "Do you know what that means, Dale? It means the Authority is no longer interested in containment. They are interested in eradication."

"Eradication of the town?"

"Eradication of the sector. All of it.

The plant.

The houses.

The water mains. The power grid."

Dale's grip on the phone tightened until the plastic creaked. "You're going to bomb us?"

"We're going to *sterilize* the zone," Vance said. "Protocol 9. Nuclear option.

It's not literal, but it's close. We're sending a Purge Squad.

They'll be here in twenty minutes."

"We can stop it," Dale said. "It's thirsty. It's dehydrating. We can starve it out."

"You're wasting time, Dale. The Foreman has already spawned. Look at your phone."

Dale looked down. His screen was flickering. The icons were rearranging themselves.

The camera app opened on its own.

The image on the screen wasn't the street. It was a close-up of Dale's own eye.

Blinking.

*New Entry: The Foreman.* *Weakness: Host Connection.*

Dale dropped the phone. It shattered on the pavement.

"Miller," Dale said.

His voice was steady. Too steady. "Cut the power to the street.

The main breaker."

"It's on the substation," Miller said. "Which is where it's going!"

"No," Dale said.

"It's already there.

It's waiting for us to cut it. It wants us to isolate the grid.

If we cut the power, it becomes purely digital. It jumps to the backup generators. Then to the cars.

Then to the pacemakers."

Dale looked at the pulsing lights. The shapes in the windows were growing larger. They were breaking the glass.

"We don't cut the power," Dale said. "We overload it."

Miller stared at him. "That will explode the transformers."

"Yes."

"And kill us."

"Only if we're standing next to them."

Dale grabbed Miller's arm.

"Run to the utility shed. The one with the manual override. I need to manually short the phase lines.

I need to create a surge so big it burns out the Foreman's connection to the physical infrastructure."

"That's insane," Miller said. "You'll be electrocuted."

"I'm wearing rubber-soled boots," Dale said. "And I have a Class I grounding rod in my truck.

It's not for monsters.

It's for lightning. But electricity is electricity."

He didn't wait for Miller to argue. He ran toward the utility shed.

The air was getting hotter.

The hum was deafening now.

The ground beneath his feet vibrated with the energy of a thousand angry insects trapped in copper wire.

He reached the shed. The door was rusted shut. Dale kicked it open.

Inside, the breaker panel was glowing red. The smell of ozone was thick enough to taste.

Dale pulled out his grounding rod. It was a simple copper spike, wrapped in insulated rubber. He jammed it into the earth. Then he grabbed the main phase wire.

It was hot. Burning hot. He didn't flinch. He held it.

"Miller!" Dale shouted. "Pull the master disconnect! Count to three!"

Miller stood by the main lever. His face was grim. He looked at Dale, then at the pulsing street.

"One," Miller yelled.

The shadows in the alleyway elongated. They detached from the walls. They had legs. Many legs.

"Two," Miller yelled.

The Foreman was coming. It was in the wires. It was in the dark.

"Three!"

Miller pulled the lever.

The world went white.

Not a flash. A *sound*. A scream of tearing metal and arcing electricity that shattered every window on the block.

Dale felt the current surge through him. It didn't hurt. It felt like being plugged into a wall outlet. Like being part of the grid.

For a second, he *saw* it.

He saw the Foreman.

It wasn't a bug. It wasn't a beast. It was a face.

A face made of wires and wet concrete, with eyes made of broken glass.

And it was smiling.

The System notification exploded in his vision.

*Thoroughness Rating: 10/10.* *Achievement Unlocked: System Shock.* *New Certification Track Unavailable.*

Then, darkness.

Dale woke up on the pavement. Smoke curled from his boots. His head throbbed.

Miller was kneeling beside him, shaking his shoulder. "Dale. Dale!"

Dale blinked. The street was dark. The lights were out. The pulsing had stopped.

"Did we do it?" Miller asked.

Dale sat up.

He looked at his hands. They were shaking. Not from fear.

From residual voltage.

He looked at the plant. The door was gone. Blown off its hinges.

He looked at his Field Guide. It was blank.

No entries. No notes. No map.

*Field Guide Corrupted.* *Data Lost.*

Dale's blood went cold. He wiped his glasses. The guide flickered, trying to reload.

*Recovering Data...* *0%...* *1%...*

The Foreman hadn't just escaped. It had stolen his memory. It had stolen his knowledge.

And then, a voice. Not from Miller. Not from Vance.

From the drain in the street. A small, wet, clicking sound.

*Click.*

Dale looked down. A single, black beetle the size of a thumb crawled out of the grate. It had no eyes. Just a smooth, shiny shell.

It looked up at him.

And then, it spoke.

"Hello, Dale."
