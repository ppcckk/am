Chapter 397: Sample of the Law

# Chapter 397

The asphalt of Route 9 was slick with something that wasn't rain. It smelled like burnt sugar and copper. Dale kept his left hand on the clipboard, his right on the tactical flashlight. The beam cut through the fog, illuminating a trail of viscous black sludge leading from the cracked sidewalk toward the abandoned gas station.

Vance was breathing hard. His boots slipped on the oil-slicked pavement.

"Keep up," Dale said, not looking back. "We're losing the perimeter."

"They're moving fast, Dale. Those things at the end of the trail. They weren't there ten seconds ago."

Vance's voice cracked. The kid was fresh out of Level II training, still treating the Authority's protocols like suggestions than laws. He was wrong. Laws had teeth.

Dale stopped. He crouched, shining the light on a smear of slime against the curb.

He pulled a sample vial from his belt.

His hands didn't shake. They never did, not anymore. The trembling had left him years ago, replaced by a cold, mechanical precision.

He scraped a sample. The substance hissed against the glass.

"Corrosive," Dale muttered. "High acidity. pH lower than battery fluid."

He capped the vial and labeled it with a marker. **Sample 397-1: Unidentified Acidic Residue.**

"Write it down," Dale commanded.

Vance scribbled furiously on his own clipboard. His pen scratched against the paper, a frantic, jagged sound.

"Who sent the Auditor?" Vance asked, his voice dropping to a whisper. "If we go back to Field Station 9 without knowing who hired him, the Authority will reject the report. They'll say we fabricated the encounter."

"We didn't fabricate it.

We documented it.

" Dale stood up. "The pen is the evidence.

The Auditor is the witness.

We just need to get the witness to the station before the next shift change.

"

The next shift change was in four hours. The deadline for logging the pen was twelve. The window was closing.

A sound echoed from the gas station. It wasn't a scream. It was a chittering, like dry leaves skittering over concrete.

Dale clicked off his flashlight. Darkness swallowed them.

"Protocol 7," Dale said. "Silence. Move to cover. Do not engage unless containment fails."

Vance fumbled with his weapon. The clatter of metal on metal was deafening in the quiet.

"Stop," Dale hissed.

Vance froze.

Dale moved to the side, using the shadow of a rusted fuel pump as cover. He pulled a flare from his kit.

Not a combat flare.

A standard-issue identification flare.

Red. Bright.

Visible from orbit, and he cracked it. The red glow painted the gas station in bloody hues.

"Light up!" Dale shouted.

The darkness broke. Three creatures stood in the center of the pump island.

They were tall, spindly things made of shadow and wet bone.

Their limbs had too many joints. Their faces were smooth, featureless ovals.

**SYSTEM NOTIFICATION:** *Hostile Entities Detected: Shadow Stalkers (Class III).* *Status: Unregistered Infestation.* *Warning: Unauthorized Use of Light Sources may escalate aggression.*

Dale didn't care about the warning. He needed to see them.

"Vance! Identify the weak point!"

Vance stared at the creatures. His eyes were wide.

"I don't have them in my guide! I've never seen them!"

"Look closer," Dale said, his voice flat. "They're not solid. Look at the edges."

Vance squinted.

"They're... they're leaking."

"Correct.

They're dimensional bleed-throughs. They're trying to anchor themselves here.

If they anchor, the Rift widens.

If the Rift widens, the Auditor's pen becomes useless because the station won't exist.

"

"Kill them?"

"No," Dale said. "Isolate them."

Dale pulled a canister from his belt. It wasn't a spray. It was a canister of aerosolized chalk-dust, standard for marking boundaries. But he wasn't marking the ground.

He threw the canister at the lead Stalker.

The canister burst. A cloud of white dust exploded around the creature. The Stalker shrieked, a sound like tearing metal. It thrashed, trying to escape the dust, but the particles clung to its semi-corporeal form.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** *Contamination Detected. Shadow Stalker marked.* *Effect: Visibility increased by 400%. Anchoring disrupted.*

"Vance! Tag them! Use the red light as a guide!"

Vance raised his crossbow. His hands were shaking.

He fired.

The bolt struck the marked Stalker in the chest.

It passed through the dust and embedded in a fuel pump behind it.

The Stalker didn't fall. It turned toward Vance.

"Missed," Dale said. "You aimed for the body. Aim for the anchor point.

Look at their feet. There's a black line connecting them to the ground.

"

Vance looked down. Thick, tar-like tendrils connected the Stalkers to the asphalt.

"The roots!" Vance yelled.

"Shoot the roots, Vance! Not the monster! Exterminate the infestation, not the symptom!"

Vance reloaded. His breathing was ragged. He lined up the shot.

The second Stalker lunged. It moved faster than its size suggested. It closed the distance in a heartbeat, its clawed hand reaching for Vance's throat.

Dale stepped in. He didn't use a weapon. He used his clipboard.

He slammed the thick, reinforced board into the Stalker's face. The impact cracked the smooth oval of its head. The creature recoiled, screeching.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** *Improvised Impact. Damage: Negligible.* *Behavioral Disruption: +15%.*

Dale didn't care about the damage. He cared about the distraction.

"Vance! Now!"

Vance fired. The bolt severed the tendril connecting the second Stalker to the ground. The creature dissolved into mist, then reformed three feet to the left.

"It's re-anchoring!" Vance yelled.

"Cut it again!"

Vance fired. The bolt hit the new anchor point. The Stalker flickered. It was destabilizing.

The third Stalker, the largest one, stepped back. It seemed to be assessing them. Its featureless face turned from Vance to Dale.

Dale raised his hands, palms out. A universal sign of surrender. Or so it looked.

"Stop shooting," Dale said.

Vance froze, bolt nocked.

"It's communicating," Dale said. "Look at its hands. It's making shapes."

Vance looked. The Stalker's long, multi-jointed fingers were weaving in the air. Patterns. Geometric patterns.

"It's casting," Vance said. "It's trying to open a portal."

"No," Dale said. "It's filing a complaint."

The air around the Stalker shimmered. A notification appeared in Dale's peripheral vision.

**SYSTEM NOTIFICATION:** *Entity Attempting Administrative Appeal.* *Violation: Excessive Use of Force by Licensed Technician.* *Penalty: Immediate License Suspension.*

Dale's eyes narrowed, which was new. The Authority didn't just punish sloppiness. It punished *unauthorized* aggression.

If the Stalker successfully filed this appeal, Dale would be stripped of his certification.

He'd become just another guy with a flashlight and a bad attitude.

"Vance, drop the crossbow."

"What? It's killing us!"

"It's killing my license," Dale said. "If I'm suspended, I can't go to Field Station 9. If I can't go there, the pen is lost. If the pen is lost, the Auditor wins."

"Then what do we do?"

Dale looked at the marked Stalker. The chalk dust was still clinging to it. The dust was an official Authority substance. It was a marker.

"We prove it's not an infestation," Dale said. "We prove it's a *violation*."

He walked forward.

"Dale, no!"

Dale walked until he was ten feet from the Stalker. The creature watched him. The air hummed with static. The geometric patterns in its hands were spinning faster.

Dale pulled out his pen. The black, ordinary pen from the Auditor's crime scene. The one Vance had asked about. The one Dale had said to log.

He held the pen up.

"Hey!" Dale shouted. "You're filing an appeal. Good. That means you recognize the Authority's jurisdiction."

The Stalker paused. The spinning hands slowed.

"But you're filing it under 'Excessive Force.' That's a procedural error," Dale said.

His voice was steady, loud, projecting like he was addressing a city council meeting.

"You are an unregistered entity. You have no standing to appeal.

You are a nuisance. A code violation.

"

The Stalker tilted its head.

Dale took a step closer.

"I am citing you under Statute 44-C: Unauthorized Dimensional Presence. Your penalty is immediate containment and recycling."

He uncapped the pen.

The Stalker shrieked. It lunged.

Dale didn't dodge. He didn't shoot. He slammed the pen into the chalk-dust mark on the Stalker's chest.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** *Action: Administrative Citation.* *Requirement: Physical Contact with Evidence Marker.* *Success: 100%.*

The pen pierced the Stalker's chest. It didn't bleed. It froze. The creature's form hardened, turning from mist to solid, white stone.

The geometric patterns vanished. The appeal was dismissed.

The other two Stalkers, seeing their leader neutralized, dissolved into the asphalt. Gone.

Dale pulled the pen out. It was covered in white stone dust.

He stared at it. The pen hadn't broken. It had hardened the creature.

**SYSTEM NOTIFICATION:** *New Mechanic Unlocked: Administrative Containment.* *Description: Valid citations can solidify Class III entities, rendering them portable for transport.* *Warning: Overuse results in permanent petrification of Technician's hands.*

Dale looked at his hand. The skin was turning white. Like stone.

"Dale?" Vance whispered.

Dale clenched his fist. The joints cracked. The white color faded, but the stiffness remained.

"It cost," Dale said. "Every time I use it, I lose mobility."

"That's... that's insane. You're going to freeze up."

"Then I have to be careful," Dale said. He pocketed the pen. "And I have to be quick."

He looked at the statue-like remains of the Stalker. He grabbed a plastic bag from his kit and wrapped the statue tightly.

"Help me carry this," Dale said. "It's heavy."

Vance stepped forward, his face pale.

"What is it?"

"Evidence," Dale said. "And leverage."

"Who sent the Auditor, Dale? Really?"

Dale looked at the bag. Inside, the statue's eyes were still open. Terrified.

"The Auditor wasn't sent to kill us," Dale said. "He was sent to stop us from finding the source. Which means the source isn't in Ohio anymore."

"Where is it?"

Dale pointed north. Toward the city. Toward the capital.

"The Authority's headquarters is in Columbus. And the Auditor's pen... it's keyed to the Administrator's office."

Vance looked at the statue.

"So we're going to the top of the food chain?"

"We're going to the filing cabinet," Dale said. "And we're going to audit them."

A rumble shook the ground. Not from the earth. From the sky.

Above them, the clouds parted. A massive, translucent structure descended. It looked like a bureaucratic stamp, the size of a skyscraper. It pressed down on the gas station, crushing the fuel pumps flat.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** *Priority Intervention Detected.* *Entity: Senior Auditor.* *Reason: Unauthorized Use of Administrative Containment.* *Status: Immediate Detainment.*

Dale grabbed the bag of stone.

"Vance. Run."

"Where?"

"North," Dale said. "To the station. We have to file the report before they catch us."

Vance looked at the stamp descending. It was crushing the street. The road was buckling.

"They're going to flatten us!"

Dale looked at his hand. The stiffness was spreading to his wrist. He couldn't feel his pinky.

"Run," Dale said.

"And write the report in your head.

If they catch us, you say nothing. You say you were with a Level IV technician.

You say I had authorization.

"

"You don't have authorization!"

"I have a Field Guide," Dale said. "That's enough."

He turned and ran. The asphalt cracked under his feet. The stamp above them groaned, a sound of grinding gears and ancient judgment.

Dale ran toward the city lights. The pen in his pocket burned against his thigh. The statue in the bag grew heavier with every step.

He wasn't running from monsters. He was running from the law. And for the first time in fourteen years, Dale Kowalski didn't know if he was right.

His phone buzzed: Vance was already missing.
