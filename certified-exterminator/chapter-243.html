Chapter 243: The Real Infestation

# Chapter 243

The silhouette against the burning skyline was not a warrior. It did not carry a sword, nor did it glow with the aura of a Chosen One. It stood in the center of the intersection like a statue made of beige polyester and resignation.

To the untrained eye, Dale Kowalski looked like a man having a breakdown. His hands shook.

His face was a mask of pale sweat.

The sludge from the dissolved Hollow Mouth coated his boots, turning the pavement into a slick, black mirror. He stood motionless while the city screamed around him, sirens wailing, debris falling, and the great, unblinking Eye of the Authority hanging in the sky like a judgmental god.

But Marcus Thorne, standing on the roof of the ruined bank across the street, saw something else. He saw a man who was not fighting the end of the world. He was filing paperwork against it.

Thorne lowered his binoculars, and the lenses were cracked from the shockwave, but they still worked. He watched Dale's lips move.

Rapidly.

Rhythmically. Not in prayer, and not in panic. In dictation.

"Who is he?" Thorne whispered, his voice tight with confusion.

Beside him, a young rookie named Elara checked her own interface.

"Class Two Technician.

Low-level.

Barely registered.

Why is the Observer watching *him*?

"

Thorne didn't answer. He watched the numbers on Dale's wrist display. They weren't counting down to death.

They were counting down to a deadline. And Dale was meeting it.

Word by word.

Clause by clause.

The rookie frowned.

"His interface is locked. He's not targeting the entity. He's targeting... the record."

"He's reconstructing it," Thorne said, a cold realization settling in his gut. "He's rewriting history while it's still happening."

Elara looked at the clock. *09:45.*

"If he misses a comma," Elara said, "we all get erased."

"He won't miss a comma," Thorne said. "He's an exterminator. He knows that if you miss the nest, the infestation returns."

***

Dale did not feel the cold. He did not feel the sludge soaking through his socks, numbing his toes. He felt only the weight of the words in his throat.

He had spoken for three minutes. Four.

The Authority's interface hovered in his peripheral vision, a translucent blue box that pulsed with a rhythmic, red warning light. The timer was no longer just a number; it was a physical pressure, squeezing his temples. Every second that ticked away shaved a layer of his reality thin.

He could feel the edges of his vision fraying. The color draining from the world, replaced by a static gray void that waited to consume him.

But he kept talking.

"...observed secondary chitinous plates on the thorax," Dale said, his voice flat, professional, and detached from the terror clawing at his lungs.

"Fracture points located at joints three and four. Weakness identified as calcium-poor exoskeleton.

Treatment Protocol: Acidic solvent, concentration forty percent.

Application method: Directed spray.

"

The Observer did not blink. The Eye remained open, vast and terrible. But the static in Dale's vision receded slightly. The Authority was listening.

It was recording.

Dale's hand twitched. He wanted to reach for his clipboard, but it was gone.

Dissolved, and the stylus was broken.

He was writing with air.

He remembered the smell of the creature. Acrid, like burning hair and ammonia. He remembered the texture of the slime.

Viscous, clinging, stubborn. He remembered the exact angle of the attack.

If he got the angle wrong, the report would be rejected.

If the report was rejected, the job was incomplete. If the job was incomplete, the penalty was total erasure. Not just of Dale, but of the memory that Dale had ever existed.

The world would heal over the hole where he had been, smooth and seamless, as if he had never taken out the trash, never checked the drains, never filed a single report.

He refused to be a rounding error.

"Sarah!" he barked, without turning his head.

Behind him, the sound of footsteps skidding on wet asphalt. Sarah arrived, out of breath, her face streaked with grime. She held a broken stylus and a small, jagged piece of plastic.

"It's not enough," she said, her voice trembling. "The carbon copy is shattered. I can't print anything."

"Then don't print it," Dale said. "Transmit it."

Sarah froze. "I can't transmit. The tower is down. The signal is jammed by the Rift interference."

Dale stopped speaking. For one terrifying second, the silence was absolute. The Eye in the sky seemed to lean closer.

*08:12.*

The pressure in his skull spiked. A sharp, white pain behind his eyes.

He looked at Sarah. He looked at the broken stylus. Then he looked at the sludge on his boots.

"Vance," Dale said. His voice was dangerously calm.

Vance, the team leader, was crouching behind a overturned car, clutching his rifle. He looked up, eyes wide.

"What?"

"The Authority rewards thoroughness," Dale said. "It does not care about the medium. It cares about the accuracy."

He pulled his wallet from his back pocket. Inside was his driver's license. He ripped it in half.

The paper was thin. Fragile. Useless.

"Use this," Dale said, tossing the half-plate onto the wet ground. "Scan it. Upload it as a secondary citation document."

Vance stared at him.

"Are you insane? That's your ID. That's your legal identity."

"If I cease to exist, my ID is moot," Dale said. "But if the Authority sees a documented breach of protocol—specifically, the use of non-standard media to preserve evidence—it triggers a Compliance Override."

Sarah's eyes went wide.

"Dale, that's... that's a loophole. Section 4, Subsection C. 'In the event of catastrophic equipment failure, the technician may submit raw observational data in any legible format.'"

"Legible," Dale said. "My memory is legible. My voice is legible. The sludge is legible."

He stepped forward. He dipped his finger into the black muck on the pavement. It was cold, slimy, and smelled of death.

He drew a line in the sludge. A violation code. **K-7**.

Then he drew another. **K-8**.

"Sarah, scan the pavement," Dale ordered. "Now."

Sarah didn't hesitate. She raised her tablet, the camera lens flickering with low battery. She aimed it at the wet concrete. The device whirred, struggling to focus in the rain and the dim light.

"Scanning..." Sarah said. "It's... it's recognizing the symbols. It's cross-referencing with your voice log."

The blue box in Dale's vision flickered.

**COMPLIANCE OVERRIDE INITIATED.**

**EVIDENCE QUALITY: ACCEPTABLE.**

The red warning light on the timer turned amber.

Dale exhaled. The pressure behind his eyes eased, just a fraction. But the timer was still ticking.

*07:45.*

"It's not enough," Vance shouted over the sirens. "The Observer is still active. It's not recording the *job*. It's recording the *technician*."

Dale looked up at the Eye. The pupil was dilating. Expanding.

"He's right," Dale said softly. "I'm documenting the extermination. But I haven't documented the *cause* of the extermination."

"The Rift," Sarah said.

"No," Dale said. "The infestation vector."

He looked at the sludge. He looked at the destroyed tape. He looked at the spot where the Hollow Mouth had died.

"The creature didn't just walk out of a hole," Dale said. "It was *deployed*. There was a nest. A localized breeding ground."

"Where?" Vance asked.

Dale closed his eyes. He accessed his Field Guide in his mind.

The entry for **Hollow Mouth** was there, populated with the data he had shouted.

But it was incomplete. It lacked the origin point.

Without the origin point, the Thoroughness Rating capped at 9.8. And the Observer required a perfect 10 to close the eye.

"I need the nest," Dale said.

"It's gone," Sarah said. "The explosion destroyed it."

"The nest is never gone," Dale said. "It's just displaced."

He opened his eyes. He looked at the sludge again. This time, he looked closer.

There was a pattern in the residue. A swirl.

A spiral.

It wasn't random, which was a pheromone trail. Hollow Mouths tracked their prey using chemical markers left on their feet.

The trail led back to the source.

Dale walked toward the bank. The street was a wasteland.

Rubble, broken glass, twisted metal.

But the sludge... the sludge was fresh. And it was leading *up*.

Toward the bank.

"Vance," Dale said. "Cover me."

Vance raised his rifle, aiming at the shadows.

"I'm out of ammo, Dale."

"Then use your eyes," Dale said. "And keep talking. If I stop, we're dead."

Dale walked up the steps of the bank. His boots slipped. He caught himself, his heart hammering against his ribs. He forced his breathing to slow. *In for four, hold for four, out for four.*

Protocol, and he reached the front doors. They were shattered. Inside, the bank was dark. But the floor was wet.

The sludge continued.

It pooled in the lobby. And in the center of the lobby, embedded in the marble, was a patch of chitinous debris.

Not a whole creature.

Just fragments. A leg. A piece of shell.

Dale knelt. He picked up the piece of shell. It was warm.

"Identify," he whispered.

His Field Guide pulsed. **Hollow Mouth Fragment.**

"Document," he said. He placed the fragment on the floor. He arranged it in a neat pile.

"Isolate," he said. He poured a canister of industrial bleach from his belt onto the fragment. The bleach hissed, reacting with the organic material. The smell was overpowering.

"Eliminate," he said. He crushed the fragment with his heel.

"Verify," he said.

The blue box in his vision flashed green.

**TREATMENT POINTS AWARDED: 50.**

**THOROUGHNESS RATING: 10.0.**

**COMPLIANCE OVERRIDE: SUCCESSFUL.**

The timer stopped.

*05:12.*

The Eye in the sky blinked.

A single, slow blink. Like a giant eyelid sliding shut.

When it opened again, the red light was gone. The interface in Dale's vision shifted. The warning text vanished. In its place, a new notification appeared.

**CERTIFICATION LEVEL UP: CLASS II -> CLASS III.**

**NEW PROTOCOL UNLOCKED: CROSS-CONTAMINATION CONTAINMENT FIELD.**

**WARNING: UNAUTHORIZED ENTITY DETECTED IN SECTOR 4.**

Dale stood up. His knees shook. He looked at Sarah and Vance.

"We're alive," Sarah whispered.

"For now," Dale said.

He looked at his Field Guide. The entry for Hollow Mouth was complete. But there was a new line at the bottom.

**NOTE: The Hollow Mouth was not native to this dimension. It was a carrier.**

A carrier?

Dale looked at the sludge on his boots. He realized then that the slime wasn't just from the monster.

It was from something that had ridden the monster.

Something smaller.

Something that had survived the acid, and he looked at his hand.

There was a tiny, black speck on his thumb. It moved.

It wasn't a bug. It was a mite.

And it was looking at him.

"Dale," Vance said, his voice dropping to a whisper. "Look at the bank vault."

Dale turned, and the vault door was open. Inside, the money was gone. But the floor was covered in thousands of tiny, black specks.

They were moving. Swarming.

They weren't attacking. They were organizing.

They were building a nest.

And they were looking at Dale, and he had just leveled up. He had just saved the city from erasure. And now, he had discovered that the real infestation hadn't been the giant rat.

It had been the fleas.

And they had just seen him take the job.
