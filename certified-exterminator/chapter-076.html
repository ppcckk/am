Chapter 76: Entry Cannot Be Deleted

# Chapter 76

The entry was rewriting itself.

Dale kept his eyes on the road and watched the text scroll in the corner of his vision. The Field Guide's entry for Dimensional Vermin had been stable for months. He'd refined it twice. The weakness notation was his own handwriting, translated into system language: *sensitive to concentrated ammonia vapor at concentrations above twelve percent.* He'd written that after three failed attempts with commercial-grade repellent in a crawlspace in Boardman.

Now the entry was changing, and the ammonia notation was gone. In its place: *sensitive to sustained psychological pressure when subject is separated from documentation tools.*

That wasn't his notation. That wasn't anything he'd written.

He blinked. The text shifted again.

*Behavioral pattern: subject will not engage threat without workspace preparation. Exploit: introduce contamination event during protocol initiation.*

Someone was building a treatment plan. For him.

His hands were steady on the wheel. His breathing was steady.

Fourteen years of this.

You don't panic at a frass trail. You document it. You trace it back to the source.

The source was the Authority, and the Authority had his data; the Authority had fourteen years of his reports, his Field Guide entries, his Thoroughness Scores.

All of it.

Every weakness he'd ever documented about anything, the Authority had cross-referenced against his own behavioral patterns.

He was the infestation. And someone had been doing a pre-treatment inspection.

The dashboard clock read 11:47. Forty-three hours until the hearing.

He was on Route 9, heading northeast toward the regional office in Youngstown, and the road was empty.

Too empty. He'd passed three other vehicles in the last hour, all of them moving in the opposite direction.

His phone buzzed. Not a text. A system notification, routed through the phone because the Authority had learned that humans respond to phone vibrations before they respond to floating text.

TREATMENT TEAM DISPATCHED. SUBJECT: KOWALSKI, D. CLASSIFICATION: UNLICENSED INFESTATION (PROVISIONAL).

OPERATIVE ASSIGNED: VOSS, R. CERTIFICATION LEVEL IV.

PROTOCOL: TARGETED ELIMINATION.

He read it once. He read it again. He put the phone in his breast pocket.

Targeted elimination. Not containment. Not isolation.

Elimination, and the Authority had skipped three steps in the standard protocol. That wasn't a hearing.

That was a hit.

He kept driving.

The checkpoint appeared at mile marker 84. A sedan, dark gray, parked at an angle across both lanes. A man stood beside it.

Tall. Lean.

Wearing a field vest with the Authority's insignia on the breast pocket.

His hands were empty.

He stood with the easy confidence of someone who knows exactly what they're going to do.

Dale slowed the truck. Stopped. Didn't get out.

The man walked toward the truck. He had a clipboard. A good clipboard.

Heavy-duty, waterproof, with a spring-loaded clip. Dale recognized it.

Class IV standard-issue model.

Cost more than Dale's truck.

The man tapped on the driver's window.

Dale rolled it down two inches.

"Kowalski." The man's voice was flat. Professional. "I'm Voss. I'm here to treat you."

"Treat me."

"You're classified as an unlicensed infestation. Provisional. The classification is under review, but I'm authorized to proceed with elimination pending the hearing outcome."

Dale looked at the clipboard, and he looked at Voss's hands. Voss's hands were clean.

No contamination.

No residue. This man had prepped his workspace before he got here.

"What protocol?" Dale said.

"Dimensional Vermin, targeted. I've reviewed your Field Guide entries. I know your patterns."

Something cold moved through Dale's chest. Not fear.

Recognition.

Voss had read his entries.

All of them. The ammonia notation.

The behavioral patterns. The workspace preparation requirement. Voss knew that Dale wouldn't engage without a clean workspace.

Voss had probably contaminated the area around the truck already.

"You put something on the road," Dale said.

Voss smiled.

Small. Professional. "Oil slick.

Twelve feet in front of your truck.

You won't drive over it. You won't get out.

You'll stay in the cab.

And while you're in the cab, I'll apply the protocol."

Dale looked at the road. An oil slick lay on the ground. He hadn't seen it. He'd been watching the Field Guide.

"You're using a Dimensional Vermin protocol on a human being," Dale said.

"You're classified as Dimensional Vermin, provisional."

"Provisional. Not confirmed. The hearing hasn't happened."

"The classification stands until the hearing. My authorization is valid."

Dale looked at the Field Guide. The entry for himself was still updating. Voss was editing it in real-time. Every observation Voss made was becoming a weakness notation.

*Subject exhibits elevated stress response when documentation tools are withheld.*

That was Voss's notation. Written in Voss's system-language.

Dale could see the difference, and the font was slightly different.

The phrasing was more aggressive. Less precise.

Dale's own entries used precise language. *Sensitive to.* *Pattern: subject will.* Voss's entries used imperative language. *Exploit: introduce.* *Response: eliminate.*

Voss wasn't documenting. Voss was targeting.

And that was the loophole.

Dale reached into his breast pocket. Pulled out his clipboard. Pulled out his pencil.

"What are you doing?" Voss said.

"Writing a report."

"You don't have a workspace."

"I have a clipboard. I have a pencil. I have fourteen years of professional practice. That's a workspace."

Dale opened the clipboard. Wrote at the top of a fresh page.

INSPECTION REPORT. SUBJECT: KOWALSKI, D. INSPECTOR: KOWALSKI, D.

DATE: TODAY. PURPOSE: SELF-INSPECTION.

He wrote it with steady hands, and he wrote it with the same handwriting he'd used on ten thousand reports. The system recognized it. He could feel the recognition.

A small pulse in the Field Guide.

NEW ENTRY DETECTED: SELF-INSPECTION.

The system flagged it. Of course it flagged it.

No protocol existed for self-inspection.

The Authority's rules covered identification, documentation, isolation, elimination, and verification. All of them assumed the inspector and the subject were different entities.

Dale had just broken the frame.

Voss's clipboard was still raised. He was waiting for the protocol to deploy.

But the Field Guide was shifting.

The entry for himself was being overwritten.

Not by Voss's notation. By Dale's.

*Subject: Kowalski, D. Species: Homo sapiens. Classification: LICENSED TECHNICIAN, CLASS II.

Status: ACTIVE. Infestation status: NOT AN INFESTATION.*

He wrote it in his own handwriting.

His own system-language. Precise. Accurate.

Fourteen years of professional documentation backing every word.

The system accepted it.

And then the cross-contamination penalty triggered.

Voss's protocol was a Dimensional Vermin protocol. He'd been preparing to deploy it on a target classified as Dimensional Vermin, and the target's classification had just been corrected to Homo sapiens. The protocol was now misapplied.

CROSS-CONTAMINATION DETECTED. PROTOCOL MISMATCH.

DEBUFF APPLIED: 40 PERCENT.

OPERATOR: VOSS, R. FIELD GUIDE ENTRY CONTAMINATED: 24 HOURS.

Voss staggered. His clipboard dipped.

His hands went unsteady.

The debuff struck him just as it had struck Dale in the crawlspace four months prior.

A physical weight. A wrongness in the body.

The system punishing a misapplied protocol.

"That's not right," Voss said. His voice was different now.

Thinner.

"You can't self-inspect. There's no protocol for that."

"There's no protocol that says you can't," Dale said.

"I checked. There's no rule against it.

The rules assume the inspector and the subject are different.

They don't prohibit them being the same."

Voss stared at him. The debuff was visible in his face. The color draining. The hands shaking.

"You contaminated your own entry," Voss said. "You'll lose access to your Field Guide data for twenty-four hours. You can't do that. You can't go into the hearing without your data."

Dale looked at his Field Guide. The entries were there, but they were blurred.

Unreadable.

Contaminated.

He'd lost fourteen years of documentation. All of it.

For twenty-four hours.

He'd traded his data for Voss's debuff, which was a bad trade. He knew it was a bad trade. But it was the only trade available.

"I'll survive," Dale said.

Voss raised his clipboard again. The debuff was at forty percent.

He was still standing, and he was still licensed; could still try to deploy the protocol, but it would be degraded. Ineffective.

He lowered the clipboard.

"This isn't over," Voss said.

"No," Dale agreed. "It isn't."

Voss turned.

Walked back to his sedan. Got in.

Drove away.

The oil slick remained. Dale didn't drive through it. He put the truck in reverse, backed up two hundred yards, found a side road, and took that.

He drove for twenty minutes in silence. The Field Guide pulsed every few miles, but the entries were blurred. Useless. He was operating blind for the first time in his career.

His phone buzzed.

SYSTEM NOTICE. SELF-INSPECTION FLAGGED AS PROFESSIONAL MISCLASSIFICATION CHALLENGE.

ADJUDICATION IN PROGRESS.

SUBJECT KOWALSKI, D.

WILL BE NOTIFIED OF OUTCOME WITHIN 48 HOURS.

Forty-eight hours. The hearing was in forty-three. The adjudication would come after the hearing. The system was running two processes in parallel.

He thought about that. The system didn't have a process for self-inspection. It had created one on the fly.

It had created a new violation type. Professional Mis

classification. A new category. The Authority had never had a category for a licensed technician who inspected himself, because the Authority had never had a licensed technician who inspected himself.

He pulled into his driveway.

Killed the engine. Sat with his hands on the wheel and watched the house. The porch light was on.

He'd left it off, and he always left it off.

His phone buzzed again.

ADJUDICATION COMPLETE.

PRELIMINARY FINDING: SUBJECT KOWALSKI, D. RECLASSIFIED UNDER STATUTE 14-C: VECTOR-CLASS ORGANISM, ACTIVE INFESTATION. SELF-INSPECTION RULED INVALID ON GROUNDS OF CONFLICTING INTEREST.

DEBUFF ON SUBJECT VOSS, R. REVERSED EFFECTIVE IMMEDIATELY.

Dale read it twice. Then a third time.

Reversed. Voss was back at full power. The debuff was gone. The forty percent penalty he'd traded fourteen years of data for had been undone by a bureaucratic ruling that hadn't existed an hour ago.

And the other finding. The one that wasn't about Voss.

VECTOR-CLASS ORGANISM. Active infestation.

The Authority had looked at his self-inspection and determined that the thing being inspected was not a technician. It was the problem. The infestation. The thing that needed to be treated.

His Field Guide entries were still blurred. He couldn't look up his own classification. Couldn't read what the Authority had written about him. Couldn't find the protocol designed to eliminate what he'd just been told he was.

The porch light flickered. Then went off. Then came back on. Then went off again.

His phone buzzed.

TREATMENT ORDER ISSUED.

SUBJECT VOSS, R.

ASSIGNED AS PRIMARY TECHNICIAN. TARGET: KOWALSKI, D.

PROTOCOL: CLASS-VII ERADICATION. ETA TO SERVICE AREA: 11 MINUTES.

Eleven minutes. Voss was coming back.

With a protocol designed to kill him.

And Dale was operating blind. No Field Guide. No weakness data.

No protocol toolkit that wouldn't contaminate itself if he tried to use it against his own classification.

He got out of the truck. Walked to the front door. Opened it.

The house was empty. The porch light was off. He hadn't left it on.

He looked at the doorframe. The sealant was intact.

No entry points.

No frass. No signs of intrusion.

But the air smelled like the crawlspace. The old crawlspace.

The one where he'd found the broodmother.

Fourteen years ago.

Before the Rift. Before the Authority.

Before any of it.

His phone buzzed one more time.

FIELD GUIDE UPDATE: NEW ENTRY UNLOCKED. SUBJECT: KOWALSKI, D.

CLASSIFICATION: VECTOR-CLASS.

WEAKNESS: UNKNOWN. RECOMMENDED TREATMENT: TOTAL ERADICATION. NOTES: THIS ENTRY CANNOT BE DELETED.
