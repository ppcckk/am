Chapter 210: Jump into the Core

# Chapter 210

The fingers closed. They were wet, cold, and smelled of stagnant runoff mixed with copper.

Dale didn't scream.

He didn't thrash. He held his clipboard tight against his chest with his left arm, shielding the paperwork from the crushing pressure of the black water hand. His right hand gripped the flashlight, aiming the beam directly into the center of the palm.

"Subject is a Class IV Hydro-Construct," Dale said.

His voice was flat, steady, and loud enough to carry over the rushing water. "Identification confirmed.

Rattus profundus variant.

Sub-species: *Hydro-Drainer*."

The hand tightened. Bones creaked. Not Dale's bones. The construct's internal skeleton, which was a lattice of fused vertebrae from a dozen different mammals.

*System Notification: Cross-Contamination Warning.* *Protocol Mite-Isolation (Class II) is incompatible with Target.* *Penalty: 40% Damage Reduction. Field Guide Entry Corrupted.*

Dale ignored the red text flashing in his peripheral vision. He knew the penalty.

He also knew that using the Mite protocol now would be suicide.

He needed the Class III protocol, but Class III required three consecutive successful exterminations without a single documentation error.

He had two. He needed one more to unlock *Structural Severance*, the only ability capable of cutting through the hydrostatic pressure holding this thing together.

The construct yanked him forward. The tunnel walls blurred. Dale dug his boots into the slime-slicked concrete, but the grip was absolute. He was being pulled into the throat of the beast, a cavernous maw of black water and jagged bone that opened in the darkness ahead.

He checked his wrist gauge. Oxygen levels at 88%. The respirator was holding, but the humidity inside the construct's throat was rising.

If the water level reached his chest, the electrical components of his flashlight would short out. Then the Field Guide would lose power.

Then he would die.

"Vex," Dale said into his comms. "I'm being consumed. Do not attempt rescue. Maintain perimeter."

Static crackled. Then Vex's voice, sharp and panicked. "Dale, abort! You don't have the clearance!"

"I have the paperwork," Dale replied. He dropped the flashlight. It didn't shatter.

It clattered against the ribcage floor of the construct's interior.

He reached into his utility belt and pulled out a canister of *Industrial Solvent, Grade A*.

It was not a weapon. It was a cleaning agent.

Most hunters used fire.

Or arrows. Or spells. Dale used solvent.

Specifically, a mixture designed to break down chitinous bonds in subterranean arthropods. It worked on *Rattus profundus* because their dermal layers were calcified, not fleshy.

This construct, however, was water and bone.

The solvent would do nothing.

Unless he changed the target.

Dale watched the inside of the maw. It wasn't just water.

It was a suspension. Tiny, glowing particles floated in the liquid. *Nematomorpha*. Horsehair worms.

They were parasitic, feeding on the neural pathways of the host creature.

*System Notification: New Species Detected.* *Entity: Rift-Parasite (Phylum: Nematomorpha, Genus: Riftworm).* *Weakness: Osmotic Shock.* *Status: Unidentified. 0% Bonus.*

Dale smiled. A thin, grim expression.

He didn't aim for the hand. He aimed for the eyes.

The construct had no eyes.

It sensed pressure. But it had a nerve cluster located at the base of the skull, where the worm colony anchored. That was the weak point.

It was also the point where the water pressure was highest.

Dale uncapped the solvent. He didn't throw it. He poured it directly onto his own faceplate.

"Protocol Override," Dale muttered.

He activated his respirator's purge cycle. The system tried to clear the chemical, but Dale jammed his thumb over the exhaust valve. The pressure in the mask spiked. The solvent, now pressed against the interior lenses, seeped into the microscopic vents of the respirator, mixing with the humid air inside.

It was a violation of Safety Protocol 12-B. Chemicals must never be introduced into the breathing apparatus.

*Ding.*

*Violation Recorded. Fine: 50 Treatment Points.* *Bonus: Improvised Chemical Delivery System created.* *Effect: Targeted aerosolization of solvent within sealed environment.*

The construct's hand squeezed harder. Dale's ribs groaned. Black water seeped into the cuff of his suit.

Inside the mask, the solvent vaporized. It coated the inside of the lenses. It coated the filter.

Dale opened his mouth. He screamed, not in pain, but to force the vapor out. He exhaled hard, directing the stream of aerosolized chemicals out of the respirator's intake port, which he had partially blocked to create a nozzle effect.

The cloud of solvent hit the floating *Nematomorpha* in the construct's throat.

The worms reacted instantly. The solvent disrupted their osmotic balance. They writhed, their translucent bodies turning opaque and brittle.

*System Notification: Weakness Identified.* *Field Guide Updated: Riftworm.* *Damage Bonus: +15% to Structural Integrity targets.*

The worms died. And when they died, the neural anchor failed.

The hydrostatic pressure holding the black hand together fluctuated. For a split second, the grip loosened.

Dale didn't try to escape. He tried to go deeper.

He slid down the construct's arm, using the reduced friction from the dying worms' secretions. He dropped into the throat.

It was warm here. The water was dark, lit by the bioluminescence of the remaining parasites. The smell was unbearable—rotting meat and ozone.

Dale landed on the ribcage floor. He rolled, coming up in a crouch. The hand was gone. The arm was still attached, dangling from the ceiling of the cavern, but it was limp.

*System Notification: Partial Extermination.* *Target Status: Incapacitated, not eliminated.* *Points Awarded: 120.* *Thoroughness Rating: 6.4 (Poor). Reason: Failure to verify elimination.*

6.4. It was a bad score. It would hurt his progress toward Class IV. But it wasn't a zero.

Dale stood up. He adjusted his respirator, checking for leaks.

The solvent had corroded the rubber seal slightly.

A hiss of air escaped near his ear. He noted it in his mental checklist. *Repair required. Priority: High.*

He looked around. The cavern was vast.

The walls were lined with thousands of these black water hands, fused into the rock like stalactites.

They were dormant.

Waiting.

And in the center of the cavern, there was a hole. A perfect circle, glowing with a sickly purple light.

The Rift.

Dale walked toward it. His boots squelched in the shallow water.

He reached the edge of the hole. It dropped straight down, seemingly into infinity. But at the bottom, about fifty feet down, there was a structure.

It was a pump.

A massive, industrial pump, made of rusted iron and bone. It was churning. The purple light pulsed with each rotation. The black water was being pushed up through the shaft, feeding the hands above.

Dale pulled out his tape measure.

"Distance to primary flow source: 14.2 meters," he said. "Flow rate: approximately 400 gallons per minute, upward."

He wasn't going to destroy the pump. That would cause a backlash. The water would explode outward, drowning the entire sewer network.

He needed to clog it.

He looked at his inventory.

*Item: Duct Tape (Industrial Grade, 50 ft).* *Item: Trash Bags (Heavy Duty, 12 count).* *Item: Spray Can (Empty).* *Item: Clipboard.*

No explosives. No acid. No fire.

Dale teared the trash bags. He worked quickly, efficiently. He cut the bags into strips.

Then he braided them. The plastic was strong, resistant to water.

He needed a plug. A physical barrier.

The Authority's rules stated that *Isolate* was a step in the protocol. Isolation didn't always mean walls. Sometimes it meant blockage.

He braided the plastic strips into a thick rope, then wound them into a dense, tangled ball. It was ugly. It was crude. It was exactly what a municipal technician would use to stop a drain backup in a residential home.

He threw the ball into the shaft.

It didn't fall straight. The updraft caught it. It bobbed, suspended in the air.

Dale pulled out his flashlight. The beam was flickering. Battery low.

He aimed the beam at the pump's intake valve. It was a grate, like a sink drain, but the size of a swimming pool. The plastic ball was drifting toward it.

He needed to push it in.

He picked up the canister of *Industrial Solvent, Grade A*. It was nearly empty. Maybe two seconds of spray left.

He sprayed it at the pump's blades.

The solvent hit the rusted metal. It sizzled. The blades slowed.

Just slightly.

The friction increased.

The ball drifted closer. It jammed against the grate.

The pump groaned. The sound was deafening. Metal shrieked against bone.

The water level in the cavern rose. The hands on the ceiling twitched.

Dale stepped back. He watched.

The ball was holding. But the pressure was building. The plastic was stretching. It was going to tear.

*System Notification: Structural Failure Imminent.* *Prediction: Catastrophic Rupture in 10 seconds.*

Dale didn't run. He checked his clipboard. He wrote a note.

*Recommendation: Install secondary check-valve. Material: Cast iron. Cost: $400.*

Then he looked up.

The hands were waking up. They were pulling away from the ceiling. They were reaching for him.

He had stopped the flow.

But he hadn't killed the source. The pump was still turning. The water was still coming.

And now, it was trapped.

The pressure had to go somewhere.

The nearest exit was the tunnel he came from. The tunnel where Vex was waiting.

Dale pulled out his comms.

"Vex," he said. "The tunnel is about to flood. Evacuate. Now."

"Dale, come back! The tunnel is unstable!"

"It's not unstable. It's full."

He dropped his clipboard.

It splashed into the rising water.

He didn't care. He had the data.

He had the identification. He had stopped the flow.

But the Authority wanted *elimination*.

He looked at the pump. It was vibrating violently. The bolt holding the intake grate was shearing off.

If he couldn't stop the pump, he had to break it.

He looked around. There was no heavy machinery. No explosives.

There was only him.

And the water.

Dale realized something. The *Nematomorpha* were gone.

The solvent had killed them.

But the *Rattus profundus* host was still alive. The black water hand was part of a larger organism. The organism was dying because the worms were dead.

The organism was in pain.

And pain made things erratic.

Dale grabbed a loose rebar rod from the wall. It was heavy. Rusty.

He didn't swing at the pump. He swung at the water.

He struck the surface of the pool. Hard.

The vibration traveled through the water. It hit the pump's intake. The plastic ball shuddered.

*System Notification: Physical Interaction Recorded.* *Action: Percussive Maintenance.* *Effect: Dislodged foreign object.*

The ball popped free.

The water rushed in.

The pump swallowed it. Then, the blades caught the plastic. They tore it. Shreds of plastic flew into the shaft, jamming the gears.

The pump seized.

The purple light flickered. Died.

The hands on the ceiling froze.

Silence.

The water stopped rising.

Dale stood there, breathing hard. His respirator hissed. His ribs ached. His suit was torn.

*System Notification: Infestation Isolated.* *Verification Pending.* *Points Awarded: 200.* *Total Points: 1,450.* *Progress to Class IV: 85%.*

85%. He was close. So close.

But the job wasn't done.

He had isolated the flow. He hadn't eliminated the source.

The pump was broken, but the Rift remained.

And the hands were still there. Dormant, but present.

A new notification appeared.

**NOTICE: AUTHORITY INSPECTION TEAM ARRIVING.** **ETA: 2 MINUTES.** **WARNING: UNAUTHORIZED INTERVENTION DETECTED.** **STATUS: PENDING REVIEW.**

An inspection team.

Dale felt a cold knot in his stomach. The Authority didn't send inspectors to help.

They sent inspectors to fine.

To correct.

To demote.

He had broken protocol. He had used solvent in a respirator. He had failed to verify elimination. He had caused structural damage.

His Thoroughness Rating would tank.

He looked at the dark tunnel behind him. Vex was gone. He was alone.

The water dripped from the ceiling. One drop. Then two. Then a steady rain.

The hands were twitching again. Not waking up. *Healing*.

Dale looked at his Field Guide. The entry for *Riftworm* was corrupted. He couldn't use the bonus again.

He needed a new way.

He looked at the pump. It was smoking. The plastic shreds were still jammed.

If he could extract them, he could restart the pump. And if he restarted the pump, he could reverse the flow.

He could flush the infestation *back* into the Rift.

It was a violation. A massive one.

But it was thorough.

Dale reached into his pocket. He pulled out his pen. He clicked it.

He walked toward the pump.

He wasn't going to fix it. He was going to break it completely. And he was going to document every single piece of debris.

Because if he didn't, the Authority would fine him for it.

And Dale Kowalski hated fines.

He stepped into the water. It was waist-deep now. Cold.

He reached the grate. He jammed his fingers into the jagged metal. He felt the vibration of the engine beneath.

He pulled.

A piece of plastic came loose.

He pulled another.

The engine groaned.

And then, from the darkness of the tunnel, a voice echoed. Not Vex's voice.

It was deeper. Older. It sounded like grinding stones.

"Technician Kowalski."

Dale froze.

He turned.

A figure stood at the edge of the tunnel.

It was tall. Thin. Made of the same black water as the hands, but shaped like a human.

It had no face.

Just a smooth, wet surface where a face should be.

It held a clipboard.

Dale's breath caught.

The Authority didn't send inspectors.

The Authority sent *auditors*.

And auditors didn't take notes.

They took licenses.

The figure stepped into the water. The ripples spread outward, darkening the light.

Dale looked at his own clipboard. It was gone. He had dropped it.

He had no proof of his work.

He had no defense.

The Auditor raised its hand. It pointed at Dale.

**NOTICE: LICENSE SUSPENDED.**

Dale's Field Guide went blank.

The pen in his hand snapped.

The Auditor took another step.

Dale didn't run. He didn't fight. He looked at the broken pump.

He had one move left.

He kicked the grate.

It flew off.

The pump exposed its core. A swirling vortex of purple light.

Dale jumped in.
