Chapter 62: Filing the Mimic

# Chapter 62

Dale tore the page off his clipboard.

Not the whole clipboard. Just the page.

The one with the mimic entry.

He folded it once, twice, into a tight rectangle no bigger than a matchbook, and dropped it into the concrete crack where the smudge had been. The paper hissed against the residue like a wet rag on a hot pan.

The recorder pulsed.

FIELD GUIDE ENTRY: FILING MIMIC (Mimicra documentalis). STATUS: DESTROYED. RE-IDENTIFICATION REQUIRED FOR RECOVERY.

He tore the next page.

And the next. Acid beetle.

Broodmother variant.

The chitin skitterer from the parking garage job. Each one he crumpled and fed into the crack. The Authority logged every destruction with a flat, bureaucratic beep.

Like a printer confirming a cancelled order.

His hands shook, and he noted that.

Made a mental mark. Contamination anxiety was spiking.

He breathed through it as he had during the crawlspace job and the Chapter 41 water main break, when three feet of contaminated runoff seeped toward his boots. Slow in. Slow out.

Hands steady.

The last page.

The one with the Level II entry. The one he'd written in the fluorescent hum of a gas station bathroom at two in the morning, three hours after the first big job, when he'd still been excited. He remembered the pen slipping on the paper.

He had underlined the weakness twice, proud of it.

He tore it.

The crack swallowed it. The smudge on the wall was gone. The concrete was just concrete.

FIELD GUIDE STATUS: EMPTY. ALL ENTRIES POST-LEVEL-II DESTROYED.

TECHNICIAN D.

KOWALSKI NOW OPERATING AT PROVISIONAL STATUS. TARGETED DAMAGE BONUSES: ZERO. FIELD GUIDE VERIFICATION: UNAVAILABLE.

He felt it immediately. The hollowness.

Like pulling a tooth you'd forgotten was infected, and the knowledge was still in his head.

He remembered every creature he'd documented. But the system didn't recognize it anymore.

The Authority didn't care what he remembered. The Authority cared what was written down.

Footsteps on the stairs. Heavy boots.

The kind with steel toes and the Authority-issue sole pattern that left a distinctive tread in wet concrete.

Dale counted three sets. A Class IV team was three-person minimum.

He stood up. Wiped his hands on his coveralls. The paper dust clung to his palms and he shook it off.

The basement door swung open.

Light poured down. Three figures silhouetted against it, then stepping into the stairwell.

The lead figure was a woman, maybe forty, with a recorder rig strapped to her chest and a clipboard in her left hand.

Her vest read CLASS IV in reflective tape. Behind her, two others. One male, younger, carrying a containment sprayer.

One female, older, with a documentation kit.

The woman scanned the basement. Her recorder pinged.

"Kowalski." Not a question.

"Yes."

"Field Guide status?"

"Empty. Destroyed. All entries post-Level-II."

She looked at him for a moment.

Her expression didn't change.

Authority technicians had learned not to react to the things they saw. Dale recognized the blankness.

It was the same look he got when he walked into a kitchen and found the cockroach population had reached critical density and the homeowner was still standing there in a bathrobe, smiling.

"Self-generated contamination," she said. "Tier Three violation."

"I know."

"Thoroughness Rating impact?"

"Pending."

"It's not pending. It's already calculated. Your rating dropped to 7.4."

He felt that one. 7.4. He'd been at 9.2 until the audit.

The drop was enormous.

Below 8.0 meant he couldn't take Class II jobs. Below 7.0 meant he lost his license entirely.

"Seventy-four," he repeated.

"You have thirty days to rebuild above 8.0 or you're demoted to Class I. You know the protocol."

"I know the protocol."

The woman stepped down the last three stairs, and she was close enough now that he could see the recorder's display. Her Field Guide was open.

He could see the entries.

Dozens of them. Neat handwriting. Professional formatting.

He looked at the first one. A chitin skitterer. Weakness identified: ventral membrane at the second thoracic segment.

He'd written that same weakness. In his own Field Guide. The one he'd just destroyed.

"Your guide," he said.

She looked down at it. "What about it?"

"Open it to the chitin skitterer entry. The ventral membrane."

She turned the page. Read it. Her face didn't change, but her hand tightened on the clipboard.

"That's the correct weakness," she said.

"It's the correct weakness I identified. I wrote it in my guide three weeks before you did. You copied it from the shared database."

"I didn't copy anything."

"Then someone fed it to both of us."

She was quiet for two seconds. Then she looked at her recorder. She looked at the wall where the smudge had been. She looked at the crack in the concrete where Dale had fed his pages.

"You destroyed your guide."

"Yes."

"Because you found contamination."

"Yes."

She flipped to another page in her guide. The filing mimic. Her entry. Weakness: the central filing node, located at the junction of the document stack.

Dale read it. His stomach turned.

That was the weakness he'd written, and the one that had been wrong.

The one the mimic had fed him through his own documentation. And now it was in her guide too.

"That entry is contaminated," he said.

"Prove it."

"I destroyed mine. Yours is still active. That's the proof. If it were clean, it wouldn't match what I destroyed."

She stared at him. The two team members behind her shifted. The younger one, the sprayer guy, looked between them with the particular blankness of someone who hadn't yet been given context.

"Re-identification protocol," the woman said. Slowly. Like she was testing the words.

"Yes."

"You're proposing we destroy our entries and re-identify from scratch."

"I'm proposing you check your guide against the Authority's master database. Compare your entries to the pre-Level-II versions. If they match exactly, you're clean. If they diverge in any way, specifically in the weakness field, you're contaminated."

She pulled out her phone. Scrolled. Compared. Her jaw tightened.

"Three entries," she said. "Chitin skitterer. Broodmother variant. Filing mimic."

"Same as mine."

"Same as mine."

She looked at the sprayer guy. "What about yours?"

He pulled out his phone. Scrolled. His face went flat.

"Two," he said. "Chitin skitterer and the acid beetle."

The older woman, the documentation specialist, didn't move.

"Mine's clean," she said. "I only have Level I entries."

The woman with the clipboard made a decision. Dale could see it happening. She was weighing the cost.

Destroying her entries meant losing her targeted bonuses. She'd be fighting blind.

But keeping them meant fighting with a guide that was actively lying to her.

She tore the page.

The sprayer guy tore his, and the documentation specialist kept hers.

FIELD GUIDE STATUS: CLASS IV TEAM MEMBER [REDACTED]. ENTRIES DESTROYED: 3. STATUS: PROVISIONAL. TARGETED DAMAGE BONUSES: ZERO.

FIELD GUIDE STATUS: CLASS IV TEAM MEMBER [REDACTED]. ENTRIES DESTROYED: 2. STATUS: PROVISIONAL. TARGETED DAMAGE BONUSES: ZERO.

The woman looked at Dale. "Now what?"

"Now we re-identify. The Authority's re-identification protocol requires a fresh encounter. We have to find the mimic.

Observe it.

Document it from scratch. The provisional entry gets marked as first contact, which means it's immune to the contamination that was already baked into the old entries."

"That means we have to fight it without knowing its weakness."

"Yes."

"That's a Class IV creature."

"I know."

She looked at the stairwell.

The light from above flickered. Something moved in the hallway. A sound like paper being shuffled.

Rapid. Wet.

Like someone was rifling through a stack of wet documents.

"It's already here," Dale said.

The sound stopped. The flickering stopped. The basement was silent except for the recorder's ambient hum.

The woman raised her clipboard.

Not as a weapon. As a shield.

She'd learned that much from the Authority's training, and a clipboard in front of your chest, recorder pointed forward, identification voice ready. Standard containment posture.

"Kowalski. You found this. You identified the contamination. What's your protocol?"

Dale looked at the empty space where his Field Guide had been. He looked at the three people in front of him, two of them now operating at provisional status. He looked at the stairwell where the paper-shuffling sound had come from.

"Re-identification," he said. "We document it live.

No pre-existing knowledge.

No weakness field. We observe, we write, we identify. The provisional entry gets a fresh weakness field that the mimic can't have pre-seeded, because the mimic's contamination vector was the old entries.

New entries, new vulnerability."

"That's a hypothesis."

"It's the only protocol that works."

The paper sound started again. Closer.

The top stairwell door began to move.

Not open. The door itself was changing. Its surface was rippling.

Document pages were pushing through the wood grain from the other side, spreading across it like mold.

The woman stepped back. "What is that?"

"It's using the door as a substrate. It's writing itself into the wood."

"Can it do that?"

"It's a filing mimic.

It files documents, and the door is a document. A permit.

A building code. It's all the same thing to it."

The door was fully covered now. The pages had consumed it.

They were still growing, spreading down the stairwell steps, and the pages were blank. Every one of them. Waiting to be written on.

Dale pulled out his phone. Opened a blank document. His hands were steady.

"Everyone watch the door.

Don't engage. Don't spray.

Don't swing anything.

You observe. You write. You identify.

The Authority will register the provisional entry the moment you submit the first observation."

"And then what?" the sprayer guy asked.

"And then we know what it is. And knowing what it is is the only thing that gives us a weakness."

The pages on the door began to fill.

Not with text.

With diagrams. The same diagrams Dale had drawn in his own Field Guide.

The same anatomical sketches. The same weakness annotations. The mimic was reconstructing his destroyed entries.

It was pulling them from the crack in the concrete, from the paper dust on the floor, from the memory of the Authority's system logs.

It was writing his Field Guide back onto the door.

Dale watched it happen.

Every entry. Every weakness. Every protocol.

The mimic was rebuilding his guide from the fragments he'd destroyed, and it was doing it faster than he'd written it the first time.

"It's not using the old entries," he said. "It's using the destruction itself.

The act of destroying the entries left a residue.

The Authority logged the destruction. The mimic is reading the log."

The woman stared at him. "So destroying the guide made it stronger."

"No. Destroying the guide made it visible.

The mimic was feeding quietly, and the destruction was loud.

The Authority logged it. The log is a document.

The mimic files documents."

He looked at his phone. The blank document.

He had one observation to make.

One identification. If he could name the mimic's current behavior, the Authority would register it as a provisional entry. A new entry.

One the mimic hadn't pre-seeded.

But he had to name it correctly. And the mimic was still changing. The diagrams on the door were shifting. The weakness fields were rewriting themselves in real time.

He typed.

OBSERVATION 1: SUBSTRATE USE. THE ORGANISM IS CONVERTING ARCHITECTURAL ELEMENTS INTO DOCUMENT SUBSTRATE. IT IS NOT ATTACKING. IT IS FILING.

The recorder beeped.

PROVISIONAL ENTRY CREATED: FILING MIMIC (Mimicra documentalis). STATUS: FIRST CONTACT RE-IDENTIFICATION. WEAKNESS FIELD: EMPTY. TARGETED DAMAGE BONUS: UNAVAILABLE UNTIL WEAKNESS IDENTIFIED.

He had a provisional entry. He had a name. But no weakness. Not yet.

The door was fully consumed now, and the pages had spread past the stairs and were crawling along the basement ceiling.

The mimic was expanding.

Filing the ceiling. Filing the walls. The entire basement was becoming a document.

And the pages were no longer blank. They were filling with text.

Not diagrams.

Text.

Names.

His name.

The woman's name.

The sprayer guy's name.

The documentation specialist's name.

Names. Dates. Certification numbers.

Treatment Points. Thoroughness Ratings.

Every piece of professional information the Authority held on them, printed in the mimic's hand across the basement ceiling.

"It's filing us," Dale said.

"What does that mean?" the woman asked.

"It means we're in its guide now. We're the infestation."

The recorder pulsed. A new notification. Not for Dale. For all four of them.

INFESTATION DETECTED: 4 UNLICENSED DOCUMENTS. CLASSIFICATION: FILING TARGET. STATUS: ACTIVE.

They were no longer technicians. They were paperwork. And the mimic was filing them.

Dale looked at the ceiling. His name was there. His certification number.

His Thoroughness Rating. 7.4.

Printed in the mimic's hand, and he looked at the woman.

Her name was there too. And her certification number. And below it, in the mimic's hand, a note.

A correction. Her rating.

Her real rating.

Not 9.1 like her vest said.

Not 8.7 like her record showed.

The number the mimic had written was 3.2, and she saw it. Her face went white.

"That's not right," she said.

"It is," Dale said. "It's what you actually are. The mimic filed it. The Authority hasn't corrected it yet."

"Correct it. Do something."

Dale looked at his phone. The provisional entry. No weakness.

No targeted bonus. He had a name and nothing else.

He looked at the ceiling.

The mimic's filing. Four names. Four ratings.

Four people reduced to documents, and he looked at the woman. She was Class IV.

She had a containment sprayer and a team and a vest that said she was qualified.

But the mimic had filed her at 3.2. And the Authority hadn't corrected it.

Which meant the Authority agreed.

Which meant she was not what she claimed to be.

"Who are you?" Dale asked.

The woman didn't answer. She raised her clipboard.

Not as a shield.

As a weapon.

The metal edge caught the light.

The recorder beeped.

CLASSIFICATION UPDATE: 4 DOCUMENTS. STATUS: FILING TARGET. ONE DOCUMENT FLAGGED FOR RECLASSIFICATION.

Dale read the notification. He read it again.

One document flagged for reclassification. That was the woman. The Authority was reclassifying her.

Not as a technician. As something else.

The mimic's filing was correct. She wasn't a technician. The Authority had filed her under the wrong category.

The sprayer guy stepped back. The documentation specialist didn't move.

The woman lowered her clipboard. Her face was calm.

"I'm not going to fight you," she said. "I'm going to file you."

She opened her Field Guide.

The one she'd just destroyed. Except it wasn't destroyed anymore. The pages were back.

The mimic had rebuilt them.

And they were in her hands, and she was holding the mimic's copy of her own guide.

And it was open to a page that didn't exist before. A page with Dale's name on it. A weakness field. Filled in.

His weakness. Written by the mimic. Filed under his name.

She looked at him. She read the weakness out loud.

"Contamination anxiety.

He won't engage until his workspace meets protocol standards. He'll stop. He'll clean.

He'll organize. He'll refuse to fight until the room is right."

Dale felt it land. Not physically. Professionally. Like a code violation notice slipped under a door.

The woman closed the guide. She raised the clipboard again.

"Protocol," she said. "You need a clean workspace. I'm going to give you one."

The sprayer guy aimed his containment unit at Dale.

Not at the mimic.

At Dale. The nozzle hummed.

Charged.

The documentation specialist stepped aside. She'd already made her choice. She was clean. She was watching.

Dale looked at the sprayer. He looked at the clipboard. He looked at the ceiling where his name was printed next to his rating.

He had one move. The provisional entry.

The one with no weakness.

If he could identify the woman's true classification before the sprayer fired, the Authority would register it. A new entry. A new target.

But the woman had his weakness. And she was going to use it.

The sprayer fired.
