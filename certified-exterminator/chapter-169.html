Chapter 169: Wet Concrete Claim

# Chapter 169

The concrete slab was still wet.

Dale pressed his palm flat against the surface, feeling the tackiness of the fresh pour. It held.

Good.

A structural integrity check on a wet foundation was sloppy work, but he didn't have time for proper curing protocols. The violation notice had already started eating at the edges of his vision, a red border pulsing like a migraine.

Sector: Municipal Infrastructure. Type: Unlicensed Construction. Status: Active.

He pulled a pen from his breast pocket.

The ink smeared on his thumb. He ignored it.

The form in his hand was not the standard Authority Inspection Sheet, which required three copies and a witness signature.

This was a blank sheet of legal pad paper, folded into a tri-fold. On it, he had written a single clause.

*Claim of Right: Operator possesses exclusive licensure to rectify dimensional breaches within Sector 4. All unauthorized construction constitutes an obstruction of lawful pest control.*

It was a lie. Dale did not have exclusive licensure for Sector 4.

He had a Class II Residential Pest Control certification, valid only for dwellings under two thousand square feet.

The Authority's system, however, operated on a strict hierarchy of definitions.

If the system accepted the definition, the penalty shifted. If the system rejected it, Dale was demolished.

The pre-authorization he had submitted in the van was a gamble. He had bet that the Authority valued procedural correctness over moral intent. He had bet that "Demolition of Operator" was a clause that could be waived if the Operator was performing their licensed duty.

He needed to prove he was licensed. He needed to prove the building was an infestation.

A sound came from the center of the slab. Not a footstep. A wet, sucking noise, like mud giving way under weight.

The purple hue of the sky deepened, casting long, bruised shadows across the construction site. The air smelled of ozone and rot.

Dale crouched. He didn't look up at the sky. He looked at the ground.

The concrete wasn't just wet. It was breathing.

Tiny bubbles rose to the surface, popping with a faint hiss. Each pop released a puff of gray spores. Dale checked his Field Guide. The entry for *Bureaucratic Phage* was still there, its weakness pinned to the top of his mind like a sticky note.

*WEAKNESS: PAPERWORK.* *DAMAGE BONUS: +15% AGAINST ENTITY THAT CANNOT READ.*

The entity building this structure was the Phage.

It had grown into the foundation, using the construction crew's labor to manifest a physical form. It was a creature of red tape, a parasite that fed on regulatory confusion. If Dale couldn't find a way to make the creature "read" the paperwork, he couldn't exploit the weakness.

He couldn't kill it with a spray or a trap.

He had to kill it with a citation.

But the Phage didn't have eyes. It had tendrils of shadow that mimicked steel rebar. It didn't have a mouth. It had a queue.

Dale stood up. He walked to the edge of the slab, where the violation notice hovered in the air, a translucent rectangle of red text. He held his legal pad up to it.

"Submit for review," he said. His voice was flat, the same tone he used when telling a homeowner their kitchen drain was a biohazard.

The red text didn't change.

**[ERROR: INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE.]**

Dale gritted his teeth.

He needed more evidence. He needed to document the infestation's behavior, not just its presence. The System required identification before elimination.

He had identified the species, but he hadn't identified the *nest*.

He pulled out his phone.

The screen was cracked, a web of glass spidering across the display.

He opened the camera app. He didn't take a photo.

He started a video recording.

"Inspection log, fourteen-fifteen hours," he narrated, his voice steady. "Subject: Unlicensed dimensional growth.

Location: 4th Street and Oak.

Observation: Substrate exhibiting active biological expansion. Indicators of infestation: Spore release, thermal inversion, auditory mimicry of construction machinery."

He panned the camera to the center of the slab.

The tendrils writhed, trying to hide from the lens. They were smart enough to avoid direct observation, but Dale knew the trick.

He turned the camera off and on again, quickly.

Blinking.

The System hated gaps in documentation. A blink created a discontinuity. A discontinuity was a code violation.

**[NOTICE: TEMPORAL GAP DETECTED. LOG INCOMPLETE.]**

Dale swiped the screen. He started the recording again.

"Correction: Observation continues. Substrate attempting to obscure documentation via rapid occlusion. This action confirms intent to evade inspection. Classification: Hostile Infestation."

The purple sky pulsed. A low hum vibrated through the soles of his boots.

The tendrils shot out, not at him, but at the camera.

They wrapped around the phone, squeezing the screen.

The glass shattered completely.

Dale didn't flinch. He let the phone fall. It cracked against the wet concrete.

He reached into his pocket and pulled out a physical logbook. Old school. Carbon paper.

No digital record for the System to hack. No battery to run out.

He uncapped his pen.

The tendrils recoiled. They couldn't process the analog data. The System couldn't scan it fast enough.

Dale wrote.

*14:18. Entity attempting to destroy recording device. Action classified as obstruction of justice. Penalty: Immediate termination of infestation rights.*

He tore the page out. He held it up.

The red violation notice flickered. The text changed.

**[REVIEWING EVIDENCE...]** **[EVIDENCE CLASS: PHYSICAL ARTIFACT.]** **[ACCEPTANCE: PENDING.]**

It was working. But it wasn't fast enough. The Phage was growing.

The concrete slab was rising, bulging upward like a tumor.

The rebar tendrils were thickening, becoming solid wood, becoming bone. They were building a structure, a tower that would pierce the sky and expand the Rift.

If the tower completed, the Rift would stabilize. The infestation would become permanent. Dale would be buried under it.

He needed a witness. The Authority's system favored collaborative verification for high-tier violations. Two signatures validated a claim faster than one.

He looked around.

The construction site was empty. The crew had fled when the ground breathed. But they were nearby.

He could hear them.

Shouting. Screaming. The sound of a car engine revving in panic.

Dale walked to the edge of the site, toward the chain-link fence.

A police cruiser was parked ten yards away, lights flashing.

Two officers were crouched behind the hood, drawing their service weapons. They were trying to shoot the tendrils.

The bullets hit the wood-bone hybrids and splintered harmlessly.

Officer Miller. Dale recognized him. He had written a noise complaint for Miller's neighborhood three years ago. Miller hated him for it.

"Hey!" Dale shouted, waving his logbook. "Officer Miller! I need a co-signer!"

Miller looked up, his face pale, sweat cutting tracks through the dust on his cheeks. He didn't lower his gun.

"Stay back, Kowalski!" Miller yelled. "It's a demon! Don't get near it!"

"It's not a demon," Dale said, walking closer. He held up the logbook.

"It's a Class II Structural Infestation.

I have pre-authorization for demolition. I need a witness to validate the hazard."

"You're insane," Miller said. Another officer, a rookie named Evans, pointed his rifle at Dale.

"Put the gun down, Evans," Miller said, his voice tight. "He's not the threat. But he's right. It's getting bigger."

The tower on the slab groaned. It rose another three feet. The air pressure dropped. Dale's ears popped.

"Miller," Dale said.

He didn't raise his voice. He used the tone he used for stubborn homeowners who refused to fix their gutter overflows.

"If that thing finishes, it eats the block.

You know my rating. Nine point two. I've cleared every Rift in this county.

Let me finish the paperwork."

Miller hesitated. He looked at the tower, then at Dale. He looked at the logbook.

"Sign it," Dale said. He tore off another page. He held out the pen.

Miller lowered his rifle.

He stepped over the chain-link fence.

He walked toward Dale, his boots crunching on the gravel. The tendrils didn't attack him.

They seemed to recognize the uniform. The System recognized the badge.

Miller grabbed the pen. His hand shook. He looked at the form.

"What am I signing?"

"Confirmation of hostile infestation," Dale said. "And waiver of liability for subsequent structural collapse."

Miller signed. His signature was jagged, hurried.

Dale tore the page. He held it up to the red notice.

**[EVIDENCE VALIDATED.]** **[WITNESS: MUNICIPAL LAW ENFORCEMENT.]** **[STATUS: ACTIVE AUTHORIZATION.]** **[PROTOCOL AVAILABLE: DEMOLITION ORDER.]**

Dale felt a surge of cold energy in his chest. Not power. Permission.

He didn't have a sword. He didn't have a spell. He had a form.

He took the signed page and placed it on the wet concrete. He pressed his hand over it.

"Authority," Dale said. "Execute Protocol: Demolition."

The system responded.

**[EXECUTING...]** **[SOURCE: OPERATOR BODY.]**

The pain hit him instantly. It wasn't physical pain.

It was the sensation of his own memories being deleted.

His childhood. His training. The smell of ant spray.

It was being stripped away, converted into raw kinetic force.

The concrete slab exploded.

Not outward. Upward.

A column of white light erupted from the wet cement, piercing the purple sky. The tendrils shrieked, a sound like metal tearing.

They tried to flee, but the light caught them.

The Phage's body, woven from shadow and regulatory confusion, unraveled. The wood-bone hybrids turned to ash. The spores burned away in the glare.

Dale fell to his knees. The logbook was gone.

The legal pad was gone.

His Field Guide, projected in his mind, flickered.

The entry for *Rattus profundus* faded. The entry for *Bureaucratic Phage* remained, but the text was bleeding out, the ink turning white.

**[INFESTATION NEUTRALIZED.]** **[TREATMENT POINTS AWARDED: 50.]** **[THOROUGHNESS RATING: 4.1.]**

Four point one.

Sloppy.

Dale gasped, clutching his chest. He looked at the crater where the slab had been. The tower was gone. The purple hue in the sky was fading, replaced by the normal gray of an Ohio afternoon.

Miller ran to him. Evans was right behind him, eyes wide.

"Kowalski!" Miller shouted. "You're alive?"

Dale nodded. He couldn't speak. His throat felt like sandpaper.

He tried to stand, but his legs wouldn't hold him. He looked at his hands.

They were shaking.

He looked at his Field Guide.

The System was quiet. No notifications. No pings.

Then, a new line appeared.

**[SYSTEM ERROR: MEMORY CORRUPTION DETECTED.]** **[REBOOTING OPERATOR PROFILE...]**

Dale blinked. A name flashed in his mind. Not his name. A name he didn't know.

*Valeria Thorne.*

He had never heard that name. He had never met her. But the System was pulling a file from the depths of the Authority's archive. A file that belonged to someone else.

Miller grabbed his arm, helping him up. "We need to get you to the medic. You look like you've been hit by a truck."

Dale stared at the sky.

The purple bruise was gone. But something else was there.

A faint, golden line tracing the horizon.

A seal.

The Authority hadn't just deleted his memories to pay for the damage. It had overwritten them.

Dale looked at Miller. He tried to remember the last time he had spoken to this officer. He tried to remember the noise complaint.

He couldn't.

He only remembered Valeria.

"Who is Valeria?" Dale asked. His voice was raspy, unfamiliar.

Miller frowned. "What?"

"Valeria," Dale said. "The one who signed off on the Sector 4 expansion."

Miller's face went white. He looked at Evans. Evans looked at Dale, then at the crater, then back at Dale.

"That's classified," Miller said quietly. "That's Class IX clearance."

Dale looked down at his hands. He expected to see his own hands.

He saw something else.

A scar on his palm he didn't recognize.

A tattoo of a symbol he didn't know.

The System hadn't just learned. It had recruited.

And it had used Dale as the vessel.

A new notification appeared, sharp and bright, cutting through the fog in his mind.

**[CERTIFICATION LEVEL IX UNLOCKED.]** **[STATUS: CANDIDATE.]** **[TASK: LOCATE VALERIA THORNE BEFORE THE NEXT RIFT OPENS.]** **[WARNING: FAILURE TO LOCATE SUBJECT RESULTS IN TOTAL ERASURE.]**

Dale stood up. He straightened his tie. He looked at Miller with eyes that were no longer his own.

"Write the report," Dale said. "I'll handle the rest."

Miller nodded, too shaken to argue. He pulled out his own notebook, his hands trembling.

Dale turned and walked away from the site. He didn't look back. He walked toward the van, but he didn't get in.

He started walking toward the center of town. Toward the old library.

Toward the place where the records were kept.

He didn't know why he was going there. He just knew it was the only place where Valeria Thorne's name was written in ink that the System couldn't erase.

And the purple line in the sky was widening.
