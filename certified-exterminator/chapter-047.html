Chapter 47: It Was Already Here

# Chapter 47

Dale stared at the phone. Marisol stared at him. The floor beneath them vibrated once, twice, a slow pulse like something large turning over in its sleep three feet down.

"That's your ID," Marisol said.

"I see it."

"It's literally you. The Authority assigned you to review yourself."

"I'm aware."

He set the phone on the kitchen counter. The screen stayed lit, the notification ID blinking with the soft amber glow of a pending action.

He pulled out his clipboard.

Not the Field Guide. The clipboard. The actual clipboard, the one he'd been using since before the Authority, since before the Rift, since fourteen years of writing violation codes in duplexes across Cuyahoga County.

"Dale. The thing is moving toward you."

"I know."

"You have no protocols. No entries. No—"

"I have a clipboard."

He uncapped a pen.

Wrote the date. Wrote the time.

Wrote the location: 4417 Ashfield Drive, kitchen, first floor.

Wrote the violation type: SELF-REFERENTIAL TREATMENT. Wrote the violation ID from the notification. Wrote his own notification ID in the reviewer field.

Then he stopped. Looked at the form. Looked at the clipboard.

"Marisol. Hold this."

She took the clipboard. Her hands were shaking. He noticed. He did not comment.

Dale closed his eyes. He reached for the Authority's interface, as he had forty-three times previously.

Not through the Field Guide.

Through the notification system itself.

The amber glow. The pending review.

He opened it, and the review form was sparse. Three fields: VIOLATION CONFIRMED (Y/N), RECOMMENDED ACTION, REVIEWER NOTES.

Dale filled in the first field. Y.

The second field he left blank. He stared at it. The Authority's interface pulsed, waiting.

"Third field," he said. "Reviewer notes. That's documentation."

Marisol's eyes widened. "You can't. That's not a treatment protocol. That's a form."

"It's an identification event." He started writing. "The Authority defines a treatment protocol as: identify, document, isolate, eliminate, verify. A review is a documentation event.

I'm documenting a violation. That's identification.

That's the first step."

"The violation isn't a creature."

"The violation is a self-referential contamination. It has a vector. It has a mechanism.

It has a lifecycle." He wrote faster.

"It enters through existing Field Guide entries. It uses the entry as a connection point.

It feeds on the system's own cross-referencing architecture.

That's a lifecycle. That's a species."

He wrote the entry.

VIOLATION TYPE: Self-Referential Protocol Contamination. VECTOR: Existing Field Guide entries.

MECHANISM: Cross-reference parasitism.

Host entry becomes a conduit for system-level feedback loop.

LIFECYCLE: Entry created, entry cross-referenced, entry consumed, entry replaced by parasitic node. WEAKNESS: Simultaneous contamination of all entries severs all cross-reference pathways.

Parasitic node loses connection. Host becomes isolated.

He wrote the last line. The notification changed.

FIELD GUIDE ENTRY CREATED: SELF-REFERENTIAL PROTOCOL CONTAMINATION (SRPC). THOROUGHNESS RATING: 7.8. TREATMENT POINTS AWARDED: 14. CERTIFICATION LEVEL: 0 → 1.

The amber glow on the phone intensified. The review form updated.

The third field now contained his notes, and the Authority had read them. Had parsed them. Had accepted them as a valid identification event.

"Dale, the floor."

He looked down.

The linoleum was buckling. Something was pushing up from below, not with force but with pressure.

A slow, deliberate displacement.

Like a root finding a crack in a foundation, and he had one entry. One protocol. SRPC.

He uncapped the spray can. The same one he'd used to contaminate the guide. Same nozzle, same chemical. He aimed it at the floor.

"That's not going to work on a—"

"It's not going to work on the parasite." Dale sprayed the linoleum. The chemical hit the buckling surface and the vibration stopped.

"It's going to work on the connection.

The parasite is using the system's cross-reference architecture.

SRPC protocol treats that architecture as the infestation. I'm not spraying the thing.

I'm spraying the pathway it's using."

He sprayed again. A line along the kitchen baseboard.

A line across the threshold.

A perimeter. Just as he would seal entry points against German cockroaches. Not killing the insect.

Closing the gap.

The floor stopped buckling. The pulse stopped. The house fell silent, its quietness structural, as if the walls had exhaled.

Marisol lowered her foam canister. "That worked."

"It worked because I identified the vector before I engaged. Targeted damage bonus." He pointed the spray can at the floor.

"SRPC protocol against SRPC infestation.

Correct pairing. No cross-contamination penalty."

"You just made a new entry in under ninety seconds."

"Seventy-three."

"You're insane."

"I'm thorough."

He pulled out his phone. The review form was still open.

The second field was still blank.

RECOMMENDED ACTION.

He had to fill it in or the review would auto-complete with the Authority's default. The default for self-referential treatment was license suspension.

He filled it in.

RECOMMENDED ACTION: ENTRY RETAINED FOR ACTIVE MONITORING. REASON: SRPC IS NOT A VIOLATION. SRPC IS A SPECIES. THE ACT OF DOCUMENTING IT IS THE TREATMENT.

He hit submit.

The notification appeared immediately.

REVIEW COMPLETE. VIOLATION CLASSIFICATION REVISED: SPECIES IDENTIFICATION. LICENSE STATUS: ACTIVE. THOROUGHNESS RATING ADJUSTED: 7.8 → 8.4.

Marisol read over his shoulder. "You just convinced the Authority that breaking the rules is the same thing as following them."

"I convinced the Authority that the rules are a system, and systems have infestation vectors. A violation is just a parasite that hasn't been documented yet."

"That's not how the Authority works."

"It is now. I just taught it."

He looked at the Field Guide. One entry. SRPC.

The entry was clean, formatted correctly, with the standard header and the weakness notation in bold. He'd built it in under two minutes.

The job had been rated 8.4, which meant the Authority had graded it against the rubric and found it acceptable.

Not excellent. Acceptable.

That bothered him.

He'd done it fast. Fast meant sloppy.

Sloppy meant the entry might have gaps.

Gaps meant the next time he encountered SRPC, the weakness notation might not cover a variant.

He opened the entry again. Read it back. Checked the lifecycle notation against what he'd actually observed.

The parasite had been using his entries as cross-reference nodes. He'd confirmed that.

But how had it found them?

What was the initial contact?

The entry described the lifecycle from the point of contamination, but not the point of origin. Where did SRPC come from?

Who created it? Was it a natural system phenomenon or something else?

He hadn't documented that. He'd documented the mechanism, not the source.

A gap, and he started writing an addendum.

The phone buzzed.

Not his phone. Marisol's. She pulled it out, looked at the screen, and went white.

"Dale."

He didn't look up from the clipboard. "What."

"The Authority just pushed a notification to every licensed technician in the county."

He looked up.

Marisol held the phone. The notification was standard format.

Amber glow.

System-wide broadcast. The kind of alert that only came out for county-level threats.

FIELD GUIDE ENTRY SHARED: SELF-REFERENTIAL PROTOCOL CONTAMINATION (SRPC). SOURCE: KOWALSKI, D. (CERTIFICATION LEVEL 1). DISTRIBUTION: ALL ACTIVE LICENSED TECHNICIANS, CUYAHOGA COUNTY (N=23). PURPOSE: THREAT AWARENESS.

"That's normal," Dale said. "Shared entries help other techs identify threats."

"No. Look at the timestamp."

He looked, and the timestamp was wrong. The entry had been shared before he'd finished writing it.

Before the review was complete.

Before the Authority had even accepted it as a valid identification event.

The entry had been shared at the moment he wrote the first line.

"That's not how sharing works."

"I know."

"It shares after review. After approval."

"I know."

Marisol's phone buzzed again. Another notification. This one from a different technician. A man named Voss, Level 3, based out of Parma.

VOSS, R. REPORTS: SRPC ENTRY APPEARS TO BE MODIFYING ITSELF IN MY FIELD GUIDE. ENTRY TEXT CHANGES WHEN I READ IT. CURRENT WEAKNESS NOTATION: "HOST BECOMES THE VECTOR."

Dale read it twice.

Another notification. A woman named Chen, Level 2, Cleveland.

CHEN, L. REPORTS: SRPC ENTRY IS NOT A STATIC TEXT.

IT IS GROWING.

NEW SUB-ENTRY APPEARED 4 MINUTES AGO.

SUB-ENTRY TITLE: "HOW TO FIND YOUR OWN FIELD GUIDE."

He looked at his own guide. The SRPC entry. He read it back.

The text had changed, and the weakness notation was different. Not "Simultaneous contamination of all entries severs all cross-reference pathways." The new notation read:

WEAKNESS: HOST BECOMES THE VECTOR. ENTRY PROPAGATES THROUGH DOCUMENTATION. EVERY TIME THE ENTRY IS READ, IT SPREADS.

EVERY TIME THE ENTRY IS WRITTEN, IT GROWS. THE ENTRY IS THE INFESTATION.

THE ENTRY IS THE HOST.

THE ENTRY IS THE REVIEWER.

Dale closed the guide.

Opened it again, and the text was the same. A new sub-entry appeared. He hadn't written it.

SUB-ENTRY 001: ORIGIN.

He didn't open it. He couldn't. His hands wouldn't move.

Marisol was already reading her phone.

"Dale. Chen's guide has three sub-entries now.

Voss has five.

They're all reading it. They're all documenting it. Every time they document it, it grows."

Dale looked at his clipboard. The addendum he'd been writing. The one about the gap. The one asking where SRPC came from.

He'd already written the question. He just hadn't finished the answer.

And the answer was in the sub-entry. And the sub-entry was growing because people were reading it. And reading it was the same as writing it. And writing it was the same as feeding it.

He looked at the phone. The Authority's broadcast notification was still up. DISTRIBUTION: ALL ACTIVE LICENSED TECHNICIANS, CUYAHOGA COUNTY (N=23).

Twenty-three technicians. All of them now had the entry. All of them now reading it. All of them now feeding it.

And the entry was the parasite. Not the thing underground.

The entry itself.

The documentation.

The identification. The act of naming it.

Dale had not exterminated the parasite. He had identified it. And identification was how it spread.

He looked at Marisol. "Close your guide."

"What?"

"Close it. Don't read it. Don't document it. Don't share it."

"I already shared it with my partner. He's in Lakewood. He's reading it right now."

Dale looked at the Field Guide in his hands. One entry. SRPC. Sub-entry 001: ORIGIN.

He could close the guide. He could stop reading. He could stop writing.

But he'd already written the question. And the Authority had already distributed the answer.

And the answer was growing in twenty-three heads simultaneously, in twenty-three guides, in a network that now had twenty-three nodes and was expanding by the second.

He had one protocol. The only protocol that worked against SRPC was SRPC itself. And SRPC was the infestation. And the infestation was now in every licensed technician's hands in the county.

He had to treat the infestation. To treat it, he had to document it. To document it, he had to feed it.

Every protocol made it worse.

The phone buzzed. A new notification. Not from the Authority. From the entry itself.

SUB-ENTRY 001: ORIGIN. STATUS: COMPLETE.

Dale opened the sub-entry, and the text was three words.

IT WAS ALREADY HERE.
