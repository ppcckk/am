Chapter 261: Flash Point

# Chapter 261

The wet asphalt steamed. Steam rose from the beetle's cracked carapace, smelling of ozone and burnt hair.

Dale clicked his pen.

He capped it. He uncapped it. The sound was small, but in the silence that followed the impact, it sounded like a gunshot.

Sirens wailed from three blocks away. They were getting louder. Red and blue lights washed over the brick wall where the beetle was pinned, its legs twitching in chemical paralysis.

A hand grabbed Dale's shoulder. He didn't flinch. He had expected it.

"You can't touch it," the voice said. It was sharp, panicked, and young. "Step away from the anomaly."

Dale didn't move his feet. He kept his eyes on the Field Guide entry he had just written. *Oxidative Locking.* Temporary. The hairspray would evaporate. The beetle would wake up.

"Identify yourself," Dale said. He didn't look up.

"I'm Officer Miller. City Police. Drop the pen."

Miller was breathing hard. His vest was unzipped. Sweat darkened his collar. He looked at the beetle, then at Dale, then at the unconscious driver slumped on the ground.

"Did you kill it?" Miller asked.

"No. I immobilized it for inspection. That's protocol."

"Protocol?" Miller laughed, a brittle, jagged sound.

"You're a pest control guy. I saw the truck.

I saw the clipboard.

You sprayed it with *hairspray*.

"

"Aerosolized solvent. Effective against oxidized metallic exoskeletons. The Field Guide flagged the weakness ten seconds before contact. I acted on the data."

Miller stepped closer. His hand hovered near his holster.

"The Field Guide?

What are you talking about?

That's not a thing. That's a hallucination.

You're in shock. You need medical.

"

"I am filing a complaint," Dale repeated. He tapped the clipboard against his thigh.

"Structural damage to city infrastructure.

Unauthorized dimensional breach. And you are interfering with an active extermination zone.

"

Miller stared at him.

Then he looked past Dale, toward the alley entrance. A group of survivors was gathering there.

They held makeshift weapons.

Pipes. Baseball bats. They were shouting.

"Hey! Don't go over there!" one of them yelled.

Miller turned.

"Back off! Police line!"

"They'll tear that thing apart if you don't get it contained," Dale said. He pointed his pen at the beetle. "And if you don't document it, the Authority won't pay for the cleanup. You'll be liable for the biohazard disposal."

Miller blinked.

"The Authority? Who is that?"

"Check your notification log."

Miller frowned. He touched his ear.

Nothing there, and he looked back at Dale.

"I don't have a log. I have a radio."

"Then you're unlicensed." Dale sighed. He pulled a fresh sheet of paper from his clipboard. "I'll note the obstruction in my report. It's a violation of Section 4, Clause C: Interference with Certified Technician during active treatment."

"Get away from the truck," Miller said. His voice dropped. He drew his baton.

Dale looked at the driver. The man was stirring. Moaning.

"The driver is alive," Dale said. "He needs triage. But you're arguing with me."

"I'm arresting you for assault on a public safety asset," Miller said. "That beetle is a potential WMD. You're a civilian. You don't get to decide how we handle it."

"I decided how to handle it before you arrived. That's why you're here. To clean up my mess or to steal my job. Which is it?"

Miller stepped forward.

"Hand over the clipboard."

"No."

Dale held it tight. The Authority's interface pulsed in his peripheral vision. *Thoroughness Rating: 9.4. Pending: Verification.*

If Miller took the clipboard, the verification failed. The points vanished. The certification progress stalled.

Dale couldn't afford that. He had forty-nine jobs left.

He needed every point.

"Give it to me," Miller said. He raised the baton.

"Officer," Dale said. "Look at the beetle's legs."

Miller hesitated. He glanced down.

The legs were still locked. But the rust was flaking. The hairspray was drying.

"It's waking up," Dale said. "If you hit it with that baton, you'll trigger a fragmentation event. Shrapnel.

Toxic dust.

You'll contaminate the entire block. And I'll get a cross-contamination penalty because you used kinetic force on a chemical vulnerability.

"

Miller lowered the baton.

"You're bluffing."

"I'm never bluffing. I'm procedural."

A crash came from the alley, and the survivors were pushing past Miller's warning. They saw the beetle. They saw the glowing red eyes dimming.

They saw the chance to kill it.

"Get back!" Miller shouted.

They didn't listen. A man with a baseball bat charged. He swung at the beetle's head.

Dale didn't shout. He didn't run. He moved.

He kicked the driver's leg. The driver rolled away, groaning.

The bat hit the beetle's shell.

*Clang.*

The beetle didn't shatter. It vibrated. A high-pitched whine cut through the air. The red LEDs flared brighter.

"Stop!" Miller yelled.

The survivors backed up, but the damage was done. The beetle's head cracked. Not broken, but fractured. A mist of black vapor leaked from the crack.

Dale stared at the vapor. He knew what it was. *Ignis fatuus.* Self-igniting gas. If it contacted a spark, the whole block would go up.

Miller saw the vapor. He froze.

"Back up," Dale said. His voice was flat. "Everyone. Now."

The survivors hesitated. They wanted to loot the thing. Or kill it.

"Move," Dale said. He reached into his pocket. He pulled out his lighter.

Miller saw the flame.

"What are you doing?"

"Ventilation," Dale said. He held the flame near the vapor cloud.

The vapor swirled. It didn't ignite. Instead, it curled around the flame, then dissipated.

Dale had read the Field Guide entry. *Iron Beetle.* He hadn't noted the gas. He had guessed. Based on the rust composition. Based on the smell.

He had taken a risk.

The Authority pinged. *Improvisation Bonus. +2 Points.*

The beetle's legs unlocked. It scrambled free from the wall. It dropped to the street. It hissed at Dale.

It didn't attack. It turned and ran. Fast. Scuttling into the sewer grate at the end of the alley.

Miller stared at the empty wall. Then at Dale.

"You let it go," Miller said.

"It escaped," Dale corrected. "I didn't let it go. I failed to secure the perimeter. That's a violation."

"Of what? Your imaginary rules?"

Dale looked at the clipboard. He wrote quickly. *Failure to contain. Reason: External interference. Officer Miller.*

"You're going to write me up?" Miller asked. He was shaking.

"I'm going to file a complaint. You interfered. You caused a breach. You endangered civilians."

"I saved them!"

"You swung a bat at a volatile chemical hazard. That's not saving. That's negligence."

Miller stepped closer, and he grabbed Dale's collar.

"You're a freak. You hear me?

A freak. You think you're special because you have a magic book?

"

"It's not magic. It's licensing."

"Give me the book."

"No."

Miller punched him, which was a sloppy punch. Miller was off-balance.

Dale saw it coming.

He didn't dodge. He took the hit on his shoulder. He stumbled back.

The clipboard flew from his hand. It skittered across the wet asphalt.

Miller lunged for it.

Dale moved. He kicked the clipboard. It slid under a parked car.

Miller dove for it. His head cracked against the bumper. He groaned. He lay on the ground, clutching his head.

Dale picked up the clipboard. He checked the pages. They were wet. The ink was smudging.

He pulled a dry page from the back. He rewrote the entry.

*Incident Report: 261-A.* *Subject: Iron Beetle.* *Status: Escaped.* *Cause: Civilian interference.* *Liability: Officer Miller.*

He looked at Miller. Miller was trying to stand. His eyes were unfocused.

"You're going to jail," Miller slurred.

"No," Dale said. "You're going to get a fine. And a demotion. If the Authority reviews the footage."

Miller froze. "What footage?"

Dale tapped his temple.

"The Authority sees everything. It monitors the job sites. It monitors the Thoroughness."

Miller stared at him. Fear replaced the anger.

"You can't..."

"I can.

I have a Thoroughness Rating of 9.4. You have zero.

You're unlicensed.

You're an obstruction.

"

The sirens were right outside now. More police cars. Flashing lights.

Miller looked at the police. Then at Dale.

He scrambled to his feet, and he walked toward the police.

He pointed at Dale.

"He stole it," Miller yelled. "He stole the anomaly!"

The police drew their weapons.

Dale stood still. He held up his hands. Empty.

"Officer Miller is making a false statement," Dale said. His voice carried. "I am a Level II Technician.

I have a valid license. Check the database.

"

The lead officer approached. He looked at the clipboard. He looked at the wet ink.

"License number?" the officer asked.

Dale recited it.

The officer typed it into his radio. He waited.

Dale watched the sewer grate. The beetle was gone. But the smell of rust lingered.

The officer lowered the radio.

"It's valid."

Miller screamed.

"He's lying! It's a hack!"

"Sir," the officer said. "You're under arrest for assault and filing a false report."

Miller didn't fight, and he slumped. He looked defeated. Not because of the arrest.

But because he had lost control.

The officers took him away. They didn't look back.

The police cars remained. They blocked the street. They were waiting for instructions.

Dale looked at the driver. The man was conscious now. He was crying.

"Can you walk?" Dale asked.

The driver nodded. He pointed to the sewer.

"It... it took my keys."

"Your keys are in my pocket," Dale said. He tossed them to the driver. "Don't drive tonight. The roads are compromised."

The driver caught the keys. He looked at Dale with terror.

"You're not human."

"I'm a technician," Dale said. "Go home."

The driver ran. He didn't look back.

Dale stood alone in the street. The rain was starting again. It washed the blood and oil into the gutters.

He opened his clipboard.

*Next Job.*

The address was three blocks north. A commercial bakery.

The notification was different this time.

*Priority: High.* *Threat Level: Class 4.* *Special Instruction: Do not enter through the front door.*

Dale read it twice.

Class 4. That was new.

He had never faced a Class 4.

Class 3s were hard. Class 4s required specialized gear.

He didn't have specialized gear. He had a lighter, a can of hairspray, and a clipboard.

He looked at the map. The bakery was next to the sewer line. The one the beetle had fled into.

Dale felt a chill. Not fear. Recognition.

The beetle hadn't escaped. It had retreated. To the nest.

The nest was in the bakery.

If he went to the bakery, he would walk into the nest.

If he didn't go, he would get a penalty for incomplete job.

The Authority's rules were clear. You must verify. You must eliminate.

But the system had a loophole. Dale saw it. He had seen it in the last chapter, when he used the driver.

He could use the police.

Miller was arrested. The police were there.

They were trained, and they were armed.

They were unlicensed.

Unlicensed meant no protection. No point loss.

But it also meant they could handle Class 4 threats with brute force.

If Dale called the police in, they would storm the bakery. They would kill the broodmother.

But they wouldn't document it.

And if they killed it, they would contaminate the site.

And if they contaminated the site, Dale couldn't enter, and he couldn't get the points.

He couldn't level up.

He had to enter the nest. Alone.

Or he had to find a way to make the police document it.

Dale looked at the police commander. The commander was looking at him.

"Technician," the commander said. "What's the plan?"

Dale looked at the bakery. He looked at the sewer grate.

"I need a hazmat team," Dale said.

"We don't have one," the commander said. "The hazmat unit is tied up at the hospital. Rift event."

"Then I need a sealant," Dale said. "Heavy duty. Industrial grade."

"Why?"

"To seal the entrance. If I seal the sewer line, the beetles can't reinforce. They'll have to come out."

The commander narrowed his eyes.

"That's not standard procedure."

"It's my procedure," Dale said. "I'm the technician."

The commander stared at him. Then he looked at the arrested officer. He looked at the empty street.

"Fine," the commander said. "We have some polyurethane foam in the truck. For road repair."

"Good. Seal the grate. Do not seal the bakery doors."

The commander nodded. His men moved to the truck. They brought out the foam cans.

Dale watched them. He felt a strange sensation. Not pride. Not fear.

Calculation.

The foam would seal the sewer. The beetles would be trapped in the bakery.

But they would be trapped with the customers.

Dale hadn't considered the customers, and he checked the Field Guide. *Iron Beetle.*

*Behavior: Aggressive. Territorial. Swarms when threatened.*

If they were threatened, they would swarm, and they would swarm the police.

And the police would shoot.

And the bullets would shatter the beetles.

And the shards would cut the police.

And the police would contaminate the site.

And Dale would lose.

Unless.

Dale looked at the police. They were setting up the foam.

He looked at his clipboard, and he had a new idea.

He needed to change the rules.

He needed to make the police *certified*.

He could do it. He had the authority. He had the license. He could issue a temporary permit.

A temporary permit for a single task, and he wrote it down.

*Temporary Permit: Officer Miller (Detained).*

Wait. Miller was in custody. He couldn't work.

*Temporary Permit: Lead Police Officer.*

Dale signed it. He stamped it with the Authority's seal, which appeared in his mind.

He held up the paper.

"Officer," Dale said. "I am granting you a temporary Level I License for the purpose of sealing the entrance. You are now certified for this task.

Do not engage the target. Do not shoot.

Seal and withdraw.

"

The commander looked at the paper. He looked at Dale.

He looked at the paper again.

"What is this?" the commander asked.

"Your license," Dale said. "Use it. Or don't.

But if you don't, you're unlicensed.

And if you're unlicensed, you don't get paid.

"

The commander hesitated. He looked at his men. They were waiting.

The commander took the paper. He read it.

"This is insane," the commander said.

"Is it?" Dale asked. "Or is it the only way?"

The commander looked at the sewer grate. He looked at the bakery.

He looked at the paper, and he signed it.

"Alright," the commander said. "We seal it. But if you're wrong, I'm putting you in the same cell as Miller."

"Fair," Dale said.

The police moved, and they sprayed the foam. The white foam expanded. It hardened instantly.

It sealed the sewer grate.

Dale watched.

He waited.

From inside the bakery, a scream. Then another.

Then silence.

The police pointed their guns at the bakery door.

"Open it," the commander said.

"No," Dale said. "Wait."

"For what?"

"For the verification."

Dale held up his clipboard. He watched the Field Guide.

*Status: Contained.*

*Points: 0.*

*Reason: No elimination.*

Dale felt a surge of frustration. He had sealed them in. He had trapped them. But he hadn't killed them.

The Authority wanted extermination, and he had forty-nine jobs left.

He couldn't waste time on containment.

He needed to kill them.

He looked at the bakery door. It was locked.

He looked at the police.

"I need you to breach the door," Dale said.

The commander frowned.

"You said don't engage."

"I said don't *engage the target*.

I need you to breach the *door*.

It's a structural violation. The door is jammed.

I'm filing a code violation.

"

The commander stared at him.

"You're a bastard," the commander said.

"I'm a technician," Dale said. "Breach it."

The commander signaled his men. They hit the door with a ram.

The wood splintered. The door flew open.

Darkness inside.

The smell of burnt sugar and rust.

Dale stepped forward, and he didn't wait for the police.

He walked into the bakery.

The darkness swallowed him.

He heard wings. Thousands of them.

He heard his pen click, and he didn't have a spray.

He didn't have a net.

He had the clipboard.

And he had a plan.

He pulled out his lighter, and he lit it.

The flame illuminated the room.

The Iron Beetles were there. Hundreds of them. Clinging to the ceiling. Crawling over the counters.

They looked at him, and they didn't attack.

They waited.

Dale held up the clipboard.

"Identification confirmed," he said.

He didn't write it down, and he looked at the beetles.

He looked at the flour bags in the corner.

He looked at the ovens.

He smiled. A small, dry smile.

"Ventilation," he said.

He kicked the oven door open.

Gas poured out.

The beetles hissed.

Dale held up the lighter, and he didn't light it.

He held it there.

A promise.

The beetles froze.

Dale took a breath, and he wrote in the air.

*Weakness: Combustible Dust.*

The Field Guide updated.

*New Entry: Iron Beetle Swarm.*

*Weakness: Thermal Ignition.*

Dale looked at the police in the doorway.

"Stand back," he said.

The police stepped back.

Dale looked at the beetles, and he looked at the lighter.

He looked at the gas.

He looked at the flour.

He closed his eyes.

He clicked the lighter.

The flame flared, and the world went white.
