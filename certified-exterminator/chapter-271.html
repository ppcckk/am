Chapter 271: License Revoked

# Chapter 271

His clawed hand pierced the membrane of the sky. The sound was wet, like a boot stepping into a puddle of oil.

Dale pulled.

The Rift didn't tear. It stretched.

Above the suburban street, the massive eye blinked. The pupil contracted.

A sound rolled across the neighborhood, not a roar, but a low-frequency hum that rattled the teeth of every person standing on the lawn.

Soldiers dropped their rifles, hands clamped over ears. Corliss fell to her knees, nose bleeding.

Dale ignored them, and he dug his fingers into the void, feeling for the anchor point. Pest control wasn't about force. It was about leverage.

*Field Guide updated: Overlord. Weakness identified: Structural Instability in dimensional membrane.*

The grey skin on his neck stopped spreading. The burning in his chest cooled. The Authority's notification pinged in his peripheral vision, crisp and bureaucratic.

*Violation Detected: Unauthorized Interdimensional Breach.* *Protocol Required: Containment before Elimination.*

Dale grunted, pulling a massive, translucent flap of reality down like a tarp. He wrapped it around the edges of the tear, sealing it with his bare hands. The void resisted, burning his palms, but Dale held the line. He pinned the flap, then pinned the corners.

"Sealant," he muttered. "Need more sealant."

Corliss scrambled up, wiping blood from her lip.

"Dale! Stop! You're feeding it!"

"It's not feeding," Dale said, his voice sounding like gravel grinding in a mixer. "It's leaking. I'm patching the roof."

"You look like a monster," a soldier shouted. He raised his rifle again, trembling.

Dale didn't turn around. He kept pulling the void-shutters tight.

"Put that down. You'll pop the seal."

"He's compromised," another soldier argued. "Command said shoot on sight of Class-9 corruption."

"Command didn't see the nest," Dale said. "I did. Stand down or I treat you as an obstruction."

The soldier hesitated. His finger hovered over the trigger.

"Listen to me," Dale said, finally turning.

His face was half-human, half-chitinous. The left eye was gone, replaced by a cluster of black orbs.

The right eye was still brown, still human, still tired.

"You want to kill the Overlord? You can't. It's not an animal.

It's a jurisdiction.

"

"A what?" Corliss asked, breathing hard.

"A jurisdiction," Dale repeated.

"You can't exterminate a government.

You file a complaint. You wait for the higher authority to step in.

But until then, you contain the infestation so it doesn't spread to the next block.

"

The soldier lowered his weapon.

"Containment? You just pulled a hole in the sky shut."

"Temporary," Dale said. "The pressure will build. Like a clogged pipe. If I don't install a valve, it bursts."

"Install a valve," Corliss repeated, staring at his clawed hand. "You're talking like a plumber."

"I'm talking like a professional," Dale said. He reached into his vest, but the pockets were empty.

He'd left his clipboard in the house.

He'd left his spray cans in the driveway. He had nothing but the void-sealant and his own body.

"Where's the kit?" Dale asked.

Corliss pointed to the house.

"Gone. The house collapsed when the eye opened."

"Damn." Dale looked at the sealed Rift.

It was holding, but the edges were fraying. Black mist leaked out, smelling of ozone and rotting lilacs.

"We need a Class-4 containment field.

Or at least a heavy-duty adhesive.

"

"We have duct tape," a soldier offered weakly.

"Not enough surface area," Dale said. "And the adhesive needs to be dimensional. Ordinary glue won't stick to non-Euclidean surfaces."

Corliss stepped forward. Her hands were steady now. She looked at Dale, not with fear, but with calculation.

"What do you need?"

"Chitin," Dale said. "Hard, dense chitin. And a binding agent. Something with high viscosity and low volatility."

"Chitin," Corliss said. "You mean from the Overlord's minions?"

"From anything with a shell," Dale said. "The Overlord shed skin when it opened the eye. It's all over the street."

The soldiers looked at the ground. Scattered across the asphalt were flakes of black, iridescent material. Some were the size of dinner plates.

"Those are dangerous," a soldier said. "The radiation—"

"Not radiation," Dale said. "Excretion.

It's waste, and the Overlord is molting.

It's leaving its old skin behind because it's growing too fast for the rift.

"

He walked to the nearest flake. He picked it up. It was light, like plastic, but hard.

He scratched it with a claw. Sparks flew.

"Good density," Dale said.

He looked at Corliss.

"I need a solvent. Alcohol.

Acetone.

Something strong.

"

"We have fuel," the lead soldier said. "For the generator."

"Is it high purity?" Dale asked.

"It's premium gasoline," the soldier said. "For the SUVs."

"Close enough," Dale said. "Pour it on the flakes. Let it soak. Then heat it."

"Heat it?" Corliss asked. "With what?"

Dale looked at the burning house, and the fire was still roaring, fed by the gas lines.

"Melt it. Fuse it.

Make a patch."

The soldiers exchanged glances. This was insane. Fusing monster skin with gasoline in a fire?

"Do it," Corliss ordered. "Now."

They moved.

One soldier dragged a can of gasoline to the fire.

Another collected flakes. Dale supervised, directing the placement with precise hand signals.

He didn't speak much. He pointed. He nodded.

He shook his head.

Within ten minutes, a thick, black paste formed in a metal drum they'd dragged from the garage. It bubbled in the heat. The smell was toxic, burning the eyes.

"Bring it here," Dale said.

Corliss lifted the drum.

"You're insane."

"Certified," Dale corrected.

He stepped toward the Rift. The seal was buckling. The black mist was pouring through faster now. The eye above was narrowing, watching him.

Dale dipped his clawed hand into the drum. The liquid sizzled against his chitinous skin. He didn't flinch. He scooped up a handful of the molten paste and slapped it onto the edge of the seal.

He pressed. He smoothed. He layered.

"More," he said.

Corliss tipped the drum.

Dale grabbed more. He worked with a rhythm that was hypnotic. Slap.

Smooth.

Press. Slap.

Smooth.

Press.

The soldiers watched, silent. The fear was gone, replaced by awe. This wasn't magic. It was craftsmanship.

"Left side," Dale said. "Need more pressure."

A soldier grabbed a shovel and pushed the paste against the void. Dale's claw held the other side. They worked together, human and monster, sealing the breach.

*Field Guide updated: Overlord.* *Status: Contained (Temporary).* *Treatment Points awarded: 0.* *Reason: Incomplete Protocol.*

Dale ignored the notification. Points didn't matter. Survival did.

The last layer went on. Dale smoothed it with his palm.

The black surface hardened instantly, turning from liquid to solid, and the seal was complete.

The Rift was closed.

Silence returned to the street. The hum stopped. The eye vanished.

Dale stepped back. He looked at his hand. The claw was cracked.

The skin underneath was blistered. He flexed the fingers.

They worked.

"It's done," Dale said.

Corliss slumped against the car.

"We're alive."

"For now," Dale said. He looked at the sealed patch. It was ugly.

Uneven.

A amateur job. But it held.

"Command is going to want to know what happened," the lead soldier said. "They'll want a report."

"Tell them it was a code violation," Dale said. "Structural integrity failure in the local spacetime manifold. Cite ordinance 44-B."

"44-B?" Corliss asked. "That's for termite damage."

"Same principle," Dale said. "Wood rots. Dimensions rot. You patch it, you inspect it, you charge the client."

"Who's the client?" Corliss asked.

Dale looked up at the sky, and the clouds were parting. Sunlight hit the street. But the light didn't feel warm.

It felt cold.

"The Authority," Dale said.

A notification flashed in Dale's vision. It wasn't the usual blue text. It was red.

*ALERT: UNAUTHORIZED INTERVENTION DETECTED.* *License Status: Under Review.* *Reason: Exceeding Certification Level III authority.* *Penalty: Immediate suspension of Field Guide access for 48 hours.*

Dale felt the link snap.

The thermal map of the county vanished.

The species names disappeared from his mind. The weakness of the Overlord was gone.

He was blind to the supernatural. He was just a man with a clawed hand and a bad back.

"Suspension," Dale said, his voice flat.

"What does that mean?" Corliss asked.

"It means I'm fired," Dale said. "Or I'm being audited."

"By who?"

"The Licensing Authority," Dale said. He looked at his hand.

The claw was retracting, and the grey skin was fading. He was becoming human again.

"But if you're fired, who stops the next one?" Corliss asked.

Dale picked up his clipboard from the rubble. It was charred, but the paper was dry. He clicked his pen.

"I'm not fired," Dale said. "I'm being called into court."

The ground shook.

Not a tremor. A heartbeat.

The sealed patch on the sky didn't break. It bulged. Something was pushing from the inside.

Dale looked at the bulge. It was small. The size of a coin.

"Pressure release," Dale whispered.

A hole opened in the seal. A single drop of black liquid fell onto the street. It hit the asphalt and sizzled.

Then another.

And another.

Corliss backed away.

"It's leaking again."

"No," Dale said. "It's sampling."

The soldiers raised their weapons.

"What do we do?"

"Dale," Corliss said. "What do we do?"

Dale looked at the drop. It wasn't just liquid. It was an eye. A tiny, perfect eye, staring up at him.

The Authority's notification appeared, but this time, it wasn't a system message. It was a direct voice in his head.

*Subject Kowalski. You have violated Protocol 9: Do not engage the Apex until authorized.*

*You are now designated as a Hazardous Material.*

*Containment team en route.*

Dale looked at Corliss.

"They're coming to arrest me."

"Who?"

"The Cleanup Crew," Dale said. "The ones who don't use sprays. They use nukes."

The tiny eye on the ground blinked. It recognized him.

Dale's Field Guide was locked. He had no weaknesses. He had no protocols. He had only his clipboard.

He looked at the approaching black car on the horizon. The roof was lined with antennas. The doors were sealed with lead.

"Hide," Dale said to the soldiers. "Hide in the house. Don't come out until I say so."

"Why?" Corliss asked.

"Because they're not here to save you," Dale said. "They're here to scrub the site."

The car stopped.

The doors opened. Men in white hazmat suits stepped out.

They didn't carry guns, and they carried canisters.

Dale stepped forward.

"Stop," he said. "You're not certified."

The lead hazmat worker tilted his head.

"Identify yourself."

"Dale Kowalski. License Class II. Certified Exterminator."

The worker laughed. It was a dry, harsh sound.

"Class II?

You're a child.

You're a hobbyist.

"

"I sealed the breach," Dale said.

"You contaminated the site," the worker said. "You touched the Void with bare hands. You are infected. You are evidence."

"I followed procedure," Dale said.

"Procedure is for civilians," the worker said. "We are the Authority."

Dale looked at the canisters. They were labeled *Nullifier*.

"Nullification is a Class VI protocol," Dale said. "It requires a Level VII license. You don't have one."

The worker stepped closer.

"We have Level IX. We are the End."

Dale looked at the sealed sky. The tiny eye was growing. The patch was failing. The Overlord was waking up.

And the Authority was trying to kill the only person who knew how to patch it.

Dale looked at Corliss.

"Run."

"To where?"

"To the nest," Dale said.

He turned to the hazmat worker. He didn't attack. He didn't fight. He pulled out his pen.

"I am filing an appeal," Dale said.

The worker raised his canister.

"Your license is revoked."

"My license is in the Field Guide," Dale said. "And you just took it away."

He dropped the pen.

It hit the ground.

And the ground opened.
