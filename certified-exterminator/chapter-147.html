Chapter 147: Protocol of the Mouth

# Chapter 147

The floor was gone.

Gravity reversed, snapped back, then reversed again. Dale Kowalski slammed into a surface that felt less like concrete and more like compressed ash. The impact knocked the breath out of him, a sharp, mechanical exhalation that tasted of ozone and old dust.

He lay face-down, fingers digging into the gritty substrate. His clipboard was still strapped to his chest. Good. The clipboard was the only thing that mattered.

Light pulsed in the periphery. Not the sickly green of the tunnel collapse.

This was a sterile, blinding white.

It scanned him. Top to bottom. Side to side.

**UNAUTHORIZED ACCESS DETECTED.** **USER: DALE KOWALSKI.** **STATUS: CLASS X PENDING.** **PENALTY: IMMEDIATE TERMINATION.**

The text burned into his retinas.

Dale rolled onto his back. The ceiling was far above, a swirling mass of geometric shapes that defied Euclidean geometry. They shifted, clicking and grinding like gears in a dry machine. Below him, the floor stretched into infinity, populated by rows of identical filing cabinets that stretched to the horizon.

Gary stood ten feet away. He looked solid, real, and terrified.

"Did you see it?" Gary shouted.

His voice didn't echo. The air here absorbed sound.

"The System didn't crash.

It recontextualized you.

"

Dale sat up. He checked his hands.

No blood.

No missing fingers.

Just dirt and the faint, vibrating hum of his Field Guide interface.

He opened the menu.

**THE AUTHORITY: [VULNERABILITY: SYNTAX ERROR]**

The entry glowed red. It wasn't a monster. It wasn't a species.

It was a bug. A glitch in the fundamental code of reality.

"You wrote 'Vulnerability' as a keyword," Gary said, stepping closer.

He kept his hands away from Dale, as if he were radioactive.

"That's not a Field Guide entry. That's a command injection.

You didn't identify a weakness.

You tried to overwrite the host.

"

"I followed protocol," Dale said.

His voice was flat. He wiped his palms on his jumpsuit.

"If you can't kill it, you document it.

If you can't document it, you edit it.

"

"You just broke the game, Dale."

"It wasn't a game. It was a workplace."

The white light intensified. A new notification popped up, overlapping the first.

**NEW INFESTATION DETECTED.** **TYPE: BUREAUCRATIC PARASITE (CLASS IV).** **LOCATION: LOCAL SECTOR 4.** **ACTION REQUIRED: QUARANTINE OR ELIMINATE.**

From the horizon, a shape detached itself from the filing cabinets.

It unfolded.

Legs. Too many legs.

Each leg ended in a stamp that clicked against the floor with the force of a hammer blow. The body was a massive, spherical mass of parchment and ink, pulsing with the same red error light as Dale's edited entry.

It wasn't a monster. It was an auditor.

Dale scrambled to his feet. His boots slipped on the ash-dust. He stabilized himself, leaning his weight on the clipboard.

"Run," Gary said.

"No."

"It's a Class IV. You're a Class I. You don't have the protocols."

"I don't need protocols." Dale pointed at the approaching entity. "I need paperwork."

The Auditor moved. It didn't walk; it flowed, a tide of paper and chittering legs. The air around it grew heavy, smelling of ink and rot. The ground beneath it dissolved, replaced by fine, gray dust.

**WARNING: CORRUPTION SPREADING.** **FIELD GUIDE INTEGRITY AT 85%.**

Dale looked at the status bar. It was dropping. Fast.

The Auditor reached them. It raised a front leg, the stamp head glowing with a harsh blue light. It struck the ground between Dale and Gary. A shockwave of pure administrative force rippled outward.

Gary flew backward, slamming into a filing cabinet. He slid down the metal face, groaning.

Dale didn't move. He stepped forward.

The stamp leg swung at him.

Dale ducked. The leg passed over his head, the wind of its passage stripping the loose threads from his jumpsuit. He rolled, coming up with his pen drawn.

*Identify.*

The creature was made of rejected reports. Failed inspections. Unfiled complaints. It was the accumulation of sloppy work.

*Document.*

Dale scribbled on the clipboard. *Subject: Bureaucratic Parasite. Vector: Administrative Negligence. Source: Unresolved Violations.*

The System blinked.

**IDENTIFICATION CONFIRMED: PARTIAL.** **BONUS DAMAGE AVAILABLE IF WEAKNESS DOCUMENTED.**

Dale needed a weakness. He couldn't fight a creature made of red tape. He couldn't spray it. He couldn't seal it.

But he could file it.

The Auditor reared back, preparing a second strike. Its body convulsed, shedding sheets of paper that flew into the air, turning into razor-sharp confetti.

Dale looked at the confetti. It wasn't random.

It was organized.

Alphabetical. Chronological.

*Entry Point.*

The creature had a primary intake valve. A mouth, if you will, but it was shaped like a shredder. And it was currently jammed.

A single, crumpled form was stuck in the mechanism.

The same form Dale had filled out in Chapter 1. The initial code violation for the duplex.

K-7.

Structural Entry Point.

It hadn't been processed.

Dale lunged, and he didn't attack the legs. He didn't attack the body. He attacked the jam.

He jammed the tip of his ballpoint pen into the shredder intake. The plastic snapped. The ink cartridge ruptured, leaking blue fluid into the creature's core.

The Auditor shrieked. A sound like tearing metal.

**ACTION LOGGED: INTERFERENCE WITH PROCESSING UNIT.** **PENALTY: CONTEMPT OF AUTHORITY.** **TREATMENT POINTS: -5.**

The penalty hit Dale like a physical blow. He staggered. His vision blurred.

Gary was getting up.

"Dale! Stop! You're contaminating yourself!"

"I'm correcting the record," Dale gritted out.

He twisted the pen. The blue ink spread, reacting with the creature's red error light. A chemical reaction, not a magical one. Acid on base.

The Auditor's legs buckled. The paper body unspooled, turning into a slurry of wet pulp.

Dale pulled the pen out. He stepped back, watching the creature dissolve into a puddle of black water and ink.

Silence returned to the sector.

Gary limped over. He looked at the puddle. Then at Dale.

"You cost points," Gary said. "Negative points. You just made your certification progress worse."

Dale checked his interface.

**CURRENT BALANCE: -5 TP.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 0.0.** **CERTIFICATION LEVEL: REVOKED.**

He felt a hollow ache in his chest. Not fear. Shame.

"I need a new pen," Dale said.

"You lost your license, Dale."

"I lost my license," Dale agreed. He picked up his clipboard. The paper was stained with blue ink. "But I still have the evidence."

"Of what?"

"Of the flaw."

Dale walked toward the horizon, where the filing cabinets stretched into eternity.

The environment changed. The sterile white faded to a dull, bureaucratic beige. The ash-dust floor became linoleum. The geometric ceiling lowered, becoming a drop-ceiling grid with flickering fluorescent lights.

They were no longer in the deep architecture. They were in the Break Room.

Dale stopped. He looked around.

Coffee machines. A vending machine with no snacks. A bulletin board covered in notices.

One notice was new. It was pinned with a red thumbtack.

**MEMO: REASSIGNMENT.** **EMPLOYEE: KOWALSKI, D.** **REASON: SYSTEM INTEGRITY VIOLATION.** **NEW ASSIGNMENT: ARCHIVE DEEP STORAGE.** **NOTE: SUBJECT IS TO BE KEPT IN ISOLATION UNTIL FURTHER REVIEW.**

Gary read over his shoulder. His face went pale.

"Deep Storage," Gary whispered. "That's where they keep the things they can't delete."

Dale nodded. He walked to the vending machine.

He pressed B4.

A bag of pretzels dropped, and he picked it up.

He opened it. He ate a pretzel.

"Where is the exit?" Gary asked.

"There isn't one," Dale said. He looked at the bulletin board. Another notice had appeared. It was handwritten, the ink still wet.

*The nest is not in the walls. The nest is in the office. And you just let them hatch.*

Dale looked at the ceiling vents, and a sound came from above. A chittering. Not the mechanical grind of the Auditor.

This was organic. Wet.

Clicking.

Something dropped from the vent, which was small. No bigger than a rat. But it had no eyes.

It had no mouth.

It had a face made of text. The letters rearranged themselves as it landed, spelling out words.

**HELP ME.**

**HELP ME.**

**HELP ME.**

It was a drone. A low-level servant. But it wasn't attacking. It was pleading.

Gary raised his hands.

"Don't touch it. It's a trap. It's mimicking distress to lure you in."

Dale watched the creature. He didn't pull out a weapon. He pulled out his Field Guide.

He opened to the blank page, and he wrote: *Class IV Drone: Emotional Mimicry.*

The creature stopped changing its text. It looked at Dale. Or seemed to.

Then it split.

The body unfolded. From inside the text-face came a dozen smaller, black insects. They didn't fly. They crawled out of the drone's shell, skittering across the linoleum.

They were fast. They moved in a coordinated swarm, a single mind with twelve bodies.

They formed a circle around Dale.

**WARNING: SWARM INFESTATION DETECTED.** **SPECIES: UNRECOGNIZED.** **FIELD GUIDE: EMPTY.**

Dale looked at the swarm. He looked at Gary.

"Gary, how many legs does a Class IV have?"

"Eight," Gary said, his voice tight. "Why?"

"These have twelve."

"So?"

"So they aren't Class IV."

Dale stepped into the center of the circle. The swarm tightened. They didn't attack. They waited.

"If I identify them as something they aren't, I get a debuff," Dale said.

"So don't identify them," Gary said. "Run."

"I can't run from a problem I haven't defined."

Dale looked at the insects. They were uniform. Identical. Perfect copies.

That was the flaw, and he pulled out a fresh sheet of paper from his clipboard. He wrote.

*Observation: Uniformity indicates lack of genetic diversity. High replication rate indicates... what?*

The insects twitched.

Dale remembered the broodmother in Chapter 1. He remembered the acid beetles. He remembered the logic of the System.

The System rewarded thoroughness. But it also punished assumptions.

He looked at the swarm again. One of them had a slight deformity. A bent leg.

*Imperfection.*

Dale wrote it down.

**IDENTIFICATION: MUTATED REPLICANT (CLASS V).** **WEAKNESS: CHAOS.**

He didn't speak the word. He thought it. He focused on the bent leg.

He projected his intent not as a command, but as a correction.

*You are flawed. you are defective. you are to be culled.*

The swarm froze.

The one with the bent leg exploded.

It wasn't a magical explosion. It was a structural failure. Its carapace cracked. The others recoiled.

Then they attacked.

Not with bites. With speed. They surged toward Dale, a wave of black chitin.

Dale didn't dodge. He held up his clipboard.

**ACTION: BARRIER (PHYSICAL).** **PROTOCOL: UNSANCTIONED.** **DAMAGE: MINIMAL.**

The insects slammed into the hard plastic cover. They bounced off.

Dale stepped forward. He kicked the pile of insects. They scattered.

He looked at Gary.

"They're afraid of order," Dale said.

"They're attacking you!"

"They're testing the boundaries."

Dale opened his Field Guide again. He looked at the entry for **THE AUTHORITY**. It was still there. Still glowing red.

He had a choice now, and he could try to fight the swarm. He could try to escape.

Or he could use the one thing he had left.

He looked at the **VULNERABILITY** tag he had typed in the tunnel.

It was still there.

*Vulnerability: Syntax Error.*

Dale looked at the swarm. They were waiting.

He looked at Gary.

"Gary, do you know what a syntax error is?"

"Is it a mistake in the code?"

"It's a mistake in the *structure*," Dale said. "It's when you try to speak a sentence that doesn't exist."

Dale looked at the air in front of him. He focused on the interface.

He didn't attack the insects, and he attacked the space they occupied.

He focused on the concept of *location*.

*You are here.*

*You are not here.*

*You are both.*

The System lagged.

The insects flickered.

Dale felt a tearing sensation in his mind. A headache, sharp and sudden.

**WARNING: REALITY STABILIZATION FAILURE.** **LOCAL SECTOR COLLAPSING.**

The walls of the Break Room dissolved. Not into ash. Into static.

Gary screamed. He grabbed Dale's arm.

"Dale, stop! You're deleting the room!"

"I'm deleting the *context*," Dale said.

The floor beneath them turned to glass. Then to void.

They were falling again.

But this time, Dale wasn't afraid, and he was watching the swarm.

In the void, the insects didn't fall. They floated. They hung in the nothingness, waiting for him.

And behind them, a shape was forming.

It wasn't an Auditor. It wasn't a drone.

It was a figure in a suit. A tall, thin man with no face, only a smooth, white mask.

He held a clipboard.

The figure raised a hand.

Dale's interface flashed.

**NEW COMPETITOR DETECTED.** **NAME: THE INSPECTOR.** **CERTIFICATION LEVEL: IX.** **OBJECTIVE: TERMINATE UNLICENSED OPERATOR.**

The Inspectior clicked his pen.

Dale looked at his own clipboard, and he looked at the void.

He looked at the Inspector.

Dale reached into his pocket. He pulled out the pen he had used to jam the Auditor. It was broken. But the tip was still sharp.

He pointed it at the Inspector.

"Protocol," Dale said.

The Inspector tilted his head.

And then, he smiled. The white mask cracked.

Underneath, there was no face.

There was only a mouth.

And the mouth spoke.
