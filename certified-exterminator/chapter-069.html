Chapter 69: Entry Fifty-Three

# Chapter 69

The gap had a frass trail.

Dale knelt in the nothing and pressed his thumb against the edge of a void cell. The residue came away grey and granular, like the droppings of a powderpost beetle, except these droppings were made of deleted information.

He could feel the shape of what had been removed.

A name. A date. A jurisdiction code.

He logged it on the clipboard.

ENTRY 51, SUBSPECIES A: External Authority (erased). Note: removal was surgical.

Clean cuts.

No collateral damage to adjacent records. This was not a containment breach. This was a termination.

The sharpening sound behind him changed pitch. Higher. Closer.

He checked his watch. Three minutes and twelve seconds.

He moved faster. The void cells weren't uniform.

Some were larger, some smaller, and the pattern of their arrangement was wrong for random deletion.

Random deletion produces a scatter pattern, like a shotgun blast through a filing cabinet, which was systematic. Sequential.

Someone had gone through these records in order, removing them one by one, and the gaps left behind had a consistent size and spacing.

Dale had seen this pattern before. Not in a void between records.

In a kitchen.

Specifically, in the pantry of a duplex on Elm Street where a family of brown-banded cockroaches had established a satellite colony behind the drywall. The frass trails had the same spacing. Same size.

Same logic.

A satellite colony. The External Authority wasn't hidden. It was relocated. Someone had moved it to a secondary location and sealed the original site.

He stopped. Looked down at his hands.

They were shaking, which was unusual, because his hands did not shake during inspections.

They shook during code violations. They shook when the violation was structural and the building might collapse.

He was not in a building. He was in the space between buildings. The space between buildings could not collapse.

Unless the Authority was the building.

Three minutes, and he walked the trail. The void cells got smaller as he went, which meant the records were getting more recent.

He was walking backward through a deletion sequence.

The most recent records had been removed first.

That was wrong. Standard protocol removed the oldest records first, to minimize disruption to active operations.

Whoever had done this was not following protocol.

Whoever had done this was someone who didn't care about protocol.

The sharpening sound stopped.

Dale turned. The Auditor was gone.

The space where it had stood was empty in a different way than the void cells.

The void cells were absences. The Auditor's absence was a presence. It had left something behind.

A mark.

A notation. A timestamp.

He read it.

APPLICABLE IN: 00:02:15. STATUS: AWAITING TARGET.

It hadn't stopped sharpening, which had finished. It was now ready to apply. It was waiting for a target.

And the target was him.

Two minutes and fifteen seconds.

He walked faster.

The trail narrowed.

The void cells became dense, packed close together, like a nest in a wall cavity, and the density meant the records here were important.

High-value. The kind of records you don't delete unless you have to.

He found the nest, which was a woman. She sat in a void cell that was slightly larger than the others, slightly less empty.

She had a clipboard.

She had a pen. She had a Field Guide that was open on her lap, and every entry in it was crossed out.

She looked up at him.

"You're late," she said.

Dale stopped, and he looked at her clipboard. He looked at her Field Guide.

He looked at her hands.

They were steady.

"You're the External Authority," he said.

"I was the External Authority." She closed the Field Guide. "Before they removed me from the records. Before they made me a gap."

"Who?"

"You know who."

Dale looked at the void cells around her. Dozens of them.

Hundreds.

All surgical.

All sequential. All recent.

"They deleted everyone," he said.

"Not everyone. Just the people who could resolve examinations. The people who had jurisdiction." She stood up.

She was shorter than him. Her clipboard was the same model as his.

"You passed your examinations, Dale.

Do you know how?"

"I identified the threats. I documented them. I eliminated them."

"You identified the threats.

You documented them. You eliminated them.

And then you verified." She stepped closer.

"The verification step. Do you know what it actually does?"

Dale's pen stopped moving.

"The verification step doesn't verify the threat is gone," she said. "It verifies that someone with jurisdiction confirms the threat is gone.

That's the External Authority.

That's what Entry 51 is.

Not a species. A requirement.

Every protocol has a verification step, and every verification step requires a second pair of eyes with legal standing. You've been doing it alone because the Authority never gave you one."

"Then how did I pass?"

"Because the Authority is the Authority. It verifies itself.

It has been the External Authority for every examination you've ever taken.

It wrote the questions and it graded the answers. " She tapped her crossed-out Field Guide. " I used to do that. I was Auditor Nine.

I verified every examination in this sector for forty years.

Then I found something in the verification logs that shouldn't have been there, and they erased me."

The timestamp pulsed.

APPLICABLE IN: 00:01:48.

"I need you to verify," Dale said.

"I can't. I'm not in the records. I have no jurisdiction. I'm a gap."

"Then I'll put you back in."

She laughed. It was not a pleasant sound. "You can't. You don't have the authority to reinstate an auditor."

"No. But I have the authority to document a new species."

Her face changed.

Dale opened his Field Guide, and he turned to the blank page at the back. Entry 51. External Authority (species: unknown).

He wrote beneath it:

SUBSPECIES B: External Authority (species: known).

Common name: Auditor Nine.

Status: ERASED. Reinstatement pathway: NEW ENTRY.

Classification: UNLICENSED PERSONNEL WITHIN EXAMINATION SPACE. Violation code: E-11.

He looked at her. "You're not an auditor anymore.

You're an unauthorized person in a restricted area.

That's a violation. And violations get logged."

"That doesn't give me jurisdiction."

"No.

But it gives you a Field Guide entry. And a Field Guide entry means you exist in the system.

And if you exist in the system, you can be the subject of a verification protocol." He held up his clipboard.

"I'm not verifying you. I'm verifying that the space between records has been treated. And the treatment requires an External Authority to confirm.

And the only External Authority available is the one I just documented as a violation."

She stared at him.

"That's not how the system works," she said.

"The system says: identify, document, isolate, eliminate, verify.

It doesn't say the External Authority has to be licensed.

It says the verification step requires confirmation from a second party with jurisdiction over the space. You have jurisdiction over this space.

You were the Auditor here. They erased you, but they didn't erase the space. The space still has your name on it.

I can see it in the void cells. Your jurisdiction code is still embedded in the gaps."

APPLICABLE IN: 00:01:12.

She looked down at her Field Guide. At the crossed-out entries. At her own name, which was the first entry, struck through with a clean line.

"If I verify you," she said slowly, "the Authority will see me. It will know I'm here."

"Yes."

"And then it will come for me again."

"Yes."

APPLICABLE IN: 00:00:58.

She picked up her pen.

She opened her Field Guide to the first page. She wrote her name above the strikethrough. Not erasing it.

Writing over it.

A new entry. A violation entry.

E-11.

Unauthorized personnel.

Then she looked at Dale.

"Write the verification," she said.

He wrote. His hand was steady.

VERIFICATION COMPLETE. External Authority: Auditor Nine (status: ERASED, reinstated via violation code E-11). Examination resolved. Thoroughness Rating: pending recalculation.

The white space shuddered. The void cells filled. Information flooded back into them like water into a cracked foundation, and the Auditor's timestamp went dark, and the sharpening sound stopped forever.

Then the system spoke.

NEW ENTRY DETECTED IN FIELD GUIDE. ENTRY 51 SUBSPECIES B has been classified under violation code E-11.

Cross-contamination check: protocol deployed (documentation/verification) does not match protocol designed for species type (External Authority).

Cross-contamination penalty applied.

Field Guide entries 1 through 50 contaminated. Duration: 24 hours.

Damage bonus against all previously identified species: SUSPENDED.

Dale read it twice.

Fifty entries. Gone.

Every weakness he'd documented.

Every targeted damage bonus. Every identification that had kept him alive for sixty-eight chapters of escalating threats. Contaminated.

Inaccessible.

For twenty-four hours, and he looked at Auditor Nine.

She was already walking away.

The void cells were closing behind her, filling with information, reasserting their original contents, and she was becoming part of the records again. Visible.

Findable.

"Wait," he said.

"The examination.

The Thoroughness Rating. It's pending recalculation.

What does that mean?"

She didn't turn around. "It means the Authority is recalculating your rating based on the new data.

The new data includes you reinstating an erased auditor.

The new data includes you identifying the space between records as an infestation. " She paused. " The Authority doesn't like being identified as an infestation, Dale."

"Is it?"

She turned. Her expression was flat. Professional. The expression of someone who had verified four hundred examinations and had seen the one thing that made her erase herself.

"The Authority is not an infestation," she said.

"The Authority is the pest control company. And you just documented the pest control company as a pest." She held up her clipboard.

On it, in fresh ink, was a notification he hadn't seen before.

NEW VIOLATION DETECTED. TYPE: UNLICENSED ENTITY PERFORMING LICENSED FUNCTIONS. SOURCE: THE AUTHORITY.

STATUS: REPORTABLE.

"You didn't report it," she said.

"You documented it.

There's a difference. Documentation is passive.

Reporting is active. And now the Authority knows you know."

APPLICABLE IN: 00:00:00.

The white space collapsed. Dale was back in the examination room.

The door was there, and the clipboard was in his hand. The pen was in his hand. His Field Guide was closed, and when he opened it, the first fifty entries were greyed out.

Contaminated.

Blank.

Entry 51 was still there. Both subspecies. Auditor Nine's name was legible. And below it, a new entry that had not been there before.

ENTRY 52: The Authority (species: unknown).

STATUS: UNIDENTIFIED. NOTE: You need one. You do not have one.

You have never had one, and he looked at Entry 52. He looked at Entry 51. They were identical. Word for word.

The examination room door opened.

A man in a grey suit stood in the doorway.

He held a clipboard. He held a pen.

His Field Guide was open, and Dale could see that every entry in it was crossed out.

"You're the External Authority," Dale said.

The man smiled. It was the same smile Auditor Nine had worn when she said you're late.

"No," the man said. "I'm the next examinee. And I need you to verify me."

He stepped inside. The door closed. The examination room sealed.

And Dale's Field Guide populated with Entry 53.

ENTRY 53: Dale Kowalski (species: unknown). STATUS: UNIDENTIFIED.

NOTE: You need one.

You do not have one. You have never had one.
