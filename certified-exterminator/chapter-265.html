Chapter 265: Audit at the Door

# Chapter 265

The parking lot of the Kowalski residence was a crime scene of wet asphalt and shattered glass. Dale stepped out of the truck with the precision of a man stepping into a hazard zone.

His boots crunched on the debris.

He did not look at the truck. He looked at the house. The front door was closed.

The windows were dark.

The motion-sensor light above the porch flickered, then died.

A notification pulsed in his peripheral vision, red and urgent.

**VIOLATION: UNAUTHORIZED IDENTITY FLUX DETECTED.** **STATUS: ACTIVE.** **REMEDIATION WINDOW: 15 MINUTES.**

Dale ignored it, and he pulled the clipboard from his jacket pocket. The paper was damp. He didn't care.

He needed to write. Writing was grounding.

Writing was protocol.

He walked to the front door.

His hand hovered over the knob. The handle was cold.

Colder than the October air. He pulled a pair of nitrile gloves from his belt. He snapped them on, the latex sounding like a gunshot in the silence.

*Item recovered. Pending review.*

He hadn't recovered anything. He had thrown it away.

But the Authority didn't care about physical objects.

It cared about data integrity. And Dale had just corrupted his own data stream by discarding a Class-V artifact.

The door opened before he touched the lock, and a child stood in the doorway.

Not his child. His niece, Maya.

She was seven.

She held a stuffed rabbit with one ear missing. Her eyes were wide, fixed on the space behind Dale's left shoulder.

"Uncle Dale," she said. Her voice was flat. Too flat.

Maya wasn't home. She was at her mother's house, three towns over. She was supposed to be asleep.

Dale's hand tightened on the clipboard. The knuckles turned white.

He looked past Maya, and the hallway was empty.

The coat rack held three jackets. His, his wife's, and one that was too small.

Maya's.

Maya stepped back. She didn't invite him in. She waited.

Dale stepped inside. He closed the door behind him.

He locked it, and he engaged the deadbolt, then the chain. Protocol required physical barriers.

"Maya," Dale said. "Where are your parents?"

"They're in the kitchen," Maya said. "They're making soup. The good soup. The one you like."

Dale didn't smell soup. He smelled ozone. He smelled copper. He smelled the distinct, sulfurous rot of a Void Mimic.

The Mimic wasn't just in the mirror, which was in the house. It had learned him.

It had learned his routine.

It had learned his daughter.

He walked toward the kitchen. His footsteps were heavy.

Deliberate, and he made sure they were audible.

If it was mimicking him, it would mimic his movement. If it wasn't, it would react to the sound.

The kitchen was bright. Yellow light. Clean counters.

A pot simmered on the stove. Steam rose in perfect, rhythmic curls.

His wife, Sarah, stood at the counter, and she chopped carrots. Her knife moved with mechanical precision. *Chop. Chop.

Chop.*

Maya sat at the table. She didn't eat. She watched the steam.

Sarah turned. She smiled.

It was a perfect smile.

Too perfect. The symmetry was flawless. The crow's feet were identical on both sides.

"Hey, hon," Sarah said. "You're late. The audit team is coming in an hour. We need to clean the garage."

Dale stopped. He didn't answer.

He looked at the carrots, and they were all the same size.

He looked at Sarah's hands. No scars.

No calluses. Sarah had a burn scar on her left thumb from a soldering iron in 2019.

This was not Sarah.

**TARGET IDENTIFIED: VOID MIMIC (HUMANOID VARIANT).** **FIELD GUIDE ENTRY: UNVERIFIED.** **WEAKNESS: UNKNOWN.** **RECOMMENDED ACTION: DO NOT ENGAGE.**

Dale's heart hammered against his ribs. He forced it to slow. Panic was contamination. Panic was unprofessional.

"I'm not cleaning the garage," Dale said. His voice was steady.

Boring.

"I'm here to inspect the perimeter. There's a code violation."

Sarah's head tilted. A fraction of an inch.

"What kind of violation?"

"Structural compromise," Dale said. "Entry points. You left the window open in the bedroom."

"It was closed when you left," Sarah said.

"I know," Dale said. "But the air pressure changed. The seal broke."

He took a step forward. Sarah didn't move. Maya didn't move. The steam didn't move.

Dale reached into his belt, and he didn't pull a weapon. He pulled a marker.

A thick, black permanent marker.

The kind used for labeling hazard bins.

"Maya," Dale said. "Do you like the rabbit?"

Maya looked down at the toy.

"It's dirty."

"Take it to the sink," Dale said. "Wash it.

Hot water.

Soap.

Scrub it."

Maya hesitated. Then, she stood up. She walked to the sink.

She turned on the faucet. She scrubbed the rabbit.

The water ran over her hands.

Real water. Real soap.

Sarah watched Maya. Her eyes narrowed.

"She's not supposed to touch the tap water. The municipal supply is compromised."

"Compromised how?" Dale asked. He uncapped the marker.

"Biological agents," Sarah said. "From the Rift."

Dale stepped closer. He was now within three feet of the counter. He could see the steam rising from the pot. He could see the reflection of the kitchen in the window above the sink.

The reflection was wrong.

In the glass, Sarah was not chopping carrots, and she was holding a knife. A jagged, black blade that wasn't there in reality.

The reflection was smiling.

A smile that had too many teeth.

Dale looked at the pot. The soup was boiling. It was bubbling violently now. The steam had turned black.

"Get away from the stove," Dale said.

"Why?" Sarah asked. Her voice was no longer Sarah's. It was layered. Multiple voices speaking in unison.

"Cross-contamination risk," Dale said. He raised the marker. "I'm documenting a biohazard."

He didn't attack Sarah. He attacked the stove.

He slammed the marker onto the burner control. He didn't turn it off. He scribbled over the dial. A thick, black X.

The Authority system registered the action.

**PROTOCOL ATTEMPT: MARKING AND ISOLATION.** **TARGET: KITCHEN APPLIANCE (CONTAMINATED).** **RESULT: PARTIAL SUCCESS.**

The black steam hissed. It recoiled from the marker ink. The ink smelled like solvent and ozone. It burned the Mimic.

Sarah screamed. Not a human scream. A digital glitch sound. Static.

Her face flickered. The skin rippled. For a second, Dale saw the void beneath. A swirling, hungry darkness.

Maya dropped the rabbit. It hit the floor.

It bounced.

It didn't break, which was real.

Dale grabbed Maya. He pulled her behind him. He put himself between the child and the thing that looked like his wife.

"Run," Dale said. "Go to the basement. Lock the door. Do not come out until I say the code word."

"What code word?" Maya asked.

"OSHA," Dale said.

Maya didn't question it. She ran. She knew the rules.

Dale had taught them. Safety first.

Always.

Sarah lunged. Her arms elongated. The elbows bent backward. Her fingers stretched into long, black claws.

Dale didn't dodge. He stood his ground. He held the clipboard up like a shield.

The claws struck the clipboard. The cardboard tore. The metal clip bent.

Dale felt the impact shudder through his arms. He gritted his teeth. He looked at the Field Guide entry in his mind.

**VOID MIMIC.** **TRAIT: MIMICS AUTHORITY.** **TRAIT: FEEDS ON DOUBT.** **TRAIT: CANNOT CREATE WHAT IS NOT PERCEIVED AS REAL.**

That was the loophole. The Mimic couldn't be stronger than Dale believed it was. But it could be stronger than Dale's *fear* believed it was.

Dale looked at the torn clipboard. He looked at the pen in his hand.

He didn't attack the monster. He attacked the paperwork.

He clicked his pen. He wrote on the remaining sliver of paper.

*Subject: Void Mimic. Status: Non-Compliant. Action: Quarantine.*

He wrote it with authority. He wrote it with the boredom of a man who had seen a thousand roach nests. He wrote it as if this was a routine Tuesday. He denied the eldritch horror its dramatic significance.

The Mimic paused. Its form wavered. It looked confused.

It expected fear.

It expected awe. It expected a hero's struggle.

It got a code violation.

"File not found," Sarah whispered. Her voice was glitching again. "Access denied."

Dale stepped back. He picked up the torn clipboard. He held it out.

"Sign here," Dale said. "Acknowledgement of receipt."

The Mimic recoiled.

It couldn't process the bureaucratic absurdity. It fed on emotional chaos. Dale offered none.

He offered protocol.

The creature shrieked. The sound shattered the windows above the sink. Glass rained down.

Dale threw the clipboard at it. Not as a weapon. As a projectile of intent.

The clipboard hit the Mimic's chest. It stuck there. Stuck to the void.

The black ink bled off the marker. It spread across the Mimic's form. The ink acted as a seal. A containment field.

The Mimic froze. It was trapped by the ink. Trapped by the label.

Dale grabbed his phone. He opened the Authority app.

He selected **FILE COMPLAINT: FALSE REPRESENTATION OF PERSONNEL.**

He uploaded the photo of the frozen Mimic. He tagged the location. He tagged the time. He tagged the violation code.

**SUBMISSION RECEIVED.** **PENDING REVIEW.** **ESTIMATED PROCESSING TIME: 3-5 BUSINESS DAYS.**

The Mimic dissolved. Not into death. Into dust. Into paperwork.

Dale watched it fade. His hands shook. He hid them behind his back.

Maya's voice came from the basement door.

"Code word."

"OSHA," Dale said.

The door opened.

Maya peeked out, and she saw the kitchen. She saw the black dust on the floor.

She saw the empty stove.

"Mom?" Maya asked.

Dale looked at the empty space where Sarah had stood. He looked at the knife on the counter.

It was still there.

Real. Steel.

"Your mother is... pending," Dale said.

Maya's face crumpled. But she didn't scream. She knew the system. She knew that some things took time.

Dale walked to the kitchen sink. He turned on the tap. He washed the marker off his hands. He scrubbed until the water ran clear.

His phone buzzed.

**NOTIFICATION: IMMEDIATE AUDIT INITIATED.** **REASON: UNLICENSED USE OF PROTOCOL: MIMICRY OF THE VOID.** **PENALTY: REVOCATION OF CERTIFICATION LEVEL IV.** **COUNTER-CHARGE: FRAUDULENT DOCUMENTATION.**

Dale dried his hands. He looked at the mirror above the fridge.

It was still cracked.

But it was just a mirror now. No four arms.

No faceless void.

Just his own face. Tired. Gray.

Human.

But in the reflection, his eyes were black. Solid, obsidian black.

He blinked. The eyes were brown. Normal.

He looked down at his phone. The text from the unknown number was still there.

*You signed the wrong form, Dale. The Hearing is tonight.*

Dale deleted the message. He opened the Authority app. He looked at his status.

**CERTIFICATION LEVEL: IV (SUSPENDED).** **PENDING ACTIONS: 12.**

He had just saved his daughter. He had just neutralized a Class-V threat. And he had just lost his license.

Without a license, he couldn't treat the next infestation. Without a license, he was just a man in a house with dead people's dust on the floor.

Dale walked to the garage, and he opened the door.

The workbench was cluttered with tools. Hammers.

Saws. Bug spray.

He pulled out a large, heavy box from the top shelf. It was labeled **CLASSIFIED**.

He hadn't opened it. He had thrown it out the window.

But the shards had turned into insects.

Insects that scurried into the darkness. Into the walls.

He had failed to verify the elimination.

The box wasn't in the truck. It was in the garage. Had it always been?

Dale picked up the box. It was warm. It hummed.

He put it on the workbench. He opened his Field Guide. He flipped to the page for **VOID MIMIC**.

He drew a new line.

**WEAKNESS: BUREAUCRATIC INERTIA.**

He closed the book. He picked up his phone. He dialed a number he hadn't called in ten years.

"Kowalski residence," he said. "I need a lawyer. A specialist in supernatural liability."

The line clicked.

"This is Dale Kowalski. I have a Class-V incident at my residence. I need a witness. And I need a notarized statement of innocence before the sun goes down."

The voice on the other end was smooth. Calm.

"Dale," the voice said. "It's too late for statements. You're on the list now."

"What list?"

"The list of things that are hungry."

The line went dead.

Dale looked at the front of the house. The motion-sensor light flickered on. Then off. Then on.

Something was walking up the driveway.

It didn't look like a monster. It looked like a man in a suit.

Dale grabbed a hammer. He didn't look like a pest control technician anymore. He looked like a man who had just been told he was next.

He walked to the front door. He opened it.

The man on the porch smiled. He held a clipboard. His own clipboard.

"Mr. Kowalski," the man said. "I'm here for your audit."
