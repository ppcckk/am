Chapter 317: Certification Expired

# Chapter 317

The clipboard was heavy.

It weighed nothing, technically. It was paper and plastic and a cheap clip. But holding it felt like holding the anchor of a ship that had already sunk.

Dale Kowalski stared at the blank page. The white space wasn't empty.

It was a void.

A vacuum. It pulled at the edges of his vision, trying to suck the memory of the last three hundred chapters right out of his skull.

The leather figure stood ten feet away. Its eyes were buttons. Sewn on tight.

"The Auditor does not carry a Field Guide," the figure said.

Its voice sounded like dry leaves skittering across pavement.

"You are a blank slate.

You are reset.

You are Level I again in practice, if not in title.

"

Dale didn't blink. He didn't check his pockets for the old spray cans. He didn't look at the ash pile that used to be the Grey Suit.

He looked at the sky, and the rift was closing. The jagged tear in the atmosphere, bleeding violet light, was sealing up like a wound receiving stitches.

The monsters pouring through had stopped.

But the ones on the ground?

They weren't gone.

They were confused.

A swarm of Chittering Skitterlings—Class-3 dimensional pests, roughly the size of house cats, with too many legs and mandibles that clicked like castanets—crawled over the rubble of the park. They didn't know who to kill anymore. The hierarchy had been decapitated.

One of them lunged at Dale.

Dale didn't raise a weapon. He raised his pen.

He scribbled on the blank page.

*Entry: Chittering Skitterling. Status: Unverified.*

The System notification pinged. Softly. Like a receipt printing.

**Unverified Entry. No Damage Bonus.**

The Skitterling's claws raked across Dale's forearm. Blood welled up, bright and red against the grey dust. Dale didn't flinch. He watched the blood drip onto the pavement.

"Contamination," Dale muttered.

He kicked the Skitterling in the thorax. It wasn't a hero's kick. It was a technician's kick.

He aimed for the joint where the leg met the body, where the chitin was thinnest. The leg snapped.

The creature shrieked, a sound like tearing metal, and scuttled backward.

Dale wrote faster.

*Weakness: Articulation joints. Vulnerable to blunt force trauma to segment three.*

The pen moved across the paper, but the words didn't stick. The ink slid off the page, pooling in a black puddle at the bottom of the sheet.

Dale frowned, and he shook the pen. The ink didn't flow. It was dead.

The leather figure tilted its head.

"You cannot document what you do not remember. Your Field Guide is gone, Dale. You are fighting blind."

"I have a working memory," Dale said. His voice was flat. Procedural. "And I have observation."

He stepped over a pile of broken asphalt. The ground was vibrating. The rift wasn't fully closed.

It was pulsing.

And with each pulse, the Skitterlings grew larger, and they were feeding on the dimensional bleed.

If he didn't stop them, they would multiply. If they multiplied, the Authority would classify this as a Class-5 Infestation. That meant a quarantine zone. That meant the county would be burned down to save the neighborhood.

Dale hated burning down neighborhoods. It was messy. It wasted resources. It required permits that took six months to process.

He needed a solution. Now.

He looked at the remaining Skitterlings. There were twelve. Maybe fifteen. Hard to count with the dust in the air.

He needed a trap.

But he had no net. No poison. No bait.

He had a clipboard. A pen. And the clothes on his back.

The leather figure watched him.

It didn't help. It waited. The Editors, those shadowy figures in the periphery, waited too.

They were waiting to see if the Auditor would fail. If he failed, they could take his license.

Permanently.

Dale ran toward the fountain, and the fountain was dry, filled with debris. But the pipe feeding it was still intact. A copper conduit, severed from the main line, still held pressure.

Dale slid across the gravel. He grabbed the end of the pipe. He twisted it.

Nothing happened.

The water wasn't there.

But the air was, and the dimensional breach was leaking gas. Not oxygen. Something heavier. Something that smelled like ozone and burnt sugar.

Dale sniffed. He knew the smell. He'd smelled it in Chapter 42, when he'd had to deal with a colony of Aerovores.

*Dimensional Gas. Combustible. Heavy. Settles low.*

He looked at the Skitterlings. They were low to the ground. They lived in the gas.

He looked at the pipe, and he looked at his pen.

The ink was dead. But the ballpoint tip was metal. Hardened steel.

Dale jammed the pen into the end of the copper pipe. He twisted it, using the pen as a lever to dislodge a blockage of rust and debris inside the conduit.

A hiss.

A stream of white vapor shot out. Not water. Gas.

The nearest Skitterling hissed, confused. It turned toward the source.

Dale held the pen steady. The gas cloud expanded, drifting low across the pavement, pooling around the ankles of the creatures.

"Ventilation failure," Dale said. "Structural compromise. Immediate containment required."

He grabbed a loose piece of concrete. He struck it against the metal pipe.

Sparks.

Not enough, and he needed a spark. A real one.

He looked at the leather figure.

"Do you have a lighter?"

The figure didn't move.

"Fine."

Dale pulled a flint from his pocket. He always carried one. Old habit. You never knew when you'd have to light a pilot light on a gas heater in a crawlspace that didn't have electricity.

He struck the flint against the steel pen.

Click.

Nothing.

Click.

A tiny spark. It died in the wind.

The Skitterlings were closing in. They were growing. Their legs were lengthening, turning into blades.

Dale struck again.

Click. Click. Click.

The gas cloud was thick now. It swirled around his boots. If he ignited it now, he'd be in the blast radius.

He needed distance, and he looked at the leather figure.

"You're standing in the gas."

The figure looked down. Its feet were indeed submerged in the white vapor.

"I am immune to fire," the figure said.

"Are you immune to oxygen deprivation?" Dale asked.

He struck the flint one last time, and he aimed not at the gas, but at the leather figure's button eyes.

The spark hit the button eye.

It didn't ignite the figure. It ignited the gas trapped *inside* the hollow space of the figure's coat.

The figure exploded.

Not with fire. With pressure.

The leather coat burst outward, scattering shards of bone and cloth across the clearing. The shockwave knocked the Skitterlings back.

Dale didn't stop. He ran.

He didn't run away. He ran *into* the cloud.

He held his breath. He closed his eyes. He moved by memory, by muscle, by the map of the park he had memorized in Chapter 12.

He reached the fountain's base. He grabbed the valve.

He turned it.

Water.

Real water.

It sprayed out, hitting the gas, cooling it, washing it away.

The Skitterlings, deprived of their fuel source, shrank. Their legs snapped back into normal proportions. They collapsed, confused, vulnerable.

Dale walked out of the mist, and he was soaked. His clothes clung to him. His hair was plastered to his forehead.

He looked at the pile of leather and bone that had been the Auditor's assistant.

He looked at the shivering Skitterlings.

He looked at his clipboard.

The page was still blank.

But the ink puddle at the bottom was drying.

And next to it, a single word had appeared.

*Archived.*

Dale stared at it, and he hadn't written it.

He picked up the pen. He tried to write another word. *Status.*

The pen moved. But the ink didn't come out.

Instead, the paper absorbed the pressure of the pen tip. A groove formed. A physical indentation.

Dale realized the loophole, and he couldn't write words. But he could draw maps.

He drew a circle around the fountain.

He drew an X on the pipe.

He drew a line connecting the X to the spot where the leather figure had stood.

The System notification appeared. Not in his vision. In the air. Written in floating, golden letters.

**Improvised Documentation Accepted.** **Protocol: Ventilation Failure Mitigation.** **Thoroughness Rating: 8.4.** **Treatment Points: 1,200.**

Dale exhaled, and he hadn't killed them. He hadn't exterminated them. He had changed their environment until they were no longer a threat.

It wasn't a kill. It was a control measure.

The Authority accepted it.

The Skitterlings scurried away into the sewer drains, seeking the damp dark.

Dale stood alone in the park.

The rift in the sky was gone. Just a faint scar of purple light, fading.

The leather figure was ash and bone, and the Editors were gone.

Silence.

Dale looked at the clipboard. The indentation of the circle remained.

He flipped the page.

The back of the sheet was blank too, and he flipped it back.

On the front, the word *Archived* was gone.

In its place was a new line.

*Next: The Suburbs.*

Dale frowned, and he looked at the street beyond the park fence.

The houses were quiet.

Too quiet.

He walked to the fence. He gripped the chain-link.

A dog barked.

Then another.

Then ten more.

The sound wasn't from dogs. It was from the walls.

From the foundations.

Dale looked down at the grass, and the grass was moving.

Not with wind. With something underneath.

Bulges. Ripples. Like a field of ants, but the size of cars.

The ground was infested.

Dale's hand shook. Not from fear. From recognition.

He knew this pattern, and he had seen it in Chapter 89. The Hollow Earth Breach.

But that had been a localized event. This was everywhere.

He looked at the horizon.

Across the entire city, the grass was rippling, and the entire suburb was a nest.

And the eggs were hatching.

Dale looked at his blank clipboard.

He had no Field Guide. No protocols. No knowledge of the species.

He had one pen. One page. And a city that was about to burst open.

He needed to identify the threat before it killed him.

But to identify it, he had to get close.

And to get close, he had to go into the ground.

Dale opened the gate, and he stepped onto the sidewalk.

The ripples in the grass stopped.

A single tentacle broke through the soil, twenty yards away. It was translucent. Pulsing.

It looked at him.

Dale didn't run, and he crouched.

He looked at the tentacle.

He looked at the pen.

He looked at the blank page.

He didn't write, and he drew.

A circle.

And inside the circle, he drew a question mark.

The System notification appeared.

**Query: Identify Species.** **Response: Unknown.** **Penalty: None.** **Reward: Unlock 'First Contact' Protocol.**

A new item appeared in his inventory.

*First Contact Probe.*

It wasn't a weapon, which was a microphone.

Dale held it up.

He pointed it at the tentacle.

And he waited.

The tentacle moved toward him, which had no mouth.

But it had a voice.

A low, grinding hum that vibrated in Dale's teeth.

It said: *"Certification Expired."*

Dale looked at the blank page, and he looked at the tentacle.

He looked at the pen.

He wrote, in the only way he could, with the pressure of his hand:

*Appeal.*

The ground beneath his feet gave way.

The fall did not end.
