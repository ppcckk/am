Chapter 27

The parent organism was not a mimic.

Dale knew this because the legs were wrong. Mimics had four. This thing had six, and they were jointed backward at the knee, bending the wrong direction like a grasshopper's hind legs scaled up to the size of a man's forearm. Each one ended in a pad that spread against the asphalt with a wet, suctioning sound. The body was segmented. The head was not a head. It was a cluster of mandibles arranged in a radial pattern around what Dale, after three seconds of staring, identified as a compound eye.

He wrote: *Six legs. Retrograde jointing. Radial mandible array. Single compound eye, approximately 40mm diameter. Body length estimated 1.8 meters. Segmented thorax, seven visible plates.*

The Authority's Field Guide slot pulsed. Empty. Unidentified. He needed a species name. He needed a weakness. He had neither, and the thing was six meters away and closing.

He backed up. Wrote: *Movement pattern: lateral, non-linear. Speed approximately 0.8 m/s. Antenna-like filaments extend from anterior segment. Possibly chemoreceptive.*

The thing turned. The compound eye caught the light from the parking lot's sodium lamps and scattered it into a thousand dull points. It made a sound like a wet zipper. Dale wrote: *Vocalization: low-frequency stridulation. Possibly communication. Possibly threat display.*

Five meters.

He needed closer. He needed to see the ventral side. Every arthropod had a vulnerable point where the exoskeleton met the soft tissue between segments. The intersegmental membrane. That was where the spiracles were, where the tracheal system opened to the outside. You hit the spiracles, you hit the respiratory system. You hit the respiratory system, the thing suffocated in its own armor.

He'd done this with cockroaches for fourteen years. You found the spiracles. You injected dust into them. The roach died choking on its own shell.

This thing was a roach the size of a car. The principle held.

Four meters. Dale crouched. He angled his flashlight under the creature's body and watched the ventral plates flex. Between each pair, a thin membrane stretched and contracted. Breathing. The spiracles were there, tucked into the membrane at regular intervals along the lateral line. He counted them. Nine pairs.

He wrote: *Spiracle count: 9 pairs, lateral, intersegmental. Vulnerability: direct penetration of intersegmental membrane at spiracle site. Respiratory system likely tracheal.*

The Authority's Field Guide slot flickered. A progress bar appeared. 40%. 55%. It needed more. More data. More time.

The thing lunged. Not at him. Past him. Toward the east, where Webb was herding the group.

Dale ran.

He ran because the thing was heading toward people who had no idea what they were looking at. They'd see a monster. They'd swing at it. They'd hit the thorax plates, the head cluster, the legs. None of those would work. The exoskeleton was too thick. The legs were too fast. The only way to kill it was to get a blade or a spike into a spiracle, and that meant reaching under the body, into the membrane, at the lateral line, while six backward-bending legs were trying to turn you into paste.

Dale caught Webb at the chain-link fence. The group was behind it. Twenty people. Some wounded. All terrified.

"Field Guide entry," Dale said. "I need you to look at my clipboard."

Webb stared at him. "What?"

"The thing. I identified its weakness. The Authority will grant a damage bonus if you engage with the entry visible. You need to see it."

"You want me to read your notes before I fight it."

"I want you to read my notes and then stab it under the legs."

Webb looked at the creature. It was twenty meters away now, moving through the parking lot with that wrong-legged gait, mandibles clicking. The group was scattering. Someone screamed.

Webb grabbed the clipboard.

Dale watched the Authority's interface update. A new line appeared, one he'd never seen before:

FIELD GUIDE ENTRY: ARTHROPODA PROFUNDA (TEMPORARY CLASSIFICATION) WEAKNESS IDENTIFIED: INTERSEGMENTAL MEMBRANE / SPIRACLE CLUSTER (9 PAIRS, LATERAL) TARGETED DAMAGE BONUS AVAILABLE: +45% AGAINST A. PROFUNDA ENTRY STATUS: ACTIVE. SHAREABLE.

Shareable.

Dale read it twice. The System had never offered a share option. Every entry had been his. Every bonus had been for his use only. But there it was. The Authority hadn't restricted distribution. The rule said: *Identifying a weakness in your guide before combat grants a targeted damage bonus against that species.* It didn't say the person with the guide had to be the one fighting.

"Under the legs," Dale said. "Between the plates. The soft parts. You'll see the holes. Hit the holes."

Webb nodded. He'd been a volunteer firefighter before the Rift. He knew what to do with a knife. He walked toward the creature with the clipboard in his left hand and a tire iron in his right.

Dale wrote: *Engagement initiated by unlicensed personnel. Field Guide relay active. Monitoring.*

The Authority logged it. A new notification appeared:

UNLICENSED PERSONNEL ENGAGING DOCUMENTED INFESTATION. TREATMENT POINTS: NOT ELIGIBLE. DOCUMENTATION CREDIT: ACTIVE.

Dale didn't care about the points. He watched Webb circle the creature. The thing reared up, front legs raised, mandibles spreading. Webb didn't flinch. He'd seen worse. He slid under the raised body, drove the tire iron into the lateral line, and twisted.

The sound the thing made was not a scream. It was a pop. Like a vacuum seal breaking. The legs went rigid, then limp. The body sagged. Webb pulled the tire iron out and hit the next spiracle. And the next.

Three seconds. Four. The thing collapsed.

Dale wrote: *Elimination confirmed. Unlicensed personnel. Field Guide relay: functional. Targeted damage bonus: applied. Estimated kill time: 11 seconds.*

He looked at the Authority's interface. The kill was logged. No points for Dale. No points for Webb. But the documentation credit was active. His Field Guide entry for A. profunda was now complete. The progress bar hit 100%. The entry populated. Species confirmed. Weakness confirmed. Entry locked.

Then the Authority did something it had never done before.

A notification appeared in red. Not the usual amber. Red.

PROTOCOL VIOLATION DETECTED. ACTION: FIELD GUIDE DISTRIBUTION TO UNLICENSED PERSONNEL. CLASSIFICATION: UNAUTHORIZED. REQUIREMENT: LEVEL V CERTIFICATION OR ABOVE. CURRENT LEVEL: II. PENALTY: PENDING REVIEW.

Dale read it. He read it again. He looked at Webb, who was wiping black fluid off the tire iron and breathing hard.

"Webb."

"Yeah."

"Did you see a notification?"

Webb squinted at the air. "Something appeared. I don't know what it says."

"Good. Don't look at it again."

Dale pulled up his own interface. The red notification was still there. Below it, a second line:

AUDIT TRIGGERED. PROTOCOL AUDIT: LEVEL II CANDIDATE. MANDATORY RESPONSE REQUIRED.

Below that, a location. A set of coordinates. Dale recognized them. They were the coordinates for the storm drain at the intersection of Maple and 9th. The one he'd sealed in Chapter 19. The one he'd sealed too well.

Below the coordinates, a time limit. Six hours.

Below the time limit, a line that made Dale's hands go cold:

FAILURE TO REPORT: PERMANENT REVOCATION OF ALL TREATMENT POINTS. ALL FIELD GUIDE ENTRIES PURGED. CERTIFICATION REVOKED. NO APPEAL.

He looked at the clock. 11:47 PM. The drain was four miles away.

"Webb."

"Yeah?"

"I need a car."

Webb looked at him. Looked at the dead thing. Looked at the group behind the fence. "Where are you going?"

"Audit."

"Audit for what?"

Dale closed his clipboard. He thought about the red notification. He thought about the fact that the Authority had never seen a Field Guide relay before. He thought about the fact that the Authority was not punishing him. It was auditing him. That meant it was interested. That meant what he'd done was new, and the Authority wanted to understand it.

That meant the Authority didn't know what to do with him yet.

That meant there was a window.

"I'm going to the storm drain," Dale said. "I need to be there before the Authority decides what I am."

He started walking. Webb followed. Behind them, the parking lot was quiet. The parent organism lay on its back, legs curled, spiracles exposed. Dale didn't look at it. He was already writing.

*Field Guide Relay: confirmed functional. Unlicensed personnel can access documented weaknesses. No points generated. Documentation credit active. Authority response: audit triggered. Implication: relay action is novel to the System. Authority has no precedent. Precedent is being created.*

He underlined the last line.

*Precedent is being created.*

The Authority's notification pulsed again. A new line appeared, smaller than the others:

ADDENDUM: AUDIT LOCATION UPDATED. NEW COORDINATES ASSIGNED.

Dale stopped. He looked at the coordinates. They were not the storm drain. They were a point three miles northeast. A place he'd never been. A place he didn't recognize.

Below the coordinates, a single word:

NEXUS.

He'd never seen that word in the Authority's interface. He'd seen infestation. Protocol. Certification. Violation. Contamination. Thoroughness. Never this.

He looked at Webb. Webb looked at him.

"That's not the drain," Webb said.

"No."

"Where is it?"

Dale pulled up his phone. Opened a map. Plotted the coordinates. They were outside the town limits. Past the interstate. Past the power lines. In a stretch of farmland that had been empty since the Rift opened.

"It's where the Rift started," Dale said.

The Authority's notification pulsed one more time. A countdown appeared. Five hours, fifty-three minutes.

Dale started running.
