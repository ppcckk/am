Chapter 31

The thing was sitting in the middle of Route 9 like a dog waiting for a treat. Except it had too many legs. And the legs were wrong.

Dale stopped. Voss stopped behind him.

"Class three," he said.

He didn't say it to Voss. He said it to the air. The Authority logged everything. If he was going to get nailed for operating while suspended, he was at least going to get nailed with accurate documentation.

The creature was roughly the size of a large dog. Maybe seventy pounds. Its body was segmented like a pill bug, but the segments were plated in something that looked like wet ceramic. Six legs on each side. No head he could see. Instead, the front end had a flat triangular face with three vertical slits where the mouth should be. The slits were closed. The thing was breathing through them.

Dale's Field Guide pulsed.

He looked at the entry.

*Species: Unregistered. Morphological markers consistent with Order Isopoda, Family Armadillidiidae, but dimensional variance exceeds known parameters. Recommend: do not engage without full morphological documentation.*

"New species," Dale said.

He crouched. Pulled out his phone. Took three photos. Front, side, rear. Standard documentation protocol.

"You can't do that," Voss said. "You're suspended."

"Documentation isn't a protocol." He snapped another photo. "Identification isn't a protocol. I'm not treating it. I'm looking at it."

Voss didn't argue. She'd learned not to argue when Dale was in documentation mode.

The creature shifted. Its legs clicked against the asphalt. The slits opened. Something came out. Not sound. Not quite. A vibration Dale felt in his molars.

He took another photo.

The thing turned toward them.

"Back up," Dale said. "Slow. Don't run."

"What is it?"

"I don't know yet. That's the problem."

The creature moved. Fast. Not a charge. A lateral shuffle, like a crab testing the tide. It covered twenty feet in two seconds and stopped ten feet from them. The slits opened wider. The vibration intensified. Dale's vision blurred at the edges.

"Voss. Get behind the truck."

She moved. Dale didn't.

He was still looking at the Field Guide. The entry hadn't updated. No weakness listed. No vulnerability flagged. He was fighting blind.

The creature lunged.

Dale stepped left. Not fast. Not heroic. He stepped left because the thing's legs moved in a pattern. Six on the left, six on the right. When it lunged, it committed to a single vector. You could read the vector in the leg sequence. He'd seen it in pill bugs. He'd seen it in the Dimensional Vermin. Everything with six legs on each side had the same basic locomotion problem. It couldn't change direction mid-lunge.

He stepped left. The thing went right. It crashed into the guardrail and bounced.

Dale backed up. Still taking photos.

"Helpful," Voss said from behind the truck.

"I'm building a file."

The creature recovered. Shook itself. The ceramic plates rattled. It turned again. This time it didn't lunge. It advanced. Slow. Methodical. The slits were fully open now. The vibration was constant. Dale could feel it in his chest. A pressure behind his eyes.

"Voss. The spray."

She handed him the can. He didn't spray. He wasn't treating. He was testing. He held the can up and watched the creature's reaction to the aerosol cloud. It recoiled. Slightly. Not much. But it recoiled.

"Chemical sensitivity," he muttered. "Weak. Mild. Not a vulnerability. But not nothing."

The creature advanced again. Ten feet. Five feet. Dale backed up. The guardrail was behind him. He was out of lateral space.

Then a sound. Engine. Fast. Coming from the north.

A truck. Black. Unmarked. It skidded to a stop thirty yards away. The door opened. A woman got out. Tall. Short hair. Grey work jacket with a Franklin County patch on the shoulder. She had a tool on her hip that Dale didn't recognize. Something like a crowbar but with a glowing tip.

"Step away from the specimen," she said.

Dale stepped away.

The woman walked toward the creature. She didn't look at it. She looked at her wrist. At something Dale couldn't see. Probably her own Field Guide.

"Unregistered," she said. "No entry. No weakness. No protocol match." She looked up. "You got an ID?"

Dale nodded. "Partial. Morphological. No weakness flagged."

"Send it."

Dale hesitated.

The Authority's notification appeared in his peripheral vision.

*WARNING: You are operating under suspension. Sharing Field Guide data with another technician constitutes Unauthorized Knowledge Transfer, Violation Type 7. Penalty: additional thirty-day suspension. Second offense: permanent revocation.*

He looked at the creature. It was ten feet from Voss. The vibration was making her eyes water. She was squinting hard, trying to keep them open.

He sent the data.

The woman's Field Guide updated. Her eyes flicked down. Read. Looked back at the creature.

"Three vertical slits," she said. "Respiratory. You said chemical sensitivity?"

"Mild."

"Then the slits are the weak point. Not the plates. The slits." She raised the crowbar. The glowing tip pulsed. "Cooperative Identification confirmed. Targeted damage bonus active."

She swung. The crowbar hit the creature's face. The tip went into one of the slits. The thing screamed. Not a vibration. A sound. A real sound. High and wet. The ceramic plates cracked. The legs went rigid. The thing collapsed.

The woman pulled the crowbar out. Looked at it. Wiped it on her jacket.

"Class three," she said. "Clean kill. Four hundred points."

Dale's notification appeared.

*Treatment Points awarded: 85. Partial credit for Cooperative Identification contribution.*

Eighty-five points. Not much. But it was something. And it was a violation. He'd committed a violation and gotten points for it. That was new.

The woman walked over. Looked at Dale. Looked at his jacket. No county patch at all.

"You're Kowalski."

"Yeah."

"You're suspended."

"Yeah."

She looked at him for a long moment. Then she pulled out a card. White. Similar to the one the Inspector had dropped. She held it up.

"Marchetti. Class IV. Franklin County. I was reassigned to your service area six hours ago."

"Reassigned."

"You're not covering your territory anymore. I am." She put the card away. "Which means that creature is my kill. Those four hundred points are mine. The eighty-five you got are a courtesy."

"Understood."

Marchetti looked at the truck. Looked at Voss. Looked back at Dale.

"You're heading to the Authority office."

"Yeah."

"For the hearing."

"Yeah."

Marchetti nodded. She walked back to her truck. Opened the door. Didn't get in. She leaned on the door frame and looked at Dale.

"I'm going to tell you something. I want you to hear it before you walk into that hearing."

Dale waited.

"The Authority didn't just suspend you. They restructured your service area. While you were suspended, they ran a coverage audit. You know what they found?"

Dale shook his head.

"Fourteen untreated Class-2 infestations in your territory. Two Class-3. One Class-4 that's been active for nine days and hasn't been flagged because your Field Guide was the only one with entries for that species."

Something cold moved through Dale's chest.

"The Authority knew," Marchetti said. "They knew the coverage gap existed before they suspended you. They ran the audit anyway."

"Why?"

"Because they needed a reason to pull you out of your territory." Marchetti's voice was flat. "The NEXUS. You sealed it. Good. But the Authority doesn't want it sealed."

Dale stared at her.

"They want it open. They've been monitoring the seal. They know it's holding. They also know it won't hold seventy-two hours. They think it'll last about forty."

"Then why not send a Class VII technician?"

Marchetti looked at him. "Because they don't have one. They don't have anyone who can perform a Class VII structural protocol. Not in this region. Not in this state."

"Then what's the plan?"

"There is no plan. That's the point." She got into her truck. "The Authority's protocol for an uncontainable breach is evacuation and containment perimeter. They're going to let the NEXUS open. They're going to let the infestation spread. And then they're going to classify the entire region as a permanent infestation zone."

Dale's hands went cold.

"That's not extermination," he said.

"No," Marchetti said. "It's quarantine."

She started the engine.

"Good luck at the hearing, Kowalski."

She drove south.

Dale stood on the road. The creature's body was cooling on the asphalt. The ceramic plates were cracking further in the cold air. Voss walked up beside him.

"Forty hours," she said.

"Yeah."

"That's not enough time to find a Class VII technician."

"No."

"Then what do we do?"

Dale looked at the card on the ground where the Inspector had dropped it. Then he looked north. The Authority office was in the next county. Four hours away. Maybe five.

He looked at his Field Guide. The new entry. The unregistered species. No weakness flagged. He'd identified it. He'd contributed. But he hadn't treated it. He hadn't eliminated it. The protocol was incomplete.

Identify. Document. Isolate. Eliminate. Verify.

Two and a half out of five.

He pulled out his phone. Opened the Authority's notification system. Scrolled through the violation types.

Type 1: Unlicensed Infestation. Type 2: Incomplete Protocol. Type 3: Cross-Contamination. Type 4: Unauthorized Treatment. Type 5: Neglected Infestation. Type 6: Failure to Report. Type 7: Unauthorized Knowledge Transfer.

He scrolled further.
