Chapter 399: Stamping the Inspector

# Chapter 399

The stamp hovered three inches from the vial.

Dale couldn't breathe. The air in the room tasted like copper and old dust, thick with the particulate matter that had already begun to calcify in his lungs. His neck was a rusted hinge; he could not turn to look at the door, though he could hear the heavy *click* of the lock engaging. It was a mechanical sound, final and absolute, the kind of noise a building makes when it decides you are no longer a resident but a tenant in debt.

He focused on the vial, and the powder inside was still swirling. It had formed the shape of his face again, a miniature mask of gray grit suspended in glass. *Kowalski, Dale (Post-Mortem).* The system had already processed the classification. It had already categorized him as a Class I Civilian, Threat Level Zero.

In the Authority's eyes, Dale was no longer a technician. He was the pest.

The stamp lowered. It was not made of wood or rubber. It was made of bone, carved with sigils that predated the invention of the wheel. It smelled of formaldehyde and bureaucratic malice.

Dale's right hand trembled. He couldn't lift his arm fully, the muscles locked by the contamination spreading through his spine, but his fingers could still twitch.

He had a clipboard in his left hand, which was the standard issue slate-gray polymer, chipped at the corners from fourteen years of fieldwork. He had a pen. It was a ballpoint, blue ink, running low.

He had to file a complaint against his own existence.

The stamp touched the glass.

It didn't crush it. It pressed against it, a gentle, terrible pressure.

A red ink pad, invisible to the naked eye, was being transferred onto the surface of the vial.

If it completed the impression, Dale's license would be revoked. His status would be reclassified. He would be subject to immediate extermination protocols.

He had thirty seconds. Maybe twenty. The stiffness in his neck was spreading to his shoulders.

Dale looked at the clipboard. The top line was blank. The header required a date, a location, and a complaint code.

He didn't have a date. Time had stopped mattering when the walls closed in.

He didn't have a location. This room existed in a liminal space, a pocket dimension created by the Authority's processing queue. It wasn't on any map.

He didn't have a complaint code. Section 4, Paragraph B of the Licensing Authority Handbook covered

"Unlicensed Infestations." It did not cover "Licensed Technician Becoming Infestation."

The stamp pressed harder. The red ink bleeded through the glass, a crimson stain spreading over his own miniature face.

Dale's mind raced, not with fear, but with procedure. Fear was inefficient.

Fear wasted oxygen.

Procedure saved lives.

Or in this case, preserved the possibility of them.

If he was the infestation, then the Authority was the exterminator.

And exterminators have to follow protocol.

The Authority's rules were explicit. Treatment Points are awarded only for completing full extermination protocols: identify, document, isolate, eliminate, and verify. Killing a monster without documentation yields zero points.

But there was a loophole. A tiny, bureaucratic crack in the foundation of the law that Dale had found twenty chapters ago and had kept in his back pocket like a spare key.

*Cross-contamination penalty: deploying a protocol designed for one creature type against another inflicts a 40% debuff and contaminates your Field Guide entry for 24 hours.*

The Authority wanted to stamp him. To mark him as *Kowalski, Dale*.

But the Authority did not have his Field Guide entry for *Kowalski, Dale*. They had generated it from the sample. It was a new entry. An unverified entry.

If Dale could force the Authority to process him under the wrong protocol, he could trigger the penalty.

He couldn't fight the stamp. He couldn't run.

But he could file the paperwork.

Dale's pen hovered over the clipboard. His handwriting was shaky, the letters slurring together as his motor functions degraded.

**DATE:** *Current.*

**LOCATION:** *Authority Processing Queue, Sub-level 4.*

**COMPLAINT:** *Improper Identification. Protocol Violation.*

The stamp paused. It hovered, waiting. The system recognized the input. A new notification flickered in his peripheral vision, bright and urgent.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** *Input Received: Self-Referral Complaint.* *Status: Under Review.* *Note: Claim requires evidence of misclassification.*

The stamp lifted an inch. The red ink smeared, but the impression was not complete. The system was reading the complaint.

Dale had to provide the evidence, and he looked at the vial. The powder was settling. The face was fading, replaced by the swirling gray dust.

He looked at his own hands. They were turning gray. The skin was hardening, cracking like dry earth.

He was becoming the thing they were trying to identify.

But identification requires a subject. And a subject requires a clear distinction between the hunter and the hunted.

Dale wrote the next line.

**CLAIMED SPECIES:** *Rattus profundus (Corrupted).*

The system paused. A long, agonizing second of silence.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** *Conflict Detected.* *Sample Source: Kowalski, Dale.* *Claimed Species: Rattus profundus.* *Analysis: Genetic divergence exceeds 99%. Misclassification likely.* *Action: Requesting Second Opinion.*

The stamp lowered again. This time, it didn't press against the vial. It moved to the clipboard.

It hit the paper with a sound like a gunshot.

**COMPLAINT LOGGED.** **REF: 894-DELTA-KOWALSKI.**

The walls of the room shuddered. The gray powder on the floor was sucked into a vent in the corner. The air grew clearer, though still metallic.

Dale slumped against the wall. His legs gave out, but he caught himself on the edge of the desk. He could turn his head now. The stiffness receded, replaced by a throbbing pain that radiated from his spine.

He looked at the door. The lock had disengaged. The handle turned.

A man walked in, and he was wearing the gray suit of an Authority Inspector. His face was obscured by a mask, a featureless white porcelain thing with no eyes. He held a tablet.

"Mr. Kowalski," the Inspector said. His voice was synthesized, flat and devoid of inflection.

"Your complaint has been accepted. Preliminary review indicates a procedural error in the initial classification."

Dale spat on the floor. The spit was gray.

"I told you," Dale rasped. His voice sounded like grinding stones. "The data was poisoned. You can't trust the sample."

The Inspector tilted his head.

"The Authority does not make errors. The Authority processes data. If the data is inconsistent, the processing is suspended until a verified source is provided."

"I am the verified source," Dale said.

"You are the suspect," the Inspector corrected.

The Inspector stepped forward. He didn't look at Dale's hands. He looked at the vial.

"The sample must be disposed of. As the suspect, you are ordered to surrender the physical evidence to the Authority for destruction."

Dale clutched the vial to his chest.

If they destroyed the sample, they destroyed the only proof that he was *not* a monster. They would reclassify him. They would stamp him.

They would burn him.

"If I surrender the sample," Dale said, "I lose my defense."

"If you retain the sample," the Inspector said, "you are in possession of hazardous biological material. This is a Class 3 violation."

The Inspector held out a hand.

"Surrender the vial, Dale. Or be removed."

Dale looked at the vial. The powder was still there. It was still his face. It was still his life.

He looked at the Inspector. The porcelain mask reflected the dim light of the room.

Dale made a decision, and he uncapped the vial.

The Inspector stiffened.

"Do not—"

Dale poured the gray powder onto the Inspector's outstretched hand.

It landed on the white glove with a soft *puff*.

The Inspector flinched, pulling his hand back. The powder coated the glove, turning it a sickly gray.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** *Contamination Detected.* *Subject: Authority Inspector (Unit 74).* *Status: Cross-Contamination.*

The Inspector stared at his hand. The powder was reacting to the Authority's protective warding. It was eating the fabric of the glove.

"You have deployed a protocol against a non-hostile entity," the Inspector said. His voice was no longer synthesized.

It was human, which was angry.

"This is a violation of Section 9."

"Section 9," Dale said. "Cross-contamination penalty."

The Inspector's hand was smoking. The glove was dissolving, revealing skin underneath. The skin was turning gray.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** *40% Debuff Applied to Unit 74.* *Field Guide Entry: Authority Inspector (Unit 74) is now contaminated.* *Duration: 24 Hours.*

The Inspector stumbled back. He looked at Dale with wide, terrified eyes.

"What did you do?" the Inspector whispered.

"I filed a complaint," Dale said. "And I provided evidence."

The Inspector's knee buckled. He fell to the floor.

The gray powder was spreading up his arm, tracing the veins.

The system notification flashed above the Inspector's head: **STATUS: INFESTED.

THREAT LEVEL: RISING.**

The door behind the Inspector opened. Two more Inspectors entered. They saw their colleague on the floor, their hand turning to stone.

They didn't attack Dale. They raised their weapons, not at him, but at their own colleague.

"Containment protocol initiated," one of them said.

Dale stood up. His legs shook, but they held.

He walked past the falling Inspector. He walked past the two guards, who were busy trying to sedate their own teammate with a stun baton.

The main door of the room was open.

Beyond it lay the hallway. The hallway of the Authority's headquarters.

Dale stepped into the corridor, and the air here was sterile. White walls. Fluorescent lights. The smell of bleach and ozone.

At the far end of the hall stood a woman.

She was wearing a clipboard. She was wearing a lanyard. She was looking at him with an expression of profound disappointment.

It was Margaret.

His former supervisor. The woman who had fired him ten years ago for

"excessive caution." The woman who had said he didn't understand the nature of the job.

Margaret was dead. She had died in the first wave of the Rift, in the opening chapter. He had found her body in the crawlspace with the broodmother.

But here she was.

Alive.

Walking toward him.

Her eyes were black pits. Her skin was covered in the same gray powder that coated Dale's hands.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** *New Entry Detected.* *Species: Supervisor, Margaret (Reanimated).* *Threat Level: Class IV.* *Weakness: None.*

Dale stopped walking.

Margaret stopped walking, and she held up her clipboard. Her pen was poised over a line.

"Your performance review is due, Dale," she said. Her voice was smooth, professional, and wrong. "And you're late."

Dale looked at the vial in his hand. He looked at Margaret. He looked at the two Inspectors, who had stopped attacking their colleague and were now pointing their weapons at Dale.

He had triggered a cross-contamination penalty. He had debuffed an Authority Inspector.

But he had also opened a door he wasn't sure he could close.

Margaret tapped her pen against the clipboard.

"Write," she said.

The stamp returned. It was larger now. It hovered in the air above Margaret's head.

And it was aimed at Dale.
