Chapter 229: Self Certification Violation

# Chapter 229

Dale's clipboard clicked.

The sound echoed inside his own skull, louder than the fissure widening beneath his feet. He didn't look down at the hand.

That hand—*his* hand, pale and scarred and wearing the same work glove he'd put on at 0600—was merely an appendage.

A biological anomaly. A breach in the structural integrity of his current location.

The Authority notification pulsed in his left eye, overlaying the cracked asphalt with glowing blue text.

**TICKET #89402-B: SELF-CERTIFICATION VIOLATION.** **SUBJECT: DALE KOWALSKI.** **STATUS: UNLICENSED INFESTATION (CLASS V).** **PROTOCOL REQUIRED: SELF-CONTAINMENT OR TERMINATION.**

A Class V.

That was higher than anything he'd cleared in the last three months. Class V usually meant things that ate reality, not guys in khaki pants.

But the system didn't care about biology.

It cared about taxonomy. If it had six legs, it was an insect. If it had tentacles, it was a mollusk.

If it was Dale, it was a parasite.

He flipped to a fresh page.

The paper was blank, pristine.

His handwriting was steady. That was the first rule of the job.

If your hands shake, you contaminate the scene. If the scene is contaminated, you can't bill the Authority.

"Kowalski," a voice called out. Not from the fissure. From behind him.

Dale didn't turn. He uncapped his pen. The ink was black. Permanent.

"Licensing Authority Inspector Halloway is listed as *Missing, Presumed Terminated*," the voice said. "But the ticket says *You* are the infestation. Explain."

This was a new mechanic. He hadn't seen it before. The Authority wasn't just issuing tickets; it was cross-referencing personnel records. Halloway was gone, but his shadow was still active in the system, or rather, the system was using Halloway's absence to validate Dale's presence.

Dale wrote: *Observation Log, 02:14 AM. Location: Sector 4, Suburban Ohio.

Subject: Self.

Action: Standing still. Threat Level: High due to proximity.*

He paused.

Proximity. That was the key.

The fissure wasn't just under his feet.

It was *of* him. The dimensional breach hadn't just opened nearby; it had latched onto his specific magical signature. His thoroughness had drawn the Rift's attention like a flashlight beam in a dark room.

"Explain, Dale," the voice insisted.

A figure stepped into the perimeter of the streetlight. It was a young man, maybe twenty-five, wearing a crisp suit that cost more than Dale's truck. He held a tablet, not a clipboard. The tablet glowed with red data streams.

"Who are you?" Dale asked, not looking up from his notes.

"Junior Auditor Vance," the man said. "I'm here to audit your Thoroughness Rating.

You've been pushing it dangerously high.

The Authority likes thoroughness, Kowalski, but they don't like ego.

And this?" He gestured at the hand in the ground. "This is a containment failure.

You let your own identity become a vector for infection."

Dale kept writing. *Vector: Identity. Cause: Over-specialization. Effect: Self-infestation.*

"I didn't let it happen," Dale said flatly. "It happened. I'm documenting it."

"You're stalling," Vance said. "The protocol for a Class V Self-Infestation is immediate termination. You pull the trigger, you wipe the slate. You get a clean start, maybe a demotion to Class III, but you survive."

Vance raised a small, silver device. It looked like a staple gun, but the barrel was lined with runes that hummed with low-frequency violence.

A Discharge Stapler.

High-grade. Designed to pin metaphysical entities to their anchor points before neutralizing them.

Dale looked at the device.

Then he looked at his own hand in the fissure. The hand was twitching.

Fingers spreading.

It wasn't trying to grab him. It was trying to *write*.

A new notification flashed: **FIELD GUIDE UPDATE: PARASITIC ECHO (Homo sapiens, corrupted). Weakness: Cognitive Dissonance.**

Dale blinked. Cognitive Dissonance.

The system was offering a targeted damage bonus. But how did you inflict dissonance on yourself?

"Put the stapler down," Dale said.

"Or what?" Vance smiled. It was a thin, predatory smile.

"You'll file a complaint?

I'm the Authority, Dale.

I *am* the code violation."

Vance stepped closer. The ground beneath him didn't crack. He was standing on stable reality. Dale was standing on the edge of it.

If Dale moved, he might fall. If he stayed, he might be pinned.

He looked at the Field Guide entry in his mind. *Parasitic Echo.* It was a copy of him. A reflection. And reflections didn't have agency. They reacted.

Dale stopped writing. He capped the pen.

"I need a witness," Dale said.

Vance lowered the stapler slightly, confused. "I'm right here."

"No," Dale said. "I need a *witness* to the violation. For the report.

If you terminate me now, the paperwork says I resisted. That's a Class I felony.

But if I *voluntarily* submit to the protocol while documenting the hazard, it's a successful containment operation.

Thoroughness Bonus: +50%."

Vance's eyes narrowed. "You're trying to game the system."

"I'm optimizing it," Dale corrected.

"You want points. The Authority wants points.

I want points.

If you shoot me, I die. You get zero points for my death because you didn't follow the documentation protocol. You skipped the isolation step.

You skipped the verification step. Your Thoroughness Rating drops."

Vance hesitated. The tablet in his hand flickered. Red numbers turned to yellow. Then orange.

"That's... not how the math works," Vance said, but his voice wavered.

"It is now," Dale said. "New patch update. Section 4, Paragraph B: *Killing a Class V entity without a completed isolation log results in a -200 point penalty to the auditor.*"

Dale was lying.

He knew he was lying.

The Authority didn't issue patches in real-time. But the System *did* reward specificity.

If he stated a rule with enough conviction, and if the entity believed it, the System might interpret that belief as a temporary local law.

Vance looked at his tablet. He scrolled. He frowned.

"There's no such paragraph," Vance said.

"Check the fine print," Dale said. "It's always in the fine print. Like the terms of service for a software update."

Vance glanced down at the fissure again. The hand was still there. It had picked up a piece of chalk from the pavement. It was drawing a circle around itself.

Dale watched. This was new. The echo was trying to seal its own boundary. It was trying to follow protocol.

If the echo followed protocol, and Dale followed protocol, they were no longer two enemies. They were two technicians working the same site.

And two technicians can't work the same site. That was the rule. *Cross-contamination penalty.*

If Dale stepped back, he broke the isolation. If Vance stepped forward, he broke the isolation.

But if they *both* stepped back, the fissure would collapse under its own weight. Or it wouldn't. The outcome was unknown.

Vance took a step back.

Dale stayed put.

The hand in the ground stopped drawing. It looked up. For the first time, the face in the shadow was visible. It was Dale's face, but the eyes were black voids filled with static.

"Sign it," the echo whispered.

Dale looked at Vance. "You have to choose," Dale said. "Do you want a dead pest control tech with a zero-point audit, or a live tech with a completed form?"

Vance looked at the stapler. Then at Dale. Then at the ticket.

The ticket was changing. The status line updated: **AWAITING WITNESS SIGNATURE.**

Vance tapped the tablet. A signature prompt appeared.

"If I sign this, I acknowledge the violation occurred *after* I arrived," Vance said slowly. "That means I didn't fail to prevent it. I processed it."

"Precisely," Dale said.

"But if I sign it, you have to sign it too. You have to acknowledge you're the infestation."

"I am the infestation," Dale said. "I've always been the infestation. I just didn't have the paperwork."

He opened his notebook to a fresh page. He drew a single box. He wrote *Witness: Auditor Vance* inside it. Then he drew another box. *Subject: Dale Kowalski.*

He held the notebook out.

Vance approached. The air grew cold. The birds stopped singing again. The streetlights flickered, buzzing with the sound of dying filaments.

Vance reached out. His hand trembled. He touched the paper.

The System pinged.

**CONFLICT DETECTED: DUAL SIGNATURES ON SINGLE ENTITY.**

**RESOLUTION PROTOCOL INITIATED.**

The ground shook. Not a tremor.

A shudder.

The fissure widened. More hands emerged. Not Dale's hands.

Other hands.

Dozens of them. Hands from previous jobs.

Hands from Halloway.

Hands from victims he'd failed to save.

They were all reaching for Vance.

Vance screamed. He pulled his hand back, but the tablet was stuck to the page. The ink was pulling him in.

"Help!" Vance yelled.

Dale didn't move. He watched the ink absorb the auditor's fingers.

The System notification flashed one last time.

**VERIFICATION COMPLETE.** **THOROUGHNESS SCORE: 10/10.** **PENALTY: LOSS OF WITNESS.** **BONUS: IMMEDIATE CERTIFICATION UPGRADE TO CLASS VI.**

Dale felt the surge of power hit him. It wasn't a warm glow.

It was a cold, hard knot in his stomach.

He felt heavier.

His bones felt like lead. He felt like he was shrinking, condensing, becoming denser.

The hands in the fissure pulled Vance down. The auditor vanished into the asphalt, screaming silently until the ground sealed over him with a wet, sucking sound.

Silence returned.

Dale stood alone.

He looked at his notebook. The page was blank again. The ink was gone.

But the ticket in his vision remained.

**NEW TICKET ISSUED.** **SUBJECT: THE RIFT ITSELF.** **TYPE: STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY FAILURE (PLANETARY).** **RECOMMENDED ACTION: EXTERMINATE SOURCE.**

Dale closed the notebook. He looked at his hands. They were shaking.

Not from fear. From exhaustion.

He turned to walk away.

The ground beneath his left foot crumbled.

He looked down. The asphalt was gone. Below it was not dirt. Not rock.

It was a wall of eyes. Thousands of them. Blinking in unison. Watching him.

And in the center of the wall, a giant clipboard. Made of bone.

And on the clipboard, a single checkbox.

*Tick.*

The System pinged.

**NEW MECHANIC UNLOCKED: PARENTAL LEAVE.** **ERROR: PARENT NOT FOUND.** **INITIATING SEARCH FOR PROGENITOR.**

Dale froze. Progenitor?

He wasn't a monster. He was a human.

Unless the System thought he was the *mother* of the things in the ground.

The eyes in the wall shifted. They focused on his gut. His lower abdomen.

A pain, sharp and sudden, hit him. Like a cramp. Like something moving inside him.

Dale looked down at his own stomach. His work shirt was tight.

He pulled the fabric away.

Under his shirt, his skin was translucent. And beneath the skin, curled in a fetal position, was a small, chitinous creature.

It had his face.

Dale vomited.

The creature didn't come out. It settled deeper.

A new notification appeared, red and pulsing.

**PREGNANCY STATUS: 4% GESTATION.** **ESTIMATED BIRTH: 3 DAYS.** **RECOMMENDED ACTION: UTERINE EXCISION OR TOTAL TERMINATION.**

Dale stared at the notification.

He couldn't kill himself. He needed the certification. He needed the points.

But he couldn't let it grow.

He looked at the bone clipboard in the ground. The checkmark was still glowing.

He had to decide. Cut it out, or become the hive.

He reached for his pen.

But his fingers were numb.

The creature inside him whispered, in a voice that sounded like his own.

*You're already the mother, Dale.*

The fissure closed. But the wall of eyes remained.

And from the deep darkness of the Rift, something else climbed out.

Not a hand.

A leg.

Long, spindly, and wearing a pair of familiar, worn-out work boots.

Dale recognized the boots. He had lost them two chapters ago.

He looked at his own feet. He was barefoot.

The creature wearing his boots stepped out of the Rift.

It stood up. It was tall. It was Dale.

But it was taller.

And its face was missing.

It looked at the real Dale.

It smiled.

"Hello, Dale," it said. "You've been neglecting your personal maintenance."
