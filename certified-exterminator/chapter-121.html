Chapter 121: Late Client Complaint

# Chapter 121

The rain hammered against the corrugated metal roof of the abandoned textile factory, a rhythmic drumming that did little to mask the sound of Dale's breathing. It was ragged, shallow. His lungs burned with the cold air of the industrial district, and the keycard in his pocket felt heavier than it had any right to be.

He wasn't running anymore. He couldn't.

Instead, he stood in the shadow of a rusted conveyor belt, watching the perimeter. The retrieval team's vehicles were still parked outside, their headlights cutting through the downpour like surgical lamps. They weren't looking for him. They were waiting for the Authority to process the glitch, to stabilize the Inspector, to decide whether Dale was a fugitive or a failed experiment.

Dale checked his watch. Three minutes. That was all the time the system's recalibration window gave him before the facility's internal locks reset and sealed the exits permanently.

A figure emerged from the darkness near the loading dock. Not a tactical soldier. Not a monster.

A woman in a high-visibility vest, holding a clipboard. Her boots crunched over broken glass and wet gravel. She moved with the same flat, procedural gait Dale recognized from his own years on the job.

She stopped ten feet away. Rain slicked her hair against her skull. She didn't look like a threat. She looked like a middle manager.

"You're out of uniform," she said. Her voice was dry, unaffected by the storm. "And your badge is invalid."

Dale didn't move. His hand hovered near his belt, where his spray nozzle used to be. Now it was empty.

"I have the keycard," Dale said. His voice came out rougher than he intended. He cleared his throat. "Admin access."

The woman tilted her head. She looked at the card in his hand, then back at his face. A small, sad smile touched her lips.

"Access level isn't permission, Mr. Kowalski. It's liability."

She stepped closer. Dale took a half-step back, his boots slipping on the wet concrete.

"Who are you?" he asked.

"Supervisor Halloway. Regional Quality Assurance." She tapped her pen against the clipboard.

"You triggered a Critical Error in Sector 4.

That's a Class-A violation. Do you know what the penalty is for a Class-A violation by an unlicensed operator?"

Dale shook his head. He didn't know. He didn't care. He needed to get out of here.

"I need to leave," Dale said. "The Inspector is unstable. He'll be back online in two minutes. If I'm here when he is, I'm dead."

Halloway looked at her watch. She looked at the sky. She looked at Dale.

"You're assuming the Inspector is the problem," she said. "He's a tool. A very expensive, very angry tool. You broke him because you used a protocol he wasn't designed to withstand."

"I used light," Dale said. "It's basic optics."

"You used a photonic disruption against a digital entity.

That's not optics, Dale. That's cross-contamination.

You hit him with a Class-1 deterrent on a Class-9 construct.

The debuff you felt? That was the system trying to protect its own integrity from your sloppy methodology."

Dale's jaw tightened. He hated that word. *Sloppy.*

"It worked," Dale said.

"It worked for three seconds," Halloway corrected. "And now you're void.

Your Field Guide is corrupted.

Your protocols are locked.

You are, for all intents and purposes, a civilian holding a piece of plastic that shouldn't exist."

She reached into her vest and pulled out a folded sheet of paper. She handed it to him.

Dale took it. His fingers were numb. He unfolded it.

It was a citation.

**VIOLATION: UNLICENSED OPERATOR USE OF HIGH-LEVEL PROTOCOL.** **PENALTY: IMMEDIATE REVOCATION OF ALL CERTIFICATIONS.** **APPEAL WINDOW: 0.00 SECONDS.**

"This isn't a warning," Dale said.

"It's a termination notice," Halloway said. "But not for you. For the Authority's relationship with you."

She pointed a pen at the dark interior of the factory.

"There's a breach in there. Behind the containment walls. A Rift leak.

You didn't stop it, Dale. You just poked it."

Dale looked at the citation. He looked at the factory.

"The source," he said. "You said recover the source."

"I said that in the objective log.

I didn't say you could do it. You're not licensed to handle Class-9 Rift debris. You're licensed to handle rats.

And even that license is gone."

Halloway gestured to the side door. The one Dale had used to escape. It was shut now. A red light pulsed above it.

"The door is sealed. Biometric lock. Requires a Level V clearance."

Dale looked at the keycard. It still glowed faintly green.

"I have Admin," Dale said.

"You have a glitched token," Halloway said. "The system thinks you're an error. Errors get deleted. Not processed."

She leaned in. Her eyes were tired. Dark circles bruised the skin beneath them.

"Listen to me, Dale. The Authority is bureaucracy.

It's rules.

And rules have loopholes. But they also have consequences. You want to survive?

You don't run. You don't fight.

You document."

Dale frowned. "What?"

"Write it down," Halloway said.

She tapped the clipboard. "Start a new inspection report.

Right here. Right now."

"That's insane," Dale said. "I have no pen."

"You have a pen." Halloway pointed to his pocket. "And you have a memory. The Authority rewards thoroughness. Even from the dead."

Dale stared at her. The rain soaked through his jacket, chilling him to the bone.

"If I write a report," Dale said slowly, "does it count?"

"It counts as an attempt," Halloway said. "If you document the breach, the breach becomes a known variable. Known variables can be treated. Unknown variables are just chaos."

Dale pulled out his pen. It was cheap, plastic, from a hardware store three towns over. He held it like a weapon.

"What do I write?"

"Time of incident," Halloway said. "Location.

Observed entities.

Structural damage. And the weakness."

"I don't know the weakness," Dale said.

"You do," Halloway said.

"You just haven't written it down. You saw the Inspector flicker.

You saw the pixels scatter.

Why did that happen?"

"Light," Dale said.

"No," Halloway said. "Reflection.

You shined the light on the keycard, not the Inspector.

The keycard is a mirror for Authority data.

The Inspector *is* data. You reflected his own code back at him.

He couldn't parse it."

Dale's hand stopped shaking.

"I see," Dale said.

"Then write it down," Halloway commanded. "Field Guide Entry: *Inspector Construct*. Weakness: Self-Reflection via Data-Transfer Surfaces."

Dale scribbled the words on the back of the citation. The ink smeared in the rain.

**ENTRY: INSPECTOR CONSTRUCT.** **WEAKNESS: REFLECTIVE DATA FEEDBACK.**

As soon as the last period landed on the paper, a chime sounded in Dale's ear. Not a notification. A sound. A clear, bell-like tone.

**FIELD GUIDE UPDATED.** **NEW ENTRY ADDED.** **TARGETED DAMAGE BONUS AVAILABLE: +15% AGAINST DIGITAL ENTITIES.**

Dale looked up. Halloway was smiling again. This time, it was sharp.

"You're still unlicensed," she said. "But you're documented. And documented things can be fixed."

The red light above the side door flickered. It turned amber.

Dale looked at the door.

"It's not open," Halloway said. "But it's not locked.

For now.

The system is confused. It has a report from you. It has an error from the Inspector.

It doesn't know if you're a threat or a technician.

It's waiting for the next step."

"What's the next step?"

"Verify the elimination," Halloway said.

"Go in there. Find the breach. Kill the thing causing the flicker.

And if you do it right, if you write it down, you might just get your badge back."

Dale looked at the amber light. He looked at the rain. He looked at Halloway.

"Why help me?"

Halloway didn't answer immediately. She looked past Dale, toward the main entrance of the factory. Headlights were moving down the road. More vehicles.

"Because you're the only one who reads the manual," she said. "Go. Before the audit comes."

Dale turned to the door. He placed his hand on the handle. The metal was cold, slick with rain.

He pushed.

The door groaned. It didn't open.

"Amber means pending," Halloway said from behind him. "You have to knock."

Dale knocked. Three times. Firm. Procedural.

The door clicked. It swung inward.

Darkness spilled out. Not just visual darkness. A pressure. A static hum that vibrated in Dale's teeth.

He stepped inside. The door closed behind him.

The factory floor was vast and empty. Crates were stacked in rows, like tombstones. In the center of the room, suspended from the ceiling by thick, pulsating cables, was a sphere of black glass.

It was the size of a car.

It wasn't flickering. It was singing. A low, subsonic thrum that made Dale's vision blur.

Around the sphere, shadows moved.

Not people.

Not monsters. They were elongated, distorted shapes.

Glitches in the air.

Dale pulled out his penlight. He didn't turn it on. He held it up, watching the sphere.

The sphere pulsed. A ripple of light moved across its surface.

Dale looked at his citation. He looked at the penlight.

He understood.

The sphere wasn't just a rift. It was a server. A physical manifestation of the Authority's database leak. And the shadows were the corrupted data trying to escape.

He had a +15% bonus. He had a reflection weakness.

But he had no weapon.

No spray. No trap.

Just a pen. And a clipboard.

Dale took a step forward. The shadows hissed. They turned toward him. Their faces were blank, white screens.

Dale didn't retreat. He raised the penlight. He aimed it not at the sphere, but at the floor beneath it.

The concrete was polished. Wet. Reflective.

He clicked the penlight on.

The beam hit the wet concrete. It bounced. It struck the black sphere.

The sphere shrieked. A sound like dial-up internet amplified through a stadium speaker.

The shadows froze.

**SYSTEM ALERT: DATA REFLECTION DETECTED.** **CORRUPTION RATE: 12%.** **RECOMMENDED ACTION: PURGE.**

Dale smiled. It was a thin, tight smile.

"Protocol," he muttered. "Identify. Isolate. Eliminate."

He moved to the left. The shadows followed. They were drawn to the light, to the feedback loop.

Dale kept moving.

He herded them. Not with force.

With optics.

He used the light to create a path of reflection that led away from the sphere and toward a large drainage grate in the floor.

The shadows gathered at the grate. They spilled over the edge, trying to escape the light.

Dale reached into his pocket. He pulled out a roll of duct tape.

He hadn't thrown it away.

He always kept a roll.

It was for sealing entry points.

He tore a piece off. He slapped it over the drainage grate.

Not to stop them. To trap them.

The shadows piled up against the tape. They screamed in digital static. Their forms degraded, pixelating, breaking apart.

Dale watched. He didn't feel triumph. He felt exhaustion.

He pulled out his phone. He opened the Field Guide app. It was grayed out. *License Void.*

But the entry he had written was still there.

**ENTRY: INSPECTOR CONSTRUCT (Variant: Rift Leak).** **WEAKNESS: REFLECTIVE DATA FEEDBACK.** **STATUS: NEUTRALIZED.**

A new message flashed on his screen.

**REPORT SUBMITTED.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 4.2.** **NOTE: SLOPPY. BUT EFFECTIVE.** **POINTS AWARDED: 5.**

Five points. It wasn't enough to buy back his license. It wasn't even enough to buy a new can of spray.

But the sphere's humming stopped. The static faded. The shadows dissolved into dust.

The lights in the factory flickered on. Bright, harsh fluorescent tubes.

Dale stood alone in the center of the room.

The door behind him opened. Halloway walked in. She looked at the grate.

She looked at the tape. She looked at Dale.

She didn't smile this time.

"You used duct tape on a Class-9 dimensional breach," she said.

"It held," Dale said.

"It's going to fail," Halloway said.

"The pressure is building on the other side. You didn't fix it, Dale. You just plugged it.

And now, whoever is on the other side is going to break that tape.

And when they do..."

The floor beneath Dale's feet shook.

A crack appeared in the concrete. It spiderwebbed outward from the grate.

Halloway stepped back.

"They're knocking," she said.

Dale looked at the crack. He looked at the duct tape. He looked at his phone.

**WARNING: STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY COMPROMISED.** **NEXT OBJECTIVE: STABILIZE THE CONTAINMENT.** **PENALTY FOR FAILURE: TOTAL SYSTEM WIPE.**

Dale looked at Halloway.

"I need a bigger roll," he said.

Halloway didn't answer. She was already running toward the exit.

The crack widened. Something pushed against the duct tape from below. It bulged. It stretched.

Dale grabbed the penlight. He clicked it on.

He shined it at the bulging tape.

Nothing happened. The reflection didn't work on the source itself. Only on its agents.

Dale looked around the room. He saw the crates. He saw the ceiling cables. He saw the sphere, still hanging, still humming softly.

He had no weapons. No protocols. No license.

But he had a job to do.

Dale walked toward the sphere.

The shadows were gone. But the breach was still open. And something on the other side was looking at him.

He raised the clipboard.

"Inspection beginning," he said to the empty room.

The sphere pulsed. A voice emerged from it. Not digital. Human.

"Dale," the voice said. "Put down the pen."

Dale's heart stopped. He knew that voice.

It was his supervisor's voice. From the old world. Before the Rift.

"Dale," the voice said. "We have a client complaint. And you're late."
