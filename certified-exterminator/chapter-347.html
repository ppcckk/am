Chapter 347: Void Class Violation

# Chapter 347

The spray can hissed.

A fine mist of ozone and caustic foam erupted from the nozzle. It wasn't insecticide. It wasn't acid. It was void.

Dale scrambled backward, his boots sliding on the concrete floor of the crawlspace. The mist expanded, eating the light. Where it touched the dead rat carcass, the fur dissolved into grey slush. Where it touched the wall, the drywall vanished, revealing not studs, but a shifting lattice of black glass.

The grey-suited man stepped forward. His face was a smooth, featureless oval.

No eyes.

No mouth. Just a suit that fit too well and a hand that held the canister with professional steadiness.

"Violation," the man said.

The voice didn't come from a throat. It vibrated in Dale's molars.

"Unauthorized timeline alteration.

Class IV temporal contamination.

"

Dale wiped his face with the back of his glove. The clipboard was still in his left hand. The pen was in his right. The contract—the impossible, signed-by-a-child contract—lay in the dust.

He didn't look at it. He looked at the mist.

"Protocol requires isolation," Dale said. His voice was steady, though his knees were knocking together like castanets.

"You're spreading the agent.

That's a code violation.

K-9."

The man tilted his head.

"You are out of certification level."

"Level I," Dale corrected. "But I know the rules."

"Rules are for those who exist in the present tense."

The mist surged forward. It wasn't moving randomly. It was hunting.

The void-taint sought coherence. It sought Dale's specific bio-signature.

Dale ducked, and he rolled behind the support beam of the duplex. The concrete splintered as the mist hit where he'd been standing a second ago. A hole appeared in the foundation, clean and perfect.

No debris.

Just absence.

This wasn't a monster. This was a cleanup crew. And they were here to delete him.

His Field Guide pulsed in his vision. A red warning.

**WARNING: CROSS-CONTAMINATION RISK. TARGET SPECIES: TEMPORAL ANOMALY. PROTOCOL MISMATCH.**

Using standard extermination tools against a temporal entity resulted in a 40% debuff. His spray was useless. His flashlight was useless. His clipboard was just wood and paper.

But he had one thing left.

Identification.

The man in the grey suit raised the can. The nozzle clicked. Another burst.

Dale didn't run. He ran toward the hole in the wall.

The void-lattice inside pulsed. It looked like the inside of a clock made of obsidian. Gears turned backward. Hands spun in reverse.

*Archivist Variant.*

The Field Guide entry glowed. *Weakness: Paradoxical Identification.*

Dale stared at the mechanism. He forced himself to see it not as magic, but as code. As a structural fault.

The anomaly wasn't just moving through time. It was *editing* it. The contract in the dust was the proof. The signature was the error.

If the Authority punished sloppy work, what did it do to a paradox?

Dale grabbed a shard of the broken drywall. It was jagged, white, and dusty.

He didn't throw it. He jammed it into the center of the obsidian gears.

The mechanism shrieked. A sound like tearing metal.

The grey-suited man stopped. The spray can lowered.

"Contamination," the man said. But the word sounded confused. Distorted.

Dale scrambled back, breathing hard. The mist halted its advance. The void-taint swirled around the jammed gear, unable to consume the physical obstruction.

A glitch.

The System didn't recognize the blockage. It tried to parse the error. It failed.

**FIELD GUIDE UPDATE:** **SPECIES: TEMPORAL ANOMALY (CORRUPTED).** **STATUS: STALLED.** **THREAT LEVEL: UNDETERMINED.**

Dale's heart hammered against his ribs. He had created a buffer. A pause in the execution.

He needed more.

The man in the suit was frozen, his hand hovering over the can.

His body flickered. For a second, he was young. Then old.

Then a child. Then a skeleton.

Dale's eyes snapped to the contract in the dust.

The signature. The girl's signature.

If the anomaly was trying to certify him at Level IX, it was trying to force a state change. It was trying to make him something he wasn't.

But Dale was a pest control technician. He knew about entry points. He knew that if you couldn't seal the hole, you had to change the environment so the pest wouldn't come in.

He looked around the crawlspace. The dead rats. The acid beetles. The trash bag containment from the previous job.

The trash bag, which was still tied shut around the broodmother's carcass. The Authority had marked it as 'Biohazard, Pending Disposal.'

Dale lunged.

The grey-suited man snapped out of his glitch. He swung the spray can like a baseball bat. The canister connected with Dale's shoulder.

Pain exploded in his arm. Numbness spread instantly. The void-taint leaked from the cracked can, touching his skin.

**SYSTEM ALERT: TEMPORAL DEATH IMMINENT. REMAINING LIFESPAN: 3 SECONDS.**

Dale didn't scream. He grabbed the trash bag with his good hand.

He ripped the duct tape seal.

Inside, the broodmother was gone. Only the egg sac remained. A pulsating, chitinous mass of half-formed nightmares.

Dale hurled the bag at the jammed obsidian gears.

The bag hit the black glass. It didn't shatter. It stuck. The adhesive from the tape bonded to the temporal lattice.

The System hesitated again.

**ERROR: BIOLOGICAL CONTAMINATION DETECTED IN TEMPORAL LOOP.** **ATTEMPTING RECALCULATION...**

The grey-suited man froze. His eyes—if he had them—seemed to widen. The smooth surface of his face rippled.

"No," the man whispered. "You're breaking the audit."

"I'm filing a complaint," Dale grit out.

He grabbed the spray can. It was still in the man's hand, but the grip was loose. Dale pried it free.

The can was heavy. Cold.

He didn't spray the man. He sprayed the floor.

The nozzle hissed. The mist hit the concrete.

Nothing happened. The concrete remained.

Dale looked at the can. He looked at the man.

"You're using the wrong propellant," Dale said.

The man stared.

"Temporal anomalies react to causality," Dale said, his voice rising.

"Not entropy.

You're trying to delete the cause. But the cause is the signature.

The contract."

Dale kicked the contract. It slid across the floor, toward the obsidian gears.

The paper fluttered. It touched the black glass.

The gears screamed.

The contract burned. Not with fire, but with *un-writing*. The ink dissolved into smoke. The paper turned into ash, then into memory, then into nothing.

The grey-suited man convulsed. His suit shredded. His form unraveled.

**SYSTEM NOTIFICATION:** **PARADOX RESOLVED.** **SOURCE OF INSTABILITY: INVALID CERTIFICATION RECORD.** **ACTION: PURGE CORRUPTED FILE.**

The man in the suit vanished. Not into mist. Into static.

He was erased.

Silence rushed back into the basement. The hum of the furnace returned. The smell of damp earth and dead rats lingered.

Dale sat on the floor. His shoulder throbbed. The numbness in his arm was fading, replaced by a tingling that felt like pins and needles.

He looked at his hands. They were steady.

He looked at the Field Guide.

**FIELD GUIDE UPDATE:** **NEW ENTRY ADDED.** **SPECIES: AUTHORITY ENFORCER (DELETION UNIT).** **WEAKNESS: BUREAUCRATIC INCONSISTENCY.** **STATUS: DOCUMENTED.**

Dale laughed. It was a dry, hacking sound.

He stood up. His legs shook, but they held.

He walked to the stairs. He needed to leave. He needed to get out of this house before the Authority realized what he had done.

He had used a biological hazard to break a temporal loop. He had weaponized his own incompetence. He had filed a complaint that got a god deleted.

But the cost, and he checked his stats.

**TREATMENT POINTS: 450.** **CERTIFICATION LEVEL: I.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 2.1.**

The rating had plummeted. The Authority didn't like messy work. Even if it worked.

He pushed open the door to the kitchen, and the sunlight was blinding.

The neighbors were still there. Mrs. Gable was on the lawn, holding a broom. The man with the bat was gone.

They looked at him. They saw a man covered in dust, holding a clipboard, with a burn mark on his shoulder.

"Did you get it?" Mrs. Gable asked. Her voice was small.

Dale looked at the house. The rift had closed. The ground was whole.

"No," Dale said. "I'm still working on it."

He walked past her. He walked to his truck.

He climbed into the cab. He locked the doors.

He pulled out his phone.

One missed call. From an unknown number.

He didn't answer it, and he opened the Authority app.

A new message.

**URGENT: LEVEL II EXAMINATION SCHEDULED.** **LOCATION: ABANDONED SUBWAY STATION, SECTOR 4.** **TIME: 18:00 HOURS.** **NOTE: CANDIDATE HAS BEEN FLAGGED FOR IRREGULAR CONDUCT.** **DISCOUNTED POINTS APPLY.**

Dale stared at the screen.

Sector 4. The subway station.

He knew that place. He'd done a rodent inspection there three years ago. He had found a nest in the ventilation shafts. He had treated it.

He had never gone back to verify, and he had been sloppy.

And now the Authority was calling him in for an exam. A practical exam. In the exact location where he had committed his first professional sin.

Dale started the engine, and he didn't go to the station. He went to the hardware store.

He needed supplies. Not spray. Not poison.

He needed tape. Lots of tape. And a label maker.

He had to prove that the infestation was still active. He had to create a new violation. One small enough to ignore, but big enough to trap the next Enforcer.

He drove with one hand, the other resting on the seat beside him.

The phone buzzed again.

This time, the caller ID wasn't unknown, which was his own number.

Dale looked at the screen.

He didn't answer.

He pulled into the parking lot of the hardware store. He got out.

As he walked toward the entrance, a man in a brown uniform stepped out from behind the shelves.

It wasn't a suit. It was a janitor's uniform.

The man held a mop bucket.

"Mr. Kowalski," the janitor said.

Dale stopped. "Who are you?"

"I'm the night shift," the janitor said. He smiled. It was a wide, too-wide smile. "And you're late for your shift."

Dale looked at the mop bucket. The water inside was black. And it was moving.

Ripples.

Not from wind. From something swimming underneath.

The System pinged.

**INFESTATION DETECTED.** **SPECIES: UNKNOWN.** **LOCATION: COMMERCIAL ZONE.** **REWARD: 500 TREATMENT POINTS.** **PENALTY FOR FAILURE: TERMINATION OF CONTRACT.**

The janitor tipped his hat.

"Clock's ticking, Dale."

Dale looked at the bucket. Then he looked at the store. Then he looked at his phone.

The exam was in two hours, and the infestation was right there.

If he ignored the job, he failed the Authority's implicit test of diligence.

If he took the job, he was walking into a trap set by the people trying to erase him.

Dale took a breath. He adjusted his clipboard.

"I need duct tape," Dale said to the janitor. "And a mop handle. The heavy kind."

The janitor's smile didn't waver.

"It'll cost you," he said.

"I have points," Dale said.

The janitor laughed. It sounded like grinding stones.

"Points don't matter here, Dale. Only results."

The janitor stepped aside. The path to the bucket was open.

Dale walked toward it, and he didn't look at the black water. He looked at the reflection in the metal bucket.

For a second, the reflection wasn't his.

It was the girl. The one who had signed the contract.

She was looking at him from inside the water, and she was screaming.

But she wasn't alone.

Behind her, in the reflection, stood the grey-suited man. And he was pointing at Dale.

Dale reached for the mop handle, and he gripped it tight.

He didn't know if he was walking into a job or a funeral.

But he knew one thing.

He was going to document it all.
