Chapter 144: Out of Compliance

# Chapter 144

The sludge hit the inside of Dale's boot and instantly eated the rubber.

He didn't look down. He looked up.

The pipe stretched upward into a vertical throat of wet brick, and the darkness was teeming.

Chitinous legs scraped against masonry, a sound like dry leaves skittering across pavement, multiplied by thousands. The smell hit him first—ammonia, rot, and something metallic that tasted like a penny held on the tongue.

Dale adjusted his grip on the shovel. The handle was splintered from the last impact. Useless for leverage. Good for distraction.

He clicked his flashlight to its highest setting.

The beam cut through the mist. A wave of *Rattus profundus* surged toward the opening, their eyes reflecting the light in twin points of malicious intelligence.

They weren't just rats anymore.

The dimensional bleed had thickened their carapaces, sharpened their teeth. They were armor-plated vermin.

Dale stepped back. Not away. Back. Into the dry service tunnel behind him.

He pulled the clipboard from his vest. The paper was damp, curling at the edges, but the carbon copy beneath was still legible.

He uncapped his pen.

His hand shook.

He ignored it.

**INCIDENT LOG: SECTOR 4 STORM DRAIN.** **SUBJECT:** *Rattus profundus*, Variant B (Armored). **STATUS:** Uncontained. Breach imminent. **ACTION:** Immediate suppression required.

He scribbled the words fast. The System responded. A blue window flickered in his peripheral vision, translucent and hovering over the swarm.

**TREATMENT POINTS AWARDED: 0.** **NOTE:** Documentation initiated. Completion pending.

Dale gritted his teeth. He needed more than points. He needed a containment perimeter.

He scanned the tunnel walls. Exposed wiring.

A rusted junction box.

A puddle of standing water that had seeped from the main line.

Protocol for Class-2 Vermin: Isolate. Eliminate. Verify.

He couldn't eliminate them.

He didn't have the firepower. But he could isolate them.

If he could trigger the junction box to short out, he could arc the current through the water and create an electric fence.

It wasn't an extermination tool. It was a hazard. But the Authority didn't care about hazards.

It cared about control.

Dale grabbed a length of exposed copper wire dangling from the ceiling. He didn't touch the live end. He used the shovel to hook the wire, pulling it down toward the puddle.

A rat lunged.

Dale sidestepped. The creature slammed into the brick wall, its claws leaving white streaks in the grout. It shrieked, a sound like grinding metal.

He slammed the shovel into the ground, propping it up to hold the wire in place.

The copper tip dipped into the water.

Sparks flew. A low hum vibrated in his chest.

The rats on the far side of the puddle recoiled. Their fur bristled. They hissed.

The electric barrier held.

Dale exhaled. He pulled out his Field Guide.

The entry for *Rattus profundus* was glowing.

He tapped the weakness field. It was empty. He needed data.

He needed to observe the interaction between the species and the environmental hazard.

He watched. The rats tested the barrier.

One threw its body against the water.

It convulsed, dead instantly. The others paused. They communicated.

A pheromone trail rose from the corpse. The swarm shifted, grouping together, waiting.

They were smart. Too smart for rats.

Dale wrote in his log: **SUBJECT EXHIBITS COLLECTIVE INTELLIGENCE. PHEROMONE-BASED COORDINATION DETECTED. WEAKNESS: SENSITIVITY TO HIGH-VOLTAGE ELECTRIC FIELDS (LOW RESISTANCE).**

He highlighted the entry. He assigned it to the current infestation.

**FIELD GUIDE UPDATE ACCEPTED.** **TARGETED DAMAGE BONUS UNLOCKED: +15% EFFECTIVENESS AGAINST *Rattus profundus* WHEN USING CONDUCTIVE AGENTS.**

The bonus applied to the shovel. The shovel was conductive. But the shovel was also his only weapon.

He looked at the barrier.

It was flickering.

The junction box wasn't designed for this load. It was overheating.

He had minutes, maybe seconds, before it failed.

He needed a backup plan. He needed a larger containment zone.

His radio crackled. Static, then a voice. Clear, calm, terrifyingly professional.

"Kowalski."

It was Gwen.

Dale keyed his radio. "This is Dale.

I'm in the storm drain.

Class-2 breach. I have a temporary electrical barrier. It's failing."

"The Authority doesn't recognize temporary barriers as extermination protocols," Gwen said. "Your Thoroughness Rating is dropping. You're at 8.9."

"I'm holding the line," Dale said. "I need a suppression drone."

"You're unlicensed for drones," Gwen replied. "You're Certification Level I. Drones are Level IV."

"Then give me a Level IV override code."

Silence. The static hissed.

"That's not how licensing works, Dale. You earn the privilege by passing the exam. You haven't taken the Level II practical."

"I'm in the practical right now!" Dale shouted.

The rats were testing the barrier again. Two more died.

The swarm pressed closer.

"I'm documenting. I'm isolating. I'm eliminating.

Give me the tool."

Gwen's voice was softer now.

"There's a loophole.

Section 4, Paragraph C of the Municipal Safety Code. Emergency containment.

If you can classify the threat as a structural hazard rather than a biological one, the Authority's jurisdiction shifts. You don't need a license to put out a fire. You need a license to kill a monster."

Dale stared at the rats. The rats stared at him.

"Structural hazard," he muttered.

He looked at the junction box. The sparks were getting worse. The plastic casing was melting. The smell of burning polymer mixed with the rot.

If he classified the rats as an infestation, he needed to exterminate them. If he classified them as a structural failure, he could seal the breach.

He grabbed his clipboard. He flipped to a new page.

**VIOLATION CODE: S-9. STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY FAILURE.** **DESCRIPTION:** Unauthorized dimensional breach causing subsurface collapse and potential infrastructure damage. **REMEDIATION:** Seal breach. Restore structural integrity.

He wrote the code. He signed it. He stamped it with his Class II seal, which was just a rubber stamp he kept in his pocket for code violations.

The System window flashed red.

**RECLASSIFICATION ACCEPTED.** **JURISDICTION SHIFT: AUTHORITY -> MUNICIPAL INFRASTRUCTURE DIVISION.** **NOTE:** Biological entities are now considered debris.

Dale felt a surge of adrenaline. It wasn't combat. It was bureaucracy. And bureaucracy was his native language.

He looked at the rats. They weren't monsters anymore. They were debris. Obstacles to the repair job.

He didn't need to kill them. He needed to move them.

He pulled a flare gun from his kit. Standard issue for confined space inspections. Not for combat. For marking exits.

He loaded a flare. Red. Bright. Hot.

He aimed it at the ceiling above the swarm.

"Hey!" Dale yelled. "Look at this!"

The rats paused. They turned their heads.

He fired.

The flare shot upward, embedding itself in the brickwork. It sizzled, spraying sparks and smoke. The rats shrieked, confused by the sudden heat and light. They scrambled back, away from the ceiling, toward the deeper, darker part of the pipe.

The barrier held.

Dale ran to the junction box. He didn't fix it.

He broke it.

He smashed the casing with the shovel, exposing the live wires. He used the copper wire to bridge the contact points, creating a massive short circuit. The sparks arced, creating a blinding wall of light and electricity.

The rats couldn't cross it. Not with the flare burning above them. They were trapped in the lower section of the pipe.

Dale grabbed a bag of quick-dry cement from his truck's emergency kit. He didn't have much. Five pounds. Enough for a patch, not a wall.

He mixed it with the water from the drain. It sloshed, thick and gray.

He slapped the cement onto the junction box, sealing the wires inside. The electricity hummed, contained but active. The heat from the flare cooked the cement, hardening it faster than normal.

He worked frantically. The rats were pressing against the electric barrier. Their bodies were charring, but they didn't retreat. They were driven by instinct, by the pull of the Rift.

Dale looked at his clipboard.

**STATUS:** Containment established. Breach sealed. **ACTION:** Verify integrity.

He tapped the verify button.

The System window updated.

**VERIFICATION FAILED.** **REASON:** Biological entities remain in proximity to sealed unit. Cross-contamination risk. **PENALTY:** -2 Thoroughness Points.

Dale froze.

The rats were still there. They were pressing against the cement. They were trying to get in.

He couldn't verify the seal if the threat was still active.

The Authority's rules were strict. Isolate, eliminate, verify.

He had isolated.

He had sealed. But he hadn't eliminated.

And he couldn't eliminate them. He had no weapons. He had no nitrogen. He had no drones.

Gwen's voice came through the radio. "You're stuck, Dale.

The Authority sees a biological threat inside a sealed structural unit.

That's a paradox.

You need to clear the interior before you can certify the job."

Dale looked at the cement. It was hardening. The rats were gnawing at it.

Their teeth were breaking against the aggregate. But they were relentless.

He looked at the flare. It was burning out.

He looked at the shovel.

He looked at the rats.

There was only one way to clear the interior. He had to go in.

He pulled on his gloves. He checked his respirator. The filters were clogged with sludge. Useless.

He took off his mask.

He grabbed the shovel.

He kicked open the partial seal he'd made. A chunk of cement fell away, revealing a small gap. The rats hissed, their eyes wide with hunger.

Dale didn't hesitate. He shoved his arm through the gap.

He didn't swing the shovel. He plunged it into the swarm.

He twisted.

The shovel wasn't designed for this.

It bent. But the blade caught three rats, pinning them against the brick. He pulled his arm back.

The rats clung to the blade.

He pulled them out.

He dropped them on the dry floor. He stomped on them.

One.

Two. Three.

He did it again. Arm through the gap.

Shovel.

Twist.

Pull. Stomp.

Again.

Again.

His arms burned. The rats bit through his gloves. He felt the sharp puncture of teeth, but he ignored it.

Pain was information. Infection was a future problem.

Documentation was now.

He counted them. Five. Ten. Fifteen.

He was killing them. Not exterminating them. Killing them.

The System window flashed warning colors.

**CROSS-CONTAMINATION WARNING:** **PROTOCOL MISMATCH.** **ACTION: NON-PROTOCOL KILLING.** **PENALTY:** Field Guide Entry Contaminated. **DURATION:** 24 Hours.

Dale didn't care. The entry for *Rattus profundus* went gray. He couldn't use the targeted damage bonus anymore. He was fighting blind.

But the gap was closing. The cement was hardening.

He pulled his arm out. Blood dripped from his gloves. The rats were retreating. The smell of death was overpowering the smell of rot.

He grabbed a handful of gravel and threw it into the gap. Then more cement. He packed it tight.

He stepped back. He waited.

The silence was heavy.

He pulled out his clipboard.

**STATUS:** Interior cleared of active threats. **ACTION:** Verify structural integrity.

He tapped the verify button.

The System window hung in the air. Red. Yellow. Green.

**VERIFICATION SUCCESSFUL.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING:** 8.7. **TREATMENT POINTS:** 45. **NOTE:** Sloppy execution detected. Excessive force used. Contamination noted.

Dale slumped against the tunnel wall. He checked his arms. Deep puncture wounds.

Bleeding, but not gushing.

He wrapped them with duct tape.

The radio crackled.

"Good work, Dale," Gwen said. "But look at your score."

"8.7," Dale said. "I lost points."

"No. You gained a new penalty."

Dale frowned. "What penalty?"

"Look at your Case List."

Dale pulled out the business card. The list was still there.

**1. THE SUBURBAN SWARM.** **2. THE MALL OF HORRORS.** **3. THE SEWERS OF CLEVELAND.**

Below the sewers entry, a new line had appeared.

**4. THE LICENSING AUTHORITY AUDIT.**

Dale stared at the card.

"What is that?" he asked.

"It's your next exam," Gwen said.

"You crossed a line, Dale. You used non-approved methods. The Authority doesn't like anomalies.

They want to know why you're so good at bending the rules."

"I'm trying to survive."

"Survival is a side effect. Compliance is the goal."

The radio went silent.

Dale looked at the sealed drain. The cement was hard. The rats were gone.

But the darkness below was still moving.

And now, the Authority was watching.

He picked up his shovel. It was bent beyond use. He dropped it.

He picked up his clipboard. It was covered in blood and sludge. He tore off the top sheet.

He started a new report.

**SUBJECT:** SELF. **STATUS:** COMPROMISED. **ACTION:** PREPARE FOR AUDIT.

He didn't know what an Authority Audit looked like. He didn't know if it involved lawyers or lasers.

He clicked his flashlight off. The tunnel plunged into darkness.

In the distance, a new sound. Not skittering. Not hissing.

Footsteps. Heavy, rhythmic, and close.

Dale turned toward the sound. He raised his clipboard like a shield.

A figure emerged from the shadows. Tall. Thin. Wearing a suit that looked like it was made of shadow and old paperwork.

It held a stamp. A large, red, heavy stamp.

"Mr. Kowalski," the figure said. Its voice sounded like a page turning. "You are out of compliance."

Dale looked at the stamp. He looked at the figure.

He clicked his flashlight back on.

"Show me your license," Dale said.
