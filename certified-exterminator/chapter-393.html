Chapter 393: License to Hunt

# Chapter 393

**Chapter 393**

The clipboard was not paper. It was a slab of obsidian, etched with veins of molten gold that pulsed in rhythm with a heartbeat that was not its own.

Vance stared at it. He stared at the chitinous hand holding it. He stared at the thing emerging from the Rift, a silhouette so tall it scraped the underside of the tearing sky. The creature wore armor made of fused carapaces, and its helmet had no face, only a grid of sensory pits that glowed a sickly, bureaucratic amber.

Dale did not stare. Dale looked at the ground. He looked at the stone in his pocket. Then he looked at Vance.

"Get the truck," Dale said again. His voice was flat, stripped of the adrenaline that had been pumping through his veins seconds ago. It was the voice of a man realizing he had forgotten to renew his vehicle registration.

"I don't have a truck," Vance whispered. His eyes were wide, fixed on the giant. "We lost the truck."

"Then we walk," Dale said. He turned his back on the approaching horror. "Move your feet, Vance. Compliance is the first step toward audit survival."

The giant took a step. The ground shook.

A dust cloud rose, coating the crater in gray.

The creature raised its free hand, the one not holding the obsidian slab. It pointed a clawed finger at Dale. A sound emitted from its throat, a grinding of tectonic plates.

**"VIOLATION DETECTED,"** the voice boomed.

It was not loud. It was heavy.

It pressed against Vance's eardrums like deep water. **

"UNLICENSED INTERVENTION.

CHRONOLOGICAL DISPLACEMENT WITHOUT PERMIT. PENALTY: TOTAL ERASURE.

"**

Vance's knees gave out. He didn't run.

He couldn't.

The sheer weight of the Authority's presence pinned him to the dirt.

He watched Dale walk away, small and fragile, walking toward the edge of the crater where the smoke from the dead drone still curled.

Dale was insane. Vance knew this. But Dale was also alive, and Vance was a dead man walking.

The giant moved faster than its size suggested. It surged forward, the obsidian clipboard raised like a shield. The amber lights on its helmet flared.

It was going to crush Dale. It was going to crush Vance.

It was going to erase the timeline.

Dale stopped. He didn't look back. He reached into his torn shirt pocket and pulled out the gray stone. He held it up.

**"STOP,"** Dale said.

The word was small, but it carried a frequency that made the giant's claws twitch. The grinding noise halted. The amber lights dimmed slightly.

Vance scrambled to his feet, his boots slipping on the loose scree. He ran toward Dale, shouting, but the air had thickened. Sound traveled here. He watched Dale hold the stone out, not as a weapon, but as evidence.

The giant paused. It tilted its helmet. The sensory pits focused on the stone.

**"AUTHENTICATION REQUIRED,"** the giant droned.

"It's a Level IX Marker," Dale said. He was breathing hard now, his chest heaving, but his hand was steady. "I found it in the sub-basement of the Kowalski duplex.

Before the breach.

Before I was licensed.

"

Vance reached them, gasping for air. He grabbed Dale's arm.

"Kowalski, put that down! It's bait!"

Dale shook him off.

"It's not bait, Vance. It's a receipt."

The giant lowered its hand. The obsidian clipboard hovered in the air, rotating slowly. The gold veins on it shifted, forming new text.

**"MARKER RECOGNIZED. TEMPORAL PARADOX SUSPENDED. AUDIT PROTOCOL INITIATED."**

A shockwave blasted outward, knocking Vance back into a pile of rubble.

When he looked up, the giant was no longer looking at them. It was looking at the Rift. The black veins in the sky were retracting, pulled back by an invisible force.

The giant raised its clipboard high. A beam of amber light shot from the slab, piercing the clouds.

The Rift closed. Not with a bang, but with a wet, sucking sound. The monsters pouring out were dragged backward, screaming, their chitin cracking under the pressure of the Authority's correction.

Dale turned to Vance. His face was smeared with soot, but his expression was unreadable.

"It's not watching us anymore," Dale said. "It's watching the paperwork."

Vance wiped blood from his lip.

"What paperwork?"

"The backlog," Dale said.

He pointed at the giant.

"That's an Auditor. Class IV.

They don't kill. They document. And they punish the infestation, not the technician.

"

The giant turned its helmet toward Dale. The amber lights were bright again.

**"TECHNICIAN KOWALSKI, DALE. CERTIFICATION LEVEL: V. THOROUGHNESS RATING: 8.4. CURRENT STATUS: UNDER INVESTIGATION."**

Dale didn't flinch. He tapped his temple.

"I'm aware."

**"SUBMIT FIELD REPORT. IMMEDIATELY."**

"I don't have a form," Dale said.

The giant hesitated. This was unexpected. The Authority did not account for lack of forms. It was a gap in the code, a bug in the universe.

**"PROTOCOL 7-ALPHA: VERBAL TESTIMONY ACCEPTED UNDER DURESS. SPEAK."**

Dale took a breath. He looked at the closing Rift. He looked at the smoking drone. He looked at the stone in his hand.

"Infestation type: Chrono-Vermin. Species: *Paradoxia temporis*.

Entry point: Structural anomaly in residential sector 4.

Cause: Previous auditor error in Chapter 392. Evidence: The stone. Mitigation: Nullification via marker display.

Outcome: Rift sealed.

No collateral damage to civilian personnel, excluding temporary sonic trauma.

"

The giant processed this. The gold veins on the clipboard raced across the surface, forming complex equations.

**"THOROUGHNESS RATING RECALCULATED. CURRENT RATING: 9.1. PENALTY WAIVED."**

The giant lowered its clipboard. It stepped back into the shrinking Rift. The amber lights faded. The grinding noise returned, softer now, like a door closing in a distant room.

The sky healed. The clouds knit themselves back together, though they remained bruised and purple. The silence that followed was absolute.

Vance sat on the ground, his legs trembling. He watched the last trace of the Auditor vanish. Then he looked at Dale.

"You just argued with a god," Vance said. His voice was hoarse.

"I filed a complaint," Dale corrected.

He put the stone back in his pocket. He checked his pockets again. Still no clipboard.

Still no spray, and he looked around the crater. The drone was still smoking, but the smell of burning plastic was mixed with something else. Something sweet. Rotting fruit.

"Vance," Dale said. "Smell that?"

Vance sniffed. He gagged.

"Yeah. It smells like... old sugar."

Dale stood up, and he brushed the dirt off his torn uniform.

He looked at the spot where the Auditor had stood. The ground there was not cracked.

It was smooth. Polished. Like the stone.

"New mechanic," Dale said. "Did you see what it did to the rating?"

Vance shook his head.

"It went up. Why?"

"Because I didn't fight it," Dale said. "I identified it.

I documented it.

I isolated it with a verbal barrier. And I verified the seal. That's the protocol.

"

"But you didn't kill it."

"The Authority doesn't want corpses, Vance. It wants clean data."

Dale walked over to the smoking drone, and he kicked the debris aside. Underneath, tangled in wires and burnt plastic, was a small, black insect.

It was dead.

Its legs were curled up tight.

Dale picked it up with two fingers. He examined it closely. The exoskeleton was iridescent, shifting color in the dim light.

"*Paradoxia temporis*," Dale murmured. "Juvenile."

He looked at the Field Guide. He didn't have it open.

He didn't need to, and he knew what it was.

He had seen the entry populate in his mind's eye, a digital ghost overlaid on his vision.

**FIELD GUIDE UPDATE: PARADOXIA TEMPORIS.** **WEAKNESS: STATIC ELECTRICITY.** **RESISTANCE: PHYSICAL FORCE.** **HABITAT: TIME LOOPS, BROKEN CAUSALITY.**

Dale crushed the insect in his hand. The shell cracked with a dry snap. A spark jumped from the remains to his glove.

"Static," Dale said. He looked at Vance. "Do you have a battery?"

Vance blinked.

"What?"

"A battery. A car battery. Anything with charge."

"I... I don't know. The truck is gone."

Dale looked at the crater. The ground was still vibrating, a low hum that resonated in his teeth. The rift was closed, but the contamination remained. The air tasted of copper and ozone.

"We have to clear the site," Dale said. "If we leave this energy signature, the next Auditor will dock us for negligence. A 9.1 is good, but it's not safe. We need a 10."

"How do we get a 10?"

"We ground it," Dale said. "We connect the residual energy to the earth. We discharge the paradox."

Vance stood up, his legs steadying. He looked at Dale with a mix of horror and awe.

"You're insane."

"I'm thorough," Dale said. He started walking toward the edge of the crater, toward the twisted metal of the drone. "Come on. Find me some wire."

Vance followed. He didn't know what to do, but following Dale was better than standing still.

They worked in silence. Vance tore cables from the drone wreckage. Dale arranged them into a crude circuit, using the polished ground of the Auditor's footprint as a conductor. He placed the gray stone in the center of the loop.

"Touch the wire to the ground," Dale instructed.

Vance hesitated.

"Will it shock me?"

"Maybe."

Dale didn't wait. He grabbed the other end of the wire himself. He closed the circuit.

A jolt of blue light erupted from the stone. It arced through the air, striking the ground, striking the sky. The hum in the earth stopped. The smell of rot vanished.

Dale's hand smoked. He didn't drop the wire. He held it until the spark died.

**THOROUGHNESS RATING: 9.8.**

The notification flashed in Dale's vision. It was bright, golden, triumphant.

Dale dropped the wire. He looked at his hand. The skin was blistered, blackened.

He flexed his fingers. They moved, but slowly.

"Vance," Dale said. "I need a new glove."

Vance stared at him.

"You burned your hand."

"It's a hazard pay deduction," Dale said. He walked away from the crater, limping slightly. "But the site is clear."

They walked back toward the ruins of the suburban neighborhood. The sun was setting, casting long shadows across the broken houses. The silence was heavy, but it was natural.

As they passed the duplex where Dale had started his career, a light flickered in the basement window.

Dale stopped. He looked at the window.

Vance stopped beside him.

"What is it?"

"Someone's in the house," Dale said.

"The house is empty. You checked it. We checked it."

"Dale," Vance whispered. "The window is dark. You saw it."

But Dale saw the flicker. A single, rhythmic pulse. Like a heartbeat.

He walked to the front door. The door was open. The doorframe was gone, replaced by a jagged hole in the wall.

Dale stepped inside. The living room was destroyed. Furniture was piled in the center, fused together by some unknown force. The wallpaper was peeling, revealing the lath and plaster beneath.

But in the center of the room, sitting in a chair that looked remarkably like Dale's old recliner, was a figure.

It was not a monster. It was not a giant Auditor.

It was a woman. She wore a gray suit, identical to Dale's. Her badge was Class IX.

She held a clipboard. Her clipboard was made of bone.

She looked up at Dale. Her eyes were white, no pupils.

"Dale Kowalski," she said. Her voice was dry, like parchment. "You are late for your interview."

Dale looked at Vance. Vance looked at the woman. Vance backed away, terrified.

"I didn't apply for an interview," Dale said. His voice was steady, but his heart hammered against his ribs.

"You passed the exam," the woman said.

"Chapter 393, Section 1. You identified the Auditor. You documented the breach.

You grounded the paradox.

You are qualified for Level VI.

"

Dale looked at his blistered hand. He looked at the woman.

"I don't want Level VI," Dale said.

The woman smiled. It was a thin, sharp expression.

"The Authority does not offer choices, Technician. It offers assignments."

She stood up. The room darkened. The shadows in the corners lengthened, reaching toward Dale.

"Your first assignment is to eliminate the previous Level IX Auditor. The one who just left."

Dale frowned. "He's gone."

"He is not gone," the woman said. "He is in your Field Guide."

A notification pinged in Dale's vision.

**FIELD GUIDE ENTRY UPDATED.** **SUBJECT: AUDITOR CLASS IV.** **STATUS: HOSTILE. ACTIVE.** **LOCATION: DALE KOWALSKI'S LEFT EYE.**

Dale blinked. Pain shot through his left eye. A sharp, stabbing sensation. He raised his hand to his face.

Vance screamed.

"Dale! Your eye!"

Dale pulled his hand away.

His palm was covered in black ichor. The pupil of his left eye had changed. It was no longer brown.

It was amber. Glowing.

The woman clapped her hands. The sound was like a gunshot.

"Promotion confirmed," she said. "Welcome to Level VI, Dale. You are now the Auditor."

Dale tried to close his eye, but it wouldn't close.

The amber glow intensified, and he could see the woman's skeleton through her skin. He could see the decay in the walls.

He could see the infestation inside Vance's chest, a small, black worm coiled around his heart.

He could see everything.

And he could not look away.

The woman turned and walked into the shadows. The room plunged into darkness.

Dale stood frozen, his left eye burning, his right eye blind.

Vance was crying.

"Dale? What did she do to you?"

Dale didn't answer. He looked at his hand. He looked at the black ichor. He looked at the amber glow in his own reflection in the broken mirror.

**PROTOCOL UNLOCKED: OBSERVATION. TARGET LOCKED.**

A new menu appeared in his vision. It was vast. Infinite.

**LIST OF INFESTATIONS: 1,402,931.**

Dale swallowed. He tasted blood.

"Vance," he said. His voice was different. Deeper. Resonant.

"Yes?"

"Get the truck," Dale said. "We have a lot of work to do."

Vance looked at him, terrified.

"We don't have a truck, Dale."

Dale smiled. His teeth looked too sharp.

"No," Dale said. "We have a license."

He stepped over the fused furniture. He walked toward the door. The shadows parted for him. The amber eye tracked every movement, every heartbeat, every secret.

He was no longer a pest control technician, and he was the pest.

And he was hungry.
