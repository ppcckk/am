Chapter 408: Eliminate Marcus Vane

# Chapter 408

Rain slicked the asphalt, turning the streetlights of the dying block into smeared halos of amber and gray. A figure stumbled out of the manhole cover, hunching over a bundle of wet fabric and unconscious flesh. The crowd that had gathered to watch the city's end recoiled, not from the monster's blood, but from the sheer, terrifying normalcy of the man carrying it.

He moved with the jerky, rhythmic cadence of a delivery driver fighting for a deadline. His boots, caked in gray mud and black ichor, planted on the cracked pavement.

He didn't look at the sky, where the rift bled light into the clouds.

He looked at his watch. He checked the angle of the ladder still lodged in the hole behind him. He adjusted his grip on Marcus Vane, ensuring the man's neck was aligned, checking for spinal injury with the practiced eye of a paramedic who had seen too many car accidents to feel anything but routine.

"Put him down," someone shouted from the periphery. "He's dead weight."

The technician shook his head, and he reached into his vest, pulling out a roll of industrial-grade adhesive tape. He wrapped it around Marcus's chest, securing the man's arms to his own torso.

It was a binding technique used for restraining aggressive wildlife, not for saving lives.

It looked wrong. It looked efficient.

The crowd murmured. Fear turned to confusion, then to a strange, hollow awe. They saw a man treating a catastrophe like a code violation. They saw a man who refused to acknowledge the apocalypse because he was still filing a report in his head.

Dale Kowalski did not hear the murmurs. He felt the weight of Marcus's head against his shoulder. The man was light, hollowed out by the tunnel's darkness. His breath hitched, a ragged sound that scraped against Dale's ear.

*Thoroughness Rating: 9.7.*

The notification pulsed in the corner of Dale's vision, red and insistent. He blinked it away. He started walking.

He needed a medical bay. He needed a secure perimeter.

He needed to separate the biological evidence from the crime scene.

The city block was three streets away, a structure Dale had inspected six months ago for a rodent issue in the basement.

It had a reinforced concrete foundation. It had a service elevator.

He could use it.

The rain intensified. Water ran down Dale's face, mixing with the black blood on his cheek. He stepped over a puddle that reflected the crumbling skyline.

His legs burned. The stolen power from the webbing still hummed in his veins, a chaotic frequency that threatened to short out his nervous system.

He forced his breathing to stay steady.

In. Out. In.

Out.

Protocol.

A siren wailed in the distance, lonely and broken. It died out quickly.

Dale reached the service entrance of the apartment complex, and the door was jammed with debris. He didn't kick it. He pulled a multi-tool from his belt.

He worked the hinges, lubricating them with a spray can he'd kept in his pocket for years. The metal shrieked, then gave way.

Inside, the lobby was dark.

Power was out.

Dale pulled Marcus through the doorway, dragging the unconscious man across the linoleum. He scanned the area.

No active hostiles. No residual infestation signatures. The air smelled of ozone and old carpet cleaner.

*Area Clear.*

He set Marcus down on the reception desk. The man's face was pale, veins standing out like blue rivers under thin skin.

Dale opened his field guide, and he didn't look at the monster entries. He looked at the *Hazardous Biological Contamination* protocol.

He pulled out a sterile kit.

Gloves. Face mask.

Antiseptic wipes, and he cleaned Marcus's wounds. Not to heal them, but to document them. He counted the lacerations.

He noted the color of the tissue. He checked for signs of dimensional corruption.

*Subject: Marcus Vane.* *Status: Critical.* *Contamination Level: Moderate.* *Action: Isolate and Stabilize.*

Dale worked with mechanical precision. He stitched a tear on Marcus's forearm with a curved needle he had sterilized in the tunnel. The thread was nylon, meant for mending industrial nets. It held.

"Who are you?"

The voice came from the shadows near the stairwell.

Dale didn't flinch. He tied off the stitch. He cut the thread with his teeth.

"A licensed technician," Dale said, not looking up. "You're blocking the egress route."

A man stepped into the faint light of Dale's flashlight. He wore a torn suit, the kind of expensive fabric that cost more than Dale's annual salary. His face was gaunt, eyes wide with a madness that had nothing to do with fear and everything to do with loss.

"Marcus is the Key," the man said. His voice was a whisper, brittle as dry leaves. "You can't just... patch him up. He's the bridge."

Dale finished the last stitch. He peeled off the gloves and dropped them into a biohazard bag he produced from his vest. He sealed the bag with a sharp snap.

"Marcus Vane is a civilian casualty," Dale said. "He is not a bridge. He is a liability."

The man stepped forward.

"You don't understand.

The Authority doesn't want him saved. They want him used.

If he dies, the rift closes. If he lives, it opens wider. You're killing the city by keeping him alive.

"

Dale looked at the man. He recognized him.

Not from a name, but from a file.

A previous inspection report. *High-Value Client. Suspected of unauthorized dimensional resonance experiments.*

"Mr.

Halloway," Dale said. "You violated Section 4, Paragraph C.

Unauthorized resonance amplification in a residential zone.

I cited you three times. You ignored me.

"

Halloway flinched.

"The Authority sent you here to finish me."

"No," Dale said. "The Authority sent me here to finish the job."

Dale stood up. He walked to the elevator.

He pressed the call button.

It didn't light up, and he pried the doors open. The shaft was dark.

*New Loophole Detected: Environmental Override.* *Condition: Critical Infrastructure Failure.* *Effect: System will prioritize asset preservation over protocol compliance.* *Cost: Permanent reduction in Certification Tier.*

The notification hung in the air, gray and cold. It was a trap. A bureaucratic trap.

If Dale used the elevator, he would save Marcus, but he would break the rules that defined him. He would be demoted.

He would lose the status that allowed him to command the arachnid swarms, that allowed him to command the attention of the Authority.

But if he didn't, Marcus died. And Marcus was the witness. The second witness. The one the Authority needed to validate the final report.

Dale looked at Halloway.

"Do you have a battery?"

Halloway blinked.

"What?"

"A backup power source. For the elevator controls."

Halloway stared at him. Then he laughed, a dry, rattling sound.

"You're trying to bypass the grid.

You can't.

The rift drains everything. It feeds on order.

You're feeding it by trying to organize this mess.

"

Dale ignored him. He turned back to Marcus. He checked the man's pulse.

Faint. Thready.

Dale pulled out his flashlight, and he removed the casing.

He took out the lithium-ion battery pack. It was small, heavy, and dangerous.

He had used it to power his high-intensity inspection lamps. It was the only thing keeping his vision clear.

He looked at the elevator controls. Then at the battery. Then at Marcus.

He stripped the wires. He connected the positive terminal of the battery to the elevator's auxiliary input. Sparks flew. The smell of burning plastic filled the lobby.

*Warning: Unauthorized Modification.* *Thoroughness Rating: 9.5.* *Penalty: -0.2 for improper tool usage.*

The elevator lights flickered. The doors slid open.

Dale grabbed Marcus. He threw the unconscious man into the cabin. He stepped in after him. He pulled the doors closed.

Halloway screamed something, but the sound was cut off by the rising hum of the motor.

Dale pressed the top floor. The elevator jerked.

It climbed.

Slowly. Grinding against the shaft.

Inside the small metal box, Dale watched the floor indicator. 1... 2... 3...

Marcus groaned. His eyes fluttered open.

"You..." Marcus rasped. "You... crazy..."

"Protocol," Dale said. He checked his watch. "We have four minutes before the structural integrity of this building fails due to resonance feedback. I calculated the load-bearing capacity based on the foundation reports."

Marcus coughed. Blood flecked his lips.

"Why? Why save me?"

"Because the Authority requires a witness," Dale said. "And I don't have enough treatment points to fake it."

The elevator stopped. The doors opened.

They were on the roof. The rain was a torrent here. The sky was a churning vortex of gray and violet. The rift was directly above them, a wound in the world that pulsed with a sickening rhythm.

Dale walked to the edge, and he looked out at the city. The blocks were dark.

The lights were gone.

The silence was absolute, broken only by the wind and the distant, dying sound of the siren.

*System Alert: Final Objective Updated.* *Objective: Verify Extermination.* *Condition: Witness Must Observe the Source.* *Penalty for Failure: License Revocation. Permanent.*

Dale turned to Marcus.

"Look."

Marcus looked up. He saw the rift. He saw the things crawling out of it, not rats or beetles, but shapes that defied geometry. Shapes that hurt to look at.

Marcus screamed. It wasn't a scream of pain. It was a scream of recognition.

"It's... it's my fault," Marcus whispered. "I opened it. I tried to talk to them. I thought they were gods."

"They are pests," Dale said. He pulled out his clipboard. He clicked his pen. "And they are infesting my service area."

Dale looked at the rift. He could feel the power inside him, the stolen energy from the tunnel.

It was unstable, which was pulling him apart.

If he tried to use it, he would burn out. He would die.

But the Authority was watching. The Authority was waiting.

Dale had one protocol left. One ability he had never used, because it required a sacrifice he wasn't sure he was willing to pay. It was called *Total Annihilation*.

It didn't kill the monster. It erased the location.

It erased the witness.

It erased the technician, and he looked at Marcus. Marcus was looking at him. For the first time, Marcus saw Dale not as a bureaucrat, but as a weapon.

"Do it," Marcus said.

Dale raised the pen.

He didn't write. He pointed.

The rift pulsed.

*Command Accepted: Initiate Final Protocol.* *Warning: Self-Destruct Sequence.* *Time Remaining: 10 Seconds.*

Dale closed his eyes, and he thought of the kitchen in the duplex. The German cockroaches.

The frass trails.

The simple, clean logic of a world that could be fixed if you just found the nest.

He opened his eyes.

He didn't point at the rift. He pointed at Marcus.

Marcus's eyes went wide.

"No," Marcus said.

Dale's hand didn't tremble. His voice didn't waver.

"Identification: Marcus Vane." "Species: Dimensional Conduit." "Status: Threat Level 10." "Action: Eliminate."

The System didn't hesitate, and the light took them.

The light did not take Marcus.
