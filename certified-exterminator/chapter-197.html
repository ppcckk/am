Chapter 197: License Suspension

# Chapter 197

The first door burst open, spilling a cascade of chitinous limbs onto the cracked concrete.

Dale didn't scream. He didn't flinch. He kicked the splintered remains of his clipboard into the gap, using the heavy wooden half to jam the door hinge.

"Vane, move left!" Dale shouted, his voice cracking under the strain of the sudden atmospheric pressure change.

Vane scrambled sideways, tripping over a tangle of pulsing resin roots. The bureaucrat-face in the ceiling watched him, its expression shifting from recognition to irritation. A low, grinding hum vibrated through the floorboards, shaking the dust from the rafters.

"Left!" Dale repeated, more this time.

A massive beetle, its shell slick with black oil, lunged at Vane's exposed ankle. Dale grabbed a handful of salt and chalk mixed with his own blood, flung it at the creature's mandibles, and slammed his foot on the jammed door.

The beetle shrieked, a sound like tearing metal, and recoiled. The salt burned its sensory pits. It wasn't an attack. It was a deterrent.

Dale's vision flickered. The world tilted gray, then snapped back into focus with a jagged overlay of red warning glyphs.

*SYSTEM ERROR: CLASSIFICATION CONFLICT.*

*UNIT IDENTITY: UNVERIFIED.*

*PROTOCOL: EXTERMINATION SUSPENDED.*

Dale ignored the text. He couldn't use his Field Guide entry for *Rattus profundus* because these weren't rats. They were something else. Something that had walked out of a door in the floor of a basement in suburban Ohio.

He needed data.

"Keep them off the doors!" Dale ordered. "Don't kill them. Jam them."

"I can't hold them!" Vane yelled, swinging a rusted pipe at a swarm of centipedes emerging from the third door. "They're endless!"

"They're not endless. They're queued." Dale scanned the room.

The doors weren't random.

They were arranged in a grid. Standard municipal utility access points. Water, gas, electric.

Someone had mapped this sub-basement like a service blueprint.

And the creatures pouring out were wearing badges.

Tiny, bioluminescent tags glowed on the thoraxes of the beetles. Not insect tags. Municipal inspection stickers.

Dale froze. The blood drained from his face, replaced by a cold, procedural dread.

These weren't monsters invading. They were inspectors arriving.

And the thing in the ceiling wasn't a monster either. It was a permit.

"If I classify them as Hosts, the Authority kills them," Dale muttered, his mind racing faster than his hands. "If I classify them as Infrastructure, the Authority protects them. But what are they to *me*?"

The face in the ceiling smiled wider. The resin stretched, forming a new mouth. A wide, wet grin that exposed rows of human teeth.

*AUDIT COMMENCING.*

The air pressure dropped. Dale's ears popped. The beetles stopped moving. They stood still, antennae twitching in unison.

"They're waiting for orders," Vane whispered, lowering his pipe. "Why are they waiting?"

Dale stepped forward, ignoring the swarm of insects that parted around his boots like water. He approached the nearest door. The badge on the beetle's back read: *CODE 4-B: ILLEGAL DIMENSIONAL APPENDAGE.*

Not an infestation. A violation.

"They're here to cite us," Dale said, his voice flat. "We're the violation."

The ground shook. More doors popped open.

Dozens.

Then hundreds. The room was filling with creatures, but they didn't attack. They lined up.

They formed a perimeter. They held clipboards made of bone and chitin.

Vane backed into the wall, hyperventilating. "What do we do? Do we fight?"

"No," Dale said.

He reached into his pocket and pulled out a pen.

It was out of ink. He squeezed the barrel until a drop of blood welled up at the tip.

"We file an appeal."

He walked toward the central door, the largest one, which had erupted directly beneath the face in the ceiling. The face looked down at him, its eyes narrowing.

"Who is the primary stakeholder?" Dale asked, addressing the ceiling.

The face did not answer. It pointed a clawed finger at the beetle at Dale's feet.

The beetle stepped forward. It held up a bone tablet. On it, scratched in jagged lines, was a single word: *DENIED.*

"You can't deny entry to the Authority," Dale said. "You're the Authority."

*CORRECTION,* the voice in his bones replied. *WE ARE THE LICENSING BOARD. YOU ARE THE CONTRACTOR.*

Dale stopped. The implication hit him harder than the physical blow that had broken his clipboard earlier.

He wasn't the hero. He wasn't the victim. He was the contractor who had failed to clean up his mess.

The beetles were here to shut down the site. To condemn the property. To demolish the structure.

If they condemned the structure, they condemned Dale.

"Vane," Dale said, not turning around. "Grab the resin. The sticky stuff on the walls."

"Why?"

"Because we're filing for a variance."

Dale looked at the field guide in his peripheral vision. The entry for *Rattus profundus* was grayed out.

Blocked by contamination.

He couldn't use it. He couldn't use any of his high-level protocols. They were flagged as *Unlicensed Use in Active Litigation.*

He had to improvise.

He looked at the beetle holding the tablet.

It was a Class-4 Inspector Beetle. High threat level.

But it was also a bureaucrat.

It followed rules.

Dale looked at the floor. The resin was pooling around the roots of the ceiling membrane. It was thick, viscous, and smelled of formaldehyde.

"Vane, smear the resin on your hands."

"Dale, this is insane—"

"Do it!"

Vane hesitated, then dipped his hands into the glowing black sludge.

Dale turned to the Inspector Beetle. He held up his bloody, empty pen.

"I am requesting a temporary suspension of enforcement," Dale said, his voice steady. "Based on the presence of an unlicensed sub-contractor."

The beetle tilted its head. Its antennae twitched.

"Who is the sub-contractor?" the beetle clicked.

Dale pointed at the face in the ceiling.

"The entity above. It has not filed a Form 12-R: Notification of Metamorphosis. It is operating illegally within the residential zone."

The face in the ceiling roared. The resin cracked. The face twisted into a mask of fury.

*FALSE,* the voice boomed. *I AM THE LICENSE.*

"Show me your number," Dale said.

Silence.

The beetles looked up. They looked at the face. They looked at Dale.

The face didn't speak. It couldn't. It was a manifestation, not a filing.

Dale smiled. It was a thin, cruel smile. The kind he used when he found a cockroach nest in the insulation behind the drywall.

"No license," Dale said. "That means you're unregistered. That means you're vermin."

The face screamed. The sound shattered the glass in the single window high on the wall. Shards rained down.

The Inspector Beetle dropped its bone tablet. It raised its mandibles. It turned toward the ceiling.

The other beetles followed. They didn't attack Dale. They attacked the source of the illegal activity.

They swarmed the walls, climbing the resin roots, biting into the membrane of the face. The face thrashed, tearing chunks of its own body away as the beetles tore into it.

But the face was strong. It pulsed, releasing a wave of disorienting light. The beetles fell back, their shells cracking under the pressure.

Dale grabbed Vane's arm. "The resin! Throw it!"

Vane understood. He scooped up handfuls of the black goo and hurled them at the beetles.

The resin hit the chitin. It hardened instantly, gluing the beetles' legs together. The beetles froze, trapped in their own sticky mess.

But more were coming. From the other doors. The queue was endless.

Dale looked at his hands. He was covered in ink and blood and resin. He looked like a mess. He looked like a violation.

He needed to change the narrative.

He walked to the center of the room. He stood between the trapped beetles and the thrashing face.

"I am invoking the Doctrine of Least Harm," Dale said, loudly.

The room went quiet. Even the face stopped thrashing.

*DOCTRINE RECOGNIZED,* the voice whispered. *STATE YOUR TERMS.*

Dale took a breath. The air tasted of copper and rot.

"The infestation," Dale said, pointing at the beetles, "is not a threat. It is a symptom. The source of the symptom is the unlicensed entity in the ceiling."

He pointed at the face.

"Therefore, the extermination protocol must target the source, not the symptom. If you kill the beetles, the infestation continues. If you kill the face, the infestation ends."

The face laughed. It was a wet, gurgling sound.

*THE FACE IS THE AUTHORITY. THE BEETLES ARE THE ENFORCEMENT.*

"Then the Authority is out of compliance," Dale said. "And I am filing for immediate revocation of your license."

He pulled a piece of the broken clipboard from his pocket. It was the corner of the form. The part with the header.

He held it up.

This was the loophole. The System rewarded documentation.

It rewarded procedure.

It didn't care about truth.

It cared about *process*.

By filing for revocation, Dale was forcing the System to evaluate the Authority itself.

But the System couldn't audit its own administrator.

Unless the administrator was no longer the administrator.

Unless the administrator was classified as a hostile entity.

Dale looked at Vane. "Vane. Did you see the ID number on the beetle's badge?"

Vane blinked, sweat dripping from his nose. "Yeah? It was... it was mine."

Dale froze.

"Mine?"

"The badge. On the beetle. It had my name. And my license number."

Dale looked at the beetle nearest to him. The one whose legs were glued shut. The bioluminescent tag glowed brighter.

He squinted.

The tag didn't say *Code 4-B*.

It said: *DALE KOWALSKI - PROVISIONAL.*

The System hadn't labeled the beetle as an inspector. It had labeled the beetle as *Dale.*

And it had labeled the face in the ceiling as *Vane.*

Dale looked up. The face in the ceiling was smiling. But the smile wasn't Dale's anymore.

It was Vane's.

Vane looked at his own hands. The resin was seeping under his fingernails. His veins were turning black.

"Dale?" Vane whispered. "Why do I feel like I'm being audited?"

The face in the ceiling spoke again. But it wasn't the Authority's voice.

It was Vane's voice.

*SUBMISSION RECEIVED.*

The doors slammed shut.

The beetles dissolved into black smoke.

The face in the ceiling peeled itself off the roof and dropped to the floor. It landed in a heap of wet tissue and bone. It looked up at Dale.

It was Vane.

But it was also not Vane. It was a hollow shell, filled with ink.

Dale backed away, his heart hammering against his ribs.

He looked at his field guide. The entry for *Dimensional Vermin* had vanished.

In its place, a new entry had populated itself.

*SUBJECT: KOWALSKI, DALE.* *STATUS: MERGED.* *THREAT LEVEL: UNKNOWN.*

Dale looked at his hands. They were changing. The skin was hardening, turning into a dark, shiny shell.

He tried to scream, but his throat was full of resin.

The thing that looked like Vane stood up. It straightened its back. It adjusted its tie, which had made of chitin.

"Mr. Kowalski," the thing said, using Dale's voice. "Your license has been suspended. Please report to the sub-basement for retraining."

Dale's vision went black.

The last thing he saw was the new doors opening.

And this time, they weren't opening into the room.

They were opening inside him.
