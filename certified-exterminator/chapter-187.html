Chapter 187: Drain into Cannery

# Chapter 187

The grate cover was already off.

Dale didn't question why. He just stepped over the jagged metal lip, his boots finding purchase on the slick, algae-coated rungs.

The air rising from the darkness smelled of sulfur and rotting kelp.

It was a thick, cloying scent that clung to the back of the throat. His nose wrinkled. He pulled his respirator down, not for protection, but for protocol.

Down here, the silence was heavier than the concrete above.

He checked his wrist unit. The screen flickered, red warning bars pulsing in a slow, mocking rhythm. *Level III Protocols: LOCKED. Field Guide Entry: Corrupted. Treatment Points: 0.*

The Authority was watching. The surveyor was watching. And something in the sewers was listening.

Dale unclipped his flashlight.

The beam cut a clean white cone through the particulate haze. It caught the wet sheen of tunnel walls, the slime trails that glistened like fresh paint.

He moved with the practiced efficiency of a man who had spent fourteen years crawling under houses.

No wasted motion. No panic. Just procedure.

He wasn't looking for a monster. Monsters were unlicensed, chaotic, and difficult to bill for.

He was looking for an infestation.

Infestations had patterns.

They had entry points. They had nests.

The tunnel branched. Left went toward the treatment plant. Right vanished into a darkness so complete it seemed to absorb the light. Dale went right.

A skittering sound echoed off the curved brickwork. Small, sharp impacts. Chitinous legs hitting stone.

He stopped. Tilted his head.

The sound came again. Closer.

Dale raised his hand. He didn't draw a weapon. He drew his notebook.

The leather cover was stained with old coffee and fresh blood. He flipped it open to a blank page.

*Observation: Auditory signature suggests Class 2 arthropod. Size estimate: 30-40 centimeters. Movement speed: high.*

The Authority text flashed in the corner of his vision. It was grayed out, a ghost of the usual bright notification. *[Documentation Mode Active. Points reduced by 50% due to Level Lockout.]*

Dale didn't care about the penalty. He cared about the data.

A shape burst from the side alcove.

It was a crab, but wrong. Its shell was translucent, revealing the pulsing, violet organs inside. Its claws were serrated, designed for tearing than crushing.

It clicked its mandibles, a sound like snapping twigs.

Dale didn't attack. He didn't even flinch. He clicked his pen.

*Species Identification: Pending. Morphology consistent with deep-sea crustacean variants found in pre-Rift industrial runoff. Potential hybridization with Rift energy.*

The crab lunged.

Dale stepped back. Not away, but sideways.

A precise, calculated shift of weight.

The claw missed his hip by a millimeter. He used the momentum of the miss to kick the creature's leg joint. It stumbled, its chitinous armor cracking at the weak point.

He didn't finish it. He couldn't. The Cross-contamination penalty for using a Level I spray on a Class 5 threat would have wiped out his remaining credibility.

Instead, he grabbed a handful of gravel and threw it at the crab's eye cluster.

The creature shrieked, a wet, gurgling noise, and scuttled into a side pipe.

Dale marked the location in his notebook. *Entry Point: Pipe 4-B. Blockage: Potential nest site. Recommend chemical flush, but Level II access required.*

He kept moving.

The tunnel widened. The walls were no longer brick.

They were organic.

Fused cartilage and bone, growing over the original infrastructure like coral reef.

This wasn't just an infestation anymore. It was a colonization.

At the center of the chamber stood a structure. It looked like a hive made of dripping slime and discarded human debris—shopping carts, tires, the occasional shoe. And at the top, suspended by thick, ropy tendrils, was the source of the relocation.

It was a Queen. Not a rat. Not a beetle.

It was a humanoid shape, elongated, with too many joints in its limbs. Its face was a smooth, featureless mask of pale skin. It hung upside down, its legs wrapped around a concrete support beam.

Dale froze.

This was above his pay grade. This was above his certification.

The Authority text blazed, unignorable.

*[Threat Level: 7. Unidentified.]* *[Recommendation: Retreat.]* *[Penalty for Engagement: Immediate License Revocation.]*

Dale stared at the Queen. It didn't have eyes. It didn't have a mouth.

But it was vibrating. A low hum that rattled Dale's teeth.

He looked at his notebook. He looked at his empty spray cans. He looked at the locked icons on his wrist unit.

He couldn't kill it. He couldn't contain it.

But he could document it.

And the Authority, for all its bureaucratic tyranny, had one rule that even Dale knew was absolute: *Information is currency.*

If he couldn't fight it, he could sell it.

He clicked his pen again. His hand didn't shake. It never shook when he was working.

*Subject: Class 7 Bio-construct. Primary Feature: Vocalization resonance. Secondary Feature: Structural integration with municipal sewage grid.*

The Queen turned its head. The vibration intensified.

Dale didn't run. He walked closer.

*Observation: Subject reacts to proximity. No defensive posture. Potential predatory ambush?*

He stepped over a pile of rotted meat. The smell was overwhelming. He breathed through his mouth.

*Observation: Subject's tendrils are anchored to the sewer's main drainage valve.*

He saw it.

The thick, pulsing roots of the creature were wrapped around the iron wheel of the valve. The creature was using the sewer's infrastructure as an anchor. It wasn't just hiding.

It was controlling the flow.

If Dale broke the valve, the creature would be swept away.

But the valve was rusted shut.

He pulled a wrench from his belt. It was a standard issue tool, not a magical artifact. It weighed four pounds.

He approached the valve. The Queen's head tilted. The hum became a roar. The water in the chamber rose, churning around Dale's boots.

*Warning: Environmental hazard. Slippery surface. Depth increase: 10cm.*

Dale ignored the warning. He wedged the wrench into the rusty spokes of the valve. He pushed.

Nothing.

He gritted his teeth. He put his back into it. The metal groaned.

The Queen dropped.

It landed with a wet slap, its legs unfolding with terrifying speed. It lunged at Dale, its smooth face inches from his visor.

Dale didn't pull the wrench out. He twisted it.

The valve squealed. A tiny leak began.

Dale twisted harder. His muscles burned. The Authority text flashed red. *[Physical Exertion: High. Risk of Injury.]*

The Queen's claw struck his shoulder. The impact spun him around.

He hit the water, gasping.

The cold was shocking. The taste of bile and sewage filled his mask.

He scrambled up. The Queen loomed over him. It raised both claws.

Dale slammed his elbow into the wrench handle.

The valve gave way.

A torrent of black water erupted from the pipe. It wasn't just water. It was raw, untreated sewage from the upper district. It smelled like death and chemicals.

The Queen shrieked. The sudden influx of pressure and toxic filth disrupted its resonance. Its tendrils slipped from the valve.

Dale didn't wait. He grabbed his flashlight and swam.

The current dragged him backward, but he kicked hard, fighting the flow. He reached the ladder just as the chamber filled with churning filth.

He climbed. His arms burned. His lungs screamed.

He burst out of the grate, gasping for air.

The surveyor was standing there, holding a tablet. The woman from the house was still on the couch in the background, visible through the open window.

The surveyor looked at Dale. He looked at the sludge dripping from Dale's uniform.

"You broke the valve," the surveyor said.

"I documented the vulnerability," Dale said, coughing.

"You flushed a Class 7 threat into the main treatment line."

"I isolated the entry point. I removed the anchor.

The threat is now moving with the flow.

It is no longer static.

It is mobile."

The Authority text appeared in Dale's vision.

*[Action: Structural Sabotage.]* *[Result: Infestation Displaced.]* *[Thoroughness Rating: 4/10.]* *[Penalty: Fine of 500 Credits.]* *[Bonus: Field Guide Entry Updated. Weakness: Resonance Disruption via High-Pressure Fluid Injection.]*

Dale wiped sewage from his visor.

He looked at the surveyor.

"I need to talk to the cannery guy," Dale said.

"Why?"

"Because he sent me there. He knew the valve was there. He knew I'd break it."

The surveyor frowned. "Who?"

"The guy who sent the text. *I'm the reason you're still licensed.*"

The surveyor checked his tablet. He tapped the screen.

"We have no record of that number."

"I have the text," Dale said. He held up his phone.

The surveyor zoomed in on the message.

*Meet me at the old cannery. Bring the guide. We need to talk about the sewers.*

Below it, the sender's ID was blank.

But the timestamp was wrong.

It was sent ten minutes *before* Dale had even arrived at the house.

Dale stared at the screen.

The Queen hadn't just been hiding in the sewers. It had been waiting for him.

And someone had known it would be there.

Someone had known Dale would go down there.

Someone had known he would break the valve.

"Kowalski," the surveyor said, his voice low. "You're being framed."

"For what?"

"For unauthorized modification of municipal infrastructure. But also... for leading the infestation to the treatment plant."

Dale's blood went cold.

The treatment plant. The main pump station. That was where the city's entire sewage volume was aggregated.

If the Queen went there, it wouldn't just relocate. It would multiply.

He looked at the sewer grate. The water was still bubbling up, dark and oily.

"I have to go to the plant," Dale said.

"You're locked out. You have no protocols. You have no gear."

"I have the guide," Dale said. He tapped his notebook. "And I have the location."

He started walking toward his truck.

"Kowalski, stop. If you go to the plant, you're going to get killed. Or worse. You'll get fired."

Dale opened the truck door. He didn't look at the surveyor.

"If I don't go, the city gets killed."

He climbed in. He started the engine.

The radio crackled to life. Static. Then a voice.

It wasn't the Authority.

It wasn't the surveyor.

It was a woman's voice. Calm. Professional.

*"Technician Kowalski. This is the Licensing Authority. Your certification is under review.

Please report to the Central Processing Hub immediately. Your presence is required for... re-evaluation."*

Dale froze.

The Authority didn't send voice messages. They sent text.

He looked at the rearview mirror.

The purple tear in the sky was pulsing faster.

And in the distance, sirens wailed. Not police sirens. Disaster sirens.

The plant was already breached.

Dale slammed the truck into gear.

He didn't drive to the hub.

He drove to the cannery.
