Chapter 381: Bleach Tape Walk

# Chapter 381

"Inspection complete."

Dale didn't look up. His pen hovered over the clipboard, the tip trembling with a microscopic precision that terrified onlookers more than the monster currently pinned to the wall behind him.

The man in the blue jumpsuit shifted his weight. His boots squeaked on the polished concrete. It was a sound that grated against Dale's auditory nerves, a frequency that suggested improper maintenance of the shoe soles.

"Sir?" the man said. "I asked if you had a pest problem."

Dale capped the pen. He clipped it to the board. He straightened his tie, though it was already perfectly aligned with the placket of his shirt.

"You are standing in a designated Redaction Zone," Dale said. His voice was flat, devoid of inflection.

"You are also standing in a puddle of acidic residue from a Class-4 Acidic Scarabaeus.

Step back. You are cross-contaminating the evidence trail.

"

The man blinked. He looked down at his boots. A thin, translucent slime coated the rubber. He tried to wipe it off on his pants.

"Stop," Dale commanded.

The man froze. His hand hovered in mid-air.

"Contamination penalty," Dale said, pointing at the man's leg with his pen. "If you smear that residue onto your uniform, you breach protocol 7-B. I will be forced to redact you as well. Do you have the proper containment suit?"

The man looked at Dale. He looked at the dead scarab, which was roughly the size of a minivan and currently oozing green fluid onto the whiteboard wall.

"I...

I'm just here to inspect," the man said. "I'm a Level II technician.

New hire.

I got the notification about a Code Red in Sector 4.

"

"Code Red is for structural breaches," Dale said. He picked up the clipboard.

"This is a localized infestation event.

You are underqualified to be here.

You are, in fact, a hazard.

"

The man swallowed.

"The Authority sent me. Halloway."

At the name, Dale's jaw tightened. A single muscle feathered in his cheek. He didn't react further, but the air in the cubicle seemed to grow heavier.

"Halloway does not send inspectors," Dale said. "Halloway sends exterminators. And exterminators wear lead-lined aprons, not cotton jumpsuits."

"I don't know what you mean."

Dale stepped closer. He didn't raise his voice. He never raised his voice. But he leaned in until the man had to tilt his head back to maintain eye contact.

"Your Field Guide," Dale said. "Show it."

The man hesitated, then reached into his pocket. He pulled out a tablet. The screen was cracked. The interface was sluggish.

Dale snatched it, and he scrolled through the entries. His fingers moved with blurring speed.

"Entry for *Rattus profundus*: Incomplete."

Dale tossed the tablet back. It clattered against the desk.

"Entry for *Araneus abyssalis*: Weakness analysis is generic. You listed 'large spider.' That is not a weakness. That is a description. If you encounter a broodmother in the crawlspace, you will die in three seconds."

The man picked up the tablet. He looked offended.

"I passed the written exam."

"The written exam tests your ability to read," Dale said. "The practical exam tests your ability to survive. You failed the latter before you even entered the field. Look at your hands."

The man looked down. His fingernails were bitten to the quick. Skin around the cuticles was red and inflamed.

"Stress response," Dale said. "You are anxious. Your workspace is not sterile.

Your documentation is sloppy. You are a vector for failure.

"

"I'm here to help," the man said, his voice rising. "There's a breach. People are dying. I can fight."

"You cannot fight," Dale said. "You can only process."

He turned back to the whiteboard. On it, equations swirled in real-time ink, connecting the location of Rifts to population density and sewage flow. Dale picked up a dry-erase marker.

"Observe," Dale said.

He drew a line from a sewer grate in Sector 4 to a school playground in Sector 5.

"The Rift opened at 0400 hours," Dale said. "The infestation started in the sub-basement of the public library. Why?"

The man watched. He didn't answer.

"Because the library has a faulty HVAC unit," Dale said.

"The condensation created a humidity gradient. *Rattus profundus* are attracted to moisture. They followed the water line. If you had documented the humidity levels in your initial report, you would have predicted the migration path to the playground.

You didn't.

You went straight to the library. You engaged.

You got hurt.

You ran."

The man went pale.

"How do you know that?"

"Because your uniform has a tear at the shoulder. Scuff marks on the left knee. Mud from the library's basement on your right boot. And a smell of bleach that is outdated, meaning you tried to clean the site yourself and failed."

Dale uncapped his marker.

"You are a mess. I am cleaning it up."

He turned to the dead scarab. He pulled a canister from his belt. It wasn't spray. It was a solvent gel, thick and blue.

"Protocol: Isolation," Dale muttered.

He sprayed the gel around the carcass. It formed a perfect circle.

"Protocol: Verification," he said.

He scanned the gel with his Field Guide. The device beeped. Green.

"Clean," Dale said.

He looked at the man.

"Now, help me with the rest."

The man blinked.

"Help you?"

"You have a clipboard?"

The man patted his pockets. He found a crumpled sheet of paper.

"Fold it," Dale said. "Make a crease down the center. I need a temporary containment barrier for the residual ectoplasm leaking from the scarab's thorax."

The man folded the paper. He did it awkwardly, the crease jagged.

Dale sighed. He took the paper. He refolded it. The crease was now sharp enough to cut skin.

"Better," Dale said. "Now, go to the north wall.

There is a second entry point.

A crack in the foundation.

I need you to document the width. Use the calipers in your kit.

"

"I... I don't have calipers."

Dale stopped. He looked at the man with an expression of profound disappointment.

"Of course you don't."

Dale reached into his own suit pocket. He pulled out a pair of stainless steel calipers. He handed them to the man.

"Measure the crack. Record the depth. If it is wider than two centimeters, the broodmother is already inside.

If it is narrower, she is approaching. I need to know which protocol to deploy.

"

The man took the calipers. He walked to the wall. He measured.

"1.5 centimeters," the man said.

Dale nodded. "Approaching. Good. That gives us forty minutes."

The man looked back.

"What are we going to do?"

"We are going to set a trap," Dale said.

He moved to the desk. He opened a drawer. Inside were dozens of labeled jars.

He selected one labeled *Bait: Fermented Yeast and Blood*.

"We need to lure her out," Dale said. "She is territorial. She will not enter if the space is too open. We need to make her feel cornered."

"How?"

"We block the exit," Dale said. He pointed to the door.

"You go out.

You seal the hallway with tape. Not just any tape. Industrial strength.

If you use masking tape, she will tear through it and eat you.

"

"And you?"

"I will stay here. I will prepare the nest."

The man looked nervous.

"Alone?"

"You have a flashlight," Dale said. "And you have a license. Use it."

The man left. The door clicked shut.

Dale was alone, and the silence was heavy. He could hear the hum of the lights. He could hear the drip of the scarab's acid.

He picked up his clipboard. He looked at the file marked **REDACTION**.

He looked at the entry: **SUBJECT: DALE KOWALSKI (FORMER).**

He smiled. It was not a nice smile.

He opened his Field Guide. He turned to a blank page.

He wrote: **SUBJECT: HOLLOWAY.**

He paused. He thought about the woman in the white room. The smile. The contract.

He wrote: **WEAKNESS: BUREAUCRATIC ARROGANCE.**

He underlined it.

The door opened again. The man rushed in.

"It's not working!" he shouted.

Dale didn't look up from his writing.

"The tape is peeling," the man said. "And there's a sound in the hallway. Like clicking."

"Chelicerae," Dale said. "She is testing the perimeter."

"She's coming," the man whispered. "Halloway sent me to kill you, didn't she? She said you were... obsolete."

Dale stood up. He smoothed his suit jacket.

"Halloway did not send you to kill me," Dale said. "Halloway sent you to test if I was still useful. And you are failing."

"What do I do?"

"Get behind me," Dale said.

The man obeyed.

Dale picked up his pen, and he walked to the door. He looked out into the dark hallway.

Two giant eyes reflected the light from the room. They were black, glossy, and wet. Mandibles clicked against the peeling tape.

Dale raised his clipboard.

"Attention," Dale said. His voice echoed in the narrow space. "This area is under lockdown. By order of the Municipal Licensing Authority, all unauthorized biological entities must submit to immediate containment."

The spider hissed. It lunged.

Dale didn't flinch, and he snapped his fingers.

A loud *clap* echoed through the hallway.

The spider froze.

"Protocol: Auditory Disruption," Dale said. "Effective against *Araneus abyssalis* due to their reliance on vibration for hunting."

The man stared at Dale.

"You... you just stopped it."

"No," Dale said. "I interrupted its feeding sequence. It will resume in three seconds."

"Then what?"

Dale looked at the man. He looked at the calipers in the man's hand.

"Hit it," Dale said.

The man raised the calipers.

"What?"

"The calipers are steel.

They are dense, and they are a blunt instrument. The spider's eyes are soft tissue.

Strike the left anterior eye. It will cause a localized hemorrhage. The cross-contamination penalty will apply to you, but the damage bonus to *Araneus* will offset the debuff.

"

"I don't know how to fight!"

"You are holding a tool," Dale said. "Tools are for working. Swing."

The spider shrieked. It dropped from the ceiling.

The man swung the calipers, and he missed.

The spider landed on the desk. It scuttled toward Dale.

Dale stepped aside. He didn't draw a weapon. He drew a marker.

He circled the spider on the whiteboard.

"Target identified," Dale said.

The spider looked confused. It paused.

Dale pointed at the wall.

"Look at the diagram," Dale said. "You see the line from the sewer to the playground?

That is your weakness.

If you do not move to the playground, you will starve. The Authority knows this. I know this.

"

The spider clicked. It looked at the diagram. It looked at Dale.

"It understands," the man whispered.

"It is processing the logic," Dale said.

"Spiders are ambush predators. They rely on surprise.

We have removed the element of surprise by documenting the environment.

You are no longer prey. You are a variable in an equation.

"

The spider turned. It looked at the man.

"Run," Dale said.

The man ran.

The spider followed.

Dale stayed in the room, and he watched them leave.

He closed the door.

He picked up his clipboard.

He wrote one final note on the file.

**STATUS: UNLICENSED.**

**ACTION: OBSERVATION.**

He sat down at his desk. He folded his hands.

He waited.

The phone on the desk rang, which was a landline. A black rotary phone. It hadn't rung in thirty years.

Dale stared at it.

The ringing was loud. Jarring.

He picked up the receiver.

"Hello?"

A voice crackled on the other end. It was static, distorted, but recognizable.

"Dale," Halloway said. "You are not supposed to be alive."

"I am compliant," Dale said. "I am working."

"You are interfering," Halloway said. "The new technician is a distraction.

I sent him to distract you.

To keep you busy.

While I handled the real problem.

"

"What is the real problem?"

Dale asked, and the line went dead.

Dale looked at the phone.

He looked at the whiteboard.

On the diagram, the line from the sewer to the playground had turned red.

It was pulsing.

Dale stood up, and he walked to the window. He looked out at Sector 4.

The sky was green.

And in the distance, hundreds of figures were walking toward the library. They were not running. They were marching.

They were holding clipboards, and they were holding flashlights.

They were all wearing blue jumpsuits.

Dale grabbed his Field Guide.

He looked at his reflection in the glass.

He looked tired, and he looked dangerous.

He opened the door.

He stepped into the hallway.

The new technician was gone.

Only the tape remained.

And the smell of bleach.

Dale walked, and he did not run.

He walked.

Because running is sloppy.

And Dale Kowalski did not do sloppy work.
