Chapter 136: Pending Appeal

# Chapter 136

Dale kept walking.

The stairs groaned under his weight.

Above him, the basement door kicked open.

"Freeze! Authority Compliance! Hands on your head!"

A beam of light hit Dale's chest. It was blinding. White. Sterile.

He didn't raise his hands.

He adjusted his clipboard.

"Identification required," the voice said. It belonged to a woman. Her tone was flat. Professional.

Dale squinted against the glare. "Kowalski. Class II Technician. License number 44-B-9."

"License status: Revoked."

"Status: Pending appeal," Dale corrected. "You're three minutes early for the dispatch window. That's a scheduling violation."

The light wavered. The woman behind it shifted her stance. Her boots crunched on the broken drywall.

"Step away from the wall, Technician."

"I'm not touching the wall."

"You are in possession of a Class-IV biological hazard."

Dale tapped his chest pocket. The egg inside was warm. It pulsed against his ribs like a second heartbeat.

"It's not a hazard," Dale said. "It's evidence."

"Evidence of what?"

"Of misclassification."

A second figure stepped into the light. Taller.

Broader.

He wore a grey tactical vest with the Authority logo stitched over the heart. A recovery team.

"Cut the crap, Dale," the big man said.

His face was scarred. Old burns.

"Hand over the egg.

The Authority flagged it as a rogue dimensional variant. We're authorized to neutralize it on sight."

"Neutralize is a strong word," Dale said.

"It's a monster."

"It's a larva."

"It's a pest."

"It's a person," Dale said.

The big man laughed. It was a dry, humorless sound.

"You've lost your mind.

The system says PEST.

The system says TERMINATION. You're a technician.

You follow protocols."

"I follow *certified* protocols."

Dale held up his clipboard. The paper was crisp. White. Uncreased.

"Show me your Field Guide entry for *Homo sapiens (Dimensional Variant)*."

The big man blinked. "Why?"

"Because you're about to commit a cross-contamination violation."

Silence.

The light lowered slightly.

"Explain," the woman said.

Dale flipped to a new page. He wrote a single word: *HOST.*

"Your guide lists this species as a pest," Dale said. "But the Authority's own taxonomy classifies *Homo sapiens* as a keystone host for multiple Class-3 infestations.

If you terminate the host without removing the vector, you trigger a systemic backlash.

The infestation doesn't die. It spreads."

"That's a theory," the big man said.

"That's a regulation. Section 4, Paragraph 12: Host Preservation in Vector-Control Scenarios."

The woman lowered her flashlight. She pulled out a tablet. Her fingers flew across the screen.

"Checking," she said.

"You have thirty seconds before my Thoroughness Rating drops for insubordination," Dale said.

"And I need to document the egg's condition. It's pulsing at a rate of forty beats per minute.

That's not fear.

That's growth."

The big man stepped forward. "I'm taking the egg."

Dale took a step back. "If you touch me, I will file a worker's comp claim for workplace assault. It will trigger an automatic audit of this entire recovery unit. Do you want to deal with the paperwork?"

The big man froze.

The woman looked up from her tablet. Her expression had changed. It wasn't aggressive anymore. It was confused.

"He's right," she said.

"About what?" the big man asked.

"Section 4, Paragraph 12. It exists. I didn't think it applied to human variants. But the text doesn't exclude them."

"Then we classify it as non-human," the big man said.

"You can't," Dale said. "The shell is transparent.

The genetics are human.

The System recognizes it.

If you kill it, you're not exterminating a pest. You're killing a civilian.

And the Authority hates bad optics."

The big man growled. He reached for his holster.

Dale didn't flinch. He pointed his pen at the wall.

"Look at the hole."

The big man glanced at the gap in the drywall.

"What about it?"

"That's a structural breach. Class K-7. But look at the residue around the edges."

The big man frowned. He walked closer. He leaned in.

"Slime?"

"Mucus," Dale said. "It's acidic. It's burning the wood. But it's not burning the concrete."

"So?"

"Acid beetles eat wood. They don't eat concrete. But the broodmother did. Which means the broodmother wasn't an acid beetle."

The big man straightened up. "So what is it?"

"It's a mimic," Dale said.

The woman's head snapped up. "A mimic?"

"Specifically, a *Chitin-Polymorphus*. It mimics the dominant species in the area to maximize infiltration. In this suburb, the dominant species is human."

"You're saying the broodmother was human?" the big man asked.

"I'm saying the broodmother was *a* human. A dimensional variant.

That's why the egg is human-shaped.

It's not a monster egg. It's a reproductive cycle of a parasitic humanoid species."

The big man's face went pale.

"If we kill the egg," Dale said, "we might trigger the parent's defense mechanism. The parent is still out there. The Authority's sensors missed it because they were scanning for pests. Not for a predator."

"Where is it?" the woman asked.

"That's the problem," Dale said. "I don't know. But the egg does."

He reached into his pocket. He pulled out the egg.

It was larger now. The veins inside glowed faint blue.

Dale held it out.

"Scan it."

The big man hesitated. "It's a hazard."

"It's a key," Dale said.

"The Authority wants thoroughness. Thoroughness requires data.

If you take the egg and destroy it, you get zero points for documentation.

You get a demerit for unauthorized termination of a potential asset. If you let me scan it, you get the location of the parent. You get the weakness.

You get a perfect score on this job."

The big man looked at the woman.

She looked at her tablet.

"The Thoroughness Scale," she said quietly. "He's right. If we terminate without identification, our rating drops to 2.0. We'll be reassigned to sewer maintenance."

"Sewer maintenance?" the big man whispered.

"It's a downgrade. It means no hazard pay. No certification progression. Just cleaning sludge."

The big man stared at Dale.

"You're playing us," he said.

"I'm working," Dale said. "You're interfering."

The woman walked forward. She held out her scanner. It looked like a Geiger counter, but with a green lens.

"Give me the egg," she said.

Dale handed it over.

She scanned it. A blue light washed over the shell. The machine chirped.

*Identification: *Chitin-Polymorphus*. Stage: Larval. Host Compatibility: 98%.*

*Warning: Parent Signal Detected. Range: 15 meters. Direction: Subterranean.*

The big man backed away. "Where?"

"Below," Dale said. "In the crawlspace. It's not gone. It's waiting."

"For what?"

"For the egg to hatch.

The parent needs the host to be ready.

If we interrupt the hatching, the parent attacks. If we let it hatch, the parent takes the egg and leaves.

The infestation ends."

The big man shook his head. "We can't let a monster walk away. It's a breach."

"It's a migration," Dale said. "If you fight it here, in a confined space, with poor lighting and no containment field, you'll cause structural collapse.

The house will come down.

The neighbors will complain. The Authority will fine you for property damage."

"How do you know that?"

"Because I've read the code." Dale tapped his clipboard. "OSHA Regulation 114-C: Prohibited Activities in Structural Failure Zones."

The big man looked at the woman.

"Can we trap it?" she asked.

"Yes," Dale said. "But we need to do it properly. I need to set up a barrier. A containment line."

"We don't have containment equipment," the big man said.

"I do."

Dale reached into his other pocket. He pulled out a roll of heavy-duty duct tape.

"That's it?" the big man asked.

"It's certified industrial adhesive," Dale said. "It's rated for vapor resistance. If we line the crawlspace entrance, we can isolate the parent. Then we can vent it."

"Vent it how?"

"With the HVAC system.

The house has a central unit. If we seal the ducts and reverse the fan, we can push the parent out into the yard.

Where it's visible.

Where you can shoot it from a distance. With proper clearance."

The big man looked at the hole in the wall. Then at the dark stairs leading down.

"It's a long shot."

"It's thorough," Dale said.

The woman nodded. "Do it.

I'll monitor the signal.

Kowalski, you have five minutes.

If the egg signals a hatching event, we terminate the protocol."

Dale nodded.

He walked to the wall. He tore off a strip of duct tape. He pressed it against the wood.

He worked quickly. Methodically.

The big man watched him. He didn't help. But he didn't stop him.

The woman watched the scanner.

"Signal strength increasing," she said. "It's moving toward the crawlspace door."

Dale ignored her. He taped the base of the stairs. He taped the gap around the doorframe. He used his pen to mark the perimeter.

"Clearance zone established," Dale said.

"Ready," the big man said. He raised his weapon.

"Wait," Dale said.

"What now?"

"We need a bait."

"We have the egg."

"The egg is in the scanner," Dale said. "The parent can sense the scanner's frequency. It knows it's been found. We need to draw it out."

"With what?"

Dale looked around the room. He saw the kitchen. He saw the refrigerator.

He walked to the fridge. He opened it.

"Get me the light," Dale said.

"The light?"

"The bulb. From the pantry."

The big man looked confused. Then he understood.

He went to the pantry. He unscrewed the bulb.

He brought it to Dale.

Dale took the bulb. He held it in his hand.

"Turn off all other lights," Dale said.

The woman killed the flashlight.

The room went dark.

Only the blue glow of the scanner remained.

Dale held up the bulb.

He flicked the switch on the wall.

The bulb didn't light up. It was dead.

But the click was loud.

*Click.*

Silence.

*Click.*

Dale repeated it.

*Click. Click. Click.*

A sound came from below.

Scraping.

Heavy. Wet.

Something dragged itself against the wood.

"Parent detected," the woman whispered. "Distance: Five meters."

Dale kept clicking.

*Click.*

The scratching stopped.

Then, a voice.

It wasn't human. It sounded like voices layered on top of each other. Like a choir singing in a tunnel.

"*Give... us... back...*"

"It's talking," the big man said. He raised his rifle.

"Don't shoot," Dale said. "It's testing the barrier."

The door at the bottom of the stairs burst open.

Something spilled out.

It looked like a human. But the proportions were wrong. The arms were too long.

The head was too small. The skin was grey.

Chitinous.

It crawled up the stairs.

It moved with terrifying speed.

"Fire!" the big man shouted.

"No!" Dale yelled.

He threw the light bulb.

It shattered against the wall.

The sound was sharp.

The creature flinched.

It hissed.

The blue scanner light flickered.

"Containment breach!" the woman shouted.

The creature lunged.

Dale didn't move.

He stepped aside.

The creature crashed into the duct-taped barrier.

It hit the tape.

It stuck.

Not because the tape was strong. But because the creature was confused. The light bulb's shards had triggered its light-aversion instinct. It hesitated.

In that second, Dale kicked the crawlspace door closed.

He slammed his foot against the frame.

He used his full weight.

The door locked.

The creature screamed.

It clawed at the wood.

The tape held.

The big man fired.

Three shots.

They hit the door.

Wood splintered.

The creature shrieked.

"Stop!" Dale shouted.

The big man lowered his gun.

"It's trapped," Dale said.

The woman looked at her scanner.

"Signal is contained," she said. "But the egg... the egg is responding."

Dale looked at his pocket.

The egg was pulsing faster.

Hotter.

"It's syncing with the parent," Dale said.

"Then the containment failed," the big man said.

"No," Dale said. "The protocol is working. The parent is isolated.

The egg is dormant.

We have time."

"For what?"

"For the audit."

The phone in Dale's pocket buzzed.

Not a call.

A notification.

**JOB COMPLETE.**

**THOROUGHNESS RATING: 9.8.**

**CERTIFICATION LEVEL III UNLOCKED.**

**NEW PROTOCOL AVAILABLE: CHEMICAL ISOLATION (GAS).**

Dale looked at the big man.

"You're welcome," Dale said.

The big man stared at the door.

The scratching stopped.

The silence was heavier than the noise.

Then, the floorboards beneath the crawlspace door creaked.

A crack appeared in the foundation.

Not in the wall.

In the concrete.

Something was digging out.

Not the parent.

The parent was inside.

The thing outside was bigger.

And it was angry.

Dale looked down at the crack.

A claw, large and black, punched through the concrete.

It wasn't chitinous.

It was fur.

The System popped up.

**NEW SPECIES IDENTIFIED: CANIS LUPUS (DIMENSIONAL VARIANT).**

**CLASSIFICATION: PREDATOR.**

**TREATMENT PROTOCOL: IMMEDIATE TERMINATION.**

Dale looked at the claw.

He looked at the big man.

"Run," Dale said.

The big man didn't run.

He looked at the claw. Then at Dale.

"Why is it here?" the big man asked.

"Because you're loud," Dale said.

The claw swiped.

The big man's head snapped back.

Blood sprayed.

Dale grabbed the clipboard.

He didn't run.

He wrote.

*Entry: The Authority.*

*Weakness: Arrogance.*

He underlined it once.

Then he looked up.

The claw was coming for him.
