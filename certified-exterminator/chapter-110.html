Chapter 110: Documented and Dangerous

# Chapter 110

The pen clicked. It was a sharp, mechanical sound that cut through the low hum of the streetlights dying one by one.

Dale didn't lower his clipboard. He held it at eye level, angling it like a shield.

The man in the white suit stopped ten feet away.

His shoes were polished to a mirror finish, reflecting the neon sign of the abandoned laundromat behind him. Not a speck of dust on the hem. Not a crease in the lapel.

Miller was already three streets away, his footsteps fading into the night. Good. Miller was a liability. Miller was an unlicensed variable.

The Auditor tilted his head.

"Mr. Kowalski.

You failed to file a Containment Report for the Class III entity located at Sector 4, Grid C-9.

That is a violation of Protocol 7, Section B: *Post-Elimination Verification*."

Dale's thumb hovered over the pen. He could feel the sweat pooling at the base of his spine, but his hand remained steady.

Protocol 7, Section B.

He knew it.

He had read it in the manual during his lunch break four years ago.

"I filed the report," Dale said. His voice was flat. Deadpan.

The Auditor smiled again. It didn't reach his eyes. "You filed a *summary*.

You did not attach the Frass Analysis. You did not log the structural damage to the foundation.

You did not certify the area as clear."

"I am currently verifying."

"You are standing in a crater."

"I am standing in a designated work zone." Dale tapped the tip of his pen against the clipboard. "You are trespassing in an active infestation site without a Badge of Authority. That is a Code 14 violation. Unlicensed interference."

The Auditor's smile tightened. The air around them grew colder. The blue crystal fragment on the ground pulsed once, then went dark.

Dale had kicked it away, but he hadn't picked it up.

He couldn't. Not yet.

The warning had been clear. *Class I Felony.*

But the Auditor wasn't here for the crystal. He was here for the audit.

"Your Thoroughness Rating is 9.2," the Auditor said. "It is an anomaly. Most operatives hover around 6.0.

They kill the monster. They leave the mess.

You clean the mess.

It is... inefficient."

"Inefficiency is a choice," Dale said. "Contamination is a consequence."

"Then you will pay the consequence."

The Auditor raised his tablet. The screen glowed with complex geometric patterns.

"I am issuing a Citation. Not of force. Of *Debt*."

A notification flashed in Dale's peripheral vision. Red. Urgent.

*System Alert: Audit Initiated.* *Subject: Dale Kowalski.* *Charge: Unverified Documentation (x14).* *Penalty: Field Guide Corruption.*

Dale's stomach dropped. Field Guide Corruption.

If his guide corrupted, every entry he had painstakingly documented—the weak points, the pheromone trails, the nesting habits—would be wiped.

He would be blind. A Level II technician with no data was just a guy with a spray bottle and a death wish.

"Why now?" Dale asked.

"Because you are a threat to the Status Quo," the Auditor said.

"The Licensing Authority prefers order. Predictable kills.

Predictable loot.

You... document. You make the infestation visible. You make the Authority look negligent for ignoring the root causes."

Dale looked at the tablet. The geometric patterns were shifting, forming a web around his name. He needed to break the chain. He needed a loophole.

He looked at the rubble behind the Auditor. The remains of the Class III beast. The chitinous shell shards were scattered like broken glass. And there, half-buried in the asphalt, was a single, intact leg segment.

*Field Guide: Chitinous Armored Carapace.* *Weakness: Joint articulation at the hip.* *Note: Shell fragments retain residual mana signature for 12 hours.*

Dale's eyes narrowed. The Auditor was using a legal framework to suppress Dale's combat data. But the System operated on rules, not laws. And the System had a rule for everything.

*Rule: If a threat is identified but not eliminated, it remains an active infestation.* *Rule: Active infestations generate Treatment Points.* *Rule: Citation Force requires a valid target.*

Dale stepped forward. One step.

Then another.

He didn't look at the Auditor.

He looked at the leg segment.

"Miller," Dale said, though Miller was long gone. "Log entry 402. Secondary infestation detected. Residual biomass."

The Auditor frowned. "What are you doing?"

"Dale Kowalski. Identification." Dale pointed his pen at the leg. "Target: *Arthropoda profundus*, detached limb.

Status: Active threat. Threat Level: Minor."

A blue light flickered around the leg.

*Target Identified.* *Protocol Available: Extraction.*

The Auditor's tablet buzzed. The geometric web broke. "Stop. You are fabricating a target to bypass the audit."

Dale didn't stop.

He walked toward the leg. The Auditor moved to block him, but Dale sidestepped. He didn't attack the Auditor.

He attacked the *situation*.

He reached into his belt and pulled out his containment bag. Not the heavy-duty biohazard bags he used for broodmothers. The small, sterile sample bags.

He knelt by the rubble. The Auditor lunged, grabbing Dale's shoulder. "Do not interfere with an Authority Agent."

Dale twisted his body, using the Auditor's momentum to spin. He didn't throw a punch. He threw the clipboard.

It wasn't a weapon. It was a tool.

The clipboard hit the Auditor's chest, but not hard.

It just disrupted the balance. Dale fell back, rolling onto the asphalt. He grabbed the leg segment.

*Item Acquired: Residual Biomass (Unverified).*

The Auditor straightened his tie. His face was pale. "You have contaminated the scene. That is a Class II Felony."

"Verify the contamination," Dale said, breathing hard. He held the leg segment up. It was still warm. The joint was exposed.

"Verify," the Auditor repeated. He looked at his tablet.

The audit charge was frozen.

The System required proof of the violation before it could be enforced.

But the violation was the act of killing the monster. The act was already done.

The audit was retroactive.

Unless...

Dale stood up. He held the leg segment with both hands, like a surgeon holding a scalpel.

*Protocol: Citation Force.* *Requirement: Valid Legal Documentation.* *Current Status: Invalid.*

Dale pulled a blank sheet of paper from his clipboard. He placed it on his knee. He uncapped his pen.

The Auditor watched, confused. "What are you writing?"

"An addendum," Dale said.

He wrote. Fast. His handwriting was jagged, but legible.

*Addendum to Report #114-A.* *Subject: Secondary Infestation.* *Description: Detached limb from primary target exhibits autonomous reflex arcs. Confirmed active threat.* *Action Taken: Containment initiated.* *Status: Pending verification.*

Dale held the paper up.

The System notification changed.

*Documentation Updated.* *Target: Detached Limb.* *Status: Identified.* *Audit Charge: Suspended.* *Reason: Target is no longer a past event. It is a current event.*

The Auditor's tablet went dark.

"You..." The Auditor's voice cracked. "You reclassified the corpse as a live threat."

"I reclassified it as an infestation," Dale said. "The Authority defines infestation as 'biological matter that poses a risk to human health or structural integrity.' A severed limb with residual muscle memory can cause trip hazards, lacerations, and potential infection spread. It is a hazard. It requires treatment."

Dale walked toward the edge of the crater. He didn't run. He didn't fight. He simply walked toward the nearest storm drain.

"You cannot move the evidence," the Auditor said, but his voice lacked conviction. "That is obstruction."

"It's not evidence," Dale said. "It's a pest. And I'm removing it."

He reached the storm drain cover. He kicked it aside. The dark hole gaped open, smelling of rust and stagnant water. Dale dropped the leg segment inside.

*Threat Neutralized (Contained).* *Treatment Points Awarded: 5.* *Thoroughness Rating Adjustment: +0.1.*

The Auditor stood still. The tablet remained dark. The audit was gone. But the rivalry remained.

Dale turned back to face the man in the white suit. The grime on Dale's pants was caked into the fabric.

His shirt was torn.

He looked like a wreck. But his eyes were clear.

"Your audit is void," Dale said. "Because you audited a closed case. I opened a new one."

The Auditor stared at him.

For a second, the bureaucratic mask slipped. Something else looked out from behind those eyes.

Something hungry.

Something that hated the light of scrutiny.

"You think this changes anything?" the Auditor asked quietly. "The Authority knows.

The Registry knows.

You are a glitch, Kowalski.

A bug in the system."

"Bugs get exterminated," Dale said.

He turned and walked away. He didn't look back. He knew the Auditor would not follow him here.

The street was neutral ground. No infestations.

No active threats.

Just two men standing in the dark.

But Dale knew something else.

The System had given him 5 points. A small amount.

Trivial.

But it had unlocked something else in the background. A new field in his status.

*Hidden Attribute Unlocked: Regulatory Immunity.* *Description: Actions taken in strict accordance with protocol are immune to external administrative suppression.*

It was a defensive perk. A shield. But it had a cost.

*Warning: Regulatory Immunity requires constant documentation. If documentation lapses, immunity expires. Penalty: Immediate Debuff.*

Dale pulled out his pen. He began writing in his pocket notebook.

The name of the Auditor.

The time.

The location. The specific words spoken.

He needed to document everything. Every breath. Every step.

Because if he stopped writing, the immunity vanished. And if the immunity vanished, the Auditor could kill him without a second's hesitation.

He turned the corner onto Elm Street. The streetlights flickered back on, one by one. The city was waking up. Or maybe it was just the System rebooting the local grid.

Dale reached his truck. It was parked illegally in front of a fire hydrant. A ticket was already on the windshield.

He ripped it off.

He didn't care. He climbed into the cab. His hands were shaking now.

The adrenaline was fading, leaving behind a cold, hollow exhaustion.

He looked at the rearview mirror. His face was pale.

There was a cut on his cheek from the fall.

It needed cleaning. It needed a bandage. It needed a report.

He started the engine. The radio crackled to life.

Static.

Then a voice.

Not a news station. Not a police dispatch.

It was the Authority.

"Mr. Kowalski," the voice said. It was smooth.

Calm. "We have reviewed your addendum.

It is... creative."

Dale gripped the steering wheel. "I'm off the clock."

"You are never off the clock," the voice said. "The Rift is expanding. The Class III event was a probe.

We know who sent it.

We know why."

"Who?" Dale asked.

"A Licensee," the voice said.

"Level VIII. From the Northern District. He has been auditing your performance for six months.

He is coming to finish the job personally."

Dale's blood ran cold.

A Level VIII.

He was Level II. The gap wasn't just a power difference.

It was a species difference. A Level VIII could rewrite local laws. They could change the physics of the battlefield.

"How long do I have?" Dale asked.

"Twelve hours," the voice said. "But you have a problem. Your Thoroughness Rating has dropped to 8.9 due to the contamination risk in Sector 4. You are no longer in the top percentile for your certification level."

"Why does that matter?"

"Because the next exam is not practical. It is a survival trial. And only the top three percent of Level II Licensees are invited to participate. The rest are culled."

Dale stared at the radio speaker. Culled.

"That's not a fair test," Dale said.

"Fairness is a human construct," the voice said. "The Authority rewards efficiency.

You were inefficient.

You spent time on paperwork instead of preparation. You are now in the bottom tier."

The line went dead.

Dale sat in the silence. The engine idled. The heater blew cold air.

He looked at his clipboard.

The paper was blank. The ink was drying.

He had to fill it.

He had to find a way to increase his rating, or he would be deleted.

He looked out the window. Across the street, a dumpster was overflowing. Black bags sat on the pavement. They were moving.

Not the bags. The things inside them.

The bags bulged, shifting shapes. Something with too many limbs was pushing against the plastic.

Dale didn't panic. He didn't reach for his spray. He reached for his pen.

He opened a new page.

*Incident Report #115.* *Location: Elm Street, Dumpsite.* *Subject: Unidentified Biomass.* *Status: Active Infestation.*

He stepped out of the truck. He didn't look at the dumpster.

He looked at the street.

At the cracks in the sidewalk.

At the roots of the oak tree that had lifted the concrete.

The Authority wanted thoroughness. The Authority wanted data.

Dale walked toward the dumpster. He didn't attack. He observed.

He saw the frass. Small, dark pellets scattered near the lid.

He saw the entry point. A gap under the lid, sealed with tape that had been peeled back.

He saw the behavior. The bags weren't being attacked by animals. They were being *used*. Something was using the garbage as camouflage.

Dale wrote this down.

*Hypothesis: The creature is not hiding. It is baiting.*

The notification flashed.

*Insight Generated.* *Field Guide Update: Potential New Species.* *Bonus: +10% Damage against Unknown Entity.*

Dale smiled. It was a thin, dangerous smile.

He pulled out his spray can. Not the insecticide. The heavy-duty industrial solvent he used to strip paint.

He wasn't going to kill it. Not yet.

He was going to study it.

And then he was going to document it.

Because the only thing more dangerous than a monster was a technician who had nothing left to lose.
