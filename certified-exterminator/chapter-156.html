Chapter 156: File a Complaint

# Chapter 156

The clipboard connected with the metal faceplate.

A dull, resonant *clang* echoed through the sterile white room. The metal hand didn't flinch.

It tightened.

Dale's collarbone ground against the steel fingers, a jagged spike of white heat radiating down his left arm. His vision tunneled. The edges went gray.

Dale gritted his teeth until they ached. He didn't pull away. He pressed the clipboard harder, using it as a fulcrum to twist his body out from under the vice-like grip.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** *Unlicensed application of Class III Sedatives requires override code.*

The text pulsed in his peripheral vision, red and angry.

Dale ignored it. He kept his eyes on the woman's monitor.

The heart rate was spiking. 140. 150.

The black metal tendrils attached to her spine were vibrating, humming with a frequency that made Dale's molars ache.

The figure in the shadows stepped forward. It wore a suit that looked like it had been tailored from oil slicks. No face. Just a smooth, obsidian mask where features should be.

"Drop the clipboard, Technician," the mask said. The voice was synthesized, layered with static. "You are interfering with an authorized extraction."

Dale straightened his spine. The pain in his shoulder made his knees buckle, but he locked his elbows.

"Authorized by who?" Dale asked. His voice was steady. Professional. "Your permit number is missing from the wall placard."

The figure tilted its head. "This is not a municipal zone. This is a private medical facility. Your jurisdiction ends at the property line."

"The property line includes the structural integrity of the building," Dale said. He tapped his pen against the clipboard.

"And the biological containment of the patient.

That's a code violation.

Section 4, Paragraph B: Unauthorized biological hazard exposure in a residential zoned structure."

"Residential?" The figure laughed. It sounded like grinding gears. "Look around you, Dale Kowalski. Look at the walls."

Dale looked. The walls were lined with servers. Cables snaked across the floor like black roots.

The 'beds' were pods. The 'medical equipment' was a rack of syringes labeled with chemical codes Dale didn't recognize, but whose viscosity he judged to be wrong for human administration.

"They call it a clinic," the figure continued. "It's a farm. And you're the pest that got into the grain silo."

Dale looked at the sleeping woman. Her eyes were closed, but her fingers were twitching. The black tendrils were pulsing in sync with her heartbeat.

"You're not treating an infestation," Dale said. "You're harvesting one."

"Semantics," the figure said.

"The Authority gave you an exam. The exam is simple. Identify the threat.

Eliminate it.

Or accept the penalty for non-compliance."

"The threat is you," Dale said.

"No," the figure said.

It raised a hand. The metal fingers on Dale's shoulder loosened. "The threat is her.

She's the source. She's the nest."

Dale froze. He looked at the woman's neck. The black tendrils weren't just attached to her; they were growing *from* her. Small, black nodules were budding along her jugular vein.

"Parasitic symbiosis," Dale murmured. He flipped a page in his Field Guide. His fingers flew over the index. *Rattus profundus*. *Acid Beetle*. *Void Moth*. Nothing matched.

He looked closer. The tendrils had a segmented structure. Jointed. Chitinous.

"Arthropoda," Dale said. "Subclass... something wrong with the legs. Too many joints."

The figure stopped moving. The silence in the room grew heavy, thick with the hum of servers.

"Where did you get this specimen?" Dale asked.

"Does it matter?"

"It matters for the protocol," Dale said.

He pulled a small, yellow tag from his belt pouch.

He wrote **UNCLASSIFIED** on it and stuck it to the foot of the pod. "If I can't identify it, I can't treat it.

And if I can't treat it, I can't pass the exam."

The figure stepped back. "You're stalling."

"I'm inspecting," Dale corrected. He walked to the control console. It was locked. "Who has the keycard?"

"No one," the figure said. "The system is automated.

Once the host reaches critical mass, the extraction begins.

The host dies. The asset is preserved."

"That's sloppy work," Dale said. He turned to face the figure. "That's a Thoroughness Rating of 1. You'll lose your license if the Authority audits this."

"The Authority doesn't audit private ventures," the figure said.

"The Authority audits *everything*," Dale said.

"That's how they know you're here. That's how they know *I'm* here.

They're watching the cameras.

Look."

Dale pointed to the corner of the room. The red light blinked.

The figure glanced up. Just for a second.

Dale moved.

He didn't run. He walked to the ventilation duct he had come from.

He pulled a canister of compressed air from his belt.

It wasn't a weapon.

It was a tool for clearing dust from heat exchangers.

He sprayed it directly into the intake grille.

A cloud of freezing mist erupted into the room. The metal hand that had grabbed him earlier jerked back. The sensors on the mask flickered. Frost formed on the optical lenses.

"Protocol Alpha-7," Dale shouted. "Initiate Severance!"

**SYSTEM PROMPT:** *Authorization Required. Class III License Not Detected.*

"Override code: KOWALSKI-DELTA-9," Dale said.

The System hesitated. A second of silence.

**OVERRIDE ACCEPTED. LOCAL PROTOCOL ACTIVATED.**

The vents in the ceiling slammed shut. The lights cut out.

Darkness.

Then, emergency red lighting.

The figure roared. A sound like tearing metal. "You can't do this! The extraction is mid-cycle!"

"Containment breach," Dale said into the dark. "All personnel evacuate. This is a Class 4 Biohazard."

He heard movement. Not from the figure. From the pods.

The woman was waking up.

A scream tore through the room. It wasn't a human scream. It was a high-pitched, chittering shriek. The black tendrils retracted, pulling back into the woman's body with violent, jerky motions.

Dale fumbled for his flashlight. The beam cut through the red haze.

The woman was sitting up. Her eyes were wide, white, and unseeing. The nodules on her neck were pulsing, growing rapidly.

The black tendrils were no longer attached to the servers. They were wrapped around her own chest, tightening.

"Get out!" the figure shouted.

It was backing toward the exit. Its suit was smoking from the cold spray. "It's destabilizing!

It's going to burst!"

Dale looked at the woman. He looked at the clipboard.

He had a protocol for severance. But it required sedation. He didn't have the drugs.

He did have the clipboard.

He walked toward the woman. The figure lunged for him, but the metal limbs were sluggish, frozen by the nitrogen spray. Dale sidestepped.

"Stay back!" the figure yelled. "If you touch her, you'll be infected!"

Dale stopped three feet from the pod.

"Infected with what?" Dale asked.

"The host doesn't survive the bonding," the figure said. "But the parasite does.

And it spreads.

Airborne spores. If she bursts, this entire block is compromised."

Dale looked at the Field Guide. He had identified the weakness: *Sensitivity to rapid temperature drop.*

He had used that. He had cooled the environment. But he hadn't neutralized the source.

The woman convulsed. The black tendrils snapped taut. Her mouth opened, and a jet of black fluid sprayed out.

Dale raised the clipboard.

The fluid splattered against the plastic cover. It hissed, eating into the surface.

**FIELD GUIDE UPDATED:** *Host-Parasite Hybrid (Unnamed). Weakness: Thermal Shock. Secondary Weakness: Structural Integrity of Host.*

Dale looked at the woman's chest. The ribcage was deforming. The parasite was trying to break out.

"If I kill the host, I fail the exam," Dale said.

"If you don't, you die," the figure said. It was struggling to stand. "And everyone else dies."

Dale looked at the woman. She was terrified.

She was conscious.

Her eyes focused on Dale for a split second.

Tears streamed down her face.

He couldn't sedate her. He couldn't cut her open.

But he could seal her.

Dale reached into his pocket. He pulled out a roll of industrial-grade duct tape. Not the kind he used for pipes. The kind he used for sealing toxic waste bags.

He walked to the foot of the pod.

"What are you doing?" the figure demanded.

"Isolating the infestation," Dale said.

He grabbed the woman's arm. The black tendrils whipped at him. He dodged, the tape roll in his hand.

"Stand still!" Dale shouted.

The woman shook her head. The parasite was controlling her motor functions. Her hand lashed out, claws of black chitin extending from her fingertips.

Dale took a hit to the shoulder. The skin tore. Blood welled up.

He didn't drop the tape.

He wrapped it around her wrist. Then her other wrist. He bound them to the metal rails of the bed.

The figure stopped fighting. It watched, horrified.

"You're insane," the figure whispered.

"I'm thorough," Dale said.

He moved to her neck. The nodules were throbbing. He applied a layer of tape over them.

It wasn't enough to stop them. But it was enough to slow the spread of the spores.

He wrapped her torso. Tight. Constricting.

The woman screamed. The sound was wet, gurgling.

The black tendrils witherred.

The lack of oxygen. The lack of movement. The parasite needed to breathe, to spread.

By sealing her, Dale was suffocating the infestation.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** *Non-Lethal Containment Achieved. Partial Protocol Completion.*

**POINTS AWARDED:** *50 Treatment Points.*

**THOROUGHNESS RATING:** *6.5.*

Dale stepped back. He was breathing hard. Blood dripped from his shoulder.

The figure slumped against the wall. The suit was broken. The mask was cracked.

"You killed her," the figure said.

"No," Dale said. He pointed to the monitor.

The heart rate was slowing. The black tendrils were retreating deeper into the body, forming a dormant cocoon. The woman was unconscious, but alive.

"She's in stasis," Dale said. "The parasite is in a dormant state. It's not dead. But it's not spreading."

"That's not a cure," the figure said. Its voice was weak. "That's a time bomb."

"It's a containment unit," Dale said. "And it's documented."

He pulled out his pen. He wrote the final entry in the Evidence Log.

**SUBJECT:** *Host-Parasite Hybrid.* **STATUS:** *Contained. Not Eliminated.* **RECOMMENDATION:** *Transfer to Authority Quarantine Facility immediately.*

Dale looked at the figure.

"Now," Dale said. "You're going to open that door. And you're going to call the Authority to come pick up your mess."

The figure didn't move.

"If you don't," Dale said, "I'll report the breach of containment to the Licensing Authority. I'll include your permit number. Which, by the way, I found on your lapel when I swung the clipboard."

The figure went still.

Dale smiled. It was a thin, tired smile.

"You don't have a license, do you?"

The figure stood up. It walked to the door. It punched a code into the panel. The door slid open.

Beyond it, a hallway. Dark. Silent.

"Go," Dale said.

The figure stepped out. It didn't look back.

Dale waited until the door closed.

Then he slumped against the pod. His legs gave out. He slid to the floor.

The pain in his shoulder was a dull roar now. His hands were shaking.

He looked at the clipboard. The page was stained with black fluid. The tape was unraveling on the woman's chest.

He pulled out a fresh pen.

He wrote: **PENDING: CLASS III LICENSING EXAMINATION.**

**STATUS: PRACTICAL COMPONENT COMPLETE.** **THEORETICAL COMPONENT: PENDING.**

A notification pinged.

**AUTHORITY NOTIFICATION:** *Inspection Passed. Advancement to Class III Approved.*

**NEXT TASK:** *Report to Central Hub. New Infestation Detected: Sub-Level 4.*

Dale stared at the message.

Sub-Level 4?

He hadn't been in a basement. He had been in a medical lab.

He looked at the vents. They were open now. The cold air was gone.

Something was crawling in the ducts. A wet, slithering sound.

Dale closed his eyes. He listened.

It wasn't a rat. It wasn't a beetle.

It sounded like thousands of legs, moving in unison.

He opened his eyes. He picked up the clipboard.

He stood up.

The door at the other end of the hallway opened.

A man in a suit stepped out. Not black.

Gray.

Crisp. Professional.

He held a briefcase.

"Mr. Kowalski," the man said. "My name is Agent Vane. I'm from the Audit Division."

Dale tensed. "I passed."

"You passed the practical," Vane said. He stepped into the room.

He looked at the sealed woman.

He looked at the black fluid on the floor.

"But you violated Protocol 12-A: Destruction of Evidence."

Dale looked at the woman. "She's evidence."

"She's a hazard," Vane said. "And you're not authorized to contain hazards. You're authorized to exterminate them."

Vane opened the briefcase.

Inside was a gun. Not a firearm. A nozzle. A pressurized injector.

"Your license is suspended," Vane said. "Until further notice."

Dale looked at the nozzle. He looked at Vane.

"You can't revoke a license without a hearing," Dale said.

"We're holding the hearing now," Vane said. He aimed the nozzle at Dale's chest. "Defense."

Dale raised his clipboard.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** *Hostile Actor Detected. No Protocol Available.*

Dale looked at the Field Guide. He looked at Vane.

He didn't see a monster. He saw a bureaucrat.

And he knew exactly how to deal with those.

Dale tapped his pen on the clipboard.

"File a complaint," Dale said.

Vane pulled the trigger.
