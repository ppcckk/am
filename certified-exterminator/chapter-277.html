Chapter 277: Swallowing the Bullet

# Chapter 277

The first egg hatched in the radial artery.

Dale felt the pressure build behind his left eye, a wet, popping sensation that had nothing to do with sinus congestion and everything to do with biological invasion. His vision blurred, the edges of the suburban street warping into a heat haze. He could hear them. A high-frequency chittering, like cicadas trapped in a jar, emanating from his own wrist.

Miller was already retreating, her boots scraping against the asphalt, but she hadn't gone far enough. The briefcase was gone. The street was empty. The silence was absolute, save for the wet crunch of chitinous shells splitting open inside his flesh.

Don't look at me.

The thought wasn't his. It was the protocol. The instinct. The fourteen years of training that had wired his brain to prioritize containment over comfort.

His left hand spasmed. The fingers splayed wide, rigid, locking into a claw.

Black fluid, thick and viscous, oozed from the pores of his palm, steaming in the night air.

It wasn't blood. It was larval slurry.

Dale didn't scream. Screaming wasted oxygen. He needed air.

He reached for his utility belt with his right hand. The movement was jerky, wrong. His left arm was no longer responding to neural commands; it had become a vessel. A carrier.

The System alert pulsed in his peripheral vision, red and urgent.

**SYSTEM ALERT: INTERNAL INFESTATION DETECTED.** **SPECIES: Rattus profundus (Variant).** **LOCATION: Bloodstream.** **THREAT: CRITICAL.**

He ignored the threat level. The threat level was irrelevant. The location was the problem.

If the eggs were in the blood, they were systemic. If they were systemic, he couldn't cut them out. He couldn't poison them without killing himself. He had to isolate the carrier.

Miller froze ten feet away, her hand hovering over her sidearm.

"Dale?"

"Don't shoot," Dale said. His voice sounded distorted, vibrating through a throat that was rapidly swelling with foreign mass. "Shooting will rupture the host integrity. You'll spread the infestation."

"You're... you're changing."

"I am undergoing a rapid, unauthorized metamorphosis," Dale corrected, his tone dry, clipped. "It is a violation of the Host Containment Act. Section 4, Paragraph B."

He looked at his left arm, and the skin was bubbling. Small, dark bumps were rising to the surface, breaking the epidermis.

They weren't growing larger.

They were growing teeth.

A single, translucent fly wing erupted from his knuckles, tearing through the dermis with a wet snap.

Pain hit him then. Sharp, electric, white-hot.

He gritted his teeth, tasting copper, and the pain was useful.

Pain told him where the nest was. Pain told him what needed to be excised.

He slammed his left hand against the hood of Miller's cruiser. The metal dented. The vibration traveled up his arm, shaking the eggs loose in his veins. A few popped, spraying a mist of black eggs onto the car's paint.

Miller flinched.

"Get off it!"

"I need a heat source," Dale said. He couldn't turn. His neck was stiffening, the vertebrae fusing as the parasite took hold of the skeletal structure.

"Lighter. Now."

Miller's hand shook as she pulled a Zippo from her jacket pocket. She flicked it open. The flame danced, small and orange.

Dale lunged.

It wasn't a graceful movement.

It was a lunge of desperation, driven by the panic of the prey animal that knows it is about to be eaten. He grabbed Miller's wrist. Not to hurt her.

To hold still.

The flame touched his left hand.

The skin ignited. Not with fire, but with a chemical reaction. The black eggs, exposed to the heat, boiled. The smell was instant and overpowering—burning hair, sulfur, and the sweet, cloying scent of rotting fruit.

Miller screamed, pulling back, but Dale held on. He squeezed the flame directly against his palm. The chittering in his ears rose to a shriek.

**SYSTEM ALERT: LOCALIZED THERMAL TREATMENT APPLIED.** **EFFICACY: 12%.** **CROSS-CONTAMINATION WARNING: THERMAL PROTOCOLS ARE NOT RATED FOR RATTUS PROFUNDUS (VARIANT).** **PENALTY: -40% DAMAGE TO TARGET. FIELD GUIDE ENTRY CORRUPTED FOR 24 HOURS.**

Dale read the penalty. He didn't care.

The penalty meant the Authority knew he was using the wrong tool, which meant he was desperate. But it also meant the system recognized the action as a treatment protocol.

He wasn't just fighting. He was working.

The boiling eggs burst under the heat, spraying hot slime onto the cruiser's hood. The chittering slowed. The pressure behind his eye eased, just a fraction.

Miller kicked him in the ribs. Hard.

Dale staggered back, clutching his ruined hand. The skin was gone. Underneath, the muscle fibers were woven with black threads, pulsing. The eggs were dead, but the structure remained.

"You're infected," Miller said, her face pale, eyes wide. "You need a hospital."

"I need an inspector," Dale said. He dropped to one knee, panting. The world tilted. "And I need you to call it in."

"Call who? The police?"

"The Authority."

Miller stared at him.

"The Authority isn't a person, Dale.

It's a database.

A force of nature.

It doesn't take calls.

"

Dale looked at his hand. The black threads were spreading, moving up his forearm. He could feel them knitting themselves into his radius, replacing the bone with chitin.

He had minutes. Maybe seconds.

"If I don't document this, it doesn't count," Dale said.

He spoke to the air, to the invisible observers that watched every move he made.

"If it's not on the record, it's not an extermination. It's a murder.

And if it's a murder, the Authority will purge the witness.

"

He pointed his good hand at the hood of the car, at the splatter of dead eggs and burnt flesh.

"Write it down, Miller. 'Unauthorized dimensional entry via vector-borne pathogen. Source: Hostile agent. Status: Partially contained.'"

Miller's hands were shaking so hard she nearly dropped the lighter. She pulled out her phone.

"I'm calling 911."

"If you call 911, I die," Dale said. His voice was getting deeper, more resonant.

His jaw was unhinging further, the mandibles clicking against his teeth.

"The Authority doesn't punish infestations. It punishes negligence. If you treat this as a medical emergency, you are negligent.

You are failing to follow protocol.

"

Miller looked at the phone, then at Dale. The creature that used to be her partner.

"What do you want me to do?"

"Identify the vector." Dale pointed to the empty space where the briefcase had been. "Who sent it?"

Miller lowered the phone, and she looked at the scorch marks on the car, then at the black sludge.

She took a deep breath, forcing herself to calm down. She was a cop.

She knew how to write reports.

"Vector unknown," she said, her voice trembling but clear. "Suspected Authority agent. Method: Subdermal egg deposition. Containment: Thermal disruption."

Dale felt the air around him shift. The pressure lifted. The System notification blinked.

**REPORT FILED.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 6.4.** **NOTE: SLOPPY WORK.**

A 6.4. Not good enough. But it was on the record. It was a job.

The black threads in his arm stopped spreading. For now.

Dale stood up. He felt lighter, but not free. He felt watched.

Miller backed away again, holding her phone like a shield.

"Dale. Your face."

He touched his jaw. It was hard. Smooth. Like polished stone.

"Stay back," Dale said.

He looked down at his left hand. The black threads were retreating, sinking deeper into the muscle, hiding. They hadn't died. They had gone dormant.

**SYSTEM ALERT: DORMANCY DETECTED.** **NEW MECHANIC UNLOCKED: HOST ASSIMILATION.** **STATUS: UNSTABLE.**

A new window opened in his vision. A Field Guide entry.

**SPECIES: Rattus profundus (Variant - 'The Carrier').** **WEAKNESS: High-frequency sonic vibration disrupts larval cohesion. Low-frequency rumbling stabilizes the host.** **DAMAGE BONUS: +15% against dimensional rodents if host integrity is maintained above 50%.**

He had a weakness. He had a strength. He was a weapon.

A vehicle engine roared in the distance. Headlights swept across the empty street, blindingly bright.

Miller spun around.

"Here they come."

Dale looked at the headlights. They weren't police cars. They were black SUVs, unmarked, moving in formation.

"The Authority sent a Hunter," Miller whispered. "And then they sent me."

Dale realized it then. The briefcase wasn't a gift. It was a test.

He hadn't passed. He had barely survived.

And now the Authority was sending the cleanup crew.

Not to save him. To see if he was useful enough to keep, or if he was just another piece of waste to be incinerated.

He looked at Miller.

"Drive," Dale said.

"What?"

"Drive. Get to the depot. We need to seal the perimeter."

"The depot is three miles away. You're... you're a monster, Dale."

"I am a technician," Dale said. His voice was no longer human. It was a harmonic resonance, vibrating in his chest. "And we are in violation."

The SUVs screeched to a halt at the end of the block. Doors opened. Men in hazmat suits stepped out, carrying rifles that glowed with blue energy.

They weren't aiming at Miller. They were aiming at Dale.

Dale looked at the leader of the squad. He recognized the insignia on their chests.

It wasn't the Authority's square, which was the logo of Pest Control Unlimited. His old employer. The company he had left ten years ago for better benefits.

They were here.

Miller got into the driver's seat of the cruiser.

"Get in."

Dale didn't move. He looked at the hazmat team. He looked at his ruined hand. He looked at the black SUVs.

If he got in the car, he was a fugitive. If he stayed, he was a target.

But there was a third option, and he reached into his pocket and pulled out the folded paper. The one that said *PROVE YOU ARE NOT THE INFESTATION.*

He unfolded it. The text was gone.

In its place was a new directive, written in blood-red ink that sizzled on the page.

**SUBJECT: KOWALSKI, DALE.** **ACTION: EVIDENCE COLLECTION.** **TARGET: PEST CONTROL UNLIMITED.**

Dale laughed. It was a horrible, clicking sound.

Pest Control Unlimited. They had hired him when the Rift opened. They had taken the contract for the county. They had sent the briefcase.

They were the infestation.

Miller revved the engine.

"Dale! Move!"

He didn't move toward the car. He moved toward the SUVs.

He walked with a heavy, lurching gait, his left arm dragging like a broken branch. The hazmat team opened fire. Blue bolts of energy struck him. They burned, searing his skin, but the black threads under his flesh absorbed the impact, hardening like armor.

Dale didn't run. He didn't fight. He walked.

He stopped ten feet in front of the leader. The man raised his rifle again.

Dale raised his left hand. The black threads erupted, forming a crude, jagged shield of chitin and bone.

"I am filing a complaint," Dale said. His voice boomed, shaking the windows of the nearby houses. "Regarding unauthorized interference with municipal services."

The leader hesitated. He looked at Dale's face, at the mandibles, at the eyes that were now solid black.

"Subject is non-compliant," the leader said into his radio. "Requesting termination."

"No," Dale said. "Requesting inspection."

He slammed his chitinous shield forward. Not at the man. At the ground.

The pavement cracked.

From the fissure, hundreds of black eggs poured out. They weren't from Dale. They were from the earth. They had been there all along, planted by the briefcase, waiting for the host to wake up.

The hazmat team screamed as the eggs swarmed over their boots.

Dale stood in the center of the swarm, untouched. The eggs parted around him, recognizing the signal. Recognizing the host.

He was not the infestation, and he was the hive.

Miller watched from the car, her face pressed against the glass, tears streaming down her cheeks. She saw Dale turn to her. He raised his good hand, his human hand, and pointed to the depot.

*Go.*

Then he turned back to the terrified men in hazmat suits.

He didn't attack them. He didn't kill them.

He wrote, and he grabbed the leader's clipboard from his belt. He took a pen from the man's pocket. And in the middle of the battlefield, with eggs crawling up his legs and energy bolts sparking off his shield, Dale Kowalski wrote a violation report.

**VIOLATION CODE: K-9.** **ISSUE: UNAUTHORIZED NESTING SITE.** **LOCATION: PUBLIC THOROUGHFARE.** **ACTION REQUIRED: IMMEDIATE EXCISION.**

He tore the sheet off the clipboard and slapped it onto the leader's chest.

The man looked down at the paper. Then he looked at Dale.

For the first time, the fear in the man's eyes wasn't of Dale's monster form, which was of the paperwork.

Dale looked at Miller's car, then at the swarming eggs rising from the ground. The swarm was growing. It was connecting with the Rift. The ground beneath the street was shifting, the asphalt buckling as the underground tunnels expanded.

He couldn't stop it. Not with heat. Not with bullets.

He needed a bigger tool, and he needed Level II certification.

And he couldn't get it by killing these men. He couldn't get it by running away.

He could only get it by completing the job.

The job wasn't extermination, and the job was migration.

Dale looked at the horizon, where the sky was turning purple with the Rift's energy. He looked at the swarm.

He had to lead them.

He had to become the Queen.

Miller's taillights flashed as she sped away, leaving him behind.

Dale stood alone in the street. The hazmat team fired, but the eggs absorbed the shots. The swarm grew thicker, darker, a living carpet spreading out from his feet.

He felt a connection snap into place. A hive mind, cold and sharp and efficient.

He knew where every egg was. He knew where every larva was.

And he knew that the Authority was watching.

The notification appeared, bright and undeniable.

**CERTIFICATION EXAMINATION INITIATED.** **TEST TYPE: COMMAND.** **PASS CONDITION: GUIDE THE SWARM TO THE SOURCE. DO NOT LOSE A SINGLE UNIT.**

Dale looked at the source. The Rift was miles away, but the pull was undeniable. It called to him. It called to them.

He took a step. The swarm followed.

He took another. The swarm flowed like water, a black river of death moving down the suburban street.

He wasn't fighting the infestation anymore, and he was the infestation's manager.

And he had a deadline.

The last hazmat officer raised his rifle, aiming at Dale's head.

Dale didn't flinch. He looked at the man, and he smiled. A wide, too-wide smile that showed too many teeth.

"Next chapter," Dale said. "Is going to be a nightmare for you."

The officer pulled the trigger.

The bullet hit Dale's forehead, and he didn't die.

He just opened his mouth, and swallowed the bullet whole.

**SYSTEM ALERT: EVIDENCE SECURED.**
