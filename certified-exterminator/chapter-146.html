Chapter 146: Invalid Syntax

# Chapter 146

The moth swarm bit.

Not with teeth. With ink.

Tiny, jagged shapes of black liquid splashed against Dale's forehead, stinging like acid rain. He didn't flinch.

His hands moved with the muscle memory of fourteen years in cramped crawlspaces.

One hand held the clipboard steady against his chest. The other gripped the broken handle of his shovel, using the jagged metal edge to scrape the moths off his jacket.

*Scrape. Write. Scrape. Write.*

The Chief Examiner's voice echoed in the void, layered and wet.

"Code 12-A is the prohibition against auditing the auditor, Dale. You are not a pest. You are a compliance officer who has gone rogue."

Dale ignored the title. He focused on the data stream scrolling in his peripheral vision.

**ERROR: TARGET CLASSIFICATION UNKNOWN.** **ERROR: SPECIES NOT IN FIELD GUIDE.** **ERROR: PROTOCOL MISMATCH.**

The moths weren't biological. They were bureaucratic constructs. Fragments of rejected reports, denied claims, and stamped rejections given life by the Authority's own anxiety. Fighting them with physical force was a waste of Treatment Points.

He needed a classification.

"Identify!" Dale shouted into the swirling dark.

A single moth landed on his clipboard. It unfolded its wings. The paper was thin, translucent, and covered in tiny, screaming faces.

Dale squinted. He didn't attack. He inspected.

"Sub-dermal infestation," he muttered, his pen moving rapidly. "Larval stage of the Red Tape. Feeds on procedural uncertainty. Habitat: Bureaucratic limbo."

**IDENTIFICATION COMPLETE.** **SPECIES: Bureaucratic Moth (Insectum formicae).** **WEAKNESS: Certainty.**

A green light flashed in his vision.

**FIELD GUIDE UPDATED.** **TARGET DAMAGE BONUS: 40%.**

The moths hesitated. Their erratic flight patterns synchronized for a microsecond.

Dale slammed his shovel into the ground. The vibration traveled through the floor of the void.

"Certainly," Dale said, his voice flat and loud. "This is a Class 3 infestation of non-corporeal filing errors. I am authorizing immediate fumigation."

The Chief Examiner's throne reformed from the chaos. The figure was larger now, draped in robes made of shredded contracts. Its face was a shifting mosaic of stamp impressions.

"You cannot fumigate an idea, Kowalski," the Examiner boomed.

"I can fumigate the medium it travels on," Dale replied.

He pulled a spray can from his belt. It wasn't pesticide.

It was a high-concentration sealant he'd modified after the Dimensional Vermin incident.

"Clears the air. Removes the particulate matter that allows you to exist in this dimension."

He pressed the nozzle.

A cloud of thick, white mist erupted from the can. It didn't smell like chemicals. It smelled like old paper and dust.

The moths shrieked. High-pitched, digital screams.

They dropped from the air, dissolving into sludge before they hit the ground.

The Chief Examiner stood. The crown of red stamps rattled.

"Violation Code 88-C," the Examiner said. "Unauthorized use of environmental controls in a sealed jurisdiction."

**ALERT: PENALTY APPLIED.** **DEBUFF: CONTAMINATION OF FIELD GUIDE (24 HOURS).** **TREATMENT POINTS: -10.**

Dale frowned. The penalty stung more than the physical attack.

His Field Guide flickered.

The entry for *Rattus profundus* turned gray, and he couldn't look up the rat's habits for a day.

That was the cost.

But the moths were gone. The vortex of paper had collapsed into a puddle of black ink.

Silence returned to the tunnel.

Except for one sound.

Footsteps.

Heavy, uneven, dragging.

From the shadows to Dale's left, a figure emerged. It wasn't a monster. It was a man.

He wore a suit that had been patched with duct tape and rawhide. A clipboard hung around his neck, chained to his belt. His eyes were hollow, sunken deep into his skull. He held a pen like a dagger.

"You used the sealant," the man said. His voice was raspy, like gravel in a blender.

Dale lowered his shovel.

"Who are you?"

The man stepped into the light. A nameplate pinned to his chest read: **GARY. LEVEL IV. SPECIALIST IN ABSTRACT INFESTATIONS.**

"I'm the one who cleans up your messes," Gary said. He looked at the puddle of ink. "You just dissolved a Level 5 Abstract Entity. Do you know what that does to the local economy?"

Dale checked his HUD. The ink puddle was sizzling.

**JOB COMPLETE.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 6.2.** **POINTS AWARDED: 15.** **PENALTY: -10.** **NET: +5.**

It was a bad score. Sloppy work. But it was alive.

Gary walked over and dipped a finger into the ink. He tasted it.

"Shoddy containment," Gary said. "You didn't isolate the perimeter. You just burned it out.

Now the residue will spread to the adjacent sectors. The Ant-Hive in Sector 4 is going to have a population spike by morning.

"

"I had a deadline," Dale said.

"Excuses are Code 12 violations," Gary snapped. He pulled out a small notebook. "I'm logging you for negligence. I'm filing a complaint with the Oversight Committee."

Dale felt a cold sweat prickle his neck. Not fear. Anger.

"I followed protocol," Dale said.

"Identify. Document. Eliminate.

Verify.

I verified."

"You violated the Verify step," Gary pointed with his pen.

"You didn't check for secondary hosts. You didn't check for latent eggs. You just sprayed.

That's not extermination. That's demolition.

"

Gary looked up. His eyes narrowed.

"Do you know who I am, Dale?"

"I know you're a bureaucrat."

"I'm the Regional Supervisor for the Ohio Valley District," Gary said.

"And you're a liability.

You're too fast. You're too aggressive.

You're not thorough enough.

"

Gary snapped his notebook shut.

"The Authority is pulling your license."

Dale blinked. "What?"

"Temporary suspension," Gary said, though his tone suggested it was permanent. "Pending a full audit of your last twenty jobs. You're being reassigned to Data Entry."

Data Entry.

The lowest tier. The most boring. The most dangerous for a man whose mind worked in patterns and protocols.

"I have a Thoroughness Rating of 9.3," Dale said.

"Your rating just dropped to 6.2," Gary countered. "And your Field Guide is contaminated. You're useless to us right now. The Authority doesn't keep broken tools."

Gary turned away.

"Go to the Processing Center.

Report to Supervisor Halloway.

Don't touch the bugs. Don't touch the doors. Just type.

"

"Wait," Dale said. He stepped forward. "There's something else."

Gary stopped. He didn't turn around.

"What?"

"The Chief Examiner," Dale said. "It said I was the reason the system is failing. It said I'm treating the System itself as a pest."

Gary went still. The air around him seemed to thicken.

"Don't repeat that," Gary whispered.

"Why?"

"Because it's true."

Gary turned back. His face was pale.

"Look at your Field Guide, Dale. Look at the first entry."

Dale looked. The entry for *Rattus profundus* was grayed out. But below it, a new line had appeared. It hadn't been there before.

**SUBJECT: THE AUTHORITY.** **STATUS: ACTIVE INFESTATION.** **CLASS: IX.**

Dale's breath hitched.

**CLASS IX.**

That was beyond the highest certification level. That was theoretical. That was the stuff of nightmares.

"You didn't put that in your guide," Gary said.

"I didn't write it," Dale said. "The System wrote it."

Gary's hand trembled. The pen clattered against his clipboard.

"That entry is a virus," Gary said.

"It's a self-referencing loop. If you identify the System as a pest, the System tries to exterminate you.

Which makes you a pest.

Which makes the System a pest. It's a paradox, Dale. It breaks the logic.

"

"It's not a paradox," Dale said slowly. "It's a root cause analysis."

Gary stared at him.

For the first time, fear registered in the older man's eyes. Not of Dale. Of what Dale's logic might trigger.

"You need to delete that entry," Gary said. His voice was urgent.

"Right now.

Delete it.

Or they will come for you. Not the Auditor.

Not the Chief Examiner. The Cleaners.

"

"Who are the Cleaners?"

"They're not licensed," Gary said. "They're unregulated.

They don't leave reports, and they don't leave bodies. They just erase the problem.

"

A low hum vibrated through the floor. The walls of the tunnel glowed with a sickly green light.

**WARNING: ANOMALY DETECTED.** **SYSTEM INTEGRITY COMPROMISED.** **INITIATING PURGE PROTOCOL.**

The tunnel folded in on itself. The paper vortex reformed, but this time, it wasn't swirling outward. It was swirling inward, toward Dale.

"You have to choose," Gary shouted over the rising wind. "Delete the entry and survive as a drone, or keep it and fight a war against the architecture of reality!"

Dale looked at the Field Guide, and he looked at the green light consuming the tunnel.

He looked at his clipboard.

If he deleted the entry, he would be safe. He would go to Data Entry. He would live. He would type numbers into boxes for the rest of his life.

If he kept it, he would be hunted by things that didn't have names.

But if he kept it, he would know, and he would know that the monsters weren't the disease. They were the symptom. The Authority was the infection.

Dale's thumb hovered over the 'Delete' button on his mental interface.

The wind screamed.

Gary grabbed his shoulder.

"Do it, Dale! Before they see it!"

Dale looked at Gary.

"Protocol," Dale said.

He didn't press Delete, and he pressed **Edit**.

He typed a single word next to **THE AUTHORITY**.

**VULNERABILITY.**

The System froze.

**ERROR.** **INVALID SYNTAX.** **CRITICAL FAILURE IMMINENT.**

The green light shattered, and the tunnel went black.

Dale fell.

But before the darkness took him, he heard a voice. Not the Chief Examiner's. Not Gary's.

It was his own voice. But older. Tired.

*Welcome to Class X, Dale.*
