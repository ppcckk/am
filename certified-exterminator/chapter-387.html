Chapter 387: Inspection Has Begun

# Chapter 387

The thing in the doorway didn't have eyes. It had sockets. And from those sockets, the rain poured straight through without splashing, defying gravity to drip upward into the dark ceiling of the alley.

Dale didn't hesitate. He didn't check his flashlight. He didn't check his spray bottle. He checked his notebook.

Martinez was still on the pavement, dry heaving into a puddle of motor oil. The boy's hands shook so hard the clipboard rattled against his teeth.

Dale slapped the side of Martinez's head, not gently.

A sharp crack. Not to hurt him, but to reset his focus.

"Write," Dale said. His voice was flat. The OSHA tone. The tone that said *this is a violation, not a tragedy*.

Martinez looked up, eyes wide and wet.

"I can't. I can't see it."

"Close your eyes.

Listen. Describe the sound.

" Dale stepped back, putting himself between Martinez and the expanding shape.

The suit-man on the floor had stopped moving.

His limbs were folded at angles that made Dale's own knees ache in sympathy. But the new thing was larger. It unfolded from the doorway like a wet sheet being pulled from a dryer, pale and jointed, with too many elbows and no skin to cover them.

**CERTIFICATION STATUS: SUSPENDED.**

The text burned in Dale's peripheral vision, red and pulsing.

**LEVEL X THREAT DETECTED.** **NO PROTOCOLS AVAILABLE.** **MANUAL DOCUMENTATION REQUIRED FOR CREDIT.**

No protocols. Dale felt a cold knot tighten in his stomach. For fourteen years, he had relied on Protocol 4-B for vermin, Protocol 9-A for arachnids, and the heavy-duty solvent cannons for Class-4 intruders. Without a protocol, he was just a man with a clipboard standing in front of a nightmare.

But the System wasn't lying. The Authority didn't care about survival.

They cared about the paperwork.

And if Dale didn't document this, the infestation wouldn't be registered.

It would be treated as a ghost sighting. Unverified.

Unclaimed. Left to rot.

The creature took a step forward. The ground beneath its foot didn't crack; it liquefied. The asphalt turned to grey slurry, sizzling under the touch.

Dale reached into his belt. He didn't have a weapon.

He had a pen.

A high-vis, industrial-grade marker. And a piece of masking tape.

He slapped the tape onto the wet brick wall beside him. Then he wrote on it, in large, blocky letters: **ENTRY POINT: STRUCTURAL BREACH. ORIGIN: UNKNOWN.**

The creature paused. Its head—a smooth, egg-like surface—tilted. It wasn't attacking. It was observing.

"Don't look at it," Dale barked at Martinez.

"Look at the wall. Describe the texture.

Is it mucous?

Is it resin?"

"It's... oily," Martinez whispered, scribbling frantically. "It smells like ozone. And rotten eggs."

"Ozone and sulfur. Good. That's a chemical signature. Write it down."

Dale stepped forward. One step.

Then another, and he kept his distance, maintaining the standard two-meter clearance for unknown biological hazards.

His heart hammered against his ribs, a frantic bird trapped in a cage, but his hand was steady. The pen didn't shake.

The creature's long, spindly leg reached out. It hovered over Dale's head. A drop of viscous fluid dripped from its tip.

Dale didn't dodge. He raised the clipboard.

The drop hit the board. *Splat.*

He looked at the stain. It was black. It bubbled slightly, eating into the cardboard.

"Corrosive," Dale said. "High acidity. pH likely below one."

The creature shrieked. It wasn't a sound; it was a vibration that rattled Dale's teeth. The air around it distorted, heat shimmer rising off its pale form.

**FIELD GUIDE UPDATE PENDING.** **IDENTIFY WEAKNESS TO UNLOCK COMBAT OPTION.**

There was no combat option. There was only the guide. And the guide was empty for this species.

Dale stared at the creature. It moved with a jerky, stop-motion rhythm. It didn't walk; it *twitched*. Each movement was a separate event, disconnected from the last.

*Discontinuity.*

The word popped into Dale's mind. Not magic. Physics.

If it moved in discrete frames, it had latency.

Dale watched the legs. *Tick. Tick. Tick.*

He waited for the third tick.

"Martinez, duck."

Martinez hit the ground. Dale didn't duck. He lunged.

Not at the creature, but at the puddle of liquefied asphalt beneath it. He kicked the slurry.

It splashed upward, hitting the creature's lower joints.

The creature froze.

Not because the acid hurt it. Because the sudden change in its environment broke its rhythm. Its leg, mid-swing, hovered in the air, vibrating.

Dale saw it. The gap.

"System!" Dale shouted. "Log observation: Subject exhibits temporal discontinuity. Movement is frame-based. Latency: 0.4 seconds between actions."

Nothing happened. The red text remained. **SUSPENDED.**

Dale gritted his teeth. He couldn't use a protocol. He couldn't use a skill. He had to force the System to accept the data by creating a result that the Authority could recognize as a *treatment*.

He grabbed a handful of dry sand from a nearby construction pile. He threw it at the creature.

The sand hit the shimmering heat haze and stopped. It didn't hit the creature. It hung in the air, suspended in the temporal stasis.

"Freeze frame," Dale muttered. "You're buffering."

He ran. Not away. Toward the suit-man on the floor.

The suit was dead, but the chest plate was intact.

Dale ripped the insignia off the breastplate. It was made of a conductive alloy, standard for Level IX containment suits.

He threw the insignia into the air.

It passed through the temporal bubble and hit the creature's chest.

The creature twitched. A spark jumped from the insignia to its skin.

The System flashed.

**OBSERVATION: CONDUCTIVE MATERIAL INTERACTION DETECTED.** **SPECIES IDENTIFICATION PROGRESS: 40%.**

It wasn't enough. Forty percent wasn't a kill. It wasn't a containment.

Dale turned back to Martinez.

"The notebook. Give it to me."

Martinez shoved the notebook into Dale's chest. Dale grabbed a marker and wrote directly on the page, tearing the paper.

**HYPOTHESIS: ENTITY IS NOT BIOLOGICAL. ENTITY IS TEMPORAL PARASITE.** **VECTOR: DATA STREAM CORRUPTION.** **TREATMENT: DISRUPT FEEDBACK LOOP.**

Dale looked at the ruined building. The door was gone. The frame was twisted metal. Inside, the darkness pulsed.

The creature wasn't coming out. It was being *pushed* out.

Something was feeding it.

Dale looked at the suit-man. The man's eyes were open. They were white. Blank.

"The suit," Dale said. "Martinez. Is the suit still transmitting?"

Martinez blinked, wiping blood from his lip.

"I... I don't know. The headset was smashed."

Dale crawled toward the corpse. He didn't touch the body. He touched the earpiece, half-shattered but still glowing with a faint blue light.

He pulled it out, and he put it in his own ear.

Static.

Then a voice. Not the Authority's voice. A different one.

Older. Deeper.

Like stone grinding on stone.

*"...error... error... packet loss... re-route through local host..."*

The host. The suit-man.

The creature wasn't an invader. It was a debug tool. A patch.

And it was trying to fix the error. The error was Dale.

**CERTIFICATION STATUS: SUSPENDED.** **REASON: UNLICENSED INTERACTION WITH CORE SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE.**

Dale pulled the earpiece out. His hands were shaking.

If he killed the creature, he killed the patch. The Rift would widen. The

"error" would spread. The whole city would become corrupted data.

But if he didn't kill it, the creature would keep

"fixing" things. It would delete people. It would delete buildings. It would delete *him*.

"You wanted my certification revoked?" Dale said to the air. "You gave me Level X. You gave me the ability to see the code."

He looked at the creature, which was starting to move again. The temporal stutter was fading.

It had learned. It had adapted to the sand and the metal.

It looked at Dale.

**NEW HIERARCHY DETECTED.** **SUBJECT DALE KOWALSKI IS NOW A LIABILITY.**

The word *LIABILITY* hung in the air, glowing red.

Dale smiled. It was a terrifying smile. He knew what to do.

He didn't need a weapon. He needed a counter-agent.

He looked at Martinez.

"Give me your spray bottle."

Martinez hesitated.

"It's just water. It won't do anything."

"It's not about the water. It's about the pressure."

Dale took the bottle. He emptied it. Then he took the empty plastic bottle and held it up.

"System," Dale said. "I am initiating a Cross-Contamination Protocol."

The text flashed.

**WARNING: CROSS-CONTAMINATION PENALTY ACTIVE.** **40% DEBUFF APPLIED.** **FIELD GUIDE ENTRY CONTAMINATED FOR 24 HOURS.**

Dale didn't care about the debuff. He didn't care about the guide.

He aimed the empty bottle at the creature.

"Protocol 12-C: Aerosolized Neutralization. Target: Temporal Instability."

It was a bluff. A blatant, stupid, bureaucratic bluff. Protocol 12-C was for mold spores. It didn't exist for time parasites.

But the System was a bureaucracy. And bureaucracies loved forms.

The creature paused. Its head tilted. It looked at the bottle. It looked at Dale.

The System flickered.

**FORM SUBMITTED: PROTOCOL 12-C.** **VALIDATION CHECKING...**

The creature's leg twitched. The temporal stutter returned, stronger than before.

It was confused. The input didn't match the expected output.

"Come on," Dale whispered. "Process the request."

The creature screamed. The sound shattered the remaining windows of the duplex across the street.

The validation bar in Dale's vision slowed. It crawled toward 100%.

50%.

70%.

The creature lunged. It was faster than before. It closed the distance in a blink.

Dale didn't move. He held the bottle steady.

90%.

The creature's claw reached for Dale's throat. The air around the claw warped, aging the plastic of the bottle. It cracked.

99%.

**VALIDATION COMPLETE.** **PROTOCOL EXECUTED: FALSE.**

Dale felt a jolt in his mind. A shockwave of pure data.

The creature froze. Not in time. In *logic*.

It had received an invalid command. A false protocol.

And for a creature made of code, an invalid command was a fatal exception.

The pale joints locked. The skin turned grey, then black.

It didn't explode.

It *unwound*. The limbs folded in on themselves, collapsing into a singular point of darkness.

Then silence.

The rain fell on the empty spot.

Dale dropped the bottle, and he fell to his knees, gasping for air. His nose was bleeding.

The 40% debuff hit him like a physical blow.

His vision blurred. His muscles went limp.

Martinez crawled over.

"Did you kill it?"

Dale wiped the blood from his lip.

"I filed a violation. It got shut down."

**TREATMENT COMPLETED.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 3.1.** **POINTS AWARDED: 0.**

Zero points.

Dale laughed. It was a dry, hacking cough. Zero points. But the threat was neutralized.

Then the notification changed.

**SYSTEM REBOOTING...** **ERROR: UNAUTHORIZED ENTITY DETECTED.**

The air behind Dale split open. Not a door. A tear.

A hand reached out. Not pale. Not jointed.

Normal. Human. Wearing a grey suit.

And holding a clipboard.

The man stepped out of the rift. He looked like an accountant. Thin, balding, wearing glasses that reflected the rain.

"Mr. Kowalski," the man said. His voice was pleasant. Professional.

Dale scrambled to his feet, backing away.

"Who are you?"

"I'm from the Appeals Board," the man said. He held up the clipboard.

"You submitted a false protocol.

That's fraud.

And you're operating under suspended certification.

"

The man looked at the spot where the creature had died.

"But you also identified the core architecture. That's... unprecedented."

The man smiled. It wasn't a friendly smile.

"We have a new job for you, Dale. The Authority has been ignoring the small pests. The roaches.

The rats. The things in the walls.

"

The man pointed to the rift.

"Now, you're going to exterminate the auditors."

Dale looked at the rift.

Beyond it, he saw not a void, but a room. A large, white room filled with shelves. And on the shelves, jars.

Thousands of jars.

And in each jar, a small, pale creature.

Waiting.

"Pick up your notebook, Dale," the man said. "The inspection has just begun."
