Chapter 65: Not Shaped Like Rat

# Chapter 65

The thing that came through the basement door was not a rat.

It was shaped like one. Wrong proportions.

The tail was too long, segmented like a centipede's, and the head sat too high on the neck.

It moved with a lateral sway that no mammal would produce. Dale saw it for less than a second before it dropped below the doorframe and into the crawlspace gap he had sealed four months ago.

He backed up two steps. Then three. His boot caught the edge of the kitchen counter and he steadied himself on the refrigerator.

His clipboard was in his left hand. His pen was still uncapped. He had not written anything, and could not write anything because he did not know what he was looking at.

The vibration in the floor changed. The thumping got faster. Something was moving through the crawlspace toward the kitchen, toward the gap where his sealant used to be.

Dale looked at the floor. He looked at the gap.

The sealant was gone.

Not broken. Gone.

The caulk he had applied, the expanding foam, the mesh he had layered under it.

All of it had been eaten. The edges of the gap were clean-cut, not torn. Something with precise mandibles had removed his work in strips.

He crouched. He put his flashlight on the floor and angled it into the gap.

The beam hit a wall of chitin.

Not the creature itself.

The wall of the crawlspace had been coated in something. A secretion.

Dark, glossy, ridged in parallel lines like the inside of a termite gallery.

Frass, and he leaned closer. The pellets were small, uniform, cylindrical.

Dark brown.

They were not rodent frass. Rodent frass was irregular, pointed at both ends, scattered. This was arranged in neat columns.

Spaced evenly.

Like someone had placed them.

Like something was leaving a record.

Dale pulled his pen back to the clipboard. He wrote: *Frass pattern: cylindrical, uniform, arranged in columns. Not Rattus profundus.*

The notification appeared at the edge of his vision. Not the usual blue text. Something amber.

FIELD GUIDE ENTRY: UNCONFIRMED. EVIDENCE-BASED IDENTIFICATION IN PROGRESS.

He had never seen that line before, and the Authority had always required visual confirmation. You had to see the creature. You had to document it directly.

That was the rule. That was the whole foundation of the system.

But the system was reading his frass analysis. It was reading his observations about the gnaw pattern, the secretion texture, the structural damage. It was compiling them.

The thumping stopped.

Silence.

Then a sound like wet cardboard tearing, and the crawlspace wall was moving. The secretion was contracting.

Something was pushing through it from the other side.

Dale stood up. He stepped back to the kitchen table.

He put the clipboard down, and he picked up his flashlight and his spray can; the spray was labeled for wood-boring insects. Wrong product.

He knew it was wrong.

He held it anyway because his hands needed something to hold.

The thing came up through the gap.

It was the size of a large dog, and the body was segmented, six legs, the head was rat-shaped but the jaw was wrong. Too many teeth. Arranged in rows like a lamprey's.

The tail dragged behind it, segmented, each section clicking against the linoleum.

It turned toward him. It did not charge. It did not lunge. It stood in the kitchen, cocking its head like a dog reacting to an unfamiliar noise.

Dale wrote on his clipboard. His hand was shaking but his writing was clean. Fourteen years of writing inspection reports in basements and crawlspaces had made his handwriting immune to fear.

*Six legs.

Mandibular rows.

Segmented tail. Cylindrical frass.

Secretion lining consistent with termite gallery architecture. Jaw structure: lamellated, not gnawing.*

The amber text shifted.

EVIDENCE-BASED IDENTIFICATION: COMPLETE. SPECIMEN MATCHED TO FIELD GUIDE ENTRY: *Rattus profundus* (MODIFIED). TARGETED DAMAGE BONUS APPLIED.

The bonus was real. He could feel it.

A clarity in his peripheral vision, like the world had sharpened by one degree.

He knew where to hit it. He knew the weakness. The third abdominal segment from the tail.

That was where the chitin was thin, which was where he had documented the soft tissue in his original entry.

He sprayed.

The wood-borer spray hit the creature's flank and it screamed.

A high, thin sound like a wire being pulled through a hole; the spray was not supposed to work on this. It was the wrong product.

The cross-contamination penalty should have hit him, which did not.

The Authority notification flashed: CROSS-CONTAMINATION WAIVED. REASON: SPECIMEN CLASSIFICATION MATCHES FIELD GUIDE ENTRY. PRODUCT NOT INCOMPATIBLE WITH DOCUMENTED SPECIES.

He had identified it correctly enough that the wrong spray became the right spray, and the Field Guide entry was doing the work. His documentation was the weapon.

The creature thrashed.

It hit the kitchen counter and sent a coffee mug shattering across the floor.

It turned and went back through the gap. Dale did not follow.

He stood in the kitchen and wrote.

*Specimen retreated through structural entry point. Sealant failure: complete. Suggests prior treatment was effective but not permanent. Re-treatment required.*

He was still writing when the notification came.

JOB STATUS: ACTIVE. THOROUGHNESS RATING: PENDING.

He had not completed the job. The creature was in the crawlspace. He could hear it moving. The thumping had resumed, slower now, like something settling in.

He needed to go back down. He needed to find the nest.

He needed to document the colony structure and eliminate it.

Standard protocol. Identify, document, isolate, eliminate, verify.

He picked up his clipboard. He walked to the basement door. He looked at the gap.

He stopped, and the frass was still there. Neat columns. Uniform. Spaced evenly.

He looked at the spacing. He measured it with his eyes. Four centimeters between columns. Consistent throughout the visible area.

He had written that measurement in his original Field Guide entry.

Four centimeters. He had measured it with a ruler.

He had recorded it because it was a diagnostic feature of *Rattus profundus* colonies.

He had written it down because he was thorough.

The frass in this crawlspace was spaced exactly four centimeters apart.

He looked at the secretion on the walls. The ridges were parallel.

The spacing between ridges was consistent, and he measured it.

Three millimeters.

His Field Guide entry said three millimeters.

He looked at the gnaw marks on the doorframe. The angle of the bite. The depth.

The spacing between individual tooth marks. He had documented all of it.

He had written it down because it was a diagnostic feature.

The marks on this doorframe matched his documentation exactly.

Not approximately. Exactly.

Dale set his clipboard on the floor. He sat down on the kitchen tile. He put his back against the refrigerator.

The notification appeared. Not amber this time. Red.

DOCUMENTATION CAUSALITY DETECTED.

He had never seen that line. He had never seen the Authority use red. Red meant the system was flagging something as a structural anomaly. A problem with the system itself.

DOCUMENTATION CAUSALITY: The Authority's licensing framework uses Field Guide entries as ontological templates. Thoroughness Rating above 8.5 qualifies entries for template status. Your entry for *Rattus profundus* has been classified as a template.

Dale read it twice.

The Authority used his documentation to generate reality.

His Field Guide entry was not a description. It was a blueprint. His thoroughness, his precision, his fourteen years of obsessive detail, had made his documentation so complete that the Authority had treated it as authoritative.

Not as a record of what existed.

As a record of what should exist.

The creature in his crawlspace was not a mimic, which was not an adaptation. It was a product. His product.

His entry had spawned it.

He thought about his Thoroughness Rating. 9.2. The Authority had restored it when the reclassification file closed without a completed species field, and the highest in the county. The reason he was qualified for this tier of threats; the reason he had been called to re-verify forty-two entries.

Every entry he had written at a rating above 8.5 was a template.

Forty-two entries. Forty-two potential specimens.

He looked at his clipboard.

The entry for *Rattus profundus* was there.

His handwriting. His measurements.

His diagnostic features. All of it correct. All of it precise.

All of it alive.

The Authority notification appeared again.

ACTION REQUIRED: Amend or retract Field Guide entry for *Rattus profundus*.

He stared at the words.

Retract: the entry would be removed from his Field Guide. The template would dissolve.

The creature would cease to exist.

But his Thoroughness Rating would drop. Every entry he retracted would subtract from his score. He was at 9.2.

If he retracted this entry, he would lose the targeted damage bonus.

He would lose the certification tier it unlocked, and would lose access to the protocols he had built his entire combat capability around.

Amend: he could update the entry.

Correct the documentation. Make it match what the creature actually was than what he had originally recorded, and the Authority's own notification said the creature matched his entry exactly.

There was nothing to correct. The entry was accurate.

The entry was the cause, and he looked at the gap in the floor. The thumping had stopped. The silence was worse.

His phone buzzed.

A text from an unknown number. He almost ignored it.

He almost always ignored texts. But the number was registered in the Authority's contact list. It was dispatch.

The message read: "Kowalski. Your duplex job is flagged.

Authority wants you to hold position.

Do not amend. Do not retract. Stand by for inspection team."

He typed back: "What inspection team."

The reply came in four seconds. "Not human."

He looked at the gap, and he looked at the frass. Four centimeters apart.

His measurement.

His number. His handwriting.

The notification appeared one more time.

AMENDMENT DETECTED.

He had not amended anything. He had not written a word since sitting down. His pen was uncapped but his clipboard was blank except for the frass analysis he had done before the creature appeared.

AMENDMENT DETECTED. REASON: OBSERVATION OF TEMPLATE DIVERGENCE.

SPECIMEN HAS DEVIATED FROM DOCUMENTED PARAMETERS.

UPDATING TEMPLATE.

GENERATING REVISED SPECIMEN.

The thumping started again. Different rhythm. Faster. Wrong.

The crawlspace wall started to crack. The secretion peeled back in strips. Something was pushing through from the other side. Something bigger.

Dale picked up his clipboard. He picked up his pen. He looked at the gap.

The thing that came through was not shaped like a rat. It was shaped like a man.
