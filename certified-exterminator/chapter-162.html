Chapter 162: Authority Enforcer

# Chapter 162

The dirt hit Dale's face first. Grit, wet clay, and the copper tang of old blood.

He didn't scream. Screaming wasted oxygen. He kicked upward.

His boot connected with something soft but resistant. A tendon.

He drove his heel down, feeling the give of muscle fibers tearing under the sole of his work boot.

The thing holding his legs tightened. Not a grip, but a suction. A vacuum seal forming around his calves.

Dale twisted his torso. His shoulder slammed into the packed earth of the tunnel wall. The impact jarred his teeth. He tasted rust.

Above him, the hole in the floor was shrinking.

Sarah and the Other Dale stood at the edge, silhouetted against the flickering green light of the house's rift-stabilizer. They weren't looking down.

They were looking at each other.

The Other Dale was adjusting his cuffs. Sarah was wiping blood from her cheek with a thumb.

"Run," the Other Dale had said.

He wasn't running. He was being consumed.

The pressure on Dale's legs increased. The skin of his jeans stretched, then split.

A cold, slimy secretion oozed onto his shins.

Not water.

Mucus. High-viscosity adhesive.

*Class IV Parasitic Burrower.*

The identification flashed in his peripheral vision, blue text overlaying the darkness of the hole.

**[UNLICENSED INFESTATION DETECTED: SUB-CLASS: DIMENSIONAL VERMIN (Rattus profundus, VARIANT: OBLIVION STRAIN)]** **[STATUS: FUGITIVE. LICENSING REVOKED.]** **[TREATMENT POINTS: 0.]**

The text pulsed red.

Dale ignored it. The system didn't care about his legal status. The system cared about the biology. And the biology was currently trying to digest him.

His hand scrabbled in the dirt. His fingers brushed metal. The crowbar Sarah had dropped. It was three feet away, half-buried in the muck.

Too far.

The Burrower's throat constricted. Dale felt his knee joint pop. Not his knee.

The creature's internal mechanism, locking his legs into place. A biological clamp.

He couldn't kick anymore. The leverage was gone.

Dale reached for his belt.

His utility pouch was still there. The Authority hadn't stripped his gear, only his license. A technicality.

The Authority was bureaucratic, not vindictive.

They revoked the permit, not the tools.

He pulled out a canister of aerosolized neurotoxin. *Protocol 4-C: Rapid Sedation for Canine-Sized Vertebrates.*

It was the wrong tool for a Class IV Burrower. But it was the only tool he had.

He shook the canister. The liquid sloshed. He checked the nozzle. Clogged.

Dale spat on the nozzle. Rubbed his thumb over the aperture. The residue was thick, black. It smeared across his fingerprint.

He aimed upward. Not at the creature holding his legs. That was suicide. He aimed at the tunnel wall to his left.

*Crack.*

A single burst of aerosol hit the packed earth.

Nothing happened. The earth didn't dissolve. The toxin was for nervous systems, not dirt.

But the smell hit the air. Sharp, chemical, acrid.

The Burrower above him paused. The suction on his legs relaxed for a fraction of a second. A flinch. A sensory reaction.

*They hunt by scent,* Dale thought. *Not sight.*

He fired again. And again. Three bursts into the dark.

The Burrower's grip loosened.

Not enough to escape, but enough to shift his weight. Dale twisted his hips. His left leg slid free.

His right was still trapped.

He grabbed the crowbar. His fingers closed around the cold steel.

He didn't swing it at the monster. He slammed the flat end into the tunnel wall, inches above his trapped leg.

*Thud.*

The vibration traveled up the shaft. The Other Dale looked down. His smile faltered.

"Don't," the Other Dale said. "You'll bring the roof down."

Dale hit the wall again. *Thud.*

The tunnel was unstable. The dimensional rift had weakened the structural integrity of the soil. This wasn't just a hole; it was a wound in reality, and the earth around it was necrotic.

Dale hit it a third time.

Cracks spiderwebbed outward from the impact point. The ceiling groaned.

Sarah leaned forward. "Dale, stop! If you collapse it, you're buried!"

Dale didn't look at her. He looked at the cracks. He looked at the geometry of the stress fractures.

He wasn't trying to kill the Burrower. He was trying to isolate the workspace.

*Protocol 1: Containment.*

If he couldn't remove the threat, he had to contain the area. If the roof collapsed, the tunnel would seal. The Burrower would be trapped in a sealed environment with a small human who was running out of oxygen.

It was a terrible plan. It was a code violation. It was the only thing that worked when you were out of options and out of licenses.

He swung the crowbar one last time, aiming for the keystone of the arch above him.

*CRACK.*

The ceiling split.

Dirt poured down. Not a trickle, a avalanche.

Dale curled into a ball. He wrapped his arms around his head. He tucked his chin to his chest.

The weight hit him. Tons of earth. The smell of wet clay filled his nose. His lungs burned.

The suction on his right leg vanished. The Burrower was gone, or buried, or dead. It didn't matter.

Dale dug.

He clawed at the dirt. His fingernails broke. He didn't feel it. He dug with the desperate, rhythmic motion of a man who knows that panic is a luxury and oxygen is a resource.

After thirty seconds of suffocating darkness, he broke through.

He burst into the air of the living room, gasping, coughing up black sludge.

He was on his hands and knees in the center of the room. The hole in the floor was gone, filled with rubble. The rift-stabilizer was shattered. The green light was dead.

The Other Dale was standing ten feet away. He wasn't holding a crowbar anymore. He was holding a clipboard.

A real clipboard. Not the plastic one Dale usually carried. This one was made of black bone, bound in silver.

Sarah was still there. She looked confused, blinking rapidly, as if waking from a fever dream. She looked at her hands. They were empty.

The Other Dale clicked a pen.

"You violated Protocol 1, Subsection 4," the Other Dale said.

His voice was flat.

Bored. "Uncontrolled structural collapse in a residential zone.

No warning signage. No evacuation of non-combatant personnel."

Dale coughed. He wiped his mouth with the back of his hand.

"I saved my life," Dale rasped.

"You compromised the infestation site," the Other Dale said. "The Burrower is not dead.

It is displaced.

And you have contaminated the site with neurotoxin residue. Field Guide entry for *Rattus profundus* is now flagged for corruption."

Dale checked his vision.

**[WARNING: FIELD GUIDE ENTRY CORRUPTED.]** **[PENALTY: -40% EFFECTIVENESS vs. ALL RODENT-CLASS ENTITIES FOR 24 HOURS.]** **[CROSS-CONTAMINATION DETECTED.]**

The red text burned in his eyes.

Dale stood up. His legs shook. He looked at the rubble.

"You're not real," Dale said.

The Other Dale tilted his head. "Define real."

"You're a mimic. A high-level construct."

"I am a Certified Inspector," the Other Dale said. He tapped the clipboard. "And you are an unlicensed individual currently in possession of a Class IV biological hazard."

He pointed the pen at Dale.

"Stay exactly where you are. Do not breathe deeply. The air quality in this room has dropped to 14% oxygen and is laced with spores from the collapsed rift."

Dale held his breath.

Sarah backed away. She looked at the Other Dale, then at Dale. Her eyes were wide.

"Dale," she whispered. "Who is he?"

"He's a violation," Dale said.

The Other Dale smiled. It was a precise, geometric smile. "He's a symptom. And you're the infection."

He turned his head toward the front door.

The door didn't open. It disintegrated.

Wood splintered into sawdust. The frame twisted, warping like hot glass.

Something crawled in.

It wasn't a rat. It wasn't a beetle.

It was a hand. Pale, long-fingered, made of translucent chitin. It pulled itself through the doorway, followed by a torso that was too thin, too many joints.

The creature had no face. Just a smooth, white mask of bone.

It stood up. It was seven feet tall.

**[INFESTATION TYPE: ARCHITECT OF RUIN.]** **[CLASS: VIII.]** **[STATUS: ACTIVE PREDATOR.]**

Dale's heart hammered against his ribs.

Class VIII. He was Level II.

He was a fugitive.

He had no weapons that worked against Class VIII entities.

The Architect raised a hand. It pointed at Dale.

A sound like grinding stones filled the room.

The floor beneath Dale rippled. Not earth. Stone.

The Architect was rewriting the architecture of the house.

Sarah screamed. She threw herself at the Other Dale, tackling him.

The Other Dale didn't stumble. He didn't even look at her. He swatted her away with the clipboard.

She flew backward, hitting the wall. She didn't get up.

Dale looked at the Architect. He looked at the Other Dale.

The Other Dale was smiling.

"Thoroughness," the Other Dale said. "You failed to document the secondary threat. You focused on the primary. That is why you lose."

The Architect's hand moved. The walls of the living room folded inward. The ceiling lowered.

Dale was trapped.

He looked at his pocket. He still had the Nokia.

He pulled it out. The screen was cracked, but it lit up.

He didn't have a signal. But he had the appeal form. Form 7B.

He hadn't signed it. The Other Dale had said that.

*But the Authority doesn't require a signature from a witness if the witness is present.*

The Architect was a witness.

Dale raised the phone.

"Authority!" he shouted. "I invoke Clause 9, Subsection 12: Immediate Witness of Class VIII Entity!"

The air in the room froze.

The Architect stopped. The Other Dale stopped smiling.

**[NOTIFICATION: WITNESS CONFIRMED.]** **[PROCESSING...]** **[APPEAL RE-EVALUATED.]**

The green light in the room flickered back on.

The Other Dale's face went rigid.

"No," the Other Dale said.

Dale stared at the screen.

**[APPEAL GRANTED. LICENSING RESTORED.]** **[PROTOCOL UNLOCKED: CLASS III INTERVENTION.]** **[WARNING: ENTITY IS HOSTILE TO LICENSING AUTHORITY.]**

Dale looked at the Architect.

He didn't have a weapon. But he had a license.

And for the first time in fourteen years, he had the authority to clean up the mess.

He pulled out his spray can. He aimed it at the Architect's chest.

"K-7," Dale said. "Structural entry point."

He fired.

The spray hit the Architect's bone-mask. It hissed.

The Architect shrieked. A sound like tearing metal.

It lunged.

Dale didn't dodge. He stepped into the charge. He jammed the nozzle of the spray can into the Architect's eye socket.

*Click.*

Empty.

The Architect's hand closed around Dale's throat.

The world went black.

Then, a voice in his ear. Not the phone. Inside his head.

*"Mr. Kowalski. Your Thoroughness Rating has dropped to 4.1. You are now subject to termination."*

The Architect squeezed.

Dale smiled.

*"Correction,"* the voice said. *"You are subject to reformatting."*

The Architect's grip loosened.

The creature's head snapped back.

Behind it, the Other Dale was gone.

Standing in his place was a figure made of pure light. Tall. Faceless. Holding a pen.

The Authority.

It looked at Dale.

Then it looked at the Architect.

"Clean this up," the Authority said. "You have three minutes before the house becomes part of the permanent record."

The Architect dropped Dale.

Dale hit the floor. He gasped.

The Authority pointed at the door.

"Outside. Now."

Dale scrambled up. He ran.

He burst through the ruined doorway into the night air.

The street was gone.

The houses were gone.

There was only a vast, white void. And in the distance, a line of black shapes moving toward him.

Thousands of them.

Dale looked at his hands. They were glowing.

Not green.

White.

He looked at his Field Guide.

**[NEW ENTRY: AUTHORITY ENFORCER.]** **[STATUS: TARGET.]**

He wasn't just exterminating monsters anymore.

He was exterminating the people who hired them.
