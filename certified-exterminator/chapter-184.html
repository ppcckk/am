Chapter 184: Undocumented Subject

# Chapter 184

Dale pried the fused spring from the plastic casing, his fingers slick with sweat. Not broke. Snapped.

The spring-loaded clicker of Dale's standard-issue ballpoint had fused to the casing, rendering it useless for the first time in fourteen years of field work.

Dale stared at the plastic carcass in his hand. Across the table, the older Dale—gray-bearded, one-eyed, holding a rusted shovel that looked like it had been pulled from a grave—tapped his own pen against the desk. *Click. Click.

Click.*

The white void didn't have air. It had silence. A thick, cottony silence that pressed against Dale's eardrums until they popped.

"Protocol failure," Dale said. His voice sounded thin, scraped raw.

The older Dale didn't speak. He just watched.

A clipboard rested on the desk between them, but it wasn't blank anymore.

Lines of text scrolled across the surface, fast and frantic, like a printer jamming out error codes. Dale squinted. The text wasn't words.

It was citations. Violation codes.

K-7.

E-12.

B-44. The same codes he'd written on residential inspections in suburban Ohio, but twisted.

*Violation: Subject failed to seal dimensional perimeter.* *Violation: Subject utilized unauthorized containment vessel (trash bag).* *Violation: Subject failed to document psychological impact on bystanders.*

Dale's stomach turned. He looked down at his own clipboard. It remained empty.

The Authority's interface—the glowing blue overlay that usually hovered in his peripheral vision—was gone. Instead, the data was physically manifesting on the desk, etched into the wood grain in black ink.

"You're citing me," Dale said.

The older Dale stopped tapping.

"I am auditing you."

"Based on what standard?"

"Standard 9.2.

High Thoroughness. Low Compliance.

" The older Dale gestured with the shovel. It pointed at Dale's chest.

"You cleared the broodmother.

You documented the entry point. You got the points.

But you didn't clean up the residue.

"

Dale felt a cold itch behind his eyes. The contamination anxiety. It usually hit when he saw frass trails or fecal matter. This felt like seeing his own reflection in a mirror that showed someone else's face.

"I followed protocol," Dale said. He stood up. The chair scraped backward, making no sound.

"Identify. Document.

Isolate.

Eliminate.

Verify. I did all five.

"

"You verified the biologicals," the older Dale said. "You didn't verify the narrative."

The text on the desk shifted. New lines appeared.

*Violation: Subject allowed unlicensed personnel (neighbors, local law enforcement) to witness eradication event without proper briefing.* *Penalty: Social Contamination. Field Guide corruption imminent.*

Dale froze. "Social Contamination?"

The older Dale smiled. It was a dry, cracking thing.

"You think your job is killing bugs, Dale.

You think if you kill the bug, the job is done. But you're not a soldier.

You're a technician.

And technicians manage the environment. The environment includes the people living in it.

"

Dale looked at the scrolling citations, and he recognized them. He remembered the neighbor, Mrs.

Gable, screaming about demons.

He remembered the police officer who had tried to shoot a giant rat and failed. Dale had written them off as noise. Irrelevant to the extermination protocol.

He had focused on the nest.

But the Authority wasn't focused on the nest. It was focused on the aftermath.

"If I didn't brief them..." Dale started.

"Then you created a secondary infestation," the older Dale finished.

"Panic is a vector.

Rumor is a vector. You treated the rats.

You left the fear. The fear grew. It attracted more rifts.

It attracted *us*.

"

The older Dale raised the shovel. It wasn't a weapon. It was a tool. A symbol of manual labor, of digging things out of the ground.

"This is the Examination," the older Dale said. "Not to see if you can kill. To see if you can clean. And right now, Dale Kowalski, your workspace is a disaster."

Dale sat back down.

Slowly. He picked up the broken pen. He picked up the clipboard.

The surface was warm.

The wood grain shifted under his fingers, forming new text.

**[PEER REVIEW INITIATED.]** **[AUDITOR: DALE KOWALSKI (CLASS IX PENDING)]** **[SUBJECT: DALE KOWALSKI (CLASS II ACTIVE)]** **[ISSUE: NARRATIVE CONTAMINATION.]**

A form appeared. It was a standard Incident Report, but the fields were alien.

*Field: Witness Disposition.* *Requirement: Neutralization of Unlicensed Observer.* *Options: [Lethal] [Memory Modification] [Reassignment] [Exposure] [Ignore]*

Dale stared at the options. *Memory Modification* was new.

He'd never seen it before. The Authority didn't do memory work. They did pest control.

Pests didn't remember they were pests.

"I didn't have a way to modify memories," Dale said. "I'm a Class II."

"You have a Field Guide," the older Dale said. "Look at the entry for *Rattus profundus*."

Dale closed his eyes, and he visualized the entry.

He had written it in the crawlspace, in the dark, with a flashlight beam shaking in his hand. He had described the rat's acid glands.

Its territorial aggression. Its nesting habits.

But he hadn't described its *effect* on the human nervous system. He hadn't noted that its squeak triggered a primal flight response in adults. He hadn't noted that its scent lingered in drywall for weeks, causing insomnia and paranoia.

He opened his eyes. The field on the clipboard changed.

*Deficiency: Incomplete Species Profile.* *Remedy: Update Field Guide with Psychological Impact Data.* *Cost: 24 Hours of Active Monitoring. Or... One Peer Review Cycle.*

"One Peer Review Cycle," Dale said. "That's you."

The older Dale nodded.

"Fill out the profile.

Describe the fear.

Not the rat. The fear. If you can't document it, you can't control it.

"

Dale uncapped a different pen. He had a backup in his pocket. A mechanical pencil. He started writing.

*Subject: Rattus profundus.* *Secondary Effect: Acute Paranoia in bystanders.* *Mitigation: Immediate isolation of witness. Provision of mundane explanation (gas leak, structural settling).*

He wrote fast.

His hand cramped. The words felt wrong.

They weren't biological facts, and they were social engineering. This wasn't pest control. This was PR.

"Is this the loophole?" Dale asked, not looking up. "The Authority doesn't care if I kill the monster. It cares if I keep the house livable."

The older Dale said nothing. But the scrolling text on the desk slowed.

**[UPDATE ACCEPTED.]** **[FIELD GUIDE ENTRY REVISED.]** **[NEW MECHANIC UNLOCKED: SOCIAL BUFFERING.]**

Dale stopped writing. He looked at the new line in the system notice.

*Social Buffering: By providing a plausible mundane cause for supernatural events, the Technician reduces the Thoroughness Penalty for Unlicensed Observer exposure by 50%. Cost: Credibility.*

"Credibility," Dale said.

"You have to lie," the older Dale said. "To the neighbors.

To the police.

To yourself.

You have to maintain the fiction that this is normal. Because if the world knows it's an infestation, the infestation wins.

Not because of the monsters. Because of the panic.

"

Dale looked at the broken pen. Then at the mechanical pencil. Then at the older Dale.

"I've been doing this for two weeks," Dale said. "And I've been getting points for killing things. But I've been losing points for lying."

"You haven't been losing points," the older Dale corrected. "You've been accruing debt. Look at your Thoroughness Rating."

Dale looked. The 9.4 was gone. It had been replaced by a number in parentheses.

**[RATING: 0.0 (DEBT: -14.5)]**

Negative. He had negative thoroughness.

He had been sloppy in the ways that mattered to the Authority.

Not the kill. The cover-up.

"I need to fix this," Dale said.

"You can't fix it with a clipboard," the older Dale said.

"You need a crew. You need a chain of command.

You're a Class II.

You're a grunt. To clear this debt, you need to escalate.

"

The older Dale stood up. He walked around the desk. He placed the shovel on the table.

"Next phase," the older Dale said.

Dale looked at the shovel. It was covered in dirt. Black, oily dirt.

"What phase?" Dale asked.

The older Dale pointed to the edge of the white void. The floor ended there.

Beyond it, darkness.

Not empty darkness. *Populated* darkness.

Shadows moved. Shapes.

"Phase Four: Corporate Integration," the older Dale said.

Dale blinked. "What?"

"The Authority isn't just licensing you, Dale. It's hiring you. And right now, you're not the only contractor in the county.

There's a bigger firm. They don't exterminate.

They *manage*, and they profit from the infestation. They keep the rifts open because the maintenance contracts are lucrative.

"

The older Dale leaned in. His one good eye was milky, but sharp.

"Your next job isn't a rat in a wall. It's a competitor in the market. And they have a Level III certification."

Dale's heart hammered against his ribs. He looked at his clipboard. A new notification flashed.

**[CONTRACT ALERT: COMPETITIVE BIDDING.]** **[RIVAL ENTITY: AETERNUM PEST SOLUTIONS.]** **[OBJECTIVE: ACQUIRE LOCAL AUTHORITY LICENSE (RANK: MUNICIPAL)]** **[PENALTY FOR FAILURE: TERMINATION OF CONTRACT.]**

"They want your license," the older Dale said.

"They want your territory. And they're already working the neighborhood.

They're using cheaper protocols.

Sloppier. They're causing more infestations to generate more work.

"

Dale looked at the void. He could hear sounds now.

Sirens.

Distant, faint, but getting louder.

And something else. The hum of machinery.

Industrial-grade sprayers. The kind that cost ten thousand dollars.

"If I lose," Dale said, "what happens?"

"You become part of the infestation," the older Dale said. "You're already contaminated, Dale. Look at your hands."

Dale looked down. His fingers were stained black.

The ink from the desk?

No. It was seeping from under his nails. The black oil.

"I didn't notice," Dale said.

"You didn't document it," the older Dale said.

The white void dissolved. The desk faded. The older Dale remained, but he was becoming transparent.

"Go back," the older Dale said.

"Fix the narrative. Document the fear.

Update the guide.

And then find Aeternum. Before they seal the deal with the City Council.

"

"Wait," Dale said. "Who is the peer?"

The older Dale smiled again. It was sadder this time.

"You are. But you're not alone anymore."

The void collapsed.

Dale gasped, and he was back in the library. The clones were gone.

The Librarian was gone.

He was standing in the middle of the reading room.

The floor was cracked. The ceiling had holes where starlight shone through, even though it was noon.

He looked at his hands. Black oil under the nails.

He looked at his clipboard. It was full. Not blank.

Full of text. He hadn't written it.

It had written itself.

*Subject: Dale Kowalski.* *Status: Active.* *Task: Defend Municipal License.* *Rival: Aeternum Pest Solutions.*

The doors to the library burst open.

Not the front doors. The emergency exits.

Men in yellow hazmat suits walked in. They weren't wearing masks. They wore respirators with glowing blue filters.

They carried tanks on their backs.

Not spray tanks. *Containment units.*

The leader looked at Dale. He held up a tablet.

"Kowalski," the leader said. "Your certification is under review. You're in violation of Code 44: Unlicensed Displacement of Private Contractors."

Dale looked at the men. He looked at the tanks. He looked at the black oil on his hands.

He picked up his pen. It was working again.

He wrote on the blank page of his Field Guide.

*Species: Corporate Parasite.* *Weakness: Public Perception.*

The leader stepped forward.

"Hand over the clipboard, Dale. Or we'll treat this as a hostile infestation."

Dale didn't hand it over. He clicked the pen.

"Protocol," Dale said. "Identify."

The leader's tablet flashed red.

**[IDENTIFICATION FAILED. SUBJECT UNRECOGNIZED.]**

Dale smiled. It was a dry, cracking thing.

"Because I haven't been documented," Dale said. "And you can't exterminate what you can't see."

The leader hesitated. For a second. Just a second.

Dale ran.
