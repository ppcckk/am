Chapter 402: Retrieve and Retry

# Chapter 402

The click came from the left.

Dale didn't turn his head. He adjusted his stance, planting his boots on the slick concrete, and tilted his clipboard. The jar in his pocket burned against his thigh. Heat radiated through the denim, a low, throbbing pulse that matched the rhythm of the thing's chittering.

It was close.

"Rattus profundus," Dale said, his voice flat. "Juvenile. Sub-adult transition."

The voice was wrong. It wasn't Dale's. It was the Authority's text-to-speech, synthesized and cold, echoing in his auditory cortex.

The thing lunged from the darkness.

It moved faster than a rat should. Its limbs were too long, too many joints, snapping like dry twigs. Dale sidestepped. The creature's clawed foot scraped sparks off the tunnel wall, missing his ribs by inches.

He didn't have a spray tank. He didn't have a mask. He had a jagged shard of tempered glass he'd pulled from a broken subway window three hundred yards back.

Dale swung.

The glass caught the creature's underbelly.

It shrieked. Not a squeak. A wet, tearing sound.

Black ichor sprayed across Dale's forearm. He didn't flinch. He watched the blood pool on the concrete, mixing with the rust and the rot.

*Identification confirmed,* the Authority whispered. *Documentation incomplete.*

Dale gritted his teeth. He couldn't afford to stop and write. Not here. Not with the broodmother breeding in the next tunnel and Margaret running toward a trap she didn't know existed.

He kicked the creature's kneecap. It buckled.

Dale dropped his weight, driving his knee into its spine.

The glass shard in his hand was slick, but he held it. He drove it down, into the soft tissue behind the skull.

The creature went limp.

Dale pulled the glass out. He wiped it on his pants.

*Elimination complete. Verification pending.*

He looked at the body. It was small, no bigger than a house cat, but the anatomy was all wrong. The ribcage was external, plated with chitin. The eyes were vestigial, sunken pits.

He didn't have time to measure the carapace. He didn't have time to photograph the frass trail.

But he had the jar.

Dale reached into his pocket. The eye inside was blinking. Fast. Agitated.

He uncapped the jar.

The eye rolled in its gel, fixing on the dead rat.

"Field Guide update," Dale muttered.

The Authority's interface flickered in his peripheral vision. A new line appeared in the *Rattus profundus* entry.

**Weakness Discovered: Neurotoxin Vulnerability via Ocular Pheromone Mimicry.**

Dale stared at the text, and he hadn't analyzed the pheromone. He hadn't run a chemical breakdown. He had just brought a severed eye from a previous kill.

*Loophole accepted,* the Authority noted. *Improvised documentation accepted. Thoroughness Rating adjustment: -0.5. Reason: Unsanitized sample.*

-0.5.

Dale felt the hit in his chest.

It wasn't physical. It was a tightening, a constriction in his lungs.

His rating was a ladder he'd spent years climbing, and he couldn't afford to slip, not now. Not when the Inspector was watching. Not when the backlog was growing.

He capped the jar. The eye settled.

"Move," Dale said to himself.

He walked over the carcass. He didn't step around it.

He stepped on it, which was a hazard.

It needed to be bagged, but he didn't have bags. He'd leave it.

If the Authority wanted a report, they could come get it.

The tunnel narrowed.

The air grew thicker. The smell changed.

Rust and rot were gone. Replaced by something sweet and cloying.

Ammonia.

Dale stopped, and he pulled out his clipboard. He wrote one word.

*OZONE.*

No.

He crossed it out.

*AMMONIA.*

High concentration. Likely byproduct of rapid metabolism.

The broodmother was close, and he heard the breathing before he saw the source.

It wasn't a breath. It was a venting. A rhythmic expulsion of gas from pores in the walls.

The walls were pulsing.

Not the concrete. The *organic* matter growing over the concrete. Fungal mats, thick and gray, veined with purple. They covered the tracks, the signals, the support beams.

Dale raised his flashlight.

The beam cut through the haze.

At the end of the tunnel, the tracks split. Three tunnels. Three entrances.

And in the center of the split, a mound, which was massive. A hill of twitching flesh and chitin, easily twenty feet high. It shifted, rippling.

From the mound, things poured out.

Not rats. Not beetles.

Larvae.

Translucent, slug-like creatures, each the size of a football. They crawled over the mound, over each other, into the three tunnels.

Dale counted.

One hundred.

Two hundred.

Three hundred, and he did a quick mental calculation.

If he killed one, the Authority awarded points. If he killed three hundred, he got three hundred points.

But he had no tools. No containment grid. No bait stations.

And he had one jar.

One eye.

Dale looked at the jar, and the eye was staring at the mound.

It wasn't blinking anymore. It was locked on the central mass.

*Target identified: Dimensional Broodmother (Provisional).*

Dale frowned.

Provisional?

That wasn't a class. That wasn't a level.

The Authority didn't use that term, and he looked at the mound again.

A face pushed out of the flesh.

Not a monster face.

A human face.

Pale. Distorted. Stretched over too many bones.

Margaret.

Dale's grip on the glass shard tightened until his knuckles turned white.

"Margaret," he said.

The face on the mound didn't move. But the voice did.

It came from everywhere.

From the walls. From the larvae. From the air itself.

"Dale."

The sound was wrong. It was layered. A chorus of thousands of voices speaking her name.

"Dale," the mound said. "Come closer."

Dale didn't move, and he opened his Field Guide. He flipped to the *Rattus profundus* page. He added a new entry.

**Host Integration: Humanoid.**

He wrote it fast. His handwriting was shaky.

*Hypothesis: Broodmother is not a singular entity. It is a swarm intelligence utilizing a human host as a processing node.*

He didn't know if that was true. He hoped it was.

If the broodmother was using Margaret, then Margaret wasn't dead. She was part of the infestation.

And if she was part of the infestation, she could be treated.

Or she could be removed.

Dale looked at the jar.

If he threw the eye into the mound, would it disrupt the pheromone network? Would it break the swarm's cohesion?

Or would it just make him look like a fool?

*Cross-contamination penalty warning,* the Authority flashed. *Protocol mismatch detected. Do not deploy non-standard agents against non-standard threats without full analysis.*

Dale ignored the warning, and he had to act.

He couldn't fight the larvae. He couldn't fight the mound.

But he could change the environment.

He looked at the ammonia smell, and he looked at the fungal mats.

Ammonia was basic. The fungal mats were acidic. They were reacting.

Dale pulled the cap off the jar, and he didn't throw the eye.

He held the jar up. He tilted it.

The gel spilled out. The eye slid out, wet and slimy, and landed on the concrete.

The eye rolled.

It didn't move. It just sat there, staring up at the mound.

Dale stepped back, and he waited.

The larvae stopped moving.

The mound shuddered.

The face of Margaret turned. It looked at the eye.

For a second, the chorus of voices went silent.

Then, a single voice. Clear. Human.

"Dale?"

It wasn't the swarm. It was her.

Dale's breath hitched, and he took a step forward.

"Stay back," Dale said. "I'm running a diagnostic."

"You're going to kill me," Margaret said.

"I'm going to exterminate the parasite," Dale said.

"You're going to kill *me*," she said.

The mound surged.

A tendril of black slime shot out, snatching the eye off the ground. It pulled it into the flesh.

The mound screamed.

A high, piercing shriek that shattered the remaining glass in the tunnel windows.

Dale covered his ears, and he didn't retreat. He watched.

The mound was convulsing. The fungal mats were turning black. The larvae were writhing, curling into balls.

*System Alert,* the Authority said. *Host rejection detected. Neurotoxin surge. Critical instability.*

The eye wasn't a weapon. It was a key.

It had unlocked something in the broodmother's biology. A flaw. A rejection response.

The mound was tearing itself apart.

Dale looked at the three tunnels, and the larvae were dying. They were liquefying.

But the face of Margaret was still there.

It was melting.

The skin was sloughing off, revealing the wet, red muscle beneath. The eyes were bursting. The mouth was stretching wider, wider, until the jaw unhinged.

But the voice was still clear.

"Dale! The code! K-7!"

Dale froze.

K-7.

Structural entry point.

The duplex. The first job. The first mistake.

He hadn't sealed it properly. He had patched it. He had put a bandage on a cancer.

And now the cancer was here.

Margaret was screaming about the code.

Not the monster's code.

The building code.

Because she was still a technician. Even inside the monster. Even inside the swarm.

Dale looked at the crumbling mound.

If he walked away, the Authority would count it as an unverified kill. He would lose points. He would lose his rating.

But if he went in…

If he tried to separate the host from the parasite…

He would have to touch it, and he would have to break protocol.

He would have to get dirty.

Dale looked at his hands.

They were covered in black ichor.

He looked at the clipboard, and the page was blank.

He didn't have time to write a plan. He didn't have time to get a permit.

He walked forward.

The larvae parted for him. They were dying, but they recognized him. They smelled like him.

Dale stepped onto the mound, and the flesh was soft. It gave way under his boots.

He walked toward the face.

Margaret's voice was fading. The chorus was returning, louder, more desperate.

"Run," she whispered.

Dale reached into his pocket, and he pulled out the glass shard.

It was small. Useless.

But he had a new idea.

He didn't need to kill the broodmother, and he needed to isolate it.

He needed to create a containment zone.

He looked at the tunnel walls. The fungal mats were growing over the exits.

If he didn't stop them, the infestation would spread to the surface. To the city. To the rest of the county.

Dale dropped to his knees, and he dug his glass shard into the mound's flesh.

He didn't strike the face.

He struck the base. The anchor.

Where the human flesh met the dimensional mass, and he carved.

He carved a ring around the face.

A containment circle.

*Improvised barrier,* the Authority noted. *Low efficacy. High risk of collapse.*

Dale didn't care, and he carved faster.

The mound bucked.

Pain shot through Dale's arm. The flesh pulled back, tearing into his hand.

He didn't stop, and he wrote in the air.

Not with a pen. With the glass.

*ISOLATE.*

The ring closed.

The face of Margaret was trapped in the center.

The swarm around her recoiled. The fungal mats retreated from the circle.

The broodmother was separated, and the host was alone.

Margaret's eyes opened.

They were clear.

For the first time since the Rift opened, they were just eyes.

"Dale," she said.

"I have you," Dale said.

"Let go," she said.

"No," Dale said.

"You're contaminating yourself."

"I'm treating the source."

"You're killing me."

Dale looked at the Authority's interface.

*Verification complete. Host isolation successful. Threat neutralized (partial).*

*Points awarded: 50.*

*Thoroughness Rating: 9.1.*

He had lost 0.1, which was a small number.

But it was a start.

The mound dissolved. The body of the broodmother was failing without the swarm's support.

Margaret's body was failing with it.

Her skin was gray. Her veins were black.

Dale reached out, and he grabbed her wrist.

It was cold.

"Help me," he said.

"Help me die," Margaret said.

"No," Dale said. "Help me document this."

He pulled out his pen, and he wrote on her skin.

*Patient: Margaret. Status: Critical. Action: Extraction.*

He couldn't extract her.

But he could extract the data.

The broodmother's last act was to scream.

Not a sound.

A data burst.

A flood of information.

Species. Weakness. Breeding cycle. Location of the next nest.

All of it.

It hit Dale's mind like a truck, and he staggered.

He fell forward onto Margaret.

His face pressed into hers.

He inhaled her breath.

Ammonia. And something else. Something old.

And then, a voice.

Not the Authority.

Not the swarm.

A voice from the jar, and the eye was gone, but the voice remained.

*Certification Level II unlocked.*

*New Protocol: Host Severance.*

Dale pulled back.

He looked at Margaret, and she was smiling.

It wasn't a happy smile.

It was a relief.

"Go," she said.

"Where?" Dale asked.

"To the next one," Margaret said. "It's already out."

"What's already out?"

Margaret pointed down.

Not at the mound.

At the ground.

The concrete beneath Dale's knees was cracking.

Not from the broodmother.

From something below.

Something bigger.

Dale looked at the crack.

It wasn't empty, which was filled with eyes.

Thousands of them.

All blinking.

All watching him.

And one of them, the largest, looked familiar.

It looked like the Inspector.

Dale's clipboard fell.

The glass shard slipped from his hand, and he stood up.

He looked at the Authority's interface.

*New infestation detected.*

*Classification: Unknown.*

*Recommendation: Retreat.*

Dale didn't retreat, and he picked up the clipboard.

He wrote one word.

*RETRIEVE.*

Then he wrote another.

*RETRY.*

He stepped into the crack.
