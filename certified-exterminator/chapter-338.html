Chapter 338: Exterminator Becomes Hive

# Chapter 338

The tunnel was a throat, and it was swallowing things whole.

Kael pressed his forehead against the wet concrete, breath fogging the visor of his scavenger helmet. He watched the figure ahead. The man in the grey suit moved with a jerky, broken rhythm, favoring his left leg. His clipboard hung at his hip, useless as a shield, tapping against his thigh with every step.

"Look at him," Kael whispered into his comms. "He's walking like he owns the place."

"Target confirmed," static crackled back. "Subject 004.

Unlicensed.

High-risk. Do not engage. Observe only.

We need to see what he does with the anomaly.

"

Kael adjusted his grip on the modified nail gun strapped to his back. He'd seen plenty of survivors try to fight their way through the Undercity.

They screamed, and they swung weapons. They died screaming. This guy didn't do either.

He just walked.

Ahead, the grey-suited man stopped. He raised a hand, not in a weapon stance, but in a gesture that looked eerily like a traffic cop directing flow. His eyes weren't on the shadows. They were on the floor.

"Movement at two o'clock," Kael murmured. "Something's skittering."

The shadows detached from the wall.

It wasn't a single creature, which was a swarm. Black, chitinous legs, too many of them, unfolding from a central mass of wet muscle.

They poured out of a drainage grate, clicking against the stone like rain on a tin roof.

Kael flinched. His finger tightened on the trigger.

The man in the suit didn't flinch. He crouched.

One knee down, then the other.

His movements were precise, surgical. He pulled a small, square device from his belt. It hummed with a low, green light.

*Documentation phase,* Kael's analyst voiceover said in his ear. *He's tagging them.*

The swarm surged forward.

Kael expected gunfire. Or a spell. Or a scream.

Instead, the man slammed a heavy, rubber-soled boot into the ground. *Thump.*

The ground shook. The swarm hesitated.

Then, the man spoke. His voice was flat, bored, and projected perfectly down the tunnel.

"Class IV Infestation detected.

Unsanctioned breeding ground. Entry point: grate 4-B.

Structural integrity: compromised.

Recommendation: immediate evacuation of non-essential personnel and targeted chemical suppression.

"

Kael blinked. "He's… filing a report? In the middle of a swarm?"

"Wait for it," the voiceover said.

The swarm ignored the report. It lunged.

The man in the suit didn't dodge. He stepped back, a precise six inches, and raised the green device.

A beam of concentrated light hit the center of the mass.

It wasn't a laser, which was a scanner.

*Ping.*

A holographic display bloomed in the air above the creatures. Text scrolled rapidly.

*SPECIES: Arachnida profundus variant. WEAKNESS: Chitin joint vulnerability at segment 4. STATUS: ACTIVE THREAT.*

The man nodded once.

"Targeted."

He reached into his other pouch. Not a gun. A canister. He twisted the cap.

"Protocol: Vaporization. Sector 4."

He tossed the canister. It spun through the air, end-over-end, landing squarely in the center of the swarm.

*Hiss.*

White fog erupted. It wasn't smoke. It was dense, viscous, and smelled of ammonia and bleach, even from fifty yards away.

The black legs thrashed, then slowed. Then stopped.

The mass collapsed into a heap of twitching, dissolving goo, and the man stood up. He brushed some dust off his sleeve. He looked at the heap.

He looked at his clipboard.

He made a checkmark.

"Verification complete," he said.

Kael lowered his nail gun. His mouth hung open.

"Did he just… fumigate them?"

"He treated the nest," the analyst said, voice tight with something like awe.

"He didn't kill the adults. He collapsed the nursery. The adults starve in six hours.

The young die in twelve.

"

Kael stared at the dead mass. It was gone. Not beaten. Erased.

"Subject 004 is moving again," Kael reported. "He's heading toward the central vent."

"Don't follow," the analyst said. "We don't know what he's carrying. That canister… the chemical signature is unknown. It's not standard Authority issue."

Kael watched the grey figure disappear around a bend. The silence that followed was heavier than the noise.

***

Dale Kowalski sat on a crate in the dark, his back against the cold concrete.

The pain in his leg was a dull, throbbing ache that synchronized with his heartbeat.

He rubbed the tear in his sleeve. The black dust was still there.

Embedded.

*YOU ARE THE PEST.*

The words burned in his mind. He pulled his Field Guide from his belt.

The screen flickered, battery low, and he tapped it. The entry for *Rattus profundus* was greyed out. Contaminated.

He couldn't access the data.

He couldn't use the weakness bonus.

He was flying blind.

And he was alone.

Seven was gone. The Auditor had vanished, leaving Dale with a dead clipboard and a suit that felt more like a cage than armor.

A soft *click* echoed from the tunnel entrance.

Dale froze. He didn't reach for a weapon. He reached for his clipboard. He held it up, the blank page facing the darkness, a shield of bureaucracy.

A shape emerged from the shadows.

Tall. Thin. Clad in scavenged armor made of stop signs and rebar.

A helmet obscured his face.

"Hey," the shape said. Voice young. Terrified. "You're the one who killed the swarm with the fumes?"

Dale didn't lower the clipboard.

"I filed a report. The extermination was incidental to the compliance audit."

The shape stepped forward. He held a nail gun, but it was pointed at the floor.

"Who are you?"

"Licensed Technician. Class II."

"That's a lie," the kid said. He looked at the dead pile of goo in the distance. "Class II doesn't have vaporizers. Class II doesn't have Authority clearance to enter Sector 7."

Dale lowered the clipboard slowly, and he looked at the kid. looked.

Scavenger markings. Painted on the shoulder.

A spiral barcode.

*YOU ARE NOT THE EXTERMINATOR.*

The kid saw Dale looking at the logo. He flinched.

"Don't look at it. It's not safe."

"It's a violation," Dale said dryly. "Unauthorized branding on municipal infrastructure. That's a code K-9 fine."

The kid laughed. A sharp, brittle sound.

"You're crazy. You're really crazy."

"I'm thorough," Dale corrected. "Who are you?"

"Kael. Scavenger.

We've been watching you for ten minutes.

The Authority doesn't send people like you. They send Auditors. They send Enforcers.

"

"I'm not here by invitation," Dale said. He stood up, wincing as his leg protested. "I'm here because there is an infestation. And the infestation is spreading."

Kael lowered his gun slightly.

"It's not just bugs. It's people.

The ones from the upper city.

They're down here. They're changing.

"

Dale felt a cold prickle at the base of his neck. Not fear. Recognition.

"Changing how?"

"Their skin turns black. Like the dust on your suit.

They start moving weird.

Clicking.

And they stop talking. They just hunt.

"

Dale looked at his sleeve. The black dust.

*UNIDENTIFIED BIO-SIGNAL DETECTED.* *MATCH CONFIDENCE: 0%.*

He remembered the alert. He remembered the quarantine recommendation.

*Quarantine.*

The word hung in the air.

Kael stepped closer.

"We have a nest. The big one.

The one making the signal. We can't kill it.

Every time we shoot it, it gets faster.

Stronger. But you…

" Kael pointed at the clipboard. "You stopped the swarm without firing a shot.

You used a protocol.

"

"I used a protocol for arachnids," Dale said. "I don't have a protocol for humans."

"They're not human anymore," Kael said. "Not really. But they're still bugs inside. If you can identify the weak spot…"

Dale looked at the kid. He looked at the tunnel. He thought about the message painted on the wall.

*YOU ARE THE PEST.*

If he was the pest, then the Authority wasn't the landlord. They were the exterminators. And he wasn't working for them. He was being hunted by them.

But Kael was right about one thing. Dale knew how to treat infestations.

Whether the target was a rat, a beetle, or a mutated former neighbor.

The principles remained the same. Identify. Isolate.

Eliminate. Verify.

He needed a weakness. He needed a weakness he could find in the Field Guide.

But his guide was contaminated.

Unless he could find a clean entry.

Kael watched him. Waiting.

Dale pulled a pen from his pocket, and he flipped his clipboard over.

On the back, he drawed. Not a map.

A diagram.

"Show me the nest," Dale said. "And tell me exactly what it eats."

Kael hesitated. Then he nodded.

"It eats fear.

Literally. It absorbs panic. That's why we can't fight it.

Our fear feeds it.

"

Dale's pen stopped.

*Absorbs panic.*

A new mechanic clicked into place. Not a bug in the system. A loophole.

The Treatment Points system rewarded *thoroughness*. It rewarded *documentation*. It rewarded *verification*.

If the creature fed on panic, then *calm* was poison.

If Dale could maintain perfect, OSHA-compliant detachment in the face of a horror that fed on emotion, he wouldn't just be immune. He would be a toxin.

He looked up at Kael.

"I need you to do something for me," Dale said.

"What?"

"I need you to panic. Right now. Loudly. Viscerally."

Kael stared at him.

"Are you insane?"

"No," Dale said. He opened his suit's internal comms.

He routed the audio to the tunnel speakers.

"I need to calibrate the target's response threshold. If it feeds on fear, then I need to measure its hunger. And then I need to starve it."

"You want to feed it?" Kael whispered.

"I want to bait it," Dale said. He stood up.

He straightened his tie, and he checked his cuffs.

"If you scream, it comes. If I stay quiet, it chokes.

We're going to play a game of control.

"

Kael raised his hands.

"I don't want to die for your experiment."

"You won't die," Dale said. "You're the distraction. I'm the exterminator."

Dale turned his back to Kael. He walked toward the dark.

"Run," Dale said. "Scream. Tell it everything you're afraid of."

Kael took a step back. Then another. He opened his mouth. He let out a shriek that echoed off the walls, raw and terrified.

The shadows ahead coalesced. They didn't just move. They *leaned* toward the sound.

Dale watched them. His hands were steady.

His breathing was regulated.

Four counts in. Four counts hold. Four counts out.

He looked at his Field Guide.

The screen flickered. A new entry populated.

Not from the Authority.

From his own analysis.

*SPECIES: Panic-Eater (Pseudoscientific designation).* *WEAKNESS: Emotional Nullification.* *STATUS: ACTIVE.*

The weakness wasn't physical. It was psychological.

The creature lunged. It was huge, a mass of limbs and screaming mouths, twisting and writhing. It crashed toward Dale, drawn by Kael's screams.

Dale didn't move. He didn't flinch. He didn't care.

He simply raised his clipboard.

"Cease vocalization," Dale said, his voice cutting through the chaos. "This is a restricted zone."

The creature hit the invisible wall of Dale's indifference. It shuddered. The black dust on his sleeve flared with white light.

*SYSTEM ALERT: THOROUGHNESS RATING INCREASING.* *EMOTIONAL CONTAMINATION: 0%.* *TARGET VULNERABILITY: EXPOSED.*

The creature froze, confused by the lack of fuel.

Dale stepped forward. He raised his hand. He snapped his fingers.

"Protocol: Silence."

The creature screamed, a sound of pure, unadulterated frustration. It turned, not toward Dale, but toward Kael, who had stopped screaming, shocked by the sight.

But Dale was already there. He reached into his belt. He didn't pull a weapon. He pulled a blank label.

He slapped it onto the creature's chest.

*VIOLATION: UNLICENSED EXISTENCE.*

The creature convulsed. The label glowed red.

Dale looked at the creature's core, where the panic was generated. He saw the knot of black energy. He saw the weakness.

And then, a new message flashed across his vision, not from his suit, but from the Authority itself.

*SUBJECT 004: LICENSE SUSPENDED.* *REASON: UNAUTHORIZED EXTERMINATION OF CIVILIAN ENTITY.* *ACTION: TERMINATE.*

The creature didn't die. It changed. The black dust on Dale's sleeve spread to his hand. It crawled up his arm.

It wasn't trying to kill him, which was trying to *join* him.

Dale looked at his hand. The skin was turning black.

He looked at Kael. The kid was backing away, eyes wide.

"Run," Dale said, his voice changing, deeper, rasping. "Run now."

He wasn't the exterminator anymore, and he was the hive.

The Authority’s kill signal locked onto his new heartbeat.
