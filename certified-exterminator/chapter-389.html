Chapter 389: Approved by the Earth

# Chapter 389

The silence lasted four seconds.

In that span, the paper skin of the Reviewer crumbled at the edges. Ink bled from the tear, staining the pavement like oil. Halloway's ticket pad trembled in his hand, the carbon copy sheets fluttering in a wind that did not exist.

Dale didn't look at Halloway. He looked at the glowing pen in his own hand.

The ink wasn't just ink anymore. It was a binding agent.

If he signed the approval form, the Authority would rewrite his biographical data.

He would become a statistic in their endless filing cabinet, his memories archived but not accessible. His body would remain, but the *Dale* who knew the difference between a sewer rat and a river rat would be overwritten with generic employee data.

Refusing was suicide. Signing was annihilation.

But Dale had spent fourteen years finding the gap between the regulation and the reality. He had spent decades learning that code violations were not errors to be fixed, but opportunities to be exploited. If the system relied on data integrity, then bad data was a weapon.

Halloway's lie had created a paradox. The Reviewers could not process an entry that contradicted their own observational logs. The system was hanging. A fatal error.

The Reviewer with the folded fingers twitched. Its head, a flat sheet of parchment stamped with a seal, tilted. A sound emerged from it, not a voice, but the rustle of turning pages. It sounded like a thousand inspectors arguing in a single throat.

*"Data inconsistency detected. Source: Inspector Halloway. Target: Technician Kowalski. Status: Unresolved."*

The creature stepped forward. Its foot, a crease in the air, touched the ground. The pavement beneath it didn't crack; it faded. The asphalt lost its texture, becoming a smooth, gray void.

Dale moved, and he didn't run away. He walked toward the Reviewer, keeping his clipboard raised. The clipboard was his shield, his identity, his anchor to the physical world. If he dropped it, the Authority would treat him as unlicensed, and the Reviewers would delete him as an outlier.

"Halloway," Dale said, his voice flat. "Don't stop writing."

"I'm trying," Halloway wheezed, his eyes wide. "But my hand… it's shaking."

"Write faster."

"I can't! The pen is stuck!"

Dale glanced back.

The ink in Halloway's pen was crystallizing. The paradox was spreading.

The Reviewer paused, confused by the lack of input.

It needed to see the violation documented to process the correction. Without the paperwork, it couldn't execute the erasure.

But it couldn't ignore the discrepancy either.

Dale uncapped the pen in his own hand. He didn't sign the approval. He opened his Field Guide.

The interface shimmered in the air, translucent and hovering over the wet concrete. *Dimensional Vermin (Rattus profundus)*. *Acid Beetles (Scarabaeidae mutatis)*. And now, a new entry blinking with a red warning border: *Bureaucratic Construct (Forma administratio)*.

He hadn't identified this species before. It wasn't in the manual. It wasn't in the code.

But Dale had a protocol for everything, and he tapped the screen, selecting *Documentation Protocol: Alpha*.

"You're doing it wrong," Dale said to the Reviewer.

The creature stopped. The fading pavement around its feet stabilized, just for a moment.

*"Clarify,"* the rustling voice demanded.

"Your data is incomplete," Dale said. He pointed his pen at the creature's chest, where the seal was stamped.

"You're missing the entry point.

You're missing the vector.

You can't approve an infestation you haven't isolated.

"

The Reviewer's head tilted further. The pages of its face fluttered violently.

*"Isolation confirmed. Entity Kowalski is contained within the dimensional boundary."*

"False," Dale said. He tapped his clipboard. "I am currently engaged in active treatment.

The workspace is not secure. The perimeter is compromised.

Protocol requires a quarantine seal before approval can be granted.

"

He was bluffing. He had no quarantine seal. He had no authority to stop them.

But he was speaking their language.

The Reviewer hesitated. This was a procedural objection. In the Authority's rigid hierarchy, a technician's objection based on safety protocols held weight. It was the one thing that kept Dale alive this long: the absolute, unyielding faith in procedure.

Halloway stared at Dale.

"What are you doing?"

"Filing a complaint," Dale muttered.

He wrote. Not on the form. On the Field Guide. He created a new entry. *Forma administratio*.

*Identification:* Bureaucratic Construct. *Weakness:* Procedural Paradox. *Protocol:* Intentional Data Corruption via False Certification.

The system flashed. *New Species Identified. Field Guide Updated.*

A chime sounded in Dale's ear. *Treatment Points Awarded: 0. Reason: Incomplete Elimination.*

But something else happened. The Reviewer froze. The ink on its fingers stopped dripping. The creature looked at Dale, then at Halloway, then back at Dale.

*"Technician Kowalski. You are attempting to classify a High-Review Entity as a Class-4 Pest."*

"It's an infestation," Dale said. "And you're spreading."

He pointed to the gray void spreading across the street.

"Look at that. That's cross-contamination.

You're bleeding structural integrity into the local grid.

That's a Class-3 violation under Statute 99.4: Unauthorized Dimensional Leakage.

"

The Reviewer's head snapped up. The seal on its forehead glowed brighter.

*"Statute 99.4 is reserved for Level VI Certifications."*

"I know," Dale said. "And you're violating it."

This was the loophole, and the Authority was obsessed with compliance. If Dale could prove the Reviewers were breaking their own rules, the system would pause to investigate. It wouldn't approve him.

It wouldn't kill him. It would audit him.

And during an audit, time stopped.

Halloway's pen clicked. The crystallized ink cracked. He scribbled frantically, copying the Statute number onto his ticket pad.

"Statute 99.4," Halloway whispered. "I'm citing it. I'm filing the violation."

The paper people shuddered. One of them, the one with the folded fingers, stepped back. The gray void at its feet receded, snapping back into asphalt.

*"Pending Investigation,"* the voice said. *"All entities to hold position. Audit initiated."*

The world went silent. Not just the Reviewers. The wind stopped. The rain froze in mid-air, hanging like glass beads.

Dale lowered his pen. His hand was cramped, the muscles screaming from the tension. He looked at Halloway.

The younger man was pale, sweat mixing with the rain on his forehead. He looked at his ticket pad, where the lie was written in bold, black letters. *Violation: Procedural Non-Compliance. Status: Active.*

"We bought time," Dale said. "Not much."

"How long?" Halloway asked.

"Depends on how good they are at checking their math."

Dale turned back to the Reviewers. They were motionless now, frozen in a stasis field of bureaucratic suspension. They were waiting for the system to verify the claim.

But the system was Dale's domain, and he opened his Field Guide again. The entry for *Forma administratio* was still incomplete. To fully eliminate a threat, he needed to document its weakness. He had identified the paradox, but he hadn't verified the cure.

He needed to finish the job.

If he could complete the protocol for *Forma administratio*, he could force the Authority to classify the Reviewers as pests. Not judges. Pests. And pests could be exterminated.

But the field guide entry was locked. *Verification Required: Physical Interaction.*

He had to touch them.

"Halloway," Dale said. "Get the spray."

Halloway blinked.

"The what?"

"Your kit. The aerosol can. The one with the neurotoxin base. It's useless on these things, but it's the only tool we have that registers as a 'treatment' to the system."

Halloway fumbled with his belt. He pulled out a small, orange can. It was meant for wasps. It had a warning label that said *Do Not Use Indoors*.

Dale took the can. He shook it. The rattle was loud in the frozen silence.

He walked toward the nearest Reviewer. The creature didn't move. It couldn't. The audit held it in place.

Dale raised the can. He aimed at the creature's chest, at the seal.

"This is a violation," Dale said. "Improper use of chemical agents on protected entities. I'm documenting it."

He pressed the nozzle.

A mist of orange gas sprayed out.

It hit the paper skin of the Reviewer.

The paper didn't burn. It didn't dissolve.

It just got wet.

The system chime sounded. *Protocol Initiated: Chemical Treatment.*

*Error: Target Immune.*

Dale didn't care. The error was a confirmation. The system was recognizing the interaction. It was trying to process the data.

He sprayed again. And again. He sprayed the second Reviewer. He sprayed the third.

He was contaminating the field. He was making a mess. And in the Authority's eyes, a mess was a problem.

But Dale wasn't trying to kill them. He was trying to tag them.

He looked at his Field Guide. The entry for *Forma administratio* flickered. The red warning border turned yellow.

*Weakness Identified: Data Saturation.*

It worked. The Reviewers were paper.

Paper absorbed ink.

Too much ink, and the structure failed. The logic became illegible.

Dale sprayed the one with the folded fingers directly into its face. The ink of the gas mixed with the ink of the creature. The pages of its face blurred. The seal on its forehead smeared into a black smear.

The Reviewer let out a sound. A shriek of tearing paper.

*"Data… corrupted… re-evaluating…"*

The stasis field wavered. The rain droplets fell, one by one.

Halloway yelled, "It's breaking!"

"It's rebooting," Dale corrected. "Don't touch it. Let it crash."

The Reviewer staggered. Its body, made of folded paper and redacted lines, slumped against a lamppost. The lamppost bent, not from impact, but from the weight of the paradox it carried.

The other two Reviewers looked at their companion. They seemed to communicate in the rustle of pages. Then, they looked at Dale.

They didn't attack. They didn't retreat. They just watched.

The system notification popped up in Dale's vision.

*Thoroughness Scale: 4.2.* *Reason: Sloppy Application. Chemical agent ineffective. Documentation incomplete.*

*Dale felt a surge of adrenaline. 4.2 was low. But it was non-zero. He had engaged. He had survived.*

*Certification Progress: +15 Points.*

It wasn't enough to level up. It wasn't enough to change his status. But it was a crack in the wall.

"Halloway," Dale said, turning away from the slumping Reviewer. "We need to move. Now."

"Where?" Halloway asked, his voice trembling. "They're still here. The audit isn't over."

"The audit is a distraction," Dale said.

He pulled his clipboard tight against his chest.

"We bought time, not safety.

The Authority will realize I'm manipulating the data.

When they do, they won't send Reviewers. They'll send Enforcers.

"

Halloway paled.

"Enforcers? What are those?"

"Cleaners," Dale said. "And they don't file reports."

A sound came from the sky. Not thunder.

A low, grinding hum.

The clouds above the street swirled, forming a spiral.

Inside the spiral, a shape emerged. It wasn't paper.

It was metal. Cold, angular, and vast.

It was a drone. But not a human-made drone. It was a construct of the Authority, a physical manifestation of enforcement.

And it was scanning the area.

Dale looked at the slumping Reviewer. The creature was dissolving.

The ink was running, turning the paper into pulp, which was dying. But its death was releasing energy. A wave of distortion rippled out from the corpse, washing over Dale and Halloway.

Dale fell to his knees.

His vision blurred. For a second, he saw the world differently.

He saw the codes.

The green lines of the simulation. The red errors. The yellow warnings.

He saw Halloway, glowing with a faint blue light. *Data Integrity: Compromised.*

He saw himself. *Status: Rogue.*

The drone in the sky locked onto him. A red laser grid swept across the street.

*Target Acquired: Technician Kowalski. Status: Violation 99.4. Action: Erasure.*

Dale scrambled to his feet. His legs felt heavy, like he was wading through molasses.

"Run," Dale said.

"I can't," Halloway whispered. He was staring at his hands. His fingers were turning gray. Like paper.

Dale grabbed Halloway's arm. The skin felt dry, brittle.

"Halloway. Look at me."

Halloway's eyes were wide, filled with a terror that went beyond fear. It was the terror of non-existence.

"I'm losing it," Halloway said. "I'm losing the data."

"You're not," Dale said. He slapped Halloway's cheek.

Hard.

"Focus.

The protocol. What's the next step?

"

Halloway blinked. The gray spread to his wrists.

"Verify," Halloway croaked.

"Verify," Dale said. "Verify that you are still you."

He looked at the drone. The laser grid was closing in. They had seconds.

Dale pulled out his Field Guide. He tapped the entry for *Forma administratio*.

*Verification Required: Physical Interaction.*

He had already sprayed them. He had already corrupted them. But the entry wasn't complete.

He needed a sample. He needed proof.

He looked at the dissolving Reviewer. A piece of its paper skin, saturated with orange chemical and black ink, lay on the pavement. It was small. No bigger than a stamp.

Dale dove, and he grabbed the piece of paper. It was slimy, cold, and vibrating.

The laser grid hit him.

Pain, white and blinding, seared through his shoulder. Dale screamed, but the sound was swallowed by the grinding hum of the drone.

He rolled, clutching the paper scrap. He crawled toward Halloway, who was now half-transparent, his edges fraying into ash.

"Halloway!" Dale yelled. "Take it!"

Halloway looked down at the paper in Dale's hand.

"What is it?"

"Proof," Dale gasped. "It's proof of the violation. If you log it, you're not a rogue. You're a whistleblower."

Halloway's hand, now almost entirely ash, reached out. He grabbed the paper.

The moment their skin touched, the gray receded from Halloway. The ash turned back to flesh. The transparency vanished.

*Data Restored.*

The drone's laser grid snapped off. The hum dropped an octave, becoming a confused whine.

*Anomaly Detected. Source: Whistleblower Protocol.*

*Halt Execution.*

Dale slumped against the curb, breathing hard. Blood soaked through his shirt from the laser burn on his shoulder. He clutched his clipboard, his knuckles white.

Halloway stared at the paper scrap in his hand. He looked at Dale, then at the drone in the sky.

"You did it," Halloway said. "You turned the system against itself."

"No," Dale said. He wiped blood from his forehead. "I just made it look at the paperwork."

He looked up at the drone. It was hovering, waiting. Waiting for instructions. Waiting for the Authority to decide if Halloway was a traitor or a hero.

And then, a new notification appeared in Dale's vision. Not the standard green text.

Red.

*Breach Detected.* *Location: Local Node.* *Status: Unsealed.*

The ground beneath them shook. Not from the drone. From below.

The street split open.

A fissure, jagged and black, tore through the asphalt. From the darkness, something crawled out. It wasn't a rat.

It wasn't a beetle, which was a hand. A massive, multi-jointed hand, made of chitin and bone, reaching up from the depths.

And on its palm, etched in glowing white runes, was the Authority's logo.

The Authority wasn't just judging them.

It was coming to collect.

Dale looked at Halloway.

"Did you write it down?" Dale asked.

Halloway nodded, his face grim.

"It's in the log."

"Good," Dale said. He stood up, ignoring the pain in his shoulder. He picked up his spray can. It was empty.

He looked at the giant hand rising from the earth.

"Now we're in trouble," Dale said. "Because that thing has a seal on it. And if it's sealed, it's approved."

The hand curled, ready to crush them.

"Cover me," Dale said. "And for God's sake, find me a new can."
