Chapter 307: License Suspended

# Chapter 307

Dale woke up on his back.

Concrete floor. Cold. Smelled like ozone and old copper.

He sat up. A sharp pain spiked behind his left eye, then faded.

His clipboard was gone.

His spray canister was gone. His badge was gone.

The room was empty.

Not the laboratory.

Just the room he was in. A holding cell.

Six by eight.

No windows. No door handle on the inside.

A single steel tray sat in the center of the floor. On it rested a new clipboard. A black one. Not the standard gray plastic of a Level II technician.

He picked it up. The weight was wrong. Heavy. Metallic.

**SYSTEM ALERT: LICENSE SUSPENDED.** **STATUS: PROBATIONARY.** **PENALTY: FIELD GUIDE CORRUPTED (72 HOURS).** **ACCESS TO PROTOCOLS: LOCKED.**

The text burned red in his peripheral vision. It pulsed. A steady, rhythmic throb.

Dale rubbed his temples. He checked his pockets.

Empty.

No flashlight.

No marker. No sealant.

He looked at the black clipboard. The first page was blank. But the margins were filled with fine print, microscopic text that shifted when he tried to read it.

*You have committed an Act of Unlicensed Engagement.* *You have contaminated a Class-9 Entity.* *You are now liable for cross-contamination damages.* *To restore license: Submit Form 11-B (Post-Incident Debrief).* *To avoid execution: Complete the Audit.*

Dale dropped the clipboard. It clattered against the concrete.

The door hissed.

It didn't open. It dissolved.

The metal turned to liquid, sliding down the frame like mercury. The Auditor stood in the frame. He wore a suit that seemed to absorb the light.

His visor was still up. His face was pale, smooth, and devoid of expression.

Behind him, two figures in hazmat suits dragged Ms. Evans. She was limp. Her eyes were rolled back.

Dale stood. He smoothed his work shirt. It was torn at the shoulder. Blood crusted on his collar.

"I didn't know the subject was hostile," Dale said. His voice was steady. Dry. "I followed protocol."

The Auditor stepped forward. The floor didn't creak under his feet. It bent.

"Protocol requires identification before engagement," the Auditor said. "You engaged a blind spot. You treated the symptom, not the source."

"I filed the report," Dale countered. "I documented the breach. I isolated the area. I requested containment."

"You used a clipboard as a blunt instrument."

"It was the only tool available."

Dale pulled out a pen. A standard blue ballpoint. It wasn't his. It appeared in his hand when he reached for it.

He uncapped it. He clicked the top.

"Name," the Auditor said.

"Dale Kowalski. Class II."

"Current status?"

"Suspended."

The Auditor smiled, which was a thin, horizontal line.

"Correct. You are now an unlicensed entity.

Under the Authority's code, unlicensed entities are classified as vermin.

"

Dale felt a chill crawl up his spine. He looked at the two hazmat suits.

They were holding Ms.

Evans by her arms. They were wearing respirators.

They weren't looking at Dale. They were looking at the wall behind him.

There was a stain there. A dark, wet smear where the Herald had unfolded.

Dale stepped toward it.

"Stay back," the Auditor said. "That is a biohazard."

"It's not a hazard if you know the vector," Dale said. He walked past the Auditor.

The Auditor didn't move. He watched.

Dale approached the stain. It looked like dried blood, but it smelled like sulfur and rot.

He leaned in, and he didn't touch it.

He sniffed.

Sulfur. Silt. Rotting orchids.

His Field Guide was corrupted. The entries were scrambled. *Rattus profundus* read as *Lupus infernalis*. *Acid Beetles* read as *Spiders of Light*. The data was useless.

But his memory wasn't, and he remembered the shape of the Herald. No eyes. Too many mouths.

He remembered the sound. Shattering glass.

He remembered the smell. Sulfur.

Dale pulled a small vial from his pocket. It wasn't his. It appeared when he reached for it. A clear liquid.

He uncorked it. He poured a drop onto the stain.

The stain hissed. It bubbled. The black sludge turned gray.

The Auditor's visor flickered.

"Unauthorized chemical application," the Auditor said. "Penalty: Two weeks of labor service in the Sewer Levels."

Dale ignored him. He watched the gray sludge. It coalesced. It formed a small, perfect sphere.

He capped the vial. He picked up the sphere with tweezers that appeared in his hand.

"Sample collected," Dale said. "Entry: *Sulfuric Sludge, Variant 4*. Weakness: Alkaline neutralization. Habitat: Rift interfaces."

The System prompt vanished.

The black clipboard on the floor slid toward Dale's feet.

**SYSTEM UPDATE:** **FIELD GUIDE PARTIALLY RESTORED.** **NEW ENTRY: SULFURIC SLUDGE (VARIANT 4).** **BONUS: +15% DAMAGE TO RIFT-SPECIFIC ENTITIES.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 6.4.**

Dale stepped back. He looked at the Auditor.

"You tested me," Dale said.

"I audited you," the Auditor corrected. "You passed the observation phase. You failed the compliance phase."

"I identified the vector."

"You used a non-approved substance."

"It was in my pocket."

"It was not in your inventory list."

Dale stared at the Auditor. The man was playing a game. A bureaucratic game. One where the rules were weaponized to keep people small.

"Ms. Evans," Dale said. "What did she know?"

The Auditor looked at the two hazmat suits. They dragged Ms. Evans into the corridor. Her head lolled back.

"She knew you were coming," the Auditor said. "She knew you would try to document the Herald. She knew the Authority would punish you for it."

"Why?"

"Because the Herald is not a pest."

The Auditor turned. He walked out of the cell. The walls solidified behind him.

Dale was alone again.

But the door was gone.

In its place was a wall of black glass.

And on the glass, words formed. Not in the System prompt. In the glass itself.

**AUDIT PHASE 2: THE PURGE.** **LOCATION: SECTOR 4 SUB-LEVELS.** **OBJECTIVE: ELIMINATE ALL UNLICENSED ORGANIC MATTER.** **TIME LIMIT: 00:59:59.**

Dale looked at the timer. It was ticking down.

He looked at the black glass. He tapped it. It was solid.

He looked around the cell. No exits. No vents. No hidden doors.

He looked at the clipboard. The black one.

He opened it to the second page.

It wasn't a form. It was a map.

A schematic of the facility. Sector 4. Sub-levels.

The path was marked in red.

But the red line didn't go to an exit. It went to a room labeled **THE INCINERATOR.**

Dale frowned, and he looked at the map again.

There was a second line. Faint. Gray.

It branched off the red line. It went to a storage closet.

**SUPPLY DEPOT: CLASS I CHEMICALS.**

Dale ran, and the cell was no longer a cell. The walls had shifted. The black glass had become a corridor.

He sprinted. His shoes slapped against the metal floor.

His breath came in short, sharp gasps.

He reached the corner. He slid around it.

The corridor stretched ahead. Dark. Dripping.

Something moved in the shadows.

A rat. But not a rat. It was the size of a cat.

Its skin was translucent. You could see its organs pulsing inside.

It hissed. A wet, clicking sound.

Dale stopped, and he didn't have a weapon. He didn't have a spray.

He had a pen.

He had a clipboard.

He had a Field Guide entry for *Sulfuric Sludge*.

He looked at the rat. It wasn't sulfuric. It was wet. Slimy.

He looked at his Field Guide.

*Entry: Dimensional Vermin.* *Status: Corrupted.* *Weakness: Unknown.*

The rat charged.

Dale didn't move, and he watched the rat's feet. They left a trail. A clear, viscous trail.

He remembered the smell from the lab. Sulfur.

He remembered the reaction. Alkaline neutralization.

He looked at the walls. They were painted with mold. Black mold.

He looked at the floor. It was covered in grime.

He pulled the pen from his pocket, and he wrote on the clipboard.

*Target: Unknown Rodent.* *Observation: Trail indicates high salinity.* *Hypothesis: Salt-sensitive.*

The System prompt flashed.

**HYPOTHESIS REGISTERED.** **CONFIDENCE: 30%.** **PENALTY FOR INCORRECT HYPOTHESIS: LICENSE REVOCATION.**

Dale didn't care about the penalty. He cared about the rat.

It was five feet away.

Dale kicked the wall. Hard.

A chunk of paint and mold flew off. It landed in front of the rat.

The rat skidded. It sniffed the mold.

It recoiled.

It hated the mold.

Dale smiled. A tight, thin smile.

He grabbed a handful of the loose mold from the floor. He threw it at the rat.

The rat shrieked. Its skin bubbled where the mold touched it.

It writhed. It clawed at the air.

Dale stepped back, and he didn't kill it. He didn't have to. The mold was doing the work.

He watched the rat dissolve. It melted into a puddle of slime.

He checked his watch.

00:58:12, and he ran.

The corridor branched. Left. Right.

The map on the clipboard showed the left path was blocked by a **CONTAINMENT BREACH**.

The right path led to the Supply Depot.

Dale went right, and he heard footsteps behind him. Heavy. Metallic.

He didn't look back.

He reached the door to the Supply Depot. It was locked.

A keypad, and he looked at the clipboard. The map was updating.

*Door Code: Derived from previous incident.*

Dale frowned.

Previous incident.

The Herald.

The date, and he looked at the black clipboard's header.

*Incident Date: October 14.*

He typed: 10-14.

The door clicked.

It slid open.

Inside, shelves. Shelves of bottles. Jars. Canisters.

Dale grabbed a canister labeled **AMMONIUM HYDROXIDE**, and he grabbed a bag of **CALCIUM CARBONATE**.

He grabbed a roll of **DUCT TAPE**.

He grabbed a **FLASHLIGHT**.

He grabbed a **MARKER**.

He turned to leave, and the Auditor stood in the doorway.

"You stole supplies," the Auditor said.

"I requisitioned them," Dale said. "For the audit."

The Auditor raised his hand.

Light formed in his palm.

"You are out of time," the Auditor said.

The timer on Dale's vision hit 00:00:00.

The world turned red.

**AUDIT PHASE 3: THE HUNT.** **HUNTER: THE AUDITOR.** **PREY: DALE KOWALSKI.** **RULES: NO KILLING UNTIL DOCUMENTED.**

The Auditor fired.

Dale threw the Ammonium Hydroxide canister.

It hit the beam of light.

The beam split. It hit the ceiling.

Explosion.

Dale was thrown backward. He hit a shelf. Jars shattered. Glass bit into his arm.

He scrambled up. Blood ran down his sleeve.

The Auditor was walking toward him. Slowly.

"You cannot win," the Auditor said. "You are a pest. Pests die."

Dale grabbed a jar of Calcium Carbonate. He smashed it on the floor.

White powder, and he kicked the powder toward the Auditor.

The Auditor breathed in.

He coughed.

His visor fogged.

Dale ran, and he ran through the supply depot. Through the back door. Into a tunnel.

Darkness.

Damp.

He clicked on his flashlight.

The beam cut through the dark.

It revealed a tunnel. Endless.

And at the far end, a light.

Not the Auditor's light.

A green light.

A sign.

**EXODUS.**

Dale ran faster.

His lungs burned. His arm bled.

He reached the end of the tunnel, and the light was bright. Blinding.

He stepped into it.

He was outside.

Night.

Rain, and he was in the woods behind the facility.

He checked his watch.

The timer was gone.

The System prompt was gone.

He was alone, and he looked back at the facility.

The lights were off.

Silence.

He checked his pockets.

He had the marker. He had the duct tape.

He didn't have the clipboard, and he didn't have the Field Guide.

He looked at his hand.

There was a mark on his palm. A small, black symbol.

A skull, and he rubbed it. It didn't come off.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** **LICENSE REVOKED.** **STATUS: ROGUE.** **REWARD FOR CAPTURE: CLASS IX.**

Dale looked at the woods.

He heard a sound.

A chittering.

Hundreds of chitterings.

From the trees.

From the ground.

From the sky.

Something was coming.

Not the Auditor.

Something else.

Something that had been waiting for the license to be revoked.

Something that fed on unlicensed entities.

Dale raised his marker. He drew a circle in the mud.

He drew a line through it.

**DO NOT ENTER.**

It wasn't a protocol. It wasn't a rule.

It was a suggestion.

The chittering grew louder.

Dale stood in the mud. He held his marker.

He didn't have a spray, and he didn't have a plan.

He had a marker.

And he had a hunch.

He looked at the trees.

The shadows moved, and they were getting closer.

Dale stepped back.

He bumped into a tree.

A branch snagged his shirt.

He looked up.

A face.

A face in the bark.

It smiled, which had no teeth.

"Hello, Dale," the tree said.

Dale froze.

The System prompt appeared one last time.

**NEW SPECIES DETECTED: MIMICRY FLORA.** **WEAKNESS: UNKNOWN.** **ACTION: VERIFY OR DIE.**

Dale looked at the marker in his hand, and he looked at the face.

He uncapped the marker.

He wrote on the tree.

**ENTRY: MIMICRY FLORA.** **WEAKNESS: INK.**

He pressed the marker into the bark.

The tree screamed.

It wasn't a scream of pain, which was a scream of recognition.

The chittering stopped.

The shadows retreated.

The face in the bark looked at the ink. It looked at Dale.

It winked.

**SYSTEM UPDATE:** **HYPOTHESIS CONFIRMED.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 9.8.** **NEXT PHASE: THE INFECTION.**

The ground beneath Dale's feet cracked.

Something large rose from the mud, which had Dale's face.

And it was wearing a Class II badge.
