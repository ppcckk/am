Chapter 176: Serve and Contain

# Chapter 176

The County Courthouse smelled of floor wax and old paper, a scent that usually soothed Dale Kowalski's nerves. Today, it made his stomach churn.

Miller parked the truck with a screech of tires that echoed off the limestone facade. The engine died. Silence rushed in, heavy and suffocating.

Dale stared at the dashboard. The chat window remained open. The_Landlord_1984 had stopped typing.

"Get out," Miller said. His voice was tight. "I don't like this."

Dale grabbed his clipboard. He checked his pocket for his pen.

It was there.

Standard issue, black ink, ballpoint. Reliable.

"We're here," Dale said. "Let's get the citation served."

Miller pointed at the front doors. They were barred. Not locked—barred. Thick iron bars welded across the glass, pulsing with a faint, rhythmic violet light.

"That's not a courthouse," Miller said. "That's a containment unit."

A woman stepped out of the shadows near the entrance. She wore a suit that fit too perfectly, the fabric shimmering like oil on water. Her smile was sharp, devoid of warmth.

"Mr. Kowalski," she said. "I'm here to represent the plaintiff, The Landlord."

Dale didn't look at her, and he looked at the bars. He pulled out his Field Guide.

The entry for *Dimensional Vermin (Rattus profundus)* was clear.

But this wasn't vermin. This was infrastructure.

"Is this a legal proceeding or an extermination?" Dale asked.

The woman tilted her head.

"Does it matter?

The Authority recognizes both.

One pays in points, the other in favors.

The Landlord has filed a formal grievance under Section 4, Paragraph 9 of the Inter-Dimensional Zoning Code.

"

"Section 4 is about noise complaints," Dale said.

"Not anymore," the woman replied. "It was amended this morning. To include 'Unlicensed Auditing of Private Entities.'"

Miller stepped forward.

"You can't change the law in three minutes."

"Watch me," she said. She snapped her fingers.

The iron bars on the courthouse doors dissolved into mist. The doors swung open.

Inside, the lobby was empty. No security. No clerks.

Just a long table in the center of the room. On one side sat a creature that looked like a pile of wet newspapers animated by static electricity.

On the other side, an empty chair.

"Sit," the woman said. "The hearing is in session."

Dale walked to the chair. He sat down. He placed the clipboard on the table. He clicked his pen.

"State the grievance," Dale said.

The newspaper creature rustled. A voice emerged from the crinkle of paper, sounding like leaves skittering across pavement.

"Mr. Kowalski violated the sanctity of the Private Sector Audit," the creature said. "He destroyed a certified asset without filing Form 11-B: Prior Notice of Regulatory Intervention."

Dale opened his Field Guide. He flipped to the blank page at the back. He wrote: *Plaintiff: The_Landlord_1984.

Species: Undetermined.

Likely Class V Entity. Weakness: Bureaucratic rigidity.*

"Form 11-B does not exist," Dale said.

"It does now," the creature said. "The Authority updates in real-time. Did you miss the patch notes?"

"I check the errata daily," Dale said. "Form 11-B is a myth. It's a hazing tradition for first-year inspectors."

The woman smiled.

"You're wrong. Look at your interface."

Dale blinked. A new icon had appeared in his peripheral vision. A red seal.

*Pending Grievance: Violation of Protocol. Penalty: Suspension of Certification. Status: Under Review.*

Dale felt a cold sweat prickle his neck. If he lost his Certification, he couldn't deploy protocols. He would be just a man with a clipboard in a world full of monsters.

Miller leaned over.

"Dale, ask for a recess."

"No recesses in emergency hearings," the woman said. "Proceedings are continuous until resolution."

"Then proceed," Dale said.

He stood up. He walked around the table. He inspected the newspaper creature.

He didn't look at its face. He looked at its edges.

The paper was damp.

Mold spores clung to the corners.

Dale pulled a small vial from his belt. He unscrewed the cap.

"What are you doing?" the creature asked.

"Sampling," Dale said.

"You are not authorized to collect evidence from a sentient defendant."

"I am authorized to inspect environmental hazards in any Authority-designated courtroom," Dale said. He dipped a cotton swab into the vial. He dabbed the creature's left corner.

The creature shrieked. The sound was high-pitched, like a drill hitting bone.

"Objection!" the woman shouted.

"Overruled," Dale said. He held up the swab. The cotton was black.

*Field Guide Updated: Entity identified as Mycelial Construct. Class: Fungal Infestation. Weakness: Desiccation.*

Dale uncapped his spray can. It wasn't the standard poison. It was a desiccant agent, Class II, designed for mold in basements.

"Cross-contamination penalty," the woman warned. "You are using a protocol designed for fungus against a sentient legal entity. This will debuff you by forty percent."

"I know," Dale said. He sprayed the swab.

The mist hit the newspaper creature. The paper instantly curled and turned grey. The static electricity fizzled out. The creature collapsed into a pile of dry, brittle leaves.

The silence in the courtroom was absolute.

Miller stared at Dale.

"You just killed the plaintiff."

"I treated the infestation," Dale said. He put the spray can away.

He sat back down. He clicked his pen.

"The grievance is moot.

The plaintiff is deceased. Case closed.

"

The woman stared at the pile of dust. Her face was unreadable. Then, she laughed. It was a dry, rattling sound.

"You think this is over?" she asked.

"The Authority rules," Dale said. "No plaintiff, no case. The audit stands."

"The Landlord is not a person, Mr.

Kowalski. The Landlord is a portfolio. You destroyed one employee.

There are others.

"

She pointed to the ceiling.

Above them, the ceiling tiles bulged. The drywall cracked. Green tendrils pushed through the plaster.

Miller drew his weapon.

"Dale, move!"

Dale didn't move. He watched the tendrils. They were thick, wet, and pulsing.

*Field Guide: Fungal Construct. Weakness: Desiccation.*

Dale reached for his spray can. Empty.

He looked at the desiccant vial. Empty.

He looked at the woman. She was backing away.

"Your resources are depleted," she said. "The Landlord knows your inventory.

That's why we hired me.

I'm not a lawyer. I'm a logistics manager.

"

The ceiling gave way. A massive fungal body dropped onto the table. It was the size of a van, covered in eyes that blinked independently.

Miller fired. The bullets hit the mold and vanished, absorbed into the wet mass.

"Fire is useless," Dale said. "Heat encourages growth."

"So what do we do?" Miller yelled. He backed toward the doors.

Dale looked at his clipboard. He looked at the System interface.

*Status: Debuffed (-40% Efficiency). Protocol: Desiccation (Unavailable).*

He needed a new protocol. He had one option.

*Protocol: Structural Remediation.*

This was a Class I ability. It was meant for sealing cracks in foundations.

It consumed high amounts of Treatment Points, and he didn't have enough points left.

He had spent them all on the Auditor.

Unless.

Dale looked at the woman. She was holding a briefcase.

"Give me the case," Dale said.

"It's evidence," she said.

"It's leverage," Dale said. "If you don't give it to me, I'll classify this room as a biohazard zone. I'll seal the doors. You'll be trapped in here with the infestation."

The woman hesitated. The fungal monster grew larger, spilling off the table. It reached for Miller.

Miller screamed as a tendril wrapped around his ankle.

"Do it!" Miller shouted.

The woman threw the briefcase.

Dale caught it. He opened it. Inside were files. Hundreds of them.

*Evidence: Landlord Holdings. Assets: 4,000 Untreated Infestations.*

Dale saw it. The loophole.

The Authority rewarded Thoroughness. The Landlord's grievance was based on the idea that Dale had acted without permission. But if Dale could prove that the Landlord had committed a more severe violation, the grievance would be dismissed to preserve the integrity of the Authority's records.

Dale opened his Field Guide. He started writing. Fast. Furiously.

*Violation: Failure to Declare Assets. Penalty: Immediate Seizure.*

He slammed his hand on the table.

"Authority!" Dale shouted. "I am filing a Counter-Grievance under Section 1, Paragraph 1. The Plaintiff has concealed four thousand Class IV Infestations. This constitutes fraud against the Licensing Authority."

The air in the room froze.

The fungal monster stopped. Its eyes widened.

The woman's face went pale.

"You can't do that. You haven't verified those assets."

"I don't need to verify them," Dale said. "I need to declare them. The Authority's system is automated.

If I file the claim, the system assumes good faith unless proven otherwise. You have thirty seconds to prove it's a lie.

"

"That's absurd," the woman whispered. "That's not how law works."

"It's how *this* system works," Dale said. He looked at the clock on the wall.

Twenty seconds.

The woman looked at the monster. The monster looked at Dale.

"Prove it's a lie," Dale said.

The woman opened her mouth.

Nothing came out. She didn't know the locations. She didn't have the access codes.

She was a logistics manager, not the Landlord.

Ten seconds.

Miller struggled against the tendril.

"Dale, do something!"

Dale stared at the woman. He clicked his pen.

Five seconds.

"Access denied," Dale said. "Claim accepted."

The world shifted.

The golden light of the Authority interface exploded in Dale's vision.

*Grievance Dismissed. Counter-Grievance Accepted.*

*Asset Seizure Initiated.*

*Thoroughness Rating: 10.0.*

*New Protocol Unlocked: Asset Forfeiture.*

The fungal monster didn't die. It changed.

The green tendrils turned grey. The wet mass hardened. The eyes closed. The body slumped, becoming inert stone.

The woman screamed. Not in anger. In fear.

"You've bankrupted him," she said. "The Landlord's portfolio is gone. You've taken it all."

Dale stood up. His knees shook. The debuff was gone, replaced by a surge of energy so potent it made his teeth ache.

He picked up the briefcase.

"The Landlord has a lawyer?" Dale asked.

"He had," the woman whispered. "Now he has creditors."

Miller pulled himself free from the stone mass. He looked at Dale.

"What the hell did you just do?"

"I audited his books," Dale said. He opened the briefcase.

Inside, the files weren't just paper anymore. They were glowing.

Each file contained a location.

A map. A target.

Four thousand targets.

Dale's Field Guide updated. A new entry appeared.

*Class IV Regulatory Enforcer: Active. Mission: Liquidate Assets.*

A notification popped up. It was red. Urgent.

*Priority One Infestation Detected: The Landlord's Primary Server.*

*Location: The Old Water Treatment Plant.*

*ETA: 10 Minutes.*

Dale closed the briefcase. He looked at Miller.

"We have work to do," Dale said.

Miller looked at the stone monster. He looked at the woman, who was now cowering in the corner.

"You just made a friend," Miller said. "Or an enemy. I can't tell which."

"Both," Dale said. He walked toward the doors.

"The Landlord isn't dead.

He's just insolvent.

And insolvent things are desperate.

"

He pushed open the doors. The sun was setting. The sky was bleeding orange.

In the parking lot, the other survivors were gathering. They saw the stone monster through the window. They saw the woman. They saw Dale holding the briefcase.

They didn't cheer. They stepped back.

Dale got in the truck. He put the briefcase on the passenger seat.

Miller got in. He started the engine.

"Where to?" Miller asked.

Dale looked at the map. The red dot pulsed.

"Water Treatment Plant," Dale said. "And Miller? Call Sarah."

"Why?"

"Tell her to bring bleach," Dale said. "A lot of it."

Miller drove. The tires spun on the gravel.

As they pulled away, Dale checked his clipboard, and he crossed out *Threat Neutralized*.

He wrote: *Threat Identified.*

Then, he added a new line.

*Recommendation: Total Eradication.*

The truck sped toward the industrial district. Behind them, the courthouse lights flickered and died.

In the rearview mirror, Dale saw the woman running out of the building. She wasn't running away. She was running toward him.

And she wasn't alone.

Shadows detached themselves from the buildings. Dozens of them.

Dale gripped the clipboard. He didn't reach for a weapon.

He reached for his pen.
