Chapter 138: The Auditor's Obituary

# Chapter 138

The Auditor's hand snapped.

Not with a crack, but with the wet, fibrous sound of drywall tearing under immense pressure. The fingers didn't break. They folded inward, collapsing like a paper lantern crushed by a careless thumb. Bone shards didn't spray; they compressed, grinding into one another until the hand became a dense, jagged fist of skin and calcium.

Marcus flinched. He was a former linebacker, three hundred pounds of muscle and bad decisions, but he knew fear when he saw it.

This wasn't fear of death, which was fear of the uncanny. The thing rising from the crater wasn't a monster in the traditional sense. It didn't growl.

It didn't salivate.

It adjusted its collar.

The high-visibility vest was neon orange, pristine, and stiff.

The reflective stripes caught the emergency floodlights from the perimeter, glowing with a sterile, bureaucratic intensity. The face was Dale Kowalski's, but wrong. The pores were too uniform.

The eyes lacked the red-rimmed exhaustion of a man who had been awake for thirty hours. They were wide, unblinking, and filled with a terrifying, hollow curiosity.

Marcus watched the Auditor step out of the rubble.

The debris didn't scatter.

It rearranged itself beneath the creature's boots, forming a clean, paved surface. The Auditor held the clipboard in its left hand.

In its right, it held nothing but air, yet the air distorted, shimmering with heat haze.

"It's a mimic," Marcus whispered, his voice trembling. He gripped his baton, knuckles white. "Dale, hit it with the spray."

Dale didn't move. He stood ten feet away, holding his own clipboard like a shield.

His posture wasn't defensive, which was procedural. He was waiting for the other party to speak.

The Auditor tilted its head. The neck cracked audibly, a sound like stepping on dry pasta.

"Subject: Dale Kowalski," the Auditor said.

The voice was Dale's, but pitched lower, stripped of all inflection.

"Status: Active.

License: Class II.

Thoroughness Rating: 9.2. Anomaly detected: Unauthorized engagement with Class-X entities.

"

Marcus lowered his baton slightly.

"What is that thing?"

"It's an inspection," Dale said, not looking at Marcus. "Don't attack it. Attacking an inspector is a citation."

"Citation?" Marcus blinked. "It's trying to kill you."

"It's trying to audit me. There's a difference."

The Auditor raised its clipboard. A stylus, made of bone, extended from its wrist. It tapped the surface. The sound was sharp, echoing in the ruined basement.

"Violation 1: Failure to quarantine Zone 4. Penalty: Immediate suspension of license. Suspension of biological functions."

Marcus looked at Dale.

"What does that mean? What happens if you fail?"

Dale finally looked at his assistant. His face was pale, but his hands were steady. He adjusted his safety goggles.

"It means I get fired," Dale said. "And if the Authority fires me, I don't exist anymore. Not in this world. Not in the other one."

The Auditor took a step forward. The floor beneath its foot turned gray and smooth, like polished concrete.

The infestation wasn't just physical; it was bureaucratic, which was erasing the chaos of the basement and replacing it with order.

A terrible, sterile order.

"Objection," Dale said.

The Auditor paused. The stylus hovered over the paper.

"State your defense," the Auditor droned.

"Entry 44-B: The infestation originated from a structural failure in the building's foundation, not from an active breach by the technician. Therefore, the quarantine zone was established per protocol, but the breach source was outside the jurisdiction of Class II licensing."

The Auditor's eyes narrowed. The skin around them rippled.

"Jurisdictional dispute noted. Cross-referencing with Municipal Code 7.4.2."

The air in the room grew heavy. Marcus felt a pressure in his ears, a low hum that vibrated in his molars. The System notification flared in his peripheral vision, but it wasn't his. It was Dale's.

**SYSTEM ALERT: LEGAL LOOPHOLE DETECTED.** **ACTION: CITE PRECEDENT.** **REWARD: +500 TREATMENT POINTS. TEMPORARY IMMUNITY TO CLASS-X AGENTS.**

Marcus saw the text. He looked at Dale. Dale was sweating.

Not from heat, but from effort. He was writing on his clipboard, his handwriting cramped and fast.

"Cite precedent?" Marcus asked, confused.

Dale didn't answer. He ripped a page from his clipboard. He tore it in half. He held the left half up.

"Precedent: The Case of the Suburban Sinkhole, Chapter 12," Dale shouted. "The technician was not held liable for rifts caused by tectonic shifts unless negligence was proven in maintenance logs."

The Auditor froze. The stylus stopped moving.

For a second, the creature looked confused. Its face flickered, the features shifting like static on an old TV. The high-visibility vest rippled, the reflective tape peeling slightly.

"Insufficient data," the Auditor said. "Processing."

"Process it!" Dale yelled. He stepped forward.

"Dale, stop!" Marcus shouted. He raised his baton. "It's loading! It's going to crash and then it's going to eat you!"

"It's not going to eat me," Dale said. "It's going to wait."

Dale walked right up to the Auditor.

The creature towered over him, three feet taller, its shoulders broad and unnatural. The smell coming off it wasn't rot. It was ozone and old paper.

The scent of a filing cabinet left in a damp basement.

Dale held out his clipboard.

"Check my logs," Dale said. "Page 4, line 12.

I reported the foundation crack three weeks ago.

Ticket number 8940-K. You have it.

"

The Auditor looked down. Its hand, the one that had snapped, was reassembling. Fingers clicked back into place, joint by joint.

The Auditor looked at Dale's clipboard. Then it looked at its own.

A long, agonizing silence stretched across the room. The hum in the walls grew louder, shaking dust from the ceiling. Debris rattled. The lights flickered.

Marcus held his breath. He watched the Auditor's eyes scan the paper. He watched Dale's knuckles turn white as he gripped his clipboard.

Finally, the Auditor nodded.

"Ticket 8940-K verified. Liability shifted to Municipal Infrastructure Department. Suspension revoked."

The Auditor lowered its clipboard.

"Recommendation: Upgrade technician to Class III. Reason: Proficient in jurisdictional maneuvering."

The air pressure dropped. The humming stopped.

The gray concrete under the Auditor's feet cracked, reverting to dirt and rubble.

The creature stepped back, fading slightly, which was becoming less solid, less present.

But it didn't leave.

"However," the Auditor said. "A new violation has been noted. You have engaged in direct dialogue with an Auditor without a supervisor present. This is a protocol breach."

Dale's face went blank.

"I didn't know the rule."

"Ignorance of the protocol is not a defense," the Auditor said. "Penalty: Demotion to Class I. Loss of Field Guide access for seven days."

Marcus let out a breath.

"That's it? That's the penalty?"

Dale looked at Marcus. His eyes were dark.

"No. That's the public penalty."

The Auditor smiled. It was the same fake smile from before.

"The private penalty," the Auditor continued, "is that you are now marked. You have demonstrated that you can exploit the System. This makes you a threat to the Authority's integrity."

The Auditor raised its hand again. This time, it didn't snap. It pointed.

"New Objective: Eradication."

The System notification exploded in Marcus's mind.

**CLASS-X ENTITY DESIGNATION: AUDITOR.** **STATUS: HOSTILE.** **PROTOCOL: ELIMINATE OR ESCALATE.** **WARNING: CROSS-CONTAMINATION PENALTY ACTIVE.**

Marcus looked at his baton. It was useless. He looked at Dale.

Dale was already moving. He wasn't retreating. He was running toward the crater.

"Dale!" Marcus screamed. "We need to evacuate! The Authority said evacuate!"

"The Authority is lying," Dale said. He didn't look back. "It wants us to leave so it can seal the breach.

If we leave, the infestation spreads. If we stay, we get erased.

"

Dale jumped into the hole.

Marcus ran after him. He couldn't let him go. He had to be there. He had to witness it.

He slid down the rubble, the rocks shifting under his boots, and the darkness below was absolute. But then, a light appeared. Not a flashlight.

A glow.

Dale was standing at the bottom of the shaft. He was holding his flashlight, but the beam was dead.

Instead, he was holding a small, metallic device.

A bug zapper. A cheap, plastic thing he'd used for gnats in his garage.

The Auditor descended behind him, silent and swift. It landed on the platform with a thud. The ground didn't shake. It absorbed the impact.

"You cannot kill me with a zapper," the Auditor said. "I am a concept."

"You're a creature," Dale said. He clicked the zapper on.

A small, purple arc of electricity snapped between the two grids.

"And concepts have weaknesses.

You're made of rules. Rules can be broken.

"

Dale threw the zapper.

It wasn't aimed at the Auditor. It was aimed at the ground.

The zapper hit the dirt. The electricity arced into the soil.

Nothing happened.

The Auditor laughed. A dry, rasping sound.

"Futile."

"Wait for it," Dale said.

The dirt vibrated. The zapper's current was low, but it was precise. It was targeting the root system of the infestation. The mycelial network that connected the rats, the beetles, and now the Auditor.

The ground erupted.

Not with monsters. With roots. Thick, pale tendrils burst from the earth, wrapping around the Auditor's legs. The creature struggled, its face twisting in frustration.

"Protocol violation," the Auditor shrieked. "You are contaminating the field guide!"

"I'm not contaminating it," Dale said. He pulled a spray can from his belt. Not poison.

Glue. Industrial-grade adhesive.

He sprayed the roots, and they hardened. Instantly. The Auditor was trapped in a cage of its own biology, fused to the earth.

The creature's form unravelled. The high-visibility vest dissolved into dust. The face melted, not like wax, but like paper being erased.

But it didn't die, which kept smiling.

"You have won the battle," the Auditor said, its voice fading. "But you have lost the war. The Authority will send more. They will send a Supervisor."

The Auditor's body collapsed into a pile of ash and old paper.

Silence returned to the basement.

Marcus stood over the pile, breathing hard. His heart hammered against his ribs. He looked at Dale.

Dale was on his knees, shaking. He was trying to write on his clipboard, but his hands were shaking too much. The pen slipped, leaving a jagged line across the page.

"Did you get points?" Marcus asked.

Dale looked up. His eyes were wide, terrified.

"I don't know," Dale whispered. "I don't know if it counts."

A notification pinged in Marcus's vision.

**JOB COMPLETE.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 10/10.** **POINTS AWARDED: 10,000.** **CERTIFICATION LEVEL: CLASS III UNLOCKED.**

Marcus let out a whoop.

"Dude! We did it! We actually did it!"

He grabbed Dale's shoulder, pulling him up.

"We're Class III! We can take on the big stuff now!"

Dale didn't celebrate. He stared at the pile of ash.

"No," Dale said. "We didn't kill it. We just delayed it."

He picked up a piece of the ash. It was paper. He unfolded it.

It was a receipt.

*Transaction: 1 Soul.* *Value: Null.* *Status: Pending.*

Marcus frowned.

"What is that?"

Dale crushed the paper in his hand.

"It means we didn't pay for the kill. We're in debt."

Before Marcus could ask what that meant, the ground shook again. But this time, it wasn't the basement.

It was the house above them, and a crash came from the ceiling. Plaster dust rained down.

Then, a voice. Not from the Auditor. From the living room.

"Mom? Dad? I'm home!"

Marcus froze. "That's... that's my kid's voice."

Dale looked at the ceiling. His face went slack.

"It's not your kid," Dale said. "It's the next step."

The System notification appeared one last time.

**INFESTATION UPDATE.** **NEW HOST ENTRY DETECTED.** **SPECIES: CHILDHOOD TRAUMA (VARIANT: ABANDONMENT).** **THREAT LEVEL: PSYCHOLOGICAL.** **PROTOCOL: ENGAGE OR SURRENDER?**

Marcus looked at Dale. Dale looked at the stairs.

"We have to go up," Dale said.

"To save him?" Marcus asked.

Dale shook his head. He picked up his clipboard. He wrote a new entry.

*Entry: Family Unit.* *Weakness: Expectation.*

"We have to go up," Dale said, "because if we don't, we don't have a home to go back to."

Marcus looked at the stairs. They were dark. And from the darkness, something small and human was calling his name.

"Dad?"

Marcus took a step forward.

Dale grabbed his arm.

"Don't," Dale said. "Don't answer it. It's not a monster. It's a trap."

"But it's my son," Marcus said, his voice breaking.

"It's a reflection," Dale said. "And reflections don't have licenses."

The thing in the house screamed. It wasn't a scream of pain. It was a scream of joy.

"Marcus! Come here! I found something!"

Marcus jerked his arm free. He ran toward the stairs.

Dale didn't chase him. He stayed in the basement. He sat down in the pile of ash.

He pulled out his Field Guide. He flipped to the new entry.

*Species: Auditor.* *Weakness: Bureaucracy.*

He underlined it. Then he wrote a new line beneath it.

*Weakness 2: Grief.*

The screaming from above grew louder. Marcus was yelling now, pleading with the thing.

Dale closed his eyes. He listened.

He wasn't afraid of the monster. He was afraid of what Marcus would do when he realized the monster was right.

The stairs groaned. The wood splintered.

Something climbed up from the basement, into the house, which had Marcus's face.

Dale opened his eyes. He stood up.

He picked up his clipboard.

He walked toward the stairs, and he had to document this. He had to write it down. Because if he didn't, no one would know what happened here.

And if no one knew, the Authority would win.

The first step of the protocol was identification.

Dale Kowalski raised his pen.

He was ready to write the obituary.
