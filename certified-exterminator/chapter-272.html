Chapter 272: Frass and Static

# Chapter 272

The ground did not merely open. It unzipped.

Reality peeled back like wet cardboard, revealing the raw, pulsating static of the Rift beneath. Corliss screamed, a high-pitched sound that cut through the hum of the Nullifier canisters. Dale didn't look at his apprentice. He looked at the dirt.

The soil wasn't turning to dust. It was turning to frass.

Millions of tiny, chitinous pellets poured from the fissure, cascading over Dale's boots. The Authority's hazmat team froze. Their canisters sputtered, the white mist failing to neutralize the sudden influx of biological matter.

Dale stepped back. His boot crunched on a pile of waste.

"Contamination," Dale said. His voice was flat, devoid of fear. "You're using aerosols on a granular infestation."

The lead worker, whose suit bore the insignia of a Level IX Warden, took a step forward. His eyes narrowed behind the visor.

"Step away from the breach, Technician. This is a containment zone."

"You're spreading spores," Dale said. "Look at the air."

The worker glanced up. The air above the fissure shimmered with microscopic motes.

They weren't just dust, and they were eggs. Microscopic, airborne, and rapidly hatching.

"Nullify the source," the worker ordered. He raised his canister.

"No," Dale said. He didn't shout. He didn't beg. He simply raised his hand.

The worker fired.

The jet of white chemical hit Dale squarely in the chest.

Dale didn't fly backward, and he didn't dissolve. He absorbed it.

The Nullifier hit his Class II protective coating, a standard-issue polymer derived from chitin and lead.

It smeared, failed, and slid off.

Dale's Field Guide flashed in his peripheral vision.

**SYSTEM ALERT: CROSS-CONTAMINATION DETECTED.** **Agent: Nullifier (Class VI).** **Target: Human Host (Non-target).** **Penalty: -40% Efficacy. Field Guide Entry 'Void Warden' corrupted for 24 hours.**

The worker stared. The canister was empty. The chemical had done nothing. It had only tainted the air, mixing with the airborne eggs to create a sticky, black resin that webbed between the workers' legs.

"Your license is revoked," the worker said, but his voice wavered. He looked at his boots. Black sludge was climbing his shins. "You are a hazard."

"I am the site manager," Dale corrected.

He walked toward the fissure.

The hazmat team parted. Not out of respect, but out of instinct.

The black resin was moving.

It wasn't just sludge, which was a colony. The Void's waste had a hive mind.

And it was hungry.

Dale pulled a spray bottle from his hip. Not a weapon. A solvent. Industrial-grade degreaser, modified with a pinch of salt and a dash of his own blood to bind the magic.

"Corliss," Dale said. "Do not touch the ground. Do not breathe the air. Start the dehumidifier."

Corliss was shaking, her face pale beneath her respirator.

"Dale, they have guns. They have Level IX clearance."

"They have a Class VI protocol," Dale said. "And they're using it wrong. That's a code violation."

He reached the edge of the fissure. The eye in the sky blinked.

A massive, lidless thing, swollen with malice.

It looked at the hazmat team. It looked at Dale.

Dale looked back. He pulled out his clipboard.

"Observation," he muttered. "Subject exhibits localized reality degradation. Symptom: Frass rain. Secondary symptom: Spontaneous chitinous growth on unauthorized personnel."

He wrote it down.

The act of writing stabilized him. The ink flowed smoothly, black against white.

The Authority's system responded to his focus.

A faint blue glow emanated from the paper.

**TREATMENT POINTS AWARDED: 50.** **Reason: Accurate Identification of Breach Signature.**

The worker shouted something. A command.

A threat.

Dale ignored it, and he uncapped the spray bottle.

The black resin on the workers' legs twitched. It rose, forming jagged spikes. Two of the hazmat suits burst open, spraying blood and viscera. The Void was harvesting them.

Dale sprayed.

Not at the monster. At the ground.

The degreaser hit the frass pile. The reaction was immediate. The black sludge hissed, bubbling, then solidifying into a hard, brittle crust.

The airborne eggs dropped dead. The spores ceased to float.

Silence rushed into the clearing.

The remaining hazmat workers stared at their legs, and the resin had hardened into a shell, trapping them in place. They couldn't move. They couldn't run.

They were statues of their own mistake.

Dale turned to the lead worker. The man was still upright, his suit intact, but his eyes were wide with a terror that went beyond fear. It was the terror of a god realizing it was mortal.

"You used a Class VI agent on a Class VIII infestation," Dale said. "You didn't document the interaction. You didn't isolate the vectors. You didn't verify the clearance."

Dale pointed to the hardened shell encasing the worker's legs.

"That's your containment. You're stuck."

The worker tried to raise his empty canister. His arm jerked, but the hardened resin locked it in place.

"This is treason," the worker wheezed. "The Authority does not tolerate insubordination."

"The Authority tolerates thoroughness," Dale said. "You're sloppy. You left gaps. You didn't seal the entry points."

Dale walked to the fissure. The frass was still falling, but slower now.

The eye in the sky pulsed, confused.

It sensed the change in the air. The chemical barrier was holding.

Dale pulled a roll of duct tape from his belt.

Corliss gasped.

"Dale.

The Void.

It's not physical.

You can't tape it.

"

"Everything is physical if you have enough leverage," Dale said.

He stepped into the edge of the fissure. The static burned his skin. His hands blistered instantly.

He didn't flinch. He didn't look at his hands.

He looked at the tear in space.

It wasn't a hole. It was a rip. A tear in the fabric of the dimensional membrane. And like any tear in a sheet, it needed to be hemmed.

Dale applied the tape, and he pressed the adhesive side against the glowing edge of the Rift. The tape sizzled, melting slightly, then hardening. He pulled the other end across the gap, overlapping the first strip.

He worked in silence.

*Strip one.* *Strip two.* *Strip three.*

The hazmat worker watched, paralyzed.

"You're insane. You're trying to patch a hole in reality with... with office supplies."

"It's not office supplies," Dale said, applying the fourth strip. "It's a temporary sealant. Class I."

The eye in the sky screamed. A sound like tearing metal. The ground shook.

Dale ignored it. He applied the fifth strip.

The field around the fissure dimmed. The static died down. The frass stopped falling.

But the eye didn't close. It stared at Dale. And for the first time, Dale felt a pressure in his mind.

Not pain.

A question.

*Who are you?*

Dale applied the sixth strip.

"I am a technician," Dale said aloud. "And you are in violation of Section 4, Paragraph B: Unauthorized Dimensional Intrusion."

He pulled the last strip. He pressed it down with all his weight.

The Rift sealed.

The sky returned to a dull, bruised gray. The eye vanished. The pressure in Dale's skull dissipated, leaving behind a throbbing headache and the smell of burnt rubber.

Dale sat back on his heels. His hands were raw, red, and shaking. He breathed out, a long, ragged exhale.

Corliss ran to him, and she checked his hands, her face twisted in disgust.

"We need to get you to a clinic. Or a hospital.

Or anywhere that isn't here.

"

"No," Dale said. He picked up his clipboard. "We need to finish the report."

He looked at the hazmat worker. The man was still trapped in his own resin shell, his face contorted in a silent scream.

"Documentation," Dale said. He wrote a final entry.

**SUBJECT: Void Warden (Class IX).** **ACTION: Containment Failure. Site contaminated by unauthorized agent.** **STATUS: Suspended. Pending Review.**

He tore the page off the clipboard and handed it to Corliss.

"File this with the local Authority branch. Do not go to the main hub. Go to the satellite office in Dayton. They have a better Thoroughness Rating."

Corliss stared at the paper.

"And then what?"

"And then we wait," Dale said.

A sound approached. Not footsteps. Not engines. The hum of a drone.

A black quadcopter descended from the clouds. It didn't land. It hovered ten feet above Dale's head, its rotors spinning with unnatural precision. A small speaker grille on its underbelly lit up.

"Dale Kowalski," a voice said.

It was smooth, synthesized, and cold.

"You have violated three protocols. You have assaulted Authority personnel.

You have used unlicensed materials on a Class VIII breach.

"

Dale looked up. He didn't look away.

"I completed the job," Dale said. "The breach is sealed. The site is secure."

"The site is compromised," the drone said. "You touched the Void.

Your DNA is altered.

Your Field Guide is corrupted. You are no longer a Certified Exterminator. You are an anomaly.

"

Dale felt the system alert blink.

**STATUS: ANOMALOUS ENTITY DETECTED.** **CLASSIFICATION: UNLICENSED.** **ACTION: PURGE.**

Dale's stomach dropped, and he looked at his hands. The raw skin was changing.

The redness was fading, replaced by a gray, chitinous texture.

His fingernails were thickening.

He looked at Corliss. She was backing away, her eyes wide.

"Dale?"

"I'm fine," Dale said. But his voice sounded different. Guttural. Clicking.

The drone dropped a payload.

It wasn't a weapon. It was a tag. A small, metallic square that shot toward Dale's chest.

He caught it.

It bit into his palm.

**SYSTEM ALERT: TRACKING DEVICE DEPLOYED.** **SUBJECT: DALE KOWALSKI.** **STATUS: MARKED FOR DELETION.**

The drone ascended.

"You have twenty-four hours to report to the Licensing Authority for re-evaluation. Failure to appear will result in immediate termination of your license and your life."

Dale looked at the tag in his hand. It pulsed with a red light. He looked at his gray, hardening skin. He looked at the sealed fissure, now just a patch of ordinary dirt.

He stood up. His joints cracked. The sound was like breaking glass.

"Twenty-four hours," Dale said.

He walked toward his truck. Corliss followed, hesitant, keeping a distance.

The hazmat worker watched them go, trapped in his own waste, screaming silently into the empty air.

Dale opened the truck door. He didn't look in the mirror.

He didn't need to, and he could feel the changes happening inside him.

The Void wasn't gone. It was inside him.

And it was hungry.

He started the engine.

"Where are we going?" Corliss asked.

Dale gripped the steering wheel. His fingers were too strong. The plastic cracked.

"We're going to Dayton," Dale said. "I need to find out what I am."

The truck pulled away.

Behind them, the sealed fissure cracked. A single, tiny eye opened in the dirt, looking up at the retreating vehicle.

It blinked.

And then it smiled.
