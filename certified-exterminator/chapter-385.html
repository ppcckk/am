Chapter 385: Refusal of Zero Points

# Chapter 385

The word HELP faded from the page before Dale could blink.

No notification flashed. No golden light bathed the crumbling library. The Authority remained silent.

Dale lowered the clipboard. His hands shook, a fine tremor that traveled up the forearms and into the shoulders.

He forced them steady by gripping the metal edges until his knuckles turned white.

Class IX. The highest tier. The theoretical ceiling.

He had studied the certification manuals, memorized the abstract warnings, but he had never seen one.

Not in the field. Not in the archives.

The hand continued to pull.

Concrete slabs shifted with a grinding roar, sliding past one another like teeth in a broken jaw. Dust choked the air, thick and gray, coating Dale's safety goggles in a fine layer of particulate matter.

Martinez screamed, a high, thin sound that cut through the structural groaning. The junior technician clung to Dale's sleeve, his face a mask of terror and white powder.

"Run," Martinez gasped.

Dale didn't run. He couldn't. Running meant abandoning the workspace. It meant leaving the evidence incomplete.

"Stay down," Dale said.

His voice was flat, devoid of the panic clawing at his throat. He scanned the fissure. The hand was pale, slick with subterranean mucus, and possessed seven fingers on the left side.

The joints bent in directions that defied human anatomy, snapping backward with sickening wet pops.

Dale's Field Guide, projected into his peripheral vision, remained blank for the entity. **NAME: UNKNOWN. CLASS: IX.**

No weakness. No protocol.

He looked at the chalk. He looked at the hand. He looked at the empty fire extinguisher.

A new notification pinged, soft and indifferent.

**SYSTEM ALERT: LOCALIZED STRUCTURAL FAILURE IMMINENT.** **RECOMMENDED ACTION: EVACUATE.** **PENALTY FOR ABANDONMENT OF ACTIVE INFESTATION ZONE: -50 TREATMENT POINTS.**

Dale stared at the message.

The Authority wanted him to run.

It wanted him to save his own skin, to preserve the resource. But the job wasn't done.

The nest was exposed. If he left now, the hole would remain open. The infestation would spread.

He picked up a piece of rebar from the rubble. It was rusted, jagged, useless as a weapon.

"Martinez," Dale said, not looking at the man. "The napkin."

Martinez blinked, tears cutting tracks through the dust on his cheeks.

"What?"

"The napkin. You're still holding it."

"I... yes."

"Throw it at the hand."

Martinez looked confused, but his body obeyed. He flung the damp, soiled napkin toward the fissure. It fluttered through the dust-choked air, landing on the pale skin of the creature's wrist.

Nothing happened.

The hand paused. The seven fingers twitched. It tilted its wrist, examining the foreign object.

Dale's eyes narrowed. He watched the System log.

**INTERACTION DETECTED: CONTAMINANT CONTACT.** **ENTITY RESPONSE: CONFUSION.**

Confusion. Not pain. Not anger. Confusion.

The creature was reacting to the napkin. Why?

Dale's mind raced through the protocols. Contamination.

Cross-contamination.

The rules stated that deploying a protocol designed for one creature type against another inflicted a debuff. But this wasn't a protocol. This was raw material.

He looked at the napkin. It had been used to wipe the face of the S-9 inspector. It was soaked in the sizzling chemical from Dale's sprayer. A standard Class II solvent.

The entity was reacting to the chemical residue.

Dale grabbed the spray can, which was empty. He grabbed the other can from his belt.

Half full.

Class II. Useless against Class IX.

Or was it?

He looked at the System rules scrolling in his mind. **Cross-contamination penalty: deploying a protocol designed for one creature type against another inflicts a 40% debuff.**

The debuff applied to Dale. It contaminated his Field Guide.

But what if the debuff applied to the target?

The Authority rewarded thoroughness. It rewarded documentation. And right now, the documentation showed the entity reacting to a specific contaminant.

Dale didn't have a protocol for Class IX. He had a field guide entry for Class II vermin. And he had a half-full can of Class II solvent.

He stepped forward.

Martinez grabbed his belt.

"You're insane."

"Keep your head down," Dale said.

He approached the edge of the fissure. The hand was pulling harder now, dragging a shoulder through the cracked concrete. The arm was long, disproportionate, stretching like taffy.

Dale aimed the spray can. He didn't aim for the hand. He aimed for the gap between the shoulder and the wall, where the flesh pressed against the rebar.

He squeezed the trigger.

The nozzle sputtered. A mist of orange chemical hit the pale skin.

The entity shrieked.

It wasn't a sound of pain. It was a frequency.

A vibration that rattled Dale's teeth.

The hand jerked back, pulling the shoulder away from the wall.

The fingers spasmed, curling into claws.

**SYSTEM LOG:** **PROTOCOL MISMATCH DETECTED.** **CROSS-CONTAMINATION PENALTY APPLIED TO TARGET?** **CALCULATING...**

Dale held his breath. The calculation hung in the air, visible only to him.

**PENALTY APPLIED TO TARGET: 40% DEFENSE DEGRADATION.** **REASON: INCOMPATIBLE BIOLOGY INTERACTION.**

Dale's heart hammered against his ribs. He had exploited a loophole. The penalty for using the wrong tool was usually borne by the user.

But here, the "wrongness" of the interaction had disrupted the entity's defensive resonance. It wasn't damaging the flesh.

It was unraveling the structure, and the hand went limp. It dropped back into the fissure.

The shaking stopped.

Silence rushed back into the library, heavy and suffocating.

Dale lowered the spray can. His hands were steady now. The tremor was gone, replaced by a cold, clinical focus.

He looked at the System.

**FIELD GUIDE ENTRY UPDATED.** **NAME: UNKNOWN (Preliminary Designation: Class IX Entity).** **NOTED WEAKNESS: Chemical Solvent (Class II) causes structural destabilization when applied to exposed connective tissue.** **NOTE: THIS IS A CROSS-CONTAMINATION EVENT. DO NOT REPEAT WITHOUT AUTHORITY APPROVAL.**

The Authority had logged it. It had accepted the data, even though the method was inefficient. Inefficient, but effective.

Dale walked back to the center of the room. The floor was a ruin. The ceiling hung in precarious shards. The inspector lay unconscious near the exit, his suit smoking.

Dale checked his points.

**TREATMENT POINTS EARNED: 0.** **REASON: INFESTATION NOT ELIMINATED. NEST STILL ACTIVE.**

Zero, and he hadn't killed it. He hadn't documented the kill. He had only paused the threat.

"Martinez," Dale said.

The technician was shaking, sitting up slowly. He looked at the fissure. The hand was gone.

"Did we kill it?" Martinez whispered.

"No," Dale said. He picked up his clipboard. The chalk was broken. He used the stub, writing in small, precise letters.

**STATUS: CONTAINED. TEMPORARY.** **ACTION REQUIRED: CLASS IX PROTOCOL.** **RESOURCE SHORTAGE: CRITICAL.**

He looked at the exit. The sun was setting, casting long, bloody shadows through the shattered windows.

"We need to leave," Dale said. "Now."

Martinez scrambled to his feet. He grabbed the unconscious inspector by the collar and dragged him toward the door.

Dale followed, his eyes scanning the room one last time. He needed to see the nest. He needed to know what had been down there.

He paused at the edge of the fissure, and the darkness below was deep. Absolute.

And then, a sound.

Not a roar. Not a scream.

A click.

Like a camera shutter.

Dale froze.

From the depths of the fissure, a lens emerged. Small. Black. Round.

It focused on Dale.

The System notification appeared, red and urgent.

**NEW ENTRY DETECTED.** **NAME: OBSERVER UNIT.** **CLASS: VIII.** **STATUS: ACTIVE.** **ACTION: DOCUMENTATION IN PROGRESS.**

Dale's blood ran cold.

Someone was watching. Someone with a Class VIII protocol.

He looked at the lens. It didn't blink. It didn't move. It just recorded.

Then, a second notification.

**AUTHORITY ALERT:** **UNAUTHORIZED OBSERVATION DETECTED.** **CITATION ISSUED TO UNLICENSED ENTITY.** **PENALTY: TERMINATION IMMINENT.**

The lens shattered.

Not from an explosion. From a force outside the frame. Something had crushed it from the other side.

The fissure sealed. The ground groaned, rising. Dirt and concrete pushed upward, filling the hole.

Dale stared at the rising earth.

Who had crushed the camera?

Who had the authority to terminate a Class VIII entity in seconds?

He looked at the inspector, still unconscious on the floor. The inspector's tie was covered in the sizzling chemical.

Dale remembered the inspector's face. The calm, professional demeanor. The way he had checked his watch before stepping into the line of fire.

Dale walked over to the inspector. He checked the man's pulse. Strong. Regular.

He searched the inspector's pockets.

A wallet. A keycard. A notebook.

Dale opened the notebook. The pages were blank.

He turned to the back.

There was a stamp. Small, blue, official.

**LICENSING AUTHORITY - INTERIOR DIVISION.**

**CLEARANCE: LEVEL X.**

Dale's breath hitched.

Level X.

There was no Level X in the public certification tracks. Level IX was the max.

He looked at the inspector's face. The man wasn't an auditor. He wasn't a bureaucrat.

He was a cleaner.

And he had just been fired.

Dale stood up. He looked at Martinez, who was waiting by the door, eyes wide.

"We're not leaving," Dale said.

"Why?" Martinez asked.

Dale held up the notebook. The blue stamp glowed faintly in the dim light.

"Because they're not here to fix the infestation," Dale said, his voice dropping to a whisper. "They're here to hide it."

A shadow fell over them from the entrance.

Dale turned.

Standing in the doorway was a man in a black suit. No helmet. No gear. Just a crisp, white shirt and a pair of latex gloves.

He held a clipboard.

"Mr. Kowalski," the man said. His voice was smooth, devoid of accent. "You've been flagged for unlicensed interference with a Level X operation."

Dale looked at the man. He looked at the clipboard.

"Show your license," Dale said.

The man smiled. It was a polite, terrifying smile.

"I don't need one," he said. "I have immunity."

He raised a hand. The air around his fingers warped.

"Your certification is revoked. Effective immediately."

Dale's System notification flashed.

**CERTIFICATION STATUS: REVOKED.** **ALL PROTOCOLS LOCKED.** **FIELD GUIDE: OFFLINE.**

Dale felt the connection to the System sever. The familiar hum in his ears vanished. The peripheral vision went dark.

He was just a man. In a broken building. With a broken clipboard.

The man in the black suit stepped forward.

"Please put down the notebook, Mr. Kowalski."

Dale looked at the notebook in his hand. He looked at the man.

He thought about the rules. Thoroughness. Documentation. Verification.

He thought about the zero points, and he thought about the Level X stamp.

Dale tightened his grip on the notebook.

"No," Dale said.
