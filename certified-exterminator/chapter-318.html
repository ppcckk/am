Chapter 318: Interview with Darkness

# Chapter 318

The woman in the hazmat suit was screaming, but not with her mouth.

She was screaming with her radio.

Static crackled, sharp and white, cutting through the wet air of the subterranean cavern. Dale hung suspended in the void, his boots dangling over a drop that didn't seem to have a bottom.

The pen in his hand was gone, swallowed by the fall, and the clipboard was gone. Only the microphone remained, clutched so tightly his knuckles had turned the color of old bone.

Above him, through the jagged hole in the earth, a figure in bright orange vinyl struggled against a harness.

Her helmet was fogged. Her hands shook as she adjusted the winch.

She wasn't looking down into the pit.

She was looking at the readout on her wrist computer, her eyes wide behind the visor, calculating the tensile strength of a rope that was already fraying.

She didn't know what was down there. She only knew the budget for the retrieval team was capped at forty minutes, and she was currently thirty-two minutes into a job that technically shouldn't exist.

Dale watched her. He watched the way her body language screamed *liability*.

He didn't scream back. He breathed.

The air here tasted like copper and ozone. It was thick, heavy, pressing against his eardrums with a pressure that suggested depth, not just distance. The grinding hum from before had faded, replaced by a silence so absolute it felt like a physical weight.

**System Alert: Environmental Hazard Detected.** **Type: Atmospheric Pressure / Bioluminescent Spores.** **Status: Unidentified.**

Dale ignored the text floating in his peripheral vision. He focused on the sound.

A wet, clicking noise, rhythmic and slow, echoing off the wet walls of the shaft.

It wasn't a monster moving, which was something settling.

The figure above jerked. The rope went taut. A jolt rattled Dale's teeth.

"Pull up," the radio crackled, a distorted voice barking instructions from the surface. "We're losing integrity on the anchor point. If you hit the bottom, you're on your own."

Dale didn't pull. He let the rope go slack.

He dropped the last ten feet, landing in a crouch on a surface that was soft, yielding, and warm. It wasn't rock. It was chitin. Massive plates of dark, iridescent shell covered the floor of the cavern, rising and falling with a slow, labored breath.

He was standing on the back of something.

The System notification flashed red, a color Dale had never seen before.

**Critical Error: Species Identification Mismatch.** **Field Guide: Empty.** **Action: Immediate Termination Recommended.**

"Recommended," Dale muttered, his voice sounding flat and dead in the heavy air. "Right. Terminate. How do I terminate a thing I can't see?"

He looked around. The cavern was vast, a cathedral of underground horror. Stalactites dripped with a viscous, glowing fluid that pooled in cracks in the chitin.

The clicking noise was coming from everywhere now. Shadows shifted along the walls, but nothing took form.

The rope above snapped.

Not a clean break. A slow unraveling, strand by strand, the fibers giving way under the stress of the winch and the sudden shift in weight.

Dale looked up. The orange-clad figure vanished into the darkness above, swallowed by the shrinking circle of sky.

He was alone.

And he was standing on a nest.

The ground beneath him shuddered.

The chitin plates shifted, revealing a gap. A fissure. From the dark space below the shell, a limb emerged.

Not a tentacle this time.

A leg. Jointed, segmented, covered in fine hairs that stood up like static charge.

It was the size of a sedan.

Dale didn't move, and he couldn't. The First Contact Probe was still in his hand, useless without an audio source to amplify. He had no weapon.

He had no training for this.

But he had the protocol.

*Identify.

Document.

Isolate. Eliminate.

Verify.*

He couldn't eliminate. He couldn't even isolate. He had to identify.

The leg moved. It didn't strike.

It tapped.

A rhythmic, percussive tap against the chitin. *Click. Click. Click.*

It was a language. Or a lure.

Dale stepped off the creature's back. He hit the floor of the cavern proper, his boots slipping on the slick, glowing fluid. He scrambled back, putting distance between himself and the massive leg.

The creature retracted the limb. The chitin plates sealed shut with a wet, suctioning sound.

Silence returned.

Then, a new sound. A whisper. Not from the creature. From the air.

"Help us."

The voice was small, childlike, which came from a cluster of stalagmites to Dale's left. They weren't rock.

They were eggs.

Thousands of them, translucent and pulsing with a faint blue light. Inside each one, a shape curled, waiting.

Dale raised the microphone. He pointed it at the eggs.

Nothing happened. The audio input was clear, but the System didn't register a threat. The eggs were passive. Dormant.

Until they weren't.

One egg cracked. A sliver of black fluid oozed out. Then another. Then a dozen.

The clicking noise started again, faster this time. A chorus.

Dale realized the problem. The creature wasn't the threat.

The eggs were.

And they weren't waiting to hatch, and they were waiting to be fed.

He looked at his inventory.

*First Contact Probe.* *Pen (Lost).* *Clipboard (Lost).* *Standard Issue Spray Can (Empty).*

He had nothing.

Except his mind, and he thought back to the duplex. The German cockroaches. The way they scuttled.

The way they avoided light. The way they reacted to specific pheromones.

These things were insects. Big ones. Ancient ones. But the biology held.

If the adults communicated through vibration, the larvae communicated through... what?

Dale looked at the glowing fluid on the floor. It wasn't water. It was nutrient slurry. And it was leaking from the chitin plates.

The creature wasn't just resting. It was lactating.

The eggs were drinking the runoff.

Dale stepped forward, and he didn't run. He walked with the deliberate, heavy steps of a man assessing a foundation. He approached the nearest cluster of eggs.

The blue light intensified as he got closer, pulsing in sync with the creature's heartbeat.

He could feel the vibration in the soles of his boots.

He looked at the crack in the nearest egg. The black fluid inside was moving, swirling, forming eyes.

Dale took the microphone. He didn't speak into it. He tapped it against his boot.

*Click. Click. Click.*

A simple, sharp rhythm.

The nearest egg stopped pulsing.

Dale tapped again. Harder.

*Click. Click. Click.*

The other eggs dimmed. The swirling in the fluid slowed.

The System notification flickered.

**Action: Acoustic Disruption.** **Effect: Minor.** **Reward: +1 Treatment Point.**

It wasn't much. But it was a start.

Dale understood then. The creature wasn't attacking because it was hostile.

It was attacking because it was in pain.

The vibration from the surface, from the drilling, from the war above, had shattered its sensory organs. It was thrashing blindly. The eggs were the collateral damage, or perhaps the lifeboat.

He needed to stabilize the host to stop the spawn, and he needed to find the source of the pain.

Dale looked at the chitin plate he had stepped off. There was a lesion there. A wound. Glowing, angry red.

The creature had been injured by the surface conflict.

If he could heal the wound, or at least cover it, the thrashing would stop. The eggs would settle. The infestation would pause.

He had no bandages. He had no medical kit.

He had a microphone.

And he had the knowledge of how to seal an entry point.

Dale moved to the lesion. It was a tear in the armor, about two feet long. The edges were jagged, oozing the blue nutrient fluid that the eggs craved.

He couldn't close it. But he could redirect the flow.

He looked around. The cavern was littered with debris.

Broken stalactites.

Shards of rock.

And the glowing fluid itself, and he grabbed a large, flat shard of obsidian-like rock. It was sharp, heavy, and perfect.

He slid it under the lesion, catching the flow of blue fluid. He held it there, bracing it with his shoulder. The pressure was immense, vibrating through his bones, threatening to knock him over.

The fluid pooled in the rock, forming a basin.

The eggs nearby stopped drinking. They turned, their translucent shells darkening as they deprived of the feed.

The creature let out a low, mournful groan. The thrashing ceased. The chitin plates smoothed out, settling into a dormant state.

**System Alert: Infestation Stabilized.** **Method: Environmental Modification.** **Thoroughness Rating: 6.5.** **Penalty: Documentation Failure. No Species Identified.** **Reward: +15 Treatment Points.**

Dale gritted his teeth. The rock was digging into his shoulder. His legs burned.

The effort of holding the barrier was taking a toll, but it was working. The eggs were going into torpor.

But the lesion was still open. The creature was still bleeding. And if it woke up, if the pain returned, the cycle would start again.

He needed to seal it. Properly.

He looked at his hands. They were covered in the blue fluid. It was sticky, viscous. It hardened when exposed to air.

A natural sealant.

Dale dropped the rock.

The fluid surged out, hitting the floor with a splash. He didn't hesitate. He scooped up the hardened, sticky residue from the rock with his bare hands.

It burned, a cold, chemical burn, but he ignored it, and he slapped the globs of hardened resin onto the lesion. He pressed it down, smoothing it out with his palms. He worked with the speed and precision of a man patching a roof in a hurricane.

Layer by layer.

The creature shuddered. The red glow of the wound faded, replaced by the dull, opaque black of the seal.

The eggs remained dormant.

The silence returned, but this time it was peaceful.

Dale stood up, his breathing ragged. His hands were ruined, the skin peeling back from the chemical burn. He looked at his System interface.

**Certification Level I: Verified.** **Field Guide: Incomplete.** **Next Objective: Identify Parent Species.**

He had stabilized the nest, but he hadn't identified the tenant. And the Authority wanted a name.

A shadow fell over him.

Not from the eggs. Not from the creature.

From above.

A light, bright and harsh, cut through the darkness of the shaft. A spotlight.

Dale squinted. He shielded his eyes with a burned hand.

A figure descended on a new rope. Faster than the first.

Professional gear.

Tactical harness. And a rifle slung across their back.

It wasn't the hazmat woman.

The figure landed on the chitin, silent and efficient. They moved with the grace of a predator, scanning the cavern, checking the eggs, checking the lesion Dale had sealed.

They looked at Dale.

The figure raised a hand, signaling *halt*, and they stepped closer, reaching for the radio on their chest.

"Command," the figure said, their voice calm, authoritative, and devoid of panic. "Target is neutralized.

Infestation is dormant.

I have a civilian on site.

Male, mid-thirties. No weapon.

No visible injuries.

"

Dale straightened his spine. He smoothed his shirt, though it was torn and stained with blue slime.

The figure looked at him, looked at him. Their eyes narrowed behind the visor.

"You sealed the breach," the figure said. It wasn't a question.

"Structural integrity requires patching," Dale replied, his voice dry. "Standard protocol."

The figure tilted their head.

"That's not standard protocol. That's Level IV containment. Who licensed you?"

Dale looked at the System notification still hovering in the air. *Certification Level I: Verified.*

"I'm in the process of appealing my license suspension," Dale said.

The figure's hand moved to their rifle.

"Appealing," the figure repeated. "To whom?"

"The Authority," Dale said.

The figure laughed, a short, sharp bark.

"The Authority doesn't deal with individuals. They deal with infestations. And right now, you're looking less like a technician and more like a vector."

They raised the rifle.

"Identify yourself, or I treat you as part of the contamination."

Dale didn't raise his hands. He didn't step back. He reached into his pocket, where his wallet was, miraculously intact despite the fall.

He pulled out his Class II Pest Control license. It was faded, laminated, and dated.

He held it up.

The figure lowered the rifle an inch, squinting at the ID.

"That's expired," the figure said.

"It's under review," Dale corrected. "Due to a jurisdictional conflict involving a dimensional rift and a failure to document initial contact."

The figure's eyes widened. They looked at the sealed lesion. They looked at the dormant eggs. They looked at the burn marks on Dale's hands.

"You did that?" the figure whispered.

"I documented the hazard," Dale said. "I isolated the source. I eliminated the immediate threat. I'm just waiting for the verification paperwork to come through."

The radio on the figure's chest crackled.

"Unit 7, report status. Why are you holding fire?"

The figure hesitated. For the first time, uncertainty crept into their posture.

"If I shoot him, I lose the data," the figure muttered to themselves.

They looked at Dale.

"Come with me," the figure said. "If you're lying, I'll let the Authority feed you to the next broodmother."

Dale nodded. He stepped forward, his burned hands trembling slightly.

As he moved, he glanced back at the sealed lesion.

A hairline crack appeared in the black resin.

Just a tiny one.

But through it, a single drop of blue fluid leaked out.

And from the darkness of the cavern, far deeper than the eggs, a voice echoed. Not a whisper this time. A command.

*"Let him in."*

Dale stopped.

The figure behind him raised the rifle again, aiming not at Dale, but at the darkness.

"What did it say?" the figure asked, their voice tight.

Dale looked at the crack in the seal. He looked at the dark shape shifting in the distance, larger and more terrible than anything he had seen before.

He looked at his burned hands, and he looked at the System notification, which had changed.

**New Quest: The Auditor.** **Objective: Survive the Inspection.** **Reward: Unlock Class III Protocols.** **Failure: Termination.**

Dale took a breath.

He looked at the figure.

"It said I'm next," Dale said.

And then he walked toward the darkness, because the Authority wanted an interview, and he was never late for an appointment.

The Authority never let a witness leave alive.
