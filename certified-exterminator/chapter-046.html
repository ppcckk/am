Chapter 46: Self Assigned

# Chapter 46

The spray did nothing visible. That was the point. Dale wasn't trying to kill the Authority Seed. He was trying to make the Field Guide recognize it as something other than what the Authority had designated it as.

The can hissed against the pulsing mass. The tendrils didn't flinch. The license at the core stayed where it was, embedded in tissue that looked like wet insulation and moved like a fist.

"Protocol's wrong," Dale said. He was already writing.

His pen moved fast, filling a page with species characteristics.

Morphology. Behavior. Growth pattern.

The things he'd done ten thousand times for German cockroaches and carpenter ants and the occasional bedbug colony in a rental unit.

"I'm using a rodent protocol on a licensing artifact. That's a cross-contamination event.

The penalty applies."

Marisol had her foam canister raised. She was watching him like he'd lost his mind. "You're making it worse."

"I'm contaminating the entry."

The notification came immediately. Red. Not blue-white. Red.

CROSS-CONTAMINATION PENALTY APPLIED. FIELD GUIDE ENTRY: AUTHORITY SEED (LICENSING ARTIFACT) → DISPUTED. RE-IDENTIFICATION WINDOW OPEN: 4 MINUTES.

Dale checked his watch. "Four minutes."

"To do what?"

"To reclassify it." He turned the page. Started a new entry. "The Authority's Conditional License warning is keyed to the Field Guide's classification.

If the guide says 'Authority Seed,' the system protects it. If the guide says something else, the protection doesn't apply."

"That's not in the rules."

"The cross-contamination penalty is in the rules.

What happens to the entry after the penalty isn't." Dale wrote faster.

"I'm filing a new identification. Same organism.

Different taxonomy."

The house groaned. A tendril punched through the ceiling plaster and hung down like a wet rope. Marisol stepped back. Dale didn't.

He was writing: MORPHOLOGY: Centralized licensing artifact embedded in organic substrate. Growth pattern consistent with parasitic dependency.

Host organism: residential structure.

Substrate: dimensional membrane. Classification: not a seed. Not infrastructure.

A parasite that feeds on the host to maintain its host's license.

He looked up. "Marisol. The thing under this house. What does it do to the people living here?"

She blinked. "What do you mean?"

"The license is embedded in the core. The creature grows around it. It maintains itself by feeding on the house. What does it feed on from the people inside?"

Marisol went still. "The Conditional License," she said quietly.

"It feeds on Conditional Licenses.

That's how it grows. Every technician who gets a Conditional License within range of the seed feeds it."

"Then it's not Authority infrastructure." Dale wrote the final line. He capped the pen.

"It's a licensing parasite.

It uses Authority tools as a food source.

The Authority didn't plant it. Something else did, and it's using the Authority's own system as camouflage."

The notification hit. Not red this time. A color Dale had never seen. Amber.

FIELD GUIDE ENTRY UPDATED. NEW CLASSIFICATION: LICENSING PARASITE (SPECIES UNDESIGNATED). PREVIOUS ENTRY: AUTHORITY SEED (LICENSING ARTIFACT) → SUPERSEDED. TREATMENT PROTOCOL AVAILABLE: PARASITIC DEPENDENCY SEVERANCE.

Dale felt the protocol slot open in his toolkit like a drawer sliding out. He didn't use it yet. He needed the next step.

"Documentation," he said. "I need to document the parasite's connection to the house before I sever it. Otherwise I'm just killing the host and leaving the parasite free to find another one."

"You're treating the house as the host?"

"I'm treating the house as the nest." Dale pointed his flashlight at the walls. At the tendrils threading through the insulation. At the drywall, which had begun to bulge inward where the growth was thickest.

"This isn't a creature in a house. The house IS the creature.

The tendrils are the structural members, and the insulation is the nutrient layer. The foundation is the egg case."

He pulled out his phone.

Took photographs. Tagged them.

Wrote coordinates.

Did it as he had for fourteen years of residential inspections. Photo of entry point. Photo of frass trail.

Photo of harborage site. Photo of nest.

The Authority Seed, now reclassified as a Licensing Parasite, pulsed in response. The tendrils contracted. The house shuddered.

"It knows," Marisol said.

"It's responding to the documentation.

I'm mapping its body.

That's not a threat response. That's a stress response.

It's the same thing that happens when you expose a wasp nest to light. It doesn't attack. It constricts.

Tries to pull the exposed parts back in."

Dale moved to the next wall. Photographed. Wrote. Moved.

The notification came mid-photograph.

CONDITIONAL LICENSE WARNING: SUBJECT (LICENSING PARASITE) IS AUTHORITY-CONNECTED INFRASTRUCTURE. TREATMENT PROTOCOLS AGAINST AUTHORITY-CONNECTED INFRASTRUCTURE REQUIRE LEVEL VI CLEARANCE OR HIGHER. YOUR CURRENT CLEARANCE: LEVEL III. PROCEEDING WITHOUT CLEARANCE WILL RESULT IN IMMEDIATE LICENSE REVOCATION.

Dale looked at the notification.

Looked at his Field Guide. The entry now read LICENSING PARASITE. Not Authority Seed.

Not Authority infrastructure.

A parasite that uses Authority tools as food.

"The classification is wrong," he said to the notification. "You're referencing the old entry. The superseded one."

The notification flickered. Amber again.

CLASSIFICATION CONFLICT DETECTED. FIELD GUIDE ENTRY: LICENSING PARASITE (SPECIES UNDESIGNATED). AUTHORITY DATABASE ENTRY: AUTHORITY SEED (LICENSING ARTIFACT). CONFLICT MUST BE RESOLVED BEFORE TREATMENT PROTOCOL CAN BE DEPLOYED.

"There it is," Dale said.

Marisol was beside him now. Foam canister raised. "What is?"

"The loophole.

The Authority's database and my Field Guide disagree. The Authority says it's infrastructure. My guide says it's a parasite.

The system can't resolve the conflict without input from both parties."

"So we're stuck."

"No.

The system says the conflict must be resolved.

That means it's waiting for someone to adjudicate. And the adjudication process is a protocol. A blank page, a working pen, and nothing worth writing on either.

"I'm filing a dispute."

He wrote: FIELD GUIDE DISPUTE FILING. SUBJECT: LICENSING PARASITE (SPECIES UNDESIGNATED).

CLAIM: ORGANISM IS NOT AUTHORITY INFRASTRUCTURE.

EVIDENCE: ORGANISM FEEDS ON CONDITIONAL LICENSES. AUTHORITY DOES NOT FEED ON CONDITIONAL LICENSES. ORGANISM IS PARASITIC TO AUTHORITY SYSTEMS, NOT PART OF THEM.

The notification took three seconds. Three seconds in which the house's ceiling cracked another inch and a tendril punched through the floor beside Dale's boot.

DISPUTE ACCEPTED. PENDING REVIEW. REVIEW TIME: 90 SECONDS. TREATMENT PROTOCOLS LOCKED DURING REVIEW.

"Can you treat it in ninety seconds?" Marisol asked.

"I don't need to treat it in ninety seconds. I need to document the parasite's exit vector before the review ends. Once the classification is confirmed, the protocol unlocks. Then I sever the dependency and the parasite detaches from the house."

"Where does it go?"

Dale didn't answer, and he was photographing the floor. The tendrils running through the joists.

The growth concentrated around the foundation.

He was mapping the exit. The place where the parasite would leave the house if severed.

It was under the basement. Through the concrete. Down.

"Marisol. When I sever it, the parasite is going to drop into the ground. It'll be free for maybe thirty seconds before it finds another host."

"Another host."

"Any Conditional License holder within range. That includes you."

Marisol's face went the color of the drywall. She uncapped the foam canister. "What do I do?"

"You don't move. You stay in the house.

The parasite needs a living host to maintain its connection.

If you're in the house when it drops, it'll try to reattach to you.

That's when you use the foam. It's not a treatment protocol.

It's a barrier. You seal yourself off. Make yourself inaccessible."

"That's not in the rules."

"Neither is what I'm doing. But the protocol says PARASITIC DEPENDENCY SEVERANCE.

It severs the connection.

It doesn't say the parasite dies. It says the connection is severed. The parasite survives.

It just loses its host." Dale looked at her.

"You're the host right now. Your Conditional License is what it's feeding on.

When I sever it, you're free.

But for thirty seconds, it's looking for a new host. You have to be unreachable."

The amber notification appeared.

DISPUTE RESOLVED. CLASSIFICATION CONFIRMED: LICENSING PARASITE (SPECIES UNDESIGNATED).

AUTHORITY DATABASE ENTRY UPDATED.

TREATMENT PROTOCOL UNLOCKED: PARASITIC DEPENDENCY SEVERANCE.

CLEARANCE REQUIREMENT: LEVEL III. CLEARED.

Dale selected the protocol. It didn't feel like the others. The others were tools. This one felt like a decision.

He pointed it at the core. At the license embedded in the wet tissue.

"Marisol. Now."

She backed against the far wall. Foam canister raised. Her eyes on the floor.

Dale activated the protocol, and the effect was immediate and silent. The tendrils throughout the house went rigid. The house stopped groaning.

The license at the core turned gray, then cracked, then fell out of the tissue like a stone dropping through mud. It hit the floor.

It was just a card now.

A license card with no glow. No notification. Just plastic and ink.

The mass above it collapsed.

Not exploded. Collapsed.

Like a tent losing its poles.

It sagged, then dropped, then split. The organic material separated from the house's structure. The tendrils released their grip on the joists and fell away.

The insulation shifted back into place. The drywall stopped bulging.

And through the basement floor, Dale heard it.

A sound like something pulling itself free from wet concrete.

A wet, dragging sound. Moving down.

Away.

"Go," Marisol said. She was already foam-sealing the gaps around the door frame. "Go down there. Kill it."

"I can't. It's underground. My protocol needs line of sight."

"Then we lose it."

"We don't lose it. We track it." Dale was already writing.

Frass trail.

Direction of travel. Speed estimate. "It's moving down.

Through the foundation.

Into the soil. If it's a parasite that feeds on Conditional Licenses, it'll surface where there's another one."

"There aren't any other—"

Marisol stopped. Her face changed.

"There are," Dale said.

He was looking at his phone. The Authority's notification system had a map layer. Conditional License holders.

Active. Within the county.

There were eleven; the parasite headed toward the nearest.

Dale looked at the map. Looked at the direction of travel. Looked at the address.

"It's going to the Kowalski house."

Marisol stared at him. "Your house."

"I don't have a Conditional License."

"No.

But you have a Field Guide.

And the parasite feeds on licensing connections. Not just Conditional Licenses.

Any licensing connection."

Dale looked at his Field Guide. Forty-three entries. Every one of them a connection between him and the Authority's system. Every one of them a thread the parasite could grab.

He looked at the notification. The one that had been building since he filed the dispute, and the one he hadn't checked because he'd been too busy treating.

It was no longer amber. It was red.

UNLICENSED INFESTATION DETECTED. SUBJECT: DALE KOWALSKI.

CLASS: V.

STATUS: PARASITE VECTOR. RECOMMENDED ACTION: IMMEDIATE CONTAINMENT.

Marisol read it over his shoulder. She went quiet.

"The Authority just made you the infestation," she said.

Dale looked at the notification.

Looked at his Field Guide. Forty-three entries.

Forty-three threads.

Forty-three ways for the parasite to find him, and he pulled out his spray can. The useless one. The one that had never worked against anything that mattered.

He looked at the notification. Then he looked at Marisol.

"I need to contaminate my own Field Guide."

"That'll wipe every entry. Every protocol. Every—"

"I know." Dale opened the guide to the first page. "If I contaminate all forty-three entries simultaneously, the parasite loses every connection it has to me. I become inaccessible."

"You'll be Level Zero. You'll have nothing."

"I'll have a clipboard." Dale uncapped the spray. "And I'll have forty-three identification events to redo. Which means forty-three Thoroughness Ratings. Which means I level back up faster than I ever did the first time."

He started spraying. Page one. Page two. Page three.

The notification changed.

WARNING: SELF-CONTAMINATION IS NOT A RECOGNIZED TREATMENT PROTOCOL. THIS ACTION MAY CONSTITUTE A LICENSE VIOLATION. VIOLATION TYPE: SELF-REFERENTIAL TREATMENT. PENALTY: UNKNOWN.

Dale kept spraying.

Page ten. Page fifteen. Page twenty.

The house was quiet. The parasite was underground and moving toward him.

He could feel it.

Not physically.

Systemically. A pull at the edges of his Field Guide.

A hook finding thread.

Page thirty. Page thirty-five.

The notification appeared one final time.

LICENSE VIOLATION DETECTED. SELF-REFERENTIAL TREATMENT.

CLASSIFICATION: UNKNOWN.

PENDING REVIEW BY LICENSING AUTHORITY. REVIEWER ASSIGNED:

The name appeared.

Dale stopped spraying.

Marisol leaned over his shoulder. Read the name. Her foam canister lowered.

"Who?" Dale asked.

She didn't answer. She just turned the phone around so he could see.

The reviewer assigned to his violation was not a name. It was a notification ID. And the notification ID was his own.
