Chapter 263: Bureaucratic Overlap

# Chapter 263

The briefcase did not open. It snapped shut again, the magnetic latch clicking with a finality that echoed off the wet asphalt. The agent in the grey suit adjusted his cufflinks, his face a mask of bureaucratic indifference despite the chitinous hand currently strangling his partner.

"Protocol violation," the agent said, his voice flat. "Interference with Authority personnel is a Class IV offense. You are hereby placed under administrative detention. Remain stationary."

The creature rising from the sewer grate ignored the summons. It was not a beetle.

It was not a rat, which was a torsion of limbs and wet, translucent membranes, pulling itself up with a sound like tearing canvas. The agent's partner screamed, a thin, reedy sound that was cut short by a wet crunch.

Dale Kowalski did not remain stationary.

He was currently three yards to the left, crouched behind a overturned municipal trash bin. His clipboard was tucked under his arm, the plastic surface slick with rain.

He watched the agent's hand hover over a pistol holstered at his hip.

The agent hesitated. He was looking at the monster, then at Dale, then at the sky.

The Rift above them pulsed. A sickly violet light spilled down, illuminating the steam rising from the open manhole.

Dale clicked his pen. The sound was tiny, insignificant against the roar of the emerging horror.

*Entry Started.*

*Subject: Unknown.*

*Location: Sector 7G, Storm Drain Outlet.*

*Observation: Exoskeletal integrity compromised by rapid expansion. Moisture content high.*

The agent fired. The bullet struck the creature's shoulder, sparking against the chitin.

It didn't slow down.

It surged forward, the hand releasing its dead grip to swipe at the remaining agent.

The suit-clad man scrambled back, slipping on the oily pavement.

Dale stood up. He walked toward the creature, not away.

"Hey!" the agent shouted, backing up against a streetlight. "Get back! Hazardous material!"

Dale raised a finger, signaling for silence. He tilted his head, squinting at the creature's thorax. The thing was huge, easily eight feet tall when fully upright, but it was struggling to maintain balance. Its legs were jointed wrong, too long for its center of gravity.

"Structural failure," Dale muttered.

The agent blinked.

"What?"

"Look at the legs," Dale said, pointing with his pen. "They're not supporting the weight. It's dragging its abdomen. It's not attacking; it's tripping."

The creature lunged. The agent ducked, rolling awkwardly. The clawed hand smashed into the streetlight, bending the metal pole until it sparked and died, plunging the area into darkness save for the Rift's glow and the agent's tactical flashlight.

Dale didn't flinch at the sudden dark. He pulled a UV flashlight from his vest pocket. It was a standard inspection tool, calibrated for detecting mold spores and urine trails.

He clicked it on. The beam hit the creature.

The translucent membranes along its sides glowed a faint, sickly yellow.

"Bioluminescent sacs," Dale noted, scribbling on his pad. "Likely reproductive or waste storage. Vulnerable to thermal shock."

The agent stared at him.

"You have a flashlight. Use it to see."

"I am using it to identify," Dale corrected. "You're using it to panic."

The creature turned, sensing the light.

It shrieked, a sound like metal grinding on glass, and charged the light source. Dale sidestepped. The creature's momentum carried it past him, slamming into the brick wall of the adjacent building.

Bricks rained down, and the agent was still on the ground, scrambling for his radio.

"Backup, Code Black!

We have an unregistered entity!

Sector 7G! I need containment, not extermination! I repeat, containment!

"

Dale frowned. "Containment is inefficient if the subject is already dying."

The agent looked up, eyes wide with rage and fear.

"Shut up! You're suspended! You have no jurisdiction!"

"I have eyes," Dale said. "And a pen."

He stepped closer to the struggling creature. It was trying to pull itself up, its legs flailing.

The UV light revealed a network of pulsing veins beneath its shell.

They were rhythmic.

Too rhythmic.

"Respiratory system is external," Dale said, speaking to the air, documenting as much as the agent. "Spiracles located along the lateral thoracic ridge. Blocked by mud and debris from the breach."

The agent stood up, shaking.

"Get away from it!"

"If I don't clear the spiracles, it suffocates," Dale said. "If it suffocates, it thrashes. If it thrashes, it brings the building down.

Or you. Which would you prefer?

"

The agent froze. The logic was mundane, boring, and terrifyingly sound.

Dale approached the creature's side. He could smell it now—ammonia and rot. He pulled a pair of heavy-duty nitrile gloves from his belt. He snapped them on, the rubber popping against his wrists.

"Don't touch it," the agent warned.

"I'm a technician," Dale said. "I don't fight. I service."

He reached out, his gloved hand hovering near the mud-caked ridges of the creature's flank. The creature snapped its head toward him, mandibles clicking. Dale didn't pull back. He waited.

*Wait for the exhale.*

The creature's side expanded. The spiracles flared open.

Dale moved, and he didn't attack. He used a specialized tool from his belt—a pressurized air duster, modified for clearing debris from high-voltage junction boxes. He aimed the nozzle at the blocked spiracle.

*Pffft.*

A burst of cold, dry air hit the mud.

The creature convulsed.

A gasp, wet and guttural, tore from its throat. The yellow glow in its sacs dimmed as the oxygen flow normalized. It stopped thrashing.

It slumped, its massive weight settling onto the asphalt.

The agent lowered his gun, confused.

"What did you do?"

"Unclogged the filter," Dale said, clicking off the UV light. "Standard maintenance."

A notification pinged in Dale's peripheral vision. Red text, flashing urgently.

**SYSTEM ERROR: UNAUTHORIZED ACTION.** **ACTION: TREATMENT ATTEMPT ON UNLICENSED SUBJECT.** **STATUS: REVOCATION IMMINENT.**

Dale ignored it. He turned to the agent.

"You're bleeding."

The agent looked down. A shard of brick had cut his cheek. Blood dripped onto his tie.

"Call the medics," Dale said. "Then call the Authority. Tell them the entry is stabilized. Tell them I identified the species."

"The species?" the agent spat. "You don't have a Field Guide! You're off the grid!"

"I have a pen," Dale said. "And a memory."

He tapped his clipboard. The page was blank where the entry should be.

The System had wiped it, and the data was gone. But the knowledge was still there, etched into his mind by the sheer terror of the last two minutes.

The agent stared at him, then at the dying creature. The creature was slowing down, its breaths becoming shallow. The purple light from the Rift above flickered, intensifying.

More sounds came from the sewers. Not one hand. Many.

Clattering. Scratching. The sound of hundreds of legs on wet concrete.

The agent's radio crackled.

"Sector 7G, this is Dispatch.

Do you copy?

We have multiple thermal signatures rising from the grid.

Recommend immediate evacuation.

"

The agent looked at Dale.

"You said they won't freeze."

"I said they won't freeze when you light a match," Dale corrected. "This isn't fire. This is air."

The ground shook. A second hand, smaller but sharper, punched through the asphalt ten feet away. Then a third.

Dale looked at the sky. The Rift was widening. The violet light was no longer steady; it was swirling, like oil in water.

"Listen," Dale said. "The Authority revoked your license. They revoked mine. That means we're both freelancers now."

The agent laughed, a harsh, broken sound.

"Freelancers? Against this?"

"Look at the entry," Dale said, pointing to the first creature. "It's dead. I documented the cause of death. Respiratory failure due to environmental contamination."

"So?"

"So, the System awards points for Thoroughness," Dale said. "It doesn't care who did the killing. It cares about the *data*."

The agent's eyes narrowed. He looked at the dead creature, then at Dale's empty clipboard.

"You're saying you can feed the System with your memory?"

"I'm saying I can feed it with my *report*," Dale said. "If I write it, and if you verify it, the Authority has to accept the data. They can't revoke what they can't delete."

The agent hesitated. He was a suit. A bureaucrat.

He knew the rules better than anyone. He knew that the Licensing Authority was a machine that ran on logs.

If the log existed, the license existed.

"If we file it together," the agent said slowly, "we share the credit. And the liability."

"Agreed," Dale said.

The agent pulled a pen from his own pocket. He walked over to Dale, his shoes crunching on the broken glass. He looked at the clipboard.

"Write," the agent commanded.

Dale wrote.

*Subject: Unknown Chitinous Entity (Provisional Name: Torsion Beast).* *Cause of Death: Hypoxia induced by external obstruction removal.* *Witness: Agent V. Thorne, Licensing Authority Enforcement.* *Technician: Dale Kowalski, Class II (Suspended).*

The agent stared at the words. Then, he signed his name next to Dale's.

A chime sounded. Not a warning chime. A confirmation chime.

**DATA RECEIVED.** **VERIFYING...** **VERIFICATION COMPLETE.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 8.4.** **TREATMENT POINTS AWARDED: 150.**

The red *Revocation Pending* alert in Dale's vision vanished. In its place, a new line appeared.

**LICENSE STATUS: PROBATIONARY.** **CONDITIONS: FILE ALL INCIDENTS WITHIN 10 MINUTES.**

Dale looked at the agent.

"We have ten minutes."

The agent looked up at the sky. The Rift was screaming now, a high-pitched frequency that made teeth ache. The ground was buckling, paving stones popping up like corks.

"More are coming," the agent said. "How many?"

Dale looked at the sewers. The scratching was deafening.

"Enough," Dale said.

The second hand broke the surface. It was larger than the first. It had no fingers, just a blunt, crushing pincer. It swept across the street, smashing a parked car.

The agent raised his gun again.

"Fire!"

"Wait," Dale said.

"They're right there!"

"They're *anchoring*," Dale said. He pointed to the pincer. It had dug into the asphalt, creating a leverage point. The creature was using the street itself as a brace to pull the rest of its body out.

"If you shoot the anchor, the recoil will throw it off balance, but it won't die," Dale said. "You'll just make it angry."

"Then what?" the agent yelled, panic rising in his voice.

Dale looked at the storm drain. He looked at the massive, half-emerged creature. Then he looked at the agent.

"Protocol: Hydro-Jet," Dale said.

"We don't have a hydro-jet."

"Check your briefcase," Dale said.

The agent froze.

"What?"

"You're an Enforcement Agent. You carry a containment unit. It's probably a vacuum or a suction device."

The agent looked at his briefcase. He opened it. Inside, nestled in foam, was a device that looked like a high-powered industrial vacuum cleaner, with a hose attached to a nozzle.

"Dust Mite Containment Unit," the label read.

"It's not a vacuum," the agent said, horrified. "It's for small bugs. It's rated for Class 1."

"Physics doesn't care about ratings," Dale said. "It cares about volume and pressure."

Dale grabbed the hose. He ran toward the creature.

"Stop him!" the agent shouted, but his feet wouldn't move. He was watching.

Dale aimed the nozzle at the creature's exposed thorax. The gap where the shell was splitting open. The weak point.

"Thorne!" Dale yelled. "Turn it on!"

The agent fumbled with the switch. He hit it.

The machine whined, a high-pitched drone that vibrated in Dale's bones. The suction was immense. The air around the nozzle distorted.

Dale pulled the trigger.

A stream of superheated steam and concentrated vacuum force hit the creature's soft tissue.

The creature shrieked. Not a scream of pain, but of pressure. Its body imploded.

The shell cracked inward.

Fluid sprayed out, caught by the suction, creating a black mist.

The creature slumped. It fell back into the sewer, its weight dragging the rest of its body down with it.

Silence returned. Just for a second.

Then, the sewer grate exploded upward.

Not a hand. A head.

It was massive, human-shaped but wrong. The face was a void of black eyes and a mouth full of needle-teeth. It wore a tattered, wet business suit.

The agent dropped his gun.

Dale kept the hose on. The suction held. The thing was pulled back, inch by inch, fighting with clawed hands that scraped against the asphalt.

"It's wearing clothes," the agent whispered.

"It's wearing a uniform," Dale said. "Look at the patch."

On the shoulder of the wet, rotting suit was a logo. A shield. Inside the shield, a stylized eye.

The Authority's logo.

The thing roared, a sound that shattered the windows of the nearby apartments. It reached out, grabbing Dale's arm. The suction whined, struggling against the weight.

Dale didn't let go. He held the hose tight, his boots sliding on the wet pavement.

"Let go!" the agent screamed.

"No," Dale said. "It's an infestation. And it's inside the perimeter."

The creature's black eyes stared into Dale's. There was no malice in them. There was recognition.

It knew him.

It knew the System.

The creature spoke. The voice was a wet gurgle, but the words were clear.

*"Audit,"* it gurgled.

The suction stopped, and the creature was fully out of the sewer now. It stood on the street, ten feet tall, dripping black sludge. It looked at Dale.

It looked at the clipboard.

It reached for the clipboard.

Dale didn't pull away. He held it out.

The creature took it. It examined the page with its clawed hand.

*"Entry accepted,"* the creature said.

Then, it turned to the agent.

*"Your license is void,"* the creature said. *"You failed to document."*

The agent backed up, trembling.

"I... I verified it."

*"Late,"* the creature said. *"Protocol breach."*

The creature raised a hand. Not to strike. To point.

It pointed at the sky, and the Rift was closing. But not shrinking. *Folding.*

The purple light turned black.

Dale looked up. The sky wasn't above them anymore. The sky was a ceiling. And something was crawling on it.

The creature looked back at Dale.

*"Level Nine,"* it said. *"The Auditor is coming."*

It dropped the clipboard. It fell back into the sewer, dissolving into the darkness.

Dale stood alone on the street. The agent was on his knees, hands over his face.

Dale picked up the clipboard, and the page was no longer blank.

It was filled with his own handwriting, but the ink was moving. The letters were rearranging themselves.

**NEW MECHANIC UNLOCKED: BUREAUCRATIC OVERLAP.**

**WARNING: REALITY HAS A CODE VIOLATION. YOU ARE THE INSPECTOR.**

Dale looked at the sky. The blackness was pressing down.

He clicked his pen.

"Let's see what the code is," he said.
