Chapter 365: Forged Signature

# Chapter 365

The cold metal of the Auditor's chassis pressed against Dale's spine.

It wasn't a hug. It was containment.

Dale's clipboard clattered to the concrete floor, the data port sparking. The air smelled of ozone and burnt hair. Jenkins screamed, a raw, ragged sound that bounced off the dissolving walls of the processing center. She scrambled backward, her boots slipping on the slick tile, eyes wide and fixed on the void that had just opened behind Dale.

The entity did not have a face. It had a texture.

Like wet leather stretched over a geometric framework.

It wore a tie. A perfectly knotted, charcoal-grey tie that sat against a collar that shouldn't exist on a non-humanoid form.

"License suspension is automatic," the voice said. It was Jenkins's father, but pitched down an octave, stripped of warmth. "For non-compliant reporting of Class-IX anomalies."

Dale didn't struggle, and he couldn't. The Auditor's limbs—four of them, jointed like a spider's—had locked around his torso.

The pressure was immense, crushing the air from his lungs.

He felt a rib crack. A sharp, hot spike of pain in his chest.

Pain was irrelevant. Pain was just a notification.

He looked at the Auditor's chest plate. The surface rippled with static, trying to resolve an image.

A badge?

No.

A warning label.

**VIOLATION: UNSANCTIONED COMMUNICATION WITH LOCAL FAUNA (JENKINS).** **PENALTY: IMMEDIATE TERMINATION.**

Jenkins froze, one hand on a support pillar.

"Dale?"

Dale's vision narrowed. The world blurred at the edges. His Field Guide interface flickered in his peripheral vision, red text scrolling too fast to read.

He needed to breathe. He needed to think.

But the Auditor was already raising a manipulator arm, its tip forming a sharp, crystalline spike, which was going to execute him. No trial. No hearing. Just a quick, efficient end to a loose end.

Dale's hand moved. Not to fight. To adjust.

His right hand, trapped against his hip, twitched. He reached for the pen. The burnt, smoking pen that had killed the previous Auditor.

It was still there, clutched in his fingers, and he had dropped it, but the magnetic lock of his belt had caught it.

He couldn't stab the new Auditor. That would be combat. Combat was inefficient. Combat resulted in zero points if the documentation wasn't filed.

He needed to document.

The Auditor's head tilted. The static cleared for a second, revealing a smooth, featureless plane where eyes should be.

"State your employee ID," the voice demanded.

Dale coughed, blood flecking his lips.

"Kowalski. Dale. Class IX.

Unlicensed."

"Unlicensed personnel are to be removed," the Auditor said. The spike descended.

Time slowed.

Dale's mind raced through the System rules. He knew them better than he knew his own mother's face. He knew the loopholes. He knew the bureaucratic absurdity that underpinned the Authority's logic.

*Rule 4.2: Cross-contamination penalty applies to protocol mismatch.* *Rule 7.1: Identification must precede elimination.*

The Auditor was labeled an Inspector. Inspectors were administrative. They were not combat units. They were not *vermin*.

If Dale treated it as a combat threat, he was wrong. The System would reject the input. The Auditor would see the aggression and counter it with lethal force.

But if he treated it as a *violation*?

The spike touched his skin. A drop of blood welled up.

"Hold," Dale whispered.

The Auditor paused. Its logic processors hung. *Hold* was not a command it recognized from a suspect.

Dale's left hand, still free, reached into his pocket. His fingers brushed against the crumpled remains of the previous report. The one he'd filed on the first Auditor. The one with the error code.

He pulled out a small, folded slip of paper. It was damp, stained with sweat and blood.

"Complaint filed," Dale said, his voice flat, professional. "Regulation 11-B. Harassment of licensed personnel during active service."

The Auditor's head tilted further. The static swirled.

"This is not a service call," the voice said, but it wavered. "This is a termination protocol."

"Incorrect classification," Dale said.

He pressed the paper against the Auditor's chest plate.

The paper sizzled, curling at the edges.

"You are operating outside the scope of your jurisdiction.

This is a municipal zone. You are interfering with a Class-IX extermination.

"

The Auditor hesitated. The spike hovered an inch from Dale's heart.

Dale could feel the heat radiating from the creature. It was waiting.

It was processing the contradiction.

An Auditor could terminate a suspect. But could it terminate a *report*?

The System demanded consistency. If Dale was filing a complaint, the Auditor had to log it. If it terminated him before logging, the audit trail was corrupted.

Corruption was worse than death to a bureaucratic entity.

The spike retracted.

The static cleared. The voice changed. It became neutral. Robotic.

"Complaint logged," the Auditor said. "Processing queue: Immediate."

Jenkins stared, mouth open.

"What are you doing?"

"Doing my job," Dale grunted. The pressure on his ribs eased slightly. The Auditor had shifted its grip, not to crush, but to hold. To wait.

Dale's vision was darkening, and the blood loss was significant. His ribs were broken, maybe three of them.

He couldn't stand.

He slumped, supported by the Auditor's limbs.

He needed to finish the documentation.

He looked at his Field Guide. The entry for **The Inspector General** was blinking.

**WEAKNESS: BUREAUCRATIC RIGIDITY.**

He knew this. He had used it before.

But this Auditor was different, which was the *next* one.

It was stronger. Faster.

And it was already aware of him.

"Report the hazard," Dale said, gasping. "Structural instability. Ceiling collapse imminent. You are obstructing evacuation routes."

The Auditor looked up. The ceiling was indeed dissolving, chunks of concrete raining down.

"Hazard confirmed," the Auditor said. "Priority shift: Evacuation assistance."

It released Dale, and he hit the floor, curling into a ball. The pain was blinding. He tasted copper.

Jenkins ran to him.

"Dale! Get up! We have to move!"

"No," Dale wheezed. "I have to verify."

"Verify what?"

"Isolation," he said. He pointed a shaking finger at the dark hole in the ceiling.

"The breach.

You can't leave it untreated. It'll spread. The Authority will fine us both.

"

Jenkins grabbed his arm, trying to pull him up.

"You're insane. We're running."

"Fine me," Dale said. "I accept the fine. But don't leave the nest active."

He reached for his clipboard. It was still on the floor, the screen cracked but functional. He dragged it toward him. His hand shook, but he typed.

**JOB STATUS: IN PROGRESS.** **HAZARD: DIMENSIONAL BREACH (CLASS-IX).** **ACTION: ISOLATION ATTEMPTED.** **CLIENT: JENKINS, R.**

He hit send.

The Auditor watched him. Its four limbs unfolded, moving to the ceiling. It sealed the breach, using a blue light that hardened into a temporary barrier.

It was helping, which was obeying the hierarchy. A hazard report trumped a termination order.

Jenkins watched the Auditor work, then looked at Dale. Her eyes were filled with a mixture of horror and awe.

"You're talking to it," she said.

"It's an inspector," Dale said. "I'm just pointing out the code violation."

The barrier solidified. The breach was closed. The air stopped smelling of ozone. It started smelling of dust and old drywall.

The Auditor turned back to Dale.

**HAZARD MITIGATED.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 2/10.** **NOTES: SLOPPY. EXCESSIVE DELAY. INCOMPLETE PHYSICAL RESTRAINT OF SUSPECT.**

**PENALTY: -50 TREATMENT POINTS.** **BALANCE: 14,200 TP.**

"Two out of ten?" Jenkins spat. "You just survived a Class-IX Auditor and you got a two?"

Dale winced, pulling himself up. He leaned heavily on Jenkins.

"I didn't document the secondary exit points. That's a deduction."

"You're broken," she said, noticing the blood on his shirt.

"Administrative error," Dale said.

He straightened his tie. His own tie was crooked.

He fixed it.

"We need to leave. This location is flagged for deep cleaning."

"Where do we go?"

Dale looked at his Field Guide. The map of the city was overlaid with red zones. The Central Processing Center was a black hole in the data.

"Out," Dale said. "Away from the center."

They limped toward the exit. The Auditor remained in the center of the room, standing perfectly still, watching them leave. It did not follow. Its job was done.

They reached the door. Jenkins pushed it open.

The hallway was empty. Silent.

Then, the floor beneath them vibrated, and a sound came from the vents. A chittering. Low, rhythmic. Like a thousand fingernails scraping against metal.

Dale stopped. He sniffed the air.

Sulfur. Rotting meat.

And something else.

Sweet.

Like overripe fruit.

"New species," he muttered.

Jenkins gripped her weapon.

"Is it another Auditor?"

"No," Dale said. He pulled out his flashlight. The beam cut through the dust. "Auditors don't smell like fruit."

He pointed the light down the hall.

At the far end, the shadows were moving. Not one shape. Many.

Small, skittering shapes. Then larger ones.

Unfolding from the walls themselves, and they weren't creatures. They were *parts* of creatures. Eyes.

Hands.

Jaws. Detached, crawling independently.

**FIELD GUIDE UPDATE:** **NEW ENTRY: FRAGMENTED HIVE.** **CLASS: 5.** **WARNING: CONTAGION RISK.**

Dale's stomach dropped.

Contagion risk.

If one of those fragments touched him, it would contaminate his Field Guide entry. And if his entry was contaminated, he couldn't use his protocols. He'd be powerless.

Jenkins stepped forward, raising her rifle.

"I can clear them."

"Don't," Dale said.

He grabbed her wrist.

"If you shoot one, the shrapnel spreads. It's a viral infection.

You'll spread it to the whole building.

"

"So what do we do?" Jenkins asked, her voice trembling.

Dale looked at the approaching mass. It was moving fast. Too fast for him to run. Too complex for him to fight.

He needed a solution. A protocol.

He looked at the Auditor, still visible through the open door behind them. It was watching.

Dale's hand went to his belt. He had no spray. No bait. No traps.

He had a pen. And a clipboard. And a broken rib.

And a rule.

*Rule 9.3: In cases of extreme infestation density, the technician may request emergency containment via Authority asset.*

He hadn't done it before. It cost a fortune in TP. And it required a specific trigger.

He had to be in *immediate, life-threatening danger* from an *unidentified* threat.

The fragments were ten feet away. Five feet.

Jenkins fired.

A single shot. It hit one of the larger fragments. It exploded into a cloud of black spores.

The spores hit the wall. The wall dissolved.

More fragments poured out of the hole.

Jenkins screamed, backing away.

"Dale!"

Dale didn't move. He stood still, letting the spores drift toward him. He breathed in.

He felt the tickle in his nose. The itch in his lungs.

Contamination.

His Field Guide flashed red.

**SYSTEM ALERT: CROSS-CONTAMINATION DETECTED.** **PROTOCOLS DISABLED FOR 24 HOURS.**

He was defenseless.

But he had the trigger, and he raised his hand. Not in surrender. In demand.

"Authority!" he shouted. "Emergency Containment Request! Authorization Code: Kowalski-Delta-Nine!"

Nothing happened.

The fragments reached him. They crawled up his pants. Cold, wet, slimy.

Jenkins was crying now, hitting at them with her rifle butt.

Dale closed his eyes. He counted.

One. Two. Three.

He felt a fragment reach his neck. He felt it try to burrow into his skin.

Then, a chime.

Not from his interface. From the ceiling.

The blue light from the Auditor's barrier in the next room flared. It expanded, rushing down the hallway like a wave.

It slammed into the fragments, and they froze. Then they crumbled into dust.

Jenkins fell to her knees, gasping. The spores were gone. The air was clear.

Dale opened his eyes. The Auditor was gone. The hallway was sealed behind a wall of blue light.

A notification appeared.

**CONTAINMENT EXECUTED.** **COST: 50,000 TP.** **BALANCE: NEGATIVE 5,800 TP.**

**STATUS: DEBT COLLECTION INITIATED.** **ASSET SEIZURE: PENDING.**

Dale stared at the number. Negative five thousand eight hundred.

He was broke. He was broken. And he had just invited the Authority's bailiffs into his life.

Jenkins looked up at him.

"You owe them money?"

"I owe them my soul," Dale said flatly. He wiped the black dust from his face. "And the license is still under review."

He looked at the sealed wall. Something was moving behind it. Something bigger than the fragments.

The voice of Jenkins's father returned. Not from the air. From the wall itself.

"Mr.

Kowalski.

Your debt is overdue. Please report to Collection Agency 4.

Failure to appear will result in license revocation and physical seizure.

"

Dale straightened his tie. He checked his pockets. Empty.

"We're going," he said.

Jenkins scrambled up.

"Where? To the collection agency?"

"No," Dale said. He looked at the crack in the floor where the first Auditor had died. He picked up the burnt pen. It was still warm.

"We're going to find a way to forge a signature," he said. "Before they take my hands."
