Chapter 128: Off the Books

# Chapter 128

The gray suit did not fit the man wearing it. The shoulders were too narrow, the collar too high, and the fabric possessed the dull, light-absorbing quality of wet wool found in a basement. Dale Kowalski watched the figure through the peripheral blur of his own exhaustion, noting the discrepancy not with fear, but with the detached precision of a health inspector spotting a cracked tile.

It wasn't fear that froze Dale. It was the clipboard.

Empty. Burned.

The three figures fanned out, blocking the alley exit. They moved in perfect unison, a hive-mind of bureaucracy.

No weapons.

No magic. Just paperwork.

The lead figure raised a hand. The fingers were long, too many joints, pale as maggot meat.

"Mr.

Kowalski. You are in violation of Code 44-B.

Unauthorized destruction of Authority property.

Civil liability pending. Please surrender your Field Guide for immediate seizure."

Marcus stepped in front of Dale, hands raised, palms open. "Wait.

We have to explain.

The Auditor—it was a trap.

The library was compromised. If we hadn't burned it—"

"Irrelevant." The figure's voice sounded like dry leaves skittering over concrete. "Evidence is destroyed. The infestation is unverified.

By the Authority's statutes, an unverified infestation is classified as 'Fabricated Hazard.' You have falsified a report by omission. You are no longer a technician.

You are a hazard."

Dale didn't speak. He looked at the ground. Ash.

Wet ash.

Black, oily residue pooling in the cracks of the pavement.

Not normal fire residue.

The Auditor hadn't burned. It had dissolved.

The lead figure took a step forward.

The air pressure dropped. Dale felt it in his teeth, a low-frequency hum that vibrated in the jawbone. This wasn't an Authority siren.

It was a containment field.

"Pick up the clipboard," the figure said.

Dale bent down.

His knees popped.

The cardboard tube was warped, blackened at the edges. He picked it up.

It was light. Too light.

Inside, the pages were ash. But not just ash.

Dale ran his thumb over the residue. It stuck. It clung to his skin like glue.

He looked at the System interface. The red banner was still there.

**CERTIFICATION SUSPENDED.** **STATUS: DEFENDANT.**

But beneath the red, a faint, pulsing amber light flickered.

**NOTE: EVIDENCE CONTAMINATED. PROTOCOL 9-ALPHA AVAILABLE FOR DEFENSE ONLY.**

Dale's heart hammered against his ribs. Protocol 9-Alpha.

He didn't know what that was.

He had never reached Level IX. He wasn't even supposed to know it existed.

The level IX protocols were theoretical. Mythical. They were the stuff of old war stories told by retired exterminators who had gone mad.

The figure extended a hand. "Surrender the guide."

Dale didn't move.

He stared at the black residue on his thumb. It wasn't ash.

It was chitin.

Microscopic fragments of the Auditor's shell, fused with the paper fibers of his report.

The Auditor had been made of paper.

And paper was flammable. But this paper hadn't burned with heat. It had burned with *identity*.

A realization hit Dale with the force of a physical blow. The Auditor wasn't a monster.

It was a record.

A physical manifestation of the Authority's own bureaucratic inertia.

And by burning it, Dale hadn't destroyed evidence. He had destroyed the Authority's ability to track the rift.

He had blinded them.

That was why they were here. Not to arrest him. To silence him.

"Marcus," Dale said, his voice flat. "Run."

"What?" Marcus looked confused, sweat dripping from his forehead. "Dale, they're just guys. We can—"

"They aren't guys." Dale dropped the clipboard. He didn't surrender it. He let it hit the wet pavement with a wet slap. "They're redactions."

The lead figure stopped. The blurred face turned toward Dale.

"Insolence," the figure said. "Add violation: Contempt of Authority."

Dale closed his eyes. He reached into his mind, past the fear, past the exhaustion, into the cold, hard architecture of his professional training.

He didn't need to fight them. He needed to document them.

*Identify.*

*Document.*

*Isolate.*

He couldn't kill them. They weren't alive. But he could define them.

And if he defined them, he could apply a protocol.

Dale opened his eyes. He didn't look at the suits. He looked at the black sludge on the ground. He focused on it.

**TARGET IDENTIFIED: REDACTIVE FUNGUS (TYPE: BUREAUCRATIC). WEAKNESS: LACK OF DEFINITION.**

The System pinged. A new entry populated his Field Guide. It wasn't a monster. It was a classification error.

**NEW MECHANIC UNLOCKED: PARADOX PROTOCOL.** **DESCRIPTION: By identifying an entity that defies standard biological or magical classification, the Technician may exploit the Authority's own rule-set to create a legal loophole.** **COST: -50% Efficiency.** **RISK: If the identification is incorrect, the Technician is liable for 'False Reporting' penalties.**

Dale had never tried this. It was a lawyer's trick, not an exterminator's. But Dale was both.

He took a deep breath. The air smelled of ozone and rotting paper.

"Hey," Dale said. Loudly. Projecting his voice like he was addressing a crowd at a town hall meeting. "I am filing a formal objection."

The lead figure tilted its head. "Objection is not a protocol."

"It is," Dale said. "Section 12, Paragraph 4. 'In cases of ambiguous entity classification, the Technician may request a Clarification of Status.' I am requesting clarification."

The figure hesitated. Its hand lowered. The humming in the air stuttered.

Bureaucracy hated ambiguity. It fed on it, but it also feared it.

If Dale was formally requesting a classification, the Authority was legally required to process the request before executing the suspect.

It was a stall tactic. A procedural delay.

Dale had bought himself ten seconds. Maybe twenty.

He needed to use them.

He turned to Marcus.

"Don't fight them. Don't run.

Just stand still.

Let me work."

Marcus stared at him, wide-eyed. "Work? Dale, we're going to jail."

"We're going to survive," Dale said. He turned back to the suits.

"I identify the entity as *Redactive Fungus*.

Non-living.

Non-magical. A byproduct of administrative decay."

The lead figure stepped back. The other two shuffled nervously.

"Your identification is incorrect," the figure said. "The entity is a Sentinel."

"Then define 'Sentinel,'" Dale countered. "Provide the biological or magical parameters. Where does it breathe?

What does it eat? If it is a living organism, show me its metabolic rate.

If it is magical, show me the mana signature.

You have neither. Therefore, by Authority Code 1-Alpha, it is classified as 'Unclassified Debris.'"

Silence.

The hum in the air died.

The lead figure looked at its companions. They looked back. They were confused. They were code, and code couldn't handle logic that looped back on itself.

Dale felt the System surge.

**STATUS CHANGE: PROBATIONARY -> DEFENSE ACTIVE.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: UPDATING...**

The rating climbed. 2.1. 3.5. 4.8.

It wasn't enough to save him. Not yet. But it was enough to pause the execution.

Dale needed more. He needed to isolate the problem.

He looked at the ground. The black sludge was spreading. It was seeping into the cracks of the alley, moving toward the sewer grates.

If the Auditor was made of records, and the records were burning, then the fire was spreading. Not through heat. Through *data*.

The rift wasn't opening in the library. The rift was opening in the *network*.

Dale realized what he had done. He hadn't just burned a building. He had burned the Authority's firewall.

He looked up at the sky. The clouds were swirling. Not rain clouds. *Clouds of ink*.

The suits looked up too. For the first time, they looked afraid.

"The breach," the lead figure whispered. "It's spreading."

Dale didn't wait. He didn't wait for the System to tell him what to do. He acted.

He pulled out his spray can. It was empty. He knew it was empty. He had checked it ten times since the library burned.

He shook it anyway.

The sound was hollow.

But the *gesture* mattered. To the Authority, the gesture was a protocol initiation. A spray can meant containment. It meant *treatment*.

Dale pointed the empty can at the lead figure.

"I am initiating containment," Dale said. "Protocol: Sterilization of Administrative Error."

The System flashed.

**WARNING: NO TREATMENT AGENT DETECTED.** **PENALTY: CONTEMPT OF PROCEDURE.**

But the figure didn't attack. It flinched.

It was afraid of the *idea* of containment.

Dale saw the opening. The loophole.

The Authority treated everything like a pest. Even itself. If he could convince the System that the *Suits* were the infestation, he could treat them.

He couldn't kill them. But he could *quarantine* them.

He needed a containment vessel.

He looked around.

The alley was small. Dumpsters.

Trash bags.

Duct tape.

Standard pest control supplies.

Dale ran to the dumpster. He grabbed a roll of heavy-duty contractor tape. He grabbed a thick black trash bag.

The suits advanced. Slowly.

They knew what he was doing, but they couldn't stop him.

Not legally.

Not yet.

Dale ripped the tape with his teeth. The sound was sharp, violent.

He didn't aim at the suits. He aimed at the ground. At the black sludge.

He sprayed air.

Nothing came out.

But he *acted* as if something did. He mimed the motion. The broad, sweeping arc of a professional.

The confident stride. The decisive snap of the wrist.

He was selling it.

He was selling the Authority on the idea that he was exterminating the corruption.

And the Authority, being a system of rules, believed in the performance of the rule.

If he acted like he was exterminating, the System registered it as an extermination attempt.

**TREATMENT INITIATED: UNKNOWN TARGET.** **EFFICIENCY: 5%.**

Five percent. That was garbage. But it was *something*.

And then, he saw it.

The black sludge on the ground reacted to his presence. It recoiled.

Not because of the spray.

Because of his *intent*.

Dale realized the truth. The Auditor wasn't a monster. It was a symptom of the Authority's fear. And the Suits were the Authority's immune response.

To fight them, he had to stop being a technician.

He had to become a *virus*.

He dropped the tape. He dropped the bag.

He walked toward the lead figure.

"I am not requesting clarification," Dale said. His voice was low, steady. "I am requesting *excommunication*."

The figure froze.

That wasn't a protocol. That wasn't a rule. That was a human word. A dirty, emotional, non-bureaucratic word.

The System glitched.

The text in Dale's vision fractured.

**ERROR: CONCEPT NOT FOUND.** **RETRYING...** **NEW PROTOCOL GENERATED: CHAOTIC NEUTRALITY.**

**WARNING: USE OF NON-STANDARD TERMINOLOGY MAY RESULT IN PERMANENT LOSS OF CERTIFICATION.**

Dale didn't care.

He reached into his pocket. He pulled out his ID badge. The laminated card with his photo, his name, and his Class II license.

He held it up.

"I revoke my license," Dale said.

The air stopped vibrating.

Marcus gasped. "Dale, don't!"

"It's the only way," Dale said. "If I'm not a technician, I can't be held to their rules. And if I'm not held to their rules, I'm not part of their system. I'm outside it."

He dropped the badge on the ground.

The lead figure looked at the badge. Then at Dale.

For the first time, the figure spoke without the voice of the Authority. It spoke with its own voice. A rasping, wet sound.

"You... cannot... leave."

"Why?" Dale asked.

"I'm just a guy. A guy with a dead job. I'm not a hazard.

I'm not a technician.

I'm not an asset."

The figure stared at him.

The gray suit rippled. The blurred face seemed to gain features. A nose.

A mouth. A look of pure, unadulterated panic.

"If you leave," the figure said, "the firewall fails."

"I know," Dale said.

"You will let the rift expand."

"I know."

"You will let the monsters win."

Dale looked at Marcus. Marcus looked back, tears in his eyes.

"Marcus," Dale said. "Run. Now."

Marcus shook his head. "I'm not leaving you."

"You have to. Because I'm going to do something stupid."

Dale turned back to the suits.

"I am not a technician," Dale said. "I am a civilian. And civilians don't have to follow protocol."

He kicked the badge.

It slid across the wet pavement, into the black sludge.

The sludge hissed. It ate the badge.

And then, it ate the suits.

They didn't dissolve. They *unraveled*. The gray fabric came apart into threads of ink. The blurred faces smeared into static.

The figures screamed. A digital scream. A sound of corrupted data.

They vanished.

The alley was empty.

Except for the black sludge.

And the sky.

The ink clouds were descending.

Dale looked at his hands. They were clean. No ash. No glue.

His System interface was blank.

No points. No rating. No license.

Just a single, blinking cursor.

**STATUS: UNLICENSED.** **WARNING: UNLICENSED ACTIVITY DETECTED IN SECTOR 4.** **ALERT: NEW THREAT LEVEL: OMNICIDAL.** **RECOMMENDATION: FLEE.**

Dale laughed. A dry, humorless bark.

He picked up his clipboard. It was still empty.

He picked up a pen from his pocket.

He started writing.

Not a report.

A manifesto.

He wrote the first line.

*The Authority is the infestation.*

And then, the ground cracked open. Not in the library. Not in the alley.

Under his feet.

A hand, made of black ink and bone, reached up.

Dale didn't spray. He didn't run.

He smiled.

Because now, he was off the books.

And the monsters didn't know how to fight a man who didn't care if he survived.
