Chapter 161: Doppelganger Appeal Void

# Chapter 161

The darkness smelled of wet drywall and copper.

Dale pressed his back against the door frame. His breath came in short, sharp bursts.

He counted them.

Four seconds in. Four seconds out. Standard respiratory control for confined-space entry.

The glass shard bit into his palm.

A small, grounding pain. Good.

Pain meant nerve function.

Pain meant he wasn't dead.

The figure across the room shifted. The floorboards groaned under a weight that shouldn't exist. It wasn't just the other Dale's mass. It was the density of the air around him, thick with unresolved dimensional pressure.

"You're breaking protocol," the other Dale said.

His voice sounded like Dale's, but filtered through gravel. Static. The sound of a radio tuned between stations.

Dale didn't answer. He scanned the room.

The living room was intact, but the geometry was wrong.

Angles that should have been ninety degrees twisted at impossible obtuse ones.

The shadows pooled in the corners, thick and viscous.

"Identify the hazard," the other Dale said. "Use your words. You always use your words."

Dale raised the glass. It caught the faint, sickly green light leaking from the ceiling cracks.

"Structural compromise," Dale said. His voice was steady. Too steady.

He sounded like he was reading a manual. "Secondary entry points unsealed.

Contamination risk high."

The other Dale tilted his head. The movement was jerky, like a puppet with tangled strings.

"Wrong," the figure said. "The hazard isn't the structure. The hazard is the technician."

Dale looked at his hands. They were shaking. He clenched them tighter.

The glass cut deeper.

Blood welled, bright and red against the grey skin.

"I am the technician," Dale said.

"No." The figure stepped forward.

The crowbar dragged across the floor, screeching. "You are the residue. The leftover.

The part that wasn't certified."

A notification pinged in Dale's peripheral vision. Red text. Aggressive.

**FIELD GUIDE LOCKED: 23:58:12 REMAINING.**

Time.

He had time.

The lock was a timer. It wasn't a permanent curse.

It was a cooldown.

"If you kill me," Dale said, "you fail the final inspection."

The other Dale stopped. The crowbar hovered.

"Explain," the figure said.

Dale swallowed. His throat felt like sandpaper.

He needed to buy time.

He needed to find the loophole. The system rewarded thoroughness. It punished shortcuts.

The other Dale had taken a shortcut.

He had become a Class IX without finishing the paperwork. That was a violation.

"Class IX requires a Thoroughness Rating of 10.0," Dale said.

The figure's eyes narrowed. The smile stretched wider, showing too many teeth.

"I am perfect."

"Perfect is not thorough," Dale said. "Perfect is efficient. The Authority doesn't care about efficiency. They care about documentation."

Dale took a step forward.

Then another. He kept his eyes on the figure's feet. The other Dale's stance was wide, unbalanced.

He was compensating for a power that wasn't fully integrated. A glitch in the rendering.

"You have no Field Guide," Dale continued.

"You have no protocols.

You are operating on instinct. Instinct is Class I behavior.

You are a Class IX with Class I tools."

The crowbar lowered an inch.

"Who are you?" the figure asked again. But softer this time. Less confident.

"I'm the guy who files the reports," Dale said.

From the kitchen, a voice called out.

"Dale? Dale, talk to me."

It was Sarah. She was outside the house. But the door was locked. How could she speak through the wood?

Dale froze. The other Dale froze.

"Sarah," Dale said.

"She's not real," the figure said. "She's a hallucination. A symptom of the infestation."

"She's my client," Dale said. "And she's waiting for the invoice."

The figure laughed. It was a dry, rattling sound.

"Invoices. You care about invoices. While the world ends, you worry about billing."

"I care about the audit trail," Dale said.

He turned his head slightly, looking toward the kitchen. The door there was ajar.

A shadow moved in the hallway.

Not Sarah. Something else. Something long-legged and skittering.

A new notification.

**UNLICENSED ENTITY DETECTED: ECHO-CLASS MIMIC.** **STATUS: ACTIVE.** **THREAT LEVEL: 4.**

Dale's heart hammered against his ribs. A mimic. It fed on identity. It copied the user's voice and appearance to lure them into a trap.

The other Dale was a mimic. Or part of one.

But Sarah? Sarah was on the porch. He had seen her. He had heard her.

"Sarah, get back!" Dale shouted.

The figure smiled. "She's already inside."

The shadow in the hallway stepped into the living room.

It looked like Sarah. But the proportions were off.

The arms were too long.

The fingers had too many joints.

The mimic tilted its head.

"Are you ready for the final exam?" it asked in Sarah's voice.

Dale looked at the mimic. Then at the other Dale.

The other Dale raised the crowbar.

"Kill it," the other Dale said. "It's contaminating the workspace."

Dale didn't move. He looked at the mimic.

He looked at the ceiling.

The cracks were widening.

The green light pulsed.

"If I kill it," Dale said, "I have to document it."

"Then document it!" the other Dale snapped.

"I can't," Dale said. "My Field Guide is locked."

The other Dale paused. The crowbar hung in the air.

"Then guess," the other Dale said.

"No."

Dale dropped the glass.

It hit the floor with a clear, distinct sound.

He raised his empty hands.

"I don't guess," Dale said. "I inspect."

He walked toward the mimic.

The other Dale stepped back. Confused. "What are you doing?"

"Checking for entry points," Dale said.

He walked right up to the mimic. The creature's face was smooth, featureless plastic. Sarah's eyes were painted on, static and dead.

Dale reached out. He touched the mimic's chest.

The texture was wrong. It wasn't skin. It was chitinous.

Hard. Like the shell of a beetle.

**IDENTIFICATION PROTOCOL INITIATED: MANUAL ENTRY REQUIRED.**

The prompt flashed in his vision.

**ENTER SPECIES NAME:**

Dale stared at the prompt.

He had no knowledge of Echo-Class Mimics. He had never encountered one. But he had encountered infestations that mimicked human behavior.

He had encountered pheromone trails that confused the senses.

He typed in his mind.

**Rattus profundus mimicry variant.**

The system rejected it.

**INCORRECT.**

The mimic's hand shot out. It grabbed Dale's wrist. Cold. Hard.

The other Dale swung the crowbar.

Dale ducked.

The crowbar smashed into the mimic's shoulder. The creature shrieked. The sound was digital. Like a file being corrupted.

"Get away from it!" the other Dale yelled.

Dale pulled his arm back. The skin was bruised, black and purple.

"It's not a rat," Dale said. "It's a bug."

The mimic shrieked again. It lunged at Dale.

Dale dodged. He scrambled behind the sofa.

"Think!" Dale shouted to himself.

He looked at the other Dale. The figure was sweating. The green light was reflecting off his forehead.

"Who are you?" Dale asked. "Really."

"I am you," the figure said. "The version of you that stopped worrying about the rules."

"Liar," Dale said.

He looked at the mimic. It was confused by the dual threat. It looked at Dale. Then at the other Dale.

"Choose," Dale said.

The mimic turned to the other Dale.

"You're the original," the mimic said in Sarah's voice. "But you're broken."

The other Dale raised the crowbar.

"I'm not broken," the figure said. "I'm upgraded."

The mimic attacked.

It was fast. Faster than the other Dale expected.

It swarmed him.

Multiple limbs, multiple heads. The mimic wasn't one person. It was a hive.

The other Dale fought. He swung the crowbar.

He kicked.

He broke the mimic's limbs.

But for every limb he broke, two more grew from the stump.

Dale watched. He stayed hidden behind the sofa.

He was thinking.

The mimic was copying the other Dale. The other Dale was a Class IX. High power. Low documentation.

The mimic was a parasite. It fed on high-level threats.

But it was also a violation.

Dale pulled out his clipboard. The page was blank. The pen was gone.

He picked up the broken glass from the floor.

He looked at the other Dale. The figure was losing. The mimic was overwhelming him. The green light was flickering.

"Help me," the other Dale screamed.

Dale didn't move.

"Help me!"

The other Dale fell. The mimic pinned him. Dozens of chitinous limbs wrapped around his torso. The crowbar clattered away.

The mimic leaned down. It opened its mouth. Inside was a void. A swirling blackness.

It bit into the other Dale's neck.

The other Dale screamed. A sound like tearing metal.

The mimic swallowed.

Then it stood up.

It looked at Dale.

The face was Sarah's again. But the eyes were black.

"It's gone," the mimic said.

Dale stood up.

He walked toward the mimic.

"I'm still here," Dale said.

The mimic smiled. "You're weak."

"I'm licensed," Dale said.

The mimic laughed. It was a horrible sound.

"Your license is revoked."

"No," Dale said. "My license is suspended."

He raised the glass.

"I can't document you," Dale said. "But I can report you."

He threw the glass.

It hit the mimic in the eye.

The mimic shrieked. It recoiled. Green fluid sprayed from the wound. Acid.

Dale didn't flinch. He knew the smell. It was formic acid. High concentration.

He backed up. He moved toward the front door.

The mimic pursued. It was slower now. Injured.

Dale reached the door. He turned the knob. Locked.

He looked back. The mimic was ten feet away.

"Open," Dale said.

The door didn't open.

The mimic lunged.

Dale dove. He rolled under the coffee table. The mimic crashed into the table. It splintered.

Dale crawled. He moved on his hands and knees. He kept moving.

He reached the window. The glass was broken. The frame was wood.

He kicked the frame. It gave way.

He climbed out.

He was on the porch.

The night air hit him. Cold. Real.

Sarah was there. She was holding a flashlight. Her face was pale.

"Dale?"

Dale rolled off the porch. He hit the grass. He scrambled to his feet.

The house shook.

The mimic was inside.

Dale looked at the house. The windows were dark. The door was closed.

He looked at Sarah.

"Did you hear anything?" Dale asked.

Sarah stared at him. Her eyes were wide.

"I heard screaming," she said. "From the kitchen."

"Who?"

"My husband," Sarah said. "My husband was in the kitchen."

Dale looked at the house.

Sarah's husband was dead. He had died in the initial Rift event.

Dale looked at his clipboard.

The page was no longer blank.

A single line had appeared.

**VIOLATION: FAILURE TO CONTAINMENT.** **PENALTY: -1000 TP.** **CROSS-CONTAMINATION: SEVERE.**

Dale looked at Sarah.

She smiled.

It was the same smile as the other Dale.

The same smile as the mimic.

"You're late," Sarah said. "The inspection starts in five minutes."

Dale backed away.

He looked at his hands. His fingers were turning black. The skin was hardening. Chitin.

He looked at the ground. The grass was wilting.

He looked at the sky. The Rift wasn't closing. It was watching him.

Sarah raised a crowbar.

It was the same crowbar.

"Time's up, Dale," she said.

Dale opened his mouth to speak.

Nothing came out.

His throat closed.

The notification flashed one last time.

**CERTIFICATION REVOKED.**

Dale fell to his knees. The blackness spread up his arms. He felt the itch. The deep, burning itch of something trying to hatch beneath his skin.

Sarah stepped closer. She raised the crowbar.

"Goodbye, technician," she said.

The crowbar descended.

Dale closed his eyes.

And then he heard the sound of a phone ringing.

It was coming from his pocket.

Sarah paused. The crowbar hovered in the air.

Dale's hand moved on its own. It reached into his pocket. He pulled out his old Nokia phone. The screen was cracked.

But it was ringing.

He answered it.

"Hello?" Dale whispered.

The voice on the other end was calm. Bureaucratic.

"Mr. Kowalski," the voice said. "We have received your appeal."

Sarah's face twisted. The smile vanished.

"Who is this?" the voice asked.

"It's the Authority," Dale said.

The voice paused.

"You have filed a Form 7B: Contest of Licensing," the voice said. "The appeal is granted. Your status is reinstated. Pending further investigation."

The blackness on Dale's arms receded. The itch stopped.

Sarah dropped the crowbar.

She looked confused. Her eyes cleared. The mimicry broke.

"Dale?" she said. "Why am I holding this?"

Dale looked at the house. The door opened.

The other Dale stepped out. He was whole. He was glowing with green light.

He looked at Dale.

The other Dale smiled.

"Nice try," he said. "But you didn't read the fine print."

The other Dale pointed at Dale's phone.

"Form 7B requires a counter-signature from a Class IX witness," the other Dale said. "Who signed it?"

Dale looked at the phone.

The screen was blank.

The voice on the phone laughed.

"No one signed it," the voice said. "Which means the appeal is void. And you, Mr. Kowalski, are now a fugitive."

Dale looked up.

The other Dale raised the crowbar.

Sarah raised a second crowbar.

They both smiled.

"Run," Dale said.

But his legs wouldn't move.

The ground beneath him cracked open.

Something pulled him down.
