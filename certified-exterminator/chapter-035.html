Chapter 35

The tissue was cooling too fast.

Dale pressed his palm flat against the nearest wall of dead biomass and counted. The surface temperature had dropped four degrees in the last ten minutes. He could feel it through his gloves, the same way he could feel the difference between a wall with insulation and one without. Fourteen years of pressing his hand against drywall and knowing what was behind it.

Four degrees per ten minutes meant the cellular structure would be unrecognizable in fifty. Maybe less.

He swept the flashlight in a slow grid pattern across the interior surface. The tissue here was layered, fibrous, threaded with what he'd initially logged as vascular channels. He'd counted them. He'd measured them. He'd noted their diameter and branching pattern in his Field Guide entry and gotten his 8.7.

But 8.7 wasn't enough.

He moved deeper. The compliance warning still hung in his peripheral vision, amber text pulsing slowly. COMPLIANCE OFFICER AUDITOR-7741 HAS FLAGGED YOUR PRESENCE. He'd read it once. He'd decided it was a problem for later.

The flashlight caught something.

A node. Small, maybe two centimeters across, nestled at the junction where three vascular channels met. He'd passed over this spot before. He was sure he'd passed over it. The node was pale, almost translucent, and it sat inside the tissue like a stone inside a loaf of bread.

He leaned closer. Adjusted his flashlight angle. The node had internal structure. Not vascular. Not fibrous. Something else. A series of concentric rings, like the cross-section of a tree, but the rings were made of a material that reflected light differently than the surrounding tissue.

He pulled out his Field Guide. The entry for HIST-0041 was already populated. Vascular architecture. Thermal regulation method. Reproductive strategy. Behavioral notes. The whole thing.

He turned to the blank page at the back. The one the System had added after his Level III certification, the one that said ADDENDUM FORMS IN THIS SPACE ARE VALID FOR POST-TERMINATION OBSERVATIONS WITHIN 24 HOURS OF ORIGINAL FILING.

He'd never used it.

He started writing.

*Post-mortem morphological observation: concentric ring structure at tertiary vascular junction. Diameter 2.1 cm. Material composition unknown. Reflective index inconsistent with known tissue types. Possible function: thermal memory storage. Hypothesis: organism retains thermal data from previous environmental exposure, enabling adaptive response to repeated treatments.*

He underlined the last line. If the organism stored thermal data, that explained why his second treatment attempt had been less effective than the first. He'd assumed diminishing returns from his own technique. The System had scored him 8.7 and he'd accepted it.

But if the organism was adapting, if it was learning from his methods, then his technique hadn't degraded. The target had changed. And he hadn't documented that.

That was the thing he'd missed.

He kept writing. Sketched the node. Measured it. Noted its position relative to the vascular network. Wrote a hypothesis about how it might interface with the thermal regulation system he'd already documented.

The System pinged.

POST-TERMINATION ADDENDUM DETECTED. PROCESSING.

Dale kept writing.

*Addendum filed under sub-entry designation HIST-0041-B. New field: Thermal Memory Structure. Classification: Secondary morphological adaptation. Relevance to treatment protocol: explains observed efficacy degradation across sequential engagements. Corrective protocol adjustment: vary thermal parameters between treatment cycles to prevent adaptive response.*

He finished. Closed the notebook. Waited.

The System processed.

ADDENDUM ACCEPTED. SUB-ENTRY HIST-0041-B CREATED. THOROUGHNESS RATING FOR HIST-0041-B: 9.3.

Dale exhaled.

Then the second notification came.

THOROUGHNESS RATING FOR HIST-0041 (PRIMARY ENTRY): FROZEN AT 8.7. COMPLIANCE FLAG ACTIVE. PRIMARY ENTRY CANNOT BE MODIFIED DURING FLAG PERIOD.

He read it. The flag didn't stop him. It just locked the primary entry. But the sub-entry was separate. Independent. A different object in the system's accounting.

9.3 on HIST-0041-B.

He needed 9.0 on the pending target species. The target species was HIST-0041. But if the target was a specimen exhibiting the thermal memory structure, then the relevant species classification was HIST-0041-B. A variant. A sub-type.

He pulled up the capture request form. The form asked for target species designation. He typed HIST-0041-B.

The form accepted it.

CAPTURE REQUEST: TARGET SPECIES HIST-0041-B. MINIMUM THOROUGHNESS REQUIREMENT: 9.0. CURRENT RATING: 9.3. REQUEST APPROVED FOR REVIEW.

Approved for review. Not approved outright. The Authority wanted to check his work.

He didn't care. He had a capture protocol now. He could seal the entry.

He stood up. His knees ached. He'd been kneeling on dead tissue for forty minutes and his back had filed its own complaint.

The space around him was quiet. The walls had gone from warm to cold to something approaching ambient. The organism was dead. Properly dead. He'd killed it and documented it and now he was trying to document it again, and the Authority was watching, and the clock was still running on whatever was coming through that entry.

A sound from the corridor behind him. Footsteps. Not the wet dragging sound of the organism's tissue contracting. Real footsteps. Hard-soled shoes on concrete.

Dale turned.

A figure stood at the entrance to the chamber. Tall. Thin. Wearing a grey suit that fit poorly, like it had been tailored for someone with different proportions. The face was wrong. Not monstrous. Just slightly wrong. Too symmetrical. The kind of symmetry that made your brain file it as a threat before you consciously registered why.

The figure held a clipboard. An actual clipboard. Metal frame, paper attached with a wire clip.

"Auditor-7741," the figure said. The voice was flat. Procedural. Like Dale's own, but without the dryness. Without the exhaustion. "You have been flagged for unauthorized analysis of a terminated specimen."

"I filed an addendum," Dale said.

"The addendum was processed. The flag remains." The auditor looked down at the clipboard. Looked back up. "The flag will remain for the duration of the review period. You understand what this means."

Dale understood. The flag meant his primary entry was locked. If the Authority decided his addendum was actually an attempt to manipulate his rating, they could void the sub-entry. They could void the capture request. They could pull his certification.

"I understand," Dale said.

"The flag also triggers a secondary review of all prior entries filed under your certification number." The auditor made a note on the clipboard. "All entries. Including those filed before the flag period."

Dale felt something cold move through his chest. "That's standard?"

"It is standard when a compliance flag is active."

He thought about his service records. Fourteen years of pest control. Six months of Authority work. Dozens of entries. Some of them sloppy. Some of them filed at 2 AM with coffee-stained hands. Some of them where he'd cut corners because the job was small and the pay was worse than cutting corners deserved.

"How long is the review period?"

"Until the Authority is satisfied."

Dale looked at the dead walls around him. The thermal memory structure he'd just documented. The capture request that might be voided. The entry that needed sealing before something else came through.

"The capture request is still active," he said.

"It is pending review. The flag does not automatically void pending requests. But it does subject them to scrutiny."

Dale nodded. He picked up his flashlight. He picked up his notebook. He started walking toward the exit.

"Technician Kowalski."

He stopped.

"The organism you terminated. The thermal memory structure you documented. Did you note the orientation of the concentric rings?"

Dale turned. "Yes. They were oriented perpendicular to the primary vascular axis."

The auditor wrote something down. The pen moved in a way that suggested the handwriting was not meant to be legible.

"Correct," the auditor said. "That is what we would expect to find."

"We?"

The auditor looked up. That wrong face. That too-symmetrical face.

"The Authority has encountered this species before, Technician. In other service areas. Other certifications. Other technicians who did not survive long enough to file their reports." The auditor closed the clipboard. "Your documentation is the first to identify the thermal memory structure. That is why the review will be thorough. That is why the flag will remain."

Dale said nothing.

"You should leave the site now. Your presence here constitutes ongoing evidence of the flag violation."

He left.

Outside, the entry was still open. A crack in the ground, maybe three meters wide, lined with the same dead tissue that had been the walls of the chamber. The edges were contracting. Closing. Slowly. Without the organism's active maintenance, the entry was healing itself.

He had maybe six hours before it sealed on its own.

He pulled out his phone. Opened the Authority's request portal. His capture request was still listed. Status: PENDING REVIEW. He could see the approval queue. Three requests ahead of his.

He closed the phone.

Then the System pinged.

CAPTURE REQUEST HIST-0041-B: REVIEW COMPLETE. APPROVED. TARGET COORDINATES ASSIGNED.

Dale opened the request. Read the target coordinates.

They weren't the entry.

They were a residential address. Fourteen miles from here. In his own service area. A house he'd inspected six weeks ago. A house where he'd found a Class-1 vermin infestation, treated it, filed the report, and walked away.

The target was inside that house.

And the species designation wasn't HIST-0041-B.

It was HIST-0041-A.

The primary entry. The one he'd rated 8.7. The one that was currently frozen by the compliance flag.

The System was telling him to capture a specimen of the primary species. A specimen that was, apparently, still alive. Still active. Inside a house he'd already cleared.

He looked at the coordinates. Looked at the distance. Fourteen miles. Twenty minutes by truck.

He looked at his certification status. Level III. Active. Flagged. Under review.

He looked at the capture protocol. It was unlocked. Available. He could use it.

But the species designation on the target was HIST-0041-A. His rating on that entry was frozen at 8.7. The capture protocol required 9.0.

He couldn't use the protocol. Not on this target. Not with the flag active.

Unless he went to that house and did something that changed the situation entirely.

He started walking toward his truck.
