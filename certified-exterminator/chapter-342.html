Chapter 342: Red Tape Review

# Chapter 342

The red tape unspooled from the desk like a living tongue. It slithered across the linoleum, seeking purchase on Dale's boots.

Dale didn't kick it away. That was a rookie mistake.

Physical resistance against a bureaucratic entity only invited escalation, and he stepped back, keeping the clipboard at chest height. The pen remained capped.

**STATUS: UNDER REVIEW.**

The words flashed in his peripheral vision, jagged and crimson. Not the clean blue of standard system notifications. This was administrative red. A warning color.

Behind him, the hallway stretched infinitely, lined with filing cabinets that hummed with low-frequency anxiety. The air tasted of stale toner and forgotten lunches. To his left, a filing cabinet labeled *Kowalski, D.* vibrated. Its drawer popped open an inch.

Dale ignored it. He focused on the creature at the desk.

It had no face. Just a dense knot of ribbon, stapled and glued in layers thick enough to stop a small-caliber bullet. Two eyes formed from brass grommets. A mouth, if it had one, was a slit where the adhesive had failed.

"Name," the creature said. Its voice sounded like paper tearing.

"Dale Kowalski. Level II Technician. License number 44-B-9021."

"Your license is provisional," the creature said.

It raised a hand made of twisted ribbon. A stack of forms materialized in the air, hovering before Dale's nose.

"You failed to submit Form 7-C: *Dimensional Breach Notification of Intent* prior to engagement.

You engaged the Class X threat without pre-authorization.

"

Dale looked at the forms. They were standard Authority paperwork. Tri-part carbon copies. The kind he used to file for cockroach infestations in duplexes.

He clicked his pen.

"I submitted the notification via the Rift interface at 14:02 hours," Dale said. "Timestamp logged. You can check the server logs in Cabinet 44, Drawer B. It's behind the coffee stains."

The creature paused. The red tape rippled.

"Records indicate non-submission," the creature said.

"Records are incomplete. Like your hygiene standards." Dale gestured at the desk.

Dust motes danced in the heavy, stagnant air.

"If you're auditing my conduct, you're auditing your own.

You haven't filed a hazard assessment for this office. Dust levels exceed OSHA threshold.

Air quality is a violation.

"

The grommet eyes narrowed.

"Threat assessment is pending," the creature said.

"Pending implies delay. Delay is negligence." Dale tapped his pen against the clipboard.

"Kael is still outside.

He's holding the perimeter. If I don't resolve this, he dies. If he dies, the breach expands.

If the breach expands, your office loses jurisdiction.

You know this. It's in the bylaws.

"

The creature's form tightened. The red tape pulled taut, revealing the skeletal structure of wire beneath.

"You attempt to leverage procedural fear," the creature said.

"I attempt to leverage procedural *fact*," Dale corrected. "Check the logs, or I file a grievance for obstruction of justice. I'll request a transfer to the Sanitation Division. They have better dental."

The hallway flickered. The filing cabinets groaned.

Dale knew he was walking a razor's edge.

The Authority rewarded thoroughness, but it hated insolence. But Class X threats weren't standard combat encounters. They were administrative traps.

Fighting them with force was suicide. Fighting them with paperwork was the only protocol that worked.

A drawer to his left slammed open.

A hand reached out. Not a monster's hand. A human hand. Pale, shaking, clutching a stapler.

Kael, and he wasn't in Ohio anymore either. He had followed Dale through the blue light. Or the hallway had pulled him in.

Kael looked terrified. His armor was cracked. His sword was gone, replaced by the clipboard Dale had forced on him.

"I can't hold it anymore," Kael whispered. "The weight. It's not paper. It's memory."

Dale didn't turn around.

"Keep holding it. If you drop it, you void your certification eligibility. You want to go back to being a knight who doesn't exist in the registry?"

"I want to breathe," Kael said.

"Breathe while you file. Look at the form. Find the checkbox for *Immediate Threat Mitigation*."

Kael looked down at the clipboard. His eyes widened.

"There's no checkbox. It's all text."

"Read the fourth paragraph. Third line. It's a loophole. The Authority allows verbal consent for emergency actions if the physical form cannot be processed within ten minutes."

Kael swallowed hard. He looked at the red tape creature.

"I... I consent to immediate mitigation!" Kael shouted. His voice cracked. "I am the witness!"

The creature hissed. The red tape lashed out, snapping at Kael's ankles.

Dale stepped in front of him. He slapped his yellow tag onto the creature's chest.

**TAG APPLIED: CONFLICT OF INTEREST.**

The creature froze. The red tape went slack.

"Violation code: Internal Audit Protocol 9," Dale said, his voice flat. "When an external contractor witnesses an audit, the auditor must recuse themselves or face a malpractice review. You can't audit me if the witness is part of the team. That's a conflict."

The creature shuddered. The brass grommets rattled.

"This is... irregular," the creature said.

"It's accurate," Dale said.

"Now.

The performance review. You have thirty seconds to list the violations, or I walk out and let the infestation spread.

You lose the job. I get a new one. Maybe in Florida.

Termites. I like termites.

"

The creature's form collapsed inward, shrinking. The red tape unraveled, falling to the floor in a heap. The desk remained.

But it wasn't a desk anymore, which was a nest.

A pulsating, chitinous nest, woven from shredded documents and black sludge. It sat where the chair had been. And inside it, something was moving.

**NEW THREAT DETECTED: THE UNDERSECRETARY'S BROOD.** **SPECIES: *Bureaucratia parasita*.** **WEAKNESS: Unfiled Complaints.**

Dale looked at his Field Guide entry. It populated instantly. The text was dense, legalistic, and boring. But the weakness was clear.

"Kael," Dale said. "Write a complaint. Right now. About the noise."

Kael blinked. "What?"

"Write it.

Use the pen. Make it specific. Cite the decibel level.

Cite the date.

Make it real."

Kael looked at the nest. A chitinous leg poked out, dripping black ink.

"I don't know how to write a complaint," Kael said.

"You know how to accuse someone of crimes," Dale said. "It's the same thing. Just add a stamp."

The nest expanded. The sludge bubbled. A voice emerged from the pile of paper, guttural and wet.

"*Objection*," the voice gurgled. "*Hearsay*."

"It's not hearsay if you sign it," Dale said.

He turned to Kael.

"Sign it. With your blood if you have to.

Authority doesn't care about the medium. It cares about the intent.

"

Kael bit his thumb. He pressed it to the paper. A red fingerprint bloomed on the white space.

**COMPLAINT FILED: NOISE VIOLATION.** **STATUS: PENDING ADJUDICATION.**

The nest shrieked. The sound was high-pitched, like nails on a chalkboard amplified by a stadium speaker.

The red tape that had been on the floor crawled toward the nest. It wasn't attacking. It was feeding. The bureaucracy was being consumed by its own inefficiency.

Dale watched, and he knew this mechanism.

He'd seen it with ant colonies. When the queen is threatened, the workers swarm.

But if you disrupt the pheromone trail, the swarm collapses.

The complaint was the pheromone disruptor.

The nest convulsed. The black sludge hardened, turning into brittle plastic. The chitinous legs snapped.

**TREATMENT POINTS: +50.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 8.4.**

"Low," Dale muttered. "You didn't document the noise level. I gave you an eight because you tried. Next time, measure it."

Kael slumped against the filing cabinet. He was gasping for air.

"That was... that was a monster," Kael said.

"That was a problem," Dale corrected. "We solved the problem. Now we leave."

Dale turned to the door. It was still open. But the hallway behind it had changed.

The infinite corridor was gone. In its place was a void. A dark, swirling mass of static.

And standing in the void was a woman, and she wore a suit made of gray paper. Her face was a blank sheet, pinned down at the corners. In her hand, she held a stamp.

Not a rubber stamp. A brand. Glowing with white heat.

**ENTITY: THE ARCHIVIST.** **CLASS: IX.** **STATUS: ACTIVE.**

Dale stopped. His hand hovered over the doorknob.

The Archivist raised the stamp. The air in the hallway turned hot.

The filing cabinets burned.

Not with fire, but with data. Their contents flew out, spinning in the air, turning into ash.

"You have violated the Seal," the Archivist said. Her voice was the sound of a fax machine jamming. "You filed a complaint against the State."

"It wasn't against the State," Dale said. "It was against a specific employee. There's a distinction."

"The distinction is irrelevant. You disrupted the workflow."

The Archivist stepped forward. The void rippled behind her.

Kael scrambled back, his eyes wide.

"Dale. The sword. I need my sword."

"You don't have a sword," Dale said. "You have a pen. Use it."

"I can't fight her with a pen!"

"Then don't fight her. File a motion for dismissal."

Dale reached into his pocket. He pulled out a folded piece of paper. It was blank.

He hadn't filled it out yet, and he needed to know what she wanted before he could stop her. The Authority's system rewarded identification before elimination. If he guessed wrong, the cross-contamination penalty would wipe his Field Guide. He'd lose everything.

The Archivist raised the stamp. The heat intensified. Dale's gloves smoked.

**WARNING: IMMEDIATE TERMINATION IMMINENT.**

Dale looked at the blank form in his hand. He looked at the burning cabinets. He looked at Kael, who was trembling but holding the clipboard tight.

If Dale filed a motion for dismissal, he admitted he had no legal standing.

He would be fired. Not just from the Authority.

From existence.

His license would be revoked. He would become unlicensed. Vermin.

If he didn't, she burned him.

There was a third option. A loophole Dale had noticed in the fine print of the Level IX exams.

*Appeal via Counter-Claim.*

You couldn't dismiss the Accuser. You had to Accuse back. But you needed evidence.

Dale looked at the burning cabinets. One drawer had survived. Cabinet 44. *Kowalski, D.*

He walked toward it.

"Dale, no!" Kael shouted.

Dale ignored him. He opened the drawer.

Inside was a single folder. It wasn't his own file. It was stamped with a symbol Dale had never seen. A spiral, black and red.

He pulled it out.

The Archivist froze. Her stamp lowered an inch.

"That is not yours," she said. Her voice lacked its previous certainty.

"It's in my cabinet," Dale said. "In a system that rewards thoroughness, possession is proof of jurisdiction. If it's in my cabinet, it's my evidence."

The Archivist's blank face seemed to tilt.

"Open it," she whispered.

Dale hesitated. This was a trap.

It had to be a trap.

But the Authority didn't lie about the contents of the boxes.

It only lied about the consequences, and he opened the folder.

The first page was a photograph.

It was a picture of Dale. But not the Dale who stood there. It was Dale, older.

Bearded. Wearing a suit of armor made of bone.

The date was ten years in the future.

And the caption read: **SUBJECT: THE EXTERMINATOR.** **STATUS: CLASS ZERO. CONTAINMENT FAILED.**

Dale looked up. The Archivist was smiling. Or trying to. The paper creased.

"You think you're the hunter," the Archivist said. "You're the pest."

The folder burned in Dale's hand. The ash scattered, forming words in the air.

**NEW OBJECTIVE: SURVIVE THE AUDIT.** **TIME LIMIT: 60 SECONDS.**

Dale looked at Kael.

"Run," Dale said.

Kael didn't move. He stared at the floating text.

"Dale, what is that?"

"It's the truth," Dale said.

He grabbed the burning folder. It didn't hurt. It felt like ice.

"And now we know who's really in charge."

The Archivist raised her stamp again.

"Stamp applied: *Permanent Record*."

The white heat slammed into Dale's chest, and he didn't scream. He didn't burn.

He fell.

He fell through the floor of the office, through the void, through the layers of reality, landing in a darkness that smelled of ozone and rot.

Kael reached for him.

But Dale was already gone.

And in the dark, something chirped. Not a bird. Not an insect.

A Geiger counter.
