Chapter 367: Door Left Open

# Chapter 367

The smoke tasted like copper and ozone.

Dale didn't cough. He adjusted the respirator seal on his face, checking the pressure gauge with a thumb.

It was holding at forty PSI.

Good. The air outside the Authority building wasn't breathable, but it was treatable.

Jenkins lagged three steps behind. Her boots scraped against the fractured pavement, kicking up dust that shimmered with residual rift energy. She was breathing hard, her hand hovering near the stun-baton at her hip.

Dale didn't look back. He kept his eyes on the door.

The symbol was carved deep into the obsidian slab. Not painted. Not etched. *Burned*. A keyhole, jagged and asymmetrical, weeping black fluid that evaporated before it hit the ground.

"Kowalski," Jenkins shouted over the rising wind. "The perimeter alarm is active. Sector 4 is going to lock down."

"Let it lock," Dale said. He unclipped his clipboard from his belt. The plastic was warped from the heat, but the paper inside was dry. "If they lock it, they're confirming the containment protocol is still failing."

"Confirming? They're trying to kill us, Dale."

"No.

They're trying to keep the infestation contained.

" Dale tapped the paper. "Vane thinks I'm a threat.

The Authority thinks I'm a technician.

Only one of them is wrong.

"

He stepped forward. The heat radiating from the door singed the edges of his coveralls.

He ignored the sting.

Pain was data.

Temperature was data.

The keyhole widened.

A low hum vibrated in Dale's teeth. It wasn't sound. It was frequency.

High-pitched. Painful.

Jenkins covered her ears.

"Stop! That's the sonic array. Vane's trying to shatter the seal."

Dale didn't stop, and he pulled a pen from his shirt pocket. Not a weapon. A ballpoint.

Blue ink.

He drew a line on his clipboard. Then another. A grid.

"Jenkins, do you have your Field Guide open?"

"My what?"

"The interface. The thing you ignore while you swing that baton."

Jenkins blinked. She stared at him, sweat dripping from her nose. Then, slowly, she swiped her hand through the air. A translucent blue screen flickered into existence before her eyes.

"Okay," she whispered. "I have it."

"Look up 'Rattus profundus'. Subsection: Dimensional Anchoring."

Jenkins frowned, her fingers dancing over the invisible keys.

"Why?

That's a rat.

That's a Class-1 vermin. This is a Class-9 dimensional breach.

"

"Check the footnote."

She did. Her eyes widened.

"Parasitic symbiosis," she read. "High-frequency vibration disrupts neural pathways in smaller hosts. Larger hosts use vibration as a stabilization mechanism. Do not attempt sonic suppression on Class-4 or higher dimensional anchors."

She looked up, pale.

"We're not fighting a lock."

"No," Dale said. "We're disrupting its heartbeat."

The hum grew louder. The black fluid pooling around the keyhole trembled. The obsidian door groaned, a sound like grinding bone.

Dale stepped onto the threshold.

The system notification pinged in his peripheral vision.

*WARNING: UNLICENSED PROTOCOL DEPLOYMENT IMMINENT. CROSS-CONTAMINATION RISK: 40%.*

Dale ignored the warning. He knew the rules. He also knew the loophole.

The cross-contamination penalty applied when you used a protocol designed for one creature type on another. But Dale wasn't using a combat protocol. He was using a *diagnostic* one.

He raised his clipboard.

"Initiate Protocol: Structural Resonance Mapping," he said. His voice was flat. Bored, even.

The system hesitated. It wasn't a standard command. It wasn't in his certification tree.

*PROTOCOL RECOGNIZED: NON-COMBAT. DIAGNOSTIC UTILITY. THREAT LEVEL: NEUTRAL.*

*ACTION: PERMITTED.*

Dale didn't attack the door. He attacked the *air* around it.

He tapped the pen against the clipboard. *Tap. Tap. Tap.*

Three short, sharp rhythms.

The black fluid splashed upward, defying gravity. It formed a sphere, hovering inches above the keyhole. The sphere vibrated, matching the rhythm of the pen.

Jenkins watched, mouth open.

"You're… you're syncing with it?"

"I'm documenting it," Dale corrected. "The Auditor wasn't a monster.

It was a symptom.

A blockage in the system.

Vane put a sonic dampener on it to stop the noise, but the noise was the only thing keeping the blockage from spreading.

"

The sphere pulsed. The hum in Dale's teeth softened, turning into a dull ache.

"Vane's error," Dale murmured. "He treated the symptom. He didn't treat the cause."

The obsidian door cracked.

Not a violent shatter. A clean split. A seam appeared down the center, glowing with soft, white light.

"Jenkins, record this," Dale said. "Entry: Dimensional Anchor, Type A. Weakness: Harmonic Disruption. Treatment: Controlled Frequency Induction."

Jenkins scrambled to type it into her guide. Her hands were shaking, but she got the words down.

*FIELD GUIDE UPDATED.* *NEW ENTRY: DIMENSIONAL ANCHOR (TYPE A).* *TARGETED DAMAGE BONUS AVAILABLE.*

The system acknowledged the entry. But it didn't award points for the kill. Because there was no kill. Yet.

The light from the crack intensified.

A figure stepped out.

It wasn't a monster. It wasn't a beast.

It was a man. Or it had been, once.

He wore a suit, tailored and pristine, despite the apocalypse. His skin was translucent, revealing the pulsing organs beneath. His eyes were black voids, no whites, no irises. Just depth.

Vane.

The Auditor, and he stood in the doorway, looking at Dale. Not with anger. With curiosity.

"You used a diagnostic tool," Vane said. His voice was layered, echoing with multiple tones. "In a combat zone."

Dale didn't lower his clipboard.

"It wasn't a combat zone until you made it one."

Vane tilted his head.

"The Authority does not recognize diagnostics as valid extermination methods. You have no points.

You have no status. You are still a Level II technician.

"

"I have a Field Guide entry," Dale said. "And I have a vulnerability."

Vane smiled. It was a terrible smile, too wide, stretching his translucent skin until it threatened to tear.

"Your vulnerability is that you believe documentation solves existence."

Vane raised a hand. The air around Dale warped.

*WARNING: HOSTILE ENTITY DETECTED. CLASS: IX. PROTOCOL REQUIRED: IMMEDIATE TERMINATION.*

Dale didn't move.

"Jenkins," Dale said. "The ledger."

Jenkins flinched.

"What?"

"Vane said we're the key. But keys don't open doors. They unlock *accounts*."

Dale looked at Vane.

"The Authority isn't a government. It's a bureaucracy. And you've been filing false reports for decades."

Vane's smile vanished. The translucent skin over his chest rippled.

"You think you can audit a god?"

"I think," Dale said, "that if I can prove you're corrupt, the system will revoke your license."

Vane laughed. A dry, rattling sound.

"The Authority predates your species. We do not have licenses."

"Then you have employees," Dale said. "And employees have errors."

He held up his clipboard.

The drawing of the key was gone. In its place was a new sketch. A ledger.

With red ink bleeding through the lines.

"I found the discrepancy," Dale said. "Sector 4's population count doesn't match the biomass harvest records.

You're skimming.

Not money. Life force.

"

Vane's eyes narrowed. The black voids seemed to deepen.

"Insolence," Vane said.

He snapped his fingers.

The sonic array kicked in. A blast of pure, invisible force hit the doorway.

Jenkins flew backward. She hit the pavement, sliding ten feet. Her Field Guide shattered in her hand, the blue light fizzling out.

Dale took the hit. He didn't fly.

He absorbed it.

His respirator cracked.

The pressure gauge spun wildly, then stopped.

*HEALTH: 40%.* *STRESS LEVEL: CRITICAL.* *CONTAMINATION ANXIETY: ELEVATED.*

Dale's hands trembled. He looked at the clipboard. The paper was burning.

He didn't care about the health bar. He cared about the paper.

He pulled a fresh sheet from the side of the clipboard. He had saved it. A spare.

He looked at Vane. The Auditor was stepping forward, one foot crossing the threshold. The white light behind him dimmed, replaced by a swirling vortex of dark, insectile shapes.

The infestation was waking up.

Vane stopped. He looked at Dale's hands. Then at the paper.

"You're trying to write me out," Vane said. "With ink."

"I'm trying to cite you," Dale said. "Violation K-9: Unauthorized Modification of Biological Assets."

Vane paused. The system pinged.

*VIOLATION DETECTED: K-9.* *STATUS: PENDING VERIFICATION.* *PENALTY: IMMEDIATE TERMINATION OF LICENSED ENTITY.*

Vane's eyes flickered. For a second, the black voids showed white. Fear.

"No," Vane whispered. "That rule… that rule is dormant."

"It's not dormant," Dale said. "It's just never been triggered. Because no one ever got close enough to see the ledger."

Dale slammed his hand against the clipboard. He tore the paper off.

"Jenkins!"

Jenkins groaned, struggling to her feet. Blood ran from her nose. She reached for her baton.

"Get to the perimeter console!" Dale shouted. "Upload this! Use the override code I gave you in Chapter 30!"

Jenkins froze.

"Dale, that code is for emergency containment. It's not for audits!"

"It's for system integrity!" Dale yelled. "If Vane has no license, he's an infestation! Treat him as such!"

Vane lunged, and he moved faster than human physics allowed. One step, and he was ten feet away. Another, and he was within arm's reach.

Dale didn't fight. He didn't have a weapon. He had a pen.

He thrust the pen forward. Not at Vane. At the air.

*PROTOCOL: TARGETED INJECTION.*

The pen tip pierced the air, hitting the invisible barrier Vane was generating. A spark flew.

*ERROR: INCOMPATIBLE MATERIAL.*

Vane slammed his hand into Dale's chest.

Dale flew back. He hit the pavement, skidding until his boots dug into the asphalt. His ribs cracked. The sound was wet, distinct.

He lay there, staring at the sky. Smoke blocked the stars.

Vane stood over him. He looked down, his face a mask of translucent horror.

"You are obsolete," Vane said. "You are a relic of a system that no longer applies."

Dale coughed. Blood spattered on his chin.

"Jenkins," he wheezed. "Did you upload it?"

Silence.

Jenkins was standing by the perimeter console. Her hand was over the keypad. She was looking at Vane. Then at Dale.

Her hand hovered.

If she uploaded the code, Vane would be stripped of his powers. He would become mortal. He would be killable.

But if she uploaded the code, the Authority would recognize Vane as an infestation. And infestations were exterminated.

Dale closed his eyes. He could feel the system processing the violation. The gears of the bureaucracy grinding.

*CALCULATING PENALTY…*

Vane raised his hand to strike again.

Then, a sound.

Not from the console.

From Dale's pocket.

His old, analog pager buzzed. A physical device. Unaffected by the digital lockdown.

Dale reached into his coveralls. His fingers brushed the plastic casing. He pulled it out.

The screen lit up.

A single line of text.

*ADMINISTRATOR OVERRIDE: DENIED.* *REASON: INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE.*

Dale stared at the pager. Then he looked at Vane.

Vane smiled. "You failed, Technician."

Dale looked at Jenkins. She was crying, silent tears cutting tracks through the dust on her face. She hadn't uploaded it.

She had hesitated.

Dale realized the truth.

The system didn't care about truth. It cared about *procedure*.

And he hadn't followed procedure, and he had skipped the isolation step.

*VIOLATION: FAILURE TO ISOLATE TARGET BEFORE TERMINATION.* *PENALTY: REVOCATION OF CURRENT STATUS.*

Dale's Field Guide flickered. His certification level dropped.

*CLASS II: SUSPENDED.* *CLASS I: RESTORED.*

He was a rookie again.

Vane laughed. It was a victory laugh.

"You broke protocol, Dale. The Authority protects its own. It does not audit its own."

Dale sat up. His ribs screamed. But he didn't look at Vane. He looked at Jenkins.

"Jenkins," Dale said. "Do you have a backup of the Field Guide?"

Jenkins nodded, sniffing.

"On my personal drive. It's not linked to the Authority yet."

Dale stood up. He swayed, but he stayed upright.

He looked at Vane.

"Then we're going off-book."

Vane's smile faltered.

"What?"

"I'm going to do what you should have done," Dale said. "I'm going to treat the nest."

He pulled out his flashlight. Not to see. To blind.

He clicked it on. The beam cut through the smoke, hitting Vane directly in the eyes.

Vane shrieked. A sound of tearing metal.

"Jenkins, run!"

Dale didn't run. He walked toward the vortex behind Vane.

He walked toward the infestation, and he walked toward the thing that was actually crawling out of the Rift.

And he left the door open.
