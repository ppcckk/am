Chapter 141: Certified Level Three

# Chapter 141

Dale pressed the nib of his pen into the damp wood of the clipboard. The ink smeared, a black blot blooming across the grain like a bruise.

*Accept,* he thought, though he didn't write it. He just held the pen steady, his knuckles white, while the air around him shimmered with static.

The claw retracted. The floorboards groaned, settling back into place with a sound like a heavy door slamming shut. The vibration ceased. The silence that followed was thicker than the darkness of the crawlspace.

A soft chime rang in his left ear. Not the harsh buzz of an alert, but a polite, bureaucratic ping.

*Transaction Complete. Balance: Zero. Certification Level III Granted. Temporary Status.*

Dale exhaled. The breath fogged in the cold air.

He capped the pen and slid it into his shirt pocket, his hand trembling only slightly.

He checked his Field Guide. The new entry sat at the top, bold and red: *Rattus profundus (Variant B).* It was empty. No weakness identified.

No protocol listed.

Just a name and a terrifyingly high threat rating.

He climbed out of the crawlspace hatch, pulling himself up into the kitchen of the duplex.

His knees popped. His back ached. The kitchen was a mess of splintered wood and shattered tile, but the rats were gone.

The broodmother's remains were contained in the trash bag, sealed with duct tape. A job well done, if one ignored the fact that he had just sold his soul to a cosmic bureaucracy to survive.

The front door banged open.

Dale turned. Standing in the doorway, silhouetted against the gray morning light of suburban Ohio, was a woman in a suit that looked far too expensive for a pest control technician. She wore a face mask, but her eyes were sharp, assessing, and devoid of sympathy. Behind her stood two men in tactical gear that looked like it belonged to a private military contractor than the Licensing Authority.

"Mr. Kowalski," the woman said. Her voice was clipped. "You are late for your orientation."

Dale wiped his hands on his coveralls. "I was busy. We had a Class IV breach. Unauthorized contact."

"We saw the notification," she said, stepping into the house.

Her boots clicked on the broken tile.

"That is why we are here. I am Supervisor Halloway.

These are Inspectors Vane and Kovic. We are here to audit your incident."

Audit. The word made Dale's stomach tighten. He hated audits. They meant someone was looking for errors.

"I filed the variance request," Dale said, walking past her to grab his toolbox. It was battered, dented, and filled with tools that had saved his life more often than his clients' warranties. "The system approved it. I have Level III status for the next twenty-four hours."

"Level III is a probationary tier," Halloway said, not moving from the center of the kitchen. She looked at the ceiling, where the cracks radiated outward like spiderwebs. "It is designed for technicians who have shown aptitude but lack the resources to handle high-tier entities without supervision. Or, in your case, without a supervisor."

"I don't need supervision. I need a Field Guide update. That entry is blank."

Inspector Vane, a tall man with a shaved head and a tablet in his hand, stepped forward. "It's blank because you didn't kill it.

You ran.

You contained the immediate threat, yes. But you didn't document the specimen. You didn't identify the rift signature.

You didn't verify the elimination."

"I got the broodmother," Dale said. He pointed to the trash bag in the corner.

"Sealed.

Double-taped. Protocol compliant."

"That was a Class I vermin," Halloway said. "The thing that punched through your floor was a Level IV. You paid for a license you aren't qualified to use. Do you know what happens when a technician exceeds their certification level without proper support?"

"I survive," Dale said.

"You contaminate," Vane said. He tapped his tablet.

"The Authority's algorithms are currently flagging your service area as a high-risk zone.

Not because of the rat.

Because of the *gap* you created. You forced a promotion.

The system doesn't like gaps. It fills them."

Kovic, the second inspector, was scanning the walls with a handheld device. It beeped rapidly.

"Residual dimensional static is high. 40% above baseline.

The rift isn't closed, Kowalski. It's just... paused."

Dale felt a cold prickle at the base of his neck. "I sealed the entry points. I used standard structural fill."

"Fill is for ants and termites," Halloway said.

She walked over to the hole in the floor. She didn't look at it.

She looked at Dale.

"You used a Level I protocol on a Level IV event. The cross-contamination penalty should have kicked in. You should be debuffed.

You should have lost your Field Guide entry for the rat."

Dale paused. He hadn't noticed the debuff. Had he? He checked his status screen.

*Status: Stable. Debuff: None.*

"I didn't use a protocol," Dale said slowly. "I used a variance request. The system waived the penalty as part of the transaction cost."

Halloway's eyebrows rose.

A fraction of an inch.

"Clever. But dangerous.

The Authority doesn't usually waive penalties. It trades them."

"Trade me what?" Dale asked.

"Your Thoroughness Rating," Halloway said. "You're currently sitting at a 9.2.

High.

Very high. You're the top scorer in this county. The Authority likes thoroughness.

It means you document.

You isolate. You verify.

It means you don't leave messes."

"I try," Dale said.

"But now you have a Level IV entity that you failed to fully process," Halloway said. "And you have a pending job. A residential inspection in the next block.

Apartment 4B. A German cockroach infestation.

Standard Class I."

"That's my next job," Dale said. He picked up his clipboard. "I have an appointment."

"You do," Halloway said. "And you have a visitor."

She gestured to the window.

Outside, parked on the lawn, was a sleek black van with no logos. The back doors were open.

A man was getting out. He was wearing a pristine white jumpsuit, unblemished. He held a silver briefcase.

He moved with a fluid, predatory grace that made Dale's professional instincts scream.

"Who is that?" Dale asked.

"That is Mr.

Silas Vane," Halloway said. "No relation to Inspector Vane, despite the name. He is a freelancer.

High-end.

He specializes in 'cleanups' for clients who want their infestations handled without the Authority's paperwork."

Dale frowned. "Freelancers aren't licensed."

"Mr.

Vane has a special dispensation," Halloway said. "He works for the clients who don't want the Authority to know about the rifts. They pay him to kill the monsters and hide the evidence.

No Field Guide entries. No Treatment Points.

No documentation."

"That's illegal," Dale said.

"It's inefficient," Halloway corrected.

"But it's profitable. And right now, Mr.

Vane is in your service area. He knows about the Level IV event. He knows you stopped it.

He wants to see if you can finish the job."

Dale looked out the window. Silas Vane was walking toward the house. He didn't look like a pest control technician. He looked like a surgeon.

"Why is he here?" Dale asked.

"Because you bought your life," Halloway said.

"And the Authority wants to see if you're worth the investment. If you fail this next job—if you let Vane take the credit for the cleanup, or if you leave the rift unstable—your Level III status will be revoked. You'll be demoted.

Or terminated."

"Terminated?"

"Your license will be stripped," Halloway said. "And without a license, you can't see the notifications.

You can't use the protocols.

You'll be a civilian in a war zone. And civilians in this county have a very low survival rate."

Dale looked at the trash bag. Then at the hole in the floor. Then at Silas Vane, who was now at the front door.

"I have a job to do," Dale said.

"You have two jobs," Halloway said. "Apartment 4B.

And the thing in your basement.

Vane is going to try to steal the kill.

He wants the Level IV carcass. There's black market value in unprocessed dimensional biomass."

Dale felt a surge of anger. Not at the monster. At the idea of waste.

"Stealing a kill is a code violation," Dale said.

"It's theft," Halloway said. "But it's also a way to avoid the Thoroughness penalty. If Vane kills it, and you don't, you haven't 'failed' to document it.

You've just been outcompeted. The Authority can't penalize you for losing to a freelancer.

They can only penalize you for sloppiness."

Dale's mind raced. If Vane killed the thing, Dale wouldn't get points. But he wouldn't lose points either.

He'd keep his rating.

But he wouldn't have the Field Guide entry. He wouldn't have the experience.

He'd be stuck with a gap in his knowledge.

And if he killed it himself, he'd have to process a Level IV entity with a Level III toolkit.

"You're setting me up," Dale said.

"I'm giving you a choice," Halloway said. "Kill it. Document it.

Prove you deserve the Level III. Or let Vane take it, and spend the rest of your career chasing crumbs from his table."

The door opened. Silas Vane stepped in. He smiled. It was a tight, practiced smile.

"Mr. Kowalski," Vane said. "Good morning. I see you've been busy."

Vane's eyes scanned the room. They landed on the trash bag. They lingered on the hole in the floor.

"Rough night?" Vane asked.

"Standard procedure," Dale said. He didn't look up from his clipboard. He was writing. *Incident Addendum: Third-party interference detected.*

Vane laughed.

A short, dry sound.

"Always with the paperwork, Dale. It's a habit.

A crutch. Monsters don't care about your forms."

"Monsters care about entry points," Dale said. "And entry points are documented."

Vane walked over to the hole in the floor. He peered into the darkness.

"I can hear it," he whispered.

"It's still down there. Breathing."

"It's not breathing," Dale said. "It's waiting."

Vane turned to Halloway. "Is he licensed for this?"

"He's probationary," Halloway said.

"Then he's not licensed," Vane said.

He pulled a weapon from his silver briefcase. It wasn't a spray bottle.

It was a gun.

A strange, humming device that looked like it fired concentrated ionized plasma. "I'll take it from here. Consider this a courtesy.

I'll split the credits with you. Fifty-fifty."

Dale looked at the gun. Then at Vane. Then at Halloway.

"No," Dale said.

Vane raised an eyebrow. "No?"

"I'm on the clock," Dale said. "And you're not in the service area. You don't have a key."

Vane smiled.

"I have a key to the house.

The owner gave it to me. They want it quiet.

They don't want the Authority knocking."

"The owner is in the hospital," Dale said. "I spoke to him this morning. He didn't give you a key. He gave me his code."

Vane's smile faltered. "You're lying."

"I'm documenting," Dale said. He held up his clipboard.

"Here's the transcript.

You entered the premises without a service order. That's a trespassing violation. And if you fire that weapon in a residential zone without a permit, you're looking at a felony charge from the Authority."

Vane's face hardened. "You think I care about the Authority?"

"I think you care about your rating," Dale said. "Freelancers don't have Thoroughness Scores. But they have 'Discretionary Credits.' And if you cause a scene, you lose them. You lose your dispensation."

Halloway stepped forward. "He's right, Silas. If you engage here, you're breaking the terms of your contract. I'll have to log it."

Vane looked at Halloway. Then at Dale. The tension in the room was thick, suffocating.

"You're gambling," Vane said to Dale. "That thing is huge. You have a can of bug spray and a clipboard."

"I have a protocol," Dale said.

"You have a death wish," Vane said.

He holstered his gun. "Fine.

Do your paperwork, Dale.

But when you get crushed, don't blame me. And don't blame the Authority. Blame your own stubbornness."

Vane turned and walked out. He didn't look back.

Halloway looked at Dale. "He'll be watching.

If you fail, he'll step in.

And he'll take the kill.

And he'll claim you were incompetent."

"I won't fail," Dale said.

"Then show me," Halloway said. "Show me your plan. Show me how you're going to kill a Level IV entity with Level III tools."

Dale looked at the hole in the floor. The cracks were still there. He could feel the vibration again. Faint, but rhythmic.

"I'm not going to kill it," Dale said.

Halloway stiffened. "What?"

"I'm going to trap it," Dale said. "And then I'm going to bait it out."

"With what?"

"With the next job," Dale said. He pointed to his clipboard. "Apartment 4B.

German cockroaches. But not just any cockroaches.

These are *Blattella germanica*.

They're attracted to pheromones. And dimensional entities are attracted to biological waste. If I can synthesize a lure..."

Halloway's eyes narrowed. "You're going to use cockroach pheromones as bait for a Level IV monster?"

"It's in the Field Guide," Dale said.

He flipped to the entry for the rat. *Rattus profundus*. Under 'Weaknesses,' it listed: *Attracted to concentrated protein sources.* "Cockroach frass is concentrated protein.

If I create a trail of high-density frass, I can draw it out.

Then I can isolate it. Then I can eliminate it."

Halloway stared at him. "That's insane. That's not a protocol. That's a hack."

"It's a workaround," Dale said. "And it's within the rules.

The Authority rewards creativity in documentation.

If I can prove the lure works, I get a bonus.

If I fail, I get demoted."

"You're betting your career on cockroach poop," Halloway said.

"I'm betting it on thoroughness," Dale said. He picked up his toolbox. "I need to get to Apartment 4B.

I need to collect samples. And I need you to delay Vane.

Keep him out of my service area for the next hour."

Halloway looked at her watch. "I can give you forty-five minutes. After that, Vane's contract overrides my jurisdiction."

"Forty-five minutes is enough," Dale said.

He walked to the door. He stopped.

"Supervisor," he said.

"What?"

"Did you see the claw?"

Halloway looked at the hole in the floor. "Yes."

"Gray skin. Smooth. Like the inspectors."

Halloway's face went pale. "What are you implying, Dale?"

"I'm writing a report," Dale said. "And I'm noting that the 'inspectors' who came here earlier didn't have badges. And they didn't answer questions. And their skin was wrong."

He stepped out the door.

Halloway didn't follow.

Dale walked across the lawn. The morning sun was weak, gray. He felt the weight of the clipboard in his hand.

It was his shield.

It was his weapon.

He approached the neighboring duplex.

Apartment 4B. The door was unlocked. The air inside smelled of stale grease and decay.

Classic German cockroach habitat.

Dale pulled out his flashlight.

He swept the kitchen.

The counters were cluttered with dirty dishes. The sink was clogged.

And there, in the corner, was a pile of dark, peppery specks. Frass.

He smiled. A small, grim smile.

He opened his toolbox. He took out a vial. He scraped the frass.

The floor beneath him vibrated.

*Thump. Thump. Thump.*

It was coming.

Dale held the vial up to the light. The brown powder glinted.

"Come and get it," he whispered.

And then, from the shadows of the living room, a voice spoke.

"Hello, Dale."

Silas Vane was sitting in the armchair. He wasn't holding a gun. He was holding a sandwich.

"I told you I'd be watching," Vane said. "And I'm not alone."

Behind Vane, the air shimmered. A rift opened.

Not small.

Not a crack. A tear.

And through it stepped not a monster, but a figure in a gray suit. Like Halloway. Like the inspectors.

But this one had no face. Just a smooth, gray mask.

The figure raised a hand.

Dale stopped scraping. He looked at the vial of frass. He looked at the faceless figure.

The System notification flashed.

**UNAUTHORIZED PERSONNEL DETECTED.** **THREAT LEVEL: UNKNOWN.** **ACTION: DOCUMENT OR FLEE.**

Dale looked at Vane.

"You set this up," Dale said.

Vane took a bite of his sandwich. "I told you. I specialize in cleanups. And sometimes, the cleanup involves... adjusting the variables."

The faceless figure stepped forward. The air warped around it.

Dale raised his clipboard.

"I'm not fleeing," Dale said. "I'm filing a complaint."

The figure tilted its head.

**NEW MESSAGE:** **COMPLAINT FILED. REVIEW PENDING.** **PENALTY FOR FALSE REPORT: TERMINATION.**

Dale's hands shook. Not from fear. From rage.

He uncapped his pen.

He wrote: *Witness: Silas Vane. Collusion confirmed.*

The faceless figure lunged.
