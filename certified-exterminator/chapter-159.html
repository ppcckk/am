Chapter 159: Unlicensed Archive

# Chapter 159

The darkness did not feel like absence. It felt like insulation. Thick, fibrous, and damp.

Dale's hand remained on the handle, but the metal was gone. His fingers closed around a cold, smooth surface that yielded under pressure, like wet clay. The air smelled of old paper and formaldehyde.

He blinked.

His eyes adjusted to the gloom. It was not a void.

It was a room.

A long, narrow corridor stretching infinitely in both directions, lined with shelves that vanished into shadow. Books. Thousands of them.

Millions.

The spines were blank.

A notification chimed in his left peripheral vision. Not the usual amber ping of the Authority. This was red. Urgent.

**SYSTEM NOTICE: UNAUTHORIZED ARCHIVE ACCESS.** **STATUS: ERASED.** **CANNOT READ ENTRY 492-B.**

Dale tapped the air. The text remained. He tried to summon his Field Guide. The interface flickered, glitching into static, then stabilized into a single, jagged line of text.

**FIELD GUIDE CORRUPTION DETECTED.** **SOURCE: SELF-REFERENTIAL PARADOX.**

He didn't panic.

Panicking caused a drop in OSHA compliance metrics. He checked his clipboard. It was still there.

The paper was blank. He uncapped his pen.

He wrote: *Location unknown.

Lighting poor.

Ventilation stagnant.*

Under the writing, the ink didn't dry. It floated, suspended in the air, glowing faintly blue.

"Welcome back," the voice said again. Closer this time.

Dale didn't turn around. He walked forward. The floor was covered in a thin layer of dust that didn't stir when he stepped on it. His boots made no sound.

"Stop walking," the voice commanded.

Dale kept walking. He passed a shelf where the books were vibrating. A low hum emanated from them.

He stopped and looked. The titles were written on the spines in handwriting he recognized.

His own.

*Chapter 1: The Duplex.* *Chapter 45: The Beetle Nest.* *Chapter 112: The Water Main Breach.*

He picked up one. *Chapter 89: The Burn.*

He opened it. The pages were empty. No, not empty.

They were filled with a fine, black powder that shifted when he tilted the book.

Ash.

"You're trying to sanitize reality," the voice said.

The tone was tired. Resigned. "You think if you document it, it's under control.

If you spray it, it's dead. But you can't exterminate memory, Dale."

Dale put the book back.

He pulled out his flashlight.

The beam cut through the dust, illuminating a trail of wet footprints leading away from him. They were barefoot.

Small.

He followed them.

To his right, a new notice appeared.

**VIOLATION LOG: CONTEMPLATION OF SELF.** **PENALTY: -50 TREATMENT POINTS PER MINUTE.** **NOTE: This infestation is classified as Cognitive Class IX.**

Class IX. Above his current level. Impossible for him to interact with under standard protocols.

He ignored the penalty counter ticking down in his vision. He had other priorities. The footprints led to a desk. A small, wooden desk, identical to the one in the previous room, except this one was piled high with red folders.

Dale approached the desk. He didn't touch the folders.

He inspected the legs.

They were reinforced with lead. A standard containment measure for high-radiation materials. Or high-risk psychological hazards.

He pulled a chair out. It scraped against the floor, a harsh, real sound in the silent library.

He sat.

The voice came from behind the desk. He didn't see the speaker, but he saw the shadow cast by the lamp. It was a silhouette of a man, slumped, head in hands.

"I'm tired," the shadow said.

"I know," Dale said.

He opened the top folder. It contained a single sheet of paper. A job order.

**JOB ID: 994-DELTA.** **CLIENT: DALE KOWALSKI.** **SERVICE: TOTAL ERADICATION OF PREVIOUS IDENTITY.** **STATUS: PENDING APPROVAL.**

Dale stared at the document.

It was a standard Authority form. The font was Courier.

The margins were one inch on all sides.

Everything was correct. Except for the client name.

"Is this a trap?" Dale asked.

"It's a review," the shadow said. "You've been running for years.

You never stop.

You never rest.

You don't let anyone close enough to help you. You isolate every problem.

You seal every entry point. You're becoming the seal, Dale. Not the exterminator."

Dale picked up a pen from the desk. It was a ballpoint, blue ink. He uncapped it.

"Who is the supervisor on this order?"

"Me," the shadow said. "And you."

Dale looked at the signature line. It was blank. He looked at the shadow.

"If I sign this, what happens?"

"You quit," the shadow said. "You wake up.

You go home.

You pay the mortgage. You forget the Rift. You forget the monsters.

You become a normal man with a boring job."

It sounded appealing. For a second, the exhaustion hit him like a physical blow. His shoulders dropped. His eyes burned.

But then he looked at the form again. He looked at the checkbox at the bottom.

*OPTION A: ACCEPT TERMS AND RESIGN.* *OPTION B: CONTEST TERMS VIA ARBITRATION.*

There was no Option C.

Dale flipped to the back of the folder. There was a second form. This one was yellow. A Hazardous Materials Incident Report.

He scanned it.

**INCIDENT TYPE:** MEMETIC LEAKAGE. **VECTOR:** BORAX CONTAMINATION. **SOURCE:** UNLICENSED USER [DALE KOWALSKI]. **CONTAINMENT STATUS:** BREACHED.

The shadow leaned forward. The light caught its face.

It was Dale.

Older. Grayer. The eyes were hollowed out.

"You used borax," the older Dale said. "You broke the rule.

You introduced a chemical agent into a purely informational space.

That's why you're here.

The Archive doesn't allow external contaminants. It tried to purge you.

I tried to stop it. I failed."

Dale looked at the yellow form. Then he looked at his clipboard.

He didn't ask for a sword. He didn't ask for a spell. He asked for a protocol.

He wrote.

**ADDITIONAL NOTES:** **1. The environment is unventilated.

Risk of spore inhalation: High.** **2.

The subject is displaying signs of terminal burnout. Not a monster. A symptom.** **3.

Borax was used correctly as a desiccant, but applied to the wrong substrate.

The Archive is not an infestation. It is a filing system.** **4.

To clean a filing system, you do not spray chemicals.

You organize.**

The older Dale watched him. "Organize?"

Dale stood up. He walked to the nearest shelf.

He pulled out a book.

He checked the call number.

It was scrambled. The letters were floating off the spine.

He took a piece of tape from his kit. He taped the spine down. He wrote a new label: *CLASS 1 VERMIN: RATTUS PROFUNDUS.*

He placed the book on the lower shelf.

The room shuddered.

"You can't just relabel things," the shadow said.

"I can," Dale said. "If the metadata is wrong, the system rejects the file. If the file is rejected, the system creates an error. Errors are vulnerabilities."

He pulled out another book. *CLASS 4 INSECT: ACID BEETLE.* He taped the spine. He moved it.

The hum in the room changed pitch. It dropped an octave.

The older Dale stood up. "Stop. You're destabilizing the core."

Dale ignored him. He worked faster. He grabbed books by the handful.

He sorted them. Not by title.

By threat level.

By treatment protocol.

He was building a Field Guide. Not in the interface. In the physical space. He was mapping the Archive against his own operational parameters.

The red folders on the desk glowed. They were heating up.

"You're going to get us killed," the shadow said. His voice was losing its calm. It was becoming jagged.

Dale didn't look up. "I'm going to get us certified."

He reached for the last book on the desk. The one with no title. The one that pulsed with a dark, rhythmic light.

When he touched it, the world tilted.

**SYSTEM ALERT: CRITICAL FAILURE.** **FIELD GUIDE OVERLOADED.** **NEW MECHANIC UNLOCKED: INDEXING.**

Dale froze.

Indexing. He had never heard of it. The Authority never taught it. It was a Class IX protocol, reserved for archivists who had been dead for centuries.

A menu popped up. It was simple.

**SELECT ENTRY TO DELETE:** **1. FEAR.** **2. DOUBT.** **3. REGRET.**

Dale looked at the three options.

If he deleted them, he would be efficient.

Perfect. He would never hesitate.

He would never make a mistake.

He would be the perfect technician.

But he would not be human.

He looked at the older Dale. The shadow was fading, turning into dust. The red door was visible again, miles away, glowing with warning lights.

"Which one?" the shadow whispered. "Pick one, Dale. Before it picks for you."

Dale looked at his pen. He looked at the menu.

He didn't pick one.

He reached for the fourth option that wasn't there. He tapped the air where a fourth line should have been.

**4. IDENTITY.**

The menu vanished.

The darkness screamed.

It wasn't a sound. It was a tear in the fabric of the room. The shelves exploded outward. Books flew like shrapnel.

Dale braced himself, raising his clipboard as a shield.

A piece of paper stuck to his chest. He looked down.

It was a new job order.

**CLIENT: THE AUTHORITY.** **SERVICE: REASSIGNMENT.** **STATUS: ACCEPTED.**

The floor dropped out from under him.

He fell.

As he fell, he saw the older Dale smiling. A sad, pitying smile.

"You didn't quit," the voice said, echoing in the freefall. "You just changed jobs."

The fall ended abruptly.

Dale hit hard concrete. The wind was knocked out of him. He gasped, rolling onto his side.

He was outside.

It was night. Rain was falling.

He was standing in the middle of a suburban street. The asphalt was cracked. The houses were intact, but the windows were boarded up with plywood.

People were running past him. They were screaming.

Dale sat up. He checked his clipboard.

The paper was blank again. But the pen was still in his hand.

He looked at his status bar.

**CERTIFICATION LEVEL: 0.** **TREATMENT POINTS: 0.** **FIELD GUIDE: EMPTY.**

He had lost everything.

A woman stopped near him. She was holding a baseball bat. She looked at him with wide, terrified eyes.

"Hey," she said. "Hey, are you licensed?"

Dale looked at his empty hands. He looked at the boarded-up house next to him.

A sound came from the attic of the house. A scratching sound. Heavy. Wet.

He stood up. His knees cracked.

"I don't know," Dale said.

The scratching got louder. The plywood bulged outward.

The woman raised her bat. "You need to leave. It's a Class III. We don't have a permit."

Dale looked at the bulging plywood. He looked at the woman.

He reached into his pocket. He pulled out a roll of duct tape.

He walked past the woman.

"Stand back," he said.

He tore off a strip of tape. He stuck it over the crack in the plywood.

The scratching stopped.

For a second, there was silence.

Then, the tape bleeded. Black, viscous fluid seeped through the adhesive.

The woman screamed.

Dale didn't flinch. He tore off another strip. He layered it over the first.

"Protocol requires full containment," he muttered.

He applied a third layer. Then a fourth.

The bleeding stopped.

The woman lowered her bat. "Did you just... seal it?"

"I isolated it," Dale said. He looked at the empty space where his Field Guide used to be.

He needed a way to identify the threat without the system.

He needed to do it the old way.

By observation.

He looked at the shape of the bulge. It was angular. Too sharp for a rat. Too small for a bear.

He looked at the fluid. It was not black. It was dark red.

Blood.

Not monster blood. Human blood.

Dale stepped closer to the door. He put his ear against the plywood.

He heard breathing. Ragged. Gasping.

"Who's in there?" he asked.

The breathing stopped.

A voice came through the wood. It was faint. Broken.

"Please," the voice said. "Let me out."

Dale pulled back. He looked at the woman.

"Is that your neighbor?"

The woman shook her head. "I don't live here. I'm just hiding. Everyone is hiding."

Dale looked at the door. He looked at the duct tape.

He realized something.

The Archive hadn't erased him. It had stripped his access. But it hadn't taken his knowledge.

He still knew how to identify a German cockroach from a brown-banded one. He still knew that if you didn't treat the nest, it came back.

And right now, the nest was inside that house.

And the thing inside that house was not a monster.

It was a victim.

Or a carrier.

Dale pulled out his pen. He wrote on the side of his clipboard: *SUBJECT: UNIDENTIFIED. SYMPTOMATIC. LIKELY HOST.*

He looked at the woman.

"Do you have a crowbar?" he asked.

She pointed to a toolbox on the curb.

Dale walked to the toolbox. He picked up the crowbar. It was heavy. Cold.

He walked back to the door.

He didn't try to spray it. He didn't try to seal it.

He jammed the crowbar into the gap between the plywood and the frame.

He pried.

The wood splintered.

The door creaked open.

Darkness spilled out again. But this time, it was accompanied by the smell of copper and rot.

A hand reached out. It was pale. The fingers were too long. Too many joints.

Dale stared at the hand.

It wasn't an infestation.

It was a mutation.

And for the first time in his life, Dale Kowalski didn't know which protocol to apply.

He looked at the hand. He looked at his empty Field Guide.

He looked at the darkness behind the door, where something vast and hungry was waiting for him to make a mistake.

He tightened his grip on the crowbar.

"Show me your license," the darkness whispered.
