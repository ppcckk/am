Chapter 315: Scheduled for Deletion

# Chapter 315

The printer's hum vibrated through the soles of Dale's boots. It wasn't a mechanical whine, but a subsonic thrum that rattled his teeth.

He didn't stop at the school doors.

He bypassed the locked main entrance and kicked in the side door leading to the custodial closet. The lock shattered. Dust plumed into the air, gray and thick.

Dale didn't cough, and he pulled a respirator from his belt. The straps snapped tight against his skull.

This was muscle memory.

Panic was an unlicensed variable. Procedure was constant.

He moved down the hallway. The linoleum floor was slick with a substance that looked like toner but smelled like ozone and burnt hair. His boots left no footprints. The slurry repelled him, sliding his weight sideways before he corrected.

Page 402 pulsed in his mind. *Map of the county. Future timestamps.*

The Authority wasn't predicting the future. They were scheduling it.

He reached the cafeteria. The tables were overturned.

Not from violence, but from arrangement, and they were stacked in a precise, geometric spiral that led toward the center of the room.

In the middle stood a machine that defied the building's architecture. It was a massive, blocky unit, black plastic and chrome, towering twelve feet high.

Paper trails spilled from its output tray, piling up in the shape of a human torso.

The paper figures twitched.

Dale stopped. He raised his clipboard.

The pen hovered over the first line. *Subject: Dimensional Paperwork Construct.

Class: Unknown. Size: Oversized.*

He had to identify it. Without identification, there was no protocol. Without protocol, he was just a man standing in a room with a haunted photocopier.

The nearest paper figure lunged. It didn't run; it slid across the floor with a dry, rasping sound. Its edges were sharp. A corner of its shoulder clipped Dale's forearm.

Fabric tore. Skin split.

Dale didn't flinch. He noted the bleed. *Laceration, superficial. Contamination risk: Low.*

He sidestepped.

The second figure approached from the left. It moved in a perfect arc, mirroring the first.

They weren't attacking randomly, and they were following a grid.

*Wait.*

Dale froze. He looked at the stack of papers on the floor.

The pages weren't blank, and they were filled with dense, microscopic text.

He squinted.

The text was his own inspection reports.

Every violation code he had written in the last month. Every entry for *Rattus profundus*. Every note on *Chitinous Infestation, Grade 3*. The creature was printing his own data.

The mimicry wasn't visual. It was bureaucratic.

The printer emitted a sharp *ding*. A new sheet slid out. It unfolded itself in mid-air, stiffening into a four-armed humanoid. The face was a smudge of ink, resembling Dale's own features distorted by a fax machine's poor resolution.

"Violation," the construct rasped. Its voice was the sound of paper tearing. "Improper filing."

Dale backed away. His heart hammered against his ribs, but his hands remained steady. He flipped to the Field Guide.

He needed a weakness. He needed a rule.

The Authority's system rewarded thoroughness. But what was the protocol for a creature made of documentation?

He scanned the new entry forming on page 403. The ink bled fast, racing to fill the box.

**Entry: Archival Mimic (Paper-Based Variant)** **Weakness:** Redundancy.

This entity exists to enforce single-file accuracy. It cannot process conflicting data. **Protocol:** Introduce contradictory documentation. Force a logic loop.

Cause system crash.

Dale looked at the construct. It was advancing, arms raised like claws.

Contradictory data, and he didn't have a second file. He only had the truth.

Unless he lied.

The construct lunged. Dale ducked under the sweeping arm.

The paper edge sliced the air where his neck had been.

He scrambled to his feet, sliding over the toner-slick floor. He needed to get to the main control panel of the printer. It was mounted on the machine's chest, a small keypad glowing red.

He couldn't hack it. He didn't have the code.

But he had a clipboard. And he had his pen.

The construct paused. It tilted its head. The ink-smudge eyes seemed to focus on the clipboard.

"Report," the construct said. "Submit your report."

It wasn't asking. It was demanding. If he didn't comply, it would attack. If he did, it would process him.

Dale uncapped his pen. He clicked the barrel. *Click-click.*

He held up the clipboard. He wrote, in large, block letters, *K-9: Structural Compromise.*

He held it out.

The construct froze. It reached out, taking the clipboard. Its paper fingers wrapped around the plastic. It scanned the text.

"Error," the construct buzzed. "Code K-9 does not match Zone 4 parameters.

Zone 4 is Class-1 Vermin.

K-9 is Structural.

Incompatible."

The construct dropped the clipboard. It convulsed. The paper arms folded in on themselves. A low groan emanated from the machine.

It wasn't enough. The error was too small. It needed a bigger contradiction.

Dale grabbed the clipboard back. He crossed out the K-9. He wrote, *K-1: Non-Hazardous.*

He thrust it forward again.

The construct screamed. A sound like a jammed feeder. It lashed out, knocking Dale backward into a stack of chairs.

His head hit the metal frame. Dizziness washed over him. He tasted copper.

He stayed on the ground. He watched the construct. It was vibrating. The paper body was unraveling at the seams.

"Non-Hazardous," the construct repeated. "You are a threat. You are a violation. You are..."

It glitched. The face dissolved into static.

Dale realized the loophole. The Archival Mimic enforced the rules of the Authority. But the Authority's rules were binary.

A thing was either compliant or non-compliant. By labeling himself *Non-Hazardous*, he was claiming to be outside the scope of the pest control mandate.

He was declaring that he didn't need to be managed.

But the Authority *always* managed things, and the contradiction was in the classification. He was a certified technician. By definition, he was part of the system. To call him non-hazardous was a lie within the system's own framework.

The construct couldn't accept the lie because it violated the integrity of the data.

But it also couldn't accept Dale because he was a threat.

The logic loop was forming. The air around the printer warped. Heat radiated from the machine. The toner slurry on the floor boiled.

Dale scrambled up. He pulled his spray can from his belt. The label read *Insecticide, Broad Spectrum, Class II*.

It was useless against paper. He knew that. The Field Guide had a warning: *Do not use solvent-based protocols on cellulose constructs. Risk of ignition.*

Ignition, and he didn't want to burn the school. But the construct was growing. The paper torsos on the floor were standing up.

Three of them.

Then five. The printer was feeding them more ink.

He had to escalate.

Dale uncapped the spray. He aimed not at the construct, but at the ventilation grate above it.

He sprayed a fine mist into the intake. The chemical odor hit the air, sharp and acrid.

He then turned back to the construct. He held up his clipboard. He wrote one word, huge, filling the page.

*VOID.*

He held it up.

"Void," the construct said. "Null set. Invalid entry."

The machine shuddered. The vents above the printer sucked in the insecticide. The chemical reacted with the hot toner dust.

A spark jumped.

The construct shrieked.

It raised its arms to protect the intake, but it was too late. The paper caught fire. Not with a large flame, but with a slow, smoldering curl.

The edges blackened. The heat rose rapidly.

Dale backed away. He shielded his face with the clipboard.

The fire spread. The paper figures on the floor caught light from the main machine. They didn't burn quickly; they disintegrated into ash and gray flakes. The printer itself melted, the plastic casing bubbling and running.

The hum stopped. The silence was absolute.

Dale stood in the center of the cafeteria.

Ash fell like snow.

His respirator filter was clogged. He pulled it down.

He coughed, hacking up black phlegm, and he looked at his Field Guide.

**Entry: Archival Mimic** **Status: Eliminated.** **Thoroughness Rating: 6.4** **Penalty: Collateral Damage. Structural integrity of facility compromised. Fire hazard created. Deduction applied.**

6.4. A failing grade for this kind of job. Sloppy work.

He needed better. But he had survived.

He walked toward the exit. His legs felt heavy. The adrenaline was fading, leaving behind a cold, shaking tremor in his hands.

He pushed through the side door. The night air was cool. The stars were still wrong. They hung in a grid pattern, watching.

His phone buzzed, and he didn't look at it. He walked to his truck.

The engine was dead.

He'd tried to start it earlier. It was sputtering.

He sat in the driver's seat, and he opened the glove compartment. He pulled out his backup field kit.

Fresh clips.

New pens. A new respirator.

He checked the mirror. His face was smeared with ash. His eyes were red-rimmed.

The phone buzzed again.

Dale picked it up. The screen was dark, except for the notification.

**Notification: Infestation Escalation Detected.** **Location: County Courthouse, Basement Level.** **Threat Level: Class-4.** **New Mechanic Unlocked: Jurisdictional Overlap.** **Warning: Local Authority is now compromised. External entities are filing appeals against your certifications.**

Dale stared at the text.

*External entities.*

Who was appealing? The monsters? Or the people who worked for the Authority?

He looked out the window. Across the street, the lights of the courthouse were flickering.

Not just the bulbs.

The windows themselves seemed to be rearranging, and the glass was shifting, forming new patterns.

A figure stood on the courthouse steps. It was tall, wearing a suit that fit too perfectly. It held a clipboard. A golden clipboard.

The figure raised a hand. It pointed at Dale.

Dale didn't recognize the creature. The Field Guide didn't have an entry for it.

The figure shouted across the street. The voice carried, clear and loud.

"Mr. Kowalski! Your license is under review for excessive force and unauthorized modification of local infrastructure! Please surrender your credentials for audit!"

Dale gripped the steering wheel. His knuckles turned white.

The Authority wasn't just sending copies of him to fight monsters.

They were sending inspectors to shut him down.

And the thing on the steps wasn't a monster. It was a bureaucrat.

Dale started the truck. It caught on the second try. He put it in reverse.

He didn't go to the courthouse, and he drove toward the county line.

If the local jurisdiction was compromised, he had to go outside of it. He had to find a place where the Authority's map didn't end.

He drove fast. The stars shifted as he moved, forming new grids.

His phone buzzed. A text from the unknown number.

*They are right. You broke the protocol. Run.*

Dale typed back.

*Protocol for what?*

The reply came instantly.

*Protocol for survival.*

The road ahead split. To the left, the town. To the right, the woods.

The woods were dark. No streetlights.

No Authority markers.

Dale turned right.

The trees swallowed his headlights. The darkness was absolute.

He drove blindly, watching the rearview mirror. The courthouse lights grew distant.

Then, he saw it.

In the side mirror, reflected in the dark glass, was not his own face, which was the face of the golden-suit figure. It was sitting in the back seat.

Dale slammed the brakes. The truck skidded, tires screaming on the asphalt. He spun around, reaching for his spray can.

The back seat was empty.

But the seat was warm. And on the headrest, written in condensation, was a violation code.

*K-7: Structural Entry Point.*

The same code he had written in Chapter 1.

The same code he had used to identify the rat in the walls.

The Authority wasn't just auditing him, which was rewriting his history.

Dale opened the door. He fell out onto the road. The truck rolled forward, empty, into the trees.

He scrambled up. He looked at the woods.

Something was rustling in the underbrush. Not rats. Not beetles.

It was the sound of pages turning.

Thousands of them.

Dale pulled his clipboard from his belt. His hands shook. He uncapped his pen.

He didn't know what he was fighting. But he knew one thing.

He had to document it.

Before it documented him, and he stepped into the woods. The darkness closed in.
