Chapter 234: No Answer

# Chapter 234

The wrench was still wet.

Not with blood. With hydraulic fluid and the residue of a corrupted Authority agent.

Dale wiped it on his jumpsuit.

The fabric tore. He didn't care.

**SYSTEM NOTIFICATION: UNLICENSED INFESTATION NEUTRALIZED (TEMPORARY).** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 1/10.** **STATUS: CRITICAL. LOCAL NETWORK COMPROMISED.** **GLOBAL LOCKDOWN TIMER: 09:42 MINUTES.**

The text hovered in the air, translucent and red.

It pulsed in time with the geometric barcode scanning the sky. Dale blinked it away.

He couldn't afford the eye strain.

Not with 500 points still owed to the system.

Around him, Linda stood frozen. Her eyes remained white, but the head tilt had stopped. A thin stream of blue fluid leaked from her ear canal. It sizzled when it hit the pavement.

Dale checked his clipboard. The glass was shattered, spiderwebbed across the surface.

But the internal memory chip was intact.

He pulled it out with his teeth.

It was small, silver, and cold.

He shoved it into his breast pocket.

The other technicians didn't move. They were waiting for orders. Linda was waiting for orders.

The Authority was busy uploading itself into the stratosphere, leaving the ground-level handlers in a state of suspension. It was a buffer error.

A system hang.

Dale knew buffer errors. He'd seen them on every outdated cash register he'd ever tried to fix. The system didn't crash; it just stopped responding to input while it finished the last transaction.

He needed to finish the transaction.

He needed to document this.

"Listen," Dale said. His voice was flat. "I'm filing a code violation."

No one answered. A man in a high-visibility vest stood three feet away. His arms were at his sides. He didn't breathe.

Dale raised the wrench. He tapped it against the man's chest plate. *Clang.*

"Response required," Dale said. "Code K-9: Unauthorized Automation."

The man twitched. His eyes flickered. For a second, brown iris returned.

Then the white returned.

He slumped forward.

Dale caught him.

He lowered the body gently. Then he reached into the man's belt and pulled out a standard-issue Authority ID badge. It was magnetic.

It hummed against Dale's palm.

He looked at the badge. Then he looked at his own.

His badge read: **DALE KOWALSKI. CLASS II. SECTOR 4. ACTIVE.**

The dead man's badge read: **LINDA CHEN. CLASS I. SECTOR 2. SUSPENDED.**

That was the loophole.

The Authority recognized status.

It didn't recognize personality.

It recognized file numbers. If Linda was suspended, she was no longer a threat.

She was just clutter. Debris.

Dale had just de-authorized a Class I agent. The system had registered it as a pest removal.

He needed a witness.

He scanned the street. The other technicians were still frozen.

They were waiting for the upload to complete.

Once it did, they would wake up. They would remember the attack. They would hunt him.

He had nine minutes to write the report.

He ran back toward the service tunnel entrance. The water was still rising. The Gary-things were gone, dissolved by the flood, but the smell of ozone and rotting meat lingered.

Inside the tunnel, the emergency lights flickered.

The water was ankle-deep. Dale stepped through it, ignoring the chill.

He walked to the main junction box.

It was a heavy steel cabinet, bolted to the concrete wall.

He pried it open with his wrench. Inside, rows of fiber-optic cables pulsed with blue light. The upload stream.

He didn't cut the cables. Cutting them would trigger a local alarm. It would bring the automated drones down on him in seconds.

Instead, he looked for the maintenance port.

There. A small, rectangular slot labeled **DIAGNOSTIC INPUT**.

He pulled the memory chip from his pocket. He jammed it into the port.

**SYSTEM ERROR: INVALID MEDIA.** **PLEASE INSERT AUTHORITY-CERTIFIED DATA STORAGE.**

Dale gritted his teeth. He pulled the chip out. He wiped it on his sleeve. He tried again.

**SYSTEM ERROR: INVALID MEDIA.**

It wasn't working. The chip was from his clipboard, not a certified Authority drive. The system rejected it.

He looked at the water rising around his ankles. It was up to his shins.

He looked at the badges in his hand. Linda's. The other man's.

He looked at the diagnostic port.

A thought occurred. A dangerous, stupid thought.

The Authority's rules were rigid. Identification.

Documentation.

Isolation.

Elimination. Verification.

But the Authority also had a hierarchy. A Class II technician outranked a Class I in terms of data clearance.

Dale wasn't a Class II technician anymore. Not locally. The local node was compromised. But his *account* was still valid in the central database.

He reached into his pocket and pulled out his own badge.

He held it over the diagnostic port.

**SYSTEM DETECTS: CLASS II CREDENTIALS.** **WARNING: LOCAL NETWORK INTEGRITY COMPROMISED.** **DO YOU WISH TO INITIATE EMERGENCY AUDIT?** **Y/N**

Yes.

Dale pressed his thumb against the sensor.

The screen on the junction box changed. The blue pulse stopped. The text turned white.

**AUDIT INITIATED.** **USER: KOWALSKI, D.** **ACTION: FLAG LOCAL NODE FOR RE-REVIEW.** **NOTE: EXCESSIVE UNLICENSED ACTIVITY DETECTED.**

The upload beam outside stuttered.

Above the city, the barcode in the sky flickered. The scanning line slowed.

Dale had paused the upload.

He hadn't stopped it. He had just put it in hold. Like a file pending review.

The technicians outside waked up. But they were confused. They were looking at their hands.

They were checking their badges. They were wondering why their mission clock had paused.

Dale had bought himself time.

But he hadn't filed the report.

He still needed to document the infestation. If he didn't, the points wouldn't count. And if the points didn't count, he couldn't buy the next level.

He needed a new infestation.

He looked at the water.

The Gary-things were dead. But the Rift was still open. The beam was still shining.

Something else was coming through.

Dale felt it before he heard it. A vibration in the floor. Low frequency. Subsonic.

The water rippled.

Not from rain.

From below.

A shape rose from the flooded tunnel. It wasn't a rat. It wasn't a beetle.

It was a mass of black sludge. It had no distinct form. It poured out of the cracks in the concrete, spreading across the water like oil. It had hundreds of tiny, jagged mouths.

**UNLICENSED INFESTATION DETECTED.** **SPECIES: UNKNOWN.** **THREAT LEVEL: III.**

Dale backed up. He bumped into the junction box.

The sludge oozed toward him. It absorbed the water. It grew larger. It formed tendrils.

It was trying to connect to the network.

It was a data parasite.

It was eating the digital signal.

Dale looked at his Field Guide. He opened it with a thought.

**FIELD GUIDE:** **1. Rattus profundus (Documented).** **2. Unknown Entity (In Progress).**

He didn't have a protocol for this. He had no spray. No sealant. No wrench that could hit a liquid.

But he had a classification.

The Authority rewarded identification.

If he could name it, he could damage it.

Dale stared at the sludge. It pulsed with the same blue light as the upload stream. It was made of code. It was made of the Authority's own data, corrupted by the Rift.

It wasn't a monster. It was a bug.

A literal software bug, given physical form by the dimensional breach.

"Code B-7," Dale said. "Memory leak."

The sludge recoiled.

It shrank. For a second, it looked confused.

Dale's heart hammered against his ribs. He didn't move. He waited.

The sludge expanded again.

It reached for the junction box. It touched the exposed fiber optics. The blue light flared.

The sludge screamed.

A sound like grinding metal.

It was trying to infect the system.

Dale realized what he had to do.

He couldn't kill it. You don't kill a bug. You patch it.

He looked at the diagnostic port. The audit was still running. The system was waiting for a verdict.

If he classified the sludge as a "Corrupted Data Packet," the system would quarantine it.

But he needed to document it first.

He pulled out a marker. He had one left. Black ink.

He wrote on his palm.

**SPECIES: DIGITAL PARASITE.** **CLASS: B-7.** **CONTAINMENT: QUARANTINE PROTOCOL 4.**

He held his hand up.

The sludge paused. It seemed to read the writing. Or maybe it read the intent.

The Authority's algorithm was listening.

**SYSTEM QUERY: CLASSIFICATION ACCEPTED?** **Y/N**

Dale pressed his palm against the cold metal of the junction box.

Yes.

**CLASSIFICATION ACCEPTED.** **APPLYING QUARANTINE PROTOCOL 4.**

The air around the sludge turned gray.

A barrier of static electricity snapped into existence around the mass. The sludge thrashed against it. It couldn't cross.

It couldn't touch the cables.

It was contained.

**THOROUGHNESS RATING: 6/10.** **POINTS AWARDED: 50.** **TOTAL POINTS: 220.**

Dale slumped against the wall. The water was up to his knees. The timer in his vision ticked down.

**07:15 MINUTES TO GLOBAL LOCKDOWN.**

He wasn't done.

The quarantine was temporary. The sludge was still growing. It was feeding on the ambient data in the air.

And there were more of them.

Dale heard it. A wet tearing sound from the shadows behind him.

He turned.

Three more masses of black sludge poured out of a drainage grate. They were smaller. Faster.

They didn't pause. They didn't wait for classification.

They lunged.

Dale raised his wrench. He swung at the first one. The wrench passed through the sludge. It splashed against the wall.

Ineffective.

He needed a physical medium. Something to bind it.

He looked at his belt. He had duct tape. He always carried duct tape.

It wasn't enough.

He looked at the junction box. The audit was still running. The system was holding the first sludge in place.

But the system was also waiting for the next input.

Dale ran toward the second sludge mass. It was faster than the first. It hit him in the chest.

Cold. Wet.

It burned. Not like fire. Like ice. Like static shock.

Dale screamed. He kicked it away. It stuck to his leg. It crawled up his thigh.

**SYSTEM ALERT: CONTAMINATION DETECTED.** **DEBUFF: 40% MOVEMENT SPEED.** **FIELD GUIDE ENTRY CORRUPTED FOR 24 HOURS.**

He couldn't use the identification trick. Not with this one. It was too fast. It was already inside his personal space.

He needed to isolate it.

He grabbed his sealant canister. He sprayed the sludge on his leg. The chemical hiss of the sealant met the organic wetness of the parasite.

The sludge hardened. It turned into a black, brittle shell.

Dale kicked it off. It shattered on the concrete.

He was breathing hard. His leg was numb.

He looked at the other two sludges. They were hovering near the junction box, trying to find a way into the audit.

They were waiting for a gap in the defense.

Dale looked at the timer.

**06:45 MINUTES.**

He looked at the audit status.

**AWAITING USER VERIFICATION.**

The system needed him to verify the audit. To sign off.

If he signed off, the audit would complete. The node would be locked. The upload would resume at full speed.

If he didn't sign off, the audit would time out. The quarantine would fail. The sludges would infect the node. The Authority would lose control of the sector.

Dale had to choose.

Lock the system, and the upload finishes. The barcode scans the city. The technicians wake up. They kill him.

Leave the audit open, and the sludges eat the network. The Authority collapses. The Rift widens.

He looked at the sludges. They were vibrating. They were hungry.

He looked at the memory chip in his pocket.

He had the data. He had the report.

But he couldn't file it here. The local node was compromised.

He needed to file it remotely.

He pulled out his phone. The screen was cracked. The signal bar was empty.

But the Authority didn't use cell towers. It used the network.

He opened his Field Guide app. He went to the "Report Submission" tab.

It was grayed out. **OFFLINE.**

He couldn't submit.

Unless...

He looked at the junction box. The audit was still open. The connection was live.

If he submitted the report *through* the audit channel, the system would think he was verifying the audit.

It was a lie. A procedural lie.

But the Authority loved paperwork.

Dale typed the report into his phone.

**INCIDENT: 234-ALPHA.** **SUBJECT: DIGITAL PARASITE INFESTATION.** **ACTION: QUARANTINE APPLIED. VERIFICATION PENDING.**

He held the phone up to the diagnostic port.

He didn't plug it in. He just held it there.

The port scanned the screen.

**DATA RECEIVED.** **IS THIS YOUR VERIFICATION?** **Y/N**

Dale's finger hovered over the screen.

If he said Y, the audit closed. The upload resumed.

If he said N, the audit stayed open. The sludges attack.

But there was a third option.

The system rules stated that verification required a *signature*.

Dale didn't have his Authority signature key.

But he had a biometric scanner on his phone.

He pressed his thumb to the phone's fingerprint sensor. Then he held the phone closer to the port.

He wasn't verifying the audit.

He was *countersigning* it.

He was adding a secondary note to the audit.

**SUBMISSION: ADDITIONAL INFESTATION REPORT.** **CLASSIFICATION: EXTERNAL.**

The port blinked.

**ERROR: COUNTERSIGNATURE INVALID. USER NOT AUTHORIZED FOR EXTERNAL REPORTS DURING AUDIT.**

Dale frowned.

He looked at the sludges. They were moving closer. They sensed his hesitation.

He looked at the timer.

**05:30 MINUTES.**

He looked at the technicians outside. They were starting to move. They were raising their weapons.

Dale made a decision.

He pulled the memory chip from his pocket.

He held it in his hand.

He threw it at the junction box.

It hit the edge of the port. It bounced off. It fell into the water.

The sludges surged. They swarmed toward the chip. They were drawn to the data.

They ignored Dale.

They went for the chip.

Dale smiled. A grim, tight expression.

He kicked the water. The splash hit the chip. The chip shorted out.

**SYSTEM ALERT: DATA LOSS DETECTED.** **AUDIT SUSPENDED DUE TO MEDIA FAILURE.** **REBOOTING LOCAL NODE.**

The blue light in the tunnel died.

The sludges collapsed. They had no source. They dissolved into harmless grey water.

The upload beam outside flickered. Then died.

Silence.

Dale stood in the dark water. The silence was absolute.

Then, a new notification appeared.

**SYSTEM NOTICE: LOCAL NODE REBOOTED.** **STATUS: ONLINE.** **USER: KOWALSKI, D.** **ACTION REQUIRED: RESUBMIT INCIDENT REPORT.**

He had rebooted the node. He had cleared the infestation.

But he had lost the data.

He had no proof.

He had no points.

He had 220 points.

He looked at the timer.

**04:55 MINUTES TO GLOBAL LOCKDOWN.**

The barcode in the sky glowed again. Brighter this time.

It wasn't scanning.

It was charging.

Dale looked at the nearest technician.

Linda was back.

Her eyes were brown. She was looking at him.

She held a gun.

She didn't look confused anymore.

She looked angry.

"You killed the upload," she said. Her voice was trembling.

Dale didn't answer. He looked at his wrench.

"I filed a report," he said.

Linda stepped forward. The gun shook in her hand.

"It's gone," she said. "You deleted the evidence. You're in violation of Protocol 12. You're stripped of your license."

Dale felt the notification appear.

**LICENSE REVOKED.** **CLASS II STATUS TERMINATED.** **YOU ARE NOW UNLICENSED.**

Dale looked at the sky. The barcode was pulsing faster.

He looked at Linda.

"I need 500 points," Dale said. "You have a Class I license. You can transfer points."

Linda stared at him.

"That's not how it works."

"It is," Dale said. "It's a transferable asset. Look it up."

Linda didn't look it up. She raised the gun.

Dale didn't move.

"You have three seconds to transfer 280 points to my account," Dale said. "Or I'll tell the Authority you were the one who corrupted the node."

Linda's finger tightened on the trigger.

The barcode in the sky flashed. A single, blinding beam of white light shot down.

It hit Linda.

She didn't scream. She just dissolved.

The light moved to Dale.

He raised his wrench.

The light stopped an inch from his face.

**SYSTEM QUERY: IDENTIFY.**

Dale looked at the light.

He knew what it wanted.

He opened his mouth.

"I am Dale Kowalski," he said. "Certified Exterminator."

The light vanished.

Dale fell to his knees. The water was up to his waist.

He looked at his wrist. A new timer appeared.

**CERTIFICATION EXAM: COMMENCING IN 60 SECONDS.** **TOPIC: DIMENSIONAL BREACH ETHICS.** **FAILING CONDITION: PUBLIC EXECUTION.**

The technicians were gathering. Dozens of them. They were circling him.

The exam had started.

And the first question was already in the air, written in blood-red light.

**QUESTION 1: DID YOU SAVE THE CITY, OR DID YOU SAVE YOURSELF?**

Dale looked at the wrench in his hand.

He looked at the bodies dissolving in the water.

He looked at the camera drones descending from the barcode.

He picked up a stone.

He threw it at the nearest drone.

**SUBMIT ANSWER?**

He didn't answer.

He ran.
