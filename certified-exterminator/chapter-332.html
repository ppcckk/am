Chapter 332: Welcome to the Mill

# Chapter 332

The conveyor belt shrieked, a sound like metal tearing through wet bone.

Dale didn't look at the noise. He looked at the timestamp.

11:59:42 PM.

Eighteen seconds.

The mill wasn't a mill anymore. It was a throat.

The smokestacks had inverted, spiraling down into a pit that defied gravity, spitting out a mist that smelled of ozone and rotting fruit.

Inside the main hall, the machinery had fused with the flesh of the previous subjects. Gears turned inside ribcages. Conveyor belts made of sinew hauled chunks of gray, wet matter toward a central incinerator.

Marcus slammed the brakes. The car fishtailed, spraying mud against the rusted gates.

"Dale," Marcus whispered. His knuckles were white on the steering wheel. "Dale, look at them."

They weren't dead. That was the problem.

The "failed attempts" from the previous chapters were still moving. They were stitched together, a patchwork quilt of limbs and torsos, dragged along the belt by mechanical arms. They were being fed into the grinder.

Dale unbuckled his seatbelt. The click echoed in the silent car.

"Don't," Marcus said.

"Protocol 4-B," Dale said, opening the door. "Immediate containment of active processing. We are interfering with an unauthorized disposal site."

"You're going to get us killed."

"I'm going to get us licensed."

Dale stepped out. The rain hit his face, cold and sharp. The beige stain on his arm pulsed again, a rhythmic throb that matched the grinding of the machine. He could feel the Field Guide updating in his mind, new entries scrolling past his peripheral vision.

*Subject Type: Composite Amalgam.* *Weakness: Structural integrity dependent on central nervous bridge.*

Dale walked toward the gate. The chain-link fence had been replaced by a wall of chitin. It was warm to the touch. It vibrated.

He pulled his clipboard from his back pocket. The paper was dry, protected by the waterproof seal of his professional pride. He checked the box marked *Inspection Initiated*.

A shape detached itself from the shadows near the incinerator, which was tall. Thin. Its head was a perfect sphere of polished bone. It wore a suit that had been tailored to fit its elongated limbs.

"Mr. Kowalski," the thing said. Its voice was the sound of a fax machine jamming. "You are late."

Dale didn't stop walking.

"I am on schedule. The shift ends at midnight. You are operating on overtime without proper break documentation."

The thing tilted its head. The bone sphere reflected Dale's flashlight beam.

"The Authority does not require breaks. The Authority requires results."

"Results with safety violations are voided," Dale said.

He reached into his belt pouch. He didn't pull out a weapon.

He pulled out a sealant gun.

"You're creating a biohazard. This is a Class-3 violation."

The thing laughed. It sounded like dry leaves skittering on pavement.

"We are recycling the waste. We are making new tools."

"You're making new monsters," Dale said. "And you're contaminating the local ecosystem."

He fired the sealant gun.

The blue foam hit the chitin wall. It expanded, hardening instantly into a rigid, chemical barrier. The wall groaned. Cracks spiderwebbed through the organic material.

The bone-headed thing hissed. It raised a hand. Three smaller amalgamations leaped from the darkness, their limbs ending in jagged spikes.

Dale didn't dodge. He stepped forward.

"Marcus," Dale said, not looking back. "Drive the car through the gap I made."

"Are you insane?"

"Do it."

The car engine roared. Headlights blinded the approaching creatures.

Dale checked his Field Guide.

*Amalgam Weakness: Central Nervous Bridge.*

He aimed the sealant gun at the chest of the leading creature. Not at its heart. At the spine. The place where the different bodies had been stitched together.

He fired.

The foam hit the seam. It expanded.

The creature shrieked as the chemical reaction tore the sutures apart.

Its left arm detached, flopping onto the ground.

Its head spun on a broken neck.

Dale walked past the twitching remains.

One of the spikes grazed his shoulder. Blood welled up, dark and quick. He ignored it. He was already looking at the next group.

There were more of them. Dozens. They were being herded by the bone-headed thing, which was now floating off the ground, its legs no longer touching the earth.

"Stop," Dale said. His voice was flat. Professional. "You are interfering with a certified technician's workflow."

The bone-headed thing smiled. The bone cracked.

"You have no jurisdiction here, Technician. This is the Authority's internal recycling facility. You are an obstruction."

"Obstruction of a licensed professional is a felony in this county," Dale said. He pulled out his second canister. *Protocol: Rapid Expansion Foam (Industrial Grade).* "And I'm filing a complaint."

He threw the canister.

It wasn't a grenade. It was a sample container.

The canister shattered against the bone-headed thing's chest. The foam exploded outward, not to hurt, but to fill. It filled the cracks in the bone sphere.

It filled the gaps in its suit. It filled the space between its joints.

The thing froze.

It tried to move, but the foam had hardened into a solid block of concrete-like material. It was trapped in its own anatomy.

Dale walked up to it. He pulled a marker from his pocket.

On the frozen forehead of the creature, he wrote: **EVIDENCE**.

Then he turned to the remaining amalgamations, and they hesitated.

They had never seen a technician act like this. They were used to heroes who shouted and charged. They were used to magic that burned.

They were not used to paperwork.

"Line up," Dale said.

They didn't line up. They lunged.

Dale sidestepped the first one. He kicked the second one in the knee. The joint snapped with a wet pop.

He was fighting with his hands, but his mind was on the clock.

11:59:52 PM.

Ten seconds.

Marcus's car smashed through the gap in the chitin wall. The headlights swept across the room, illuminating the horror. The grinding machine slowed. The gears jammed on the sealant foam Dale had injected earlier.

Dale jumped onto the hood of the car.

"Drive!" he yelled.

Marcus slammed the accelerator. The car lurched forward, crushing the frozen bone-headed thing under its tires.

Dale climbed into the passenger seat. His shirt was torn. His arm was bleeding. The beige stain was spreading, creeping up his neck.

"Did we win?" Marcus asked, his voice trembling.

Dale looked at the clock.

12:00:00 AM.

The appeal window closed.

The text appeared in Dale's vision. Red. Blazing.

**CERTIFICATION REVOKED.** **LICENSE SUSPENDED PENDING INVESTIGATION.** **SUBJECT 3: DALE KOWALSKI.** **STATUS: QUEUED FOR PROCESSING.**

Dale stared at the words. He didn't blink.

He pulled out his clipboard. The paper was still dry.

He wrote one line on the top of the form.

*Violation: Unauthorized Processing of Licensed Personnel.*

He handed the clipboard to Marcus.

"Keep driving," Dale said.

"We're at the end of the road," Marcus said. The road had ended. The mill was gone. In its place was a white void, a swirling vortex of light and static.

"The Authority doesn't process people at the mill," Dale said. "It processes them in the Cloud."

"The what?"

"The Cloud," Dale said. "It's the central server for all dimensional breaches. It's where the failed attempts go to be deleted."

The white void opened.

A hand reached out. It was made of light. It was massive. It covered the entire windshield.

Dale unbuckled his seatbelt again.

"I have one minute before the deletion sweep," Dale said. "I need you to listen to me carefully."

Marcus turned to look at him.

"Dale, what are you talking about?"

"The Field Guide," Dale said. "Did you read it?"

"No. I can't read that small print."

"Page 402," Dale said. "Subsection: *Cross-Contamination Loophole.*"

Marcus shook his head.

"Why?"

"Because if I'm processed," Dale said, "I become part of the system. And if I'm part of the system, I can't be fired."

The light hand grabbed the car.

The metal groaned. The glass shattered.

Dale grabbed Marcus's collar.

"Say it," Dale said. "Say: *I am a witness.*"

Marcus stared at him. Tears streamed down his face.

"I am a witness?"

"Say it!"

"**I AM A WITNESS!**"

The text in Dale's vision changed.

**WITNESS STATUS: CONFIRMED.** **SUBJECT 3: DALE KOWALSKI.** **NEW ROLE: INTERNAL AUDITOR.** **ACCESS GRANTED: LEVEL 4 CLEARANCE.**

The light hand stopped crushing the car. It held it suspended in the void.

Dale looked out the broken window. The white void wasn't empty.

It was full of files.

Millions of them.

Floating in the air were reports. Thousands of them. *Inspection Reports. Violation Notices. Termination Orders.*

And among the files, he saw faces.

Faces of other technicians. Other survivors. Other people who had been processed.

They were watching him.

One file drifted closer. It had his name on it.

**FILE: KOWALSKI, DALE.** **SUBJECT: TERMINATION PROTOCOL 7.** **STATUS: PENDING APPROVAL.**

Dale reached out and grabbed the file, and he didn't read it. He tore it in half.

The void screamed.

The light hand tightened. The car dissolved, pixel by pixel.

Marcus was screaming, but Dale couldn't hear him. The sound was gone. Replaced by a low, humming frequency that vibrated in Dale's teeth.

He looked at his arm. The beige stain was gone.

In its place was a barcode.

It glowed blue.

**ACCESS LEVEL: 4.** **PERMISSIONS: EDIT FILE. DELETE ENTRY. AUTHORIZE NEW HIRE.**

Dale looked at the other files, and he didn't just want to survive. He wanted to clean up the mess.

He picked up the pen.

He wrote on the first file that drifted close enough to touch.

**REJECTED.**

The file vanished, and he wrote on the next.

**REJECTED.**

Another vanished.

The void pulled at him, trying to drag him into the center of the vortex, into the core of the Authority.

Dale resisted. He planted his feet on the dashboard. He leaned against the dissolving door.

He wrote faster.

**REJECTED.** **REJECTED.** **REJECTED.**

The humming grew louder. The barcode on his arm burned.

He was editing reality, and he was deleting the Authority's work.

And then, a new file appeared. It wasn't floating. It was being pushed toward him by the light hand.

It was thick. Heavy. Bound in black leather.

**FILE: THE ORIGINAL BREACH.** **SUBJECT: ZERO.** **STATUS: CLASSIFIED.**

Dale reached for it.

The light hand pulled the file back.

Dale grabbed the hand.

His fingers sank into the light. It felt like touching ice. It felt like touching nothing.

He pulled.

The hand resisted.

The car dissolved beneath him.

Marcus was gone.

Dale was floating in the void, holding onto the light hand, holding onto the black file.

The text appeared in his vision.

**ERROR: AUDITOR EXCEEDS CLEARANCE.** **ATTEMPTING RESET.**

The barcode on his arm flickered.

It turned red.

**CLEARANCE REVOKED.** **SUBJECT 3: TERMINATE.**

Dale looked at the black file, and he looked at the void.

He looked at the red text.

He smiled.

It was a small, dry smile. The kind a man gives when he finds a cockroach in a sterile kitchen.

He opened the file.

The void stopped humming, and the light hand went limp.

Everything went dark.

Then, a single word appeared in the darkness, bright and white, burning into his retina.

**WELCOME.**
