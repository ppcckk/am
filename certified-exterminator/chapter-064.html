Chapter 64: Correction Filing

# Chapter 64

The documentation specialist's boots made a clean click on the concrete as she stepped closer. Her clipboard was out. Her pen was out. Her face was the face of a woman who had done this before and did not enjoy it.

Dale couldn't move his arms. The foam had locked his elbows to his ribs, his wrists to his hips.

His fingers were free.

His head was free. His mouth was free.

He used the mouth.

"Stop."

She stopped.

Not because of the word. Because of the tone.

It was the tone he used on property managers who wanted to know why the treatment cost what it cost.

Flat. Procedural. The voice of a man reading a regulation aloud.

"Your classification is wrong," Dale said.

The documentation specialist tilted her head. "It's not my classification. It's the Authority's."

"The Authority's classification is wrong. I want to file a correction."

"You're frozen in a containment foam with a Class-7 designation. You don't get to file corrections."

"I do." Dale swallowed. His throat was dry.

"Section nine, subsection four of the Licensing Authority operational manual.

Active Monitoring Stations are exempt from Source Extermination protocols, and the distinction is between passive transmission and active documentation. A vector spreads without generating new data.

A monitoring station generates new data continuously. I am generating new data. My Field Guide has three entries added in the last forty-eight hours that predate the mimic's harvest window by six to eight hours.

I can prove it."

The documentation specialist's pen stopped moving.

"Which entries?" she said.

"Entry forty-one.

Entry forty-four. Entry forty-seven. " Dale rattled them off. " Entry forty-one is the acid-spitting variant of the subterranean isopod I documented in the parking structure on Ninth Street.

Entry forty-four is the winged larval stage I found in the storm drain.

Entry forty-seven is the hybrid I just documented twenty minutes ago in this building's mechanical room. None of these existed when the mimic accessed my guide.

I can verify by timestamp.

Your system has timestamps."

She checked. Her eyes flicked down to the clipboard. Her pen tapped twice against the paper.

"They're there," she said.

"Then I'm not a vector. I'm a monitoring station.

And you don't exterminate monitoring stations.

You maintain them.

You calibrate them. You feed them." Dale paused.

The foam was tightening around his chest. He could feel his ribs compressing by fractions of a millimeter. "Your own manual says so."

The documentation specialist was quiet for four seconds. Five. She looked at the sprayer guy, who had thawed to the knees and was staring at Dale with an expression caught between relief and nausea.

She looked back at Dale.

"If I reclassify you," she said, "the cascade initiates."

"What cascade?"

"Self-Referential Contamination Protocol. When a monitoring station's documentation has been compromised, every entry in the Field Guide is flagged for re-verification. Forty-eight hours. You re-verify or the entries purge."

Dale's stomach tightened. Not from the foam. "Purge means what?"

"Purge means the Authority no longer recognizes the extermination as valid. The nests reopen. The sealed entry points revert to unsealed status. Everything you treated in the last four months goes back to untreated."

The number hit him like a physical blow. Four months.

Forty-three entries.

Forty-three nests he had identified, isolated, eliminated, and verified. Forty-three Thoroughness ratings averaging 9.2. All of it.

Gone.

And not just gone. Reversed.

Every creature he had killed would, by the Authority's own logic, no longer be confirmed dead.

Their nests would reclassify as active infestations.

"How many nests are within the county boundary?" Dale said.

"Twenty-nine."

"Twenty-nine active infestations. Reopened."

"Yes."

Dale closed his eyes. Opened them.

The foam was still setting, and the sprayer guy was still thawing.

The documentation specialist was still standing three feet away with her pen poised over the reclassification line.

"Do it," Dale said.

She did it.

The notification in his peripheral vision flickered. The Class-7 Vector designation dissolved. A new line appeared.

RECLASSIFIED: Active Monitoring Station (Contaminated). Self-Referential Contamination Protocol initiated. Re-verification window: 48:00:00. Current entries pending: 43.

A timer appeared in the corner of his vision. Forty-eight hours, counting down.

The foam released. Dale's arms dropped to his sides. His hands shook.

He flexed his fingers, rolled his shoulders, checked his knees. Everything intact.

He picked up his clipboard from where he'd set it before the foam hit.

He checked his pen. Still worked.

"Sprayer guy," Dale said.

The man looked up. His face was gray. "Yeah?"

"Your boots. You still have the residue from the mechanical room on your boots?"

"Yeah. I didn't rinse."

"Good.

Don't rinse. That's cross-contamination evidence.

I need you to walk to the east stairwell and stand there for ten minutes.

Don't touch anything. Don't speak to anyone. I'm documenting your contact trail as part of my re-verification for entry forty-seven."

The sprayer guy blinked. "You're what?"

"Documenting your contact trail. The Authority needs a chain-of-custody for the hybrid entry.

If I can't prove the specimen was isolated and not cross-contaminated, the entry fails re-verification.

If the entry fails, the hybrid's nest reopens.

You don't want the hybrid's nest reopening. It's in this building's HVAC system."

The sprayer guy walked to the east stairwell. He did not ask any more questions.

Dale turned to the documentation specialist. "I need the re-verification forms. The standard ones."

She handed them over. Four pages. Dense.

Technical. Each one had a field for species identification, a field for nest location, a field for treatment method, and a field for verification status.

The verification status field had three checkboxes: Confirmed Exterminated, Partially Confirmed, and Unverified.

Dale sat down on the concrete floor. He uncapped his pen. He pulled his Field Guide from his jacket pocket.

The leather was soft from handling, and the pages were dense with his handwriting. Forty-three entries.

Forty-three creatures he had identified, treated, and sealed.

He started with Entry 001.

Rattus profundus. Dimensional Vermin. Broodmother.

Crawlspace, 4417 Maple Street. Treatment: containment and elimination via improvised barrier protocol.

Verification: Confirmed.

Nest sealed.

Entry point closed.

Dale read it. He remembered it. He remembered the crawlspace.

The smell. The broodmother had folded itself into the insulation like a wet towel.

He remembered the trash bag.

The duct tape. His hands had shaken while he sealed the seam.

He marked the verification field: Confirmed Exterminated.

The Authority's response was immediate. A notification.

Entry 001: Re-verification accepted. Nest status: Confirmed sealed.

Dale moved to Entry 002.

Entry 002: Acid-spitting isopod variant. Storm drain, Ninth Street commercial district. Treatment: bait station deployment and nest collapse. Verification: Confirmed.

Accepted.

Entry 003. Accepted.

Entry 004. Accepted.

Entry 005. Accepted.

Entry 006. Accepted.

Entry 007.

Dale stopped.

Rattus profundus.

Dimensional Vermin. Adult male, territorial variant.

Basement, 4417 Maple Street.

Treatment: elimination via targeted spray protocol. Verification: Confirmed.

He read it again. He read it a third time.

He remembered this one.

The adult male, and the one that had come back after the broodmother was sealed. The one that had torn through the drywall in the kitchen.

The one he had cornered behind the water heater and sprayed with a can of pyrethrin that was not rated for dimensional vermin but which he had used because it was what he had.

He remembered the spray. He remembered the rat convulsing. He remembered the body. He remembered writing the report.

He marked the verification field: Confirmed Exterminated.

The Authority's response came. But it was different.

The notification text was different, and the color was different. It was amber.

Entry 007: Re-verification FAILED.

Cross-reference indicates nest activity within sealed perimeter. Status: REOPENED.

Location: 4417 Maple Street, Basement.

Distance from current position: 0.4 miles.

Dale stared at the notification, and the rat was back.

No. Not back.

It had never been gone.

His verification had been wrong, and he had confirmed a kill that hadn't been a kill. The Authority was catching his error.

His own error. The one he had made in Chapter Seven, when he was new, when he was still learning, when he had sprayed a rat and assumed it was dead because it stopped moving.

It hadn't stopped moving. It had been playing dead.

And it had been in that basement for four months. Building.

Breeding.

Feeding on the same dimensional energy that the Rift provided. Growing.

Dale looked at the timer. Forty-seven hours, fifty-one minutes. Forty-two entries remaining.

He looked at the notification again. The distance. 0.4 miles. Four blocks. He could walk it in six minutes.

He looked at his Field Guide.

Entry 007 was still there. Still readable.

But the weakness field had changed.

Where he had written "pyrethrin-sensitive, abdominal membrane," the text now read: "Unknown. Data contaminated by mimic network. Re-identification required."

He couldn't use his old data. The mimic had touched it.

The weakness he had documented was no longer reliable.

The rat might not be pyrethrin-sensitive anymore.

It might have adapted. It might have been bred to be resistant.

The mimic might have edited his notes before harvesting them.

Dale stood up. He put the clipboard in his jacket.

He pulled his sprayer from the mechanical room where he'd left it.

He checked the tank. Half full. Pyrethrin.

Useless if the weakness had changed, and he walked outside. The night air was cold.

The street was empty.

Four blocks south, the duplex at 4417 Maple Street sat dark, and the kitchen window was still boarded from the first infestation. The crawlspace vent was still sealed with the duct tape he had applied fourteen years of professional habit had taught him to use.

He stood on the sidewalk. He looked at the boarded window.

He looked at his Field Guide.

Entry 007.

Weakness field: Unknown, and he had to re-identify the creature. Face to face. Without his notes.

Without his old data. Without knowing if his spray would work.

Without knowing if the rat had grown, changed, adapted, or been replaced by something the mimic had built from his own documentation.

He had forty-seven hours to re-verify forty-two entries. And the first failure was standing four blocks away, behind a boarded window, in a basement he had once treated and sealed and confirmed dead.

Dale walked toward the duplex. He pulled out his flashlight. He pulled out his clipboard. He uncapped his pen.

He had no idea what was in that basement. He had never seen it before. Not the thing that was in there now.

Because the mimic had built it from his data.

Something that looked like his work but wasn't. Something that knew his protocols because he had written them.

He reached the front door. It was unlocked. It had been unlocked for four months because he had sealed the crawlspace and the basement entry and called the job complete.

He pushed the door open, and the kitchen was dark.

The boarded window let in a strip of streetlight. The floor was clean. Too clean.

The kind of clean that meant something had been eating the debris.

Dale crouched, and he put his hand on the floor.

He felt the vibration before he heard the sound. A low, rhythmic thumping.

Like a heart. Like a large heart.

It was coming from the basement, and he stood up. He looked at the basement door.

It was closed.

He had sealed it. He remembered sealing it. He remembered the tape.

The tape was gone.

The door handle turned.
