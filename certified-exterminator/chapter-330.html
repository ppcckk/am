Chapter 330: Beige Infection

# Chapter 330

The ink smudged before it dried. Dale pressed the ballpoint harder against the clipboard, forcing the nib to bite into the paper, but the fiber of the nest was wet and yielding, resisting the pressure like bruised fruit. Above him, the Auditor's red pen traced a circle in the void, the sound like a dry branch snapping.

"SUBMIT," the sky-voice boomed, vibrating in Dale's molars.

Dale did not submit. He checked his watch. 14:02. The standard processing window for a Class IV administrative anomaly ended at 14:15. He had thirteen minutes to finalize the citation, or the Authority would classify this as an unauthorized dimensional breach, a violation that carried a permanent suspension of his license.

He couldn't fight the Auditor. The entity wasn't a monster; it was a function of the System, a manifestation of bureaucratic inertia given flesh and hunger. Fighting it was like trying to stab a subpoena. You had to file it out of existence.

The nest pulsed. Marcus Thorne, suspended in the center of the paper cocoon, twitched.

His translucent skin rippled as the fused invoices tightened around his ribs.

The pages were not just paper; they were compressed data, memories of every tax form, permit application, and noise complaint Dale had ever filed in the county. The System was digesting Dale's professional history, using it to construct a prison.

"Dale!" Marcus choked, the sound wet and gurgling. "Don't write! If you write, it becomes real!"

"Silence," Dale said, not looking up. "You are interfering with a public works inspection."

He turned his back on the hanging man and faced the wall of the mill. The rusted looms stood in silent rows, their spindles wrapped in thick, fibrous strings of redacted text. Dale raised his clipboard. He needed to identify the structural failure.

The Auditor hovered closer. Its face, a smooth, featureless mask of beige plastic, tilted. The red pen dipped toward Dale's shoulder.

A notification flashed in the corner of Dale's vision, bright and urgent.

**UNAUTHORIZED ACTION DETECTED.** **PENALTY: 50% LOSS OF FIELD GUIDE INTEGRITY.** **TIME REMAINING: 00:11:42.**

Dale ignored the warning, and he tapped his pen against the clipboard, a rhythmic, hollow sound. He was looking for the violation code.

The nest wasn't just paper; it was an infestation of *Redacted Matter*.

Species unknown. Habitat: Administrative voids. Behavior: Parasitic absorption of bureaucratic data.

But the Field Guide was empty. He had no entry for *Redacted Matter*. No weakness identified. No targeted damage bonus.

"Come on," Dale muttered, his hand steady despite the tremor in his stomach. "There's always a code."

He scanned the mass. The pages were arranged in layers.

Outer layer: minor violations.

Parking tickets.

Noise complaints. The middle layer: heavier citations.

Health code violations. Zoning disputes. The core, where Marcus was trapped, contained the darkest forms.

Environmental impact assessments. Eminent domain notices.

Dale's eyes narrowed, and he saw a pattern in the fusion. The pages weren't random. They were sorted by date, but out of order.

A 2019 permit application was fused to a 2021 zoning appeal, skipping the 2020 audit that linked them.

The System relied on chronological integrity. If the timeline was disrupted, the structure was unstable.

He uncapped his pen. The ink was blue. Standard issue. Non-toxic.

He wrote. Not a report. A correction.

He wrote the missing date.

The air in the mill shrieked. A sound like tearing metal. The Auditor's red pen halted, hovering inches from Dale's neck. The giant face recoiled, the plastic surface rippling with confusion.

**ERROR. CHRONOLOGICAL DISCREPANCY DETECTED.** **RECALCULATING...**

The nest shuddered. The paper cocoon tightened around Marcus, then loosened. The translucent skin receded, revealing bruised flesh. Marcus gasped, sucking in air that smelled of mold and old ink.

Dale didn't stop. He wrote the missing entry into his Field Guide. Not through combat, but through documentation.

**SPECIES: Redacted Matter (Administrative Parasite)** **IDENTIFIER: Missing Chronological Link.** **WEAKNESS: Temporal Inconsistency.**

The notification pinged.

**FIELD GUIDE UPDATED.** **NEW WEAKNESS IDENTIFIED.** **TARGETED DAMAGE BONUS AVAILABLE: +25% EFFECTIVENESS AGAINST ADMINISTRATIVE INFESTATIONS.**

The Auditor screamed. A high-pitched feedback loop that shattered the rusted windows of the mill. Dust rained down from the ceiling. The red pen slashed downward.

Dale dodged. The tip of the pen carved a trench in the concrete floor, leaving a glowing red scar. He rolled, his knees hitting the grit, and came up with his clipboard raised like a shield.

"You can't edit reality, Dale!" Marcus yelled, struggling against the loosening cocoon. "It's not a form! It's alive!"

"It's a violation," Dale said, breathless. "And violations can be corrected."

He looked at the Auditor. It was shrinking. Without the chronological integrity to sustain it, the entity was losing mass. The beige plastic of its face was cracking, revealing the white void beneath.

But the System wasn't done. The red pen hovered, trembling. The System was adapting. It was trying to patch the hole Dale had made.

**RECALCULATING. NEW VIOLATION TYPE: UNLICENSED TEMPORAL INTERFERENCE.** **SEVERITY: CLASS V.** **PENALTY: IMMEDIATE REVOCATION OF CERTIFICATION LEVEL.**

Dale felt a cold weight in his chest. His certification.

The key to his abilities.

If he lost Level VII, he lost the containment protocols. He lost the isolation fields. He was just a man with a clipboard in a room with a monster.

He looked at Marcus. The man was pulling himself free, tearing the paper strands with his teeth.

"Help me!" Marcus screamed.

Dale didn't move. He was calculating.

The cross-contamination penalty loomed. If he used a combat protocol here, he would contaminate his Field Guide for twenty-four hours.

He would be blind to everything else in the city.

The Rift over the textile mill was just a symptom.

There were others. If he went blind now, the city would be overrun by the time he got his sight back.

He had to do this manually, and he looked at the red pen. It was trying to write a new entry. A correction to *his* existence.

**SUBJECT: DALE KOWALSKI.** **STATUS: NON-COMPLIANT.** **ACTION: ERASURE.**

Dale stepped forward. He walked into the path of the red pen.

"Marcus, stop moving."

"I'm dying, Dale!"

"You're dehydrated. Stop wasting energy."

The pen descended. Dale held up his clipboard. Not to block it. To offer it.

He wrote one final line on the pad.

**CITATION: K-9. FAILURE TO MAINTAIN TEMPORAL INTEGRITY.** **REMEDIATION: IMMEDIATE REASSIGNMENT TO ARCHIVAL STORAGE.**

The pen hit the paper.

The impact didn't feel like wood or metal. It felt like heat. A searing, white-hot pain shot up Dale's arm. The red ink burned through the clipboard, through his shirt, into his skin.

He didn't drop it. He held the pressure. He forced the System to process the citation.

The Auditor shrieked again, a sound of digital corruption. The giant face dissolved into static. The red pen snapped in half. The two pieces fell to the floor, sizzling like hot wires.

The nest collapsed. The thousands of pages fell away in a dry cascade, burying Marcus up to his waist.

Dale dropped the clipboard. His right hand was blackened, the skin blistered and peeling. The pain was absolute, blinding. He cradled the arm against his chest, breathing in short, sharp gasps.

Silence returned to the mill.

Marcus crawled out of the paper debris, coughing, his eyes wide and wild. He looked at Dale's arm.

"What did you do?"

"I filed it," Dale said, his voice flat.

Marcus stumbled over, grabbing Dale's uninjured shoulder.

"You burned your hand. The System... it attacked you."

"It corrected the error."

Dale looked at the floor. The broken pieces of the red pen were fading, dissolving into gray ash. The notification in his vision flickered.

**JOB COMPLETE.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 4.1.** **POINTS AWARDED: 120.** **CERTIFICATION STATUS: SUSPENDED PENDING REVIEW.**

Dale stared at the rating. 4.1. A passing grade, barely. He had lost points for the cross-contamination risk.

He had lost points for the lack of protective gear. He had lost points for the sloppy containment.

But he was alive.

"Level?" Marcus asked, shaking him. "Are you still Level VII?"

The text was greyed out. **SUSPENDED.**

Dale felt a hollow ache in his gut.

It wasn't just pain. It was the loss of utility. Without his protocols, he was vulnerable.

He looked around the mill, and the shadows were deep. The air still smelled of rot.

"We need to leave," Dale said. "Before the Authority sends a recovery team."

Marcus nodded, pulling Dale toward the hole in the fence.

"I'll drive. You can't operate the vehicle."

Dale allowed himself to be led, and he kept his eye on the greyed-out status. *Suspended pending review.* That meant an audit. A literal, physical audit of his methods. The Authority didn't send auditors to check paperwork.

They sent them to check bodies.

They reached the parking lot.

The rain had fell, cold and hard.

Dale climbed into the passenger seat of Marcus's beat-up sedan. He placed the ruined clipboard on his lap.

The wood was warped, the paper singed.

Marcus started the engine. It coughed, then caught.

"Where to?" Marcus asked, gripping the steering wheel.

Dale looked at the city skyline. The Rift was still open, a jagged tear in the sky above the downtown district. Blue light pulsed from the wound.

"The Authority's local office," Dale said.

Marcus stared at him.

"Are you crazy? They'll lock you up."

"They have to process the appeal," Dale said. "And I have something they want."

He held up his left hand. In the dim light, his palm was unmarked.

But in his pocket, wrapped in a handkerchief, was the snapped tip of the red pen, which was still warm. It was a piece of the Auditor. A fragment of the System's enforcement arm.

It was evidence. And it was a weapon.

Marcus frowned, confused.

"That's just a pen tip."

"It's a Class IX artifact," Dale said. "And it belongs to me."

The car pulled onto the wet road. Headlights swept across the ruined facade of the mill. Dale watched the building shrink in the rearview mirror.

His phone buzzed, and he pulled it out with his good hand. The screen was cracked, but the message was clear.

**FROM: AUTHORITY ADMINISTRATION** **TO: KOWALSKI, DALE** **SUBJECT: AUDIT NOTIFICATION** **TIME: 00:00:01** **MESSAGE: THE SUBJECT 'AUDITOR' HAS BEEN LOCATED. YOU ARE NOT THE FIRST TO ATTEMPT CONTAINMENT. YOU ARE THE THIRD.**

Dale froze.

Third?

He scrolled down. The message continued.

**PREVIOUS ATTEMPTS: SUBJECT 1 (TERMINATED). SUBJECT 2 (TERMINATED).** **CURRENT STATUS: SUBJECT 3 (DALE KOWALSKI) HAS ACQUIRED FRAGMENT.** **WARNING: FRAGMENT IS CONTAGIOUS. IMMEDIATE QUARANTINE PROTOCOLS INITIATED.**

The car swerved. Marcus slammed on the brakes, tires screeching on the wet asphalt.

Behind them, the city lights flickered. A low hum vibrated through the chassis of the car. It wasn't coming from the engine.

It was coming from the pocket where the pen tip lay.

Dale looked at his hand. The blisters on his right hand were spreading, turning a pale, sickly beige. The skin was hardening, cracking.

He looked at the rearview mirror. The mill was gone. But the shadows in the back seat were lengthening.

Marcus turned to look at Dale. His face went white.

"Dale," he whispered. "Your hand."

Dale looked down. The pen tip in his pocket was pulsing. And the beige color was moving up his wrist, climbing toward his elbow.

The System hadn't just suspended him. It was integrating him.

He had filed the report. But reports require signatures. And the signature had to be permanent.

"Drive," Dale said, his voice calm, though his heart hammered against his ribs like a trapped bird. "Don't stop. If you stop, they'll catch up."

Marcus pressed the gas. The car sped forward into the rain, into the dark, with the beige infection spreading under Dale's skin, claiming him piece by piece.

Dale’s right hand moved against his will, reaching for the door handle.
