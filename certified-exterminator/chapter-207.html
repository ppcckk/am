Chapter 207: Backlog of Rifts

# Chapter 207

The glass shards still crunched under Dale's boots.

He didn't look down. His eyes were fixed on the horizon, on the tall, thin silhouettes standing in the lengthening shadows.

Quills.

The Authority's auditors were here. They hadn't come to help. They had come to watch the clock.

"Get up," Dale said, not turning from the figures. "Now."

Henderson scrambled to his feet, shaking. A smear of blood coated his left forearm, dripping onto the cracked asphalt. He didn't look at the wound. He looked at the rift, a jagged tear in the fabric of reality that pulsed with a sickly violet light.

"They're coming out," Henderson whispered. "Hundreds of them."

"Then we have a backlog." Dale pulled his clipboard from his belt.

The metal was dented, the clip jammed with dried ichor. He forced it open.

The paper inside was torn, but the form was standard. *Incident Report: Unlicensed Infestation.

Class IV.*

A Shard stepped from the violet haze. Then another. They moved with a skittering, insectoid gait, their bodies composed of translucent, jagged plates. They clicked as they walked, a sound like wind chimes made of broken windows.

Dale tapped his pen against the clipboard. *Tap. Tap. Tap.*

The rhythm was calm. It was the only calm thing in the alley.

"Stay behind me," Dale ordered. "Do not engage unless I mark a target."

"I have a knife," Henderson said, his voice cracking. He held up a rusted combat knife, the blade trembling.

"Put it away." Dale pointed the pen at the nearest Shard. "You see the thorax? The central segment where the legs attach?"

Henderson blinked. "Yeah."

"That's a structural load-bearing joint. In a standard chitin exoskeleton, it's protected by a secondary membrane.

In a Shard, the membrane is missing.

The Authority's Field Guide entry *Shard (Crystalline Mimic)* lists a 40% vulnerability to blunt force trauma at that specific junction.

Slashing the leg just makes it angry. Breaking the joint makes it stop."

Dale stepped forward.

The System window flickered in his peripheral vision, a translucent blue overlay that only he could see.

**STATUS: LICENSE ACTIVE (CLASS VI)** **CURRENT TASK: CONTAINMENT PROTOCOL (CLASS IV)** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 9/10** **PENALTY FOR INCOMPLETE DOCS: REVOCATION**

Revocation. The word sat in the back of his mind like a stone. It wasn't just losing a job.

It was erasure. The Authority didn't fire people.

It deleted them from the narrative of the world.

Dale raised his hand. He wasn't holding a weapon. He was holding a heavy-duty trash compactor bag, industrial grade, black, thirty-gallon capacity.

One of the Shards lunged.

Dale didn't dodge. He side-stepped, letting the creature's momentum carry it past him. He swung the bag like a flail, catching the Shard's thorax with the corner of the plastic.

*CRACK.*

The sound was wet and sharp. The Shard collapsed into a pile of glittering dust.

**TREATMENT POINTS +50.** **FIELD GUIDE CONFIRMED: WEAKNESS EXPLOITED.**

"See?" Dale said, not out of breath. "Documentation first. Violence second."

Henderson stared at the dust. "You just... crushed it with a garbage bag."

"It's a containment vessel," Dale corrected. "And I'm not crushing it. I'm facilitating its structural failure. There's a difference in the paperwork."

The rift pulsed again. More Shards poured out. A dozen.

Twenty.

They fanned out, surrounding the two men. The tall, thin figures in the shadows remained still, their quills poised over invisible parchment.

Dale checked the time on his smartwatch. 17:42. Sunset was in ten minutes.

"Listen," Dale said, his voice dropping to a flat, professional monotone.

"I need to clear the initial swarm to establish a perimeter. You need to hold the rear. If they get past me, you kill them.

Do not try to identify them. Just kill them.

If you try to identify them, you'll hesitate, and hesitation is a code violation."

"And if I kill them without identifying them?" Henderson asked.

"Zero points. And you contaminate my Field Guide. I lose my bonus against this species. And if I lose my bonus, I can't clear the rift before the Auditors finish their report."

"What happens then?"

"I get erased."

Henderson looked at the rift, then at Dale. "So we both die if I screw this up."

"Basically."

Dale kicked off from the pavement.

He moved with the efficiency of a man who had spent fourteen years navigating tight attics and cramped crawlspaces. No wasted motion. No wasted energy. He targeted the lead Shard, aiming for the thorax junction.

His boot connected with the creature's leg.

Not a kick. A precise, calculated impact.

The leg buckled. The Shard toppled. Before it could recover, Dale slammed the bottom of his clipboard into its central mass.

*CRACK.*

Dust.

**TREATMENT POINTS +50.**

He spun. Another Shard was closing in.

Dale sidestepped, using the trash bag to deflect a claw.

The claw sparked against the plastic. Dale grabbed the edge of the bag and wrapped it around the creature's head, twisting.

The Shard shrieked, a sound like grinding glass.

Dale tightened the bag.

He didn't strangle it. He compressed it.

Pressure applied to the thorax.

The weak point.

The creature exploded.

**TREATMENT POINTS +75.** **BONUS: EFFICIENCY.**

"Henderson!" Dale shouted. "Left flank! Two coming in low!"

Henderson swung his knife. It was clumsy.

Desperate.

He slashed at the first Shard's leg, missing the joint entirely.

The creature shrieked, its glass plates rippling as it regenerated.

"Hit the middle!" Dale yelled.

Henderson didn't hit the middle. He panicked. He grabbed a brick from the ground and threw it. The brick shattered against the Shard's chest, doing nothing but annoying it.

The Shard lunged.

Dale was there.

He didn't have time to set up the protocol. He didn't have time to document the approach. He had to improvise.

This was sloppy. This was a violation.

Dale pulled the glass shard from his belt—the one he had picked up after the first kill. He drove it into the Shard's eye.

Not a targeted weak point. A desperate strike.

The Shard staggered, blinded. Dale followed up with a kick to the thorax. The creature fell, twitching.

**TREATMENT POINTS +10.** **PENALTY: IMPROVISATION (20% REDUCTION).** **FIELD GUIDE CONTAMINATED: SHARD (24 HOURS).**

Dale felt a cold weight in his stomach. The contamination. It meant he couldn't use the targeted damage bonus for the next twenty-four hours. He was flying blind against the rest of the swarm.

"Move," Dale said. "Rift is widening."

He wasn't wrong.

The violet tear was growing. The edges were fraying, reality peeling back like rotten fruit. More creatures were emerging.

But they weren't Shards.

These were different.

They were larger. Humanoid.

Made of something that looked like wet clay, but blacker than night.

They had no eyes. Just smooth, featureless faces. They held no weapons.

They simply walked forward, their hands extending, fingers elongating into tendrils.

**NEW SPECIES DETECTED.** **IDENTIFICATION REQUIRED.**

The System prompt flashed in Dale's vision.

**IDENTIFY TO AVOID PENALTY.**

Dale stopped.

Henderson froze. "What are those?"

Dale stared at the creatures. The clay-like substance. The lack of sensory organs. The way they moved with a slow, inexorable grace.

He pulled out his pen. He uncapped it.

He wrote *Golem*?

No.

Too generic. He wrote *Clay*?

No. Too simple. He wrote *Void*?

No. Too abstract.

The creatures were closing in. Ten feet. Five feet.

Dale looked at the tall, thin figures in the shadows. One of them stepped forward. It raised its quill. It was waiting for the entry.

If Dale wrote the wrong name, the System would reject it. The creatures would be classified as *Unknown Threat*. Unknown threats could not be exterminated with standard protocols. They required a Level IX Certification, which Dale did not have.

If he couldn't exterminate them, he failed the job. If he failed the job, his Thoroughness Rating dropped. If his rating dropped below 9.0, the Authority would revoke his license for "Professional Negligence."

The cliffhanger wasn't the monsters. It was the word.

Dale looked at the creature's hands.

They weren't just tendrils. They were *tools*. They were shaping the air around them.

The air was curving, bending.

Gravity was distorting.

It wasn't a monster. It was an *editor*.

The creatures weren't destroying the world. They were *redacting* it.

Dale's pen hovered over the paper. His hand shook for the first time in fourteen years.

"Identify them," the Auditor said. Its voice was not a sound. It was a vibration in Dale's bones. "Identify them, or be redacted."

Henderson screamed.

One of the clay-figures grabbed his arm. The arm didn't bleed. It simply ceased to exist.

The flesh turned to gray ash and blew away in the wind.

Henderson looked at his stump. He didn't scream. He just stared.

Dale looked at the blank space where the arm had been.

*Redaction.*

The word floated in Dale's mind.

If he wrote *Redactor*, would it fit? Was it a valid species name? The Field Guide had rules. Names had to be biologically plausible or historically accurate. *Redactor* was a title, not a species.

If he wrote *Editor*, was it valid? No. Not a biological term.

He had to find the biological basis. The creature was made of clay. It shaped reality. It erased.

*Clay.* *Eraser.* *Void.*

The clay-creature turned its faceless head toward Dale. It extended a hand. The fingers uncurled, reaching for his throat.

Dale wrote.

*Null.*

The System window flickered.

**ENTRY PENDING.** **VALIDATION IN PROGRESS...**

It wasn't accepted. It wasn't rejected. It was pending.

The creature's hand closed around Dale's neck.

Cold. Absolute cold. The sensation of being forgotten.

Dale couldn't breathe. His vision blurred. The Auditor stepped closer, its quill touching the air, ready to strike.

*Validation in progress...*

Why was it pending? Was it wrong? Or was it... new?

The creature tightened its grip.

Dale's knees buckled.

He felt his memories slipping. His name.

His job. The smell of ant killer. The sound of the spray nozzle.

*Null.*

If the System rejected it, he died. If it accepted it, he had a new tool.

But there was a third option.

If the System *didn't* decide, and the creature *did* erase him, he didn't die. He just wasn't there.

Dale forced his hand up. He didn't attack the creature. He attacked the Auditor.

He threw his clipboard.

It was a stupid move. A desperate, unprofessional move. The clipboard flew through the air, the metal clip catching the Auditor's quill.

The quill snapped.

Black ink spilled into the air. It hung there, suspended.

The Auditor stepped back. It looked at the broken quill. It looked at Dale.

For the first time, the figure looked... surprised.

The clay-creature's grip loosened.

Dale gasped, falling to his knees. He coughed, sucking in air that tasted like ozone and old paper.

Henderson was on the ground, clutching the stump of his arm. He was pale, but alive.

The clay-creatures paused. They looked at the broken quill. They looked at the spilled ink.

They didn't attack.

They waited.

Dale looked at the System window.

**ENTRY ACCEPTED.** **FIELD GUIDE UPDATED.** **SPECIES: NULL (REALITY REDACTORS).** **WEAKNESS: UNSTABLE NARRATIVE STRUCTURE.** **THOROUGHNESS BONUS GRANTED: 50%.**

He had done it.

But the cost.

Dale looked at his hand. The stamp.

**9/10.**

He looked at the rift. The violet tear was closing, but slowly. The clay-creatures were retreating, pulling back into the shadows.

The Auditor picked up the broken quill. It didn't speak. It just looked at Dale.

Then, a new message appeared in Dale's vision.

**NOTICE: LICENSE PROVISIONAL SUSPENSION.** **REASON: UNSANCTIONED MODIFICATION OF AUTHORITY TOOLING.** **REMEDY: SUBMIT WRITTEN APOLOGY AND COMPLETE REMEDIATION PROTOCOL (CLASS V) WITHIN 24 HOURS.**

Dale stared at the words.

Unsanctioned modification. He broke an Auditor's quill.

If he didn't submit the apology, he was revoked.

If he did, he had to do a Class V job. Class V meant deep-sea leviathans or sentient fungal colonies. It meant leaving this city. It meant leaving Henderson.

Henderson looked up at him. "What do we do now?"

Dale looked at the rift. It was almost closed.

He looked at the Auditor.

He looked at the broken quill.

He picked it up.

It was warm. It hummed with power.

Dale tucked the quill into his pocket.

"We go home," Dale said. "I have a form to fill out."

He stood up. His legs were shaky. He walked toward the exit of the alley.

Behind him, the Auditor watched. It didn't follow.

But as Dale turned the corner, he heard a sound. A scratching. Like a pen on paper.

He stopped.

He turned back.

The Auditor was writing in the air. Not with the broken quill. With its finger.

It was writing Dale's name.

And underneath it, it was writing a new entry.

**CANDIDATE: DALE KOWALSKI.** **STATUS: RECRUIT.** **TASK: HUNT THE OTHERS.**

Dale's blood went cold.

The rift wasn't a problem to be solved. It was a recruitment drive.

And he had just signed up.

He looked at Henderson, who was trying to bandage his arm with a piece of his shirt.

"We're not going home," Dale said.

Henderson looked up. "Why?"

Dale pulled the Field Guide from his pocket. He flipped to the new entry. *Null.*

Underneath the description, there was a new line.

**REWARD FOR COMPLETION: LEVEL IX CERTIFICATION.**

**PENALTY FOR FAILURE: TOTAL REDACTION.**

Dale looked at the horizon. The sun was gone. The stars were coming out.

And in the stars, he saw them.

Dozens of rifts. Opening all over the city.

The Authority wasn't waiting for him to finish the job.

They were waiting for him to start the next one.

Dale took a step forward.

Then another.

He didn't look back.

He had a form to fill out.
