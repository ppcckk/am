Chapter 324: Verify Integrity

# Chapter 324

The ink refused to dry.

Dale watched the black pool spread across the *Final Assessment Form, Section 4*, refusing to coalesce into legible text. He tapped the nib of his pen against the clipboard's edge.

Once.

Twice. Three times. The Authority's paper was standard-issue, water-resistant, and immune to most dimensional solvents.

This was not standard.

A low hum vibrated through the soles of Dale's boots. It wasn't the tectonic groan of the Rift closing. It was the frequency of a hard drive spinning up in a server room located somewhere in the fabric of reality.

"Status?" Dale asked aloud.

The air in front of him shimmered. A holographic interface, styled like a municipal water bill, flickered into existence.

**SUBJECT: OFFICER MILLER.** **STATUS: TRANSFERRED TO ARCHIVE.** **ACTION REQUIRED: VERIFY INTEGRITY.**

Dale frowned, and he reached into his vest pocket and pulled out a pair of latex gloves. Not the blue nitrile kind he used for routine vermin, but the thick, rubberized black gloves required for Class IV biological containment.

He snapped them on.

The sound was sharp, a dry crack in the humid night air.

His hands were shaking.

He didn't look at his trembling fingers. He looked at the crater.

The hole in the earth where the Rift had been was gone.

In its place was a slab of obsidian glass, smooth and reflective.

Standing on that slab was a figure, which was shaped like Miller. It had Miller's uniform. It had Miller's posture, slumped and tired.

But the surface was wrong. It was translucent, like frosted glass.

Inside the hollow shell, swirling patterns of light moved like trapped gas.

"Officer Miller?" Dale called out. His voice was flat. Professional. He treated the entity like a client who had called to dispute a charge.

The figure didn't speak. Its head turned. The neck joint didn't bend; it rotated on a ball-and-socket mechanism that clicked audibly.

*Inspection required.*

The voice echoed in Dale's skull, bypassing his ears entirely.

Dale clicked his pen again. The ink finally flowed, but it came out red.

He ignored the color. He started writing.

**CLIENT ID: 74-992-B.** **ISSUE: CORRUPTION OF PERSONNEL FILE.** **PROTOCOL: REMOVAL AND REINTEGRATION.**

The glass-Miller took a step forward. The sound was like sandpaper on stone. It raised a hand. The fingers were fused together, forming a solid block of silica.

Dale stepped back. He didn't run. Running compromised the workspace.

He looked at the Field Guide entry for *Human-Adjacent Constructs*. It was new. Populated by the Authority in the last ten seconds.

**SPECIES: Living File.** **WEAKNESS: Logical Inconsistency.** **RESISTANCE: Physical Force.**

Dale's brow furrowed. He pulled a small, silver device from his belt. It wasn't a weapon. It was a Spectral Frequency Analyzer, usually used to detect termite mounds inside drywall.

He pointed it at the glass figure.

The device beeped. A single, flat tone.

"Your file is incomplete," Dale said.

The figure froze. The light inside its chest pulsed.

Dale walked closer. He held up the clipboard. The paper now contained a transcript. Not of Miller's death, but of the radio call that had preceded it.

**TRANSCRIPT LOG:** **02:14:03 - CENTRAL: Detecting signal.** **02:14:05 - BR-4: Where?** **02:14:06 - CENTRAL: Inside you.** **02:14:07 - BR-4: [Unintelligible]**

Dale tapped the last line.

"This is a data gap," Dale said. "You cannot be archived with a data gap. The Authority requires full documentation."

The glass figure tilted its head. The clicking sound accelerated. *Click-click-click-click.*

The ground beneath Dale's feet vibrated. Cracks spider-webbed across the obsidian surface.

A shadow detached itself from the edge of the crater. It was tall, wearing a suit that looked like it had been tailored by a spider. The face was a mask of polished bone.

"Mr. Kowalski," the shadow said. The voice was smooth, oily. "You are exceeding your certification level."

Dale didn't look up from his clipboard.

"I am conducting a final assessment. Officer Miller's file is corrupted. I am correcting the error."

The shadow stepped onto the glass. It didn't make a sound. It moved like smoke given shape.

"Miller is gone," the shadow said. "He has been filed. You are interfering with the natural order of record-keeping."

"Corruption is not natural order," Dale said. He scribbled a code on his form. *Violation 9-B: Improper Archiving.* "The Authority does not accept incomplete records. You know this."

The shadow's bone-mask tilted.

"And if the Authority accepts it?"

"Then the Authority is flawed," Dale said. He looked up. His eyes were cold. "I am here to fix the flaw."

The shadow laughed. It was a dry, rasping sound, like leaves skittering across pavement.

"You are a technician, Dale.

You spray for ants.

You do not rewrite reality.

"

"I treat the infestation," Dale said. "You are an infestation."

He tapped his pen against the clipboard again. This time, the ink turned blue.

Dale activated his Field Guide. He selected the entry for *Living File*. He targeted the weakness: *Logical Inconsistency*.

He didn't have a weapon. He had a form.

He spoke into the air, addressing the Authority directly.

"Requesting override for Protocol 7-Alpha: *Cognitive Dissonance Injection*."

The holographic interface flashed.

**ERROR: PROTOCOL NOT UNLOCKED. CERTIFICATION LEVEL V REQUIRED.**

Dale cursed under his breath. He was a Level IV. He had earned it by cleaning out the abandoned subway tunnels, but he hadn't passed the final exam yet.

The shadow lunged. It didn't strike. It reached out a long, spindly hand and touched the clipboard.

The paper turned to ash.

The clipboard remained. But the form was gone.

"You cannot document what you cannot hold," the shadow said.

Dale stared at the empty clipboard. His jaw tightened.

He reached into his pocket and pulled out a backup form. Blank. White. Standard issue.

He placed it on the clipboard. He clicked his pen.

"I don't need the old file," Dale said. "I can write a new one."

The shadow paused. It seemed confused.

Dale wrote. He didn't write about Miller. He wrote about the shadow.

**SUBJECT: BUREAUCRATIC ENTITY (CLASS: UNREGISTERED).** **LOCATION: CRATER SITE.** **ACTION: INTERFERENCE WITH LICENSED PERSONNEL.** **VIOLATION: OBSTRUCTION OF DUTY.**

The ink flowed smoothly. Black. Normal.

The shadow's bone-mask twitched. A crack appeared in the cheekbone.

"What are you doing?" the shadow hissed.

"Documenting your presence," Dale said.

"You are not on the registry. You have no ID number. You have no clearance.

You are an illegal entrant.

"

Dale's hand moved faster, and he was filling out the *Identification* section.

He was using the professional jargon he had memorized during his training. *Unlicensed Interloper. Class III Hazard.

Immediate Containment.*

The air pressure dropped. The wind picked up, swirling dust around their feet.

The glass-Miller shatterred. Small flakes of silica peeled off its body, drifting into the air.

"Stop," the shadow said. It wasn't a request anymore. It was a command.

Dale ignored it. He reached the *Elimination* section.

He didn't have a spell. He didn't have a sword.

He wrote the word: *REJECTED.*

He drew a large red X across the box.

The System rules dictated that identification preceded elimination. He had identified the shadow as a violation. He had documented the violation.

Now, he was verifying the threat.

The shadow screamed. It was a sound of tearing metal.

The ground around the crater cracked open. Roots burst through the pavement. They were black, twisted, and thorny. They wrapped around the shadow's legs, pinning it to the spot.

"Protocol 4-Delta: *Entry Refusal*," Dale said.

He hadn't unlocked that ability. But he had written it.

The System hesitated. The rules were clear: *Treatment Points are awarded only for completing full extermination protocols.* But the rules also stated: *Certification Levels unlock new Treatment Protocols.*

Dale was exploiting a loophole. He was using a higher-level protocol by manually overriding the System's permission check through the act of documentation. He was forcing the Authority to accept his work because the work was already done.

The shadow thrashed. The roots tightened.

"You are breaking the license," the shadow gasped.

"I am enforcing it," Dale said.

He signed the form. His signature was a sharp, jagged scrawl.

**SIGNED: D. KOWALSKI.** **LICENSE: IV.**

The world flashed white.

Dale blinked, and the crater was gone. The glass slab was gone. The shadow was gone.

He was standing in the middle of an empty parking lot. The asphalt was cracked, but there was no hole.

In his hand, he held the clipboard. The form was gone. In its place was a new piece of paper, printed on heavy cardstock.

**NOTICE OF CERTIFICATION ADVANCEMENT.**

Dale stared at it. He shouldn't have leveled up. He hadn't passed the practical exam. He hadn't passed the written exam.

He looked at the bottom of the page.

**EXCEPTION GRANTED:** *Thoroughness Score: 10/10.* **REASON:** *Successful application of Manual Protocol Override.* **WARNING:** *This action is logged as High-Risk. Future unauthorized overrides will result in license suspension.*

Dale let out a breath he didn't know he was holding.

His wrist burned. The symbol there was different now. It was no longer a simple circle. It was a shield, with a key inside it.

Level V, and he had jumped two levels in one night.

A car door slammed.

Dale turned. Officer Miller was standing by his police cruiser.

He was whole.

Opaque. Solid.

Miller looked at Dale. He looked at the clipboard. He looked at the air where the shadow had been.

"Did you see it?" Miller asked. His voice was hoarse.

"See what?" Dale asked. He clicked his pen.

"The thing. The shadow."

"I saw a violation," Dale said. "I corrected it."

Miller rubbed his temple.

"My head hurts. I feel... empty."

"Normal side effect of processing," Dale said. "You're back in the system. You're clean."

Miller looked at his hands. They were shaking.

"I heard a voice," Miller said. "It told me I was part of the file."

Dale didn't answer. He was looking at the new notification in his peripheral vision.

**NEW JOB AVAILABLE.** **LOCATION: DOWNTOWN LIBRARY.** **TYPE: INFESTATION (CLASS: MEMETIC).** **THREAT LEVEL: VI.**

A Memetic Infestation. Ideas that ate people.

Dale had never dealt with those. They weren't physical. They couldn't be sprayed. They couldn't be trapped.

He needed a new tool, and he looked at Miller.

"Officer," Dale said. "I need you to do something for me."

Miller straightened up.

"What?"

"I need you to forget everything that happened tonight."

Miller blinked.

"What?"

"I need a clean slate," Dale said. "The Authority doesn't like witnesses who remember too much. It complicates the paperwork."

Miller's face went blank. Confusion. Then fear.

"I can't just forget," Miller said.

"You can," Dale said. He pointed to the new Field Guide entry that was forming in his mind. *Memetic Dampener.* "I can help you. But you have to trust the protocol."

Miller looked at the dark road. He looked at Dale.

"If I forget," Miller whispered, "who am I?"

Dale clicked his pen.

"You are a certified citizen," Dale said. "And that is all you need to be."

The radio in Miller's car crackled. It wasn't Central. It wasn't the Authority.

It was a voice that Dale recognized. It was his own voice, but older. Weary.

"Stop, Dale," the voice said.

Dale froze.

The radio continued.

"If you take his memory, you break the chain. The chain is all that holds the Rift shut."

Dale looked at the radio. He looked at Miller.

Miller was staring at him, waiting.

Dale realized then that the System had not just given him a new level. It had given him a choice.

To exterminate the horror, he had to destroy the evidence.

To keep the world safe, he had to let the monster live in the man's mind.

Dale looked at the clipboard. He looked at the blank space where he could write the command to erase Miller's memory.

Or he could write a warning.

A warning that would alert the other survivors. A warning that would cause panic.

A warning that would trigger a *Full Containment* event, which would likely level the entire district.

The radio crackled again.

"Choose, Dale."

Dale raised his pen. The ink hovered over the paper, trembling.
