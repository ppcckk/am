Chapter 333: Clipboard from the Void

# Chapter 333

The fluorescent lights of the breakroom flickered with a sickly, intermittent buzz. Mara Halloway sat hunched over her desk, her knuckles white as she gripped the edge of her monitor. The screen displayed a live feed from Sector 4, but the image was corrupted, washed out in hues of bruised purple and static gray.

"System diagnostic," Mara muttered, tapping her headset. "I'm getting a null signal on the Kowalski unit."

Silence crackled in her ear. Then, a flat, metallic voice responded.

"Unit 33-K is unresponsive.

Biometric telemetry shows heart rate at three hundred and twelve beats per minute. Core temperature is dropping.

"

Mara didn't breathe. She watched the static resolve into a jagged black shape. A void. It swirled with impossible geometry, swallowing the pixels of the suburban street it had once overlaid.

"He's not dead," Mara whispered, her voice trembling. "He's in the buffer zone. The Authority's reset protocol is trying to purge him."

"Ma'am, protocol dictates we sever the link," the technician said, his voice too calm, too detached. "If the auditor breaches the containment field, the feedback loop will fry every licensed unit in the tri-county area. We have to cut him loose."

"Cut him loose?" Mara slammed her palm against the desk.

"That's a Class IX Auditor. Do you know what that means?

It means he's the only thing standing between this county and a total dimensional collapse.

If you cut the link, the breach widens. It doesn't close. It eats.

"

The technician hesitated.

"We have no authority to keep the connection open. The system flagged him as terminated. Clearance revoked."

"Override it," Mara said.

"I can't."

"Then I'll do it."

Mara reached for the manual disconnect lever, a physical toggle on the side of her console. It was archaic, dangerous, and strictly forbidden. If she pulled it, she might burn out the relay. If she didn't, Dale Kowalski ceased to exist in this reality, leaving a hole in the world that monsters would crawl through.

Her hand hovered. Sweat beaded on her forehead.

On the screen, the static shifted. A single, crisp line of text appeared, overriding the corruption.

**FILE: THE ORIGINAL BREACH.** **STATUS: OPEN.**

Mara froze. That wasn't supposed to be possible. The Authority did not open files for terminated subjects.

"Wait," she said, leaning closer. "Did he just... access the root directory?"

The technician went pale.

"He's hacking the licensing database."

"No," Mara corrected, her eyes widening. "He's filing an appeal."

***

The darkness had weight.

Dale hung suspended in the void, clutching the black leather file with both hands. His fingers were numb, the joints locking up from the cold that radiated off the light hand he still gripped with his left. The red warning text burned in his peripheral vision, pulsing in time with the throbbing of his temples.

**CLEARANCE REVOKED.** **SUBJECT 3: TERMINATE.**

He ignored the text. He opened the file.

The pages weren't paper. They were sheets of compressed starlight, thin and translucent.

As Dale turned the first page, the void around him fractured.

Shards of reality peeled away, revealing not monsters or magic, but data.

Streams of binary code cascaded like rain, but the code was wrong. It was messy.

Sloppy.

Dale frowned. He adjusted his grip on the file, treating it like a clipboard. He scanned the first entry.

**SUBJECT: ZERO.** **ORIGIN: NON-DIMENSIONAL SOURCE.** **CAUSE: PROCEDURAL ERROR (TYPE 7).**

Type 7. Dale knew Type 7 errors.

He dealt with them every day in the field, which was a failure to isolate the source of an infestation before treating the symptom. Someone, at some point in the history of the Authority, had tried to patch a rift without sealing the perimeter. They had sprayed pesticide on a leaky roof.

He turned the page.

**REMEDIATION STATUS: INCOMPLETE.** **NOTICE: UNLICENSED INTERVENTION DETECTED.**

The void shuddered. The light hand jerked, trying to pull away. Dale tightened his grip. He wasn't fighting the entity; he was documenting the violation.

*This is a code violation,* Dale thought. *Section 4, Paragraph B. Unauthorized modification of foundational infrastructure.*

The red text flashed violently.

**ERROR: AUDITOR EXCEEDS CLEARANCE.**

Dale pulled out his own pen. It was a standard-issue ballpoint, blue ink, chewed at the cap. He uncapped it.

The void resisted.

The pressure increased, crushing against his eardrums. The humming noise escalated to a scream.

Dale ignored it, and he wrote on the first page of the starlight file.

**CORRECTION: PERIMETER BREACH UNDOCUMENTED. SOURCE ISOLATION FAILED. RECOMMEND FULL QUARANTINE AND RE-CERTIFICATION OF ALL PREVIOUS TREATMENTS.**

The writing glowed gold. The red text flickered, confused.

The Authority's system was designed to process protocols, not legal appeals from terminated subjects.

But Dale wasn't appealing, and he was conducting an audit.

He turned to the second page. The handwriting was cramped, urgent.

**INFESTATION VECTOR: THE

"HERO" NARRATIVE.** **CLASSIFICATION: MALICIOUS CONTAMINANT.**

The void stopped swirling. For a second, there was absolute silence. Then, the text in his vision changed.

**ANALYZING...** **ANALYZING...**

Dale kept writing. He listed the flaws in the current system. The reliance on heroism.

The lack of standardized documentation. The cross-contamination penalties that punished thoroughness.

He cited specific incidents, chapter and verse, from his own field reports.

He attached the evidence of the broodmother in the crawlspace, the failed containment in Sector 7, the misidentified beetle swarms in the industrial district.

He was building a case file. And he was winning.

The light hand softened. It stopped pulling. It waited.

The red text vanished. In its place, new words appeared in crisp, white font.

**AUDIT COMPLETE.** **PRELIMINARY FINDING: SYSTEMIC FAILURE CONFIRMED.** **PROVISIONAL CLEARANCE RESTORED (LEVEL: OBSERVER).**

Dale didn't smile. He capped his pen.

He wasn't out of danger. The Authority had acknowledged the error, but it hadn't fixed it. It had just admitted the leak. Now he had to fix it, and he had no tools, no team, and no authority other than his own stubborn adherence to protocol.

He looked down at the file. The pages were still thin, still cold. But the binding was holding.

"Where am I?" Dale asked aloud. His voice sounded dry, scratchy.

The void answered. Not with words, but with a sensation. A pull. A tug in his gut.

**RETURN TO FIELD. JOB PENDING.**

The darkness rushed away.

***

Dale hit the pavement.

Hard.

The asphalt scraped against his cheek, tearing skin. He gasped, sucking in air that smelled of gasoline, wet earth, and something metallic, like old blood.

He lay there for a second, checking his limbs.

Ten fingers. Ten toes.

His clipboard was in his right hand, and the black file was gone.

He sat up, rubbing his neck. The car was gone. Marcus was gone. The vortex was gone.

Instead, he was in an alleyway. Narrow. Dark. Lined with dumpster lids and graffiti.

Rain had fell, mixing with the grime on the ground. Dale stood up, brushing off his uniform.

It was torn, stained with void-ash, but his boots were intact, which was what mattered.

The boots.

He checked his wrist. The barcode on his arm was no longer red. It was a dull, pulsing gray.

**STATUS: PROBATIONARY.** **CLEARANCE: OBSERVER.**

Dale frowned. Observer. That meant he couldn't treat.

He could only watch. And judging.

A scream echoed from the end of the alley.

It wasn't a human scream. It was high-pitched, chittering, like a bird caught in a trap.

Dale turned.

At the far end of the alley, three figures huddled against the brick wall.

They were survivors. Unlicensed. Civilian.

Dressed in rags, armed with baseball bats and tire irons.

And crawling out of the storm drain beneath them was a creature.

It looked like a centipede, but the size of a school bus. Its legs were translucent, filled with swirling black sludge. Its head was a cluster of human faces, screaming silently, fused into a single mass of flesh.

The survivors were backing away, their weapons raised.

"Hit it!" one of them yelled. "Stab the head!"

Dale watched. He didn't move. He didn't intervene. He was an Observer.

But he could see the data.

**SPECIES: MIMIC-ROACH (HYBRID).** **WEAKNESS: UNDOCUMENTED.** **RISK LEVEL: CLASS 4.**

The survivor lunged. He swung his tire iron at the central face of the creature.

The impact did nothing. The face absorbed the blow, rippling like water.

Then, the creature lashed out with a leg.

It struck the man in the chest. The body folded, bones snapping audibly.

The other two survivors froze.

Dale stepped forward. He couldn't attack. He couldn't deploy a protocol. But he could speak.

"Stop," Dale said. His voice carried, flat and authoritative. "Don't hit the face."

The survivors looked at him, panicked.

"Who are you?" one screamed. "Help us!"

"I can't help you," Dale said. "You're contaminating the kill zone."

"What?"

"The faces are decoys. They're sensory nodes.

You're hitting the wrong part.

Look at the legs.

The third pair from the rear. They're translucent.

That's the ventilation port. Hit that, and the internal pressure builds. It explodes.

"

The woman with the baseball bat stared at him.

"You're insane."

"Move to the left flank," Dale ordered. "Don't touch the walls. The slime is acidic."

The man with the bat hesitated, then moved. He swung at the leg.

*Crack.*

The leg splintered. Black fluid sprayed out. The creature shrieked, its many faces turning to look at the wound.

"Again!" Dale shouted. "Hit the same spot! Do not switch targets! Consistency is key!"

The woman swung. And swung. And swung.

The leg fractured further. The creature thrashed, its other legs flailing. The acid slime pooled on the ground, eating into the brick.

"Keep going!" Dale said. He wasn't fighting. He was coaching.

The creature convulsed. A low rumble built in its torso.

"Back!" Dale yelled. "Get back!"

The survivors scrambled away.

The creature erupted.

Not in blood, but in pressure. The translucent leg burst, sending shards of chitin flying. The rest of the body deflated, collapsing into a puddle of black sludge and screaming faces that dissolved into the acid.

Silence returned to the alley, and the survivors stood panting, covered in grime. They looked at Dale.

"How did you know?" the woman asked, her voice shaking.

Dale pulled out his clipboard. He opened a fresh page.

"I didn't know," Dale said. "I inferred it from the lack of structural integrity in the facial musculature. You were trying to decapitate a bug. Bugs don't have necks."

He wrote.

**INCIDENT REPORT:** **LOCATION: ALLEY 4, SECTOR 9.** **TARGET: MIMIC-ROACH (HYBRID).** **STATUS: ELIMINATED.** **THOROUGHNESS: 6.5.** **NOTES: CIVILIANS USED IMPROPER WEAPONS. RECOMMEND CIVILIAN EDUCATION CAMPAIGN.**

He tore the page off.

"Name," Dale said, holding out the paper.

"We... we're just survivors," the man said.

"Name," Dale repeated. "For the file.

You assisted in an extermination.

You need to be registered, or you'll be flagged as unlicensed combatants. And unlicensed combatants get debuffed if they enter a licensed zone.

"

The man blinked.

"Debuffed?"

"Dropped stats. Reduced strength. Slower movement. It's a penalty for unauthorized engagement."

The woman looked at Dale, then at the dissolving monster. She took the paper. She wrote her name. *Sarah Jenkins.*

Dale nodded. He tore the top copy and handed it to her.

"Keep this.

If the Authority asks, you helped. You didn't fight.

You assisted.

There's a difference.

"

He turned to walk away.

"Wait," Sarah called. "Who are you?"

Dale stopped. He looked at his wrist. The gray barcode was pulsing faster.

**NEW OBJECTIVE DETECTED.** **TARGET: THE ARCHITECT OF THE BREACH.** **LOCATION: UNKNOWN.** **THREAT LEVEL: CLASS IX.**

Dale stared at the text. Class IX. The top of the ladder. The end of the line.

He looked at the survivors. They were alive because he'd told them where to hit.

But he hadn't treated the nest, and the storm drain was still open.

More of them would come.

And he couldn't treat it. He was an Observer.

He couldn't spray. He couldn't seal. He could only watch.

Unless he broke the rules again.

Dale looked at the clipboard. He looked at the rain falling into the black sludge.

He uncapped his pen.

"Stay here," Dale said. "Keep the area clear. I'm going to find the source."

"Where?" Sarah asked.

Dale walked toward the storm drain. The black fluid was still seeping out.

"To write a better report," Dale said.

He stepped into the drain.

The water rose to his knees. It was warm.

And then, the System notification changed.

**ERROR: OBSERVER CANNOT ENTER RESTRICTED ZONE.** **OVERRIDE REQUIRES LEVEL 5 CLEARANCE.** **CURRENT CLEARANCE: OBSERVER.**

Dale didn't stop. He kept walking.

The water turned black.

And from the depths, a voice echoed. Not the Authority's. Not a monster's.

It was his own voice.

*"You can't file a complaint against the building owner, Dale. You have to move out."*

Dale stopped.

The water rose to his chest, and he looked down.

His reflection wasn't in the water.

Instead, a hand reached up from the black fluid.

It was pale. Long-fingered.

It held a clipboard.

Dale reached for it.

The hand grabbed his wrist.

And pulled.
