Chapter 275: Step One Identification

# Chapter 275

The clipboard was gone. That was the first problem.

Dale stared at the empty space where his hands had been holding the board moments before. The wood was gone. The paper was gone. The clip that held the inspection forms for *Rattus profundus* and the harmonic resonance charts had simply ceased to exist.

His right hand remained raised in the air, fingers curled in a grip that held nothing. His left arm pinned him to the doorframe, knuckles white against the splintering oak.

The thing in the rift—the mimic, the doppelganger, the unauthorized duplicate—stepped fully onto the porch. Its suit was crisp. Its tie was knotted with geometric precision. It looked at Dale with eyes that were voids of ink, reflecting nothing from the chaotic sky above.

"License Class I," the mimic repeated. Its voice sounded like a recording of Dale's own voice played through a speaker submerged in water. "Insufficient clearance for dimensional breach containment. Your authority is revoked."

Dale tried to lower his hand. The mimic's hand stayed raised. Dale's hand followed the mimic's, jerky and involuntary. A puppet on a string made of static.

The officer on the ground groaned. Blood trailed from her ear, mixing with the dust.

She wasn't looking at the mimic, and she was looking at the sky, where the purple clouds were now spiraling into a black vortex. The wind howled, sucking debris into the center. A mailbox lifted off its hinges and flew into the dark.

Dale's field guide flashed red again, but the text was garbled.

**ERROR: IDENTITY COLLISION.** **PROTOCOL LOCK: ACTIVE.** **USER: UNKNOWN.**

The Authority wasn't just crashing. It was being overwritten.

Dale's breath hitched.

His contamination anxiety spiked, a cold sweat breaking along his spine. If his workspace was compromised, he couldn't operate.

He needed a sterile environment, and he needed documentation. Without the clipboard, he had no evidence. Without evidence, he had no points.

Without points, he had no defense.

But the mimic didn't have a clipboard either. It held one, but it was blank. White. Perfect.

Dale blinked.

The mimic's clipboard was pristine.

No coffee stains. No torn corners.

No red tape.

It wasn't a copy of Dale's tools. It was a reset.

The mimic raised the clipboard. A pen appeared in its other hand.

"Initiating deletion sequence," the mimic said. "Target: Dale Kowalski. Status: Non-compliant."

Dale's left hand twitched. The doorframe splintered further under his grip.

He couldn't fight the physical compulsion, but his mind was racing.

He was a technician. He knew the code. He knew the loopholes.

The system demanded identification, documentation, isolation, elimination, and verification. The mimic had skipped the first step. It hadn't identified Dale as a threat within the protocol framework. It had declared him invalid.

That was a procedural error.

Dale forced his jaw to unclench, and he stopped trying to pull his hand down. Instead, he let it stay raised.

He leaned into the wind.

The suction was stronger now, pulling at his clothes, trying to drag him into the rift.

"Wait," Dale said. His voice was flat, professional. "You're missing a step."

The mimic paused. The pen hovered over the white paper.

"Clarification requested," the mimic said.

"You're acting as the Authority," Dale said. "But you're not on the payroll. You're an anomaly."

"I am the correction," the mimic replied.

"Anomalies require a variance report," Dale said.

The mimic tilted its head. The black eyes narrowed.

Dale's hand was still raised, but he shifted his weight. He wasn't fighting the mimic's control; he was using it.

The mimic wanted him to hold the pose.

It needed him stationary to 'delete' him.

If he stood still, he died. If he moved, he died.

But the protocol for 'Deletion' usually required a signed waiver.

Dale looked at the officer. She was crawling backward, away from the vortex. Her eyes were on Dale, terrified but focused. She was a witness.

A witness was a requirement for Class III and above infractions.

Dale hadn't reached Class III yet. He was Class II, barely. But the mimic was treating the event with the severity of a Class IX breach. That was an overreach.

Overreach triggered a jurisdictional dispute.

"Who authorized this intervention?" Dale asked.

"The Rift," the mimic said.

"The Rift is not a licensing body," Dale said. He spoke to the air, to the crashing system, to whatever was left of the Authority. "The Rift is an entry point.

It requires a Permit to Open. Do you have the Permit?

"

The mimic stiffened. The pen stopped moving.

For a fraction of a second, the static in Dale's head cleared. The compulsion wavered.

Dale seized the opportunity, and he didn't try to move his body. He moved his eyes. He looked at the officer.

He nodded once, sharply.

She understood. It was a universal sign. *Document.*

She pulled a small notepad from her vest. A standard issue police pad. She scribbled something.

The mimic turned its head toward her.

"Witness status: Irrelevant. You are contaminated."

"Contamination is a state of matter, not a legal status," Dale said. He kept his voice steady, boring, like he was reading a meter.

"If you are deleting me, you are altering the record.

That requires a Form 12-B. Cross-Dimensional Asset Transfer. Do you have Form 12-B?

"

The mimic's face twitched. The smooth skin rippled.

"I am the record," the mimic said.

"No," Dale said. "You're a draft."

The word *draft* seemed to glitch the system. The red error text on Dale's peripheral vision shifted.

**NEW OBJECTIVE: VERIFY JURISDICTION.** **TIME REMAINING: 00:14:59.** **PENALTY FOR FAILURE: TOTAL ERASURE.**

A timer. A hard deadline.

Dale's heart hammered against his ribs, but his hands were steady. The shaking had stopped. The routine had kicked in. The mimic was trying to intimidate him with authority, but Dale knew that authority without paperwork was just bullying.

And bullies could be audited.

The mimic raised the pen again.

"Erasure commencing."

"Stop," Dale said.

The command wasn't directed at the mimic. It was directed at the Air.

Dale shouted, projecting his voice over the wind.

"I am invoking the Right to Appeal based on Unlicensed Entity Interference! I demand an audit of the acting authority!"

The air shimmered. The black vortex slowed.

The mimic looked confused. The blackness in its eyes swirled.

"You do not have standing," the mimic said.

"I have a witness," Dale said, pointing to the officer. "And I have a field guide."

"I destroyed your guide," the mimic said.

"No," Dale said. "You destroyed the physical medium.

The data remains in the cloud, and the Authority has it.

You don't."

The mimic stepped forward. The suction increased. Dale's feet slid across the porch. The wood groaned.

"You are obsolete," the mimic said. "Class I is a nuisance tier. You are being consolidated."

Dale's fingers dug into the doorframe. A splinter pierced his thumb. The pain was sharp, grounding.

Good. Pain was data.

"Consolidation requires a Thoroughness Rating of 10," Dale said.

"You're acting on a rating of... what? Zero? You haven't completed the job.

You haven't verified the elimination.

"

"I am the elimination," the mimic said.

"Then show me the body," Dale said.

The mimic froze.

It didn't have a body. It had a target.

Dale was still alive.

The protocol stated: *Eliminate, then Verify.* The mimic had skipped the elimination because it assumed Dale was already deleted by the system override. But the system hadn't deleted him. It had only locked him out.

That was a critical failure. A Class 5 Process Error.

The sky flickered. The black vortex stuttered.

A new notification burned into Dale's vision, gold instead of red.

**SYSTEM ALERT: CRITICAL PROTOCOL VIOLATION DETECTED.** **ACTING AUTHORITY: INVALID.** **JURISDICTION: TRANSFERRED TO LOCAL LICENSEE.** **WARNING: LOCAL LICENSEE LACKS CLEARANCE FOR CURRENT THREAT LEVEL.** **OPTION: REQUEST BACKUP.**

Dale stared at the words. *Request Backup.*

He didn't have backup. He was alone in a suburban neighborhood with an officer who was bleeding and a doppelganger that was trying to erase his existence.

But the system had acknowledged his appeal. The Authority, or what was left of it, had recognized the mimic's procedural error.

That bought him time.

It didn't give him power, which gave him a lever.

He looked at the officer.

"What did you write?"

She held up the pad. Her hands were trembling.

Dale squinted. It wasn't a report. It was a code. *Code 404. Unit Not Found.*

She was treating the mimic like a broken appliance.

Dale grinned. It was a tight, grim expression.

"Good," Dale said. "Because that's exactly what this thing is."

The mimic shrieked. The sound was a digital screech, high-pitched and painful. Its form distorted.

The suit rippled. The tie dissolved into pixels.

"You are interfering," the mimic screamed.

"I'm inspecting," Dale said. He took a step forward. His body was still heavy, still resisting, but the lock had loosened. The jurisdiction transfer had broken the mimic's direct control link.

Dale reached into his pocket. He didn't have a clipboard. He didn't have a spray can.

He had a ballpoint pen. A cheap, blue-ink Bic pen he used for filling out invoices.

It wasn't a weapon. It was a tool.

He uncapped it. The click was loud in the sudden silence of the porch.

"You're a software bug," Dale said. "And I'm here to patch you."

The mimic lunged. It didn't use claws or magic. It used gravity. The air around it collapsed, trying to crush Dale into the floorboards.

Dale didn't dodge. He stepped into the collapse.

He jammed the pen into the mimic's chest.

It didn't pierce skin. It sank into the fabric of the suit, into the glowing void beneath.

The mimic convulsed. Black smoke poured from the wound.

**ACTION LOGGED: PHYSICAL INTERACTION WITH UNLICENSED ENTITY.** **STATUS: INVALID. NO DAMAGE DEALT.** **CROSS-CONTAMINATION RISK: 100%.** **FIELD GUIDE CORRUPTION IMMINENT.**

Dale twisted the pen, and he wasn't trying to hurt it. He was trying to mark it.

"Property of Dale Kowalski," he muttered. "Do not remove."

The mimic howled. The blue suit tore. Underneath, there was no flesh. There was only a swirling mass of raw data, the same purple-black energy that made up the rift.

Dale pulled the pen out. A drop of black fluid coated the plastic barrel.

He looked at the sky. The vortex was unstable. The mimic was destabilizing the rift by failing to maintain the containment field.

If the mimic died, the rift might stay open. Or it might explode.

Dale needed to close the rift. But he couldn't do it alone. He needed a Level IV clearance at minimum to handle a harmonic inversion of this magnitude.

He had Class II, and he looked at the officer. She had stopped crawling. She was watching him, wide-eyed.

"Officer," Dale said. "Do you have a radio?"

She nodded, reaching for her shoulder mic.

"Call dispatch," Dale said. "Tell them we have a Code Black. Unauthorized dimensional intrusion. Request immediate support from the Licensing Authority Regional Office."

"That's not a police code," the officer said.

"It is now," Dale said. "And tell them I'm invoking Emergency Provision 77-C. Immediate promotion pending audit."

The officer hesitated. Then she pressed the button.

"Dispatch, this is Unit 4. We have a... a situation. We need the Authority. Send everyone."

Dale turned back to the mimic. It was kneeling now, its form flickering between the image of Dale and a shapeless blob of shadow.

"You're going to fail the exam," Dale said.

The mimic looked up. The black eyes were gone. For a second, Dale saw his own face, terrified and human, looking back at him.

"I..." the mimic whispered. Its voice was no longer a recording. It was Dale's voice, raw and broken.

Then the shadow swallowed the face.

The ground shook. The porch collapsed. Dale jumped back, balancing on the edge of the sinking wood.

The rift was contracting. The suction reversed. Instead of pulling things in, it was pushing them out.

Debris flew outward. Chunks of asphalt, shattered windows, and the twisted remains of streetlights shot into the sky.

And from the center, a new figure emerged.

It wasn't the mimic, which was larger. Taller. It wore armor made of chitinous plates, the color of bruised plums. It held a weapon that looked like a spear forged from lightning.

The Authority hadn't sent help. The Authority had sent a Hunter.

Dale stared at the armored figure. The Field Guide popped up, but the text was scrambled, unable to identify the species.

**UNKNOWN ENTITY.** **CLASS: UNKNOWN.** **THREAT: EXTREME.**

The armored figure raised its spear. It looked at Dale. Then it looked at the dissolving mimic.

It spoke, and the voice boomed like thunder.

"Who holds the license?"

Dale looked at his shaking hands. He looked at the pen, dripping black ink. He looked at the officer, who was pointing a gun at the new giant, her hands steady now.

Dale straightened his jacket. He brushed off the dust.

He held up the pen.

"I do," Dale said.

The armored figure lowered the spear an inch.

"Then prove it," the figure said. "Before the breach widens."

Dale looked at the timer in his vision.

**TIME REMAINING: 00:02:14.**

He didn't have a plan. He didn't have a weapon. He had a pen, a witness, and a reputation for thoroughness that was currently worth zero points.

He walked toward the giant.

"Step one," Dale said. "Identification."
