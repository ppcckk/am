Chapter 326: Protocol for the Failed

# Chapter 326

Dale sprinted across the cracked asphalt, his lungs burning with the taste of copper and ozone. The doppelganger didn't chase him. It stood still, watching him run with that thin, cruel smile, the glass leg dragging a slow, screeching arc behind it.

The radio in Miller's car kept screaming. A loop. A broken record of panic.

Dale didn't look back. He focused on the rhythm of his boots.

Left foot, right foot.

Breathe in for four counts. Breathe out for six. Protocol for hyperventilation.

Protocol for panic.

He'd used it on angry homeowners before. It worked here too.

He rounded the corner of Elm Street, his side stitches flaring.

The air smelled wrong. Not just wet asphalt and rotting garbage, but something sterile. Like the inside of a hospital morgue.

The streetlights flickered and died, one by one, starting from the end of the block and moving toward him.

A hand grabbed his collar.

Dale spun, elbow up. He connected with solid bone.

"Stop," a voice hissed. "Don't you dare run toward the containment zone. You'll contaminate it."

Dale blinked, and the figure was small, hunched, wearing a hazmat suit that looked two sizes too big.

The faceplate was fogged, but Dale recognized the insignia. The Authority's seal.

But it was scratched out, replaced with a crude, hand-drawn eye.

"Miller?" Dale asked.

Miller pulled his faceplate up. His eyes were bloodshot, veins pulsing black under the skin.

He looked like he hadn't slept in a week.

His hands shook. He held a clipboard, but the pages were blank.

"Miller, what is happening?" Dale gripped Miller's shoulders, shaking him gently. "The doppelganger. The chain. What does it mean?"

"It means you broke the loop," Miller whispered.

He looked past Dale, toward the darkened end of the street.

"You ran.

You didn't document.

You didn't verify. You just... ran.

"

"I was saving my life," Dale said. His voice was flat, but his knuckles were white where they gripped Miller's suit.

"Life is secondary to the record," Miller said. He shoved the blank clipboard into Dale's chest.

"Write it down.

Now.

Before it catches up.

"

Dale looked at the blank pages. He didn't have a pen. He had lost it when he dropped his clipboard.

He looked around wildly. The street was empty.

Silent, except for the distant, rhythmic thumping of Miller's radio.

"I don't have a pen," Dale said.

Miller pulled a stylus from his belt. It was made of bone. He pressed it into Dale's hand.

"Use the glass.

Use the distortion. Write with the pain.

"

Dale stared at the stylus. It was warm. It vibrated.

"What is this?" Dale asked.

"Narrative Ink," Miller said.

"You erased the Authority's witness. So the Authority erased your memory of why you were there. You need to reconstruct the file.

Or you cease to exist.

"

Dale felt a cold sweat prickle his neck, and he looked at the doppelganger.

It was closer now. It hadn't walked.

It had appeared ten feet away, as if space itself had folded. It raised a hand. Its fingers were long, too many joints.

"Write," Dale commanded himself.

He pressed the bone stylus to the paper. Nothing happened. The ink didn't flow.

"It doesn't work like that," Miller said, his voice rising in panic. "You have to identify the violation. Find the breach in the logic. Where did the story go wrong?"

Dale looked at the doppelganger. It was wearing his uniform.

But the name tag was wrong.

It read *D. Kowalski, Class III*.

Dale was Class II.

"The rank," Dale said.

Miller's eyes widened.

"Yes. The rank.

The Authority doesn't issue upgrades without a field test.

You skipped the test. You used the Narrative Erasure protocol to survive. That's a violation.

A Class IV infraction. Unauthorized protocol deployment.

"

"I didn't choose it," Dale argued.

"The System offered it.

Cost: My Certification. I refused to pay.

I ran instead."

"Running is a refusal," Miller said. "But the cost is mandatory. If you don't pay with your certification, the System takes something else. It takes your history."

The doppelganger smiled. It opened its mouth. No tongue. Just a void of static.

"Tell them," the doppelganger said. Its voice was louder now, booming in Dale's skull. "Tell them about the Rift. Tell them you killed it."

"I didn't kill it," Dale said. He looked at Miller. "Did I?"

Miller shook his head violently.

"I don't know.

I was in the car.

I heard the scream. But then... silence. And then you were here.

And the thing was here.

And you were running.

"

"Check the log," Dale said. "Miller, check your log."

Miller fumbled at his belt. He pulled out a tablet. The screen was cracked, spiderwebbed with black lines. He tapped it frantically.

"Access denied," Miller muttered. "Biometric mismatch. My eyes are wrong. The System doesn't recognize me."

"Give it to me," Dale said.

Miller hesitated, then shoved the tablet at him. Dale grabbed it. He held his hand over the screen. The scanner flashed green.

**USER: DALE KOWALSKI. LEVEL: II. STATUS: PROBATIONARY.**

**LOG: ERROR 404. MEMORY SECTOR CORRUPTED.**

"Corrupted," Dale said. He looked at the doppelganger. It was watching him with curiosity, like a technician inspecting a faulty circuit.

"The corruption is spreading," Miller said.

He pointed to his own face. The black veins were moving. Creeping up his neck.

"It's in me. It's in the radio.

It's in the street.

We're being overwritten.

"

"By who?" Dale asked.

"By the new file," Miller said. He grabbed Dale's arm.

His grip was crushing.

"The doppelganger isn't a monster. It's the replacement entry.

The Authority accepted the Narrative Erasure. It deleted you, Dale.

You don't exist in the current database.

That thing?" He pointed at the doppelganger. "That's the updated version. Class III.

More efficient.

Less anxiety. No contamination phobia.

"

Dale looked at his hands, and they were trembling. He looked at the doppelganger. It stepped forward.

The glass leg didn't make a sound this time.

"If I don't exist," Dale said, "then I can't be certified."

"Exactly," Miller said. "And if you're not certified, you're not a technician. You're just another infestation. And infestations get sprayed."

The doppelganger raised its hand. The air around it shimmered. A System prompt appeared in Dale's peripheral vision.

**UNLICENSED ENTITY DETECTED. PREPARE FOR TERMINATION.**

"Miller," Dale said. "You're a technician too, right? Class I?"

Miller laughed.

A dry, hacking sound.

"I was. I failed the practical exam.

Too much hesitation. They demoted me to observer. But I kept my Field Guide.

"

"Show me," Dale said. "Show me the entry for Rattus profundus. Show me the entry for the Rift Entity."

Miller pulled a crumpled piece of paper from his pocket. It was torn, water-damaged, but the text was legible.

Dale took it. He read quickly.

The entry for *Rattus profundus* was standard.

Weakness: High-frequency sonic disruption. Vulnerability to structural isolation.

But at the bottom, in red ink, was a new entry.

**SUBJECT: DOPPELGANGER (SELF-REPLECTION ENTITY).** **ORIGIN: SYSTEM ERROR / USER ABUSE.** **WEAKNESS: INCONSISTENCY IN SELF-PERCEPTION.** **PROTOCOL: LOGICAL CONTRADICTION.**

Dale looked up, and he looked at the doppelganger. It was waiting for him to speak.

It expected a fight.

Or a surrender.

Dale didn't fight. He didn't surrender.

He pulled out his own clipboard, and the one he had dropped.

He picked it up. The paper was blank.

The pen was gone. But he had the bone stylus.

"Miller," Dale said. "Read me the code for a Class III upgrade."

Miller squinted at his tablet.

"Section 4, Paragraph 12. Requires a Thoroughness Rating of 9.5 and zero unresolved infractions."

"I have an unresolved infraction," Dale said.

"So you can't be Class III," Miller said.

"But I have a Thoroughness Rating of 9.2," Dale said. "Which is close. But not enough."

"So you're stuck," Miller said. He was panting now. The black veins were reaching his jaw. "You're stuck in the gap."

"No," Dale said. He looked at the doppelganger. "I'm stuck in the error."

Dale stepped forward. The doppelganger watched him. It tilted its head.

"You're not real," Dale said. His voice was steady. Procedural.

"I am the update," the doppelganger said.

"You're a glitch," Dale said. "The System doesn't allow duplicate entries for a single user ID. It's a database constraint. You're an orphaned record."

The doppelganger paused. Its smile faltered. Just a fraction.

"Prove it," the doppelganger said.

Dale turned to Miller.

"Miller, write a new entry.

Right now.

Use the bone stylus. Write: *Subject: Dale Kowalski. Status: Active.

Certification: Level II.

Infraction: Pending.*

"

Miller's eyes went wide.

"That's... that's not how it works. You can't overwrite the live file."

"You can," Dale said.

"If you document the overwrite. The Authority rewards thoroughness. If you document that I am currently *both* the original and the error, the System has to resolve the conflict.

And the rule for resolving conflicts is strict hierarchy.

"

Miller looked at the blank page in his hand. He looked at the bone stylus. He looked at Dale.

"If I do this," Miller whispered, "I'm aiding an unlicensed entity."

"I'm licensed," Dale said. "I just have a pending infraction. There's a difference. Read the bylaws. *Pending* is not *Revoked*."

Miller swallowed, and he looked at the doppelganger.

The doppelganger was still watching. But it was tense.

Its glass leg twitched.

Miller took the bone stylus. He wrote.

*Subject: Dale Kowalski. Status: Active. Certification: Level II. Infraction: Pending.*

The air crackled. The streetlights flickered back on. The doppelganger let out a shriek that sounded like tearing metal.

"It's working," Miller gasped. "The System is recalculating."

"Keep writing," Dale said. "Write the contradiction. *Subject cannot exist in two states simultaneously.*"

Miller wrote. The bone stylus grew hot in his hand. He screamed, dropping it.

The doppelganger lunged.

Dale grabbed Miller's tablet. He held it up like a shield.

**CONFLICT DETECTED. RESOLVING...**

The doppelganger hit the tablet. The screen shattered.

But the doppelganger didn't pass through.

It stopped. It was solid. Real.

But it was flickering.

Dale looked at the doppelganger. It looked at him.

"You're not me," Dale said. "You're the part of me that wanted to quit."

The doppelganger's face contorted. The black eyes widened.

"I am the efficiency," the doppelganger hissed. "I am the end of the anxiety. I am the clean slate."

"Clean slates get overwritten," Dale said. He looked at Miller. "Miller, what's the penalty for a Class IV infraction?"

Miller was bleeding from his nose. He looked dazed.

"Loss of credential. Immediate termination."

"Apply it," Dale said.

"To who?" Miller asked.

"To the entity," Dale said. He pointed at the doppelganger. "Identify the violation. *Unauthorized Self-Replication.*"

Miller grabbed a pen from his own belt. He scribbled on the back of the tablet's broken casing.

**VIOLATION: UNAUTHORIZED SELF-REPLICATION. PENALTY: TERMINATION.**

The System flashed red.

**VIOLATION ACCEPTED. EXECUTING PROTOCOL: PURGE.**

The doppelganger screamed. Its body dissolved.

Not into dust, but into static.

White noise. It clawed at the air, trying to hold itself together.

Dale backed away. He watched as the thing that wore his face unraveled.

It looked at him one last time.

There was no hatred in its eyes.

Just recognition.

"You'll be next," the doppelganger whispered. Then it was gone.

The street was silent. Miller slumped against the car. He was breathing hard. The black veins had receded, but his face was gray.

Dale looked at his clipboard. It was empty. He looked at the bone stylus on the ground.

He picked it up. It was cold now.

Dead.

"Miller," Dale said. "We need a new witness."

Miller looked up. His eyes were clear, but terrified.

"The Authority is gone, Dale.

You saw it.

You erased it."

"No," Dale said.

He looked at the spot where the doppelganger had been. The asphalt was cracked, but the distortion was fading.

"The Authority isn't gone.

It's just... paused. Waiting for the report.

"

He opened his Field Guide. The entry for the Doppelganger was still blank. But the weakness was listed. *Logical Contradiction.*

Dale wrote in his own Field Guide. He used his own pen.

**SUBJECT: SELF-REFLECTION ENTITY. STATUS: TERMINATED. THOROUGHNESS: 8.5.**

The System pinged.

**POINTS AWARDED: 40.** **CURRENT TOTAL: 12,450.** **CERTIFICATION LEVEL: II (PROGRESS TO III: 98%)**

Miller looked at Dale.

"You got points? For killing yourself?"

"For exterminating an infestation," Dale corrected.

He straightened his uniform, and he tucked the clipboard under his arm.

"Now, we need to find the source of the Rift.

Because that thing wasn't the first error. It was just the latest one.

"

Miller stood up. He swayed.

"How do we find it?

The sensors are dead. The grid is down.

"

Dale looked at the radio in the car. It was silent now. But the frequency dial was still spinning. Slowly.

"Miller," Dale said. "What frequency is it on?"

Miller squinted.

"It's... it's not a number. It's a word."

"What word?"

Miller listened. His eyes went wide.

"It's saying... *Help*."

Dale felt a chill that had nothing to do with the weather.

He looked at the other end of the street. In the darkness, shadows were moving.

Dozens of them, and they weren't monsters. They were people.

But they were all wearing hazmat suits. And they were all looking at him.

Miller grabbed Dale's arm.

"They're not survivors, Dale. They're the previous failed technicians."

Dale raised his spray can. It was empty. He raised his clipboard.

"Protocol," Dale said. "Isolate. Document. Eliminate."

The shadows walked.
