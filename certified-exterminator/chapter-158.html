Chapter 158: Clipboard Shield

# Chapter 158

The neighbor watched through the peephole.

Brenda from unit 4B pressed her forehead against the cold metal. She saw Dale standing in the hallway.

He was not running.

He was not screaming. He held a clipboard like a shield.

To Brenda, Dale looked like a statue. A mad, salt-crusted statue.

She saw the Rift-Walker lunge. She saw the creature's chitinous legs blur into the air. She expected blood. She expected a wet, tearing sound that would haunt her dreams.

Instead, Dale stepped left.

He didn't dodge. He sidestepped. It was a small movement, precise and practiced. The creature's claw missed his jacket by an inch.

Dale swung the clipboard.

It wasn't a weapon.

It was a tool. The edge of the clipboard caught the creature's sensory antenna.

The plastic cracked.

The creature shrieked, a sound like grinding glass, and recoiled.

Brenda blinked. She rubbed her eyes. She looked again.

Dale was writing.

Even now, with a six-foot nightmare thrashing in the kitchen doorway, Dale was writing. His pen scratched against the paper. *Crack. Scratch. Crack.*

Brenda felt a sudden, sharp nausea. It wasn't the monsters.

It was the normalcy.

The sheer, terrifying banality of his actions.

He was treating an eldritch horror like a cockroach infestation in a rental property. And worse, he was winning.

She pulled away from the peephole. Her hands shook. She looked at her own hands, stained with the dust of a broken vase.

She grabbed her phone. No signal.

She went to the window.

The street was chaos. Cars overturned. People running.

But the front door of the duplex was sealed.

Dale had sealed it. He had used packing tape and newspaper to plug the gaps before the first vent burst.

He had prepared for a breach. Not a battle. A breach.

Brenda realized then that Dale wasn't a hero. He was a contractor. And he was doing the job.

***

Dale's vision narrowed.

The System alert flashed red in his peripheral vision. **MANUAL OVERRIDE ACCEPTED.**

The text burned white against the dark background of his mind.

**PENALTY: LICENSE REVOCATION. IMMEDIATE.** **STATUS: UNLICENSED EXTERMINATOR (PROVISIONAL).** **ACCESS TO PROTOCOLS: LOCKED.**

Dale ignored the text.

He ignored the fact that his career was over. He ignored the fact that he had just signed a contract with the void.

He looked at the Rift-Walker.

It was confused.

The creature had expected resistance, or fear. It had not expected paperwork. It twitched its head, tasting the air again.

The salt residue was still on its legs. It hated the salt.

It hated the violation.

Dale backed up.

One step. Two steps.

He kept his eyes on the creature. He kept his pen ready.

The other vents were silent. For now.

The creature advanced. Slowly. Testing the boundaries.

Dale reached the kitchen threshold. He kicked the door shut. Not to keep the creature out, but to contain the scene. To create a workspace.

Inside the kitchen, the two tenants were huddled behind the island. They were pale. One was crying silently. The other was holding a knife with white knuckles.

"Get behind the fridge," Dale said. His voice was flat. Calm.

The man with the knife hesitated. "It's coming in."

"I know where it's coming in," Dale said. "Move."

The man moved.

Dale turned back to the window.

The glass was cracked, but the frame was intact. The Rift-Walker was outside.

It was tapping on the glass with its claws. *Tap. Tap. Tap.*

Dale picked up a roll of duct tape from the counter.

He walked to the window.

The creature saw him. It paused. It tilted its head.

Dale didn't flinch. He applied the tape to the cracks in the glass.

Horizontal strips.

Vertical strips. He pressed them down with his thumb. Smoothing the air bubbles.

The creature raised a claw to smash the glass.

Dale looked up.

"Structural integrity compromised," he said. "Perimeter breach imminent."

The creature struck.

The glass shattered inward.

Dale didn't retreat.

He threw the remaining salt at the shards. The mixture of salt and glass dust flew into the creature's sensory pits.

It screamed.

It stumbled back, slipping on the wet, salty debris.

Dale grabbed the vacuum cleaner.

It was an industrial shop-vac, borrowed from the garage earlier that morning. He plugged it in. The cord stretched taut across the linoleum.

He flipped the switch.

The machine roared.

Dale pointed the nozzle at the creature's legs.

He wasn't trying to suck it up. He was trying to clear the floor. He was trying to remove the friction.

The creature stepped forward, slipping. Its legs splayed. It fell.

Dale moved in.

He hit the creature with the vacuum hose. Not a strike. A distraction. The noise confused its auditory sensors.

He swung the vacuum body. It connected with the creature's thorax. The chitin cracked. Black fluid sprayed onto the cabinets.

Dale didn't stop. He adjusted the nozzle. He switched to the crevice tool.

He aimed for the gap between the creature's legs and the wall.

**FIELD GUIDE UPDATE:** *Rift-Walker (Adult). Weakness: Sensory overload via high-frequency vibration. Secondary weakness: Joint lubrication failure under particulate interference.*

Dale hadn't documented this. He had improvised.

The System flickered. A new window appeared.

**UNAUTHORIZED FIELD NOTE DETECTED.** **REWARD: +50 TREATMENT POINTS (BONUS FOR OBSERVATION).** **PENALTY: FIELD GUIDE CORRUPTION (24 HOURS).**

The text vanished.

Dale felt a sharp headache. The knowledge of the creature's biology faded from his mind, replaced by a fuzzy gray static. He had corrupted his own data. He had used a tool for the wrong purpose.

It didn't matter.

The creature was down. It was thrashing, trying to stand, but the salt in its joints made its movements sluggish. It tried to rise.

Dale pulled the vacuum's cord.

The plug ripped from the outlet.

The machine died.

Silence returned to the kitchen.

The tenants stared at him. The man with the knife dropped it. It clattered on the floor.

Dale looked at the creature. It was twitching. Dying.

He checked his watch.

Four minutes.

He pulled out his pen. He wrote on the clipboard.

*Incident Report: Unit 3A.* *Time: 14:32.* *Action: Emergency Containment.* *Result: Subject Neutralized.* *Damage: Moderate structural, minor cosmetic.* *Status: Pending Inspection.*

He closed the clipboard.

He looked at the tenants.

"Who called the Authority?"

The woman spoke first. Her voice was raspy. "Who?"

"The Licensing Authority," Dale said. "Did you see a notification?"

"No," she said. "We saw the monsters."

"Then you're unlicensed," Dale said. "You're contaminants."

He walked to the fridge. He opened it.

He took a bottle of water.

He drank half of it.

Then he poured the rest over the creature's head.

It was a symbolic gesture. A final act of sanitation.

***

The phone rang.

It wasn't a cell phone. It was the landline in the hallway. The one that hadn't worked in ten years.

Dale froze.

The ringtone was shrill. Mechanical. It cut through the silence like a blade.

The tenants didn't move. They were too shocked to move.

Dale stared at the phone.

It was an old rotary model. Brown plastic. Dusty.

It rang again.

Dale walked to the hallway. He picked up the receiver.

He didn't say hello.

A voice spoke. It was smooth. Bored. Administrative.

"Mr. Kowalski."

Dale gripped the phone. His knuckles turned white.

"This is Unlicensed Actor Dale Kowalski," Dale said.

"You have violated Protocol 7.4," the voice said. "Unlicensed execution of a Class-3 threat. Immediate license revocation is now in effect."

Dale closed his eyes. He exhaled slowly.

"I accept the penalty," Dale said.

"You are not finished with the penalty," the voice said.

Dale opened his eyes.

"Excuse me?"

"Revocation is a state, Mr. Kowalski. It is not an end. It is a transfer."

Dale looked at the hallway. The vents were sealed with tape. The creature was dead in the kitchen. The house was quiet.

"Transfer?" Dale asked.

"Your file has been flagged," the voice said. "Thoroughness Rating: 9.8. Speed: Critical. Documentation: Minimal."

Dale frowned. 9.8.

He had never scored that high. He usually hovered around 8.5.

"Why?" Dale asked.

"Because you didn't kill it with a spell," the voice said. "You didn't kill it with a sword. You killed it with a vacuum cleaner and salt. That is a new category."

Dale felt a chill crawl up his spine.

"A new category," he repeated.

"We call it 'Sanctioned Negligence'," the voice said. "You are now eligible for the Sub-Basement Track."

Dale's breath caught.

The Sub-Basement Track was a rumor. A whisper in the pest control forums. A place for technicians who had failed the written exams, who had been fired for excessive caution, or who had gone too far.

"What is the Sub-Basement Track?" Dale asked.

"It is where we send people who know how to clean up what no one else can see," the voice said.

A door opened.

Not in the house.

In Dale's vision.

A black door appeared in the center of his field of view. It hovered over the dead Rift-Walker. It had no handle. It had no knob.

It was open.

Inside the door, Dale saw darkness. And movement.

Shapes. Hundreds of shapes.

They were not monsters. They were paperwork. Stacks of files.

Mountains of reports. They were alive.

They were crawling.

"This is your new workspace," the voice said. "Your license is gone. But your tools remain."

Dale looked at the clipboard in his hand.

It was warm.

The ink on the page was moving. The words *Pending Inspection* were rewriting themselves.

They now read: *Assignment: Unit Zero.*

"Go through the door," the voice said. "Or stay here and wait for the next wave."

Dale looked at the tenants.

They were safe. The vents were sealed. The house was clean.

He looked at the black door.

He looked at the vacuum cleaner.

He picked up the vacuum hose.

He walked toward the door.

He did not look back.

***

Dale stepped through.

The air was cold. Dry. It smelled of ozone and old paper.

He was standing in a vast, circular room. The walls were lined with filing cabinets that stretched up into the darkness.

Hundreds of them. Thousands.

In the center of the room stood a desk.

A man sat at the desk.

He wore a suit that was too large for him. His skin was gray. His eyes were missing. In their place were small, spinning dials.

The man looked up.

He held a stamp.

"Mr. Kowalski," the man said.

Dale dropped the vacuum hose. He straightened his jacket.

"I'm here for the job," Dale said.

The man smiled. It was a jagged, broken smile.

"You're here for the audit," the man said.

He slammed the stamp onto a piece of paper.

**DENIED.**

The word glowed red.

"Your license is revoked," the man said. "You are no longer a technician."

Dale looked at the paper.

"I'm an exterminator," Dale said.

"No," the man said. "You are a liability."

He pointed to the floor.

The floor was made of glass.

Underneath the glass, creatures were moving. Not Rift-Walkers. Smaller things.

Insects made of light and shadow.

They were eating the files in the walls.

"They are consuming the records," the man said. "If the records are gone, the infestations become permanent. They become part of the infrastructure."

Dale looked at the insects.

He recognized them.

They were silverfish.

But they were huge. And they were glowing.

"They are eating the history," the man said. "And you have no authority to stop them."

Dale looked at his clipboard.

He looked at the vacuum hose.

He looked at the man.

"I have the tools," Dale said.

"The tools are licensed to a dead man," the man said.

Dale thought about the salt. He thought about the duct tape. He thought about the 40% debuff for cross-contamination.

He didn't care about the debuff.

He cared about the nest.

He pulled a small bottle from his pocket.

Borax.

It was from his personal kit. Not from the Authority.

He sprinkled it on the floor.

The silverfish stopped.

They tasted the borax. They recoiled.

The man at the desk frowned.

"That is an unapproved substance," the man said.

"It's a preservative," Dale said. "It stops decay."

The man's dials spun faster.

"Stop," the man said.

Dale didn't stop.

He sprinkled more borax.

The silverfish screamed. They dissolved.

The man stood up.

He was tall. Too tall. His neck stretched as he rose.

"You are interfering with the Archive," the man said.

"I am cleaning the Archive," Dale said.

He looked at the man.

"Where is the source?" Dale asked.

The man pointed to a door behind him. A red door.

"It is behind the red door," the man said. "But if you open it, you will be erased from the system."

Dale looked at the red door.

He looked at the man.

He tapped his pen on the clipboard.

"Violation," he said. "Obstruction of sanitation services."

He walked toward the red door.

The man did not stop him.

The man just watched.

And smiled.

Dale put his hand on the handle.

It was hot.

He turned it.

The door swung open.

Darkness spilled out.

And from the darkness, a voice spoke.

It was his own voice.

"Welcome back, Dale," the voice said.

Dale froze.

He recognized the tone. It was the tone he used when he was tired. When he was done with the day. When he wanted to go home.

"Who are you?" Dale whispered.

"I'm the part of you that wants to quit," the voice said. "I'm the part that knows this isn't real."

Dale stepped into the darkness.

The door slammed shut behind him.

The lights went out.
