Chapter 299: Filing Cabinet Eats Him Alive

# Chapter 299

The citation form was bleeding.

Blue ink spread across the waterproof laminate, pooling in the crease where Dale's thumb had pressed too hard against the paper. The cheap plastic pen had finally given up its ghost, leaking a slow, viscous river of dye over the line marked *VIOLATION CODE: UNLICENSED DIMENSIONAL INTRUSION*. Dale didn't care about the stain. He cared about the timestamp.

04:12 AM.

The water in the tunnel had risen another three inches. It wasn't just rising; it was breathing. Rhythmic, tidal swells pushed against the concrete walls, carrying with them the smell of ozone and rotting kelp. The shadow in the lower chamber hadn't moved, but the water around it had begun to boil, though no heat source touched it.

"Flashlight," Dale said, not looking up from the clipboard.

Sarah's hands shook as she handed it over. The beam cut a weak cone through the murky air, illuminating the swirling silt.

The Auditor's suit remained pristine, a stark white against the grime.

No water touched it. The liquid seemed to curve away from the fabric, repelled by some invisible barrier.

"You can't write a citation on a person," Sarah whispered. Her voice cracked. "Dale, that's not a monster. That's... that's a guy in a suit."

"Regulation 44-C," Dale muttered, scribbling furiously.

The pen sputtered, skipping lines.

"Any entity operating within municipal boundaries without a Class-IV Dimensional Permit is subject to immediate cessation of activity and financial penalty.

Doesn't matter if they have a pulse.

Doesn't matter if they have a soul. It matters if they have a license.

"

A notification pinged in his peripheral vision. Red text.

**SYSTEM ALERT: FIELD GUIDE CORRUPTION.** **CURRENT STATUS: INACTIVE.** **PENALTY: IDENTIFICATION BONUSES DISABLED.**

Dale ignored it. The Field Guide was gone, lost when the Authority suspended his certification. But the rules of the Authority didn't require a guide. They required procedure.

He ripped the top sheet off the clipboard. The paper came away with a wet tear. He used the back of the sheet, writing in large, block letters.

*SUBJECT: UNIDENTIFIED ENTITY, CLASS UNKNOWN.* *ACTION: CEASE AND DESIST.* *FINE: 500 TREATMENT POINTS.*

He held the page up. The ink smeared further, turning the words into a blurry mess.

The Auditor laughed. It wasn't a loud laugh. It was dry, like paper rubbing together.

*"You're improvising, Technician. You have no data.

You have no weakness.

You have no protocol.

What makes you think you can enforce a code on me?

"*

"Because I'm filling out the paperwork," Dale said. He stepped forward, the water lapping at his shins. "And you're standing in a restricted work zone."

The shadow shifted. A hand emerged from the dark, holding a briefcase. It opened the lid. Inside, rows of glass vials glowed with a sickly green light.

*"I am here to conduct an inspection,"* the Auditor said. *"Your previous reports were flagged for 'Excessive Thoroughness.' You spent too much time documenting the broodmother's nesting habits. You slowed down the clearance rate. You are inefficient."*

Dale stopped walking. The water rose to his knees.

"Efficiency," Dale said, his voice flat. "Is not the metric for public safety. It's the metric for profit margins. And since when does the Authority care about profit margins?"

*"Since the backlog reached critical mass."* The Auditor stepped closer. The water parted around his shoes. *"You killed the broodmother. Good.

But you left the eggs. You documented them, yes.

But you didn't destroy them.

You filed them under 'Pending.' That is a violation of the Containment Protocol.

"*

Dale froze, and he looked at the dark water. He looked at the broken pen. He looked at the red notification blinking in his eye.

The eggs.

He had identified them. He had counted them. He had logged their location in the crawlspace before the tunnel collapsed. He had marked them as 'Secure, Pending Destruction.'

He hadn't destroyed them.

Because he hadn't had the right spray.

**SYSTEM NOTIFICATION:** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 4.2 (DECLINING).** **CRITICAL ERROR: INFESTATION PROPAGATION DETECTED.** **NEW THREAT CLASS: CLASS-3 HYBRID.**

The water churned. Not from the Auditor's presence. From beneath.

Small, chitinous shapes broke the surface.

Hundreds of them. They weren't rats.

They were crabs, but wrong.

Their shells were made of translucent, bubbling flesh, and their claws snapped with the force of hydraulic presses. They scuttled onto the tunnel walls, defying gravity, their legs clicking against the concrete.

Sarah screamed. A claw snapped at her head.

Dale kicked out, his boot connecting with the creature.

It burst.

Green slime sprayed across his pant leg.

It burned.

"Acid-based," Dale said. He looked at the Auditor. "You planted them."

*"We seeded them,"* the Auditor corrected. *"To test your adaptability. You failed. You treated a Class-1 problem with a Class-1 solution. You didn't anticipate the mutation."*

The Auditor closed the briefcase.

"Now. As an Inspector, I am placing you under immediate suspension for failure to eradicate a Class-3 threat.

Your license is revoked. Permanently.

"

The red text in Dale's vision exploded.

**LICENSE REVOKED.** **ACCESS DENIED.** **NO PROTOCOLS AVAILABLE.**

Dale felt the familiar drop in his gut. The loss of structure. The loss of the rules. Without the Authority's system, he was just a man in a flooded basement with a clipboard.

But he still had the clipboard, and he looked at the crabs.

They were moving in a swarm. Not chaotic. Organized.

They moved in waves, pushing toward the upper ladder.

Toward the surface. Toward the residential district above.

If they got out, the infestation would become a regional event. The Authority would send a Sweep Team. They would burn the whole block.

Dale looked at the water. He looked at the electrical panel on the far wall. It was old, rusted, and half-submerged.

"Sarah," Dale said. "Do you still have the pliers?"

Sarah nodded, her face pale. She held up the wire cutters.

"Cut the main feed," Dale said.

"That'll kill the lights," Sarah said.

"No.

It'll short the circuit.

" Dale looked at the Auditor. "You're standing in water. You're insulated.

But the wiring isn't.

"

*"You cannot damage municipal infrastructure,"* the Auditor warned. *"That is a felony."*

"I'm not damaging it," Dale said. "I'm performing an emergency shutdown to prevent electrical contamination of a biological hazard."

He ripped the citation form in half. He took the top half—the one with the fine—and stuck it to his chest. The bottom half, he folded into a tight square and shoved into his pocket.

"Cut it," Dale ordered.

Sarah hesitated. Then she lunged.

The wire cutters bit into the rubber casing of the main cable. Sparks showered the tunnel. The lights flickered, then died. The tunnel plunged into darkness, save for the glow of the Auditor's briefcase and the bioluminescent green of the crab-shell creatures.

The Auditor didn't move. He stood in the dark, perfectly still.

*"You have blinded us,"* the voice said. *"Now you cannot see your enemy.

You cannot document them.

You cannot identify them. Without identification, you have no bonus damage.

You have no protocols. You have nothing.

"*

"I have a code violation," Dale said.

He moved.

It wasn't a hero's move. It was a pest control move. He didn't attack the Auditor. He attacked the environment.

He grabbed the rusted pipe running along the wall. He slammed his shoulder into the valve wheel.

It was seized, and he kicked it. Once. Twice.

The metal shrieked.

"Sarah! The flashlight! Aim it at the water!"

The beam cut through the black. It revealed the crabs. They were clustered around the electrical panel. The acid slime from the exploded crab had dripped onto the exposed wiring.

Dale saw the connection point. A single strand of copper, bare and dripping with green acid.

He didn't have a weapon. He had the clipboard.

He swung the clipboard. The hard plastic edge cracked against the pipe. The valve spun loose.

Water gushed from the broken pipe, mixing with the flood. The pressure surged.

The Auditor stepped back, his suit still dry. *

"Pointless. You are flooding your own workspace. You have violated safety protocols."*

"I'm flushing the system," Dale said.

The water level rose faster now. The current caught the crabs. They weren't strong swimmers. The sheer force of the gushing pipe washed them back into the lower chamber.

But the Auditor didn't flush.

The Auditor reached into his pocket. He pulled out a card. It glowed blue.

*"I am authorizing the use of Class-9 Sanitation,"* the Auditor said. *"This area will be sterilized. Everyone in it. Including you."*

The air pressure dropped. Ears popped. The water vibrated.

Dale looked at his chest. The citation form was peeling off. The ink was running completely. The words were gone.

He looked at Sarah. She was clinging to a railing, eyes wide.

"Dale," she said. "What do we do?"

Dale looked at the Auditor. He looked at the vibrating water. He looked at the empty space where his Field Guide used to be.

He remembered the rule. *Killing a monster without documentation yields zero points.*

But what if you didn't kill the monster?

What if you documented the Auditor?

Dale pulled the folded square of paper from his pocket. The citation for *Unlicensed Dimensional Intrusion*. He unfolded it. It was blank, smeared, useless.

Unless he wrote something new, and he uncapped the broken pen. It was empty. No ink.

He looked at the green slime on his boot. He looked at the slime on the water.

He dipped the nib of the pen into the green mucus.

It was thick. Sticky. Acidic.

He wrote on the blank citation.

*NAME: THE AUDITOR.* *SPECIES: BUREAUCRATIC PARASITE.* *WEAKNESS: BUREAUCRATIC EGO.*

The pen tore the paper. The green slime stained the words.

A notification flashed. Not red. White.

**FIELD GUIDE ENTRY CREATED.** **SUBJECT: UNVERIFIED ENTITY.** **STATUS: HOSTILE.**

The Auditor paused. The vibration in the air stopped.

*"That is not a valid classification,"* the Auditor said. There was uncertainty in the voice.

"It's a working title," Dale said. "And I'm filing a counter-complaint."

He held up the paper.

"You're interfering with an active extermination operation. You're contaminating the evidence. You're using unlicensed technology to escalate a minor infestation into a catastrophic event."

The Auditor stepped forward. The water rushed around his legs.

*"You have no authority. Your license is revoked."*

"I have a witness," Dale said. He pointed at Sarah.

"And I have a record.

I just created a Field Guide entry for you. That means you're part of the system now. You're not an Inspector.

You're an Infestation.

"

The Auditor went still.

*"That... is a paradox."*

"It's a loophole," Dale said.

"The Authority rewards thoroughness.

I documented you. Now, if I defeat you, I get points.

If you defeat me, you have to fill out the paperwork for a failed inspection. And you hate paperwork.

"

The Auditor's face remained in shadow, but his posture shifted. He looked at the briefcase. He looked at the vials.

For the first time, the Auditor looked hesitant.

"Kill him," the Auditor said to the darkness.

The crabs didn't move. They were still being flushed away by the pipe burst.

"Kill him!" the Auditor shouted.

From the shadows, something else emerged. Not a crab. Not a rat.

A figure. Tall.

Human-shaped.

But its skin was made of the same translucent, bubbling flesh as the crabs. It had no face. Only a mouth, filled with rows of chitinous teeth.

It walked out of the water. It didn't run. It didn't scuttle. It walked with the precise, measured steps of an employee clocking in for a shift.

It raised a hand. The fingers were long, jointed, and tipped with bone-like claws.

The Auditor smiled.

*"Meet your replacement, Technician. This one has been waiting for a valid target."*

Dale looked at the creature. He looked at his pen. The green slime was drying, clogging the nib.

He had one page. One entry. One chance.

He looked at Sarah.

"Take the clipboard," Dale said.

"Dale, no—"

"Take it. Write down the time. Write down the location. Write down everything."

He stepped forward. The creature lunged.

Dale didn't dodge. He reached into his pocket and pulled out the only thing he had left.

His safety inspection pin. A small, metal badge that said *OSHA CERTIFIED*.

He threw it at the creature.

It hit the creature's chest and stuck there. A tiny, reflective shield against the bubbling flesh.

The creature paused. It looked at the pin. It tilted its head.

Then it looked at Dale.

Dale smiled. A dry, procedural smile.

"Access denied," Dale said. "You're not certified."

The creature screamed. The sound shattered the remaining light bulbs in the tunnel. Glass rained down on the water.

The Auditor's briefcase opened. A beam of white light shot out, hitting the creature.

The creature dissolved into ash.

The Auditor closed the briefcase.

*"Impressive,"* the Auditor said. *"You used a psychological deterrent against a biological weapon. It worked. Barely."*

Dale stood in the water, breathing hard. His hands shook.

"It worked," Dale said.

*"For now,"* the Auditor said. *"But you are still unlicensed. You are still in violation. And you have just attracted the attention of the High Council."*

The Auditor pointed a finger at Dale.

*"You have one hour to file your final report.

If it is not submitted to the Authority by 05:12 AM, your mind will be erased. You will become part of the infrastructure.

You will be a filing cabinet, Dale.

Forever."*

The Auditor turned. He walked into the darkness.

"Your license is not suspended," the voice echoed. "It is being audited."

Dale looked at Sarah. She was holding the clipboard, pen poised, tears streaming down her face.

04:15 AM.

Fifty-seven minutes.

Dale looked at the wet, green-stained paper in his hand. He looked at the ash where the creature had been.

He had to write a report. A full report. Identification, documentation, isolation, elimination, verification.

He had eliminated the creature. He had documented the Auditor.

But he hadn't verified the job.

Because the Auditor was still alive.

And the Auditor was coming back.

Dale uncapped the pen. He looked at the green slime on his fingers.

He needed ink, and he looked at the broken electrical panel. The wires were still sparking. Small arcs of blue electricity jumped between the copper strands.

Dale grabbed a loose wire.

He held the tip of the pen against the spark.

The nib heated up. The plastic melted. But the metal tip glowed red.

He touched the glowing tip to the paper.

It burned.

Brown, charred letters appeared on the white page.

*REPORT: INCIDENT 299.* *STATUS: PENDING.*

He had to write it fast. Before the ink ran out. Before the time ran out.

Before the filing cabinet ate him alive.

Dale wrote.
