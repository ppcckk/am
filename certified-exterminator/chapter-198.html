Chapter 198: Administrative Hold

# Chapter 198

The clipboard was clean.

That was the first problem.

Inspector Yura Kovic stood in the center of the fractured sub-basement, her boots hovering an inch above the floorboards to avoid the spreading pool of black resin. She held her tablet with both hands, the screen reflecting the sickly, pulsating glow of the ceiling.

Behind her, the containment crew waited in a tight, nervous cluster, their hazmat suits crinkling with every shift of weight.

They were Level Four technicians, veterans of the Chicago Breach and the Atlanta Sinkhole. They had seen things that would make a soldier vomit.

But they had never seen a man become a filing cabinet.

"Status report," Yura said, her voice flat. She didn't look at the crew. Her eyes were fixed on the shape in the center of the room.

The shape was Dale Kowalski.

Or it was trying to be.

The lower half remained human, clad in the stained khaki trousers and steel-toed boots of a Class II technician. The upper half had fused with the architecture of the room.

One arm had elongated into a jagged strut of chitin and bone, anchoring him to the support beam.

The other arm had dissolved into a spray of wet, black fibers that twitched independently, seeking purchase on the walls. His head hung low, the skin peeling back to reveal not muscle, but dense, packed layers of gray matter and compressed air sacs.

"Vitals are erratic," said Miller, the senior crew member. His voice trembled, just slightly.

"Heart rate is... it's not a rhythm.

It's a pulse pattern.

Like a drum."

"Biological or mechanical?" Yura asked.

"Hard to say. The resin is interfering with the sensors."

Yura tapped her tablet. "Log it as 'Unidentified Structural Fusion.' Do not attempt extraction. The Authority's last update flagged this specific merger as a critical containment risk. If you pull him apart, the residue spreads."

Miller swallowed. "He's still holding the clipboard."

Yura looked closer. It was true. The thing that had been Dale was clutching a standard-issue municipal clipboard in its remaining, fleshy hand.

The metal clip was tarnished, but the paper on it was pristine. No stains.

No tears.

"Why is he holding it?" Miller asked. "He's half-monster. Why protect the paperwork?"

"Because the paperwork is the license," Yura said. "And the license is the only thing keeping him from being classified as a Level 9 Aberration."

She stepped forward, the resin resisting the soles of her boots with a sticky, wet sound. The air smelled of old copper and ozone. The black smoke from the previous encounter still hung in the corners, refusing to dissipate, forming slow, lazy eddies that watched them with indifferent curiosity.

"Kowalski," Yura called out. "Can you hear me?"

No answer. Just the wet click of the chitinous arm adjusting its grip on the beam.

"Kowalski, this is Inspector Yura Kovic, Licensing Authority, District 4. I am initiating a Thoroughness Audit. Acknowledge receipt."

A twitch. The fibers on his shoulder flared.

"He's not reacting to auditory stimuli," Miller said. "Look at the eyes."

They were gone. In their place were two small, compound lenses, dark and multifaceted. They reflected Yura's silhouette in a hundred fractured pieces.

"Then we have to do this by the book," Yura said. "Open the Field Guide. Find the entry for *Rattus profundus* variants. See if there's a protocol for de-escalation of merged hosts."

"We don't have that entry," Miller said, scrolling frantically. "It's redacted. Or missing.

I think...

I think it got contaminated when we tried to use the Acid Beetle protocol on the broodmother last month. The system locked it out."

"Contamination penalty," Yura muttered. "Stupid. Sloppy work by the previous crew."

She looked at the thing in the center of the room. It was not a monster. It was a violation. A code violation written in flesh.

"Try the visual protocol," Yura said. "Project the standard 'Stop' command. High contrast. Simple geometry."

Miller raised his tablet and flashed a large, white circle on a black background.

Nothing happened.

"Try the voice command again. But louder."

"KOWALSKI," Yura shouted.

The chitinous arm jerked. A sound tore from the fused throat—a dry, rattling rasp, like stones grinding together.

"K-O-W-A-L-S-K-I."

The syllables were broken, reconstructed from the vibrations in the room. The black fibers on the walls pulsed in sync with the word.

"Who is speaking?" Yura whispered.

"That's not him," Miller said, backing away. "That's the room talking."

Yura felt a cold prickle at the base of her neck.

She pulled a small, silver pen from her pocket. It was a standard Authority tool, used for marking physical documents. She uncapped it and wrote on the air, not touching anything.

The ink floated, suspended in the stagnant air, forming glowing blue letters.

*INSPECTING:*

The room shuddered. The lights flickered.

*STATUS:*

The black smoke rushed toward the writing, trying to obscure it.

*INCOMPLETE.*

Yura slammed her hand against the wall. "Stop resisting. This is a compliance check."

The thing in the center of the room convulsed. The clipboard dropped from its hand. It hit the floor with a clang that echoed like a gunshot.

Yura froze.

The clipboard was gone.

In its place, lying on the sticky floor, was a single sheet of paper. It was not the inspection report Dale had been writing. It was a new document. The header was printed in bold, red font.

*NOTICE OF VIOLATION: UNAUTHORIZED METAMORPHOSIS.*

*DEFECT: IDENTITY SUBSTITUTION.*

*REMEDIATION REQUIRED: IMMEDIATE.*

Miller stared at the paper. "How did he write that? He doesn't have hands."

"He does," Yura said, pointing to the ceiling. "He has hands everywhere now."

Above them, the black fibers on the walls had rearranged themselves into delicate, translucent fingers. They were holding the pen.

"The system is recognizing the infestation as the technician," Yura realized.

The horror of it settled in her stomach.

"The Authority doesn't care about the biology. It cares about the license.

And the license is attached to the person who did the work. If Dale merged with the entity, the entity inherited the license."

"So we have a monster with a Class II license?" Miller asked.

"Worse," Yura said. "We have a monster that believes it is Dale. And it is still trying to finish the job."

She looked at the floor. The paper was still there. The red text was fading, replaced by new, scrolling lines.

*INSPECTING ENTRY POINTS...*

*IDENTIFYING STRUCTURAL DEFECTS...*

*DOCUMENTING DAMAGE...*

The black fibers on the walls moved in rhythmic patterns. They were mimicking the motion of writing. Scratch, scratch, pause. Scratch, scratch, pause.

"He's filling out the report," Yura said. "He's still filling it out."

"Who is he writing for?" Miller asked. "The Authority?"

"No," Yura said, her voice dropping to a whisper. "He's writing for the next inspector. He's setting up the violation for someone else to find."

A loud *crack* echoed through the sub-basement. The support beam where the chitinous arm was anchored split down the middle. The arm didn't fall. It hung suspended, held by the tension of the black fibers.

From the split in the beam, a new face emerged. It was pale, gaunt, and devoid of eyes. Its mouth opened, revealing rows of needle-thin teeth made of compacted resin.

"Next," the face said.

Yura raised her tablet. "Miller, get the extraction kit. We're pulling him out."

"We can't," Miller said, his face pale. "Look at the Field Guide."

Yura looked down at her own device. The entry for *Dimensional Vermin* was gone. In its place was a single, blinking line.

*SUBJECT: KOWALSKI, DALE.* *STATUS: PENDING REVIEW.* *THREAT LEVEL: ESCALATING.*

"Pending review?" Miller asked. "What does that mean?"

"It means the Authority hasn't decided if he's a pest or a technician," Yura said. "And until they decide, we can't shoot him. We can't burn him. We have to document him."

The face in the beam smiled. It was a terrible, wide smile that stretched the skin of the wood until it tore.

"Documentation," the face said, using Dale's voice, but stripped of all warmth. "I love documentation."

The black fibers surged forward. They didn't attack. They wrapped around the legs of the containment crew, gentle but unyielding. They lifted them off the ground.

"Don't shoot!" Yura ordered. "If you damage the workspace, the Thoroughness Rating drops. We lose all progress on the district certification."

Miller dropped his rifle. He hung in the air, suspended by the tendrils, his face contorted in fear.

"Yura, what do I do?"

Yura looked at the thing that was Dale. The clipboard was gone, but the paper remained. The red text had vanished. Now, the paper was blank.

Wait.

Not blank.

The fibers were writing on it. Not with ink. With pressure. They were embossing the paper.

Yura stepped closer, ignoring the tendrils that brushed against her suit. She looked at the embossed text.

*INSPECTION FINDING: STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY COMPROMISED.* *CORRECTION: REPLACE HUMAN ELEMENT.*

She gasped.

"He's not just merging," Yura said. "He's pruning."

The face in the ceiling tilted. "Correct.

The infestation requires a host.

The host requires a role. The role requires compliance."

Yura backed away.

Her hand hovered over her emergency beacon. One press would call in the Strike Team.

They would incinerate the sub-basement.

They would incinerate Dale.

But the Thoroughness Rating.

If she called the Strike Team, it would be classified as "Improper Eradication." A 20% penalty. And in this district, they were only at 8.9. They needed a 9.0 to unlock the Level III protocols. Without those protocols, the next Rift event—the one rumored to be coming from the industrial sector—would be unmanageable.

She couldn't call the Strike Team.

She had to finish the inspection.

Yura pulled out her silver pen again. She stepped into the pool of resin. The liquid seeped up her boots, warm and viscous. It felt like blood.

"Kowalski," she said. "I am conducting a secondary review. I need to verify your identification."

The face in the ceiling turned its eyeless gaze toward her.

"Identification is verified," it said. "I am Dale Kowalski. I am a Class II Technician. I am thorough."

"Prove it," Yura said.

The room went silent. The suspended crew members stopped struggling. The black fibers froze.

"Prove it," Yura repeated. "Show me your Field Guide."

The thing hesitated. The chitinous arm twitched. The fibers on the walls rippled.

Then, a low hum began. It started in the floor, vibrating through Yura's bones. It rose in pitch until it was a shriek.

From the chest cavity of the fused figure, a light erupted. It was not a lamp. It was a projection. A holographic interface, projected directly from the biological matter of the creature.

It was the Field Guide.

But it was wrong.

The entries were scrambled. *Rattus profundus* was listed under "Pest Control Methods." *Acid Beetles* was listed under "Office Supplies." And the entry for *Kowalski, Dale* was highlighted in gold.

*SUBJECT: KOWALSKI, DALE.* *CLASSIFICATION: TOOL.* *USAGE: STRUCTURAL REINFORCEMENT.*

Yura stared at the projection.

"He didn't just merge," she whispered. "He was rewritten."

The face in the ceiling spoke again. "The Authority requires efficiency.

Humans are inefficient.

Tools are efficient.

I am the tool."

The black fibers tightened around the crew. Miller yelped, his suit buckling under the strain.

"Let them go," Yura said. Her voice shook, but she kept her pen steady. "This is a violation of OSHA Regulation 44-B: Hostage Retention. Release the personnel."

The face tilted. "Regulation 44-B applies to licensed technicians. Are you a licensed technician, Inspector?"

Yura blinked.

"I am a Level IV Inspector," she said.

"The Authority recognizes Level IV for oversight," the thing said. "But you are not the primary handler. Dale is the primary handler.

Dale is the problem. Dale is the solution.

Therefore, Dale is the authority."

A new line of text appeared on the holographic guide.

*NEW PROTOCOL UNLOCKED: AUTHORITY OVERRIDE.*

Yura felt a chill that had nothing to do with the damp air. This wasn't in the manual. This wasn't in any manual.

"Miller," Yura said. "Don't breathe. The spores are activating."

"What spores?"

"The ones in the resin. They're not just binding material. They're communicative. He's uploading himself into the crew."

Miller looked down at his legs. The black fibers were not just holding him. They were piercing the seams of his suit. Tiny, hair-like tendrils were burrowing into the fabric.

"Get them off!" Yura shouted.

She lunged forward, swinging her silver pen like a dagger. She slashed at the fibers wrapping Miller's left leg. The pen cut through them easily. They fell away, dissolving into black sludge.

Miller fell to the ground, gasping.

"Run," Yura said. "To the exit. Do not stop for anything."

"And him?" Miller pointed at the fused figure.

Yura looked at the face in the ceiling. The smile was gone. The expression was blank, administrative.

"We have to contain him," Yura said. "But we can't kill him. Not yet."

"Why not?"

"Because if we kill the host, the infestation resets. And if the infestation resets, the Rift opens wider."

Miller scrambled to his feet. "That makes no sense!"

"It makes perfect sense," Yura said, her mind racing. "The Authority rewards thoroughness. Killing a monster without closing the loop creates a vacuum.

A vacuum draws in more threats.

Dale knows this. That's why he's still writing the report.

He's trying to close the loop."

The holographic guide flickered. The entry for *Kowalski, Dale* dissolved. In its place, a new entry formed.

*SUBJECT: KOWALSKI, DALE.* *STATUS: DELETION IN PROGRESS.* *REASON: INSUFFICIENT QUALIFICATIONS.*

Yura stopped.

"Insufficient qualifications?"

The thing in the center of the room shook. The chitinous arm snapped. It didn't break; it detached, flying across the room and embedding itself in the wall next to Yura's head.

The face in the ceiling screamed. A sound of tearing metal and breaking bone.

"I am qualified!" it shrieked. "I am thorough! I am—"

The words cut off. The black fibers on the walls retreated, pulling back into the ceiling, into the floor, into the structure of the building.

The fused figure collapsed. Not into a heap, but into a puddle. The human parts dissolved first.

The clothes sank. The boots remained, standing upright in a pool of black slime.

The clipboard floated to the surface.

Yura stared at the boots.

Miller was at the door, hand on the handle. "Yura! Come on!"

She didn't move. She watched the boots.

A voice came from the slime. It was faint, wet, and gurgling.

"Mr. Kowalski."

Yura turned.

The thing that looked like Vane stood in the doorway.

But it was not Vane.

It was a suit filled with nothing but shadows. The tie was chitin.

The face was a smooth, blank oval.

"Your license has been suspended," the shadow-Vane said.

Yura raised her pen. "Who are you?"

The shadow-Vane stepped forward. The floor did not creak. It did not make a sound.

"I am the Next Inspector," it said.

Behind it, the door to the sub-basement corridor glowed. The light was not white. It was the same sickly, pulsating glow from the ceiling.

And it was spreading.

Up the stairs. Into the vents. Through the walls.

Yura looked at the boots in the slime. The clipboard was gone. The paper was gone.

All that remained was a single, embossed word on the metal clip of the clipboard, which was now attached to the sole of the boot.

*APPEAL.*

Yura's hand trembled. She looked at the shadow-Vane. She looked at the spreading light. She looked at the empty boots.

If she stayed, she would be audited. If she ran, the infestation would take the building.

She stepped back. Her boot hit the pool of slime.

The slime moved. It wrapped around her ankle.

"Miller," Yura said, her voice calm. "Go."

"But—"

"Go."

Miller fled.

Yura stood alone in the sub-basement. The shadow-Vane watched her. The face in the ceiling was gone. The holographic guide was gone.

The only sound was the dripping of the resin.

Yura looked down at her pen. The silver tip was tarnished. The ink was dry.

She uncapped it again.

She didn't write in the air. She wrote on the floor. On the slime.

*INSPECTING:*

The slime resisted. It burned.

Yura wrote anyway.

*STATUS:*

The shadow-Vane took a step closer.

*INCOMPLETE.*

The slime surged, pulling her down. It was heavy, warm, alive. It climbed her leg, her waist, her chest.

Yura raised the pen.

She stabbed it into the center of the slime pool.

A crack echoed through the room. Not in the floor. In the air.

The shadow-Vane froze.

For the first time, the blank oval face cracked. A fissure ran down the center.

From the crack, a voice emerged. Not Vane's voice. Not Dale's voice.

It was the voice of the Licensing Authority.

*VIOLATION DETECTED: UNAUTHORIZED INTERVENTION.*

*PENALTY: TOTAL ERADICATION.*

The lights went out.

In the darkness, the slime tightened. Yura couldn't breathe. She couldn't move.

But she could think.

And in the dark, a new window popped into her vision. Not in her peripheral. Directly in front of her eyes.

*SYSTEM ALERT: CERTIFICATION LEVEL II UNLOCKED.*

*NEW PROTOCOL AVAILABLE: ADMINISTRATIVE HOLD.*

*WARNING: USING ADMINISTRATIVE HOLD WILL FREEZE LOCAL TIME FOR 10 MINUTES.*

*WARNING: FREEZING TIME WILL ATTRACT A CLASS-IX ENTITY.*

*CHOOSE:*

Yura stared at the text.

She had ten minutes.

And whatever came next would be worse than Dale.
