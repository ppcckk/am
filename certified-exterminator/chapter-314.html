Chapter 314: Paperwork from the Pit

# Chapter 314

The clipboard in the doppelgänger's hand was not a prop. It was real.

It held a single sheet of paper, crisp and white, contrasting with the void swirling behind him. The header read *REINTEGRATION REQUEST: KOWALSKI, DALE.* Below it, in neat, bureaucratic handwriting, was a signature.

His own.

Dale didn't move. His armored fingers, thick with chitin and scar tissue, remained locked around the severed power coupling. The silence in the hallway was absolute, save for the low, wet thrum of his own heartbeat against his ribs.

The clean version of himself stepped over the threshold. His shoes were polished. His tie was knotted with geometric precision. He looked like a man who had never spent fourteen years crawling under porches, breathing dust mites, and listening to the skittering of things that should not exist.

"You broke protocol," the clean Dale said. He didn't shout.

He didn't need to.

The Authority's voice resonated in the hollow of Dale's skull, bypassing his ears entirely.

"Class III Intervention. Unauthorized modification of containment infrastructure.

That is a termination offense.

"

Dale glanced at his own hands. The black carapace had spread past his wrists, fusing with the gauntlets he'd worn during the breach. He flexed a finger. The joint clicked, a sound like dry wood snapping.

"Termination implies removal," Dale said. His voice was gravel, scraped raw by the Defoliant fumes. "I'm still here."

"You are a contaminant." The clean Dale tapped his pen against the clipboard. "You have exceeded your Thoroughness Rating.

You have engaged in non-standard elimination methods.

You have altered your own biology to facilitate combat. This is not Pest Control. This is Infestation.

"

A new notification blinked in Dale's peripheral vision. It wasn't the usual gold text of the Field Guide. It was red, jagged, and pulsing with a rhythm that matched his panic.

**STATUS: FLAGGED FOR AUDIT.** **CURRENT LICENSE: SUSPENDED PENDING REVIEW.** **ACTION REQUIRED: SUBMIT TO BIOMETRIC SCAN.**

Dale looked at the void. The black swirling mass was expanding, eating the edges of the hallway wallpaper. The clean Dale stood at the center of it, an anchor to a reality that no longer applied to him.

"Where is the Authority?" Dale asked.

"I am the Authority," the clean Dale replied. He flipped a page on the clipboard.

"Or rather, I am the representative for your immediate removal.

The Authority is a bureaucracy, Dale, which does not care about your survival. It cares about the paperwork.

And your paperwork is a mess.

"

Dale's mind raced. He had spent years navigating municipal codes, health inspectors, and the petty tyrants of the Homeowners Association.

He knew how to stall, and he knew how to file a counter-motion. He knew that a suspended license was not the same as a revoked one.

"I have an open ticket," Dale said.

He pointed a clawed finger at the darkened hallway.

"The infestation in this building is not fully resolved.

I tore out the power coupling to stop the Defoliant spread, which was a necessary containment measure. If you terminate me before the nest is verified, you leave a Class-4 Breach uncontrolled. That is a liability.

"

The clean Dale paused. He looked at the clipboard, then at the dark hallway, then back at Dale. A micro-expression flickered across his face—annoyance, perhaps. Or calculation.

"The nest is dead," the clean Dale said. "My team has already swept the sub-basement."

"Your team didn't document the brood chamber," Dale countered. "Did they verify the exit points? Did they check for secondary colonies in the ventilation shafts?"

The clean Dale's jaw tightened.

"That is not your concern."

"It is my concern. It is the only thing that has ever been my concern." Dale took a step forward. The armored foot crushed a piece of shattered glass. "Show me the report."

The clean Dale hesitated. The void behind him rippled, agitated by the tension.

"Request denied," the clean Dale said. "You are not qualified to review your own replacement's actions."

"Then who is?"

"Me."

Dale smiled. It was a grotesque stretching of flesh, revealing teeth that were too sharp, too numerous. He reached into the pouch on his belt—the one he had kept hidden under his jacket for three years. It contained his personal Field Guide, not the digital one projected by the Authority, but a physical notebook, bound in leather, filled with sketches and notes in his cramped handwriting.

He pulled it out. The notebook was thick, swollen with pages.

"I don't need your report," Dale said. "I have my own."

He opened the book. The pages were dense with ink.

He flipped past the entries for *Rattus profundus*, past the *Acid Beetles*, past the *Chitin Stalkers*.

He stopped at a page near the back, and the paper was stained with something dark and viscous.

The entry was titled: *Authority Representative (Human Variant).*

Dale hadn't written this entry. Not yet. But the Field Guide knew.

The System knew. As Dale stared at the page, the ink bleeded upward, forming new letters.

The clean Dale watched the book with widening eyes.

"That's not possible. You can't document me. I am not an infestation."

"Are you?" Dale asked.

"You stepped out of a void. You wear a suit that doesn't age. You speak with a voice that isn't yours.

And you're holding a clipboard.

In this line of work, anyone who tries to take your job without a license is a pest.

"

The clean Dale raised his hand. A wave of force hit Dale, knocking him back against the wall. The armor absorbed the impact, cracking along the shoulder plates.

"Stop this," the clean Dale said. His voice lost its smoothness, taking on a static edge. "You are confusing classification with metaphor."

"It's not a metaphor," Dale said, pushing himself up. He felt the System shifting around him, the air growing thick with the scent of ozone and old paper. "It's a loophole."

He had learned it in Chapter 305, during the Waterworks Incident. The System allowed for *Self-Identification*. If a technician could prove that a threat posed a risk to their professional integrity, they could classify it as a *Counter-Infestation*. It was a rare, obscure rule used by Level IV technicians who had been corrupted by their work.

Dale wasn't corrupted. He was thorough.

He pointed at the clean Dale.

"Field Guide Update. Target: Imposter. Species: Mimicry Class Alpha.

Weakness: Dependency on Procedural Validation.

"

The System accepted the entry. A golden glow surrounded Dale's notebook.

**NEW WEAKNESS IDENTIFIED.** **TARGET DAMAGED: CONFIDENCE -30%.**

The clean Dale stumbled. He looked down at his hands, which were trembling. The polished shoes scuffed against the floor.

"I am not a mimic," the clean Dale whispered. "I am you. The version of you that followed the rules."

"The version of me that didn't get the job done," Dale said. He stepped closer. The void behind the imposter was shrinking, the blackness retreating as Dale's Field Guide asserted its dominance over the narrative.

"I have a Thoroughness Rating of 9.8," Dale said. "Yours is blank. You haven't done anything. You've just waited."

The clean Dale raised his clipboard like a shield.

"I will process you."

"You can't process what you can't document," Dale said.

He reached into his other pouch and pulled out a small, silver canister, which was empty. He had used it to spray the Defoliant.

But the label was still visible. *Class II Insecticide: Broad Spectrum.*

He crushed the canister in his hand. The metal shrieked, deforming under the pressure of his claws.

A mist of residual chemicals sprayed into the air.

It wasn't poison. It was a marker. A chemical signature that the Field Guide recognized as *Contaminant.*

The mist settled on the clean Dale's suit.

**CONTAMINATION DETECTED.** **TARGET STATUS: UNLICENSED.** **ACTION: ELIMINATE.**

The clean Dale screamed. It wasn't a human scream. It was the sound of tearing parchment, of snapping bones, of something trying to hold its shape while reality pulled it apart.

His suit unraveled.

Threads of white fabric flew into the air, dissolving into smoke. His skin peeled back, revealing not muscle, but pages.

Thousands of pages, stacked like layers of skin.

Words written on them, fading and darkening, and he was made of reports.

Dale watched, his heart hammering against his ribs. He didn't feel triumph. He felt the cold, clinical detachment of a technician watching a pest die.

The clean Dale fell to his knees. The void behind him collapsed inward, sucking the pages into itself. The man's face distorted, the features smearing into ink blots.

"You... failed," the imposter gurgled. His voice was no longer Dale's. It was the collective voice of every rejection letter, every code violation, every inspection report that had ever been stamped *DENIED.*

"I didn't fail," Dale said. He pulled his notebook closed. "I completed the inspection."

The imposter dissolved. The last thing to disappear was the clipboard. It hovered in the air for a second, then fell to the floor with a heavy clatter.

Silence returned. The red warning text in Dale's vision vanished, replaced by a calm, gold notification.

**THREAT NEUTRALIZED.** **AUDIT SUSPENDED.** **CERTIFICATION LEVEL: RESTORED.** **NEW ENTRY ADDED: BUREAUCRATIC MIMIC (SPECIES UNKNOWN).**

Dale stood alone in the hallway. The power coupling was still in his hand. The lights were still out.

He walked over to the clipboard. It lay on the floor, untouched by the dissolution. The paper was blank.

He picked it up.

As soon as his fingers brushed the wood of the board, the paper changed. The ink flowed from nowhere, forming words.

*Dear Dale,*

*Your performance in the recent incident has raised several concerns regarding your mental stability and adherence to Safety Protocol 7.*

*But due to the successful elimination of the Mimicry Class Alpha, your license has been reinstated with a probationary note.*

*You are hereby summoned to the Central Processing Center for a mandatory psychological evaluation.*

*Failure to appear will result in permanent revocation.*

*Regards,* *The Licensing Authority.*

Dale read the note three times. He looked at the blank hallway. He looked at his armored hands. He looked at the notebook in his other hand.

He had won. He had exposed the impostor. He had used the System's own rules against it.

But the note didn't mention the void. It didn't mention the team that had swept the sub-basement. It didn't mention why there was a copy of him standing in a hole in the wall.

He flipped the clipboard over. On the back, in handwriting that was not his, and not the Authority's standard font, was a single line.

*They are coming for the others.*

Dale's blood ran cold.

Others, and he thought of Sarah, the paramedic who had helped him seal the sewer gates in Chapter 200. He thought of Marcus, the construction worker who had taught him how to break through concrete without triggering a cave-in. He thought of the other Level II technicians, the ones who had survived the initial breach and were now scattered across the state, fighting infestations that the Authority claimed were minor.

If the Authority was sending copies of him, they were sending copies of them.

Dale dropped the clipboard. He turned and ran.

Not away from the void, but toward the exit.

Toward the outside.

Toward the place where the real world still existed, however fragile it might be.

He burst through the front doors of the building, gasping for air. The night sky was dark, but the stars were moving. They were shifting, rearranging themselves into new constellations, forming shapes that looked like filing cabinets, like grids, like cages.

His phone buzzed in his pocket. He pulled it out.

A text message from an unknown number.

*Check your Field Guide. Page 402. Read it.*

Dale opened the notebook. He flipped past the Mimic entry, past the recent combat notes, to page 402.

It was a blank page.

But as he watched, ink seeped from the edges of the paper. It wasn't forming words. It was forming a map.

A map of the county.

Every location where he had worked in the last six months was marked with a red dot. And next to each dot was a timestamp.

The timestamps were all in the future, and the first one was ten minutes from now.

The location was the elementary school on Maple Street.

Dale looked up. In the distance, the school's bell ringed. It wasn't the normal school bell. It was a low, resonant hum, like the power coupling he had torn out of the wall.

And from the school's basement, a sound emerged. Not a scream. Not a roar.

The sound of a printer warming up.

Dale started running.
