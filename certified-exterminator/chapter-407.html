Chapter 407: Stamp of Hesitation

# Chapter 407

The black veins pulsed against the white fluorescent light of the municipal records center. They throbbed in time with Dale's heartbeat, a rhythmic, wet counterpoint to the sirens wailing outside.

Dale didn't look at them. He looked at the clipboard.

He needed a stamp. Not a digital authorization, not a verbal confirmation from a drone hovering in the corner, but a physical, ink-stamped authorization from the County Clerk's office. The Authority's new directive required a witness. Specifically, it required a Level IV Witness, someone with enough bureaucratic standing to validate the hunt for the Rift Opener.

That person was currently trapped behind a reinforced steel door, holding the line against a swarm of Class-2 Void Wasps that had breached the ventilation system.

Dale tapped the clipboard against his thigh. The arachnids skittered along his forearms, their legs clicking against the skin. They were agitated. They could smell the wasps through the drywall.

*Protocol breach,* the System notification flashed in his peripheral vision. *Unsanctioned Companion Movement. Penalty: -5 Thoroughness Points.*

Dale ignored the notification. He walked toward the door.

The air inside the records center was thick with the smell of old paper and ozone. Dale's nose twitched.

He could taste the metallic tang of the wasp venom in the air, mixed with the dust of decades-old tax returns.

His lungs burned. The black veins in his neck stretched tighter, reaching up toward his jaw.

He stopped ten feet from the steel door.

"Gary!" Dale shouted.

His voice sounded flat, mechanical.

"I need the witness stamp.

Form 7-B.

Immediate execution.

"

Silence from behind the door. Then, a crash. Glass shattering.

Dale's hand went to the spray canister on his belt. It was empty.

He didn't have the acid to burn the wasps, but he had the knowledge of their nesting patterns.

Void Wasps didn't just sting; they injected a paralytic enzyme that hardened into a resinous cage around the victim's nervous system.

If Gary was still making noise, he was already infected.

Dale checked his Field Guide.

*Void Wasp (Vespa nihil).* Weakness: Thermal shock. Rapid temperature drop causes the resin cage to become brittle and shatter. Secondary weakness: Acoustic resonance.

Dale looked at the ceiling. The HVAC system was still running. The vents were open.

He didn't need a spray. He needed the building's internal temperature to drop by fifteen degrees within ten seconds.

He walked to the thermostat panel on the wall. It was locked.

Dale stared at the lock. He didn't try to pick it. He reached into his pocket and pulled out a small, heavy object.

A calibration weight. Certified for scale accuracy.

He had carried it for six years, and he smashed it against the glass of the thermostat panel.

The glass shattered. The plastic housing cracked. Dale reached inside, bypassing the digital interface, and yanked the manual override lever.

The fans roared.

The temperature in the hallway plummetted. The digital display flickered: *72... 68... 60...*

The arachnids on Dale's arms hissed. They hated the cold. They dug their claws into his skin, drawing thin lines of blood that blackened instantly.

Dale didn't flinch, and he watched the thermometer.

*50... 45... 40...*

Behind the door, the buzzing changed pitch. It became a frantic, jagged sound.

Dale waited.

*38... 37...*

A scream. Not of pain, but of confusion.

Dale kicked the door.

It swung open.

Gary was on the floor, surrounded by a cage of crystalline resin. The wasps were stuck to the walls, their wings frozen stiff, their bodies encased in the brittle shells that had formed as their venom reacted to the cold.

Gary was alive. His eyes were wide, white-rimmed, staring at Dale.

"Dale?" Gary croaked. His voice was raspy, damaged by the cold air. "What did you do?"

"Thermal shock," Dale said.

He stepped over a frozen wasp. Its body snapped under his boot.

"Protocol 4-Alpha.

Environmental control.

"

Gary struggled to sit up. The resin around his legs was cracking.

"You killed them."

"I neutralized them," Dale corrected. He pulled a pen from his vest. He didn't write on a form. He wrote on Gary's shirt.

*Witness Required. Signature.*

Gary looked at the black veins spreading up Dale's neck. He looked at the arachnids, which were now crawling onto Gary's shoulder, seeking warmth.

"You're changing," Gary whispered.

"I'm efficient," Dale said. "Sign the form."

Gary hesitated. The System notification burned in the air between them.

*Warning: Witness signature required to initiate Level III Hunt. Failure to sign results in automatic case closure. Infestation status: Active. Threat Level: Critical.*

"If I sign," Gary said, his hands trembling as he tore the fabric of his shirt, "I'm complicit in whatever you're becoming."

"You're already complicit," Dale said. "You watched me do it. You didn't run. You're part of the protocol."

Gary looked at the frozen wasps, and he looked at the hole in the ceiling where the rift had been.

He looked at Dale's hands, which were no longer shaking. They were steady.

Too steady.

"Where are we going?" Gary asked.

"To the source," Dale said. "The Authority says the one who opened the rift is still here. Hiding in the infrastructure."

"The infrastructure?"

"The sewers. The power grid.

The things you don't see because you're too busy looking at the monsters.

" Dale pointed to the floor.

"They're in the pipes. They're in the walls. They're using the city's body as a nest."

Gary signed the shirt. Blood from a cut on his finger smeared the ink.

*Witness Accepted. Thoroughness Rating: 10.0. Certification Level III Unlocked.*

*New Protocol: Substrate Tracking. You can now detect dimensional residue in non-organic materials.*

Dale felt the world shift.

The floor beneath his feet wasn't just concrete. It was a map. Lines of blue light pulsed through the foundation of the building, tracing paths into the dark. The lines were thick, pulsing with a slow, rhythmic beat.

*Found,* Dale thought, and he turned to Gary.

"Get up."

"I can't," Gary said. "My legs."

Dale looked at the resin cage. It was brittle, but Gary's bones were still encased. Breaking it would shatter Gary's legs.

"Then you don't go," Dale said.

Gary's face fell.

"Dale—"

"You're a witness.

You stay here. You file the report.

You tell them what happened.

You tell them the job is done.

"

"The job isn't done," Gary said. "If you go down there, you won't come back. The black... it's taking you."

Dale looked at his hands. The veins were black now.

Not spreading.

Settled.

Solidified, and he didn't feel fear. He felt the absence of hesitation.

"I have a job to finish," Dale said.

He walked out of the records center. The arachnids followed. They didn't crawl on him anymore.

They walked on the floor, forming a perimeter around him. A protective detail.

Outside, the rain had stopped. The streets were empty. The emergency vehicles were parked in a neat line, their lights flashing in sync. Dale could see the rhythm of the flashers in his own pulse.

He walked to the nearest manhole cover.

The blue lines in the System view converged here. They were thick, pulsing with a sickly, yellow-green light.

*Substrate Trace: High Concentration. Species: Unknown. Signature: Rift Origin.*

Dale crouched. He placed his hand on the iron cover.

The metal was warm, and he lifted it.

The smell hit him first.

Not rot. Not sewage. It was the smell of ozone and burnt sugar.

The scent of a battery acid spill mixed with vanilla extract.

He climbed down the ladder, and the ladder was slick with slime. Not water.

A viscous, translucent fluid that coated the rungs.

Dale's boots slid slightly. He grabbed the rails, his fingers digging into the metal.

The arachnids descended after him, moving with unnatural speed, and the tunnel below was not a sewer. It was a throat.

The walls were lined with a membrane that pulsed. It was gray, veined with black. It looked like skin, but it was rough, chitinous.

Dale activated his flashlight.

The beam cut through the darkness, revealing the source of the smell.

At the end of the tunnel, suspended in a web of black silk, was a creature.

It wasn't a monster. Not in the traditional sense. It was a human.

Or it had been, and the body was fused into the walls of the tunnel. The legs were gone, replaced by roots that drank from the sewage.

The arms were stretched, fingers elongated into chitinous claws that gripped the ceiling.

The face was still human.

Pale, gaunt, eyes wide and unblinking.

It was Marcus.

Dale froze.

Marcus had been the first survivor. The first person Dale had helped. The man who had screamed about demons while Dale sprayed for rats. The man who had vanished three weeks ago during the initial breach.

Marcus's mouth opened. No sound came out.

The System notification flashed.

*Target Identified: Marcus Vane. Status: Absorbed Host. Function: Anchor. He is holding the rift open.*

Dale stepped forward.

Marcus's eyes focused on Dale. Tears streamed down his face, turning black as they hit his skin.

"Help," Marcus mouthed. The sound was a dry rasp.

Dale looked at the webbing connecting Marcus to the walls. It was thick, pulsing. If he cut it, the rift would collapse.

But the black veins in Dale's neck throbbed, and he could feel Marcus's pain. He could feel the drain. The rift wasn't just an infestation.

It was a wound. And Dale was starting to heal it.

If he saved Marcus, the rift might close. But the Authority's task was to *hunt* the one who opened the rift.

The Authority didn't care if the rift closed. It cared about the *source*.

And the source was inside Marcus.

Dale raised his clipboard, and he didn't write a report. He wrote a diagnosis.

*Condition: Terminal Parasitic Integration. Treatment: Total Eradication.*

Marcus screamed. It was a silent scream, a vibration that shook the tunnel walls.

The arachnids hissed. They didn't want to kill him. They recognized him as kin.

Dale stepped closer. He could smell the vanilla now, overpowering the ozone. It was sweet. Sickly sweet.

He looked at Marcus's hands. The claws were gripping the ceiling, pulling the walls apart.

If Dale killed Marcus, the tunnel would collapse. He would be buried.

If Dale didn't kill Marcus, the rift would stay open. More monsters would come.

Dale looked at his clipboard. He looked at the System prompt.

*Task: Eliminate Threat. Option A: Destroy Host. Option B: Sever Connection.*

Option B would leave Marcus alive. But the connection would remain. The infection would spread.

Option A was clean. Thorough.

But it meant killing a man he had promised to save.

Dale's hand hovered over his spray canister. He didn't have acid. He had a knife.

He pulled it out.

The blade reflected the blue light of the System interface.

Marcus's eyes locked onto the knife. He didn't beg. He nodded.

Do it.

Dale moved, and he didn't hesitate. He didn't think. He acted.

He stabbed the webbing.

The silk tore.

Marcus shrieked.

The tunnel shook. Dust fell from the ceiling.

The black veins in Dale's arms flared. He felt a surge of power, raw and terrifying. He could feel the rift's energy flowing through him, through the web, into his body.

He was absorbing it.

The System notification flashed.

*Warning: Uncontrolled Absorption. Risk of Critical Instability.*

Dale ignored it. He slashed again.

The webbing snapped.

Marcus fell, and he hit the ground with a wet thud.

The rift behind him flickered. The gray mist receded.

Dale stood over him.

Marcus was still alive. Barely. His chest rose and fell in shallow, jagged breaths.

Dale looked down at him.

The job wasn't done. The threat was gone, but the witness was still there.

And Dale was standing in the middle of a collapsing tunnel, his body humming with stolen power, holding a knife stained with black blood.

A new notification appeared.

*New Objective: The Authority requires a second witness. The first witness is compromised.*

*Subject: Marcus Vane.*

*Status: Rescued?*

Dale looked at the knife. He looked at Marcus.

He couldn't leave him. He couldn't kill him.

He had to carry him, and he bent down.

The arachnids swarmed Marcus, not attacking, but wrapping around him, creating a living stretcher.

Dale lifted Marcus. The man was light, hollowed out.

He walked back to the ladder, and the tunnel was crumbling.

He climbed.

He reached the surface.

The rain had started again.

Dale walked to the edge of the manhole. He looked at the city.

The lights were going out. One by one. Block by block.

The city was dying.

And Dale was the only one who could hear it, and he looked at Marcus. Marcus's eyes were closing.

*System Alert: Thoroughness Rating Drop. 9.8. Reason: Emotional Hesitation.*

Dale didn't care.

He runned.
