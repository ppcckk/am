Chapter 404: Ticket Still Pending

# Chapter 404

The pen hovered over the clipboard, trembling.

Not from fear.

From protocol.

Dale Kowalski stood in the center of the sub-basement, the air thick with the coppery scent of ozone and old blood. Around him, the arachnids formed a perfect circle, their legs tapping against the concrete floor in a rhythm that matched his own heartbeat. The mirror-image arachnid, the one wearing his voice, stepped closer. Its chitinous face held a grotesque approximation of Dale's weary expression.

"Log entry," it said again. "Contamination Level: Critical. Action Required: Self-Purge."

The Authority interface flickered in Dale's peripheral vision. Red text scrolled rapidly, a cascading error report that threatened to lock out his primary functions.

*Identity Corruption: 87%.* *Field Guide Sync: Failed.* *Certification Level: Suspended.*

Dale ignored the warning. He looked at the pod.

The man inside, the first victim from Chapter 400, was still weeping.

The amber fluid bubbled softly. The Inspector's single, massive eye stared at the question Dale had projected into the air. *Who is the Inspector?*

The question hung there, glowing white. Unanswered.

But the System was answering, which was answering with Dale's own face.

The mirror-arachnid raised a front leg. It pointed directly at Dale's chest.

"Protocol Violation," it chirped.

"Incomplete documentation. Missing witness signature.

Missing timestamp verification.

You have not closed the loop, Technician Kowalski.

"

Dale's grip on his clipboard tightened until his knuckles turned white.

He didn't speak. He didn't argue.

He clicked his pen.

*Click.*

The sound echoed in the cavernous space, louder than the arachnid's mimicry.

"Form 9-B," Dale said, his voice flat and dry. "Intra-Infestation Conflict Resolution. Subsection C: Identity Theft via Mimicry Class."

The mirror-arachnid tilted its head. Confused? No. Processing.

"You are attempting to utilize my Field Guide against me," Dale continued, writing rapidly. "This is a cross-contamination event. Under Section 12, Paragraph 4 of the Licensing Authority Code, deploying a protocol designed for one creature type against another results in a 40% debuff."

He underlined the number 40%.

"However," Dale added, glancing up. "You are not a creature type. You are a user profile. And user profiles are not covered under Vermin Protocols."

The arachnid hissed. A sharp, clicking sound.

It lunged.

Dale didn't dodge. He stepped forward.

He slammed his clipboard into the creature's thorax, and the impact was dull, heavy. The arachnid skidded back, its legs scrambling for traction. The mimicry faltered. The voice distorted, pitching up into a shriek.

*System Update: User Profile Mirrored.* *Warning: Identity Corruption Detected.* *Action: Immediate Purge Required.*

The text pulsed red.

*Purge Initiated.*

A blue grid overlaid Dale's vision. It highlighted his hands, his feet, his head.

*Target: Dale Kowalski. Status: Contaminated. Deletion Pending.*

The other arachnids in the circle tensed. They looked at the mirror-arachnid, then at Dale.

They were waiting for the command, and the System was feeding them data.

To them, Dale was the anomaly. The glitch.

The thing that needed to be exterminated.

Dale looked at the blue grid. He looked at the clipboard in his hand.

He realized the loophole.

The Authority rewarded thoroughness. It punished sloppiness. But it did not punish bureaucracy. Not even when the bureaucracy was used to file a complaint against the player.

He flipped the clipboard over. On the back, in cramped, tiny handwriting, was a list of unresolved tickets from the early Rift events.

The German cockroach infestation at the duplex.

The acid beetle swarm in the sewer. The broodmother in the crawlspace.

Every single one had been closed with a 9.2 Thoroughness Rating.

Every single one had been verified.

Dale tapped the top of the clipboard.

"Ticket Closure Verification," he said. "Batch Number 404-A."

He held the clipboard up. The white surface reflected the light of the Inspector's eye.

"I am not purging myself," Dale said. "I am verifying my certification history."

The mirror-arachnid froze.

The blue grid on Dale's vision paused.

*Processing Request: Batch Verification.* *Estimated Time: 4.2 Seconds.*

Four seconds.

The arachnid shrieked, a sound of pure digital frustration. It scrambled toward him, mandibles snapping.

Dale didn't move. He couldn't. His hands were shaking, but his eyes stayed locked on the interface.

*Processing... 1.0...* *Processing... 2.0...* *Processing...*

The arachnid leaped.

Its legs wrapped around Dale's torso. The weight was immense, crushing.

The chitin scraped against his uniform, tearing the fabric.

Dale smelled the rot of the creature's exoskeleton, and he didn't scream.

He wrote.

On the back of the clipboard, in the margin, he added a new line.

*Complaint Filed: Unauthorized use of Technician Profile.* *Evidence: Audio/Visual Match.* *Status: Pending Review.*

*Processing... 3.5...*

The arachnid's weight shifted. It was trying to bite his neck.

*Processing... 4.0...*

The air in the room changed. The hum stopped.

The blue grid vanished.

The red text on the interface flickered and died.

*Verification Complete.* *User Profile: Authentic.* *Identity Corruption: Rejected.* *Reason: Historical Thoroughness Rating (9.2) exceeds Corruption Threshold.*

The arachnid flew backward.

It didn't land. It dissolved.

The mimicry broke. The creature collapsed into a pile of black sludge and broken legs, its form losing coherence as the System stripped away the borrowed identity.

Silence.

The other arachnids in the circle stood motionless. They looked at Dale. Then they looked at the pile of sludge.

One by one, they stepped back, and they returned to their positions, standing guard around the pod.

Dale gasped for air. His ribs ached. His uniform was torn. Blood trickled from his nose.

He looked at the clipboard. The complaint line was there.

*Status: Pending Review.*

He looked at the Inspector. The giant eye watched him. It didn't blink.

*Who is the Inspector?*

The question was still unanswered. But the System had moved on. It had accepted Dale's defense. It had validated his history.

But it hadn't forgiven him.

A new window opened in his vision.

*System Notice: Administrative Penalty Applied.* *Infraction: Filing false reports to evade system purge.* *Penalty: Certification Level Downgrade.* *Current Level: I.*

Dale stared at the text.

Level I, and he had been Level VII. He had unlocked the High-Pressure Acid Sprayer. He had the Containment Field Generator. He had the Multi-Spectral Scanner.

All gone.

He was a beginner again. A Level II Technician.

He looked at his hands. They were bare.

No tools.

No gadgets.

Just the pen and the clipboard.

The pod hummed. The man inside opened his eyes. He looked at Dale. He mouthed a word.

*Dale?*

The Inspector's eye widened. The black fluid from the corner of the eye dripped faster. It hit the floor with a wet slap.

*System Update: Primary Nest Verification Required.* *Objective: Identify Source of Mimicry.* *Time Limit: 00:00.*

The timer started counting down from zero.

*00:00.*

*00:01.*

*00:02.*

The arachnids took a collective step forward.

Dale looked at the mirror-arachnid's remains. The sludge was bubbling. It was reforming.

Not as an arachnid.

As a man.

A man in a Technician's uniform.

Dale's uniform.

The figure rose from the sludge. It had Dale's face. Dale's grey hair. Dale's tired eyes.

It held a clipboard.

It looked at Dale.

"Log entry," the thing said. "Subject: Self."

Dale looked at his own clipboard. The complaint line was still there.

*Status: Pending Review.*

The thing smiled. It was Dale's smile. The one he used when he wanted to go home.

"Review denied," the thing said. "You are the infestation, Dale."

The timer hit *00:10*.

The Inspector's eye flashed red.

*System Alert: Duplicate Profile Detected.* *Action: Eliminate Secondary User.*

The arachnids attacked.

Dale didn't raise his shield. He didn't have one.

He raised his pen, and he wrote a single word on the front of his clipboard.

*Appeal.*

The System froze.

*Appeal Process: Initiated.* *Wait Time: 404 Hours.*

The arachnids stopped. They were suspended in mid-air, caught in a temporal lag caused by the administrative hold.

The duplicate Dale looked confused. He checked his own clipboard.

*Appeal Process: Initiated.* *Wait Time: 404 Hours.*

"Wait," the duplicate said. "404 hours?"

"Sixteen days," Dale said. His voice was steady.

The duplicate looked at the pod. Then at the Inspector. Then at Dale.

"You can't wait sixteen days," the duplicate said. "The man in the pod dies in six."

Dale looked at the pod. The amber fluid was turning brown. The man's skin was graying.

Dale looked at the duplicate.

"Then we split the ticket," Dale said.

He tore the page off the clipboard. He held it out.

"Half the job is yours. Half is mine."

The duplicate took the page.

It read it.

*Ticket Split: Accepted.* *Liability Shared.*

The arachnids moved again. But they moved in two directions. Half attacked Dale. Half attacked the duplicate.

Dale watched the duplicate charge forward. It fought with Dale's techniques. Dale's hesitation. Dale's caution.

It fought like Dale.

And it was losing.

Dale watched his own mistakes reflected in real-time. The duplicate failed to isolate the threat. It got distracted by the Inspector's eye. It forgot to document the kill.

Zero points, and the duplicate was mauled by its own arachnids.

Dale stepped back. He watched his other self die.

He didn't help.

He couldn't, and he held the other half of the ticket. The one that said *Eliminate Secondary User.*

But he hadn't written the outcome.

The duplicate lay on the floor, broken. The arachnids swarmed over it, tearing into the flesh.

The Inspector watched. The eye did not blink.

Dale looked at the clipboard in his hand. The tear mark was jagged.

*Liability Shared.*

He looked at the pod. The man was screaming silently.

Dale looked at the Inspector, and he realized the truth.

The Inspector wasn't the boss. The Inspector was the auditor.

And the auditor didn't care who won.

It only cared who filed the report.

Dale looked at the duplicate's corpse. The arachnids were leaving it. They were turning toward him.

He looked at the System interface.

*Appeal Process: Initiated.* *Wait Time: 403 Hours, 59 Minutes.*

He had sixteen days to fix a mistake that had already killed his other self.

He looked at the pod, and he looked at the Inspector.

He walked toward the pod.

The arachnids parted for him.

They recognized the Technician.

They recognized the License.

Dale placed his hand on the glass of the pod. The glass was cold. The man inside looked at him.

"Help me," the man mouthed.

Dale looked at his clipboard, and he wrote: *Patient Status: Critical.*

He wrote: *Action: Extraction.*

He didn't have the tools. He didn't have the level.

But he had the form.

The System flashed.

*Extraction Protocol: Unlocked (Temporary).* *Reason: Liability Shared.*

The pod door hissed.

It opened.

The amber fluid spilled out, flooding the floor. The man collapsed onto the wet concrete, gasping.

Dale grabbed his arm.

"Stand up," Dale said.

The man tried to rise. His legs gave out.

The Inspector's eye narrowed.

*System Alert: Unauthorized Patient Extraction.* *Penalty: Immediate Termination of Contract.*

The arachnids lunged.

Dale pulled the man, and he pulled him toward the dark tunnel that led out of the sub-basement.

The arachnids swarmed over them. Teeth tore into Dale's shoulder.

He didn't stop.

He dragged the man through the dark.

Behind him, the Inspector watched.

And for the first time, the Inspector spoke.

Not in Dale's voice.

Not in the arachnid's voice.

In its own.

A sound like grinding stone.

"Ticket Closed."

Dale dragged the man into the tunnel, and he didn't look back.

He knew the Inspector was wrong.

The ticket wasn't closed.

The ticket was pending.

And he was out of time.
