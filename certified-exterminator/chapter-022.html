Chapter 22: The Pulsing Casing

# Chapter 22

The drywall came off in sheets. Dale worked the putty knife along the stud line with the patience of a man who had removed three hundred sheets of sheetrock in his career and expected to remove three hundred more before he died. Behind the paper facing, the cavity was dark. He angled his flashlight.

"You're destroying my wall."

Mrs. Aldana stood in the doorway with her arms crossed.

She was sixty-two, five-four, and had called the county code enforcement line twice in the last hour.

Dale had ignored both calls because he was licensed to inspect residential structures under the Authority's emergency provision and code enforcement had no jurisdiction over infestation work. He'd told her this once. She hadn't believed him.

"I'm verifying a prior treatment," he said. "Section 14.2, emergency re-inspection authority."

"That's not a thing."

"It is a thing. I have a laminated card."

He didn't have a laminated card, and he had a phone screenshot of the Authority's emergency provision, which was arguably worse. Mrs.

Aldana watched him work.

Her jaw worked. She didn't leave.

Dale moved the flashlight beam across the cavity. Insulation batts, pink and undisturbed.

Electrical wiring, standard Romex, no rodent damage.

A plumbing chase, sealed with expanding foam, and he pressed his thumb against the foam and felt it compress. Too soft.

Fresh application. Within the last three weeks.

He hadn't applied that foam.

He pulled out his Field Guide. Scrolled to the entry for *Rattus profundus*.

Dimensional Vermin.

Class I. He'd treated six of them in the first two weeks after the Rift. Standard entry points: foundation cracks, dryer vents, gaps around utility penetrations.

The foam in this wall would have been applied by someone sealing a gap.

Someone aware of the gap. Someone who knew what was coming through it.

He scrolled past the *Rattus* entry.

Scrolled to *Coleoptera acidum*. Acid beetles. He'd treated eleven of those.

They didn't use foam. They used acid.

The foam would have been eaten through in hours, and he scrolled further.

Past the broodmother entry. Past the entry he'd written three weeks ago when he'd treated the infestation at the Kowalski duplex.

His own duplex. The one where he'd written "No egg cases present" in the verification column.

He hadn't specified the species. He'd checked for *Rattus* egg cases because that's what the infestation was at the time.

But the colonial organism in the sky, and the thing that had caused the vibration. If it was related to something else. Something with a dispersal trigger.

He closed the Field Guide. Looked at the foam again.

"Mrs. Aldana. Who applied this foam?"

She shrugged. "County inspector came by. Sealed up some gaps. Said it was for the rats."

"Which inspector?"

"Brown truck. Badge on the door."

Dale pulled out his phone.

Checked the county's public works log. Cross-referenced the date.

A name came up: Marcus Webb.

Pest Control Technician, Class II. Same certification level as Dale. Same jurisdiction.

Webb had been to this house. Webb had sealed the gaps.

Webb had not been asked to seal gaps in the interior walls.

Webb had been asked to seal exterior entry points.

The foam in this wall was an interior application. Someone had gone beyond their scope.

Or someone had been covering something up.

Dale pulled his phone up again. Opened the Authority's job log. Found his own entry from three weeks ago.

The verification column. "No egg cases present." He stared at it.

The Authority's audit system would flag this in less than seventy-two hours.

They'd pull his notes. They'd see the species gap. They'd open an inquiry.

And if Webb had sealed something inside this wall, there was a reason.

He put the phone away.

Reached into the cavity with his gloved hand. Pressed against the insulation.

Felt something.

A lump. Not a rodent. Too still.

Too regular. He worked his fingers around it and pulled it free.

An egg case. Ootheca. Tan, leathery, about the size of a thumbnail. He held it up to the flashlight.

Not *Rattus*. *Rattus* didn't lay oothecae. This was insectoid.

He flipped the Field Guide open.

Scrolled to *Coleoptera acidum*.

No. Wrong shape.

The ootheca in his hand was segmented. Six chambers visible through the translucent casing. Acid beetles laid single eggs in substrate.

Not oothecae, and he scrolled further. Past his known entries. Past the blank spaces where the Field Guide hadn't populated yet. He found nothing that matched.

But he found something adjacent.

The entry for *Blatta orientalis*. The Oriental cockroach. A mundane species.

He'd treated thousands of them.

His Field Guide entry was complete, thorough, rated 9.4 for documentation quality, and the ootheca in his hand was close.

Close enough that the Authority's cross-reference system would recognize the morphological similarity.

He held the egg case steady. Focused on it. Waited.

The Field Guide flickered. A new line appeared under the *Blatta orientalis* entry: MORPHOLOGICAL CROSS-REFERENCE DETECTED. SPECIES UNIDENTIFIED.

CONFIDENCE: 62%. TARGETED WEAKNESS UNLOCKED: THORACIC SEGMENT VULNERABILITY (CONFIDENCE-SCALED DAMAGE: +18%).

That was new.

He'd never seen the cross-reference system do that.

It meant he could hit this unknown species with a damage bonus he hadn't earned, because the Authority's system recognized the morphological similarity to something he'd documented properly. A loophole.

A tiny one. But it meant he could fight something he hadn't identified yet.

He pocketed the egg case. Looked at the wall again.

He saw more of them in the insulation. A cluster. Maybe eight.

Maybe ten.

All tan. All segmented.

All sitting in the foam Webb had applied.

Webb had sealed them in.

Not to protect the house. To protect the eggs.

Dale pulled out his phone. Called the number for Marcus Webb. It rang four times.

Voicemail. He called again.

Same, and he was still holding the phone when a truck pulled into the driveway.

Brown. Badge on the door.

He looked out the window. A man got out. Fortyish.

Carhartt jacket. Clipboard.

The Authority's insignia on his chest patch, embroidered slightly larger than regulation.

Class III.

Dale had been Class II for two months. This man was a full level above him.

The man walked up the path. Knocked. Dale opened the door.

"Kowalski." The man smiled.

It was not a warm smile. "Marcus Webb.

County overflow.

I got a work order this morning. Residential infestation, unresolved, flagged for re-treatment."

"This is my job."

"It was your job. The Authority reassigned it." Webb held up his phone.

Showed the work order.

The infestation ID matched Dale's original case number.

"They said you had a documentation gap. They said you needed a second opinion."

Dale looked at the work order. The reassignment timestamp was eleven minutes ago. Webb had gotten it before Dale could file anything. Before Dale could report the eggs he'd found.

"I found evidence of active infestation," Dale said. "In the wall. You sealed it."

Webb's smile didn't change. "I sealed entry points. Standard procedure."

"You sealed eggs."

"Did I?" Webb tilted his head. "I didn't see any eggs. I sealed gaps. That's what the work order said."

The work order said re-treatment. Not inspection. Webb hadn't been sent to find the eggs.

He'd been sent to close the case. To file a verification report that said the infestation was resolved.

Which would mean the Authority accepted the closure.

Which would mean the eggs hatched. Which would mean whatever was in those eggs went free.

Dale looked at the egg case in his hand. He looked at Webb.

"I need you to open that wall," Dale said.

"I need you to file your original report," Webb said.

"Close it out. The Authority wants the backlog cleared.

You've got a Thoroughness Rating of 9.2.

That's good work. Don't let an audit take it from you. File the report.

Walk away. I'll handle the rest."

That was the threat.

Not violence.

Bureaucracy. If Dale filed his original report, the case closed.

The audit would still happen, but it would be moot. The eggs would hatch. And if Dale didn't file, his Thoroughness Rating stayed open.

Vulnerable. And Webb would file his own closure report within the hour.

Dale looked at his phone.

Opened the Authority's interface. Found his open case. The verification column.

"No egg cases present."

He could edit it. He could change it to "Egg cases present.

Species unidentified.

Ootheca morphology consistent with *Blatta orientalis* cross-reference." That would keep the case open. That would force the Authority to audit it properly. That would mean his Thoroughness Rating stayed at risk until he completed the full protocol.

But completing the full protocol meant identifying the species. And he couldn't identify it.

The Field Guide had a 62% confidence rating.

That wasn't enough for a proper identification.

He'd need a live specimen. He'd need to see one move.

He looked at the wall. The eggs were still in there. Sealed in foam.

Not hatching yet. Not hatching for another few hours, maybe.

He didn't know, and he didn't know what this species was.

Webb was watching him. Waiting.

Dale pulled out his putty knife.

"Move," he said.

Webb stepped aside.

Dale went back to the wall. He cut the foam.

He reached in, and he pulled out another egg case. He held it up. Looked at it.

Waited.

The Field Guide flickered again. CROSS-REFERENCE UPDATE. CONFIDENCE: 64%. The second sample bumped it two points.

He pulled out a third. 67%.

A fourth. 71%.

Webb shifted his weight. "You're contaminating the workspace."

Dale ignored him. Fifth egg case. 74%.

Sixth. 79%.

Seventh. 83%, and he needed ninety. He needed a live specimen or nine more egg cases. He pulled the insulation apart. Found two more. 88%. 91%.

The Field Guide flashed. SPECIES IDENTIFIED.

The entry populated. The name appeared in his guide. And Dale read it. And his hands went still.

It wasn't a new species.

He knew it did not come from the Rift. Something he'd treated a hundred times in his fourteen years.

Something mundane. Something that shouldn't have been in the wall of a suburban house in Ohio.

He looked at Webb. Webb's smile was gone.

"You know what this is," Dale said.

"I know what it was," Webb said. "Before you opened the wall."

The Authority's notification appeared in Dale's peripheral vision. INFESTATION CLASSIFICATION UPDATED.

RECLASSIFICATION: CLASS IV.

YOUR CERTIFICATION LEVEL: II. TREATMENT PROTOCOL REQUIRED: NOT AVAILABLE.

Class IV, and he was Class II. He couldn't treat this.

The protocol didn't exist in his toolkit.

He'd need to pass the Level IV examination. Which he couldn't take. Which required a Class III endorsement.

Which he didn't have, and he looked at Webb. Webb was already pulling out his clipboard. Already writing.

"I'm filing a closure report," Webb said. "Standard re-treatment. Infestation resolved. No active specimens."

"You can't. I have an open case. The Authority won't accept a duplicate closure."

Webb looked at him.

"I'm Class III.

You're Class II. My closure overrides your open case.

That's the jurisdiction rule. You should know it."

Dale did know it. He'd read it in the manual. He'd assumed it would never come up. He'd assumed he'd never be out-leveled on a job he'd found the problem on.

Webb wrote his name. Signed the closure. The Authority's interface flickered. A notification appeared.

CASE CLOSED. VERIFICATION: PENDING AUDIT. THOROUGHNESS RATING: HELD PENDING.

Dale stared at it. The case was closed.

The eggs were in the wall, and the wall was open. Webb was already walking to his truck.

And in Dale's Field Guide, the species entry sat fully populated.

A name he recognized. A name that meant the problem wasn't the Rift.

Wasn't the colonial organism in the sky.

It was not dimensional; it had been here all along. Something that had been here before the Rift. Something that the Rift had just made visible.

His phone buzzed. A new notification.

From the Authority.

Not about the audit.

Not about his rating. Something else.

FORMAL OBJECTION WINDOW: OPEN. 48 HOURS. REQUIRES: CLASS III ENDORSEMENT OR CLASS IV TREATMENT COMPLETION.

He looked at Webb's truck pulling away. He looked at the eggs in his hand. He looked at the species name in his Field Guide.

He had forty-eight hours to find a Level III technician who would back his objection. Or he had to treat a Class IV infestation with a Class II toolkit.

And the only Level III technician in the county who knew what this species was had just driven away.

Dale looked at the wall. The eggs pulsed. Faintly.

Like a heartbeat. Like something was already pressing against the inside of the casing.

He started running.
