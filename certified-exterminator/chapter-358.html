Chapter 358: Sealed in Living Walls

# Chapter 358

Miller screamed. A wet, guttural sound that tore from the back of his throat.

The lead officer was on top of him. Not attacking with intent, but with mechanical precision. The officer's hands, now blackened and slick with the dissolving insignia metal, clamped around Miller's neck. The other officer, the one whose gun had fallen, was twitching on the floor, his legs kicking out in short, jerky spasms.

Miller thrashed. His boots scraped against the concrete.

Dale watched. He did not raise his clipboard. He did not reach for his spray canister. He watched the mechanics of the attack.

The lead officer's movements were too smooth. Too efficient.

There was no wasted energy.

No hesitation. It was like watching a hydraulic press descend.

Miller's eyes bulged.

His hands clawed at the officer's wrists, but the black veins under the skin pulsed in rhythm with the officer's grip. Tighter.

Looser.

Tighter.

Dale took a step back.

His hand drifted to his pocket. His fingers brushed the cold metal of his calibration key.

*Identification complete,* the system whispered in the periphery of his vision. *Species: Authority Host (Corrupted). Weakness: Connection Severance.*

The text was small, gray, and unhelpful. He didn't have a severance tool. He had a clipboard, a pen, and a can of industrial-strength adhesive remover.

Miller gagged. His face turned a deep, bruised purple.

"Stop," Dale said.

The lead officer did not stop.

"Protocol K-7," Dale said, louder. "Structural entry point compromised. Structural integrity failing."

The officer's head snapped toward him. The eyes were gone.

Replaced by swirling, oily darkness.

The mouth opened, and the static voice poured out, which was loud now. It vibrated in Dale's teeth.

*Submit to audit.*

Dale ignored the voice. He looked at the floor. The black veins were spreading from the officer's chest, tracing patterns on the concrete.

They looked like wiring. Like circuit boards.

*The network is waking up,* Dale thought.

If the network was waking up, it was connecting.

Dale stepped forward. Not toward Miller. Toward the twitching officer on the floor.

The second officer was smaller.

Weaker. The infection had reached his neck, but not his brain. He was still conscious.

Still human.

His eyes darted around the room, terrified, fixed on Dale.

Dale crouched. He held out his hand. Not a fist. An open palm.

"Hey," Dale said. "You're off-balance. Your center of gravity is wrong."

The twitching officer stared at him. The static in his voice was quieter, buried under a layer of human panic.

"Help... me..."

"You're not being helped," Dale said. "You're being formatted."

He pulled a small bottle from his belt.

Not spray. A high-viscosity sealant. Used for sealing cracks in foundation walls against termite intrusion.

It was thick, gray, and smelled like ammonia.

Dale uncapped it.

The lead officer released Miller. Miller collapsed, gasping, rubbing his throat. He scrambled backward, kicking at the officer's legs.

"Kowalski!" Miller yelled. "Do something! He's killing me!"

"I am doing something," Dale said. He poured the sealant onto the twitching officer's chest.

The liquid hit the black veins, and the effect was instantaneous. The sealant hardened on contact, creating a rigid, gray barrier over the spreading infection. The black veins stopped pulsing. The twitching stopped.

The officer groaned. A human sound.

Dale looked up at the lead officer. The lead officer froze. The connection was broken. The network was isolated.

"Protocol: Containment," Dale said. "Isolate the source. Prevent cross-contamination."

The lead officer tilted his head. The oily eyes narrowed. *Irregular. Non-compliant. Penalty: Immediate termination.*

Dale stood up. He wiped his hands on his pants.

"You're out of protocol. Your Thoroughness Rating is dropping."

The lead officer lunged.

Miller scrambled away, crawling on his elbows.

Dale didn't run. He sidestepped. The officer's fist, hard as stone, smashed into the concrete where Dale had been standing. Dust exploded upward.

Dale checked his Field Guide.

*Dimensional Vermin (Rattus profundus).* *Authority Host (Corrupted).*

No weakness listed for the Host. The guide was empty.

*You need to document it,* the system hinted.

Dale pulled out his pen. He clicked it. The sound was sharp, precise.

"I need a sample," Dale muttered.

He waited for the officer to pull his fist free. The metal hand was stuck in the concrete. The officer strained, the black veins flaring.

Dale leaned in. He pressed his pen against the officer's forearm. He didn't write. He scraped.

A small flake of blackened metal and skin came off. Dale caught it on the tip of his pen. He held it up to his eye.

*Sample collected.* *Analysis starting...*

The lead officer roared. He ripped his hand free. The concrete cracked. A shard of stone flew off, grazing Dale's cheek.

Dale didn't flinch. He looked at the shard. Then at the officer.

"Structural compromise confirmed," Dale said.

The officer raised both hands. The air around him distorted. The static grew louder. Miller covered his ears.

"Dale! Move!"

"The audit requires documentation," Dale said.

He spoke into the air.

"I am documenting. I am verifying.

I am isolating.

"

The officer's hands glowed with a sickly violet light.

*Terminate.*

The word wasn't spoken. It was projected.

It hit Dale like a physical blow.

His vision blurred. His stomach churned. The contamination anxiety spiked.

The floor was dirty, and the air was dirty. The officer was dirty.

Dale closed his eyes. He breathed in.

*Control the workspace.*

He opened his eyes, and he reached into his belt. He pulled out a small, silver disk. A Calibration Token.

Rare. Expensive.

Used to reset a localized area's dimensional frequency.

He didn't throw it. He placed it on the floor.

The officer stepped forward. The violet light intensified.

Dale tapped the token.

The floor beneath the officer shifted.

Not physically. Perceptually.

For a split second, the officer saw the world as Dale saw it. A grid. A series of lines.

A code.

The officer stuttered. The violet light flickered.

*System error,* the static whispered. *Input conflict.*

"Protocol violation," Dale said. "You are interfering with a licensed technician's workspace. This is a Class 4 Infraction."

The officer froze.

The grid in his eyes vanished. He looked confused. For a second, he was just a man.

A confused, scared man in a melting uniform.

"Miller," Dale said. "Taser him."

Miller was still on the floor. He stared at Dale.

"What?"

"Taser him. Don't shoot.

Taser.

Low voltage. Disrupt the signal.

"

Miller fumbled for his belt. His hands shook. He pulled out a standard-issue stun baton.

"Go," Dale said.

Miller scrambled up. He swung the baton. The tip hit the officer's shoulder.

The officer convulsed. The violet light short-circuited. Sparks flew. The black veins receded, retreating into the core of the chest.

The officer dropped to his knees. He wheezed. The static stopped.

Silence returned to the alley.

Dale exhaled. His hands were steady. He clicked his pen again.

On his clipboard, a new line appeared. Not in his handwriting. In the Authority's font.

*Incident Report: Unauthorized Host Activation. Cause: Network Overload.* *Resolution: Partial Containment.* *Thoroughness Rating: 6.4.*

Dale frowned at the number. Six point four. Sloppy.

He looked at the twitching officer on the floor. The sealant had held. The man was breathing. He was alive.

"Miller," Dale said. "Check him.

Pulse.

Respiration.

If he's alive, he's a witness.

"

Miller walked over. He knelt beside the twitching officer. He pressed two fingers to the man's neck.

"He's alive," Miller said. "Barely."

Dale looked at the lead officer. The man was slumped against the wall. The black veins were dormant.

The insignia was gone. There was a scar where it had been.

A raw, open wound.

Dale walked over. He crouched. He looked at the scar.

He pulled a small notebook from his pocket. He wrote.

*Symptom 1: Metallic integration.* *Symptom 2: Auditory hallucinations (static).* *Symptom 3: Compulsive obedience to 'Audit' command.*

The lead officer's eyes opened. They were blue again. Human.

"Kowalski," the officer whispered. His voice was raspy.

"State your name," Dale said.

"Officer... Gary... Henderson."

"Rank?"

"Level... Four."

Dale paused. Level Four. Higher than Dale's current Level Three.

"Who gave you the order to terminate me?"

Henderson coughed. Blood sprayed onto the concrete.

"No one... gave the order. I... heard it."

"From whom?"

"The Core," Henderson said. "The Authority... isn't... who you think... it is."

Dale's pen stopped.

"What does that mean?"

"The Authority... is not a regulator," Henderson gasped. "It's... a collector."

Miller stood up. He looked at Dale.

"Collector? Of what?"

Henderson's eyes rolled back. The static returned. *Audit complete.*

The officer went limp. Dead. Not from the taser. From the sudden cessation of the connection.

Dale stared at the body.

*Collector.*

He looked at his clipboard. The Field Guide entry for *Authority Host (Corrupted)* updated.

*Weakness Identified: Dependency on Network Signal.* *Note: Severance causes system failure. Host death.*

Dale felt a chill run down his spine. Not fear. Recognition.

He knew this pattern, which was a hive.

Not a bureaucratic organization. A hive.

And Dale had just severed a worker.

The air changed. The ambient static that had been buzzing in the background vanished. The silence was total.

Then, a new notification appeared. Large. Red.

*WARNING: Network Disruption Detected.* *Penalty: Cross-Contamination Risk.* *Status: Hunted.*

Dale looked at Miller.

"Miller. Do you hear that?"

Miller shook his head.

"Hear what?"

"The silence."

Dale stood up. He picked up his clipboard. He brushed a speck of dust off his sleeve.

"We need to move," Dale said.

"Why? Henderson is dead. The other guy is alive. We can take him to a hospital."

"No," Dale said. "We can't."

"Why?"

Dale pointed to the sky.

Above the alley, the rift was widening. Not with monsters. With eyes.

Thousands of tiny, glowing eyes, blinking in the darkness, and they were watching.

"The Authority doesn't have officers," Dale said. "It has cells."

He looked at the twitching officer on the floor. The man was waking up. His eyes were open. He was looking at Dale.

But he wasn't seeing Dale. He was seeing through Dale.

"Miller," Dale said. "Grab him. We're leaving."

"Where?"

"Somewhere off the grid. Somewhere with no metal. No insignia. No connection."

Miller grabbed the surviving officer's arm. He dragged him up.

Dale walked toward the end of the alley. He checked his Field Guide.

*Species: The Authority (Collective Consciousness).* *Weakness: Unknown.* *Threat Level: IX.*

Level IX. The highest certification.

Dale had never fought a Level IX infestation. He had never even seen one.

He adjusted his grip on the clipboard.

"Protocol Initiation," Dale muttered. "Extermination. Level I."

He started walking.

Miller followed. He was limping. His face was pale.

"Dale," Miller said. "What did Henderson mean? A collector?"

Dale didn't look back.

"Pest control," Dale said. "They're not regulating us. They're farming us."

Miller stopped.

"Farming us?"

"Harvesting," Dale corrected. "We're the crop. And the Authority?"

He pointed to the eyes in the sky.

"They're the harvesters."

The surviving officer screamed. A high-pitched, piercing sound.

Dale turned, and the officer was convulsing. Not with static. With growth. Black spikes were bursting through his skin.

"Contamination," Dale said. "Rapid mutation."

Miller backed away.

"What do I do?"

Dale looked at the spikes. They were forming. Sharp.

Hard.

Like chitin, and he looked at his sealant bottle. Empty.

He looked at his spray. Empty.

He looked at his pen.

He looked at the clipboard, and he flipped the page.

On the blank sheet, he drew a circle.

"Miller," Dale said. "Do you have a lighter?"

Miller blinked.

"A lighter?"

"Yes."

Miller fumbled in his pocket. He pulled out a small Zippo.

Dale took it. He flicked it on. The flame danced.

He held it under the officer's chin. The black spikes were spreading to his neck.

"Kowalski," the officer gurgled. The voice was changing. Becoming plural. *We... see... you.*

Dale pressed the flame to the officer's skin.

The spikes hissed. Smoke rose. Black, oily smoke.

The officer shrieked. The sound was mechanical. Grinding.

Dale held the flame.

"Protocol," Dale said. "Burn out the nest."

The spikes slowed. Receded. The officer collapsed.

Dale blew out the flame, and he looked at the two bodies. One dead. One dying.

He looked at Miller.

"Write this down," Dale said.

Miller pulled out his own notepad. He was shaking.

"What do I write?"

Dale spoke.

"Incident: Network breach. Host: Officer Henderson. Host: Officer [Name Unknown]." "Action: Manual severance." "Result: Partial failure. Containment achieved." "Next Step: Locate the Nexus."

Miller wrote. The scratching of the pen was loud in the silence.

Dale watched him.

The eyes in the sky blinked out.

The darkness returned. Normal. Empty.

But the pressure remained.

Dale felt a pull in his chest. Stronger than before.

It wasn't a call. It was a tether.

He looked at his Field Guide. A new entry had appeared.

*Species: The Nexus.* *Location: Unstable.* *Note: The Authority is not a government. It is an organism. And you have just bitten it.*

Dale closed the guide.

"Miller," he said. "We need a new workspace."

"Where?"

"Somewhere they can't reach."

Dale looked at the sewer grate, and the one he had inspected last week.

The one he had marked as *Compliant.*

"Down," Dale said.

Miller stared at the grate.

"Dale, no."

"Protocol," Dale said. "Subterranean infestations require subterranean solutions."

He opened the grate. The smell of rot and ozone wafted up.

Dale stepped in.

Miller followed, and the ladder was cold. Wet.

Dale climbed down.

The ladder groaned.

Halfway down, the ladder snapped.

Dale fell.

Miller screamed.

Dale landed on a pile of sludge. He didn't break. He rolled. He came up.

He looked up. Miller was on the ground, dangling from the broken ladder.

The opening above them closed. The metal bars shifted, sliding shut.

Sealed.

Dale looked around, and the tunnel was dark. Dripping.

And it was moving.

The walls were breathing.

Pulsing.

Like skin.

Dale checked his clipboard. The page was blank.

He uncapped his pen, and he wrote.
