Chapter 140: Forty Points to Live

# Chapter 140

The lead inspector stepped onto the landing with the heavy, deliberate thud of a stamp hitting wet paper. Dale's back pressed hard against the drywall, the plaster crumbling under his weight. Three gray suits blocked the only exit. Two more remained behind them, their clipboards held at perfect right angles.

Dale didn't breathe. He calculated the distance.

Six feet to the nearest inspector.

Twelve to the stairs. Fourteen to the front door, which was currently occupied by Marcus and his weeping hope.

The lead inspector raised its pen again. The tip hovered over Dale's clipboard.

"Violation 44-B: Unauthorized improvisation during active containment," the inspector said. Its voice sounded like a shredder eating credit card statements. "Penalty: Immediate license suspension."

Dale's thumb brushed the edge of his clipboard. The paper was warm. The Authority's system was already logging the error. If he didn't correct the record, the Auditor would wear his face before he could blink.

He needed a loophole. He needed a protocol.

"Wait," Dale said.

His voice was steady. He hated that it was steady.

It felt like lying.

"Before you file. Inspect the containment field."

The lead inspector paused. The pen stopped moving.

"Explain," the second inspector said. It tilted its head, smooth skin catching the moonlight.

Dale pointed a shaking finger at the crawlspace entrance. The duct tape was peeling. The trash bag had collapsed inward, trapping the Auditor's shadowy form in a tight, suffocating knot of black smoke and static.

"Protocol 7-Alpha," Dale said. "Isolation via physical barrier.

I identified the species as *Auditor Profundus*.

I isolated the specimen.

I eliminated the threat vector."

"Improvised materials," the lead inspector said. "Trash bag. Duct tape. Non-compliant."

"Compliant with Section 12, Subsection C: Emergency Containment in Residential Zones," Dale said. The words came from a manual he had memorized three years ago during a particularly boring weekend seminar. "When standard gear is unavailable due to sudden dimensional breach, the technician may substitute household items provided the structural integrity prevents cross-contamination."

The lead inspector looked at the trash bag. It wobbled. A low, gurgling sound came from inside.

The Auditor was still alive. It was trying to phase out of the plastic.

Dale's heart hammered against his ribs.

He knew this was a gamble. The Authority hated improvisation. It loved paperwork.

If he could prove the *result* met the standard, the *method* might be forgiven.

"Show me the integrity test," the lead inspector said.

Dale walked forward. His legs felt like water. He reached into his pocket and pulled out a can of industrial adhesive remover. It was leftover from a previous job, a label half-peeled, smelling of acetone and regret.

He sprayed the duct tape. The adhesive softened, but the plastic held. He sprayed the trash bag. The plastic turned white and brittle, but it didn't tear.

"Structural compromise detected," Dale said. He pulled a marker from his pocket and drew a line on the bag where the Auditor's shadow was pressing against it.

"Specimen is attempting Phase-Shift through polymer barrier.

Containment is 80% effective. I am requesting a variance to apply chemical agent to reinforce the seal."

The lead inspector watched. The other inspectors watched. Marcus watched.

"If you apply the agent," the lead inspector said, "and the containment fails, the liability transfers to you personally."

"Understood," Dale said.

He sprayed the marker line. The acetone hit the plastic. The plastic hissed. The black smoke inside the bag shrieked, a high, metallic sound that made Dale's teeth ache.

The bag hardened. The shadow inside stopped moving.

The lead inspector walked over. It placed a pale, fingerless hand on the bag. It pressed. The bag didn't give.

"Containment verified," the inspector said.

Dale let out a breath he didn't know he'd been holding. His hands were shaking so hard he had to grip the clipboard with both hands to keep it from clattering.

"However," the lead inspector said, "the Thoroughness Rating remains pending. You have not completed the Verification phase. The specimen is contained, but not eliminated. You have thirty seconds to finalize the report before we audit the entire building for secondary infestations."

Dale looked at his Field Guide. The entry for *Auditor Profundus* was half-written.

He hadn't noted the weakness yet.

He had guessed.

He had hoped.

He didn't know the weakness.

If he guessed wrong, the containment would fail. The Auditor would break out. The inspectors would revoke his license.

Dale looked at Marcus. Marcus was crying silently, his eyes fixed on the bag.

"Marcus," Dale said. "Your son. What did he see before the Auditor took him?"

Marcus blinked. "He... he saw the walls. He said the walls were breathing."

Dale's mind raced. *Rattus profundus* responded to vibration. *Acid beetles* responded to pH changes. *Auditor Profundus*?

He looked at the bag. The black smoke was swirling in a counter-clockwise spiral.

"Sound," Dale muttered. "It's reacting to sound."

He pulled out his phone. The screen was cracked. He opened the alarm app.

"What are you doing?" the second inspector asked.

"Testing hypothesis," Dale said.

He set an alarm. A standard, piercing beep.

He dropped the phone next to the bag.

The alarm rang.

The black smoke inside the bag froze. Then, it rushed toward the source of the sound. The plastic stretched. The duct tape groaned.

"Negative result," the lead inspector said. "The specimen is agitated by auditory stimuli. This indicates a vulnerability to sonic disruption, but not a weakness. You have failed to identify the true neutralization point."

Dale stared at the bag. The smoke was pressing against the plastic, trying to find a gap.

It wasn't sound. It was *attention*.

The Auditor fed on observation. It was an entity of scrutiny. It wanted to be seen. It wanted to be judged.

Dale looked at the inspectors. Their faces were blank. They weren't looking at the bag. They were looking at *him*.

"The weakness isn't physical," Dale said. "It's procedural."

The lead inspector stopped writing. "Explain."

"The Auditor exists in a state of superposition," Dale said. The words felt absurd in his mouth, but they fit the data. "It collapses when observed.

But it expands when ignored. My containment worked because I focused on the protocol, not the monster.

But to eliminate it, I need to remove the observation."

"You cannot unsee it," the lead inspector said.

"I can blind it," Dale said.

He grabbed a roll of black gaffer tape from his belt. He had brought it for sealing window screens last week.

He didn't look at the bag. He looked at the lead inspector.

"Close your eyes," Dale said.

The inspector didn't move. "That is not a command."

"It's a request," Dale said. "If you don't look at it, it can't feed. If I don't look at it, I can't panic.

We both stop observing.

We starve it."

The lead inspector hesitated. The other inspectors looked at their clipboards.

"If you close your eyes, you forfeit your right to verify the kill," the lead inspector said.

"I don't care," Dale said. "I'm blind."

He slapped the black tape over his own eyes.

Darkness.

Silence.

Dale listened. He heard Marcus breathing. He heard the inspectors shifting their weight. He heard the low hum of the refrigerator in the kitchen.

And he heard the bag.

The plastic was rustling. The shadow was shrinking.

Without eyes, Dale had no visual reference. He had to rely on his hands. He reached out, feeling for the edge of the bag.

His fingers touched the cold, hard plastic. It was shrinking.

He needed to seal it. He needed to cut it off from the world.

He pulled a utility knife from his pocket.

He didn't look. He couldn't. He had to trust his proprioception.

His muscle memory. Fourteen years of crawling into tight spaces.

Fourteen years of cutting drywall without looking.

He sliced the top of the bag.

The air changed. The smell of acetone vanished. The smell of ozone vanished.

The silence deepened.

Dale kept his hand on the cut, feeling for movement. Nothing.

He felt the plastic collapse inward, then flatten.

"Time expired," the lead inspector said.

Dale ripped the tape from his eyes.

The bag was flat.

Empty. No smoke.

No shadow. No Auditor.

The lead inspector looked at the bag. It looked at Dale.

"Specimen neutralized," the inspector said. "Method: Non-standard. Risk: Extreme. Result: Successful."

Dale slumped against the wall. His knees hit the floor.

"Thoroughness Rating," the inspector said.

A number appeared in Dale's peripheral vision.

7.8.

It was a failing grade for a Class II technician, but a passing grade for a survivor.

"License remains active," the lead inspector said. "But you are now under Probationary Status. Any further improvisation will result in immediate termination of your contract and your life."

Dale nodded. He couldn't speak. His throat was too tight.

The inspectors turned. They walked back up the stairs, their gray suits blending into the shadows.

Marcus stepped forward. "Is my son...?"

Dale looked at the flat bag. He looked at the empty crawlspace.

"The Auditor is gone," Dale said. "But the Rift is still open. Your son isn't here. He's still on the other side."

Marcus's face crumpled. He turned and ran out the front door, screaming into the night.

Dale sat on the floor. He picked up his clipboard. The form was still glowing.

*Status: Pending.*

Why?

The Auditor was dead. The job was done.

He looked at the number. 7.8.

It wasn't just the rating. It was the *entry*.

He opened his Field Guide. The page for *Auditor Profundus* was filling itself in. But the text wasn't describing the monster. It was describing *him*.

*Subject: Dale Kowalski. Class II Technician. Status: Compromised.*

*Note: Subject has demonstrated ability to manipulate Authority protocols through psychological blind spots. Flag for Level III Audit.*

Dale felt a cold spike of dread in his gut.

He hadn't just killed a monster. He had exposed a glitch in the system. And the Authority didn't like glitches.

The floorboards beneath him vibrated. A low, rhythmic thumping.

Dale looked down.

The cracks in the crawlspace floor were widening. New cracks. Not from the Auditor.

From below.

Something bigger was coming up. And it had the Authority's attention.

A new notification flashed in his vision.

**NEW INFESTATION DETECTED.** **TYPE: LEVEL IV.** **SPECIES: UNKNOWN.** **RECOMMENDED PROTOCOL: EVACUATE.**

Dale looked at his empty hands. He looked at the black tape. He looked at the flat bag.

He didn't have a Level IV certification. He didn't have the tools. He didn't even have his eyes open for long enough to see what was coming.

But the Authority had sent inspectors.

And the inspectors were gone.

Dale pulled a fresh sheet of paper from his clipboard. He uncapped his pen.

He wrote: *Incident Report: Unauthorized Contact with Level IV Entity. Requesting Support.*

The system rejected it. *Insufficient Qualification.*

The floor buckled. A claw, large as a car tire, punched through the wood.

Dale didn't run. He didn't scream.

He wrote: *Variance Request: Emergency Promotion to Level III.*

The system hung. For a second, the air froze.

Then, a new message appeared.

*Request Pending. Supervisor Override Available. Cost: 50% of Remaining Treatment Points.*

Dale looked at the claw. It was covered in gray, smooth skin. Like the inspectors.

He looked at his points. He had exactly forty left.

If he paid the cost, he would have zero. Zero points meant zero protection.

If he didn't pay, he died.

Dale pressed the pen to the paper.
