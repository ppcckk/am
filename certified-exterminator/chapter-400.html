Chapter 400: Hiring Process

# Chapter 400

The pen hovered.

It was a cheap ballpoint, the kind with the blue barrel and the rubber grip that had yellowed at the edges. Margaret's fingers were gray, stiff, and jointed in places they shouldn't have been. The tip of the pen pressed against the clipboard, leaving a dent in the cardboard that didn't heal.

Dale didn't move, and he kept his eyes on the paper. The header read *PERFORMANCE REVIEW: Q3, FISCAL YEAR 2014*.

The date was wrong.

The location was wrong. The entire context was a hallucination of bureaucratic horror, a construct built from Dale's deepest professional trauma and the Rift's ability to weaponize memory.

But the system rules were absolute.

If he attacked Margaret, he triggered a new violation. If he ran, the cross-contamination penalty on his Field Guide would lock him out of using any Class IV protocols for twenty-four hours. He had already debuffed an Inspector. He couldn't afford another error.

"Write," Margaret said again. Her voice didn't come from her mouth. It resonated in the fluorescent tubes above them, buzzing with the frequency of a dying hard drive.

Dale looked at his own hands, and they were shaking. Not from fear.

From the adrenaline dump of the last fight.

He forced them to stillness. He reached into his utility belt and pulled out his own clipboard. It was battered, stained with chitin dust and black ichor, but the clip held the current job report tight.

"You're not here," Dale said. His voice was flat.

Procedural.

"You're a manifestation.

A Class IV cognitive hazard designed to disrupt workflow.

"

Margaret tilted her head. The motion was too sharp. Her neck cracked audibly.

"Protocol requires acknowledgment of supervisory authority," she said. "Failure to comply results in disciplinary action."

The stamp above her head pulsed. It was a massive, iron thing, rusted with time. It swung slightly, like a pendulum, aiming not at his chest, but at his clipboard.

A new notification flickered in Dale's peripheral vision.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** *Violation Detected: Insubordination (Historical).* *Effect: Thoroughness Rating locked at current value.* *Condition: Submit compliant report to unlock further progression.*

Dale stared at the text, which was it. This was the trap. The Authority's system rewarded thoroughness, but it also punished defiance of its own logical structure.

Margaret wasn't just a monster. She was a test.

If he fought her, he lost points.

If he ignored her, he lost points. If he submitted a report to a dead supervisor, he accepted the premise that he was failing.

But if he didn't submit, he was stuck. His rating was frozen. He couldn't unlock the next tier of protocols needed to seal the Rift.

He looked at the two Inspectors. They were still pointing their weapons at him, their faces hidden behind gas masks that leaked black smoke. They weren't moving. They were waiting for the verdict.

Dale clicked his pen, and he looked at the blank line under *Employee Comments*.

He didn't write an apology. He didn't write a defense. He wrote a correction.

*Supervisor: Margaret Vance. Status: Deceased. Date of Death: [Redacted]. Cause: Structural Collapse due to untreated infestation (Class I, Verminous).*

*Correction: Supervisor lacked certification for Class I biological hazards. Reassessment of prior disciplinary action (Termination for 'Excessive Caution') is invalid. Action was necessary. Protocol was followed.*

*Recommendation: Post-humous commendation for failure to recognize entry points.*

Dale tore the page out, and he held it up.

Margaret's eyes widened. The black pits swirled. The stamp halted its swing.

"Invalid format," she said. But her voice was thinner now. Less certain.

"Format is correct," Dale said.

"OSHA 1910.1200. Hazard Communication Standard.

You failed to communicate the hazard.

I corrected the record.

"

He stepped forward. One step. Then another.

The Inspectors lowered their weapons slightly.

Margaret raised the pen again. She tried to stab it at Dale's eye.

Dale didn't dodge. He caught her wrist.

The contact was instant. Cold. Slimy. The gray powder on her skin transferred to his glove, burning through the latex.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** *Contact Initiated: Reanimated Supervisor.* *Cross-Contamination Risk: High.* *Warning: Field Guide Entry 'Supervisor, Margaret' is now corrupted.* *Penalty: -40% Damage Output for next 10 minutes.*

Dale felt the debuff hit him like a physical blow. His muscles seized. His vision blurred. The cost of touching her was immediate and severe.

But he had her wrist, and he didn't try to break it. He didn't try to punch her. He looked at the pen.

*Weakness: None.*

The system said there was no weakness.

But Dale knew pest control. He knew that when you can't kill the source, you starve it. You remove its support.

Margaret was a construct. She was a memory given form by the Rift.

Her power came from the authority she believed she held.

Her clipboard.

Her pen. The act of judging.

Dale wrenched the pen from her grip, which came away with a sound like tearing meat. Gray fluid sprayed across Dale's chest.

Margaret screamed. It wasn't a human scream. It was the sound of a filing cabinet closing on a finger.

The stamp above her head shattered.

It broke into a thousand shards of rusted metal that rained down on the sterile floor.

The Inspectors lunged.

Dale dropped the pen. He kicked Margaret's knees. She collapsed, her legs giving out as if the strings had been cut. She hit the floor hard, her body folding into unnatural angles.

He didn't look at her. He turned to the Inspectors.

"Report filed," Dale said.

He pulled the vial from his belt. It was empty now. He had used the last dose on the colleague.

He had no weapon. No protocol. Just his hands and the knowledge that the system was watching.

The lead Inspector stepped forward. He raised his weapon.

Dale didn't flinch. He held his ground. He looked at the Inspector's chest plate.

There was a crack in the armor. A small fissure running down the center.

Dale had seen that before. Not on a person. On a wall. On a foundation.

*Structural failure.*

The Inspector fired.

The beam struck Dale's shoulder. It didn't burn. It froze.

Pain, white and absolute, shot down his arm. His left arm went dead.

Dale gritted his teeth. He didn't fall. He used the momentum of the hit to spin, grabbing the second Inspector's ankle.

He swept the leg.

The Inspector fell.

Not because Dale was strong. Because the Inspector was confused. He expected a hero.

He expected a dodge, and he didn't expect a technician who treated combat like a slip-and-fall liability claim.

Dale scrambled to his feet. He grabbed the fallen Inspector's weapon. It was heavy. Cold.

He didn't aim it at the leader. He aimed it at the ceiling.

The fluorescent lights flickered. Ozone crackled.

He fired.

The shot wasn't a bullet. It was a containment beam. It hit the light fixture, overloading it.

The bulb burst. Glass rained down.

But the important thing wasn't the glass. It was the dark.

The sterile hall plunged into shadow. The Inspectors' visors, designed for high-contrast identification, were useless. They flailed, their weapons swinging wildly.

Dale moved, and he didn't run away. He ran toward the center of the room. Toward Margaret's body.

She was still twitching. The gray powder was spreading from her, forming patterns on the floor. Like frass. Like insect droppings.

Dale kicked her clipboard away, and he looked at the body.

It was dissolving. Not dying. Sublimating.

Turning into pure data.

A new notification appeared.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** *Job Complete: Cognitive Hazard Neutralized.* *Method: Documentation Correction.* *Thoroughness Rating: Unlocked.* *New Entry Added: Supervisor, Margaret (Deconstructed).* *Bonus: +50 Treatment Points for Creative Application of Liability Logic.*

Dale exhaled.

His left arm hung useless at his side. The frostbite was spreading. He could feel the crystals forming under his skin.

He had won, and he had beaten Margaret. He had beaten the Inspectors.

But he hadn't escaped.

The darkness in the hall receded. The emergency lights kicked on, red and pulsing.

The Inspectors were back on their feet. They had repaired their visors. They were looking at him with new expressions. Not anger.

Curiosity.

The leader stepped forward. He holstered his weapon.

He pulled off his gas mask.

Underneath was not a human face. It was a lattice of black metal, interwoven with nerve endings. The Authority's true form. Or at least, a piece of it.

"You used the liability clause," the Inspector said. His voice was like grinding gravel. "Section 12, Paragraph 4: *Incompetence of the Authority is not grounds for employee termination.*"

Dale wiped blood from his lip.

"It's a loophole."

"It's a precedent," the Inspector said.

He looked at the dissolving remains of Margaret.

"You've created a new entry in the Field Guide. *Supervisor, Margaret.* But it's empty. You haven't completed the protocol.

You haven't verified the kill.

"

Dale frowned. "She's gone."

"Her data is gone. The infestation remains." The Inspector pointed to the floor.

The gray powder was moving. It was flowing toward the walls. Toward the doors.

"It's seeding," the Inspector said. "Every time you fight the Authority, you feed the Rift.

Margaret was a node.

You destroyed the node. Now the network is looking for a new host.

"

Dale looked at his hands. The gray powder was on his gloves. It was seeping into the fabric.

He looked at the vial. Empty.

He looked at the door. It was sealed.

And then he heard it.

A sound from the walls. A scratching. Thousands of tiny legs.

Not rats.

Not beetles.

*Ants.*

Giant, black, armored ants, the size of dogs. They were pouring out of the seams in the floor.

The Inspector backed away.

"Class V Infestation. Dimensional Ants. *Formica abyssus.*"

Dale's Field Guide flashed.

*Entry Not Found.* *Identification Required.* *Time Limit: 30 Seconds.*

If he didn't identify them, he couldn't use the targeted damage bonus. Without the bonus, he couldn't kill them fast enough. Without killing them, the Rift would expand.

And if he identified them, he admitted that the Authority's system was flawed. That even they didn't know what was coming.

The first ant lunged. Its mandibles snapped shut on Dale's good arm.

Pain. Hot, acidic pain.

Dale screamed. He ripped his arm free, leaving a chunk of flesh in the ant's jaws.

He looked at the ant, which had a red stripe down its abdomen.

Not a German cockroach. Not a brown-banded cockroach.

It was a soldier ant. But the mandibles were made of bone.

*Bone-jawed Ant.*

He didn't have time to think. He grabbed a shard of broken glass from the floor.

He didn't stab the ant, and he stabbed the floor.

He drove the glass into the gray powder.

*Frass.*

The ant paused. It sniffed the powder. It tasted it.

Then it turned to its sisters.

Dale had not identified the species, and he had identified the food source.

He had used the environment to mislead the colony.

The ants turned on each other. Confused. Aggressive.

The Inspector watched, silent.

Dale stood there, bleeding, his left arm black with frost, his right hand clutching a shard of glass.

He had bought ten seconds, and the ants were eating each other.

But the walls were still shaking. More were coming.

And from the far end of the hall, from the dark corner where Margaret had stood, a new figure emerged.

It was tall. Thin. Wearing a suit.

It had no face. Just a smooth, white oval where features should be.

It held a clipboard.

The Inspector looked at Dale.

"You failed the practical exam," the Inspector said. "You didn't eliminate the threat. You delayed it."

"I documented it," Dale said. His voice was raspy.

"You started a war," the Inspector said.

The faceless figure raised its pen.

It wrote one word on the clipboard.

*RECRUIT.*

The faceless figure pointed at Dale.

The ants stopped fighting. They turned. All of them. Hundreds of them.

They looked at Dale.

Not with hunger.

With recognition.

Dale realized then why Margaret had been there. Why the Inspectors had been waiting.

They hadn't been trying to kill him, and they had been trying to see if he was qualified to lead them.

The Rift wasn't an infestation.

It was a hiring process.

And Dale had just applied.
