Chapter 180: Signed in Blood

# Chapter 180

Dale pressed his bleeding thumb against the steel casing until the metal grew warm. It stung, a sharp chemical burn that cut through the numbness of the darkness. Dale didn't wipe it away. He let the black stain settle into the whorls of his fingerprint, creating a record that couldn't be erased.

The door remained shut. The keypad flashed a steady, mocking red.

*"Audit complete. Subject is ready for integration."*

The voice in his head wasn't auditory. It was tactile, a vibration in the molars.

Dale ignored it, and he turned his back on the door and faced the server rack. The hum had stopped, but the fans were still spinning down, their blades slowing with a rhythmic *whir-click, whir-click* that sounded uncomfortably like a closing eyelid.

He needed light. He needed data. And he needed to determine if the thing in the cube was still alive, or if it was merely a husk.

Dale reached into his belt and pulled out a UV inspection light. It was Class-3 equipment, meant for revealing urine trails and fungal spores in residential basements. It felt woefully inadequate against the dimensional breach, but it was what he had. He flicked the switch.

The purple beam cut through the dark.

The older Dale in the cube was gone.

Not vanished. *Integrated*, and the acrylic casing was now filled with a swirling, viscous gray slurry. The shape of the man was gone, replaced by a geometric abstraction of limbs and torso, fused together by the same black substance that stained Dale's hand.

A System notification chimed, soft and polite, in the corner of his vision.

**STATUS ALERT: HOSTILE ENTITY DETECTED.** **CLASSIFICATION: NARRATIVE PARASITE (POST-MERGER).** **THREAT LEVEL: UNRATED.**

Dale frowned. Unrated meant the Authority couldn't categorize it because it hadn't existed before. This was new. This was a violation of the standard taxonomy.

He pulled his clipboard from the briefcase. The paper was blank, save for the header. He uncapped a pen that wasn't leaking this time. He wrote, his handwriting steady despite the shaking in his left hand.

*Entry 1: Subject has undergone total biological merger. No distinct ocular or oral features remain. Mass appears to be mimicking the server rack's cooling vents. Heat signature: Ambient.*

He looked up. The slurry pulsed. It didn't breathe. It *expanded*.

The gray mass pushed against the acrylic. A crack appeared. Then another.

Dale stepped back. He didn't run. Running triggered the chase instinct in Class-4 predators, and he suspected this thing, whatever it was, had the predatory instincts of a thousand consumed technicians.

The crack widened. The slurry oozed out, pooling on the floor. It moved with the viscosity of molasses, but with the intent of a swarm.

Dale checked his Field Guide. The entry for *Narrative Parasite* was still there, but the text was scrambling, letters rearranging themselves into gibberish.

**CONTAMINATION WARNING: FIELD GUIDE CORRUPTED.** **CROSS-CONTAMINATION PENALTY ACTIVE: -40% DAMAGE EFFICACY FOR ALL CLASS-IV PROTOCOLS.**

Of course. He had used a containment protocol designed for a hive-mind insect on a sentient narrative entity. The Authority was punishing him for misclassification.

"Class IV protocols are compromised," Dale muttered. "Need to pivot to Class I."

Class I.

The basics. Identification and documentation.

He couldn't fight this thing with a spray can or a trap.

He had to *understand* it. If he could identify its core weakness, the System might grant a targeted bonus, even with the penalty active.

The slurry surged forward, forming a pseudo-limb that slammed into the server rack. Sparks showered down. The rack groaned, metal twisting under the pressure.

Dale didn't engage the limb. He engaged the environment.

He kicked open the panel beneath the main server unit. Dust and debris spilled out.

He reached in, not for the power supply, but for the coolant lines.

They were leaking.

The dark fluid dripping onto the floor wasn't just coolant; it was mixed with the black ichor from the cube.

He took a sample vial from his kit. He held it under the drip.

*Drop. Drop. Drop.*

The vial filled. He sealed it.

He held the vial up to his UV light. The liquid glowed a sickly, radioactive green.

**ANALYSIS COMPLETE.** **SUBSTANCE IDENTIFIED: LIQUID NARRATIVE (UNSTABILIZED).** **WEAKNESS: VOLATILE AT HIGH TEMPERATURES. REACTS VIOLENTLY WITH STATIC ELECTRICITY.**

Dale stared at the text. Volatile. Static.

He looked at the slurry on the floor. It was inching toward his boots. He could feel the cold of it, a void-like chill that promised not death, but deletion.

He wouldn't die. He would simply cease to have ever existed.

His wife's memories of him would fade.

His son's photo albums would go blank, which was worse than death.

"I need static," Dale said.

The server room was full of electronics. But the power was out. The backup generators were silent.

He looked at the cooling fans. They were still spinning, albeit slowly. Kinetic energy.

He ran to the main rack. The slurry had wrapped around the base, climbing the metal like ivy. It touched his boot. The leather sizzled.

Dale gritted his teeth. He didn't pull his foot back. He planted it.

He grabbed a loose fiber-optic cable from the rack. It was thick, braided. He wrapped it around his hand. Then he grabbed a second cable from the adjacent rack.

He needed to create a circuit. A spark gap.

The slurry surged, forming a face. No eyes. Just a mouth, a wide, circular hole that screamed without sound.

Dale ignored the face. He jammed the two cables together, twisting the bare ends.

*Crack.*

A tiny blue spark jumped between the wires.

The slurry recoiled. It hissed, a sound like steam escaping a kettle.

It worked. But one spark wasn't enough. He needed a chain reaction.

He looked at the ceiling. Sprinkler system.

The pipes were dry. The water had been drained for maintenance during the lockdown.

But the pipes weren't empty. They were filled with the black slurry. The system had integrated the infestation into its plumbing.

Dale looked at the sprinkler heads. They were clogged with the gray goo.

He pulled out his multi-tool. The pliers snapped the casing off a nearby conduit box. Inside, the wires were frayed, exposed.

He needed a trigger.

The older Dale's consciousness was still there, he knew. It was in the slurry. It was in the voice in his head.

*"Integration is inevitable,"* the voice whispered. *"You are part of the nest now."*

"Shut up," Dale said. He twisted the wires together. A larger spark, yellow and angry, jumped.

The slurry screamed. It lashed out, a tendril whipping toward his throat.

Dale ducked. The tendril struck the conduit box. Metal sparked. Fire flared.

The fire alarm didn't sound. The sprinklers didn't trigger.

But the heat did.

The volatile liquid on the floor bubbled. The green glow intensified.

Dale realized he had to be more precise. He couldn't just burn it. He had to *detonate* it.

He needed a confined space, and he looked at the server rack. It was sealed. Airtight.

If he could get the slurry inside...

He backed away, toward the cube. The empty shell of the cube was still on the floor. The crack had widened. The slurry was trying to get back in, trying to reform, to regain its structure.

Dale kicked the cube. It skidded across the floor, leaving a trail of black slime.

He kicked it again, sending it toward the server rack.

The slurry followed. It flowed back into the cube, drawn to the familiar geometry.

Dale watched. His heart hammered against his ribs, a frantic bird in a cage.

The slurry filled the cube. The crack sealed, not with acrylic, but with the black substance itself. The cube was whole again.

Dale grabbed a heavy wrench from his kit. He ran to the server rack.

He didn't throw it. He jammed it into the gap between the rack and the cube.

*Clang.*

The cube was pinned against the rack. The metal groaned.

Dale pulled out his lighter. A standard disposable butane.

He held the flame under the black seam where the cube met the server rack.

The volatile liquid reacted instantly. The green glow flared, then turned white.

**WARNING: EXPLOSIVE REACTION DETECTED.** **EVACUATE IMMEDIATELY.**

Dale didn't evacuate. He held the position. He needed to ensure the blast directed upward, away from the exit door. The server rack acted as a baffle.

The pressure built. The hum returned, a low, vibrating drone that shook the floor.

The black slurry inside the cube boiled.

Dale stepped back. One step. Two.

*Boom.*

The explosion was contained, but barely. A shockwave of heat and force blasted outward. Dale was thrown backward, skidding across the linoleum.

The server rack buckled. The cube shattered.

Shrapnel of black glass and burning liquid flew across the room.

Dale curled into a ball, covering his head with his arms. The heat singed his hair. The smell of ozone and burnt plastic filled his nose.

Silence returned, and he lay there, ears ringing. Smoke hung in the air, thick and gray.

He coughed. His lungs burned.

He checked his limbs. Twisted. Bruised. Intact.

He sat up, and the server room was destroyed. The rack was melted slag. The cube was gone.

But the door was still locked.

And the red line on the map on the surviving monitor was still pulsing.

But now, there was a second line. A green one.

It was moving, which was moving *away* from him.

Dale crawled to the monitor. The screen was cracked, but readable.

The green line had exited the building. It was heading toward the city center.

**TREATMENT POINTS AWARDED: 500.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 3.1.** **NOTE: PARTIAL ELIMINATION. HOSTILE CORE ESCAPED VIA NETWORK BACKBONE.**

Partial elimination. The Authority was angry. A rating of 3.1 was abysmal. It was a failing grade.

But it wasn't the worst part.

The green line wasn't just data. It was alive.

Dale looked at his hand. The ink was gone.

In its place, a single black vein pulsed on the back of his wrist.

It wasn't a stain, which was a connection.

**SYSTEM ALERT: NEW PROTOCOL UNLOCKED.** **PROTOCOL: SYMPATHETIC RESONANCE.** **DESCRIPTION: TECHNICIAN HAS MERGED PARTIALLY WITH HOSTILE ENTITY. YOU CAN TRACK THE INFESTATION THROUGH THE DIMENSIONAL LATTICE. WARNING: PROLONGED USE WILL RESULT IN TOTAL ASSIMILATION.**

Dale stared at the vein. It throbbed in time with his heartbeat.

He could feel them.

Thousands of them. In the walls.

In the wires.

In the minds of the people sleeping in the apartments above.

He wasn't just a technician anymore. He was a carrier.

The door clicked.

It opened.

Not because the lock broke.

Because the Authority let it open, and a figure stood in the doorway.

It wore a hazmat suit. White. Pristine. Unmarked.

The faceplate was reflective, showing Dale's own battered, soot-streaked face.

The figure stepped into the room. It didn't check for threats. It didn't scan for contaminants.

It looked at Dale.

Then it looked at the melted server rack.

Then it looked at the green line still pulsing on the monitor.

The figure raised a hand. A gloved hand. It pointed at Dale.

The voice that came out wasn't the Authority's. It was deeper. Grander.

*"You have failed the practical exam, Kowalski."*

Dale tried to stand, but his legs gave out.

*"However,"* the figure continued, *"you have successfully demonstrated the new integration mechanic. The Authority finds this... useful."*

The figure extended a hand.

*"Come with me. We have a Class-V infestation in the capital. And you are now qualified to handle it."*

Dale looked at the hand. He looked at the black vein on his wrist.

He knew what the figure was. It wasn't a supervisor. It wasn't an inspector.

It was a recruiter.

And if he took that hand, he wouldn't just be exterminating monsters.

He would be becoming one.

The black vein pulsed. It felt good. Warm.

Dale closed his eyes, and he reached into his briefcase. He pulled out his pen.

He wrote a single word on his clipboard.

*No.*

Then he threw the clipboard at the figure.
