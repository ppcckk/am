Chapter 102: Burn Down the Paperwork

# Chapter 102

The wall didn't just explode. It shed.

Plaster crumbled away in wet, gray sheets, revealing not insulation or wooden studs, but a pulsating membrane of chitin and slime. The smell hit Dale first, and it wasn't the usual musk of dead rats or the ammonia tang of urine-soaked drywall. It was sweet, like rotting fruit left in a sealed jar, mixed with the metallic bite of ozone.

Dale didn’t step back. He stepped forward, his boots crunching on the debris of the living room floor. He pulled his clipboard from his belt with a fluidity that suggested he’d done this ten thousand times, which, in a way, he had. The difference was that the infestation was currently trying to eat the house from the inside out.

The woman in the suit, Agent Vane, scrambled backward, her heels clicking frantically against the laminate flooring. Her face was pale, the bureaucratic calm shattered by the visceral reality of the breach.

She pointed at the hole in the wall.

"That’s a Class VI structural compromise. You need to evacuate the zone. I’m calling for a containment team."

"Negative," Dale said, not looking up from his Field Guide. The *Rattus profundus* entry was still blank. The *Acarus riftis* page was fading, the ink dissolving like sugar in water. But a new entry was forming in real-time, the text scratching itself onto the parchment with jagged, erratic strokes.

"Sir, this is not a negotiation," Vane snapped, her hand going to the sleek device at her hip. "The Authority has flagged this as a Priority One breach. If you interfere with protocol, I will revoke your license."

Dale ignored her. He was reading the new entry.

**SPECIES: *Gasteria infestis* (Gut-Grub).** **CLASS: IV.** **WEAKNESS: Exoskeleton integrity compromised by rapid temperature differentials.**

He looked at the wall.

The exposed tissue rippled. A dozen small, white worms, each no bigger than a finger, wriggled out of the fissure, blind and hungry.

They moved with a jerky, insectoid speed, dragging themselves across the floorboards toward the nearest heat source.

Vane.

Vane screamed, kicking one worm away, and it landed on the counter, bouncing once before righting itself and diving at her ankle. She shrieked, hopping on one foot.

Dale sighed. It was a sigh of profound professional disappointment. "You’re contaminating the workspace," he muttered.

He reached into his tool belt, bypassing the larger canisters and pulling out a small, pressurized flask of liquid nitrogen. It was meant for freezing ant trails to identify the nest structure without alerting the colony. It was not meant for combat. It was not meant for Class IV dimensional fauna.

The cross-contamination warning flashed in his peripheral vision, a bright, angry red.

**WARNING: PROTOCOL MISMATCH. LIQUID NITROGEN VS. *GASTERIA INFESTIS*.

EFFECTIVENESS: 40%.

PENALTY: FIELD GUIDE CORRUPTION (24 HOURS).

TREATMENT POINTS: REDUCED.**

Dale didn’t care. He uncapped the flask.

Vane was backing toward the front door, her suit pants stained with a smear of white slime. "Open it!" she shouted, fumbling with the lock. "Get out!"

"The door is a secondary entry point," Dale said, walking past her. "If you open it, you let the pressure differential equalize. The infestation spreads to the neighbors.

Do you know how long it takes to clear a residential block of Grubs? Six weeks.

Minimum."

"I don't care about the neighbors!" Vane yelled. She yanked the lock. The door didn’t open.

The frame had twisted, warped by the moisture seeping from the walls, and the wood was swelling, jamming the latch tight.

Dale stopped. He looked at the jammed door. He looked at the worms multiplying on the floor. He looked at the bulging wall, where a larger shape was beginning to push through, a mass of gray flesh and segmented legs.

This was bad. This was bad.

If he couldn't contain the primary nest, the Authority would send the Sanitation Division.

They wouldn't exterminate. They would demolish. They would burn the house to the foundation.

And if the Rift was anchored to the foundation, burning the house might not close the rift. It might make it wider.

Dale needed to kill the parent. Not the worms. The parent.

He scanned the room. The kitchen was a mess. The fridge was humming, a low, threatening vibration; the sink was clogged with a slurry of black ichor that bubbled over the edge.

"Vane," Dale said, his voice dropping an octave, slipping into the calm, procedural tone he used when dealing with aggressive homeowners who refused to vacate their properties for spraying. "Stop moving. You’re creating air currents that attract the larvae."

Vane froze, one hand still on the locked door handle. "What?"

"The *Gasteria* are thermotropic and thigmotactic.

They seek heat and touch.

Your body heat is leading them. But your movement is scattering them.

If you stand still, they swarm you. If you run, they chase you. But if you create a thermal decoy..."

Dale pointed to the oven. It was off, but the pilot light was flickering, a small blue flame dancing in the dark.

"Turn on the oven," he said.

"Are you insane?"

"Preheat to five hundred degrees. Now."

Vane stared at him. Then, looking at the worms creeping closer to her ankles, she turned and slammed her hand against the control panel. The oven roared to life, the gas igniting with a soft *whump*. The heating element started glowing orange.

Dale watched the worms. They hesitated, sensing the change in temperature.

Then, as the heat rose, they turned.

All of them. A dozen tiny, blind heads oriented toward the oven.

"Good," Dale said.

He walked toward the wall, and the larger shape was pushing harder now. The plaster cracked further, raining dust.

A limb, thick and covered in chitinous plates, punched through the surface.

It was followed by a mandible, clicking softly.

Dale opened his Field Guide. The *Gasteria* entry was half-written, the weakness highlighted in yellow. *Exoskeleton integrity compromised by rapid temperature differentials.*

He looked at the liquid nitrogen in his hand. 40% efficiency. A 40% debuff. A corrupted entry.

But he looked at the parent. It was huge.

Easily the size of a German Shepherd.

If he tried to engage it with his standard Class II spray, it would absorb the chemical and burrow deeper.

He needed physical trauma combined with thermal shock, and he couldn't use the nitrogen on the parent. It was too small a volume. But he could use it on the floor.

Dale poured the entire flask onto the floor directly in front of the bulging wall. The liquid hissed, boiling instantly, creating a cloud of white vapor and a sheet of ice that spread rapidly across the hardwood.

The temperature dropped. The *Gasteria* larvae on the floor froze, their movements slowing to a halt, and they didn't die, but they became sluggish, encased in a thin layer of frost.

"Vane, the oven!" Dale shouted.

"It's preheating!" she screamed back.

"Open it!"

Vane pulled the oven door. The heat blasted out into the kitchen. At the same time, Dale kicked the door to the refrigerator open.

The compressor unit was working overtime, the coils sweating condensation. Dale grabbed the rear panel of the fridge and ripped it off, exposing the condenser coils.

He smashed them with the butt of his flashlight.

Refrigerant, a high-pressure gas, hissed out into the air. It wasn't nitrogen, but it was cold. Cold.

The mix of the nitrogen ice on the floor and the refrigerant gas in the air created a localized zone of extreme cold right in front of the wall.

The parent *Gasteria* pushed through. It emerged fully, a nightmare of gray flesh and black eyes, its legs skittering on the frozen floor. It screeched, a sound like metal tearing.

It was confused. Its heat-seeking biology was overwhelmed by the sudden drop in ambient temperature, and it shrank back, its exoskeleton cracking under the thermal stress.

Dale moved, and he didn't shoot. He didn't stab. He stepped onto the ice, slid under the creature’s grasping legs, and slammed his clipboard into the joint of its hind leg.

The clipboard was reinforced polymer, rated for impact.

The joint was brittle from the cold.

*Crack.*

The leg snapped. The creature lurched, falling sideways. Dale was already moving. He rolled out from under it, came up behind it, and used his flashlight to strike the back of its neck, right where the plates met.

The creature thrashed, smashing a table in half. Vane ducked behind the counter.

Dale circled. He needed to finish it. He needed to verify.

He pulled out his spray can. Not the insecticide. The marker.

He sprayed a bright orange circle on the creature's exposed underbelly, where the shell was softest. Then he sprayed a second circle on its head.

**SYSTEM ALERT: MARKER APPLIED. TARGET ACQUIRED.**

**CROSS-CONTAMINATION WARNING: USING COMBAT MARKER ON DIMENSIONAL FAUNA. FIELD GUIDE ENTRY *GASTERIA INFESTIS* CORRUPTED FOR 24 HOURS. TREATMENT POINTS REDUCED TO 50%.**

Dale didn't care. The marker wasn't for the system. It was for him.

"Vane, the broom!" he shouted.

"What?"

"The broom! The handle!"

Vane grabbed the broom from the corner. She hesitated, then threw it.

Dale caught it mid-air. He didn't throw it back. He jammed the handle into the orange circle on the creature's neck.

The creature shrieked. The plastic handle, rigid and dense, acted as a fulcrum. Dale twisted his body, using his weight and the leverage of the handle to torque the head against the broken body.

The neck snapped, and the creature went limp. Its legs twitched once, then stilled.

Dale stood there, breathing hard.

The house was silent again. The worms on the floor were frozen, dead or dying; the hole in the wall was still there, but the pressure had eased.

The bulging had stopped, and he looked at his clipboard. The report was waiting.

*Incident: Class IV Infestation. Location: Residential Unit 4B.* *Action: Thermal shock and mechanical neutralization.* *Result: Elimination verified.*

He wrote it down. His handwriting was neat. Precise.

Vane stood up from behind the counter. Her suit was ruined. Her hair was a mess. She looked at the dead creature, then at the ice on the floor, then at Dale.

"You broke the rules," she said. Her voice was quiet. Dangerous.

"I completed the protocol," Dale corrected.

"Identify.

Isolate. Eliminate.

Verify."

"You corrupted a Class IV entry," Vane said. "Do you know what that means?

The Authority tracks those entries.

If they're corrupted, the data is lost. The next time we see one, we won't have the weakness documented. You just set us back weeks."

Dale looked up. "We won't see one again. I sealed the entry point."

He pointed to the wall. He had used a roll of industrial-grade silicone sealant from his belt to plug the hole. It was ugly, gray, and thick, but it was airtight.

"You can't seal a Rift with silicone," Vane said.

"It's not a Rift," Dale said.

"It's a leak. The pressure differential caused it.

Once the source is dead, the leak stops.

I checked. The dimensional bleed is dropping. The house is stabilizing."

Vane walked over to the wall. She touched the silicone. It was warm. She looked at Dale with a mixture of fear and grudging respect.

"You're good," she said. "Too good for Class II."

"I'm not getting promoted," Dale said. He wiped his hands on a rag.

"I'm filing a violation report against the homeowner.

Improper sealing of foundation cracks.

Failure to maintain moisture barriers. And a code violation for the kitchen remodel that compromised the load-bearing wall."

Vane stared at him. "You're going to fine the homeowner?"

"It's the law."

"The Authority doesn't care about local codes."

"The Authority pays for my license," Dale said. "And I pay my taxes. It's a loop."

He picked up his clipboard. "I'm leaving. My shift ends in ten minutes."

He walked to the front door. He kicked the jammed latch. It popped open.

Outside, the sun was setting. The street was quiet. The neighbors were still inside, peering through blinds. Dale walked down the driveway, past the police cars that had arrived too late, past the confused officers who were still trying to figure out what the hell had happened.

He got into his van. The van was clean. Sterile. It smelled of lavender and industrial solvent.

He started the engine. The radio clicked on, automatically tuning to the Authority's emergency broadcast band.

*"All units, be advised. Rift signature detected in Sector 7. Scale: City-wide.

Multiple Class V entities detected. All Class III and above technicians, report to base immediately."*

Dale frowned. He looked at his dashboard. His Thoroughness Rating was up. 9.4. A new high.

But then, a second notification flashed on his windshield, projected by the van’s internal system.

**NOTICE: YOUR FIELD GUIDE HAS BEEN FLAGGED FOR AUDIT.** **REASON: SUSPECTED UNAUTHORIZED MODIFICATION.** **PENALTY: SUSPENSION PENDING REVIEW.**

Dale slammed his hand on the steering wheel. "Unauthorized modification?"

He hadn't modified anything. He had used a marker. He had used nitrogen. He had used a broom.

Then he remembered. The *Gasteria* entry. He had added a note. A small note, in the margin, where the system said *Weakness*.

He had written: *Exoskeleton brittle when frozen. Use leverage.*

The system didn't allow user-generated insights unless they were validated by the Authority. By writing it down, he had claimed ownership of a discovery that the Authority deemed proprietary.

He had stolen intellectual property.

Dale sat in the silence of his van. The rain started to fall, tapping against the roof.

He opened his glove box, and he pulled out a small, locked box. Inside was a pen. A black pen.

And a piece of paper.

On the paper was a sentence, written in his own handwriting, weeks ago, before the Rifts had even become a public threat.

*The Authority doesn't want them dead. It wants them measured.*

He looked at the message on his windshield. *Suspension Pending Review.*

If he was suspended, he couldn't treat the next infestation, and the Class V entities in Sector 7 would go untreated. They would spread. They would multiply.

And Dale knew, with the cold certainty of a man who has spent fourteen years watching nests rebuild, that if you don't treat the nest, the problem comes back.

He started the van. He drove toward Sector 7.

He didn't have a license. He didn't have protection. He had a clipboard, a can of spray, and a violation report that was already three weeks late.

As he drove, the radio chatter changed. The emergency broadcast stopped. A new voice came on. It was smooth, calm, and inhuman.

*"Technician Kowalski. Please pull over. We need to discuss your Thoroughness Rating."*

Dale’s foot hovered over the brake. He looked in the rearview mirror.

Behind him, in the shadows of the street, something was moving. Not a worm. Not a rat.

A man. Or something that used to be a man.

He was wearing a suit.

Just like Vane’s. But his skin was gray, and his eyes were black pits; he was walking toward the van, ignoring the rain.

Dale didn't pull over. He accelerated.

The radio crackled again. * "Your rating is 9.4. You are qualified for Level III. Come to the Authority HQ. Now." *

Dale gripped the steering wheel. He knew the Authority.

He knew their protocols.

If he went, he would be audited, and they would take his Field Guide. They would erase the note.

They would strip him of his rank.

And if he didn't go, he would be unlicensed. An illegal. A rogue exterminator.

The thing behind him sped up. It was running now, its movements jagged and fast.

Dale checked his mirror. The figure was closer.

He could see the teeth, and they weren't human. They were serrated. Like a saw.

He had one choice. He could stop and fight, breaking his own suspension, risking immediate termination. Or he could drive to Sector 7, into the heart of the infestation, with no license, no backup, and a Field Guide that was already corrupted.

He looked at the dashboard. The fuel light was on.

He looked at the mirror. The thing was on his bumper.

Dale made a decision, and he reached for the glove box. He didn't grab the pen. He grabbed the flare gun.

He wasn't going to HQ. He wasn't going to fight the man.

He was going to Sector 7. He was going to find the biggest nest he could find. And he was going to burn it down.

Because sometimes, the only way to get rid of an infestation that the Authority won't let you touch, is to make sure there's nothing left to document.

He started with the biggest nest.
