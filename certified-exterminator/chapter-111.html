Chapter 111: Incident Report 117

# Chapter 111

The solvent can hissed, a high-pitched mechanical shriek that cut through the wet air. Dale Kowalski held the nozzle steady, aiming not at the pulsating mass beneath the plastic, but at the gap under the dumpster lid. He coated the metal rim in a thick, viscous layer of industrial stripper. The smell hit the air instantly, sharp and chemical, masking the rot.

Dale stepped back. He clicked his pen.

*Protocol initiated: Chemical Barrier Application. Species: Unknown. Hypothesis: Scent-masking camouflage.*

The black bags stopped shifting. The too-many-limbed thing inside them froze. Silence returned to Elm Street, heavy and thick.

A voice cracked from the alley entrance.

"You're poisoning the evidence."

Dale didn't turn. He kept his eyes on the dumpster. The solvent bubbled where it touched the rusted metal.

"Contamination control," Dale said, his voice flat. "Standard procedure for unidentified biomass."

"Who the hell are you?"

A figure stepped into the dim light. Tall, wearing a tactical vest that looked brand new.

The vest had a patch on the chest: *Hazard Control Unit – District 4*.

The man's hands were gloved in thick, red rubber. He held a shotgun loosely across his chest.

"Technician Class II," Dale replied, not looking up from his clipboard. "Dale Kowalski. And you are trespassing in an active work zone."

The man laughed, a short, barking sound. "Active work zone? It's a dump site, pal. Nothing's active here except a bunch of rats getting their fill."

"Rats don't weave trash into camouflage nets."

Dale finally looked up. The stranger's eyes narrowed. He looked at Dale's clipboard, then at the can of solvent, then at the trembling bags.

"You're a cert-holder," the stranger said. It wasn't a question.

"I am."

"Level?"

"Five."

The stranger's grip tightened on his shotgun. "Five. That's mid-tier. You're just spraying cans."

"I'm documenting a breach."

"Who authorized this?"

"The ground."

The stranger stepped closer.

His boots crunched on broken glass. "I'm Sergeant Miller, Hazard Control.

We got a report of unlicensed activity.

Unsanctioned use of Class-C solvents in a public sector."

"Class-C is approved for organic decay removal under Section 4, Paragraph B."

"Not if the target is classified as Threat-Level Alpha."

Dale paused. His pen hovered over the paper.

"Alpha?"

"You didn't check the registry?" Miller shook his head, disgusted. "Look at the frass.

Look at the entry point.

You're treating this like a sewer rat, Kowalski.

It's a Rift-Stalker. Nidovorous type."

Dale frowned. He looked at the bags. The shapes inside were still now. Too still.

"Nidovorous," Dale repeated. "Nest-feeding?"

"Baiting," Miller corrected. "They don't hide in trash. They use the smell of decay to lure scavengers.

Then they eat the scavengers. You're blocking their lure with chemicals.

You're interfering with a natural predation cycle."

"Nature didn't crack the sidewalk," Dale said. "The Rift did. This is an infestation, not a wildlife observation."

"Authority rules state that natural behaviors are to be monitored, not disrupted, unless direct threat is imminent."

"Direct threat is imminent," Dale said, pointing his pen at the street. "See that crack? It's widening.

The roots are lifting.

If that thing moves, the foundation of the duplex next door shifts. I'm protecting the property structure."

Miller spat on the pavement. "You're a glorified ant-farm keeper. You don't have the clearance to stop a Stalker."

"I have a Thoroughness Rating of 9.4."

Miller blinked. That number landed. It was high. Higher than his own squad leaders.

"9.4?"

"Verified this morning."

Miller lowered the shotgun slightly. He looked at the bags again. They were bulging, trying to push against the chemical barrier Dale had created. The solvent burned the plastic, creating a slick, slippery shield.

"If you stop the spray, it charges," Miller said.

"If I stop the spray, it hunts," Dale corrected.

"I'm not trying to kill it. I'm trying to identify it. Field Guide update requires observation.

Killing it blind yields zero points and contaminates my entry."

"You're stalling."

"I'm working."

Miller pulled a radio from his vest. Static crackled.

"Dispatch, this is Miller.

I'm at Elm and 4th.

I have a Level V Tech interfering with a Nidovorous Stalker. He's using chemical deterrents.

Requesting permission to neutralize the target and detain the tech for protocol violation."

Dale watched the radio. He didn't move. He kept his hand on the solvent can.

"Dispatch to Miller," a voice crackled back. Cold.

Automated.

"Negative. The target is currently designated as Unidentified Biomass. No kill order issued.

Tech Kowalski is operating under Temporary Field Authority due to high Thoroughness Score.

Stand down, Miller."

Miller's face reddened. He slammed the radio off.

"You're lucky," he hissed.

"I'm thorough," Dale said. "There's a difference."

"You're delaying my job."

"My job is to document.

Yours is to clean up after me. If you kill it now, you destroy the data. You get no points.

I get points. You get a mess."

Miller growled. He stepped forward, reaching for the dumpster lid. "I'm opening it."

"Don't," Dale said.

"Watch me."

Miller grabbed the edge of the black bag. His gloves slipped on the solvent-coated plastic. He yanked. The bag tore.

Inside, something wet and pale unfolded.

It had six limbs.

Four of them were legs. Two were arms that ended in jagged, bone-like protrusions.

Its face was a cluster of eyes, no mouth, just a slit that vibrated with a low hum.

It didn't attack Miller. It looked at Dale.

Dale didn't breathe. He wrote.

*Visual Confirmation: Six-limbed. No mouth. Ocular cluster. Skin texture: Chitinous, pale.*

The creature tilted its head. It chattered, a sound like dry leaves rubbing together.

"Back up," Dale said to Miller.

Miller froze, his hand still on the torn plastic. "What's it doing?"

"It's scanning you."

"It's looking for a weak spot."

"It's looking for a certification level," Dale corrected. "It reads threats by their Authority signature. You're a Level III. It's deciding if you're worth the effort."

Miller paled. He released the bag. He stepped back, his boots scraping on the asphalt.

"It's judging me?"

"Everything is judged here," Dale said. He pointed his pen at the creature.

"Look at its legs.

See the joint structure? It's inefficient. It's adapted for burrowing, not running.

That's why it used the bags.

It can't move fast in open ground."

Miller stared. "So if we get it out of the bags..."

"It panics," Dale finished. "It exposes its flank. The soft tissue behind the third leg pair. That's the weak point."

The creature hissed. It shifted its weight. The pale flesh behind its legs pulsed.

"Document it," Dale said softly. "Write it down. If you write it down before you strike, the System recognizes the knowledge. +15% Damage Bonus."

Miller looked at his shotgun. Then he looked at Dale.

"Why would I trust you?"

"Because if you don't, you're shooting blind. And blind shots yield zero points. Do you want zero points, Sergeant? Or do you want to advance to Level IV?"

Miller's eyes widened. Level IV was the threshold for specialized squad command. It was the difference between being a grunt and a leader.

"How much?"

"The bonus applies to the next three shots. If you hit the flank."

Miller swallowed. He raised the shotgun. His hands shook, just slightly.

"Which flank?"

"Left. Third joint."

Miller aimed. He took a breath. He fired.

The blast knocked the creature backward. It shrieked, a sound like tearing metal. A spray of green ichor hit the pavement. The creature curled into a ball, protecting its core.

"Hit," Miller said, breathless.

"Damage registered," Dale said, checking the air. The faint blue text of the System flickered in his peripheral vision, visible only because he was close to the event.

*Target Weakened. Field Guide Entry: Nidovorous Stalker (Immature). Weakness: Posterior Chitin Gap.*

*Points Awarded: 45.*

*Dale Kowalski gained 45 TP.*

Miller lowered the gun. He looked at the creature, which was now twitching on the ground, leaking fluid.

"I need to finish it," Miller said.

"No," Dale said. "You don't. It's immobilized. If you kill it now, you miss the extraction phase."

"Extraction?"

"The core.

The nidus. It's inside that sac on its back. You cut it out, you save the specimen for study.

That's worth 200 points. Killing the whole thing is worth 50."

Miller looked at the gun, then at Dale. Greed warred with caution in his face.

"You're offering me a payout."

"I'm offering you a protocol. You follow the protocol, you get the points. You break it, you get nothing."

Miller holstered his shotgun. He pulled a knife from his belt. It was a bayonet-style blade, serrated and cruel.

"Show me."

Dale stepped forward. He didn't hesitate. He walked up to the twitching creature. It snapped at him, its limbs thrashing, but the solvent barrier kept it from reaching far.

"Hold it," Dale said. "Press your boot on the left shoulder. Keep it pinned. I'll cut."

Miller stepped in. He drove his boot onto the creature's chitinous shoulder. The thing screamed. Miller gritted his teeth, pushing down.

Dale pulled a utility knife from his pocket. Not a weapon. A tool. He had used it to score drywall and cut insulation.

He reached into the sac on the creature's back.

His hand went into wet, hot flesh.

The texture was wrong, rubbery and dense. He found the core.

A pulsing, dark lump.

"Cut," Dale said. He sliced through the connective tissue.

The core popped free. It fell into his gloved hand. It was warm.

"Step back," Dale said.

Miller jumped back, retching.

The creature went limp. The green fluid stopped flowing.

Dale wrapped the core in a sterile sample bag. He sealed it. He placed it in a cooler box he kept in his truck.

"Specimen secured," Dale said.

Miller wiped his mouth on his sleeve. "That's... that's gross."

"Data is never clean," Dale said. He pulled out a new form.

"Sign here.

Witnessed extraction. Protocol 7-A."

Miller stared at the form. "I'm signing for it?"

"You participated. You get 40% of the extraction points. I get 60% because I identified the weakness and performed the procedure."

Miller signed. His hand was steady now. The adrenaline had faded, replaced by the cold calculation of points.

"Good work, Kowalski," Miller said, the words tasting like ash in his mouth.

"Standard," Dale said. He closed the form.

A notification flashed in Dale's vision.

*Thoroughness Rating Updated: 9.5.* *New Notification: Priority Infestation Detected.* *Location: Suburban Park, 2 miles East.* *Threat: Class IX.*

Class IX.

Dale froze.

Class I was rats. Class V was a broodmother.

Class IX was a Rift Anchor.

Something that could tear a hole in reality and hold it open.

He looked at Miller.

"You get your points," Dale said. "I have to go."

Miller looked at the dead creature. Then at Dale. "Why?"

"Because the Authority just sent me a ticket. And if I don't clear it, my license gets revoked."

"Class IX?" Miller's face went white. "That's a suicide run.

You need a full squad.

Heavy artillery.

Magic suppression."

"I don't have time for a squad," Dale said. He got back in his truck.

"Wait!" Miller ran to the window. "You can't go alone. That's not just a monster.

That's a breach point. You'll die."

Dale rolled down the window. His face was impassive.

"I have a clipboard," Dale said. "And I have a solvent can. It's enough."

Miller wanted to argue. He wanted to shout. But he looked at the points counter in his own HUD, glowing green with the fresh gain. He looked at Dale, who was already starting the engine.

"Send me the report," Miller whispered.

"Automated system handles it," Dale said.

He drove away. The tires spun on the wet asphalt.

Dale didn't look back. He looked at the GPS. Two miles. Eight minutes.

He opened his Field Guide app on his tablet. He searched for Class IX threats.

*No data found.*

He frowned. He typed in *Rift Anchor*.

*No data found.*

He typed in *Dimensional Scar*.

*Warning: Entry Protected. Requires Certification Level VIII to view.*

Dale was Level V.

He didn't have the data. He didn't have the weakness. He had nothing but his clipboard and his anxiety.

He pulled over.

He took a deep breath. His hands were shaking. Just a little.

He opened a new page.

*Incident Report #116.* *Location: Elm Street, Dumpsite.* *Subject: Post-Incident Protocol Review.*

He wrote. Not about the monster. About the mistake.

*Error: Reliance on visual identification without pre-combat guide access.* *Correction: Request emergency guide access via priority override.*

He hit send.

The System responded instantly.

*Override Request Denied. Authority Protocol 42: Guides are earned, not granted.*

Dale stared at the screen.

Then another notification.

*Priority Infestation Warning: Class IX Entity is moving toward Elm Street.*

Dale looked at the GPS. The red dot wasn't two miles away anymore.

It was half a mile away.

And it was moving fast.

He looked at the truck bed. The solvent can was empty. The duct tape was used up. The clipboard was full.

He had nothing.

Except the knowledge that he had failed to document the first kill correctly. He had let Miller take the credit. He had let the system dilute his points.

He had gotten greedy for efficiency.

And now the bill was coming due.

Dale got out of the truck. He walked toward the empty dumpster.

He picked up the torn black bag.

He runned.

Not away. Toward the sound of the cracking sidewalk. Toward the ground that was beginning to boil.

Because if the Authority wouldn't give him the data, he would make them give it to him.

By bleeding it out of the monster.

Or by bleeding it out of himself.

The ground shook. A hand, huge and made of shadow and smoke, burst from the pavement ten feet away.

It reached for Dale.

Dale didn't scream.

He clicked his pen.

*Incident Report #117.* *Subject: Self-Contained Field Study.* *Status: Critical.*

He ran into the monster's shadow.
