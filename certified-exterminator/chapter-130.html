Chapter 130: Forged Level Ten

# Chapter 130

The ink was wet.

It glowed a sickly, electric blue, seeping into the fibers of the clipboard like a stain on a crime scene photo. Dale held his breath, watching the forged certification seal form itself.

It wasn't real.

It couldn't be. There was no Level 10 in the manual. There was no Level 10 in the system architecture.

But the Authority didn't read manuals.

It read inputs.

The void hovered, patient and terrible. The face of the Authority had not vanished; it merely waited. Its geometric eyes bored into Dale's retinas, searching for the lie.

Dale didn't blink.

He couldn't afford to. If he blinked, he might see the truth, and if he saw the truth, he would doubt the ink. Doubt was fatal.

In pest control, if you weren't sure the nest was dead, you didn't sign the release form. You treated it again.

And again.

Until the doubt was gone or the technician was dead.

"Submit," Dale whispered.

The light pulsed. A single, sharp throb that vibrated in his teeth.

Then the clipboard jerked. Not physically, but conceptually. The paper twisted, folding inward on itself. The forged seal flared white, burning away the blue ink, revealing the raw, unformatted data beneath.

**[ERROR: CERTIFICATION LEVEL INVALID.]** **[SOURCE: UNVERIFIED MANUFACTURER.]** **[PENALTY: IMMEDIATE TERMINATION OF CONTRACT.]**

Marcus made a sound. It was a high, thin noise, like a kettle screaming. "Dale, step back."

Dale didn't step back. He stepped forward.

The white void expanded. The streetlights along the avenue snapped off, one by one, plunging the block into absolute darkness.

The only light came from the erasure line, creeping closer. It wasn't erasing matter. It was erasing *context*.

The buildings didn't crumble. They simply stopped being buildings.

They became abstract shapes, then lines, then nothing.

"Invalid manufacturer," Dale muttered. He tapped the clipboard with his pen. "I am the manufacturer. I am the process."

He looked at the data stream scrolling in his peripheral vision. The Authority was scanning his Field Guide. It was looking for the entry for *Rattus profundus*, for the *Formica titanis*, for the *Hymenoptera abyssal*. It was checking for gaps.

Dale had gaps. Everyone did. But he had filled them with procedure.

He grabbed Marcus by the collar of his tactical vest. "Give me your rifle."

Marcus hesitated. "You're going to shoot it?"

"No. I'm going to file a report."

Dale snatched the rifle. It was heavy, cold, and useless. A tool for heroes.

Dale threw it on the ground.

He pulled a marker from his pocket. A thick, black permanent marker.

The kind used for labeling hazardous waste bags.

He uncapped it.

The void surged. The erasure line jumped ten feet forward. The sidewalk where Marcus had been standing vanished. Marcus scrambled back, his boots scraping against asphalt that felt increasingly like glass.

"Level 10 doesn't exist," Marcus shouted over the rising hum of the void. "You can't hack the Authority!"

"Everything is hackable if you know the code," Dale said. He wasn't looking at the void. He was looking at his Field Guide. Specifically, the entry for *Dimensional Vermin*.

The system rule was clear: *Identify, document, isolate, eliminate, verify.*

The Authority was asking for Level 10 Certification to verify the complaint. But the Authority also had a rule about *Cross-Contamination*. If you applied a protocol designed for one creature to another, you got a debuff. But if you applied a protocol designed for *nothing* to the Authority itself?

Dale uncapped the marker. He drew a line across the forged seal. Then he drew a second line, perpendicular. An 'X'.

He wasn't faking the level. He was invalidating the premise.

*Identification:* The entity was not a pest. It was a service provider. *Documentation:* The service provided was erasure. *Isolation:* The area was closed. *Elimination:* Not possible with current tools. *Verification:* Pending.

Dale wrote in the margin of his Field Guide. Not in the printed text, but in the white space, the part of the book that belonged to the technician, not the Authority.

**SUBJECT: ADMINISTRATIVE ERROR.** **PROTOCOL: WORK STOPPAGE ORDER.**

The pen moved. The marker moved.

The void stopped pulsing. The hum dropped an octave, becoming a low, grinding growl.

**[WORK STOPPAGE ORDER DETECTED.]** **[SOURCE: LOCAL LICENSE HOLDER.]** **[STATUS: PENDING REVIEW BY REGIONAL OVERSIGHT.]**

The erasure line halted. It hung there, a wall of white static inches from Marcus's nose.

Marcus stared at the wall of nothingness. "You... you stopped it?"

"I paused it," Dale said. His hand was shaking. He hid it by putting the marker back in his pocket.

"Stoppages require a supervisor. A regional oversight."

**[REGIONAL OVERSIGHT INCOMING.]** **[ETA: 00:00:05.]**

Five seconds.

The sky above the avenue tore open. Not a rift this time. A window. A massive, rectangular pane of glass slid aside, revealing a sky that was not gray, not blue, but the deep, sterile white of a hospital ceiling.

A hand reached through.

It was not a monster's claw.

It was a human hand, gloved in white latex.

It held a clipboard. A *proper* clipboard.

Heavy, black plastic, with a metal clip that glinted in the void-light.

A man stepped through.

He was tall, thin, and dressed in a suit that cost more than Dale's house. His tie was a perfect Windsor knot.

His hair was combed with geometric precision.

He did not look at Dale. He looked at the void. He looked at the erasure line.

He looked disgusted.

**[INSPECTION INITIATED.]**

The man raised his hand. The void receded. The buildings flickered back into existence, solidifying from abstract lines into brick and mortar. The streetlights buzzed back to life.

Marcus slumped against a lamppost, gasping for air. "Who is he?"

Dale didn't answer.

He was checking his clipboard. The ink was dry.

The forgery was gone.

But the Work Stoppage Order remained. A new entry had appeared in his Field Guide. Not a monster.

A job title.

**Regional Inspector Vane.** **Certification Level: IX.** **Specialty: Bureaucratic Compliance.**

Vane turned. He looked at Dale. His eyes were the color of old paper.

"Mr.

Kowalski," Vane said.

His voice was smooth, devoid of accent, devoid of warmth. "You have filed a stoppage order against a municipal cleanup crew.

Do you know the penalty for interfering with an Active Erasure Protocol?"

Dale straightened his back. He smoothed the front of his stained work shirt. "I know the penalty for unauthorized termination of a licensed professional. Which is what you just tried to do to me."

Vane tilted his head. "You have no certification for Level 10. You cannot claim authority over this sector."

"I don't need Level 10," Dale said. "I have a Level 2 license to perform inspections on *unlicensed* activity.

And that void?" He pointed at the receding white light.

"That was unlicensed. It didn't have a permit. I checked."

Vane's lips thinned. "It is a natural phenomenon. Phenomena do not require permits."

"Rats are natural phenomena," Dale said.

"But when they get inside your walls, they require a permit. Or they get exterminated.

This thing was inside my walls.

It was in my street. It erased my neighborhood. That makes it an infestation.

Infestations require permits."

Vane looked at Marcus. Then back at Dale. For the first time, he smiled. It was a small, cruel thing.

"You are clever, Kowalski. But cleverness is not thoroughness. You stopped the erasure. You did not stop the *breach*."

Vane reached into his suit jacket. He pulled out a small, black device. It looked like a TV remote. He pointed it at Dale.

**[PROTOCOL: ADMINISTRATIVE DEMOTION.]**

Dale felt a cold pull in his chest.

His vision blurred.

The Field Guide entries faded. The *Rattus profundus* entry vanished.

The *Formica titanis* entry vanished.

"No," Dale said.

He slammed his hand onto his clipboard. He didn't write. He *pressed*. He pressed the pen against the paper so hard the tip snapped, leaving a jagged hole in the page.

He had one trick left. One loophole the Authority hadn't thought to close because it assumed technicians were too obedient to exploit it.

*Cross-contamination penalty.*

If he deployed a protocol against a creature, and that creature was not the right species, he took a 40% debuff. But what if he deployed a protocol against the *System*?

Dale shouted the word. "ISOLATE!"

He wasn't shouting at the Inspector. He was shouting at the air between him and Vane. He was trying to trigger the isolation protocol he used for acid beetles. A chemical containment field.

It was the wrong tool. Vane was not an acid beetle.

The universe punished him.

A wave of nausea hit Dale. His vision grayed.

His knees buckled.

He tasted copper and rot. The debuff hit him like a physical blow, forcing him to one knee on the asphalt.

**[CROSS-CONTAMINATION DETECTED.]** **[PENALTY APPLIED: 40% DEBUFF TO ALL STATS.]** **[FIELD GUIDE CORRUPTED FOR 24 HOURS.]**

But it had worked.

The air between them shimmered. A faint, yellow haze appeared.

Not a wall, but a barrier.

A *misapplication* barrier.

Vane frowned. He looked at the haze. He looked at Dale, who was vomiting bile onto the pavement.

"You contaminated the field," Vane said. "Now you cannot use your protocols. You are useless."

"I'm not useless," Dale wheezed. He wiped his mouth with his sleeve. "I'm *under review*."

Vane paused. The system processed the statement.

**[STATUS: UNDER REVIEW.]** **[ACTION: HOLD FOR AUDIT.]**

The debuff didn't lift. Dale's strength was gone.

His reflexes were sluggish.

But the attack stopped.

The erasure didn't resume. Vane didn't kill him.

Because you can't audit a corpse.

Vane lowered his remote. "You have bought yourself twenty-four hours, Kowalski. During that time, your Field Guide is blank.

You cannot identify. You cannot document.

You are blind."

Dale looked up. His eyes were watery, his face pale. "I don't need to identify," he said. "I know what it looks like."

Vane stepped closer. He loomed over Dale. "The breach is widening, Dale.

The Rift is not just a hole.

It is a door. And something is coming through that door that has no classification.

It has no name in your guide.

It has no weakness in your guide."

Dale tried to stand. He failed. He stayed on his knees.

"What is it?" Dale asked.

Vane smiled again. "That is the point.

You have no data.

You have no points.

You have no protection."

He turned away. "I will return in twenty-four hours. If you have not resolved the breach, I will revoke your license.

Permanently. And then I will let the void finish its work."

Vane stepped back through the window in the sky. The glass slid shut. The white ceiling vanished, replaced by the cloudy Ohio sky.

The street was silent. The erasure line was gone. The buildings were intact.

Marcus ran to Dale. He grabbed Dale's arm to help him up. "You're shaking."

Dale looked at his clipboard.

The pages were blank. Not empty. *Blank*. The ink had retreated.

The entries were gone.

The Field Guide was corrupted.

For twenty-four hours, Dale Kowalski could not see monsters. He could not name them. He could not exploit their weaknesses. He was just a man in a dirty shirt, standing in a street that had almost ceased to exist.

"We have to move," Marcus said. "He's right. The breach is widening."

Dale looked at the blank page. Then he looked at the pen. The tip was broken. A splinter of black plastic stuck in the paper.

He pulled the splinter out.

"I don't need to see them," Dale said. "I can smell them."

He stood up. His legs felt like water. He leaned heavily on Marcus.

"Where do we go?" Marcus asked.

Dale looked down the avenue. In the distance, the air was rippling. Not with white light, but with a dark, oily shadow. It smelled of ozone and rotting meat.

"We go to the source," Dale said. "We file a noise complaint."

Marcus stared at him. "You're joking."

"I'm never joking about complaints," Dale said.

He walked forward. One step. Then another.

The shadow pulsed.

It grew larger. It took shape. It was not a rat.

It was not a beetle. It was a mass of limbs and eyes, shifting and flowing, a storm of biological impossibility.

Dale didn't have a Field Guide. He didn't have a protocol. He didn't have a certification.

He had a broken pen and a blank page.

He raised the clipboard.

"Subject unknown," he said aloud.

The shadow lunged.

Dale didn't dodge. He didn't fight. He wrote.

He wrote the first word of a new entry.

*Violation.*

The shadow hit him.
