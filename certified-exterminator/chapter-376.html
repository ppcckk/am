Chapter 376: Form Seven B

# Chapter 376

The phone rang.

It wasn't the Authority's sterile, chime-less notification. It was a physical, mechanical ring from a rotary dial attached to the dashboard of Dale's truck. The sound was analog, jagged, and impossible, cutting through the hum of the idling engine like a rusty saw blade.

Dale didn't answer. He kept his eyes on the rearview mirror. The grey drop was gone from the pavement. It had been absorbed by the tire.

*"Answer the phone, Dale,"* the voice said. It came from the speaker now, not the dial.

It was smooth.

Corporate. "We have an open ticket."

Dale gripped the steering wheel. His knuckles were white, stained with ichor that wasn't his own.

"Ticket number?" Dale asked. His voice was flat. Procedural.

*"Ticket 90-412. Unauthorized dimensional transit. Improper disposal of Class-IV biomass. Failure to file Form 7-B."*

"I didn't file it," Dale said. "I was working under a Level III emergency waiver."

*"Waivers don't cover the signature, Dale. You signed it. Or rather, you didn't."*

The radio static crackled. A new voice overlaid the first. Sharper. Younger.

"Unit 4-Alpha, this is Supervisor Halloway. You are blocking the main thoroughfare in District 9. Move the vehicle or we will deploy containment fields."

Dale checked his watch. 6:14 AM. The sun was fully up. The streets were empty, but not empty enough. He could see the faint, shimmering distortion of a barrier forming around his hood.

"Containment fields are Class-2 protocols," Dale said into the radio. "They interfere with my Field Guide calibration. I'm in the middle of a secondary sweep. If I move now, I lose the data integrity of the previous kill."

*"Data integrity is irrelevant to public safety,"* Halloway said. *"Move the truck."*

Dale looked at the dashboard. The rotary phone was still ringing. The Authority's voice was gone, replaced by the aggressive static of the municipal enforcement team.

He reached for the ignition.

The engine sputtered. It wouldn't turn over.

The grey fluid from the pavement was still inside the system, which was in the fuel line. It was in the battery.

"Unit 4-Alpha," a new voice cut in. Calm.

Familiar.

His old boss, Miller.

"Dale, you're making this difficult. We can fix the paperwork.

We can make the Thoroughness Rating disappear. Just come to the basement.

"

"I don't have a basement," Dale said. He opened the door.

The air outside smelled of ozone and wet dog. The barrier around the truck hummed, a low-frequency vibration that made his teeth ache.

Two figures stepped out from the shadows of the adjacent duplex. They wore tactical gear, but it wasn't military.

It was heavy, rubberized, and branded with the logo of the Licensing Authority's Enforcement Division.

Black visors. No insignia.

Halloway. And behind him, a woman in a grey suit holding a clipboard that seemed to bleed ink onto the pavement.

"Step away from the vehicle," Halloway said. He raised a baton. It wasn't steel. It was a containment rod, glowing with a faint amber light.

"I'm conducting a post-incident audit," Dale said.

He stepped off the running board. He didn't raise his hands.

He adjusted his collar.

"Protocol 4-14: If a technician encounters Enforcement before filing the final report, the technician retains custody of the evidence until the audit is complete."

Halloway laughed. It was a dry, humorless sound.

"Protocol 4-14 was repealed three days ago. You're operating on outdated regulations, Kowalski."

"Was it?" Dale asked. He pulled his clipboard from his back pocket.

It was battered, corners frayed, but the paper was crisp.

"I have a printed copy of the 2023 Municipal Code, Section 12, Paragraph C.

It hasn't been updated in the local district. The Authority's digital server is lagging.

"

The woman in the grey suit stopped writing. She looked up. Her eyes were pale, almost colorless.

"Where did you get that code?" she asked.

"I wrote it," Dale said. "Or, rather, I drafted the amendment that was never ratified. But until it's ratified, the old law stands. You can't arrest me for following the current law."

Halloway lowered the baton slightly.

"You're bluffing."

"Check your HUD," Dale said. "Look at your license validity status. You're operating with a Level II permit.

I have a Level III provisional. Under the hierarchy, you take orders from me in active zones unless you have a judicial warrant.

"

The woman tapped her tablet. She frowned.

"His provisional status is... active," she said. "But it's flagged. Red flag."

"Flags don't override hierarchy," Dale said. "They trigger reviews. They don't revoke authority."

Halloway stared at him. The amber glow of the rod dimmed.

"Fine," Halloway said.

"You want to play rules lawyer? Fine. But you're not going anywhere until this zone is cleared.

The Rift didn't just open.

It's leaking. That truck of yours is a vector.

"

"It's not a vector," Dale said.

"It's a containment unit. The residual agent from the broodmother was neutralized. The grey drop was a symptom of incomplete sterilization.

I'm handling it.

"

"You killed it," the woman said. "You didn't sterilize it. You documented the kill. That's not the same thing."

"It's the same thing if you verify the decay rate," Dale said.

He pointed to his Field Guide.

"Entry 4,001. I noted the fluid's viscosity.

I noted its movement. It wasn't alive. It was reactive.

It responded to the tire's heat signature. That's not a biological entity.

That's a magical residue.

A glitch in the localization field.

"

Halloway stepped forward.

"Prove it."

Dale looked at the truck. He looked at Halloway.

"I need a sample," Dale said.

"You're not touching the vehicle," Halloway said.

"I need a sample of the air around the tire," Dale corrected.

"To run a spectral analysis. If it's biological, it has a DNA signature.

If it's a glitch, it has a code signature.

If I can prove it's a glitch, I can patch it. If I don't, it spreads. And if it spreads, your enforcement team gets wiped out in forty minutes.

"

Halloway hesitated. He looked at the woman. She shrugged.

"Let him," she said. "If he's wrong, we detain him. If he's right, we have a new violation type. The Authority pays extra for new violation types."

Dale nodded. He reached into his tool belt. He pulled out a sterile glass vial and a pipette. He also pulled out a small, handheld device that looked like a Geiger counter but had a lens instead of a needle.

"Clear the perimeter," Dale said. "Ten feet."

Halloway signaled. The enforcement agents backed up. The barrier flickered and died.

Dale walked to the rear tire. The rubber was black, untouched. But the pavement beneath it was stained with a faint, oily sheen.

He uncapped the vial. He held it under the tire. He activated the lens device.

A soft blue light scanned the air.

"Reading... null," the device chirped.

Dale frowned. He moved the pipette closer to the pavement stain.

"Reading... null."

He looked at Halloway.

"You see this?"

"I see a stain," Halloway said.

"It's not a stain," Dale said. "It's a hole."

He pressed the pipette tip to the wet spot on the asphalt. Nothing came up. The liquid didn't stick to the glass. It vanished into the pipette, then the pipette emptied itself into thin air.

"That's impossible," the woman said. She stepped closer. "Matter conservation."

"It's not matter," Dale said. "It's data. The Authority's system is trying to delete the anomaly, but it's failing. The glitch is eating the file."

He turned to the woman.

"Your tablet. Give it to me."

"That's non-transferable property of the Authority," she said.

"It's a tool of the trade," Dale said. "In the field, tools are shared.

If you refuse, you're obstructing a certified technician.

That's a Class-1 violation.

I can write you up right now.

"

The woman looked at Halloway. Halloway waved his hand.

"Give it to him."

She handed over the tablet. It was heavy, warm. The screen displayed a live feed of Dale's Field Guide.

Entry 4,001 was blinking red.

*Unidentified Residual Agent. Status: Contaminated. Source: Unknown.*

"Watch the code," Dale said. He held the tablet so the camera faced the pavement. "If the code is stable, it's biological. If it's scrolling, it's systemic."

The code on the screen didn't scroll. It froze.

Then, it changed.

*Error. Input Invalid. Subject: Dale Kowalski.*

"Hey," Halloway said. "Why does it say my name?"

"It doesn't," Dale said. "It's correcting itself. The system recognizes the contamination. It's trying to isolate the source."

"What's the source?" the woman asked.

"The audit," Dale said. "The one you're here for. You're the source."

Halloway stiffened.

"What?"

"The notification," Dale said. He pointed to his own head. "Audit Failed.

Flagged for Deep Inspection. You're part of the inspection.

You're the variable that changed the outcome.

The system is confused because you're interfering with the natural decay process.

"

"That's insane," Halloway said. "We're enforcing the law."

"You're enforcing a law that doesn't exist yet," Dale said.

"You're using future protocols to punish past actions. That's a time-loop violation.

The system can't handle it.

That's why the fluid is glitching. It's trying to resolve the paradox.

"

The woman tapped the screen.

"He's right. The timestamp on his kill report is four minutes before the Rift closed. But the Rift didn't close until... now."

"Exactly," Dale said. "I killed the broodmother before the Rift closed.

But the system thinks I killed it after, and the math is wrong.

The fluid is the error message.

"

"So what do we do?" Halloway asked. His voice was quieter.

"We don't clean it," Dale said. "We let it finish."

"Finish what?"

"The calculation," Dale said. "The system is trying to reconcile the timeline. If we remove the fluid, we remove the data.

If we remove the data, the system crashes. And if the system crashes, every certified technician in the county loses their licenses.

"

Halloway looked at the tablet. The error message was pulsing.

*Processing... 12% complete.*

"We can't wait," Halloway said. "The backlog is already huge. If we wait for a system reboot, the infestations in District 4 will spiral."

"They'll spiral anyway," Dale said.

"But if we crash the server, they'll be unmanageable. I've been running the numbers in my head since the notification popped up. The Authority's processing power is tied to Thoroughness Ratings.

You're slowing down the verification of my kill.

That's why the glitch is here. You're the bottleneck.

"

Halloway stepped back.

"You're blaming us for the glitch?"

"I'm diagnosing the problem," Dale said. "It's your job to fix it. My job is to exterminate. But you can't exterminate a bug if you're standing on the nest."

"Get in the truck," Halloway said. "We're taking you to the basement."

"No," Dale said.

"I'm filing a complaint. Form 12-A. Obstruction of Justice.

Interference with Field Operations.

"

"We'll take the complaint," Halloway said. "After you surrender your credentials."

"If I surrender my credentials, the glitch expands," Dale said. "Look at the pavement."

The oily sheen was spreading. It was creeping toward the curb. Toward the grass. Toward the neighbors' houses.

"It's accelerating," the woman said. "The error is 40% complete."

Halloway looked at Dale.

"What do you want?"

"I want you to sign off on my report," Dale said.

"Right now.

Digitally. Accept the kill as valid.

Close the ticket.

"

"That's not how it works," Halloway said. "We have to verify the site first."

"The site is verified," Dale said. "The glitch is the verification.

Let it complete.

If you accept the kill, the system marks the timeline as 'resolved.' The glitch disappears. The Rift stays closed. Everyone lives.

"

"And if you're wrong?"

"Then I lose my license," Dale said. "But you lose your job. Because you'll be the one who caused the crash."

Halloway stared at him. The amber rod was still in his hand, but he wasn't raising it. He was thinking.

"Sign it," the woman said. "It's a risk, but the alternative is a blackout. We haven't had a blackout in ten years."

Halloway sighed. He pulled a stylus from his belt.

"If this fails, Kowalski, you're going to prison. Not just the basement. Prison."

"Understood," Dale said.

Halloway tapped the tablet screen. He signed the digital signature.

*Report Accepted. Thoroughness Rating: 9.8.*

The blue light on the pavement flared bright white.

Then, it vanished.

The oily sheen evaporated. The tire of Dale's truck was clean. The air smelled only of exhaust and morning dew.

The barrier around the truck dissolved completely.

Halloway looked at the tablet. The error message was gone. Entry 4,001 was green.

*Verified.*

"Good," Halloway said. He sounded tired. "Go. You're cleared."

Dale picked up his tablet. He checked the time. 6:22 AM.

"Thank you," Dale said. He didn't say it politely. He said it like he was closing a case file.

He got back in the truck. The engine started on the first turn.

Halloway watched him drive away. He didn't wave. He just stood there, holding the tablet like it was a live grenade.

Dale drove down the street. He didn't check the rearview mirror. He knew the glitch was gone. But he also knew something else.

He had accepted a 9.8 rating. That was higher than his previous best.

And on his Field Guide, a new notification pulsed in the corner of his vision.

**New Protocol Unlocked: Temporal Containment.**

**Requirement: Successfully resolve a timeline paradox without losing a single point of Thoroughness.**

**Reward: Ability to perceive 'glitches' in reality.**

Dale gripped the wheel tighter. He could feel it. A shimmer in the air above the road. A seam in the world.

It wasn't just a glitch, which was a door.

And it was opening.
