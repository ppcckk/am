Chapter 297: Finish the Inspection

# Chapter 297

The red pen snapped.

Not broke. Snapped.

The ink cartridge inside the barrel ruptured under the pressure of Dale's grip, spraying a fine mist of crimson across his knuckles.

He didn't flinch. He didn't wipe it off. He stared at the man who used to be Marcus Thorne and calculated the viscosity of the blood pooling around the creature's boots.

"Thorne," Dale said. His voice was flat. OSHA-compliant. "You are currently in violation of Code 44-B: Unauthorized Biological Modification within a Municipal Service Zone."

Thorne's head tilted at an angle that defied cervical anatomy. The black voids where his eyes had been pulsed with a rhythmic, wet throb. A sound leaked from his throat, not a word, but a frequency that made Dale's molars ache.

"Threat assessment incomplete," the System whispered. It wasn't a voice in Dale's head anymore. It was a notification in his peripheral vision, sharp and red.

*TARGET: THORNE, MARCUS.* *STATUS: CORRUPTED.* *PROTOCOL: INTERNAL AUDIT.* *WARNING: DIRECT ELIMINATION WITHOUT DOCUMENTATION RESULTS IN ZERO POINTS AND CERTIFICATION REVOCATION.*

The squad stood frozen. The woman, the one who had signed the waiver, had her rifle raised but her finger hovering over the trigger. She looked at Dale, then at Thorne, then back at Dale. Her breathing was shallow.

"Do it," she said. Her voice cracked. "Just shoot him."

"No," Dale said.

He stepped closer. One step. Two.

The water in the sewer tunnel was ankle-deep and rising. It smelled of sulfur and rotting meat, a cocktail Dale had seen in enough dead pets to recognize the signs of advanced decomposition. Thorne was sinking. The black tendrils erupting from his suit were anchoring him to the concrete walls, pulling him down like a anchor.

Dale pulled a fresh clipboard from his vest. He ignored the blood on his hands. He uncapped a spare ballpoint pen.

"Identification," Dale muttered. He wrote the name. *Thorne, Marcus.* He wrote the date. *Today.* He wrote the location. *Sector 4, Sub-Level B.*

Thorne lunged.

It wasn't a human movement, which was a spasm. The legs kicked out, snapping at the air, and the torso twisted with a cracking sound that echoed off the tunnel walls.

A tendril whipped out, thick as a python, and smashed into the woman's rifle.

The metal bent like tinfoil. She fell backward, scrambling through the sludge.

"Back up!" Dale shouted. "Establish a perimeter! Do not engage!"

The two squad members on the flanks hesitated. One raised his sidearm.

"Don't fire!" Dale ordered. "Cross-contamination risk! You're using kinetic force against a biological infestation. You'll spread the spores!"

The soldier lowered his gun, confused.

"He's trying to eat us, Dale!"

"He's trying to reproduce," Dale corrected. He looked at his Field Guide. The entry for Thorne was shimmering, the text unstable. *Weakness: Unknown.*

He needed a weakness. He needed data.

Dale moved. He didn't run.

He walked with the purposeful stride of a technician approaching a difficult junction box.

He ducked under a swinging tendril that missed his shoulder by inches.

The air displaced by the strike felt cold, and he reached Thorne's feet.

The man's hazmat suit was no longer a suit. It was a chrysalis. The fabric had fused with the black biomass, turning into a rough, chitinous shell.

But at the ankles, the transformation had failed. The rubber seals had broken.

Exposed skin glistened with pale, translucent fluid.

Dale leaned down. He didn't look at the face. He looked at the ankles.

"Friction," Dale said.

He jammed the tip of his ballpoint pen into the exposed flesh of Thorne's left ankle.

It wasn't a weapon. It was a probe.

Thorne shrieked. The sound was digital, distorted, like a corrupted audio file playing at three times speed. The black tendrils recoiled, thrashing wildly. One smashed into the wall, spraying debris and sludge onto the squad.

Dale didn't pull the pen out. He twisted it.

*SYSTEM ALERT: MINOR TRAUMA INFlicted.* *DATA ACQUIRED: TISSUE REGENERATION RATE: HIGH.* *VULNERABILITY: NEURAL CONDUCTIVITY DISRUPTED BY FOREIGN OBJECT.*

Dale pulled the pen out. A dark, viscous goo oozed from the wound. It sizzled where it touched the concrete.

"Neural conductivity," Dale muttered. He scribbled it onto his clipboard. *Weakness identified: Disruption of signal transmission.*

The Field Guide entry solidified. The shimmering text turned to crisp, black font.

*WEAKNESS: TARGET RELIES ON EXTERNAL SIGNAL FOR MOTOR CONTROL. DISRUPTING NERVE PATHWAYS CAUSES SYSTEMIC FAILURE.*

Now he had the data. Now he had the protocol.

But he didn't have a weapon that could exploit it. His spray can was empty. His flashlight was too blunt. He needed something sharp, something that could pierce the neural clusters without triggering the full defensive reflex that would crush the squad.

The woman was on her knees, coughing. She looked up at Dale, her eyes wide with terror.

"What do we do?"

Dale looked at the bent rifle. Then he looked at the soldier with the sidearm.

"Give me your gun," Dale said.

The soldier frowned.

"My sidearm? It's nine millimeter. That won't pierce that armor."

"I don't need to pierce it," Dale said. "I need to conduct."

He grabbed the gun from the soldier's holster. The man resisted for a second, then let go, terrified of Dale's calmness.

Dale turned back to Thorne. The creature was pulling itself out of the water, the chitinous shell hardening around its torso. It raised a clawed hand, ready to swipe the squad aside.

Dale raised the pistol. He didn't aim for the head. He aimed for the exposed ankle where he had made the incision.

*PROTOCOL: INTERNAL AUDIT.* *EXECUTION: TARGETED ELIMINATION.*

He fired.

The bullet struck the wound. It didn't exit. The kinetic energy transferred into the black biomass.

The fluid inside Thorne's leg turned to steam.

A high-pitched whine erupted from the creature's throat.

Thorne convulsed. The tendrils went limp.

One shot wasn't enough. Dale fired again. And again.

Three rounds. Three strikes to the nerve cluster.

The System flashed.

*ELIMINATION IN PROGRESS.*

Thorne's body went rigid.

The black voids in his eyes expanded, consuming the rest of his face. The chitinous shell cracked. Not from the bullets, but from within.

The biomass lost cohesion. It slumped into the water, dissolving into a pool of black sludge that hissed and bubbled.

Silence returned to the tunnel.

Dale lowered the gun. He checked the magazine. Empty.

He pulled out his clipboard.

"Subject neutralized," Dale said. He wrote. *Time of elimination: 14:32. Method: Ballistic disruption of neural pathways. Documentation: Complete.*

He signed the bottom of the page.

The System chimed.

*JOB COMPLETE.* *THOROUGHNESS RATING: 9.8.* *TREATMENT POINTS AWARDED: 150.* *FIELD GUIDE UPDATED: MARCUS THORNE (DECEASED).*

The woman stood up. She wiped sludge from her face. She looked at the dissolving pile of black goo, then at Dale.

"You killed him," she said. It wasn't an accusation. It was shock.

"I processed him," Dale corrected. He capped his pen. He looked at the remaining squad members. They were staring at him with a mixture of awe and horror.

Dale didn't wait for their approval, and he walked over to the spot where Thorne had stood.

He pulled a small sample vial from his belt. He dipped it into the black sludge.

He corked it.

He needed to verify the species. Was this a new strain of Vermin?

Or something else?

The Authority demanded verification. You couldn't just kill it and walk away. You had to know what you'd killed.

He uncorked the vial. He held it up to the dim light of the emergency exit sign.

The black sludge wasn't settling. It was swirling.

Inside the glass, tiny, microscopic shapes were forming. Not bacteria. Not viruses.

Shapes.

Dale squinted. He pulled a magnifying loupe from his pocket. He held it over the vial.

The shapes were letters.

Tiny, jagged runes, moving in a pattern.

Dale felt a cold sweat prickle the back of his neck.

He hadn't seen this before. Not in the rats.

Not in the beetles.

Not even in the Leviathan, which was a message.

He looked up at the tunnel ahead. The darkness seemed deeper now. Thicker.

"Who was he talking to?" the woman asked.

Dale didn't answer. He shook the vial. The runes rearranged themselves.

*ERROR.* *REBOOT.* *ADMIN.*

Dale dropped the vial. It shattered on the concrete.

The black sludge splashed outward, but before it hit the floor, it evaporated.

Gone.

As if it had never existed.

The System flashed again. This time, it wasn't green. It was purple.

*ANOMALY DETECTED.* *CLASSIFICATION: UNKNOWN.* *WARNING: EXTERNAL INFLUENCE DETECTED IN TREATMENT SITE.*

Dale stared at the text. *External influence?*

He looked at Thorne's remains. The sludge was gone. But on the wall, where Thorne had been anchored, there was a mark.

A symbol burned into the concrete. It looked like a barcode, but the lines were organic, twisting like vines.

Dale approached it. He ran a gloved finger over the burn.

It was warm.

"This isn't an infestation," Dale whispered.

The woman stepped closer.

"What do you mean?"

Dale turned to her. His face was pale. His hands, finally free of the clipboard, were trembling again.

"Infestations are natural disasters.

They happen. They spread. They die.

" Dale pointed to the barcode.

"This is infrastructure.

This is a network. And we just disconnected a node.

"

A low rumble shook the tunnel. Dust fell from the ceiling.

The soldier with the sidearm checked his watch.

"Dale, we need to move. The rift energy is spiking. If another Leviathan comes through—"

"It won't," Dale said.

"How do you know?"

"Because I'm not the target anymore."

Dale pulled out his Field Guide. He flipped to the entry for Marcus Thorne. It was greyed out. But below it, a new entry was forming.

*NEW ENTRY: THE ADMINISTRATOR.* *STATUS: OBSERVING.* *LOCATION: LOCAL.*

Dale looked at the squad. He looked at the dark tunnel. He looked at the purple warning in his peripheral vision.

He made a decision. He tore the page with the Thorne report out of his clipboard. He folded it into his pocket.

"We're not leaving," Dale said.

The woman blinked.

"What?"

"The Authority isn't just watching us," Dale said. "It's auditing us. And we just failed a compliance check."

He pointed to the barcode on the wall.

"That's a service tag. Someone installed it. Someone is maintaining this infestation."

The rumble grew louder. The water in the tunnel receded, sucking downward into a drain that shouldn't have been there.

A voice echoed from the depths. Not Thorne's glitchy scream. A smooth, synthetic voice.

*"Violation logged. Technician Kowalski. Your license is under review."*

Dale felt the air in his lungs tighten. He looked at his Certification Level. *Level VI.*

Then he looked at the notification.

*CERTIFICATION SUSPENDED PENDING INVESTIGATION.*

He couldn't use his protocols. He couldn't use his Field Guide. He was just a man with an empty clipboard and a broken pen.

The woman grabbed his arm.

"Dale, we have to go!"

Dale shook her off. He looked at the drain.

"If I leave, I lose my license," Dale said. "If I stay, I lose my life."

"Then what do we do?"

Dale picked up the shattered remains of the sample vial. He picked up the bent rifle. He looked at the squad.

"We finish the inspection," Dale said. "And we find the source."

He stepped toward the drain. The darkness looked back.
