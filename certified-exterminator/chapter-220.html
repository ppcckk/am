Chapter 220: Auditor Protocol Breach

# Chapter 220

The fingers dug into the concrete.

They were long, white, and jointed like a spider's legs, but the skin had the texture of cured parchment. The hand hauled a torso upward. The creature wore a charcoal gray business suit, tailored to fit a body that lacked a proper skeletal structure. The fabric stretched and tore at the seams, revealing too many ribs and joints bending in directions human anatomy did not permit.

Dale didn't flinch. He pulled his pen from his pocket.

"Vane," Dale said, his voice flat. "Hold this."

He thrust the clipboard at the younger technician. Vane's hands shook, but he caught it. The paper was already covered in Dale's earlier notes, scribbled hastily before the system alarm triggered.

"Is this a joke?" Vane whispered. "Look at it. It's wearing a tie."

"It's wearing a violation," Dale corrected. "Look at the entry point.

That's a Class 7 dimensional breach.

Unauthorized excavation in a residential zone. No permit filed with the Authority. No environmental impact assessment.

And now we have an unlicensed contractor attempting to occupy the premises."

The creature's head fully emerged. It had no eyes, no nose, no mouth.

Just a smooth, oval surface of white chitin where a face should be.

It turned toward them. The movement was jerky, accompanied by the sound of wet cartilage popping.

**SYSTEM NOTICE: TARGET IDENTIFIED. SPECIES: BUREAUCRATIC PARASITE (HOMO LEGALIS). CLASS 7.** **RECOMMENDED PROTOCOL: TERMINATION VIA LEGAL PRECEDENT.**

Dale stared at the notification. A new mechanic.

Legal precedent.

He had never seen that before.

The standard protocol for humanoid-shaped entities usually involved blunt force trauma or chemical suppression. This one suggested something else.

"Vane, write down the time," Dale ordered. "14:02. Incident Type: Zoning Violation. Suspect: Unlicensed Dimensional Contractor."

Vane stared at him. "Dale, it's coming for us."

"It's coming for the permit." Dale took a step forward. He kept his distance, staying outside the creature's immediate reach but within the range of his Field Guide's targeting reticle. "If we don't document the violation first, the Authority treats this as a combat event.

Combat events have a high risk of collateral damage penalties. Zoning violations?

Those are administrative.

They're cheap to fix."

The creature tilted its head. A low, vibrating hum emanated from its chest cavity. The air smelled of ozone and old paper.

Dale pulled out his spray can.

Not the heavy-duty acid mix he used for Class 5 acid beetles. He used the standard solvent for organic residue.

It was weak.

It was cheap. And it was the only thing he had that wouldn't cross-contaminate his Field Guide if he used it on the wrong target.

He sprayed a short, precise burst at the creature's left shoe.

**SYSTEM: CROSS-CONTAMINATION WARNING. PROTOCOL MISMATCH. DEBUFF: 40% EFFECTIVENESS. FIELD GUIDE ENTRY 'HOMO LEGALIS' CONTAMINATED FOR 24 HOURS.**

A red error box flashed in Dale's peripheral vision. His hands clenched at his sides.

He had rushed the identification.

He had assumed the suit meant it was a mimic, a Class 4 shapeshifter.

But the suit was part of the anatomy. The creature *was* the suit.

"Damn it," Dale muttered.

The creature lunged. It moved with terrifying speed, its spindly arms sweeping forward. Dale sidestepped, dropping his center of gravity.

His shoulder hit the side of a parked sedan. Metal groaned.

Vane screamed, backing away toward the garage. "Dale! Run!"

"Can't run," Dale said, checking his phone.

The demotion timer ticked down. *09:45*. "If I run, I abandon the job site. That's an automatic zero on Thoroughness.

My average drops below the threshold.

I lose my Class II status."

He looked at the creature.

It was regenerating. The solvent had done nothing. The white mask-face turned toward him again.

It raised a hand, pointing a single, elongated finger at Dale's chest.

A beam of light shot out. It wasn't laser heat. It was a visual projection. Text appeared in the air between them.

*NOTICE OF EVICTION. FAILURE TO COMPLY WILL RESULT IN SUMMARY DEMOLITION.*

Dale blinked. He read the text. He read it again.

"Vane," Dale said, not moving. "Does it have a mouth?"

"I don't know! Look at the eyes!"

"It doesn't have eyes," Dale said.

"It has a projection unit.

It's communicating. That means it has a cognitive function.

It follows rules."

He tapped his temple, accessing his Field Guide. The entry for *Homo Legalis* was grayed out, contaminated by the earlier error. He couldn't see the weaknesses. He had to guess.

"Vane, do you have a stamp?"

Vane froze. He patted his pockets.

His face went white.

"My stamp... I left it in the truck."

"Go get it."

"It'll kill you!"

"It won't kill me if I file a grievance," Dale said.

He pulled out his own stamp. It was a small, rubber ink pad, stamped with the number 224-B.

His official technician seal.

He held it up.

The creature paused. The projection changed.

*GRIEVANCE NOT RECOGNIZED. SUBMIT FORM 10-C.*

"Form 10-C," Dale said aloud. "Where is Form 10-C?"

The creature tilted its head. It seemed confused. Its arms lowered slightly.

**SYSTEM HINT: THE TARGET IS BOUND BY ADMINISTRATIVE LOGIC. PROVIDE THE REQUIRED DOCUMENTATION TO HALT AGGRESSION.**

Dale's mind raced. He didn't have Form 10-C.

He didn't even know what it was.

But he knew how bureaucracies worked.

You didn't need the form. You needed to prove you were filling out a form.

He pulled out his clipboard. He took a pen. He wrote.

*DATE: 10/14/2024.* *SUBJECT: APPLICATION FOR CLASS 7 DIMENSIONAL ENTRY.* *APPLICANT: DALE KOWALSKI, LIC. #224-B.*

He wrote quickly, his handwriting cramped and sharp. The creature watched him. The hum in its chest grew louder, agitated.

It took a step forward. The concrete cracked under its weight.

"Stop," Dale said. He didn't look up. "I am in the middle of filing."

The creature froze.

*PROCESSING...*

The text hovered in the air.

Dale kept writing. He wrote nonsense. He copied a standard residential inspection checklist. *Check for moisture.

Check for termite damage.

Check for code compliance.*

"Vane," Dale said, not stopping.

"Get in the truck. Get the stamp. Get the blue folder.

The one with the void permits."

"It's a trap!" Vane yelled. "It's luring us!"

"It's waiting," Dale said. "Administrators wait. They like to see paperwork."

The creature's arms twitched. The light beam dimmed.

*WAIT TIME: 00:45.*

A countdown appeared in the air.

Dale's pen scratched against the paper.

He needed to fill four pages.

He had forty-five seconds. He had to be thorough.

Sloppy work would trigger a rejection.

**SYSTEM: THOROUGHNESS RATING IN PROGRESS. CURRENT ESTIMATE: 8.8. TARGET: 9.0.**

Dale's jaw tightened. He wrote faster.

His hand cramped.

He added a diagram of the patio crack, labeling it *Unauthorized Structural Modification*. He added a footnote about the lack of safety railings. He added a complaint about the noise level, citing local ordinance 44.2.

The creature's head snapped toward him. The white mask seemed to sharpen, becoming more angular. It was angry. The logic loop was straining.

"Vane!" Dale shouted. "Now!"

Vane burst from the garage, sliding across the wet grass. He slammed into the side of the pickup truck, yanking open the door. He grabbed a blue plastic folder and a heavy rubber stamp.

The creature lunged. It was faster now, its patience evaporating. Its arm swept forward, claws raking the air inches from Dale's face.

Dale didn't dodge. He held his ground. He slammed the pen down on the paper.

"It's filed," Dale said.

He looked at the countdown. *00:03*.

Vane ran toward them, the stamp raised high. The creature swatted at Vane, knocking him backward into the mud. The stamp flew from his hand, spinning through the air.

Dale caught it.

He didn't have time to think. He slammed the stamp onto the final page of his makeshift form. The red ink bloomed on the paper.

**SYSTEM: DOCUMENT SUBMITTED.** **REVIEW STATUS: PENDING.** **ACTION: SUSPEND HOSTILITIES.**

The creature stopped. Its claws hovered inches from Dale's nose. The hum ceased. The silence was deafening.

For a second, nothing happened.

Then, the creature's body dissolved. It wasn't dying.

It was receding.

The suit unspooled, the white chitin turning into smoke that drifted back down the crack in the patio. The hand retracted, sinking into the earth with a wet sucking sound.

The air cleared.

Dale slumped against the sedan. His heart hammered against his ribs, but his hands were steady. He picked up the clipboard. The paper was ruined, smeared with mud and red ink, but the stamp was visible.

**SYSTEM NOTICE: INCIDENT RESOLVED VIA ADMINISTRATIVE HALT.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 9.1.** **TREATMENT POINTS: 150.** **STATUS: CLASS II CERTIFICATION SECURED.**

Dale let out a breath he didn't realize he had been holding.

Vane crawled out of the mud, spitting dirt. "You... you stamped a form. You saved us by stamping a form."

"It wasn't a form," Dale said, wiping his hands on his pants again. "It was a request for review.

It's different.

Forms are for people.

Requests are for systems."

He looked at the patio. The crack was still there. The hand was gone, but the breach remained. The ground trembled slightly.

**SYSTEM: NEW INFESTATION DETECTED.** **SEVERITY: CLASS 8.** **STATUS: ESCALATING.**

Dale's blood went cold. A Class 8. That was higher than anything he had ever faced. That was the level where the Authority stopped sending technicians and started sending armies.

"Vane," Dale said, his voice low. "Look at the stamp."

Vane looked at the red ink on the clipboard. He frowned. "It's a Class II stamp. Why?"

"Because I used it," Dale said. "The system accepted it. That means it worked. But look closer."

Vane squinted. The red ink was moving. It wasn't drying.

It was crawling. The ink formed tiny, black letters on the paper.

*REJECTION. INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE. APPEAL DENIED. ESCALATION IMMINENT.*

"It rejected the appeal," Dale said. "It thinks I'm lying."

"Can you fix it?"

"No," Dale said.

He looked at the ground. The tremors were getting stronger. The grass was buckling.

"The Authority doesn't just reject appeals.

They audit you."

A shadow fell over them. Not from the sun. From the house.

The front door of the duplex creaked open.

A man walked out.

He was tall, thin, and wore a suit identical to the one the creature had worn. But his face was normal. Human.

He had a briefcase in one hand and a clipboard in the other.

He looked at Dale. He smiled. It was a polite, professional smile.

"Mr. Kowalski," the man said. "Your documentation has been reviewed. It is inadequate."

Dale stepped back. He raised his spray can.

"Who are you?"

"I am the Auditor," the man said. "And you have a significant amount of paperwork to do before we proceed."

**SYSTEM NOTICE: AUDIT INITIATED.** **WARNING: NON-COMBATANT STATUS.** **PENALTY FOR RESISTANCE: REVOCATION OF ALL LICENSES.**

Dale lowered the spray can. He looked at Vane. Vane looked at Dale.

If Dale fought, he lost his license. If Dale didn't fight, he was at the mercy of the thing in the suit.

"Vane," Dale said, his voice barely a whisper. "Get the camera."

"What camera?"

"The one that records everything. The one we use for the final report."

"Dale, he's going to kill us."

"He's going to audit us," Dale corrected. "And I don't think I can beat him with a spray can."

The Auditor stepped onto the grass. The ground didn't tremble under his weight. He moved with a grace that was unnatural. He held out a hand.

"Hand over the clipboard," the Auditor said. "And please, do not run. Running is considered an admission of guilt."

Dale looked at the clipboard. He looked at the Auditor. He looked at the open crack in the patio, where something darker and larger was beginning to rise, waiting for the order to be given.

Dale made a choice. He didn't hand over the clipboard. He ripped the top page off.

"Vane," Dale said. "Start the camera."

The Auditor's smile vanished.

**SYSTEM: VIOLATION DETECTED. PROTOCOL: CONTAINMENT.**

The Auditor's eyes turned black.

Dale dropped the page and ran.
