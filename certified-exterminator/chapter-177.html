Chapter 177: Auditor Class IX

# Chapter 177

The shadows hit the truck bed first.

They didn't slam into the metal. They flowed over it like oil.

The suspension groaned under a weight that shouldn't have existed.

Dale watched the side mirror. The reflection showed nothing but a swirling gray static where the driver's side window used to be.

"Miller," Dale said. His voice didn't rise. It stayed flat, procedural. "Check the rear axle seal."

"I'm checking the *demons*, Dale!" Miller shouted.

He jerked the steering wheel. The tires screamed against the gravel, then caught.

The truck fishtailed.

One of the shadow-forms snapped like a whip, leaving a frost line across the door panel.

Dale didn't look at the frost. He looked at his clipboard.

The pen moved. *Contamination event: Unsanctioned shadow entities. Source: Proximity to Landlord's insolvency. Status: Active.*

The briefcase on the passenger seat vibrated. A low hum.

The Field Guide inside was reacting to the density of the anomaly.

Dale opened the latch.

The book slid out, its pages flipping rapidly until they settled on *Class IV: Dimensional Parasites*. Underneath the entry, a new sub-category had appeared: *Spectral Debtors*.

"Sarah needs to know we're not alone," Dale said.

Miller fumbled for the radio. His hands shook. "I can't get a signal. The interference is—"

"Stop looking at the window," Dale ordered.

Miller froze.

"Look at the mirror. Check the axle seal. If the grease is leaking, we lose traction.

If we lose traction, the shadows catch up. Do you understand?"

Miller swallowed hard. He looked in the mirror. "The seal is intact. But the glass is cracking."

"Good. Drive."

Dale pulled a canister from his belt.

Not spray. Bleach. Concentrated industrial grade, stolen from the janitorial closet at the Water Treatment Plant two chapters ago.

He uncapped it.

The fumes hit him. Sharp, chemical, comforting.

It smelled like order.

The truck burst onto the access road leading to the plant. The industrial district loomed ahead, a jagged silhouette of pipes and smokestacks against the bleeding sky. Red lights pulsed from the main filtration tower. The red dot on Dale's map synced with the tower's beacon.

The shadows were gaining.

A dark tendril punched through the rear window. Shards of safety glass sprayed into the cab. The temperature dropped ten degrees in a second. Dale's breath plumed in the air.

"Target acquired," Dale muttered.

He didn't aim. He didn't hesitate. He swung the bleach canister. The liquid splashed across the back seat, coating the seatbelts and the upholstery.

The System interface flickered in his peripheral vision.

**[ACTION: APPLY CHEMICAL AGENT]** **[TARGET: SPECTRAL DEBTOR]** **[PROTOCOL MISMATCH: LIQUID VS. ETHEREAL]** **[PENALTY: -40% EFFICIENCY]**

The bleach hissed. Not physically. Spiritually.

The sound was a wet tear in the fabric of reality. The shadow tendril recoiled, dissolving into gray mist.

It didn't die.

It retreated.

"It works," Miller said, eyes wide. "It actually works."

"It's a temporary mitigation," Dale corrected. "Not a kill. The Authority doesn't count chemical dispersal as extermination for ethereal classes. It counts as *containment*."

**[TREATMENT POINTS: +2]** **[THOROUGHNESS RATING: 8.1 (DECLINING)]**

Two points. For nearly dying. Dale made a note in the margin. *Update Field Guide: Bleach effective for repulsion, ineffective for elimination. Requires solid-state anchor.*

The truck slammed into a pothole. The frame rattled. Miller slammed on the brakes.

"Water Treatment," Miller gasped. "We're here."

The gates were locked. A chain-link fence wrapped around the perimeter, topped with razor wire.

But the main gate was open. Swung wide. As if inviting them in.

"No," Dale said.

"What?"

"The gate is open. That's not an invitation.

That's a trap.

The Landlord doesn't have keys. He has leverage."

Miller looked at the open gate, then at Dale. "So we turn around?"

"We breach the perimeter wall. Section 4, East side. The fence is rusted. I inspected it last month."

Dale unbuckled.

He grabbed the briefcase. "Miller, stay in the cab.

Keep the engine running.

If I don't come out in ten minutes, drive to the extraction point. Do not wait for me."

"Dale—"

"Protocol, Miller. Don't contaminate the workspace."

Dale opened the door. The air outside was thick, humid.

The smell of chlorine and rot hung heavy.

He stepped down.

The mud sucked at his boots.

The shadows didn't follow him out. They hovered at the truck's edge, churning. Waiting.

Dale walked toward the fence. He scanned the rusted links. His eyes tracked the pattern of decay.

Weak spot. Three feet down.

Corrosion failure.

He reached into his belt and pulled out a pair of bolt cutters. Heavy. Industrial.

He didn't cut the fence. He cut the *lock*.

The padlock snapped. The chain fell away.

Dale stepped through.

The interior of the treatment plant was a cathedral of pipes. Massive concrete tanks stretched into the darkness. Water pumped from one to another with a rhythmic thrum. *Thump-thump. Thump-thump.* Like a heartbeat.

Dale moved through the maze. His flashlight beam cut through the steam rising from the aeration basins.

He wasn't looking for the Landlord. He was looking for the flow rate.

The map on his clipboard showed the red dot moving deeper into the facility. Toward the main reservoir.

*Why there?*

Because the Landlord was insolvent. Because he needed capital. Because water was liquid assets.

Dale stopped at a junction. A pipe burst overhead. Black sludge sprayed down. It hit the concrete floor with a splat.

Dale froze.

The sludge wasn't water. It was ink. Thick, viscous, black ink.

And it was moving.

It flowed against gravity. Up the pipe. Into the air.

Coalescing into shapes.

Hands. Faces.

Screaming mouths without sound.

**[NEW THREAT DETECTED: INK INFESTATION]** **[CLASS: CLASS III - CORRUPTED DOCUMENTARY]** **[WEAKNESS: UNKNOWN]**

Dale stared at the ink. He had seen this before. Not in the field. In the Authority's archives. *Bureaucratic Entities.* Monsters born from forgotten files, lost records, and denied claims.

The Landlord wasn't a beast. He was a filing error.

The ink shapes lunged.

Dale dodged. The mass of black liquid swept past his head, leaving a trail of cold. He rolled behind a pump station.

His heart hammered against his ribs. Adrenaline.

Good.

He pulled out his Field Guide. *Corrupted Documentary.* Entry populated.

*Weakness: Truth.*

That wasn't a weapon. That was a concept.

The ink poured around the pump station.

It formed a tall, slender figure. No face.

Just a smooth, black void where features should be. It held a staff made of twisted rebar.

**[COMBAT INITIATED]**

The figure raised the staff. A spike of solidified ink formed at the tip. It aimed at Dale's chest.

Dale didn't have a weapon. He had a clipboard. He had a pen. He had the truth.

"You are a violation of Code 7-B," Dale said. His voice echoed in the vast chamber. "Unlicensed retention of sensitive data. Unauthorized accumulation of debt."

The ink figure paused. The spike lowered slightly.

"Your existence is non-compliant," Dale continued. He stepped out from behind the pump. "I am initiating a forensic audit."

The figure shrieked. A sound like tearing paper. It lunged.

Dale sidestepped. The ink spike shattered against the concrete wall. Shards of black glass sprayed across the floor.

He ran. Not away. Toward the main reservoir.

The ink entity followed. Faster now. Angry.

Dale reached the edge of the reservoir. The water was churning. The red dot on his map was right below the surface.

He looked down.

In the swirling water, he saw it. The Landlord.

It wasn't a man. It was a pile of papers.

Thousands of them.

Shredded. Burned. Wet.

Fused together into a humanoid shape by the muck.

A golem of bureaucracy.

It looked up. Its eyes were two red stamps. *REJECTED.*

Dale gripped the briefcase. He opened it.

Inside wasn't a gun. It wasn't a bomb.

It was the original inspection report from the duplex. The one he'd written when the first rat came through the wall. The one that started everything.

He pulled the paper out.

The ink entity stopped. It hovered at the edge of the reservoir. It trembled.

"What is that?" Miller's voice crackled over the radio. "Dale? What are you doing?"

"Submitting evidence," Dale said.

He held the paper up.

The Authority System flared.

**[ACTION: SUBMIT LEGAL DOCUMENTATION]** **[TARGET: CORRUPTED DOCUMENTARY]** **[EFFECT: PARALYSIS]**

The ink golem froze. The water stopped churning. The red stamps on its face dimmed.

Dale didn't attack. He *read*.

"Section 4, Paragraph 2," Dale read aloud. "The tenant failed to report structural entry points within twenty-four hours of discovery. The landlord failed to repair said entry points within fourteen days. The infestation was therefore deemed *negligent* rather than *unavoidable*."

The golem's form unravelled. The papers fluttered.

"You cannot claim the debt," Dale said.

"The liability is shared. The tenant failed. But you failed first.

And you failed *repeatedly*."

He tore the paper in half.

The golem screamed. A sound of shattering glass.

It collapsed. The papers fell into the water. The black ink dissolved, washing away into the filtration system.

Dale stood there, holding the torn halves of the report.

Breathing hard.

The silence was absolute.

Then, a voice.

Not from the radio. From the shadows in the corner of the room.

"Interesting."

Dale spun around.

A woman stepped out of the darkness.

She wore a suit.

Perfectly tailored. No mud.

No blood. She held a tablet that glowed with a soft blue light.

She looked at the dissolving golem. She looked at Dale.

"You broke the fourth wall of the narrative, Mr. Kowalski," she said.

Dale frowned. "I followed protocol."

"You used a legal loophole to exploit a metaphysical weakness. That is not protocol. That is lawyering."

She walked closer. Her heels clicked on the wet concrete.

"I am from the Appeals Board," she said. "And you have just filed a motion that affects nine million pending cases in this sector."

Dale tightened his grip on the briefcase. "I didn't file anything. I destroyed a threat."

"You *changed the rules*," the woman said. She tapped her tablet.

A holographic projection appeared between them.

A scale. One side held a gavel. The other side held Dale's clipboard.

The gavel was winning.

"The Authority rewards thoroughness, Dale. But it rewards *precedent* more. By proving that documentation can harm a Class III entity, you have invalidated the current combat doctrine for all Class II and III pests."

Dale felt a cold sweat prickle his neck. "What does that mean?"

"It means your Thoroughness Rating isn't just a score anymore," the woman said. "It's a liability."

She pointed to the hologram.

**[STATUS CHANGE: WANTED]** **[REASON: UNAUTHORIZED SYSTEM EXPLOIT]** **[NEXT STEP: INTERNAL AUDIT]**

"An audit?" Dale asked.

"A purge," she corrected. "The Authority doesn't like surprises. And it doesn't like freelancers who rewrite the code."

Behind her, the shadows in the room gatherred.

Not the mindless debtors from before. These were different.

They wore suits.

They held clipboards. They had Dale's face.

Dozens of Dales.

Miller's radio crackled. "Dale! The shadows are changing! They're... they're wearing ties!"

Dale looked at the woman. "You're a trap."

"I'm a notification," she said. "Run. Or submit to compliance."

Dale looked at the briefcase. Then at the reservoir. The water was still churning. The red dot was gone.

But the map updated.

A new location appeared.

*The Licensing Authority Headquarters. Location: Unknown. Time to Arrival: 00:00.*

The woman raised her hand. The Dales with ties stepped forward.

Dale grabbed the briefcase. He didn't run for the exit. He ran for the drain.

He jumped.

The water swallowed him.

As he sank, the last thing he saw was the woman smiling. And the sound of a stamp hitting paper.

*REJECTED.*

He surfaced in the pipe network, coughing up black water. He was alone.

Miller was gone.

The truck was gone.

The only thing left was the briefcase. And the pen.

And the fact that he was now being hunted by the system that gave him power.

Dale wiped the sludge from his eyes. He opened the Field Guide.

A new entry had appeared.

*Entry 177: The Auditor.* *Class: IX.* *Weakness: Unknown.* *Note: Do not engage. Do not document. Survive.*

Dale closed the book.

He stood up in the pipe.

The water was rising.

He runned.
