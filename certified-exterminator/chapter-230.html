Chapter 230: Maintenance Required

# Chapter 230

The boot hit the pavement.

It was a heavy, thudding sound, the kind a steel-toed work boot makes when it lands on concrete, not flesh. The creature wearing the boots stood six feet tall, but its limbs were too long, jointed in places Dale's skeleton had never allowed. Its face was a smooth, pale oval, like raw dough stretched over bone, and where the nose should have been, two vertical slits twitched.

Dale's hand shook. The pen slipped from his numb fingers and clattered against the asphalt.

*You're already the mother, Dale.*

The thought echoed in his skull, wet and heavy. Inside his abdomen, the chitinous thing shifted.

It curled tighter, pressing against his intestines with a pressure that felt less like pain and more like a distinct, foreign weight.

Four percent gestation. That wasn't enough time to run. It wasn't enough time to hide.

The doppelganger smiled. The skin around its eyes didn't wrinkle, but the slits narrowed, mimicking a squint.

"Inspection protocol is mandatory for all licensed operators," the thing said. Its voice was Dale's, but stripped of the gravel, the exhaustion, the fourteen years of smoking cheap cigars. It was a clean, synthetic recording of Dale's voice. "Failure to address personal contamination results in immediate license suspension."

Dale swallowed.

His throat felt like it was lined with sandpaper. He looked at the bone clipboard lying in the dust.

The checkmark still glowed, a bright, accusatory green.

Then he looked at the notification hovering in his peripheral vision.

**PREGNANCY STATUS: 4% GESTATION.** **ESTIMATED BIRTH: 3 DAYS.** **RECOMMENDED ACTION: UTERINE EXCISION OR TOTAL TERMINATION.**

Total termination meant death. Uterine excision meant he had to cut himself open, or find someone else to do it, before the creature reached stage two.

Stage two, according to the Field Guide entry that had popped up automatically, *Parasitic Mirror-kin*, involved the host's nervous system.

The creature didn't just grow inside you; it rewired you.

It used your memories as fuel. It used your skills.

The doppelganger took a step forward. The worn-out work boots—*his* boots, the ones with the cracked sole and the yellow laces—crunched on the debris.

"Step away from the nest," Dale said. His voice was steady. OSHA compliant.

He reached for his belt, where his utility knife was sheathed. It was a simple folding blade, meant for scraping grout or cutting through drywall, not for surgery.

The doppelganger didn't move.

It tilted its head. "You are non-compliant. Your workspace is contaminated.

You are the workspace."

Dale's fingers fumbled with the belt buckle. The numbness in his hands was spreading, creeping up his wrists.

The creature inside him was leaching something into his bloodstream.

A sedative? A toxin? It didn't matter.

What mattered was the distance between them. Ten feet.

Maybe twelve.

"Identify the threat," Dale muttered to himself.

He forced himself to look at the creature's legs. They were thin, almost insectoid, ending in flat, broad feet that looked like human hands pressed flat against the ground.

But the boots... the boots were normal. They were the anchor. The only part of this thing that made sense.

*Weakness.*

Dale's Field Guide updated in real-time.

**SPECIES:** *Homo simulacrum* (Mirror-kin Variant). **CLASS:** Class-IV Parasite. **IDENTIFIED WEAKNESS:** The host's personal artifacts serve as an anchor point for the manifestation. Destroy the anchor, destabilize the construct.

The boots.

The doppelganger stepped closer. "I am you, Dale.

The better you.

The you who doesn't hesitate. The you who doesn't care about the paperwork."

"That's not an extermination protocol," Dale said. He pulled the knife. It opened with a snap. "That's just vandalism."

He didn't throw the knife. He threw his clipboard.

The bone clipboard spun through the air, a dark arc against the grey sky.

It wasn't a weapon. It was a distraction.

The doppelganger's head snapped to follow the object, its face-plate twitching as it tracked the motion.

In that split second of diverted attention, Dale lunged.

Not at the creature. At its feet.

Dale slid on his knees, the concrete scraping the skin off his shins. He grabbed the left boot.

The leather was stiff, caked with mud.

He twisted his wrist, applying leverage to the ankle joint of the creature.

The Mirror-kin shrieked, a high-pitched, digital feedback sound that made Dale's teeth ache. It wasn't pain for the creature.

It was loss of coherence.

The boot came loose.

Dale ripped it off. The foot beneath was not a foot. It was a root-like cluster of pale, fibrous tendrils, pulsing with a rhythmic, sickly light. The boot was the cap that held them together.

The creature staggered. Its right leg buckled. The faceplate flickered, the slits widening in confusion.

"Protocol violation," the creature gurgled. "Structure compromised."

Dale scrambled back, gasping for air. The pain in his gut spiked, a sharp, twisting agony that dropped him to one knee.

He could feel the thing inside him kicking.

Not a kick. A contraction.

**GESTATION: 4.2%.**

The notification flashed red.

He had thirty seconds before the creature recovered.

Maybe less. He looked at the boot in his hand.

It was heavy.

Useless as a weapon, but valuable as evidence. More importantly, it was the anchor.

"System," Dale said aloud. "Log evidence."

**EVIDENCE LOGGED: ANCHOR ARTIFACT (LEFT BOOT). STATUS: SEVERED.** **FIELD GUIDE UPDATE: WEAKNESS CONFIRMED. TARGETED DAMAGE BONUS APPLIED TO NEXT INTERACTION WITH CLASS-IV PARASITE.**

He didn't have a weapon that could exploit the bonus. He had a utility knife and a severed boot.

The doppelganger regained its footing. The right boot remained, planted firmly.

The creature leaned forward, its long arms sweeping out.

"You damaged the anchor.

This is non-standard. This is... messy."

"Contamination," Dale wheezed. He stood up, using his knees as levers. He kept his distance.

"You're not a monster. You're a code violation."

"I am the consequence," the creature said. It raised its right hand. Fingers elongated, sharpening into bone spurs. "And I am here to correct you."

Dale looked at the Rift.

The wall of eyes was still there, watching. The leg that had climbed out was gone, retracted into the darkness, but the presence remained. He needed a containment protocol.

He needed to isolate the threat.

He looked around. Debris.

Broken drywall.

A pile of shattered glass from the duplex window. And the trash cans.

Dale grabbed the nearest trash can. It was a plastic municipal bin, blue, with a lid.

He kicked it over, sending garbage spilling onto the street.

Rotting food.

Paper. Plastic wrappers.

"Isolation zone," he muttered.

The doppelganger paused. It tilted its head. "What are you doing?"

"Containment," Dale said. He grabbed a handful of the wet, rotting paper from the trash. It was soggy, heavy. He smeared it across the asphalt, creating a slick patch directly in front of the creature.

Then he took his utility knife and slashed the air, drawing a line in the spilled sludge.

"System," he said. "Initiate Barrier Protocol: Class-1 Vermin Exclusion."

**ERROR. PROTOCOL NOT AVAILABLE FOR CLASS-IV PARASITE.** **CROSS-CONTAMINATION WARNING.**

Dale gritted his teeth. He didn't care about the debuff. He needed to slow it down.

He threw the trash can lid at the creature. It bounced off the creature's chest, clanging loudly.

The creature swatted the lid away. It lunged.

Dale jumped back, his bare feet slipping on the wet sludge. He fell backward, hitting the ground hard. The wind was knocked out of him. The creature loomed over him, one foot raised to stomp.

Dale looked up. He saw the boot. The only one left.

He didn't try to dodge. He thrust the utility knife into the ground, right next to the creature's planted foot, and twisted.

The blade caught the sole of the right boot. It wasn't a clean cut. It was a rip. The leather tore, and the fibrous roots beneath were exposed to the air.

The creature screamed. It tried to pull its foot back, but the blade held. It was anchored.

Dale scrambled to his feet, ignoring the burning in his lungs.

He pulled the knife free, leaving the boot half-destroyed. The creature stumbled, its weight shifting, its structure destabilizing further. The faceplate cracked.

A fissure ran down the center of its forehead.

"Non-compliant," the creature hissed. "This is... improper."

Dale didn't listen. He walked forward.

Step by step.

He picked up the first boot, the one he had severed. He held it like a club.

"Get back," he said.

The creature retreated. It was confused.

The anchor was gone, or at least, broken.

The connection to the Rift was fraying.

It flickered, its form becoming translucent for a second, revealing the black void behind it.

Dale swung the boot.

It connected with the creature's knee. There was no thud. There was a sound like wet canvas tearing.

The leg buckled. The creature collapsed, its form unraveling.

The faceplate shattered, spraying shards of pale skin into the air.

It didn't die. It dissolved. The body melted into a pool of black ink and chitin, sucking itself back toward the Rift.

Dale stood over the puddle. He was breathing hard. His hands were shaking so badly he couldn't hold the boot. He dropped it.

**THREAT NEUTRALIZED.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 6.5.** **POINTS AWARDED: 150.** **DEBUFF APPLIED: CROSS-CONTAMINATION (40% EFFECTIVENESS REDUCTION FOR 24 HOURS).**

150 points. A sloppy job. He had used the wrong protocol, destroyed the evidence, and nearly died. But he was alive.

Dale looked down at his stomach. The shirt was still tight. The notification was still there.

**GESTATION: 5.1%.**

He had won the fight. He had lost the war.

A shadow fell over him. Not from the creature. From above.

Dale looked up.

Hovering ten feet in the air, defying gravity, was a drone. It was sleek, black, and silent. It bore the insignia of the Licensing Authority. A stylized eye inside a triangle.

A speaker on the drone crackled.

"Technician Kowalski," the voice said. It was a woman's voice. Cold.

Bureaucratic.

"You have violated Section 4, Paragraph B of the Field Operations Manual. Unauthorized use of personal artifacts as containment tools.

Destruction of potential bio-evidence.

And failure to document the termination process within the required timeframe."

Dale wiped blood from his lip. "I saved my license."

"You compromised it," the voice replied. "And you failed to address the secondary infestation."

Dale frowned. "Secondary?"

"Look at your left hand, Technician."

Dale looked.

His left hand was covered in black ink. The puddle of dissolved creature had splashed him.

But the ink wasn't drying.

It was sinking into his skin.

Veins of black spiderwebbed out from his fingertips, traveling up his wrist, toward his elbow.

**CONTAMINATION DETECTED: LINGUISTIC PARASITE (SUBTYPE: MEMETIC).** **INFECTION RATE: 1% PER MINUTE.** **SYMPTOM: VOICE ALTERATION.**

Dale tried to speak. "I need..."

His voice came out wrong. It was deeper. Distorted. It sounded like the doppelganger's voice.

"I need..." he tried again. The words came out in a whisper that wasn't his.

The drone descended. A hatch opened on its underside. A net, woven from silver wire, dropped from the drone.

**ACTION: CONTAINMENT NET DEPLOYMENT.** **REASON: HIGH-RISK CONTAMINATION. SUBJECT IS NO LONGER CLEAR.**

Dale stepped back. He looked at the net. He looked at his arm. The black veins were pulsing, moving in sync with the heartbeat of the thing inside his stomach.

If they caught him, they would quarantine him. They would take him in. And they would cut him open, or they would let him turn.

If he ran, he would spread the memetic virus. He would infect others.

He looked at the Rift. The darkness was still churning. The wall of eyes watched.

And from the darkness, a second pair of boots emerged.

Then a third.

Then a dozen.

All wearing Dale's boots. All wearing Dale's face.

The first one spoke, its voice overlapping with the memetic infection in Dale's throat.

"Hello, Dale," the dozen voices said in unison. "You've been neglecting your personal maintenance."

Dale raised his hand. The black veins glowed. He didn't know if he was raising his hand to fight, or to surrender, or to join them.

The silver net hit him.
