Chapter 285: Clean Lie Dirty Truth

# Chapter 285

The doppelganger's clipboard was clean.

Dale's was stained with mud, blood, and the residue of three different Class-IV infestations.

The difference was a lie.

Dale's knuckles whitened around his pen. The Level V override had expired, leaving his muscles locked in a tremor that he couldn't control. The tower behind the figure hummed, a low-frequency vibration that rattled his teeth. Screaming faces pressed against the black glass, mouths open in silent pleas for extermination.

"Welcome," the figure said again. The voice was Dale's, but stripped of the gravel. "We've been waiting for your report."

Dale didn't answer. He looked at the ground. The asphalt was still cracking, but the cracks weren't spreading. They were freezing in place, suspended by the Authority's administrative hold.

The figure took a step forward. The suit was tailored to perfection.

No stains.

No fraying. A certification so high it didn't need to touch the dirt.

"You didn't file the incident report for the Rattus profundus broodmother," the figure continued, its tone conversational, like a supervisor reviewing a quarterly budget.

"You identified the nest. You isolated the entry point.

You eliminated the threat.

But you never submitted Form 12-B: Post-Extermination Sanitation Verification.

"

Dale's eyes narrowed. The memory hit him harder than the physical exhaustion.

He had wanted to go home, and he had wanted to eat a sandwich.

He had skipped the final wipe-down to catch the last bus. A violation of protocol.

A minor one.

"Minor infraction," Dale said. His voice sounded foreign.

Dry.

"Penalty is two Treatment Points. Not a tower."

The figure smiled, which was a terrifying expression, devoid of humor.

"You misunderstand the jurisdiction, Dale.

This isn't about the rats.

This is about the Pattern.

"

"Pattern?"

The figure tapped its clipboard. A holographic projection sprang from the page, hovering between them.

It showed a web of red lines connecting every city in Ohio.

Every Rift.

Every monster killed. Every report filed.

"Your Thoroughness Rating is 9.2," the figure said. "Exceptional. But your Compliance Score is failing."

Dale blinked. He hadn't seen a Compliance Score before. The System tracked Treatment Points, Field Guide entries, and Certification Levels.

It did not track morality. It tracked procedure.

"Compliance requires adherence to the Hierarchy of Necessity," the figure explained. "When an exterminator identifies a potential threat, they must prioritize the Authority's interests over local stability. By hiding the full extent of the Rift under your duplex, you violated Subsection 4, Paragraph C: Concealment of Strategic Assets."

Dale's stomach dropped. He had covered the hole. He had salted the earth. He had lied to the other survivors about what was down there because he knew the Authority would send something worse than rats.

"I protected the civilians," Dale said.

"You protected the infestation's secrecy," the figure corrected.

"And now, the Authority requires a correction. A full audit. Not of your paperwork.

Of your actions.

"

The figure gestured to the tower. The black glass rippled.

"Enter," the figure commanded. "The Examination Center has opened. Question 1 is answered. Question 2 awaits."

Dale looked at the entrance. The doors were wide open. Inside, the lobby was vast, filled with rows of empty desks. The air smelled of ozone and old paper.

He didn't move.

Contamination anxiety flared. The lobby was unsterilized.

The carpet was gray, and the lighting was harsh. But worse, he saw other figures standing in the distance. Other

"Dales." Or perhaps other exterminators who had passed this test.

They stood rigid, holding their clipboards, waiting for assignment.

If he walked in, he became property.

If he stayed, the tower would collapse on him.

Dale looked at his own clipboard. The note **DO NOT TRUST THE AUTHORITY** was still written in shaky ink. He needed a loophole. A way to challenge the jurisdiction without entering the building.

He looked at the doppelganger. It was standing on a patch of cracked asphalt. Its shoes were polished.

Dale looked at his own boots. They were caked in mud from the crawlspace.

"Mud," Dale muttered.

"What?" the figure asked.

Dale stepped back. His foot scraped the ground. A clump of wet earth flew up, landing on the polished toe of the doppelganger's shoe.

The figure froze. Its smile vanished.

"Contamination," Dale said.

The air around the figure shimmered. A red warning light pulsed above its head.

**VIOLATION: CROSS-CONTAMINATION DETECTED.** **PROTOCOL BREACH: UNLICENSED PATHOGEN ON EXAMINER.**

Dale's heart hammered against his ribs. He hadn't thought it through. He had just reacted to the instinct to keep his workspace clean. But the System had heard him.

The doppelganger looked down at its shoe. It lifted its foot, disgusted.

"This is not part of the simulation," the figure said, its voice losing its smooth veneer. "This is a real-time interaction. You are contaminating a Level IX Examiner."

"I am a Level II Technician," Dale said. "My workspace was established at the point of contact. Your footprint violated my zone of professional integrity."

It was a technicality. A stretch. But the Authority's logic was rigid. It rewarded specificity.

The red light intensified. The ground beneath the doppelganger's feet sank. The black glass of the tower groaned.

**CROSS-CONTAMINATION PENALTY: 40% DEBUFF APPLIED TO EXAMINER.** **FIELD GUIDE ENTRY: [Level IX EXAMINER] CONTAMINATED FOR 24 HOURS.** **ACTION: ISOLATE SOURCE.**

The doppelganger staggered.

It tried to step away, but its movements were sluggish, weighed down by an invisible force.

The 40% debuff wasn't physical. It was administrative.

The Examiner's authority was being stripped because it had been

"touched" by unverified biological material.

Dale saw his opening, and he didn't attack. He didn't run.

He pulled a small bottle from his belt.

The last of his industrial-strength disinfectant. High concentration. Alcohol-based.

Designed for fungal spores and bloodborne pathogens, and he uncapped it.

"Cleaning protocol initiated," Dale said.

He sprayed.

The mist hit the doppelganger's face.

The figure screamed. Not in pain, but in error. The digital avatar flickered, pixels scattering like dust. The suit dissolved into static.

**WARNING: DESTRUCTIVE CLEANING AGENT APPLIED TO AUTHORITY ASSET.** **PENALTY: 500 TREATMENT POINTS DEDUCTED.** **STATUS: SUSPENDED PENDING REVIEW.**

Dale's Treatment Points bar, which had been hovering at a comfortable 14,000, plummeted. It dropped to 13,500. Then 13,000. The red numbers scrolled down the side of his vision.

But the doppelganger was gone.

In its place was a hole. Not a rift. A doorway.

The tower remained, but the entrance was now unstable. The black glass was dissolving, revealing the swirling vortex of the Rift behind it.

Dale stood there, breathing hard. His hands were steady now. The shaking had stopped, replaced by the cold clarity of a technician who had just made a mistake and was fixing it.

He had lost 500 points. A massive setback. It would take him three months of routine work to earn that back. But he had stalled the Examination.

He looked at the hole in the reality. The vortex inside was calm.

No monsters.

No screaming faces. Just a quiet, swirling gray.

A new notification popped up. Small. White.

**NOTICE: ADMINISTRATIVE ERROR DETECTED.** **CORRECTION PROTOCOL: RECALIBRATION REQUIRED.** **LOCATION: BASEMENT LEVEL 3.** **TIME LIMIT: 10 MINUTES.**

The Authority was trying to fix the glitch he had created. They were sending a repair crew.

Dale looked at his watch. Ten minutes.

He could run. He could take the bus. He could go home and forget this ever happened.

But he looked at the swirling gray. He knew what that color meant. It was the color of a nest that had been disturbed but not destroyed. It was the color of a problem that would come back in six weeks.

If he walked away, the Authority would fix the glitch. They would patch the hole.

They would bury the evidence of his rebellion.

And the next time they came for him, they wouldn't send a doppelganger.

They would send a containment squad.

Dale tightened his grip on the spray bottle. The plastic was warm from his hand.

He looked at the entrance to the basement. A narrow stairwell had formed in the dissolving glass, leading down into the dark.

He checked his Field Guide. His entry for the Level IX Examiner was still contaminated, grayed out. He couldn't identify its weaknesses for twenty-four hours. He was blind.

But he had his pen. He had his clipboard. And he had a violation to document.

Dale stepped forward.

The first step onto the stairs felt heavy. The air was colder here, smelling of ozone and rot.

He descended.

At the bottom, the basement was not a dungeon. It was a server room.

Rows of black monoliths hummed, connected by cables that pulsed with light. In the center of the room stood a console. On the console was a single, blinking red button.

And on the floor, tied up with silver cables, was a man, and the man was wearing a suit. It was torn. The face was bruised, one eye swollen shut.

It was not Dale.

It was a man Dale had never seen before. A stranger. But the System notification above the stranger's head read:

**SUBJECT: PREVIOUS EXAMINER.** **STATUS: INCOMPLETE.** **CRIME: TREASON.**

The stranger looked up. His remaining eye widened.

"You," the stranger croaked. "You're the variable."

Dale didn't speak. He looked at the blinking red button.

**EMERGENCY PURGE INITIATED.** **WARNING: TOTAL DATA LOSS.**

If he pressed it, the Authority would delete this server. The Examination would be erased. The Evidence of his rebellion would vanish. He would be free.

But he would also lose the data. The data on the Rifts. The data on the other survivors. The data on why the Authority was here.

And he would be killing the witness.

The stranger struggled against the cables.

"Don't," he whispered. "It's a trap.

The Authority wants you to destroy the evidence. Then they can rewrite the history.

You'll be the only one who remembers.

And they'll make you the scapegoat.

"

Dale looked at the button. He looked at the stranger.

He pulled out his clipboard, and he wrote:

**NOTE: WITNESS RETENTION REQUIRED FOR AUDIT.** **ACTION: NEUTRALIZE SECURITY, RETAIN SUBJECT.**

He looked at the cables holding the stranger. They were made of a metallic fiber. Conductive.

Dale uncapped his spray bottle again. He aimed it at the cables.

Not the stranger. The cables.

He sprayed the alcohol onto the metal. It dripped, ineffective, but enough to create a conductive bridge if he touched it.

He took a piece of wire from his belt. A grounding wire. Standard issue.

He wrapped the wire around the cable binding the stranger's wrists.

Then he touched the wire to the blinking red button.

Not to press it. To short it.

The console sparked. The hum changed pitch. The lights flickered.

**ERROR: POWER FLUCTUATION.** **SYSTEM LOCKDOWN ENGAGED.**

The cables holding the stranger went slack.

The stranger fell forward, gasping.

Dale grabbed the stranger's arm, hauling him up.

"Can you walk?" Dale asked.

The stranger looked at him, confused.

"Why?"

"Because you're part of the report," Dale said.

The doors to the server room burst open.

Three figures entered. They were not doppelgangers. They were clad in heavy, black armor. Their faces were hidden behind visors of smooth, reflective glass.

They held rifles that looked like modified spray wands.

**UNLICENSED INTERFERENCE DETECTED.** **TARGET: DALE KOWALSKI.** **THREAT LEVEL: EXISTENTIAL.**

Dale looked at the armored figures. He looked at the stranger, who was leaning on him, trembling.

He looked at the short-circuited console, and he had started a war with the bureaucracy. And he had just acquired a partner.

"Run," Dale said to the stranger. "I'll document the entry points."

He raised his clipboard, shielding his face, and kicked a chair at the lead armored figure.

The chair bounced off the armor harmlessly.

But the figure flinched.

Dale smiled, which was a tiny, grim smile.

They were afraid of the mess.
