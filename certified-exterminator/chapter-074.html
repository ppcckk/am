Chapter 74: Documented by the Target

# Chapter 74

The extraction window was closing. Dale could see it in the way Linda's fingers were curling inward, the way the subcutaneous lines were migrating toward her throat like a school of fish turning in unison. The node was consolidating. Whatever she'd been thirty seconds ago, she was becoming less of it by the second.

The adjudicator stood at the edge of the room, holding a device that looked like a caulking gun crossed with a defibrillator. Its tip was already charged. Blue-white. The kind of energy that didn't ask permission before entering a structure.

"Technician Kowalski. Begin protocol or step aside."

Dale looked at his Field Guide. Three hundred and forty-seven entries.

All reclassified.

All fragments. All still technically alive somewhere in the network. He'd killed them all.

He'd written the reports.

He'd collected the points. And none of it had mattered.

He looked at Linda, and she wasn't moving anymore.

The curling had stopped. Her eyes were open and fixed on a point somewhere behind Dale's left shoulder. Not at him.

Through him.

"Begin protocol," the adjudicator repeated.

Dale put his broken pencil in his shirt pocket, and he pulled out a new one from his truck.

A mechanical pencil, 0.5mm, the kind he kept in the glove compartment for inspection reports. He clicked it twice.

The sound was small in the room.

"Negative," he said.

The adjudicator's face didn't change. Adjudicators didn't have faces that changed. They had faces that were set. "Explain."

"Protocol for extraction assumes the subject is a discrete organism with definable boundaries." Dale's pencil moved across his Field Guide. Not writing.

Tracing.

Following the reclassification markers with the tip of the pencil, like he was reading a contour map. "This subject has no discrete boundaries. She's a node.

A junction point.

You extract her, you sever the junction."

"And the fragments?"

"Reconstitute. All three hundred and forty-seven of them. Simultaneously."

The adjudicator was quiet for three seconds. Dale counted them. Three seconds was a long time for an adjudicator. Their default response time was under one second.

"Then what do you propose?"

Dale flipped to the back of his Field Guide.

The pages he'd filled over three months. Each entry had a location, a time stamp, a behavioral profile, and a weakness notation. He'd documented every single one.

That was the job. That was always the job.

But now, looking at them in sequence, he saw something he hadn't seen before.

The locations weren't random, and they formed a pattern. A branching pattern.

Like a root system. Or a vascular network. Or the layout of a municipal water treatment plant, which was the closest thing he had to a professional reference.

He traced it with his pencil. The broodmother in the crawlspace was a primary node.

The beetles in the water treatment plant were secondary.

The storm drain thing was tertiary. The school basement was a capillary. And Linda was the heart.

Every fragment he'd killed was still connected. The network was intact. He'd just been removing leaves from a tree and calling it deforestation.

"I need to treat the network," Dale said. "Not the node."

The adjudicator's expression shifted. Not confusion. More like the look a supervisor gets when a Level II technician suggests something that isn't in the manual. Which was, Dale reflected, exactly what he was.

"There is no network treatment protocol."

"No," Dale agreed. "There isn't."

He opened to a blank page. Started writing.

SUBJECT: Marchetti, L. CLASSIFICATION: NODE (PRIMARY) NETWORK TOPOLOGY: DOCUMENTED VIA FIELD GUIDE ENTRIES 1-347 RECOMMENDED TREATMENT: COORDINATION DISRUPTION AT JUNCTION POINTS METHOD: TARGETED SEVERANCE OF INTER-FRAGMENT SIGNALING PATHS

He wrote fast.

His handwriting got worse when he was working, which was ironic because it was the one thing he'd never been able to improve despite fourteen years of practice. The Authority didn't care about legibility.

It cared about content.

It always had.

The Field Guide pulsed.

NEW PROTOCOL RECOGNIZED: NETWORK DISRUPTION (UNOFFICIAL). AUTHORITY REVIEW: PENDING.

THOROUGHNESS RATING: PROVISIONAL.

NOTE: THIS PROTOCOL HAS NO PRECEDENT.

AUTHORITY LIABILITY DISCLAIMER APPLIES.

"Provisional," the adjudicator said. "That means if you're wrong, the liability falls on you personally."

"I know."

"You will be reclassified as an unlicensed operator."

"I know."

"You will forfeit all Treatment Points accrued under this protocol."

"I know."

The adjudicator lowered the caulking gun device. "You have ninety seconds before I execute extraction regardless of your position."

Dale didn't answer. He was already moving.

He pulled out his spray can. Not the one from his truck. The one the Authority had issued him at Level III.

A targeted dispersal agent, calibrated for Rattus profundus. Useless against Linda.

Useless against any of the fragments, probably, since they were all just pieces of something else.

The can was nearly full. He'd been saving it.

But he wasn't spraying Linda, and he was spraying the floor.

The concrete was covered in the subcutaneous migration lines.

The organism's surface network. Dale could see them now that he knew what he was looking for.

They pulsed with a rhythm that matched his own heartbeat, which was either a coincidence or a bad sign.

He chose not to think about which, and he sprayed along the lines. Not to kill.

To document.

The dispersal agent left a residue that the Field Guide could read.

He was painting the network in real time. Mapping it from the inside.

Entry 1: Junction point, southeast quadrant. Signal path severed. Entry 2: Junction point, north corridor.

Signal path severed. Entry 3: Bifurcation node, near the baseboard.

Signal path severed.

Each spray hit a junction. Each junction pulsed brighter, then dimmed. The signal path was disrupted.

Not destroyed.

Disrupted. Like cutting a phone line without cutting the wire.

The organism could still feel the network.

It just couldn't use it.

The adjudicator watched. Didn't speak. His device stayed low.

Dale moved faster. His knees ached.

His lower back protested in the specific way it had protested since the school basement job, a sharp lateral pull on the left side that meant he needed to stop working for six weeks and hadn't.

He was thirty-eight and had been on his feet for six hours.

The spray can was getting light. He could feel the weight drop with each press.

Twenty-two junction points. He'd identified twenty-two. He needed to hit all of them before the agent ran out.

Twenty. Twenty-one.

The can sputtered, and he had one left. The primary junction. The one directly beneath Linda's chest.

The one where all the signal paths converged, and the one he could see glowing through the concrete like a vein under skin.

He looked at Linda.

Her eyes were still open. Still fixed behind his shoulder. But her mouth was moving.

Not "run" this time.

Something else. A word he couldn't hear over the hum of the adjudicator's device.

Dale sprayed, and the can was empty. He dropped it. The sound it made on the concrete was final.

The Field Guide pulsed.

NETWORK DISRUPTION: 21/22 JUNCTIONS SEVERED.

SIGNAL INTEGRITY: 4.7%. FRAGMENT COORDINATION: FAILING. NODE STATUS: STABLE.

THOROUGHNESS RATING: 6.1.

Six point one.

Not great.

He'd missed a junction. One out of twenty-two.

That was a 95% completion rate, which in any other context would be excellent, but here it meant the organism still had a path to coordinate. A thread. Thin as a hair.

Running from the missed junction to Linda's sternum.

The adjudicator raised the caulking gun again. "One junction remains. I'm executing."

"No." Dale stepped between the adjudicator and Linda.

His body did this before his mind made the decision, which was unusual. Dale didn't step between things. Dale documented things from a safe distance.

"One junction means one path.

One path means one signal. If you destroy the node, the signal reroutes through the surviving fragment network.

You'll get reconstitution through the back channel.

All of them. Simultaneously."

The adjudicator paused. His device hummed. "Then treat the last junction."

"I don't have agent."

"Then improvise."

Dale looked at the thread of signal. It was on the floor.

Concrete, and he could see it now, faintly luminescent, like a crack in glass that caught light from somewhere below.

It was running from a fissure in the floor toward Linda's chest.

He got on his knees. The concrete was cold. His knees went numb within seconds. He put his hand on the crack.

The signal was warm. It pulsed against his palm like a pulse point. Like checking someone's wrist for a heartbeat.

He pressed down.

The Field Guide flashed.

CONTACT PROTOCOL: UNLICENSED. PENALTY: 40% CROSS-CONTAMINATION DEBUFF. DURATION: 24 HOURS.

He felt it hit. The debuff came in like a wave of nausea, starting behind his eyes and spreading outward. His vision blurred at the edges.

His hands went cold. The 40% debuff meant everything he did for the next twenty-four hours would be degraded.

Every spray, every identification, every protocol.

Half-assed. Contaminated. His Field Guide entries would be unreliable.

His Thoroughness Rating would be capped, and he would be working with one hand tied behind his back for a full day.

He didn't care.

He pressed harder.

The signal line thinned. Thinned. It was like trying to pinch shut a garden hose.

The pressure built against his palm. His hand went white.

The veins in his forearm stood out.

The line snapped.

Not with a sound.

With an absence. The pulse stopped.

The luminescence faded. The thread went dark.

The Field Guide pulsed.

NETWORK DISRUPTION: 22/22 JUNCTIONS SEVERED. SIGNAL INTEGRITY: 0%.

FRAGMENT COORDINATION: TERMINATED.

NODE STATUS: DORMANT. THOROUGHNESS RATING: 7.3.

Dale sat back on his heels.

His hand was shaking. The debuff was already working.

He could feel it in the way his thoughts were getting fuzzy at the edges, like trying to read a document through steam.

Like the words were there but the meaning was sliding off.

The adjudicator lowered the device. "Extraction is no longer necessary."

"No," Dale said. "It isn't."

He looked at Linda. Her eyes were closed.

Her breathing was shallow but regular, and the subcutaneous lines were gone.

The node was dormant. She looked like what she'd been before all of this.

A woman in a kitchen. A tenant with a code violation.

He pulled out his clipboard. He was going to write the report.

That was the job.

Report first, then rest. Report first, then rest. Fourteen years of that rhythm.

The Field Guide pulsed one more time.

NEW ENTRY DETECTED.

Dale stopped. His pencil hovered over the clipboard.

SUBJECT: KOWALSKI, D. CLASSIFICATION: UNKNOWN. STATUS: ACTIVE. NOTE: DOCUMENTATION IN PROGRESS.

Dale stared at the entry, and he hadn't created it. He hadn't identified himself.

He hadn't documented anything about himself.

He'd never documented himself. That wasn't how the system worked. You documented threats.

You didn't document the technician, and he looked at the Field Guide.

The entry was there.

Written in his own handwriting. His own pencil.

His own clipboard, and he hadn't written it.

The adjudicator said his name. Something else. A question. The words came through the debuff like sound through water.

Dale couldn't hear it. The debuff was getting worse. Or maybe the Field Guide was getting louder.

The entry changed.

SUBJECT: KOWALSKI, D. CLASSIFICATION: UNKNOWN.

STATUS: ACTIVE.

WEAKNESS IDENTIFIED: COMPULSIVE DOCUMENTATION BEHAVIOR. RECOMMENDED TREATMENT: [REDACTED] THOROUGHNESS RATING: 9.8.

The organism had documented him. It had identified his weakness. It had a treatment protocol.

And its thoroughness rating was higher than his.
