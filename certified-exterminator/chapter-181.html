Chapter 181: Failed Practical Exam

# Chapter 181

The clipboard bounced off the chest of the figure in the pristine hazmat suit.

It didn't clang. It didn't thud. It absorbed into the fabric with a wet, sickening squelch, like a stone dropped into mud.

Dale didn't wait for the recruiter to react. He scrambled backward, his boots slipping on the slick floor of the server room. The air smelled of ozone and burnt hair. The black vein on his wrist throbbed against his pulse, a hot, insistent rhythm that tried to sync with the green line pulsing on the monitor.

He grabbed the edge of the melted server rack. His fingers found purchase on the twisted metal.

"Protocol violation," Dale said, his voice steady despite the tremor in his hands. "Unauthorized contact with a Class-V candidate. I am filing a formal objection."

The figure tilted its head. The reflective faceplate remained blank.

No eyes.

No mouth. Just Dale's own battered reflection staring back at him.

"Objection denied," the deep voice rumbled.

It didn't come from the throat. It resonated from the walls, the floor, the air.

"You have been flagged.

The signal is already in you."

"Then quarantine me," Dale said. He pulled a spray can from his belt.

It was empty, a relic from the last job, but he held it like a weapon.

"I am currently in a Code Red containment zone.

You are contaminating the workspace."

The recruiter stepped forward. The hazmat suit made no sound. No rustle of plastic. No creak of rubber.

Dale backed into the corner. The green line on the monitor spiked. A high-pitched whine filled the room.

"Your refusal is noted," the voice said. "But the Authority does not ask. It assigns."

"Assign what?" Dale demanded.

"Integration. You have the marker. You are compatible. You will serve the capital, or you will be purged as a biohazard."

Dale looked at the black vein. It had spread to his forearm now. Thin, branching capillaries of darkness.

It felt warm. Comfortable.

Like a heated blanket on a cold night.

He hated it.

He hated the comfort.

"I am a Class II technician," Dale said, tapping the badge on his chest. "I do not accept Class V assignments without a written exam and a site survey. That is regulation.

You know this.

You are the Authority."

Silence.

The recruiter raised a gloved hand.

Dale flinched.

But the hand didn't strike. It pointed to the door behind Dale.

"The door," the voice said. "Open it."

"I can't. The lock is engaged."

"It is not."

Dale looked at the door. The handle was still locked from the inside. He had turned it. He remembered.

The recruiter lowered its hand. "Look at the lock, Kowalski."

Dale squinted.

The lock wasn't a mechanism anymore. It was fused. The metal of the door frame had merged with the metal of the door itself.

The Authority had rewritten the physical structure of the room to prevent his exit.

"Physical containment," Dale muttered. "Inefficient."

"Effective," the recruiter corrected.

"You have one minute before the server rack releases the full payload of the dimensional spores.

If you do not accept integration, you will die. If you accept, you will live.

But you will not be human."

Dale looked at the monitor. The green line was accelerating. The whine was getting louder.

He looked at the recruiter.

"Where is the paperwork?" Dale asked.

The recruiter paused.

"What?"

"The offer," Dale said. "The contract.

The Terms of Service.

If you are recruiting me, you follow the process. I need to see the liability clauses. I need to know if the Authority covers workers' comp for dimensional trauma.

I need to know if the integration is reversible."

The recruiter remained still. For three seconds, the room was silent.

Then, a sound.

A mechanical *click*.

The door handle turned.

It didn't unlock. It rotated. The metal twisted, forming a keyhole where none had existed.

Dale didn't walk toward it. He walked toward the monitor.

"System," Dale said, speaking to the air. "Query: Is the server rack currently active?"

No response. The Authority usually responded to direct verbal queries.

"System, query: Is the server rack currently active?"

A blue box appeared in his peripheral vision.

**[SYSTEM ALERT: HARDWARE FAILURE. DATA CORRUPTION DETECTED.]**

Dale frowned. He tapped the air.

"System, query: Can I exploit the corruption to isolate the signal source?"

The box flickered.

**[ERROR: USER LACKS ACCESS TO ROOT DIRECTORY. INSUFFICIENT CLEARANCE.]**

Dale smirked. It was a dry, humorless twist of his lips.

"I don't need root access," he said. "I need a workaround."

He turned back to the recruiter. "You want me to integrate.

You want me to become a carrier.

But carriers are unregulated. They are messy. They break protocols.

You know this. I have a Thoroughness Rating of 9.2.

I do not break protocols.

I bend them."

The recruiter stepped closer. The hazmat suit seemed to ripple, like heat haze.

"Explain."

Dale pointed to the black vein on his wrist. "This is a symbiotic link.

It is not a corruption. It is a channel. The Authority uses it to push data into me.

But you are pushing too hard. The channel is saturated.

If you force more data through, the channel will burst.

I will die. And the data will be lost."

The recruiter tilted its head again.

"You are guessing," the voice said.

"I am diagnosing," Dale corrected.

"Look at the monitor. The green line is spiking.

It is not steady.

It is oscillating. That is not a signal. That is a feedback loop.

You are trying to upload a consciousness into a buffer that is too small."

Dale reached into his briefcase again. This time, he pulled out a pair of wire cutters.

"I can fix this," Dale said.

"I can reroute the feed.

I can create a dedicated channel for the upload. It will require a new protocol.

A custom one."

"You are not authorized to create new protocols," the recruiter said.

"I am authorized to identify weaknesses," Dale said. "And this is a weakness.

Your system is inefficient.

It relies on brute force. It is sloppy. It is a code violation."

The recruiter was silent.

The whine from the servers grew to a scream. Sparks flew from the rack.

Dale moved.

He didn't attack the recruiter. He attacked the server rack.

He jammed the wire cutters into the exposed wiring beneath the monitor. He didn't cut the wires. He twisted them.

*Twist. Loop. Cross-connect.*

His hands moved with the speed of muscle memory. Fourteen years of sealing entry points. Fourteen years of bypassing safety switches that didn't work.

The green line on the monitor stuttered.

The black vein on his wrist cooled.

The recruiter took a step back.

"What are you doing?" the voice demanded.

"Isolating the node," Dale grunted.

He twisted another wire. "I am creating a Faraday cage for the psychic signal.

It won't stop the upload, but it will regulate the flow.

It will prevent the burst."

"That is not a standard protocol," the recruiter said.

"It is a workaround," Dale said. "And it is effective."

The monitor flashed red.

**[SYSTEM ALERT: UNAUTHORIZED MODIFICATION DETECTED.]**

**[TREATMENT POINTS AWARDED: 50]**

**[NEW MECHANIC UNLOCKED: SIGNAL DAMPENING (TIER 1)]**

Dale stared at the text.

He hadn't killed anything. He hadn't identified a monster. He had fixed a machine.

But the Authority had rewarded him.

"Interesting," the recruiter said. The voice was softer now. Less grand. More curious.

"You have exploited a loophole," the figure admitted. "The Authority rewards efficiency. You have reduced system failure risk by forty percent. This is valuable."

Dale pulled his hands away from the wires. They were shaking.

Not from fear.

From the cold.

The warmth of the black vein had vanished, replaced by a numbness that crept up his arm.

"I want the contract," Dale said.

The recruiter extended a hand again.

"Sign here," the voice said.

A sheet of paper appeared in the air. It was blank. White. Pristine.

Dale looked at it.

"Where are the terms?" Dale asked.

"They are implied," the recruiter said.

"Nothing is implied," Dale said. "I need to see the liability. I need to see the exit clause. And I need to know what happens if I fail the next exam."

The recruiter didn't move.

"If you refuse," the voice said, "the spores will kill you. If you accept, you become part of the Authority. You will never be free. But you will be powerful."

Dale looked at the black vein. It was receding. The numbness was spreading.

He looked at the recruiter.

"I have a condition," Dale said.

"Name it."

"I want a Field Guide entry," Dale said. "For the recruiter. For you.

I need to know what you are. I need to know your weaknesses.

If I am going to work for you, I need to know how to protect myself from you."

The recruiter paused.

"You cannot document a Supervisor," the voice said. "It is against policy."

"It is against policy," Dale agreed. "But I am not asking for permission. I am asking for a trade.

I fix your signal.

You give me a name."

The room hummed. The servers were still screaming, but the pitch was lower now. Manageable.

The recruiter reached into its pocket. It pulled out a small, black card.

"Here is a name," the voice said. "But it is not mine. It is the name of the entity that will kill you if you step out of line."

Dale took the card.

It was cold. Heavier than it looked.

He flipped it over.

**[IDENTITY: THE ARCHIVIST]** **[CLASS: IX]** **[WEAKNESS: UNKNOWN]**

Dale stared at the card.

"Unknown?" he asked.

"It has no known weaknesses," the recruiter said. "Because it has never been defeated."

Dale felt a chill that had nothing to do with the air conditioning.

"Then why did you give it to me?"

"Because you need to fear it," the recruiter said.

"Fear is a motivator. You are complacent, Kowalski. You believe your procedures are enough.

They are not. The Capital is not suburban Ohio.

The infestation there does not crawl.

It thinks."

Dale looked at the card. He looked at the recruiter.

He put the card in his briefcase.

"I accept," Dale said.

The recruiter nodded.

"Welcome to the Capital, Technician," the voice said. "Your assignment begins in six hours. Do not be late."

The figure turned. It walked toward the door.

It didn't open the door. It walked through it. The metal rippled around the suit, then solidified again.

Dale stood alone in the server room.

The black vein on his wrist pulsed once. Then it went still.

He looked at the monitor. The green line was steady now.

He looked at the card in his briefcase.

He looked at his hands. They were covered in grease and soot.

He took a deep breath.

He didn't feel relief. He felt a weight. A pressure. Like the air before a storm.

He opened his Field Guide.

**[FIELD GUIDE: UPDATED]** **[ENTRY: THE ARCHIVIST]** **[STATUS: LOCKED]** **[REASON: INSUFFICIENT DATA]**

Dale closed the guide.

He pulled out his pen.

He wrote on his clipboard.

*Check entry points.*

*Verify seals.*

*Find the nest.*

He didn't know where the nest was. He didn't know what it looked like.

But he knew how to look.

He walked out of the server room.

The hallway was empty. The apartments above were silent.

But the silence felt wrong.

It felt watched.

Dale adjusted his collar. He checked his badge.

He started walking toward the exit.

He didn't run.

He walked with the measured pace of a man who knows that if you run, you miss the clues.

And in the Capital, the clues were the only things that would keep him alive.

He reached the front door.

He pushed it open.

The sun was shining.

It was noon.

He had been in the server room for an hour.

But outside, the sky was dark.

The clouds were green.

And the city was silent.

No cars. No people.

Just the wind.

And the sound of something scratching.

Dale pulled out his clipboard.

He wrote: *Atmospheric anomaly detected. Visual confirmation required.*

He stepped outside.

The scratching stopped.

Dale looked down.

The sidewalk was cracking.

Not from heat. Not from age.

From below.

Something was pushing up.

Dale stepped back.

He raised his spray can.

He waited.

The concrete bulged.

A hand punched through.

It was not human.

It was not a monster.

It was a hand made of ink.

Black, dripping, shifting ink.

It grabbed Dale's ankle.

He didn't scream.

He didn't panic.

He looked at the hand.

He looked at the crack in the sidewalk.

He looked at the sky.

He recognized the pattern.

It wasn't an infestation.

It was a signature.

Someone had drawn the rift.

Someone had signed the entry.

Dale looked at the ink-hand gripping his leg.

He looked at the black vein on his wrist.

He looked at the card in his briefcase.

*The Archivist.*

The hand tightened.

Dale smiled.

It was a small, cold smile.

"Protocol violation," Dale said.

He kicked the hand.

He didn't try to break free.

He tried to document it.

He pulled out his pen.

He wrote on the ink-hand.

*Evidence collected.*

The ink-hand dissolved.

The concrete sealed.

The green clouds parted.

And a face appeared in the sky.

It was Dale's face.

But older.

Hollower.

Wearing a hazmat suit made of skin.

The face looked down at him.

It opened its mouth.

No sound came out.

But the message arrived in Dale's mind.

*You are next.*

Dale dropped his pen.

He looked at his hands.

They were turning black.

Not the veins.

The hands.

He looked at the sky.

The face was gone.

The clouds were blue.

The city was loud.

Cars. People. Noise.

Normal.

Dale looked at his hands.

They were normal.

Pale. Scarred. Human.

He looked at the sidewalk.

No crack. No ink.

Had he imagined it?

He looked at his briefcase.

The card was gone.

He looked at his Field Guide.

**[ENTRY: THE ARCHIVIST]** **[STATUS: CONFIRMED]** **[WEAKNESS: RECURSION]**

Dale froze.

Recursion.

He looked at the sky again.

He looked at the people walking by.

One of them stopped.

He looked at Dale.

He had Dale's face.

He had Dale's clothes.

He had Dale's clipboard.

He smiled.

It was a small, cold smile.

"Protocol violation," the stranger said.

Dale stepped back.

The stranger stepped forward.

He held out a hand.

A gloved hand.

White. Pristine.

"Come with me," the stranger said. "We have a Class-V infestation in the capital. And you are now qualified to handle it."

Dale looked at the hand.

He looked at the black vein on his wrist.

It was gone.

He looked at the stranger.

He knew what this was.

It wasn't a trick.

It wasn't a hallucination.

It was the exam.

The practical exam.

And he had just failed it.
