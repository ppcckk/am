Chapter 382: Illegal Sentient Employment

# Chapter 382

The marching column hit the library steps with a rhythmic, wet slap. Dale counted them.

Four hundred and twelve.

He counted wrong. Four hundred and thirteen. He corrected the tally on his clipboard, the graphite tip scratching against the paper hard enough to tear the surface.

They wore blue jumpsuits. The same shade as the ones issued by the Municipal Authority for entry-level sanitation crews. The same shade as the ones Halloway's men had worn.

Dale stepped off the porch. The ground was slick with something that wasn't rain. It smelled like copper and old laundry.

A figure in the front rank stopped. It raised a clipboard. The clipboard was made of bone.

Dale didn't flinch. He adjusted his collar.

"Identify," he said. His voice was flat. It cut through the static hum that filled the air.

The figure tilted its head, and the face was smooth. No eyes.

No mouth.

Just a flat, pale surface where features should have been. A single crack ran down the center, leaking black fluid.

**TARGET: UNLICENSED REPLICANT (MINOR)** **SPECIES: MIMICRY PARASITE (UNVERIFIED)** **STATUS: HOSTILE**

The text in his vision pulsed green. Not blue. Green meant unverified. Green meant the Field Guide entry was corrupted.

Dale tapped the side of his head. The corruption timer in his peripheral vision read 23 hours, 59 minutes.

Halloway had played him.

The new technician wasn't a distraction, and he was a test. Or a trap.

The mimic lunged.

It didn't run. It unfolded. Its limbs elongated, snapping like dry branches, turning into long, spindly sticks that reached for Dale's neck.

Dale sidestepped. He didn't draw a weapon. He didn't have one.

He had a flashlight. He had a can of industrial-grade solvent he'd grabbed from his truck before stepping out.

The stick-hand swiped past his ear. The wind of it blew dust from the pavement.

Dale sprayed.

The solvent hit the mimic's chest. The substance hissed. Steam rose, smelling of ammonia and burning hair. The mimic shrieked, a sound like metal grinding on stone.

**TREATMENT POINTS: +5** **PROTOCOL MISMATCH PENALTY: -40% EFFICACY**

Dale cursed under his breath, and he used the solvent designed for fungal growth. The mimic was classified as a parasite. The cross-contamination debuff kicked in immediately.

The hiss weakened.

The steam turned gray.

He needed a different tool.

He reached into his pocket and pulled out a roll of heavy-duty tape. He unspooled it with his teeth. The plastic backing tore. The adhesive was strong.

The mimic lunged again. Dale threw the tape. It wasn't an attack. It was a distraction.

The sticky end hit the mimic's face. The creature paused, confused. The adhesive held.

Dale moved fast. He didn't fight. He processed.

He grabbed the mimic's extended arm.

It felt cold. Slimy. Like touching a dead fish left in the sun.

He twisted his wrist, applying leverage to the joint. The joint popped.

The arm detached.

It fell to the ground. It kept twitching.

Dale kicked it away. He looked at the main body. The bone clipboard was still clutched in its hand.

He stepped forward. He ripped the clipboard from the mimic's grasp.

The mimic didn't resist. It stood there, leaking black fluid, its face-crack widening.

Dale looked at the clipboard, and the bone surface was covered in writing. Not English.

Symbols. Sharp, angular glyphs that hurt to look at.

But in the corner, there was a stamp.

**AUTHORITY SEAL.** **CERTIFICATION: CLASS IV.** **LICENSEE: H. HALLOWAY.**

Dale's jaw tightened.

Halloway wasn't just sending technicians. He was licensing the monsters.

"Sloppy," Dale said. He wrote the word on his own clipboard. He underlined it twice.

Behind him, the rest of the column moved. They didn't attack. They formed a perimeter. They raised their bone clipboards.

Dale turned. He faced the army.

He opened his Field Guide. The page for **MIMICRY PARASITE** was blank. Corrupted.

He needed to fill it in. Now.

He looked at the severed arm on the ground. The skin was translucent.

Underneath, instead of muscle, there were fibers.

Thin, white threads. Like spider silk.

He knelt. He pulled a pair of tweezers from his belt. He plucked a single thread from the severed limb.

The thread vibrated. It hummed.

Dale held it up to the light. He observed.

He documented.

**OBSERVATION 1: TISSUE COMPOSITION IS CELLULAR, NOT TISSULAR.** **OBSERVATION 2: FIBERS RESPOND TO VIBRATION.** **OBSERVATION 3: NO VITAL ORGANS DETECTED IN EXTREMITY.**

The System pinged.

**FIELD GUIDE UPDATE: MIMICRY PARASITE (FRAGMENTARY).** **WEAKNESS IDENTIFIED: RESONANCE FRAGILITY.** **TARGETED DAMAGE BONUS AVAILABLE: +25% AGAINST STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY.**

The green text turned blue. Then gold.

Dale stood up. He snapped the tweezers shut.

"Resonance," he muttered.

He looked at the marching column. They were waiting. They wanted him to fight. They wanted him to swing.

Dale didn't swing. He whistled.

It was a high, sharp note. The same frequency he used to test for gas leaks. A pure tone. A diagnostic ping.

The column stopped.

Every single one of them turned their blank faces toward him.

The vibration traveled through the ground. It traveled through the air.

The first row of mimics shivered. The black fluid leaking from their cracks bubbled.

Dale whistled again. Louder. He added a second note. A dissonant chord.

The mimics in the front row burst.

It wasn't an explosion. It was a collapse. Their structures lost cohesion. They fell apart into piles of wet, sticky threads and bone shards.

**TREATMENT POINTS: +500** **BONUS FOR PROTOCOL ADHERENCE: +100** **TOTAL: 600**

The rest of the column hesitated.

Dale walked forward. He didn't run. He walked at a pace of 1.2 meters per second. Standard inspection speed.

He passed the piles of debris. He checked for nests.

There were none. These weren't infestations. They were employees.

He reached the edge of the playground. The swings were still. The slide was empty.

But the sandpit was moving.

Something was under the sand. Something big.

Dale stopped. He raised his flashlight.

The beam cut through the green twilight.

The sand shifted.

A shape rose. It was massive.

It wore a blue jumpsuit too, but it was torn.

Ragged, which had no face. It had a head made of stacked, human teeth.

**TARGET: CLASS III MIMICRY PARASITE (MAJOR)** **SPECIES: TEETH-LORD (UNVERIFIED)** **STATUS: AGGRESSIVE**

Dale's stomach turned. He hated teeth.

They were a biohazard, and they harbored bacteria.

They were unsanitary.

He looked at his Field Guide. The entry for **TEETH-LORD** didn't exist. He had to create it.

He looked at the pile of debris from the first wave. The threads were still there. He could use them.

He grabbed a fistful of the white fibers. They were sticky. They adhered to his glove.

The Teeth-Lord roared. The sound was wet and heavy. It slammed its hands into the sand. The ground shook.

Dale didn't retreat. He advanced.

He threw the fibers. Not at the Teeth-Lord. At the ground.

He threw them in a circle. A perimeter.

The Teeth-Lord paused. It looked at the fibers.

Dale whistled.

The fibers vibrated. They buzzed. The Teeth-Lord's teeth chattered. The vibration matched the frequency of the bone clipboard.

The Teeth-Lord screamed. The teeth rattled loose. They fell out, clattering onto the pavement.

Dale kept whistling. He kept throwing.

He was building a cage of sound and silk.

The Teeth-Lord lunged. It tried to break the perimeter.

Dale stepped back. He checked his watch.

14:02, and the shift change was at 14:05.

He had three minutes.

He pulled out his phone. It was dead. He tried again. No signal.

He pulled out a flare. He lit it. The red light flared against the green sky.

The Teeth-Lord recoiled from the light. Fire was another weakness. He hadn't documented it, but he knew it.

He threw the flare into the sandpit.

The Teeth-Lord shrieked. It scrambled back. The fibers caught fire. The flame spread quickly, consuming the white threads.

The Teeth-Lord tried to stamp out the fire. It failed. The fire bit into its leg.

**TREATMENT POINTS: +1200** **NOTE: USE OF FLAME SOURCE REQUIRES Level III LICENSE. VIOLATION LOGGED.**

A red exclamation point appeared in his vision.

**VIOLATION: UNLICENSED PROTOCOL USAGE.** **PENALTY: -200 POINTS.** **WARNING: STRIKES ACCUMULATE TO REVOCATION.**

Dale grunted. He didn't care about the points. He cared about the job.

The Teeth-Lord was burning. It collapsed. It tried to crawl away.

Dale walked up to it. He pulled out his pen. He wrote in the Field Guide.

**ENTRY: TEETH-LORD.** **WEAKNESS: FIRE, RESONANCE.** **NOTES: FLAMMABLE TISSUE. AVOID CONTACT.**

He looked at the burning mass. It was dying.

But it wasn't dead.

The fire wasn't enough. The Authority required verification.

Dale needed to kill it. Properly.

He looked around. The playground was empty. The library was silent. The library was the target.

The Teeth-Lord looked at the library. It stopped burning. It stood up. It pointed at the door.

Dale understood.

This wasn't an attack. It was an eviction notice.

Halloway wasn't trying to kill him. He was trying to clear the zone.

The marching column wasn't there to fight. They were there to occupy.

Dale looked at his hands. They were covered in soot and slime.

He looked at the library. The doors were closed. Locked.

He walked to the library. He didn't look back at the Teeth-Lord. He let it burn.

It would be gone in ten minutes. He'd check for residuals later.

He reached the library doors, and he tried the handle. Locked.

He pulled out his key card. It didn't work.

He looked at the lock. It was a standard municipal lock. Pin tumbler.

He pulled out a pick. He had one in his belt. He used it for jammed cabinet latches.

He inserted the pick. He listened.

Click. Click. Click.

The lock turned, and he pushed the door open.

The air inside was stale. It smelled like old paper and fear.

The lights were off.

Dale clicked on his flashlight.

The beam swept across the room, and the shelves were gone.

The books were gone.

In their place were cages.

Hundreds of cages.

Inside each cage was a person, and they were wearing blue jumpsuits.

They were holding clipboards.

They were silent.

Dale froze.

He knew them, and they were the technicians from Sector 4. The ones who had disappeared last month.

He walked down the aisle. He checked the tags on the cages.

**NAME: JENKINS, R.** **STATUS: ASSIMILATED.** **ROLE: DOCUMENTATION UNIT.**

**NAME: MARTINEZ, L.** **STATUS: ASSIMILATED.** **ROLE: EXTRACTION UNIT.**

He reached the back of the room.

There was one large cage.

Inside sat a man, and he was wearing a suit. A clean, white suit.

He looked up. His eyes were wide. Terrified.

It was Halloway.

Not the Halloway on the phone. The real Halloway. The one from the office.

"Dale," Halloway whispered.

Dale stopped. He raised his flashlight.

"You're licensed," Dale said.

Halloway shook his head. Tears streamed down his face.

"I didn't want to be," Halloway said. "I tried to leave. I tried to quit."

Dale looked at the bars. They were made of bone.

"Why are you here?" Dale asked.

"The Authority doesn't let you quit," Halloway said. "You just get reassigned. To the bottom tier."

Halloway pointed at the cage.

"I'm a pest now, Dale. I'm part of the infestation."

Dale looked at the other cages.

The technicians. They weren't dead. They were working.

They were writing, and they were processing.

They were the Authority's workforce.

Dale looked at Halloway.

"Can you open this?" Dale asked.

Halloway looked at the lock. He shook his head.

"It's not locked from the inside," Halloway said. "It's locked from the outside. By the system."

Dale frowned. He looked at the lock on Halloway's cage. It was different. It was digital.

He pulled out his phone again. He held it up to the lock.

The screen flickered.

**ACCESS DENIED.** **USER NOT AUTHORIZED.**

Dale looked at Halloway.

"If you're a pest," Dale said. "Then who's the technician?"

Halloway looked at the ceiling.

"The Rift," Halloway said. "The Rift isn't an invasion. It's a hiring event."

The ground shook.

Not from the Teeth-Lord.

From above.

The ceiling of the library cracked. Dust fell.

A hand reached down, which was huge. It was covered in blue fabric.

It grabbed the top of Halloway's cage.

It lifted the cage.

Halloway screamed.

"Dale! Don't let them certify you! Don't let them make you one of them!"

The hand pulled the cage up, into the darkness above.

Dale stood in the center of the library. He was alone.

The other technicians were still in their cages. They watched him.

They didn't move, and they waited.

Dale looked at his clipboard.

**STATUS: UNLICENSED.** **ACTION: OBSERVATION.**

He changed the status.

**STATUS: LICENSED.** **ACTION: RECRUITMENT.**

He tapped the screen.

The System responded.

**NEW PROTOCOL UNLOCKED: FIELD TEAM COMMAND.** **REQUIREMENT: COMPLETE 10 VERIFIED EXTERMINATIONS IN A SINGLE ZONE.** **CURRENT PROGRESS: 4/10.**

Dale looked at the cages, and he looked at the hole in the ceiling.

He looked at the technicians.

He walked to the nearest cage.

He put his hand on the bars.

"Jenkins," Dale said.

The technician looked at him. There was no recognition in the eyes. Only duty.

"Report," Dale said.

Jenkins raised his bone clipboard.

**REPORT: INFESTATION IN SECTOR 4.** **STATUS: ACTIVE.** **THREAT LEVEL: CLASS V.**

Dale stared at the report.

Class V.

Halloway had been lying. The problem wasn't a minor infestation. It was a breach. A full-scale dimensional collapse.

And Halloway had buried it.

Dale looked at his hands. They were shaking. Just a little.

He clenched his fists, and he pulled out his pen.

He wrote on his clipboard.

**ACTION: INTERCEPTION.**

He turned to the next cage.

"Martinez," he said. "Give me your flashlight."

Martinez didn't move.

Dale broke the lock, and he didn't use a key. He used the resonance whistle.

The bone bars shattered.

Martinez fell out. He hit the floor. He looked up. His eyes cleared for a second.

"Dale?" Martinez whispered.

"Get up," Dale said. "We have a job to do."

Martinez looked at the hole in the ceiling. Something was coming down.

Dale grabbed Martinez's collar. He pulled him up.

They walked out of the library.

The Teeth-Lord was gone. Only ash remained.

The marching column was gone, and the sky was still green.

But the air was changing. It was getting colder.

Dale looked at his Field Guide.

**NEW ENTRY: HUMAN REPLICANT.** **WEAKNESS: MORAL DISCORD.** **WEAKNESS: AUTHORITY DENIAL.**

Dale looked at Martinez.

"You're not licensed," Dale said.

Martinez nodded.

"I quit. I tried to quit."

"Good," Dale said. "I need a second."

Martinez looked at the bone clipboard in his hand. He threw it away.

"I'm with you," Martinez said.

Dale opened his Field Guide to a blank page, and he wrote.

He didn't write about monsters.

He wrote a violation code.

**CODE: 99-B.** **DESCRIPTION: ILLEGAL EMPLOYMENT OF SENTIENT BEINGS.** **PENALTY: TERMINATION OF EMPLOYER.**

Dale looked at the sky, and he waited for the next shift.

Dale’s pen tapped against the paper as a new name appeared.
