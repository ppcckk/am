Chapter 20: Unlicensed Aerial Vector

# Chapter 20

The sealant cracked in a line that shouldn't have been possible.

Dale had used polyurethane foam rated for four hundred pounds of lateral pressure. He'd packed it in three passes, backfilled with concrete patch, and let it cure for six hours.

The thing under the asphalt was pushing through anyway.

Not breaking the seal. Lifting it. The whole patch rose two inches, then three, and Dale could see the edges curling like the lid of a can being pried open.

He wrote.

*Entry vector: sub-surface, vertical.

Pushes through cured sealant than displacing soil. Suggests rigid body structure.

Not soft-bodied.

Not gelatinous. Rigid.*

The patch lifted another inch. A sound came from underneath.

Not a squeal.

Not a hiss.

A clicking. Rapid, rhythmic, like a thousand tiny mandibles working in sequence.

*Sound: clicking, rapid, sub-surface. Frequency too high for rodent. Consistent with arthropod mandibular activity.

But not insect. Insects don't click like that.

Insects stridulate, which is something else.*

He checked his flashlight.

Aimed it at the gap where the sealant had lifted. A leg came through.

Then another.

Both segmented, dark, with a jointed structure that looked almost like a spider's leg but too thick, too blunt. The tips weren't pointed. They were flat.

Padded.

*Legs: segmented, 8 visible.

Jointed.

Tips flat and padded. Not pointed.

Not hooked. Adhesive pads? Climbing adaptation?

No. These are for gripping smooth surfaces.

Asphalt is smooth.

It's adapted to push through sealed surfaces specifically.*

His phone buzzed. Voss.

"I'm here. Where are you?"

Dale didn't answer. He kept writing.

*Provisional entry: Species unknown.

Rigid body. 8+ legs. Flat-padded tips.

Clicking mandibles.

Sub-surface entry vector. Pushes through sealant. Adapted to sealed environments.*

He was doing differential diagnosis. Ruling things out.

Not identifying what it was.

Identifying what it wasn't.

German cockroach. No.

Wrong leg count. Brown-banded. No.

Wrong body type. Rattus profundus.

No.

Wrong leg count, wrong sound. Ant. No.

Wrong size, wrong leg structure.

Termite. No.

Termites don't have eight legs.

*Not termite. Not ant. Not spider.

Not rodent. Not insect.

Not arachnid.

Not crustacean.*

The System pinged. A new notification.

FIELD GUIDE: PARTIAL IDENTIFICATION ACCEPTED. Provisional entry created.

Confidence: 34%. Targeted damage bonus: +12%. Note: reduced from standard +40% due to provisional status.

Twelve percent. Not nothing.

Not the full bonus, but not zero.

The System recognized elimination as a valid identification method. He'd found a loophole. Or rather, the System had always had it, and he'd just been the first to think of using it.

The patch lifted another inch.

More legs. He counted eleven.

Then thirteen, and the thing was long. Long and rigid and pushing itself up through the asphalt like a piston.

"Kowalski." Voss's voice came from behind him. Dale turned.

The man was younger than him. Mid-thirties maybe.

Wore a different uniform.

Black instead of gray.

Had a different patch on his shoulder. Same Authority logo, different certification tier marking.

Class II. Same as Dale. But his stance.

His gaze on the bulging patch. Not like a technician assessing a problem.

Like a hunter sizing up prey.

"That's a Class-4 breach," Voss said. "You need to stand down."

"I'm documenting."

"You're contaminating my claim. I filed this job through the Authority portal an hour ago. It's assigned to me.

Dale knew the rest of it, because the Authority published transfers whether you wanted them published or not. Voss had come down from Berrien County six weeks ago, when Berrien dissolved its office and redistributed its roster. A transferred technician kept his certification. He did not keep his claim history. Eleven months to rebuild a record from zero, in a county that already had Dale Kowalski in it."

"I sealed that drain six hours ago. The job was closed."

"The seal failed. That's a new infestation. New claim."

Dale looked at him. "It's not a new infestation. It's the same nest.

My sealant failed because whatever's in there is adapted to defeat sealant.

That's a continuation of the same job."

Voss shook his head.

"Doesn't matter. Authority rules. Competing claim, both parties on-site, joint review.

You stand down or I file a protocol violation against you."

Dale looked at the patch, which was lifting faster now.

The thing was almost through. He could see a body section emerging.

Segmented. Dark. Hard-shelled.

The clicking sound was louder. More rhythmic.

Almost like a signal.

"Whatever you're going to do," Dale said, "do it now. It's almost clear of the surface."

Voss nodded. He reached for his toolkit. Pulled out a device that looked like a spray applicator but bulkier.

He thumbed a setting on the side.

Dale saw the protocol label.

VERMIN SUPPRESSION. Species: Rattus profundus.

"No," Dale said.

Voss fired.

The spray hit the emerging body. The creature recoiled. A sound like a car alarm. Then a System notification that Dale felt in his teeth.

CROSS-CONTAMINATION DETECTED.

Protocol deployed against non-target species. Field Guide entry for UNKNOWN SPECIES contaminated. Access locked: 24 hours.

Penalty applied: -40% effectiveness. Target status: agitated.

Voss's protocol hadn't killed it.

Hadn't even slowed it down much.

But it had agitated it. The creature's clicking changed rhythm.

Became faster. Erratic. The body surged upward.

More segments emerged. The thing was three feet long now.

Maybe four.

The padded legs gripped the edges of the cracked asphalt and pulled itself free.

Dale saw it for the first time.

It looked like a centipede crossed with a cockroach crossed with something that didn't have a name.

The body was segmented, hard-shelled, dark as wet asphalt. The legs were too many and too thick.

Mandibles clicked on the head, and its flat eyes reflected the flashlight, suggesting compound structure.

It was maybe four feet long and eight inches wide. It looked at him. Or it looked at the flashlight.

Hard to tell.

Voss was already reaching for another protocol. "I'll switch to arthropod."

"Don't."

"Your guide entry is contaminated anyway. I can't use your provisional data. I'm going with arthropod."

"It's not an arthropod."

"It has segmented legs."

"Those aren't legs.

Those are gripping appendages.

Look at the joint structure. Look at the pad configuration.

Arthropod legs are for locomotion. Those are for adhesion. This thing is adapted to grip sealed surfaces.

It's not walking. It's climbing through a sealed container."

Voss hesitated. Half a second. Then he fired the arthropod protocol anyway.

The creature absorbed it.

Shook it off. Its clicking became a roar. It lunged at Voss.

He dodged.

Barely. The thing's mandibles snapped at the air where his head had been.

Dale moved.

Not with a protocol. Not with the System. With his hands. With his knowledge.

He grabbed the sealant can.

Not to spray the creature. To spray the ground around it. Around the hole it had come through.

He sprayed in a wide arc. Polyurethane foam.

Expanding.

Filling the gap.

Sealing the entry vector.

The creature turned. Lunged at him. Dale stepped back.

The foam was expanding around the hole, closing it. The creature's legs scrabbled at the edges.

It was trying to get back through.

Trying to retreat.

But it was already out. The hole was closing. It was trapped on the surface.

Dale grabbed his wrench.

Not because the System told him to.

Because he had knowledge of creatures adapted to sealed environments. He'd seen it before.

Not with monsters.

With termites. With carpenter ants. With any creature that had evolved to live inside a sealed space.

They couldn't survive outside it.

Not immediately. Not in seconds.

But they couldn't regulate moisture.

Couldn't regulate temperature.

Couldn't handle air movement. A sealed-space creature exposed to open air was a creature in crisis.

He hit it with the wrench. Not to kill it. To flip it.

Expose its ventral side. The soft side.

The side that had evolved to press against damp earth in a sealed tunnel.

The side that was now exposed to open air, and the creature went rigid. Its legs curled. The clicking stopped.

He hit it again.

And again. Not with the System's targeted bonus.

Not with a protocol.

With a wrench and the knowledge that this thing was dying from the inside because it was exposed to air it wasn't built for.

Voss was staring at him.

Dale hit it one more time. The creature went still.

He pulled out his clipboard. Wrote:

*Elimination method: environmental exposure. Target is obligate subterranean.

Cannot regulate moisture or temperature in open air.

Exposure time to incapacitation: approximately 45 seconds.

Method: wrench to ventral surface to accelerate desiccation.*

The System pinged.

INFESTATION NEUTRALIZED. Species: UNKNOWN, provisional entry #47. Thoroughness Rating: 9.2.

Treatment Points awarded: 8. Note: elimination method not documented prior to execution.

Partial credit only.

Seven point one. Not his best. He'd been rushed.

Voss had complicated things.

But it counted.

Voss stood over the body. His face was tight. "You can't just hit things with a wrench."

"I can when the System's protocols are contaminated and you've locked my Field Guide for twenty-four hours."

"That was an accident."

"You deployed a Rattus protocol against an unknown species. That's not an accident. That's a cross-contamination violation. You just cost me a targeted bonus on a species I'm still trying to identify."

Voss said nothing. He was already on his phone. Filing something. Dale could see the Authority portal open on his screen.

Dale went back to his clipboard, and he needed to document the body. Take measurements. Note the leg configuration.

The mandible structure. The pad arrangement.

All of it.

Because tomorrow, when his Field Guide entry unlocked, he'd have a full provisional entry waiting.

And the next time he saw one of these things, he'd have the full forty percent bonus.

Unless there weren't more of them.

His phone buzzed.

Not Voss. Not a text.

The Authority.

INFESTATION DETECTED. Species: UNKNOWN, matches provisional entry #47. Location: 3.2 miles northeast. Entry vector: sub-surface.

He looked at the notification. Then another.

INFESTATION DETECTED. Species: UNKNOWN, matches provisional entry #47. Location: 1.8 miles northwest. Entry vector: sub-surface.

Another.

INFESTATION DETECTED. Species: UNKNOWN, matches provisional entry #47. Location: 0.4 miles south. Entry vector: sub-surface.

Dale counted. His hands were steady. They'd always been steady.

He'd written code violation reports while a German cockroach infestation spread across a family's kitchen and his hands hadn't shaken then. They weren't shaking now.

Forty-three notifications.

Forty-three locations. All within his service area. All sub-surface entry vectors. All matching the species he'd just killed with a wrench.

He looked at Voss. Voss was looking at his own phone. His face had gone pale.

"You're getting them too," Dale said.

Voss nodded. "How many?"

Dale checked his notifications again. "Forty-three."

"I have sixty-one."

Dale looked at the body on the ground

He looked at the body on the ground. Then at his phone. Then at the body again.

Forty-three, which was manageable. That was a Tuesday. You get forty-three notifications on a Tuesday when a water main breaks and every basement in the neighborhood becomes a breeding site.

You triage.

You prioritize by proximity and severity. You call for backup.

He started drafting a response plan.

Cluster the southern six first—tight radius, likely a nest with multiple exit points. The northern spread was different. Dispersed.

That suggested either multiple nests or a nest with a larger tunnel network. Either way, you treated the network, not the individuals.

You always treated the network.

"Voss." Dale didn't look up from his clipboard. "What's your current protocol loadout?"

"Class II. Standard vertebrate package. I've got my gel bait applicator and the perimeter sprayer."

"Useless.

These things aren't vertebrates. They're arthropod-adjacent.

Mandibles, segmented legs, chitinous plating. You need contact dust or a residual aerosol with an insect growth regulator component."

Voss stared at him. "I don't have insect growth regulators."

"No. You don't." Dale wrote that down.

Noted it.

A supply chain gap. A code violation waiting to happen. "Then we're working with what we have."

His phone buzzed again, and he looked down.

The notification was different.

Not sub-surface. Not 3.2 miles. Not matching entry #47.

INFESTATION DETECTED.

Species: UNKNOWN. No matching entry.

Entry vector: AERIAL.

Location: 0.1 miles. Directly overhead.

Dale looked up.

The sky above the subdivision was wrong. Not dark—clouds don't do that.

Wrong.

The air had a grainy texture, as if the space between the houses was filled with particulate, living matter.

A sound came down from it. Not a roar.

Not a shriek. A vibration. The kind you feel in your molars before a semi-truck passes too close on a two-lane road.

Voss was looking up too. His phone was in his hand, screen dark.

"Dale," he said. His voice was different. "How many aerial vectors do you have?"

Dale checked. "One."

"I have one too."

They both looked up. The grain in the air was getting denser. It was getting closer. And the vibration was shifting pitch, climbing, like something tuning itself.

Dale looked at his clipboard. He looked at the body on the ground. He looked at the forty-three sub-surface notifications still glowing on his screen.

He looked up.

"Voss," he said. "I think we just got reclassified."

His Authority portal opened. Not a notification. A message. From the Licensing Authority itself.

CERTIFICATION REVIEW INITIATED. CURRENT SERVICE AREA: NON-COMPLIANT. INFESTATION CLASSIFICATION: PENDING.

Below it, a second line.

NOTE: AERIAL VECTOR DOES NOT CORRESPOND TO ANY KNOWN VERMIN TAXONOMY. YOU ARE NOT EQUIPPED FOR THIS.

Dale stared at the message. Then at the sky. Then at his clipboard.

He wrote one word on it.

*Unlicensed.*
