Chapter 40: Stuttering Seal

# Chapter 40

The truck's front bumper kissed the loading dock and Dale was out before the engine cut.

The membrane was wrong.

He could see it from the parking lot. A big-box hardware store, the kind with forty-foot ceilings and a concrete slab floor that should have been stable enough to hold a dimensional seal.

The membrane sat in the middle of aisle fourteen, exposed where someone had dragged a pallet of mulch across it.

It pulsed. Not the slow, tidal rhythm Dale had learned to read at the first site. This one flickered.

Stuttered.

Like a fluorescent tube losing gas.

A man in a Level IV vest was already inside, spraying containment foam at the membrane's edges.

White foam. The standard vermin suppression compound. Dale could smell it from the parking lot.

The sharp ammonia bite of it, and he walked in fast.

"Hey." The man turned.

Young.

Maybe twenty-six. A combat cut on his Field Guide tablet, the kind that meant he'd passed the practical exam but never bothered with the written portion's theory module.

His name tag read KOWALSKI. No. KOWALSKI was Dale.

This was KOWALSKI. No.

It read KOVAC.

Dale's eyes had been doing that thing where they flattened everything into shapes.

Kovac. Level IV. Structural Containment.

"You're using vermin foam on a membrane," Dale said.

Kovac looked at him.

Looked at the vest. Saw the Level V designation.

Something flickered in his expression.

Not respect. Annoyance.

"It's the only containment protocol I have that works on anything organic," Kovac said. "I tried the structural sealant first. It wouldn't adhere. The Authority wouldn't let me deploy it."

"Because it's not a structural breach yet."

"It's literally a hole in the ground."

"It's a membrane. The Authority classifies it as biological until it ruptures. Once it ruptures, it becomes structural. Until then, it's a living thing."

Kovac stared at the membrane. It pulsed.

The foam he'd applied was already dissolving at the edges, eating into the tissue.

Dale could see the discoloration spreading.

Gray patches where the membrane's surface was being chemically degraded.

"You're killing it," Dale said.

"I'm containing it."

"You're cross-contaminating it. Every spray you fire at that thing is a protocol mismatch. The Authority doesn't flag it because there's no Field Guide entry for membranes yet. But the damage is real."

Kovac looked at his tablet. The containment gauge read nineteen percent. It had been twenty-three when Dale walked in. Four points in ninety seconds.

"It's dropping," Kovac said.

"Yes."

"What do I do?"

Dale opened his clipboard. The interface was different at Level V. More options.

More submenus. He navigated to the documentation tab, found the species classification field, and typed:

Dimensional Membrane (Membrana hiatus). Structural Entry Point. Class III.

The Authority's response was immediate.

CLASSIFICATION REJECTED. NO FIELD GUIDE ENTRY EXISTS.

Dale had expected that. He switched to the identification protocol. The one that let him document a new species in real time, and it required three observations: morphology, behavior, and interaction with environment.

He walked to the membrane.

Knelt. Put his gloved hand two inches from its surface. The heat coming off it was wrong.

Not the wet warmth of biological tissue.

Dry. Radiant.

Like standing near a kiln, and he wrote: Morphology. Tissue-like. No cellular structure visible at macro scale.

Surface exhibits peristaltic contraction at 4.2-second intervals. Coloration: translucent amber with dark vascular threading.

No visible orifices.

He watched it pulse, and the stutter was getting worse. The gray patches from Kovac's foam were spreading, and each time the membrane contracted, the gray areas tore slightly.

Micro-tears. They sealed themselves, but slower each time.

He wrote: Behavior. Passive.

No offensive response to stimuli.

Reacts to chemical contact with localized contraction and surface degradation. Reacts to physical contact with increased peristaltic rate. No evidence of predatory or territorial behavior.

He looked at the ground around the membrane, and the concrete slab was cracked in a radial pattern. Not from pressure.

From dissolution.

The concrete was being eaten from the edges inward. The membrane's roots, if that was what they were, were secreting something that broke down silicate.

He wrote: Environmental interaction. Degrades silicate substrates.

Expands radial footprint at approximately 2 centimeters per hour.

No evidence of dimensional traversal.

Function appears to be structural: the membrane is a plug. A living plug holding a hole closed.

The Authority responded.

FIELD GUIDE ENTRY CREATED. Dimensional Membrane (Membrana hiatus). Classification accepted.

Structural Entry Point. Class III.

And then, below it:

TREATMENT PROTOCOL UNLOCKED: STRUCTURAL REINFORCEMENT (Class III).

REQUIRES: FULL INSPECTION REPORT. ALL FIVE STEPS. CURRENT STEPS COMPLETED: 3/5.

REMAINING: ISOLATE.

VERIFY.

Dale looked at the gauge. Fourteen percent.

"I need twenty minutes," he said.

"You don't have twenty minutes."

"I need twenty minutes."

Kovac looked at the membrane. At the gray spreading across it. At the radial cracks in the concrete that were now three feet from the nearest shelving unit. "What happens at zero?"

"The membrane ruptures. The hole opens. Whatever's on the other side comes through."

"What's on the other side?"

Dale didn't answer, and he couldn't. He'd seen the first one. The crawlspace membrane.

He'd seen what the Authority's classification system called it before it was classified. A hole.

A hole with something breathing on the other side.

He started on isolation.

The protocol required him to create a perimeter that prevented the membrane's expansion.

Not containment. Isolation.

He had to stop the silicate degradation from spreading. The structural reinforcement protocol would work on the membrane itself, but only if the surrounding substrate was stable. Continuing concrete dissolution left no anchor for the seal.

He pulled the neutralizing agent from his kit. The same compound Vasquez used on her foam cannon.

It was designed for dimensional residue, not concrete.

But Dale had read the written exam. Section 7, subsection 14: dimensional residue is, at its chemical core, a silicate solvent, and the neutralizer was a silicate stabilizer.

It would work on the concrete, and he sprayed a ring around the membrane. Four feet out. The neutralizer hit the concrete and the radial cracks stopped spreading. Crack edges clouded as a thin film formed over the neutralizing solvent.

Kovac watched. "You just made that up."

"I read the exam."

"You read the whole thing?"

"Cover to cover."

The Authority logged it.

STEP 4 COMPLETE: ISOLATE. STEP 5 REMAINING: VERIFY.

Verification. The final step.

The one that most technicians skipped.

The one that the Authority weighted heaviest on the Thoroughness Scale. Verification meant confirming the problem was fully addressed. For a vermin infestation, it meant checking for survivors.

For a membrane, it meant confirming the structural reinforcement would hold.

He couldn't verify before applying the reinforcement.

The Authority's protocol chain was sequential.

Verify came after isolate but before eliminate. For a structural breach, eliminate meant applying the reinforcement.

So verify meant confirming the substrate was stable enough to support the reinforcement.

He needed to test the neutralized concrete, and he knelt. Pressed his thumb against the clouded film.

It was hard.

Not fully hardened. Maybe sixty percent. He needed it at eighty or above for the reinforcement to bond.

He sprayed another ring.

Closer this time. Two feet out.

The neutralizer hit the cracks and the film thickened, and he pressed again. Harder. His thumb left no mark.

He pulled out his clipboard. Found the verification field.

Typed: Substrate stability confirmed at 82 percent tensile strength.

Radial expansion halted.

Membrane perimeter isolated.

The Authority responded.

STEP 5 COMPLETE: VERIFY. ALL FIVE STEPS COMPLETE. TREATMENT PROTOCOL: STRUCTURAL REINFORCEMENT (Class III) UNLOCKED. DEPLOY?

Dale hit yes.

The protocol wasn't a spray. It wasn't a foam. It was a field.

The Authority's system generated a containment lattice around the membrane, a geometric pattern of hard light that pressed the membrane flat and sealed the radial cracks from the outside. The membrane stopped pulsing.

The gray patches stopped spreading.

The concrete stopped dissolving.

The gauge held at fourteen percent.

It didn't go up. But it stopped going down.

Kovac stared at it. "Is it done?"

"It's stable. Not healed. Stable."

"What's the difference?"

"Stable means it won't get worse today.

Healed means the hole is closed. I don't have a protocol for that.

Nobody does.

Not yet."

Kovac nodded slowly. He looked at his tablet.

His own containment gauge was still reading nineteen percent, because his protocols had been logged as ineffective.

His Thoroughness Rating for this job would be low, and he knew it. He didn't look happy about it.

"You should document your work," Dale said. "The Authority will pull it either way. Better if it's clean."

Kovac started typing. Dale watched for a moment, then turned back to the membrane. The lattice hummed. The membrane sat beneath it like a patient under a restraint.

His clipboard buzzed.

THOROUGHNESS RATING: THIS JOB. 8.9. DELEGATED AREA DEVIATION: -0.5. NET RATING: 8.4.

Dale read it twice.

His overall rating had been 9.2. The delegation to Vasquez had cost him. The Authority logged it as a protocol deviation.

A Level V primary unit was not supposed to delegate containment responsibility outside of shift-change parameters. He'd used the provision.

It was legal.

But it wasn't standard.

8.4, and he looked at the threshold.

He'd seen it once before, on the certification dashboard. The Level V maintenance threshold was 8.5.

Below that, the Authority scheduled a review.

A review meant re-examination. Written and practical. Both.

And if he failed either, he lost the level, and he was at 8.4.

His clipboard buzzed again.

CERTIFICATION REVIEW SCHEDULED. 72 HOURS. REVIEW AT CURRENT RATING: 8.4. RECOMMENDATION: IMPROVE THOROUGHNESS RATING BEFORE REVIEW.

Seventy-two hours.

Three days, and he needed to raise his rating above 8.5 before the Authority pulled him back for re-examination. That meant finding another job.

Completing it at a high rating. Fast.

His clipboard buzzed a third time.

SECONDARY BREACH DETECTED. LOCATION: PRIMARY UNIT'S DELEGATED AREA. INTEGRITY: 22 PERCENT. ESTIMATED RUPTURE: 4 HOURS.

Vasquez, and the first seal was failing. And the Authority was logging it under Dale's name.

His delegated area.

His responsibility. If it ruptured while he was four miles away, it would be on his record. Another deviation.

Another point off, and he looked at Kovac. Kovac was still typing his report. The young man looked up.

"You have to go back."

"Yes."

"The membrane here is stable."

"For now."

"And if you leave, your rating drops further."

"Yes."

Kovac set down his tablet. "I'll stay. Watch it. Log the lattice integrity every hour."

"You're Level IV."

"I know what I'm doing. You showed me."

Dale looked at the lattice. It hummed.

The membrane sat beneath it, amber and still.

Fourteen percent. Holding.

He walked to his truck. Started the engine. Pulled out of the parking lot with the tires still spinning.

Four miles. Twenty-two percent. Four hours.

His clipboard buzzed a fourth time.

NOTIFICATION: REVIEW BOARD COMPOSITION UPDATED. EXAMINER ASSIGNED: [REDACTED]. NOTE: EXAMINER REQUESTS SPECIFIC REVIEW OF DELEGATION DECISION.

Dale read it. Read it again.

The examiner was redacted.

The Authority never named its examiners.

But the note was new. An examiner requesting a specific review of his delegation decision meant someone at the Authority was watching this job.

Specifically. Personally.

He drove.

The sky above the highway was cracking. Not the slow, spreading fractures he'd seen before.

These were fast.

Branching. Like ice forming on a windshield, except the ice was made of light and the cracks were getting longer every second.

His clipboard buzzed.

UNLICENSED INFESTATION DETECTED.

CLASS: III. LOCATION: PRIMARY UNIT'S DELEGATED AREA.

SECONDARY INFESTATION.

ORIGIN: MEMBRANE RUPTURE. STATUS: ACTIVE.

The membrane had already torn. Not fully. But enough. Something was coming through.

Dale pressed the accelerator.
