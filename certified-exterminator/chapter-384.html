Chapter 384: The Clipboard Weapon

# Chapter 384

The resonance whistle screamed, a high, piercing tone that shattered the glass of the library's front doors. It wasn't a sound meant for human ears.

It was a frequency designed to shatter chitin, to liquefy internal organs, to disrupt the magical coherence of Class-IV entities.

But the inspector didn't scream. He didn't even blink. The men in suits standing in a perfect circle outside didn't move.

Their boots were planted on the cracked asphalt, and their hands were clasped behind their backs, white gloves pristine and unblemished.

Dale's hand trembled. Not from fear.

From the feedback loop, and the whistle was resonating against his own bones, vibrating in the marrow of his ulna. He dropped it. The sound cut out instantly, leaving a ringing silence that felt heavier than the noise.

The inspector picked up the clipboard. He didn't look at Dale.

He looked at the blank page.

He ran a finger over the paper.

The paper remained blank. The ink had not returned.

The data had not reappeared.

**"AUDIT FAILURE,"** the inspector said. The voice wasn't loud.

It was flat.

"Non-compliant documentation structure detected. Subject: Dale Kowalski. Status: Revoked."

A red banner flashed across Dale's vision.

It wasn't the usual amber warning. This was crimson.

Solid.

Opaque.

**CERTIFICATION STATUS: SUSPENDED.** **FIELD GUIDE: LOCKED.** **PROTOCOLS: DISABLED.**

Dale felt the sudden absence like a physical blow. The subtle hum of his Field Guide, the constant background noise of available data streams, the passive knowledge of weaknesses and entry points—it all vanished.

He was blind, and he was naked.

He was just a man in a stained jumpsuit standing in a library while bureaucratic angels watched him.

Martinez grabbed his arm.

"Dale. Run."

"No." Dale pulled his arm away. He straightened his spine. He reached into his pocket and pulled out a pen.

A standard ballpoint. Blue ink.

Cheap.

The inspector tilted his head.

"You have no active license. Your protocols are offline.

Your Field Guide is inaccessible.

What do you intend to do with that pen?

"

"Dale Kowalski," Dale said.

His voice was steady. Dry.

"Level II Technician.

Suspension order issued by Inspector Vane, Unit 7. Timestamp: 14:02:15.

Location: Public Library, Sector 4.

"

He clicked the pen. He wrote on the blank page.

The ink didn't stick to the paper, and the paper was Authority-certified. Fireproof.

Waterproof. And, ink-proof for unauthorized users. The blue ballpoint glided across the surface, leaving no mark.

The inspector smiled. It was a thin, lipless expression.

"You cannot document what you cannot access.

The System retains the truth. You do not.

"

"The System retains a backup," Dale muttered.

He flipped the page. Another blank sheet.

He tried writing on that.

Nothing. He tried pressing harder. The tip of the pen bent.

"Stop resisting," the inspector said. "Surrender the device. Surrender the field notes. We will process your removal."

Removal. Not arrest.

Removal, which was a pest control term.

It meant extraction. It meant disposal.

Dale looked at the suits outside. They weren't moving. They weren't attacking.

They were waiting. They were witnesses.

If he attacked, they would intervene.

If he ran, they would pursue. If he submitted, he was done.

But Dale hadn't submitted. He was observing.

He looked at the inspector's clipboard. He looked at the red banner in his vision. He looked at the blank paper.

Then he looked at the library itself.

The library wasn't just a building, which was a structure. It had entry points.

It had ventilation.

It had a foundation. And it had a specific type of infestation that had been festering in the walls for three days.

Dale closed his eyes. He didn't need the Field Guide. He didn't need the protocols. He needed the code.

He opened his eyes. He spoke to the air. Not to the inspector. To the System.

"File a complaint," Dale said.

The inspector paused.

"Clarify."

"Violation code K-4: Improper Containment of Class-III Dimensional Spores. Location: Public Library, Sector 4. Report filed by unauthorized observer Dale Kowalski. Witness: Inspector Vane, Unit 7."

The inspector frowned.

"You have no license to file complaints. Your account is frozen."

"I am a citizen," Dale said. "I am reporting a health hazard.

I am not using a protocol.

I am not using a protocol toolkit.

I am using the municipal code.

"

He pointed to the floor.

"Look at the carpet."

The inspector didn't look.

"Irrelevant."

"It's wet," Dale said. "Not with water. With mucus.

It's seeping from the HVAC vents. The Rift didn't just open in the ground.

It opened in the plumbing.

The steam pipes are leaking dimensional fluid. It's pooling under the floorboards.

"

The inspector looked down. He looked at the carpet. It was dark. Damp.

"The Authority monitors environmental health," Dale continued.

"If the building is structurally compromised by dimensional leakage, the site is classified as a Hazard Zone. In a Hazard Zone, all active protocols for licensed technicians are suspended to prevent cross-contamination.

That's why you're here.

You're not auditing me. You're containing the site.

"

The inspector's eyes narrowed.

"You are attempting to manipulate the classification."

"I'm reading the sign," Dale said. He pointed to a small placard on the wall near the entrance. It was faded. It had been posted before the Rift opened. **BUILDING SAFETY INSPECTION PENDING.**

Dale stepped forward. He stepped onto the damp carpet.

"Violation code S-2: Failure to Remediate Structural Moisture. Violation code S-9: Unauthorized Use of Hazardous Materials in a Public Space."

He took a deep breath. The air smelled of ozone and rot. It smelled of beetles.

"If the site is a Hazard Zone," Dale said, "then the inspector is operating without a safety permit. And if the inspector is operating without a safety permit, the audit is invalid."

The red banner in Dale's vision flickered.

**SYSTEM NOTIFICATION: ENVIRONMENTAL HAZARD DETECTED.** **CLASS: STRUCTURAL/BILOGICAL.** **STATUS: UNLICENSED ACTIVITY SUSPENDED BY DEFAULT.** **REASON: SITE SAFETY VIOLATION PRE-CERTIFICATION.**

The inspector froze. The white gloves twitched.

"You..." The inspector's voice lost its flatness. It sounded confused. "You are claiming the site is unsafe?"

"I'm claiming it's unsafe for *you*," Dale said. "You're standing in a pool of Class-III spore fluid.

You're not wearing a respirator.

You're not wearing a seal-suit.

You're wearing a suit. A nice suit.

But it's not rated for biological containment.

"

The inspector looked at his boots. Dark liquid was wicking up the leather.

"Step back," Dale ordered. "Protocol 114: Immediate Evacuation of Unprotected Personnel from Bio-Hazard Zones."

"I do not take orders from a suspended technician," the inspector said. But he didn't move. He was staring at his boots.

"Then I'm calling it in," Dale said. He pulled out his phone. It was dead. The battery was drained by the resonance.

He didn't need the phone. He needed a witness.

"Martinez," Dale said. "Write it down."

Martinez was shaking. He was holding a napkin from the cafeteria. He had a pencil. He was writing furiously on the napkin.

"Inspector Vane, Unit 7. Violation of OSHA Standard 1910.120: Emergency Response.

Failure to wear Personal Protective Equipment.

Hazard: Dimensional Spores. Date: Today.

"

The inspector looked at Martinez. Then at Dale.

"This is irrelevant," the inspector said. But his voice was tighter. Higher.

"It's on the record," Dale said.

"The napkin. The witness.

The visual confirmation of fluid on your boots.

The System logs environmental data. It sees the moisture. It sees the spore count.

It doesn't care about your certification level. It cares about the violation.

"

The red banner vanished.

A new notification appeared. Amber.

**AUDIT SUSPENDED PENDING HAZARD REMEDIATION.** **TECHNICIAN STATUS: PROVISIONAL.** **PERMISSION GRANTED: SITE SAFETY ASSESSMENT.**

Dale exhaled. His shoulders dropped.

The inspector stepped back. His face was pale.

"You are manipulating the loophole."

"It's not a loophole," Dale said.

"It's the job.

If the environment isn't safe, the technician can't work. And if the auditor isn't safe, the audit can't happen.

You can't inspect a building that's on fire. You can't audit a guy standing in a biohazard zone.

"

Dale looked at the suits outside. They hadn't moved. They were watching.

"Get out," Dale said to the inspector. "Get out of the library.

Get your people out.

We need to pump out the basement. We need to seal the vents. We need to treat the nest before you can even look at my paperwork.

"

The inspector straightened his tie. He looked at the blank clipboard in his hands. He looked at Dale.

"You have bought yourself time," the inspector said. "But the clock is still running. The Authority does not forget."

"Good," Dale said. "Neither do I."

The inspector turned, and he walked out of the library. The men in suits parted for him.

They didn't speak.

They didn't look at Dale. They just formed a perimeter, blocking the exits.

Dale was trapped. But he was licensed. Provisionally. For safety assessment only.

He looked at Martinez.

"What did you write?"

Martinez held up the napkin.

"I wrote everything. Including that you called me a liability."

"Put it in your pocket," Dale said. "Don't let them burn it. Don't let them eat it. If they try, you scream."

Martinez nodded. He put the napkin in his breast pocket. He patted it twice.

Dale walked to the center of the room. The floor was wet.

He could feel the warmth of the spore fluid through the soles of his boots, which was warm.

Too warm.

He knelt. He put his hand on the floor. He didn't use a protocol.

He didn't use a scanner. He used his fingers.

He felt the vibration. It wasn't the hum of the System. It was a heartbeat.

The Rift wasn't just leaking. It was breathing.

And something inside the walls was listening.

"Martinez," Dale said. "Do you hear that?"

"Hear what?"

"That's not the HVAC," Dale said.

"That's the broodmother. She's not in the crawlspace. She's in the foundation.

She's been using the steam pipes as a nursery.

"

Martinez's face went white.

"Can we kill her?"

"Not with protocols," Dale said. "Not while we're suspended. But we can document her."

He pulled out his pen. He looked at the blank paper on the floor. He looked at the wet carpet.

He couldn't write on the paper. But he could write on the floor.

Dale grabbed a piece of chalk from the maintenance closet. It was white. Dusty.

He drew a circle on the wet floor. He drew a line from the circle to the nearest vent. He drew a small X on the spot where the fluid was pooling.

"Field Guide Entry: Rattus profundus, Variant B. Habitat: Subterranean Thermal Vents. Weakness: Rapid thermal shock."

The chalk didn't disappear. The floor wasn't Authority-certified. It was just concrete.

**NEW FIELD GUIDE ENTRY: UNVERIFIED.** **IDENTIFICATION CONFIRMED VIA OBSERVATION.** **TARGETED DAMAGE BONUS: PENDING VERIFICATION.**

Dale stood up. He looked at the vent. It was rattling. The metal was vibrating.

"Martinez, get the fire extinguisher."

"The CO2 one?"

"The wet chemical one," Dale said. "The library has a kitchen. They have to have a kitchen."

"Where?"

"Basement. Loading dock. Behind the elevator."

Martinez ran.

Dale watched him go. Then he looked at the window.

The men in suits were still there, and they were watching him. Waiting for him to make a mistake.

Dale picked up the chalk. He drew another circle around himself.

He wasn't fighting. He wasn't fleeing. He was preparing a workspace.

The inspector's voice echoed in his mind. *The System retains a backup copy.*

Dale looked at the chalk circle. It wasn't a backup. It was a map.

If he could verify the weakness, he could get his protocols back. He could get his license back. He could end the audit.

But to verify it, he had to go into the walls.

He had to go into the nest.

And the nest was hungry.

Dale heard a click behind him. He turned.

The inspector was standing in the doorway. He wasn't leaving. He was watching.

"You are entering the hazard zone," the inspector said. "You are violating the suspension order."

Dale looked at the vent. The metal groaned. A black sludge oozed from the grate. It smelled like copper and old blood.

"I am assessing the hazard," Dale said. "Protocol 114. Safety first."

The inspector stepped into the room. He didn't step on the wet carpet. He stepped on the dry tile near the door.

"Then you have sixty seconds," the inspector said. "Before I classify you as a bio-hazard yourself and authorize immediate removal."

Dale looked at the sludge. It was moving. It was reaching for him.

He didn't move. He waited.

He watched the sludge form a shape. A claw. A leg. A head.

It was small. It was a larva. Not the mother. The scout.

Dale pulled out his flashlight. He didn't turn it on. He just held it.

He looked at the larva. He looked at the inspector. He looked at Martinez, who was running back down the stairs with a red fire extinguisher.

Dale made a decision, and he threw the flashlight at the larva.

It wasn't a weapon. It was a distraction.

The larva lunged. It hit the flashlight.

It shattered the plastic casing.

The bulb broke.

The battery leaked.

The larva shrieked. A sound like tearing metal.

**TARGET IDENTIFIED: RATTUS PROFUNDUS LARVAL STAGE.** **WEAKNESS: THERMAL SHOCK.** **VERIFICATION: SUCCESSFUL.**

The red banner vanished. The amber banner flashed green.

**CERTIFICATION STATUS: RESTORED.** **PROTOCOLS: ONLINE.** **FIELD GUIDE: UNLOCKED.**

Dale felt the hum return. It was sweet. It was dangerous.

He looked at the inspector.

"Protocol 4: Thermal Shock Application," Dale said.

He grabbed the fire extinguisher from Martinez. He pulled the pin.

The inspector's eyes widened.

"Do not."

Dale sprayed the larva.

The white cloud engulfed the creature. It froze. It shattered.

Dale didn't stop. He turned. He aimed the nozzle at the vent.

"The nest is in the walls," Dale said. "And the walls are on fire."

He pulled the trigger again.

The inspector lunged for him.

Dale didn't dodge. He stepped into the inspector's reach. He jammed the nozzle into the inspector's tie.

"Violation code S-9," Dale said. "You're in the line of fire."

The inspector stumbled back. The wet chemical coated his suit. It sizzled.

Dale turned back to the vent. The sludge was retreating. The heat was driving it back into the depths.

But the shaking was getting worse. The building was groaning.

Something big was coming up.

Dale looked at the clipboard on the floor. The inspector had dropped it.

He picked it up. He flipped to the blank page.

He took the chalk. He wrote one word.

**NEST.**

He looked at the inspector.

"Your audit is over. My extermination is starting."

The floor cracked.

Not a small crack. A fissure.

It split the library floor in half.

From the fissure, something emerged. It wasn't a rat. It wasn't a beetle.

It was a hand.

Humanoid. Pale. With too many joints.

It grabbed the edge of the hole.

It pulled.

The entire library tilted.

Dale looked at Martinez.

"Don't let go of the napkin."

Martinez looked at the hand. He looked at Dale.

"What is that?"

Dale looked at the hand. He looked at the System notification.

**NEW ENTRY DETECTED.** **NAME: UNKNOWN.** **CLASS: IX.**

Dale's blood ran cold.

Class IX, and he had never seen a Class IX. He didn't even have a protocol for Class IX.

The hand pulled harder. The ceiling cracked.

Dale looked at the fire extinguisher. It was empty.

He looked at the chalk, and he looked at the hand.

He had no weapons. He had no protocols that worked.

He only had a clipboard.

He wrote one more word.

**HELP.**
