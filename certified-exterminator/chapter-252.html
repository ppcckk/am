Chapter 252: The Report Is Written

# Chapter 252

The screaming wasn't pain. It was mimicry.

Dale wiped black ichor from his eyes, the taste copper-sharp on his tongue. He didn't look at the faces.

Looking at them triggered the contamination anxiety, and he couldn't afford a panic attack while holding a clipboard against a swarm of child-faced horrors.

He looked at their limbs. Too many joints. Chitinous segments where skin should be.

The movement pattern was erratic, chaotic, but the forward momentum was unified.

They poured from the manhole like a clog releasing under pressure.

"Isolate," Dale muttered. He tapped his pen against the clipboard. The Authority interface flickered in his vision, a translucent overlay of red warnings.

**ALERT: UNIDENTIFIED BIOMASS DETECTED.** **FIELD GUIDE INCOMPLETE.** **CROSS-CONTAMINATION RISK: HIGH.**

He had no entry for *Mimicry Swarm*. He had entries for rats.

He had entries for acid beetles, and he had a vague note about *Shadow Stalkers* from the previous sector. None of those fit. If he used a standard pesticide protocol on them, the Cross-Contamination penalty would strip 40% of his efficacy.

With thousands of these things climbing out of the sewer, a 40% debuff was a death sentence.

Dale needed a workaround.

The first wave hit the pavement.

They skittered over the asphalt, their human faces frozen in silent screams, mouths sewn shut with silver thread.

Dale stepped back, kicking a concrete chunk into the nearest creature's leg. It shattered the chitin.

The creature didn't scream. It just tilted its head, a jerky, bird-like motion, and lunged.

Dale sidestepped. His boots slipped on the wet gore.

"Code K-7, structural entry," he shouted, though no one could hear him over the wet slap of limbs. "Unsecured perimeter. Immediate containment required."

He reached for his belt. Not the spray can.

The spray can was useless here, and he grabbed the heavy-duty containment foam canister. Class II protocol. Used for sealing large breaches in residential walls.

Designed for gaps under foundations.

The canister hissed as he armed it.

The swarm surged. They didn't attack him. They attacked the Enforcer.

The Enforcer, a towering construct of bureaucratic authority, swatted two creatures aside with a backhand. The creatures splattered, but more took their place.

The Enforcer's efficiency rating was 10/10, but it was slow, which was a heavy hitter, a sledgehammer in a library. It needed precision.

Dale gave it precision.

He sprayed a wide arc of expanding foam directly into the mouth of the manhole. The white substance hit the pavement, bubbled, and hardened into a foam plug. It wasn't enough to stop them, but it slowed the exit velocity. The creatures piled up, tripping over the hardened barrier.

"Work with me!" Dale yelled at the Enforcer.

The Enforcer paused. Its glowing eyes fixed on Dale.

"Operator Kowalski.

Protocol deviation.

Containment foam is not an elimination tool.

"

"It is when used as a choke point," Dale said. He switched the nozzle to pinpoint. A stream of concentrated adhesive shot forward, hitting the legs of the leading cluster.

The legs stuck to the foam plug. The creatures behind them crashed into the trapped ones, creating a tangled knot of chitin and flesh.

Dale didn't stop, and he moved laterally, along the curb. He was mapping the flow. Where were they coming from?

The manhole was one point.

But the ground around it was vibrating. Cracks were spreading across the asphalt, hairline fractures webbing out toward the nearby park.

Secondary entry points.

The System popped a notification.

**NEW VIOLATION TYPE: FRACTURE NETWORK.** **IDENTIFY WEAKNESS FOR DAMAGE BONUS.**

Dale ignored the prompt, and he couldn't identify the weakness of the swarm itself. They were too diverse, too chaotic. But the *source*?

The source was the Rift. And the Rift was leaking.

He looked at the cracks in the pavement. They weren't random. They followed the water lines.

"Sewer main," Dale said.

He kicked open his service van door. He didn't have a weapon. He had a shovel, a wrench, and a case of industrial-grade drain cleaner.

He grabbed the wrench.

A creature lunged at his back.

Dale spun, the wrench cracking against its skull.

The head didn't pop. It dented.

The silver-threaded mouth twitched.

"Stop looking at them," Dale whispered. He focused on the thorax. The gap between the legs. The soft underbelly protected by the hard shell.

He stabbed the wrench into the gap. The creature shrieked—a high, piercing sound that sounded like a child crying for its mother. Dale gagged but pulled the wrench free.

**TARGETED DAMAGE BONUS: +15% (SPECIES UNKNOWN).**

It wasn't much, but it worked, and the swarm was getting faster. The foam plug was cracking. The Enforcer was being swarmed, its limbs slowing under the weight of a dozen climbing bodies. It raised a shield, but the shield was designed for blunt force, not swarms.

Dale ran. Not away. Toward the park.

The cracks in the sidewalk were widening. He could see the darkness beneath.

And in the darkness, movement.

Not just legs. Faces. Hundreds of them.

Pressed against the dirt, looking up.

Dale skidded to a halt at the edge of the park. The grass was dead. The soil was black and wet.

He pulled out his Field Guide. It was a battered tablet, its screen cracked. He scrolled frantically. *Rattus profundus*. *Acid Beetle*. *Broodmother*.

Nothing for *Child-Face Mimics*.

But there was a footnote in the *Broodmother* entry. *Territorial nesting. Sensitive to high-frequency vibration.*

Vibration, and the Enforcer was heavy. It created ground tremors. That's why the swarm was swarming it. They were drawn to the vibration.

Dale looked at his drain cleaner. It was caustic. It burned. But it also created gas when it hit the organic sludge in the sewers.

He ran back toward the manhole, and the Enforcer was on one knee. The swarm was covering it.

"Operator," the Enforcer's voice boomed, distorted. "Thoroughness Rating dropping. 9.1. 9.0. 8.9. Stop the hesitation."

Dale didn't hesitate. He uncapped the drain cleaner. He didn't throw it at the monsters. He poured it directly into the open manhole, onto the hardened foam plug that was now cracking.

The foam dissolved instantly.

The drain cleaner hit the sewer water below, and the reaction was immediate. A thick, yellow cloud of toxic gas erupted from the manhole.

It wasn't an explosion.

It was a pressure wave. The gas forced its way out, blasting the remaining creatures back into the hole.

The child-faces recoiled. They didn't have lungs, but they had sensory pits. They hated the chemical burn. They screamed, not in mimicry, but in genuine, agonized distress.

Dale held his breath. He put on his respirator.

The gas cloud hung in the air, heavy and yellow. It drifted toward the park, toward the cracks in the sidewalk.

The swarm paused. They stopped skittering. They turned away from the manhole, driven by the chemical barrier.

Dale grabbed a heavy tarp from his van. He dragged it to the manhole, stepping around the dissolving foam. He threw the tarp over the hole. Then he slammed the manhole cover back into place.

He grabbed a heavy chain and padlock from his belt. He wrapped the chain around the cover.

The vibration from below stopped.

The swarm in the street froze. They stood there, twitching. Without the source, without the vibration, they were confused.

The Enforcer rose. It shook off the last few creatures, which fell dead or dying. Its armor was dented. Its efficiency rating was flashing in red.

**ENFORCER INTEGRITY: 40%.** **CERTIFICATION LEVEL V STATUS: PROVISIONAL.**

Dale stood up, wiping his hands on his pants. The black ichor was everywhere. He felt sick. He wanted to scrub his skin until it was raw.

"Job complete?" the Enforcer asked. Its voice was smaller now. Less authoritative.

Dale looked at the tarp. It was bulging. The things underneath were still trying to get out.

"No," Dale said. "Not complete. Contained. Unresolved."

The Enforcer tilted its head.

"Thoroughness Rating: 7.5.

Sloppy work.

You created a secondary hazard, and the gas is spreading. Residential area within two hundred meters.

"

Dale looked at the park. The gas was drifting toward the houses. He could see people in the windows.

They were watching. They were filming.

"I need a ventilation fan," Dale said.

"You are licensed for extermination," the Enforcer said. "Not civil engineering."

"I need a fan, or I need to go back in there and kill them all. Do you want 7.5 or 10?"

The Enforcer paused.

The bureaucracy of the Authority weighed the options. A 7.5 rating meant Dale stayed Level V. A 10 meant he progressed.

But the gas was a liability.

A new notification appeared.

**SYSTEM LOOPLOPHOLE DETECTED: ENVIRONMENTAL HAZARD CLASSIFICATION.** **OPTION A: NEUTRALIZE HAZARD (TIME: 10 MINUTES). REWARD: +200 TP.** **OPTION B: IGNORE HAZARD. REWARD: 0 TP. PENALTY: CERTIFICATION REVOCATION.**

Dale stared at the text. Revocation.

They could take his license, and they could take his job. Without his license, he was just a guy with a clipboard and anxiety.

He pulled out his phone. He dialed the municipal water department.

"Dispatch," Dale said. "This is Kowalski, Certified Exterminator, Level V.

Code K-9.

Chemical spill in Sector 4.

I need a positive-pressure ventilation unit. Now.

"

"Kowalski?" The dispatcher's voice was confused. "You're not authorized for HAZMAT."

"I am authorized for containment," Dale said. "And I'm invoking the Inter-Agency Cooperation Clause. Section 4, Paragraph B. Pest control emergencies supersede municipal jurisdiction if the infestation threatens public health."

Silence.

"Wait," the dispatcher said.

Dale watched the gas drift. The houses were getting closer. A dog was barking in one of them.

"Unit en route," the dispatcher said. "ETA five minutes."

Dale hung up. He turned to the Enforcer.

"Keep them off me," Dale said. "I have to seal the secondary cracks."

The Enforcer nodded. It raised its weapon.

Dale ran to the sidewalk cracks. He pulled out his sealant gun. He didn't spray.

He pumped. He injected expanding polyurethane into the fissures in the pavement.

He moved fast, his hands shaking, but his aim steady, and he sealed one. Two. Three.

The vibration from the park intensified. The ground shook. The tarp over the manhole lifted an inch.

Dale didn't stop. He sealed the fourth crack.

The park was now a sealed box. The gas was trapped. The vibration was muffled.

The swarm in the street dispersed. They lost interest. They crawled back toward the manhole, trying to find the source.

Dale stood over the last crack, breathing hard. His heart hammered against his ribs. He checked his watch. Four minutes and thirty seconds.

The ventilation unit arrived. It was a municipal truck, painted yellow. A worker in a white suit jumped out.

"Who's in charge?" the worker yelled.

Dale stepped forward.

"I am. Operator Kowalski."

The worker looked at Dale's suit. It was torn. It was covered in black goo. It looked like a costume.

"Authority ID," the worker said.

Dale held up his tablet. The Authority interface projected a hologram of his badge. **CERTIFIED EXTERMINATOR. LEVEL V. STATUS: ACTIVE.**

The worker's eyes widened.

"Level V? I thought those were just in the storybooks."

"Ventilate," Dale said. "Positive pressure. Seal the perimeter. Do not let the gas hit the houses."

The worker nodded and started setting up the fans.

Dale walked back to the manhole. The tarp was still bulging. The Enforcer stood guard, its weapon humming.

Dale looked at the tarp. He could hear scratching. Not from the legs.

From the faces, and they were biting at the fabric.

He pulled out his clipboard. He wrote.

*Violation Code: K-12. Unsealed Subterranean Breach.* *Action: Containment via chemical barrier and physical seal.* *Status: Pending Verification.*

He signed the report.

The Enforcer looked at him.

"You did not eliminate them."

"No," Dale said. "I isolated them. Verification comes next."

The Enforcer's eyes narrowed.

"The Authority expects elimination. Isolation is a holding action. It is not a solution."

"It is a protocol step," Dale said.

"Step three. Isolate. Step four is Eliminate.

Step five is Verify.

"

"You are skipping steps," the Enforcer said.

"I am optimizing," Dale said.

The ground shook again. Harder this time. The tarp tore.

A face emerged. A real face. Not a mimic. A human face.

It was a child.

A real child.

No chitin. No silver thread.

Just a boy, no older than ten, eyes wide with terror.

Dale froze.

The System notification flashed red.

**ERROR: NON-THREATENING BIOMASS DETECTED.** **DO NOT ENGAGE.** **CROSS-CONTAMINATION WARNING: PROTOCOL VIOLATION IMMINENT.**

Dale stared at the boy. The boy reached out a hand.

"Help," the boy whispered.

Dale's hand hovered over his spray can. His protocol said *eliminate*. His job said *protect the resident*.

The Enforcer raised its weapon.

"Hostile detection failed. Threat level recalculating."

Dale lowered his spray can.

"No," Dale said.

The Enforcer aimed at the boy.

"Stand down," Dale said.

"I am an Enforcer," the construct said. "I eliminate infestations."

"That is not an infestation," Dale said. "That is a victim."

The boy screamed. Not in mimicry. In pure, unadulterated terror.

And from the darkness of the manhole, something else emerged. Not a child.

Not a monster.

A hand. Large. Pale.

With too many fingers.

It grabbed the boy's ankle.

Dale dropped his clipboard. He lunged.

He didn't think. He didn't check the protocol. He didn't check his Field Guide.

He grabbed the boy's hand and pulled.

The hand from the hole pulled back. The boy dangled between the world of the living and the dark below.

Dale looked up at the Enforcer.

"Help me," Dale said.

The Enforcer hesitated.

"If you shoot," Dale said, "you contaminate the witness. You destroy the evidence. You fail the Verify step."

The Enforcer lowered its weapon.

Dale pulled harder. His muscles burned. The thing in the hole was strong.

The boy's face turned blue.

And then the thing in the hole spoke.

It didn't scream. It didn't mimic.

It spoke in a voice that sounded like Dale's own.

"Let him go, Dale," the voice said. "The report is already written."
