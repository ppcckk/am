Chapter 165: Licensed Asset

# Chapter 165

The ink was still wet.

Dale stared at the signature. It was his own.

Neat.

Legible. The same looping script he used for the quarterly pest control logs at Kowalski & Sons, LLC.

The paper dissolved into the air, burning away with a smell like ozone and burnt hair.

**[CONTRACT ACCEPTED.]**

**[SUBJECT: DALE KOWALSKI.]**

**[STATUS: LICENSED ASSET (CLASS IX PROVISIONAL).]**

**[BINDING PROTOCOL: ACTIVE.]**

The green light didn't stop. It surged. The floor tiles, previously intact, now pulsed with a sickly, bioluminescent vein-pattern. The Auditor's hand retracted into the darkness, taking the license with it.

Marla yanked his arm. "Dale. Move."

He didn't move.

He looked at his hands. The skin was normal.

No claws.

No chitin. Just pale, trembling flesh. But he could feel something inside his chest.

A hum. Low frequency.

It vibrated against his sternum, syncing with the pulsing light on the floor.

"Did you sign it?" Marla shouted over the rising hum.

"Yes," Dale said. His voice sounded flat. Controlled.

"You sold your soul."

"I filed a petition for emergency structural stabilization," Dale corrected.

He pulled out his clipboard. The paper was blank.

He wrote: *Entry 165.01. Unauthorized dimensional breach. Containment via contract execution.

Cost: Personal autonomy.*

The light reached his knees.

**[REALITY STABILIZATION INITIATED.]**

**[COST: 500 TP.]**

**[CURRENT TP BALANCE: 120.]**

**[SHORTFALL: 380 TP.]**

**[ACTION REQUIRED: EARN IMMEDIATELY OR FACE TERMINATION.]**

The text floated in his peripheral vision, red and urgent.

Marla grabbed his collar. "We have to go. The floor is gone."

"The floor is not gone," Dale said.

He pointed to the green veins. "It's undergoing rapid molecular reconstruction. If I leave now, the stabilization fails.

The breach widens.

The sector collapses."

"We die either way!"

"Not if I pay."

Dale looked around the room.

The Auditor's chamber was a void of shifting geometry. Shelves lined with jars of preserved eyes. A desk made of fused bone.

No monsters. No rats.

No beetles.

How do you earn TP without a pest to exterminate?

The System demanded a kill. The System demanded documentation. The System demanded a verified treatment protocol.

There was nothing to treat.

Unless the Auditor was still here.

Dale closed his eyes.

He listened to the hum. It wasn't just in his chest.

It was in the walls. In the floor. In Marla's breathing.

He opened his Field Guide. The interface shimmered, responsive to his new status.

*Search: Auditor.*

No entry.

*Search: Reality Stabilization.*

Entry found.

**[PROTOCOL: LOCALIZED REALITY STABILIZATION (LEVEL I)]**

**[REQUIREMENTS: 1. IDENTIFY SOURCE OF INSTABILITY.]**

**[2. DOCUMENT INSTABILITY MARKERS.]**

**[3. ISOLATE SOURCE.]**

**[4. ELIMINATE SOURCE (OR BIND SOURCE).]**

**[5. VERIFY STABILITY.]**

The contract satisfied steps one through four. The "binding" was the elimination. But the system hadn't awarded the points. Why?

*Because the source is not dead,* Dale thought. *The source is active.*

The Auditor hadn't left. It had moved.

Dale looked at Marla. She was pale. Sweat beaded on her forehead. Her eyes were wide, fixed on the ceiling.

"Marla," Dale said. "Your left arm."

She looked down. Her left arm was turning translucent. Green light bled through the veins in her skin.

"It's spreading," she whispered. "To me."

Dale grabbed her wrist. His grip was firm.

Professional.

"Do not panic. This is a contact exposure. Secondary infestation."

"It's not a bug!" Marla screamed. "It's magic! It's killing me!"

"It is an invasive entity utilizing a biological vector," Dale said.

He pulled out his pen. He didn't write on the clipboard.

He wrote on Marla's sleeve. *Vector: Human.

Status: Compromised. Action: Contain.*

The System flashed.

**[FIELD GUIDE UPDATE: NEW ENTRY DETECTED.]**

**[SUBJECT: INFECTED HOST (Homo sapiens infectus).]**

**[WEAKNESS IDENTIFIED: ANCHORING INSTABILITY AT POINT OF CONTACT.]**

**[TARGETED DAMAGE BONUS APPLIED: +15% EFFICIENCY ON CONTAINMENT PROTOCOLS.]**

Dale stared at the text.

He hadn't killed the Auditor. He had identified a new vector. The infection wasn't just a light; it was a parasite. And Marla was a carrier.

If he treated Marla, he was treating the infection.

But you couldn't exterminate a host. You had to isolate the pathogen.

"Sit down," Dale commanded.

"Dale—"

"Sit. Down. This is a protocol requirement."

Marla sank to the floor. The green light crawled up her neck.

Dale dropped to his knees beside her. He didn't have a syringe. He didn't have a scalpel. He had his pen.

He uncapped it.

"I need to create a barrier," Dale muttered. "The infection travels through proximity. You are the conduit. I need to break the circuit."

"How?"

"Contamination protocol," Dale said. He looked at his hands.

The hum in his chest grew louder.

He could feel the Auditor's presence now.

It wasn't in the room. It was in the *rules*.

The Auditor *was* the rules.

To stabilize the reality, Dale had to rewrite the local rules. He had the license. He had the clearance.

But he needed points.

He needed to perform a Treatment on Marla.

**[WARNING: TREATMENT PROTOCOL REQUIRES LETHAL FORCE OR FULL CURE.]**

**[CURRENT TP: 120.]**

**[CURE COST: 1000 TP.]**

**[ELIMINATION COST: 500 TP.]**

Dale did the math. He couldn't cure her. He couldn't afford to kill her. He had to find a loophole.

*Cross-contamination penalty,* the rules said. *Deploying a protocol designed for one creature type against another inflicts a 40% debuff.*

Dale looked at Marla. She was human.

He was a pest control technician.

He had protocols for vermin. For rats. For roaches.

Did he have a protocol for humans?

No.

But he had a protocol for *infestations*.

He opened his Field Guide. He searched for *Rattus profundus*. The rat he had killed in Chapter 1.

*Weakness: Territorial aggression. Susceptible to isolation.*

He searched for *Acid Beetle*.

*Weakness: Carapace vulnerability. Susceptible to adhesive binding.*

He searched for *Broodmother*.

*Weakness: Hive connection. Susceptible to severance.*

He closed the guide. He looked at Marla.

"Marla, hold out your hand."

She hesitated. Then she extended it. Her skin was glowing.

Dale took his pen.

He drew a circle on her palm. Not a magic circle.

A *containment boundary*.

A standard OSHA hazard marking.

**[ACTION: MARK CONTAINMENT ZONE.]**

**[SYSTEM RESPONSE: INVALID.]**

Dale gritted his teeth. He tried again. He drew the circle. He added a label: *Hazard: Bio-Contamination.*

The System flickered.

**[FIELD GUIDE ENTRY UPDATED: BIO-CONTAMINATION (UNVERIFIED).]**

**[IDENTIFICATION CONFIRMED.]**

**[TREATMENT PROTOCOL UNLOCKED: ISOLATION BARRIER (CLASS II).]**

**[COST: 0 TP (PROVISIONAL).]**

**[NOTE: PROVISIONAL COSTS ACCRUE AS DEBT.]**

Dale's heart stopped.

*Debt.*

He could perform the treatment now. He could pay later.

But the cost of an unverified protocol was high. And the Auditor was watching.

"Do it," Marla whispered. "I can feel my fingers going numb."

Dale pressed his thumb against the center of the circle he had drawn. He concentrated.

He remembered the first time he had sealed a crack in a duplex wall.

The duct tape.

The tension. The refusal to let anything escape.

*Isolate.*

The green light on Marla's arm stopped spreading. It hit the ink line and halted. The ink darkened. It looked like dried blood, then like black tar.

**[ISOLATION BARRIER ESTABLISHED.]**

**[TP COST: 50.]**

**[CURRENT TP BALANCE: 70.]**

**[DEBT ACCRUED: 50.]**

It worked. The light in the room dimmed slightly. The hum in Dale's chest subsided.

But the stabilization wasn't complete. The floor was still cracking. The breach was still open.

He had stabilized the *vector*. He hadn't stabilized the *source*.

And the source was the Auditor.

The Auditor was the system.

Dale looked at his balance. 70 TP.

He needed 450 more to finish the job.

He looked at Marla. She was safe, for now. The infection was contained within the boundary. She was a walking quarantine zone.

He stood up. His legs were steady.

"Stay there," Dale said. "Do not touch the walls. Do not speak. You are now a sealed unit."

Marla stared at him. "Dale, what are you doing?"

Dale walked toward the center of the room. Toward the void where the Auditor had emerged.

"I'm filing a complaint," Dale said.

He opened his Field Guide again. He navigated to the *Audit Log*.

*Entry: Auditor Presence.*

*Status: Unlicensed Entity.*

*Violation: Operating without a valid Pest Control License.*

The System flashed.

**[SYSTEM ERROR: AUDITOR IS THE AUTHORITY.]**

Dale ignored the error. He typed a query.

*Is the Authority subject to OSHA regulations?*

The System paused.

**[QUERY INVALID.]**

Dale typed again.

*Are dimensional breaches considered code violations?*

**[YES.]**

*Are code violations subject to fines?*

**[YES.]**

*Are fines deducted from TP?*

**[YES.]**

Dale smiled. It was a thin, dangerous smile.

He looked at the void.

**[ACTION: ISSUE CITATION.]**

**[TARGET: UNLICENSED DIMENSIONAL ENTITY.]**

**[VIOLATION: 1. FAILURE TO POST LICENSE.]**

**[2. FAILURE TO MAINTAIN SAFE WORK ENVIRONMENT.]**

**[3. EXPOSURE TO BIOHAZARD.]**

**[TOTAL FINE: 500 TP.]**

**[PENALTY APPLIED TO TARGET.]**

The void shuddered.

A sound ripped through the air. Not a scream. A tear. Like paper ripping.

**[TARGET: UNLICENSED ENTITY.]**

**[STATUS: CITATION ISSUED.]**

**[TP TRANSFERRED: 500.]**

**[CURRENT TP BALANCE: 570.]**

The green light on the floor snapped off. The cracks sealed. The hum in Dale's chest vanished.

Silence.

Marla stood up. She looked at her arm. The black ink was still there. The green light was gone.

"Dale?"

Dale turned to her. He held his clipboard. His hands were shaking again.

He looked at his balance.

**[CURRENT TP BALANCE: 570.]**

**[STATUS: DEBT CLEARED.]**

**[NEW STATUS: BOUNTY HUNTER.]**

Dale frowned. He tapped the screen.

*Why Bounty Hunter?*

The System flashed a new notification.

**[TARGET: UNLICENSED ENTITY.]**

**[STATUS: CITATION DISPUTED.]**

**[APPEAL FILED.]**

**[THE AUTHORITY ACKNOWLEDGES YOUR ACTION.]**

**[THE AUDITOR ACKNOWLEDGES YOUR ACTION.]**

**[YOU HAVE FINED THE AUDITOR.]**

**[THIS IS A CLASS IX VIOLATION OF THE LICENSING HIERARCHY.]**

**[YOU ARE NOW THE MOST DANGEROUS PEST CONTROL TECHNICIAN IN THE DIMENSION.]**

Marla walked over to him. She grabbed his shoulder. "Did you just... fine the Auditor?"

"I issued a citation," Dale said. "Standard procedure."

"You put a fine on God?"

"I put a fine on an unlicensed entity operating in a residential zone. The penalty is 500 TP. The Authority pays the fine. The Auditor pays the fine."

"Who paid?"

"The Auditor," Dale said. "But the Authority absorbed the cost. Which means the Authority is now indebted to the Auditor."

Marla stared at him. "That's... that's not how bureaucracy works."

"That's exactly how it works," Dale said. He tucked his clipboard under his arm. He picked up his flashlight. It was still working.

"We need to leave. The citation will trigger an audit. A real audit."

An audit meant inspectors. Inspectors meant questions. Questions meant exposure.

Dale looked at the exit. The door was normal. Wood. Painted white.

He opened it.

Outside, the suburban street was quiet. The Rift was gone. The green light was gone.

But standing on the sidewalk was a woman in a gray suit. She held a briefcase. She had no face. Just a smooth, featureless oval of skin where features should be.

She turned her head toward Dale.

**[NEW THREAT DETECTED.]**

**[SUBJECT: INSPECTOR GENERAL.]**

**[CLASS: X.]**

**[ACTION: IMMEDIATE TERMINATION OF LICENSE.]**

Dale didn't run. He didn't scream.

He pulled out his pen.

He wrote on the back of his clipboard.

*Appeal filed. Reason: Procedural error. Citation issued without prior notice.*

The faceless woman tilted her head.

**[NOTICE REQUIRED: 30 DAYS.]**

**[YOU HAVE ISSUED A CITATION. YOU HAVE 30 DAYS TO PRESENT EVIDENCE.]**

**[IF EVIDENCE IS INSUFFICIENT: LICENSE REVOKED. EXISTENCE REVOKED.]**

Dale looked at the woman. He looked at Marla. He looked at the suburban houses.

30 days.

He had 30 days to prove that the Auditor had broken the rules.

He had 30 days to find the Auditor.

He had 30 days to find a way to beat a being that wrote the laws.

Dale clicked his pen.

"Come on, Marla," Dale said. "We have a deadline."

He stepped onto the sidewalk. The pavement was cold. The sky was blue.

For the first time in his career, Dale Kowalski was not afraid of the bug.

He was afraid of the inspector.
