Chapter 394: Auditor's Initial Intervention

# Chapter 394

**FORM 44-B: AUDITOR'S INITIAL INTERVENTION LOG** **DATE:** October 14, 2024 **LOCATION:** Sector 7, Residential Zone (Suburbia) **SUBJECT:** Infestation Cluster –

"The Whispering Walls" **CLASSIFICATION:** Class VI (Structural/Psychological) **AUDITOR:** Dale Kowalski (Level VI)

**ENTRY 1: ARRIVAL AND PERIMETER ASSESSMENT**

The rain did not fall on the house at 4208 Maple Drive. It evaporated three inches above the roof shingles, turning into a fine, gray mist that smelled of ozone and rotten peaches.

Dale stood in the driveway, his boots sinking into mud that felt too soft, too warm.

His left eye burned. The amber iris swirled, processing data streams that scrolled faster than a human brain could comfortably decode.

1,402,931 entries.

The number pulsed in his peripheral vision, a constant, low-frequency hum. It was not a count of monsters. It was a count of violations.

Vance stood beside him, holding a clipboard so tightly his knuckles had turned white. He was shaking. Not from fear, but from the cold that radiated off Dale's new aura. The air around the Auditor felt pressurized, heavy with static charge.

"Dale," Vance whispered. "The neighbors. They're watching."

Dale did not look at the neighbors. He looked at the front door.

Through the amber lens, the wood was not wood, which was a lattice of fungal fibers. The grain was a frass trail.

The door was not locked; it was sealed.

A biological seal. A chitinous web stretched across the keyhole, vibrating with a frequency that made Dale's teeth ache.

**NOTICE:** *Structural Integrity Compromised. Biological Contamination Detected. Protocol Required: Isolation.*

"Get the truck," Dale said.

"There is no truck," Vance reminded him, his voice cracking. "You said we have a license."

"Open the hatch."

Vance blinked. He walked to the side of the house, where the rusted husk of Dale's old service van sat abandoned.

The doors were fused shut with resin.

Dale raised his hand.

His fingers elongated, the joints popping, the skin stretching into translucent, chitinous plates. He touched the resin.

It hissed.

The metal peeled back like dead skin. The interior of the van was not empty. It was filled with tools.

Not the wrenches and sprayers of his past life. These were instruments of the Authority.

Glass vials filled with void-light.

Scythes made of compressed silence. A roll of duct tape that glowed with a faint, purple hue.

**ITEM ACQUIRED: VOID-ADHESIVE (CLASS IV).** **ITEM ACQUIRED: RESONANCE HAMMER.** **ITEM ACQUIRED: FIELD GUIDE (UPDATED).**

Dale selected the hammer. It weighed nothing. When he swung it at the air, it left a ripple in the light.

"Stay back," Dale told Vance.

"I'm not going in there alone," Vance said. He stepped forward. "I'm your assistant. It's in the contract."

"Contract expired," Dale said.

He did not mean it. But the Authority's rules were strict.

Level VI Auditors operated solo.

The risk to support staff was categorized as 'Unacceptable Liability.'

He kicked the front door.

The wood shattered.

Inside, the hallway was alive. The wallpaper peeled in strips that writhed like worms. The carpet was a thick, green moss that sucked at Dale's boots. The air was thick with spores.

**FIELD GUIDE UPDATE:** **SPECIES:** *Mycelium Sentientis* (Sentient Mold Colony) **WEAKNESS:** Sonic Resonance at 440Hz (A4 Note). **VULNERABILITY:** Light sensitivity (UV). **THREAT LEVEL:** High. **NOTE:** This species feeds on guilt.

Dale stopped. He tilted his head. The amber eye narrowed.

"Guilt," he muttered.

He pulled a small device from his belt. A tuning fork. He struck it against his knee.

The sound was pure, clean, and terrifying. The moss on the floor recoiled. The worms in the walls froze. The spores in the air settled.

For a moment, the house was silent.

Then, a voice spoke from the darkness of the living room.

"Mr. Kowalski."

It was a woman's voice. Soft. Polite. Like a receptionist on hold.

Dale walked forward. The floorboards creaked under his weight. Each step left a black footprint that sizzled and evaporated.

In the living room, a family sat on the couch. Three children.

A man.

A woman, and they were still. Too still.

Their eyes were open, but the pupils were dilated, black pits that reflected the amber glow of Dale's eye.

The woman on the couch smiled. Her teeth were too many. They were arranged in three rows.

"We have been waiting for the Auditor," she said. "The last one was... insufficient. He tried to burn us. He did not understand the code."

Dale pulled out his clipboard. The paper was blank. The pen was black ink that flowed like blood.

**FORM 12-A: INTERVIEW/IDENTIFICATION** **SUBJECT:** Primary Host (Female) **NAME:** Unknown. **STATUS:** Possessed. **CONSOLIDATION:** Partial.

"Name," Dale said.

"The infestation has no name," the woman said. "We are the house. The house is us."

"Wrong," Dale said. He wrote on the paper.

The words appeared in golden letters that floated off the page.

"Incorrect identification. Species is *Mycelium Sentientis*. Host is Sarah Miller.

Age 34.

Occupation: Nurse. Cause of Death: Cardiac arrest due to spore inhalation.

"

The woman's face twitched. The skin around her eyes cracked. Green sap leaked from the fissures.

"You are rude," she said. "And you are bleeding."

Dale looked down.

His right hand, the one holding the pen, was shaking. Black ichor dripped from his fingernails onto the clipboard. The Authority's system was reacting to his use of the Resonance Hammer.

It was a Class IV tool. He was Level VI.

The compatibility was low.

**WARNING:** *Cross-Contamination Risk. Efficiency Drop 15%. Field Guide Entry for* ***Mycelium Sentientis*** *is now unstable. Do not use Hammer on next target without recalibration.*

He had used the wrong tool, and he had relied on his old instincts, treating this like a standard mold removal. But this was not mold.

This was a sentient colony that fed on emotion. The hammer was meant for breaking physical barriers. It did not break mental barriers.

The family on the couch stood up. They moved in unison. Their joints popped. The man's arm elongated, bending at an angle that defied anatomy.

"You are not thorough," the woman said. Her voice was no longer soft. It was the sound of grinding stones. "You are sloppy."

Dale stepped back. He raised the hammer. But he did not swing it. He dropped it.

The hammer hit the floor with a heavy thud. It rolled away.

The family hesitated. Confusion rippled through the colony. Predators did not drop their weapons.

Dale closed his eyes, and he reached into his pocket and pulled out a small, glass vial. The Void-Adhesive.

He uncorked it. The smell was sharp, chemical. It smelled like a fresh hospital ward.

He walked toward the couch.

The family lunged. The man's hand, now a claw of bone and vine, swiped at Dale's throat. Dale did not dodge. He let the claw hit him.

It tore his shirt. It cut his skin.

Pain flared. Hot, white, immediate.

But Dale did not flinch. He grabbed the man's wrist. The vine-like skin burned against Dale's palm.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** *Contact Detected. Biological Integration Attempt.* **ACTION:** *Reject.*

Dale poured the Void-Adhesive onto the man's hand.

The liquid sizzled. The vines withered.

The man screamed, a sound of tearing metal.

The adhesive acted as a binding agent. It forced the biological matter to adhere to the physical laws of the room, than the magical laws of the infestation.

The man collapsed.

The woman shrieked.

"Stop! You are killing the host!"

"I am exterminating the parasite," Dale said. He pointed at the ceiling.

The amber eye focused. He saw the source.

It was not in the family, which was in the attic. A large, bulbous sac pulsed behind the drywall.

It was connected to the family by a network of thin, red threads.

Tendons. Nerves.

**NEW PROTOCOL UNLOCKED: DISSECT.** **REQUIREMENT:** Precision. **COST:** 10% of Total Vitality. **BENEFIT:** Targeted removal without host trauma.

Dale looked at Vance, who was still standing in the doorway, pale and trembling.

"Vance," Dale said. "Hold the light."

"What light?"

"Your phone. Flashlight."

Vance fumbled for his phone. He turned on the flashlight. The beam cut through the spore-filled air.

Dale raised his hand. The chitinous plates on his fingers extended, forming sharp, needle-like points.

He did not attack the family. He attacked the air.

He slashed at the invisible threads.

The Resonance Hammer lay forgotten on the floor. The Void-Adhesive had done its job. It had weakened the connection. Now, Dale could sever the link.

He sliced through the air. Invisible forces parted. The red threads snapped.

The family collapsed.

Not dead. Unconscious.

The green sap stopped leaking.

The extra teeth retracted.

The bones straightened.

The woman's eyes closed. Her breathing stabilized.

The attic sac screamed. It was a high-pitched, electronic shriek. The drywall exploded.

A large, pulsating mass of fungus fell from the ceiling. It was the size of a beach ball. It oozed black slime.

**TARGET LOCKED: THE HEART OF THE INFESTATION.** **STATUS:** Exposed. **THREAT LEVEL:** Critical.

Dale walked toward it, and he did not pick up the hammer. He did not use the spray.

He reached into his other pocket. He pulled out a small, yellow notepad. The one he had carried for fourteen years. The one with the coffee stain on the corner.

He opened it to a fresh page, and he wrote.

**FORM 12-B: FINAL VERIFICATION** **SUBJECT:** *Mycelium Sentientis* (Colony Node) **ACTION:** Documentation. **STATUS:** Pending Elimination.

The fungus lashed out. A tentacle of mold wrapped around Dale's ankle. It squeezed. His bone creaked.

He did not stop writing.

His pen moved faster. The words flowed. He described the texture of the spore.

The temperature of the slime. The exact frequency of its pulse.

The more he wrote, the more the fungus slowed.

**MECHANIC REVEALED: THE POWER OF THE PAPER TRAIL.** *The Authority rewards documentation. By formally acknowledging the existence of the anomaly, the Auditor grants it definition. Defined things can be bounded. Bounded things can be contained.*

The fungus tried to move, but it was stuck.

Not by physical force, but by conceptual weight. Dale's words anchored it to reality. It could no longer shift between dimensions.

It was forced to exist in this space, in this time, under these rules.

And the rules said: *Pest.*

Dale finished the entry, and he tore the page from the notebook.

He crumpled it.

He threw it at the fungus.

The paper hit the slime. It expanded.

It wrapped around the mass.

The white fibers of the paper absorbed the black ichor. The fungus shriveled. It turned to dust.

The house fell silent.

The rain fell properly. It hit the roof with a normal, rhythmic tap.

Dale stood in the center of the living room. His ankle throbbed. His left eye burned with a new intensity.

A notification flashed in his vision.

**JOB COMPLETE.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING:** 9.8. **POINTS AWARDED:** 5,000. **STATUS:** Certified, and he picked up the hammer. He wiped the dust from it.

Vance stepped into the room. He looked at the unconscious family. He looked at the dust where the monster had been.

"Dale," Vance said. "Did you kill them?"

"No," Dale said. "I treated them."

"They're alive?"

"Yes."

Vance slumped against the doorframe. He let out a breath he had been holding for an hour.

"Okay.

Okay.

We can go back to the station. We can file the report.

We can sleep."

Dale did not move, and he was looking at the wall.

Behind the couch, where the drywall had been shattered, there was a hole. Through the hole, Dale could see the attic.

The attic was empty.

But the hole was not ending. It was extending.

The edges of the drywall were fraying. Not rotting. Fraying. Like fabric being pulled apart by invisible hands.

And through the hole, Dale saw something else.

Another eye, which was not amber. It was red. A single, bloodshot human eye, staring back at him.

It was not in the attic. It was *inside* the wall.

Dale stepped closer.

The eye blinked.

Then, a hand reached out from the hole. It was pale. Long-fingered. It held a clipboard.

The hand held a pen, and the pen was black.

The hand wrote something on the clipboard.

Dale could not read it. But the eye stared at him. It was angry. It was jealous.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** *Rival Auditor Detected.* **ID:** *Agent X-9.* **STATUS:** *Unauthorized.* **ACTION:** *Eliminate.*

Dale's heart stopped, and he had never seen another Auditor. Not until now.

The hand reached further out. It grabbed the edge of the couch. It pulled. The couch slid aside, revealing the full extent of the hole.

The hole was not a hole, which was a door.

And on the other side of the door, standing in a dark, infinite hallway, was a man.

He wore a suit. A grey suit. Clean. Pressed.

He held a clipboard, and he smiled. It was a predatory smile.

"Hello, Dale," the man said. His voice was smooth. "You have good form. But your file is incomplete."

Dale gripped his hammer.

"Who are you?"

"I am the Inspector," the man said. "And you are a violation."

The man raised his hand. He pointed at Dale.

**NOTICE:** *CONFLICT DETECTED.* *Auditor vs. Auditor.* *Protocol: Eliminate Rival.*

Dale's amber eye flared. The number 1,402,931 changed.

It dropped.

1,402,930.

One violation less.

But it wasn't a monster, which was him.

The man in the suit stepped through the hole. He stepped into Dale's living room. He stepped into Dale's world.

"Sign here," the man said, offering the clipboard. "Or die."

Dale looked at the pen, and he looked at Vance, who was frozen, mouth agape.

He looked at the unconscious family.

He looked at the man in the suit.

Dale raised his hammer.

He did not swing, and he opened his Field Guide.

**PAGE 1:** *Dimensional Vermin.* **PAGE 44:** *Auditor (Rival).*

He wrote.

The pen hit the paper.
