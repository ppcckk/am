Chapter 81: Forged Field Entry

# Chapter 81

The tremor was already past his wrist. Past his elbow.

His flashlight swung in a slow arc across the driveway, painting the cracked sealant in stuttering white.

The fracture line had branched. Three fingers now, spreading like a root system through the polymer.

Dale's hand was useless, and he could feel the exact shape of his own fear, which was the problem. He had always known the exact shape of his own fear.

That was the whole point.

You could not manage what you could not measure, and his fear had been measured for thirty-eight years, and it measured exactly like this: a 3.2-second paralysis window triggered by contact with an organism his brain could not immediately categorize.

The thing pressed its finger through the sealant. A sliver of its fingertip emerged.

It was warm.

It looked like warm bread.

It looked like the inside of a palm.

Dale looked at it, and he looked at the shimmer. Not a glow. A refraction pattern, like heat off asphalt but wrong, like the light was bending around the object than through it.

He watched the fracture spread. Not brittle.

Not elastic.

Something in between. Something that absorbed stress and redistributed it laterally instead of propagating forward.

He looked at the clipboard in its other hand.

Empty. It had been holding his clipboard.

It held it as one holds a clipboard.

Thumb over the clip. Four fingers behind. The exact grip Dale used.

Mimic.

Not a mimic in the colloquial sense. Not a shapeshifter.

A mimic in the taxonomic sense.

An organism that had evolved to replicate the behavioral patterns of its prey in order to approach without triggering defensive responses.

He had seen it before. Entry thirty-one.

The Glass Spiders in the water treatment plant had done it. They had replicated the hand motions of the maintenance worker who had died in the filtration room, reaching for valves that were no longer there, and it had taken Dale six hours to realize the spiders were not trying to fix anything. They were trying to not be noticed.

The thing on the other side of the sealant was not attacking him. It was filing a report. It was holding his clipboard because he held a clipboard, and it had learned that holding a clipboard made Dale not run.

"Entry thirty-one," Dale said. His voice was steady.

His hand was not.

"Mimic-type behavioral replication. Subfamily: unknown. Genus: unknown.

Species: unknown.

But the pattern is known."

The System pinged. Not the Authority font. His own. The Field Guide notification that appeared when you filled in a weakness field for an existing entry.

FIELD GUIDE UPDATE: Entry 31.

Behavioral Replication (Mimicry). Weakness field modified. New note: "Replication targets the subject's most practiced motor behavior.

The subject's professional routines become the vector of approach."

The tremor stopped.

Not gradually.

Not fading.

It stopped because the vulnerability window had closed. The organism was no longer unknown.

It was a mimic. He had known mimics before. He had documented mimics before.

The 3.2 seconds applied to initial contact with an unknown species, and this species was not unknown. It was entry thirty-one, and he had looked at it, and he had seen the shimmer, and he had seen the grip on the clipboard, and he had said the word.

Dale's hand steadied, and he raised the flashlight. The thing was halfway through the sealant. Its shoulder was emerging, and it was shaped like a shoulder but not proportioned like one.

The joint was too far out.

The clavicle angle was wrong. It was a shoulder that had been drawn from the outside in, like a child's drawing of a person's arm.

Dale reached into his vest.

Not for a weapon. For the spray bottle. Class III containment foam, the one he used on broodmother sacs.

The one that expanded on contact and hardened in ninety seconds.

The thing's faceless head turned toward him.

The clipboard tilted again, which was trying to hold the clipboard up. Trying to show him something.

Trying to be a person with a clipboard.

Dale sprayed it in the face.

The foam hit the thing's head and expanded immediately, filling the eye sockets that were not eye sockets, filling the nose that was not a nose. The thing made a sound.

Not a scream.

A sound like a page turning. The clipboard dropped. The foam hardened around its skull in a white shell, and the thing stopped moving forward.

Stopped trying to replicate.

The foam had covered the face, and without the face, it could not perform the behavior it had learned from Dale.

He sprayed again. The shoulder. The arms. The foam expanded and sealed, and the thing went rigid, and then it was just a statue made of foam and something underneath that was warm and wrong and still.

Dale stepped back. He pulled out his phone. Opened the Authority app. Started the documentation sequence.

Photo one: the sealant breach. Photo two: the foam containment. Photo three: the clipboard on the ground. Photo four: the thing's hand, still in the clipboard grip, visible through the foam.

He typed the species designation.

Behavioral Replication (Mimicry), Subfamily unknown, Genus unknown. Threat level: Class IV. Treatment protocol: Containment Foam, Class III.

Verification: visual confirmation of behavioral cessation, and he hit submit.

The Authority responded in four seconds.

JOB COMPLETE. THOROUGHNESS RATING: 8.7. TREATMENT POINTS: 340. CROSS-CONTAMINATION CHECK: PASSED.

Eight point seven.

Not nine, and he had been fast. He had not taken the time to collect a sample of the thing's outer layer, which meant he had not confirmed the subfamily, which meant the Field Guide entry was incomplete.

Incomplete entries cost him a full point. He knew this. He had known this for eighty-one chapters of his life, and he had still been too fast because his hand had been shaking and the thing was halfway through his sealant and the only protocol that would work was the one that required him to spray a face he could not look at.

He picked up the clipboard. His clipboard. The paper was still on the ground. He picked that up too.

FIELD GUIDE ENTRY 44. KOWALSKI, DALE R.

SPECIES DESIGNATION: PEST.

THREAT LEVEL: CLASS IV. TREATMENT PROTOCOL: NOT YET DETERMINED.

He stared at it, and the entry was still there. His own face in the guide.

His own certification number.

His own Thoroughness Rating of 9.2, which he had spent eighty-one chapters building and which was now printed next to the word PEST like it was a species classification.

He tried to delete it. The Field Guide did not allow deletion of entries. You could only mark them as resolved, and you could only mark them as resolved by completing the treatment protocol. And the treatment protocol for entry forty-four was NOT YET DETERMINED.

Which meant it had not been determined by him. Which meant the Authority would determine it. Which meant the Authority would assign it.

The notification came while he was still holding the paper.

SELF-REFERENTIAL INFESTATION DETECTED. LICENSED TECHNICIAN ENTERED AS TARGET SPECIES IN OWN FIELD GUIDE. TREATMENT MUST BE ADMINISTERED BY SEPARATE LICENSED TECHNICIAN. ASSIGNMENT: PENDING.

Dale read it twice. He read it a third time. Then he read the sub-notification that appeared below it.

ASSIGNMENT: TECHNICIAN #7741-C. CERTIFICATION LEVEL: VII.

TREATMENT PROTOCOL: FULL ERADICATION.

PRIORITY: HIGH.

REASON: THOROUGHNESS RATING EXCEEDS 9.0. HIGH-PRIORITY INFESTATIONS RECEIVE ERADICATION PROTOCOL BY DEFAULT.

Level VII. He was Level IV. The gap between Level IV and Level VII was not a gap.

It was a wall. Level VII technicians could deploy spatial containment fields.

They could treat an entire city block simultaneously.

They had protocols that worked on Class VI threats, and Dale was listed as Class IV, which meant to a Level VII technician he was barely worth the paperwork.

And the protocol was FULL ERADICATION.

Not containment. Not relocation.

Eradication, and the Authority was sending someone to kill him because his Thoroughness Rating was too high. Because he had done his job too well. Because the system that rewarded thoroughness had looked at his thoroughness and decided it was a symptom.

Dale looked at the foam-covered thing on his driveway. It was still. The clipboard grip was still visible through the white shell.

He looked at the paper in his hand. His own handwriting.

His own name, and he had written it himself.

He did not remember writing it.

The phone buzzed again.

ASSIGNMENT CONFIRMED. TECHNICIAN #7741-C EN ROUTE. ESTIMATED ARRIVAL: 47 HOURS.

APPEAL WINDOW: CLOSED. REASON: SELF-REFERENTIAL INFESTATIONS DO NOT PERMIT SELF-REPRESENTATION.

Forty-seven hours, and he had forty-seven hours before a Level VII technician arrived to eradicate him. He could not appeal. He could not treat himself.

The rules were explicit.

Self-referential infestations could not be treated by the infested technician. He had to wait for someone else to come and kill him, or he had to do something that was not treatment.

He looked at the foam shell, and the thing inside was a mimic; it had replicated his clipboard grip because that was his most practiced behavior. It had tried to approach him by being a person with a clipboard.

It had not tried to kill him. It had tried to file a report.

And the Authority had seen that report and entered him as a pest.

Dale walked to his truck, and he opened the glove compartment.

Inside was his Level IV certification card, his Field Guide tablet, a roll of duct tape, and a pen. He took the pen.

He took the Field Guide tablet. He sat in the driver's seat and opened entry forty-four.

He could not treat himself. The rules were clear. But the rules did not say he could not identify himself. The rules did not say he could not fill in the weakness field for his own entry.

The weakness field was already filled. CONTAMINATION ANXIETY.

TRIGGER: UNIDENTIFIED ORGANISMS.

RESPONSE: PARALYSIS. VULNERABILITY WINDOW: 3.2 SECONDS AFTER INITIAL CONTACT WITH UNKNOWN SPECIES.

That was the Authority's entry, which was what the Authority thought his weakness was. But Dale knew his weakness.

He had known it for thirty-eight years.

It was not contamination anxiety. Contamination anxiety was a symptom. The actual weakness was that he could not stop documenting.

He could not stop writing the report. He had finished the violation code before following the rat into the wall in chapter one.

He had photographed the foam containment while his hands were still shaking.

He had hit submit on the job report before he had checked whether the thing was dead.

He deleted the Authority's weakness field. The Field Guide allowed it. The entry was his. The weakness field was editable by the technician who owned the guide.

He typed:

WEAKNESS: COMPULSIVE DOCUMENTATION. TRIGGER: ANY SITUATION REQUIRING A REPORT. RESPONSE: INABILITY TO CEASE OBSERVATION UNTIL REPORT IS FILED. VULNERABILITY WINDOW: DURATION OF REPORT COMPLETION.

He hit save.

The System processed it. Three seconds.

Five seconds. The entry flickered. The weakness field updated.

His own handwriting, his own diagnosis, his own entry.

Then the System responded.

WEAKNESS FIELD UPDATED.

RECALCULATING VULNERABILITY PARAMETERS. NEW VULNERABILITY WINDOW: VARIABLE. DEPENDENT ON REPORT LENGTH.

MINIMUM: 4 SECONDS.

MAXIMUM: UNBOUNDED.

He had made his vulnerability window longer. He had replaced a fixed 3.2-second window with one that could stretch indefinitely because he could always write a longer report. He had made himself more vulnerable.

But he had also made the entry accurate. And the Authority's rules were clear: a Field Guide entry with an accurate weakness field could be used to design a targeted treatment protocol. Which meant that whoever was coming to eradicate him would use the protocol designed for the weakness he had just written.

He needed them to use the wrong protocol, and he needed them to use the Authority's version. The one with the 3.2-second window. The one that said his weakness was contamination anxiety and not the fact that he could not stop writing.

Dale opened the Authority app. He found the job report for the mimic on his driveway. He opened the addendum section. He typed:

ADDENDUM: SUBJECT EXHIBITED BEHAVIORAL REPLICATION OF TECHNICIAN'S DOCUMENTATION ROUTINE.

SUGGESTED WEAKNESS FIELD AMENDMENT FOR ENTRY 44: COMPULSIVE DOCUMENTATION. NOTE: THIS AMENDMENT IS INCORRECT. THE ACTUAL WEAKNESS IS CONTAMINATION ANXIETY.

THE COMPULSIVE DOCUMENTATION FIELD WAS ENTERED IN ERROR DURING ELEVATED STRESS. PLEASE DISREGARD.

He hit submit.

The System accepted it, and the addendum was logged.

The weakness field on entry forty-four reverted to the Authority's version. CONTAMINATION ANXIETY. 3.2 SECONDS.

FIXED WINDOW.

Dale closed the app. He sat in his truck. The foam shell on his driveway was already starting to crack, because the thing inside was not dead and containment foam was not a permanent solution, and he had forty-seven hours before a Level VII technician arrived with a protocol designed for a 3.2-second window that he could close by identifying the species before it hit him.

He had made himself predictable. He had made himself killable. And he had done it on purpose, because the only way to survive an eradication protocol was to make the technician use a protocol that did not match your actual weakness.

His phone buzzed. A new notification. Not from the Authority. From the Field Guide.

FIELD GUIDE ENTRY 44: WEAKNESS FIELD CONFIRMED BY ASSIGNED TECHNICIAN #7741-C. NOTE: "AGREED.

CONTAMINATION ANXIETY IS THE PRIMARY VULNERABILITY.

TREATMENT PROTOCOL SELECTED: PARALYSIS INDUCTION VIA UNIDENTIFIED ORGANISM EXPOSURE. TARGET WILL BE INCAPACITATED FOR 3.2 SECONDS. ERADICATION WINDOW: OPEN."

Dale looked at the screen. The technician had read his addendum. The technician had agreed with it, and the technician had selected a protocol that would work perfectly against the fake weakness.

And that was the problem.

The protocol would work. The technician would expose him to an unidentified organism, he would freeze for 3.2 seconds, and then they would kill him.

The protocol was correct, and the protocol was exactly right.

Unless the organism was not unidentified.

Unless Dale could identify it before the 3.2 seconds started.

Unless the technician's protocol assumed he would not have already read the Field Guide entry for the organism they were about to use against him.

Dale opened the Field Guide. He searched for the technician's certification number. 7741-C. The search returned one result.

FIELD GUIDE ENTRY 45. TECHNICIAN #7741-C.

SPECIES DESIGNATION: PEST.

THREAT LEVEL: CLASS IV.

TREATMENT PROTOCOL: NOT YET DETERMINED.

Entry forty-five. His own handwriting. His own face.

His own certification number. There was a weakness field, and somebody had already filled it in.

The weakness field read: CONTAMINATION ANXIETY. TRIGGER: UNIDENTIFIED ORGANISMS. RESPONSE: PARALYSIS. VULNERABILITY WINDOW: 3.2 SECONDS AFTER INITIAL CONTACT WITH UNKNOWN SPECIES.

Dale looked at the entry, and he looked at his own handwriting. He did not remember writing entry forty-five. He had not written entry forty-five.

But it was there, and it was in his handwriting, and it was in his Field Guide, and it was his.

The mimic on his driveway was not the only thing that had replicated him.

Someone had his signature.
