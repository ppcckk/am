Chapter 361: Clipboard Left Behind

# Chapter 361

The white light did not burn. It hummed. A low, vibrating frequency that rattled Dale's teeth in their sockets and made the clipboard in his hand feel heavier than it should.

Miller was gone.

Not dead. Not missing.

Just... absent.

The space where she had been standing was empty, save for the lingering scent of ozone and stale coffee. Dale stood alone on the cracked asphalt, though

"asphalt" was a generous term. The ground beneath him had dissolved into a geometric lattice of white lines, stretching out into an infinite, void-like space.

Across from him stood the other Dale.

Older. The hair was greyer at the temples. A scar ran from his left ear to his jawline, a jagged souvenir of a mistake Dale had not yet made. The suit was identical to Dale's current workwear, but the fabric was worn thin at the elbows, and the badge on the chest was polished to a mirror shine.

The other Dale clicked his pen. The sound was sharp, cutting through the hum.

"Report," the older man said.

Dale blinked, and he checked his clipboard. The paper was blank.

No, not blank.

The ink was rearranging itself, forming words that hadn't been written yet.

*Subject: Dale Kowalski. Age: 38.* *Violation: Unauthorized Temporal Interaction.* *Severity: Class X.*

"You're not the examiner," Dale said. His voice came out steady.

Dry, and he forced his breathing to match the four-count rhythm of a standard containment breathing exercise.

In for four. Hold for four.

Out for four.

"I am the Gatekeeper," the other Dale replied. He adjusted his tie. "And you are out of compliance."

"I'm a Level II Technician," Dale said. "I have a valid license."

"Your license is void."

The word hung in the air. Dale felt a cold spike in his gut.

Not fear.

Anger. Professional indignation.

"Explain the citation," Dale said.

The other Dale didn't answer immediately, and he pulled a small, leather-bound notebook from his breast pocket. He flipped it open.

The pages were filled with Dale's handwriting, but the dates were wrong.

They were all in the future.

"You treated a Dimensional Vermin nest in Sector 4," the older man said, reading from the book. "You used a Class I Fungicide spray on a Class III Acidic Beetle infestation."

"That was a misidentification error," Dale said. "It's in the log. Thoroughness rating dropped to 6.4. I accepted the penalty."

"You contaminated your Field Guide entry for twenty-four hours," the other Dale continued, ignoring the apology. "But you didn't stop. You continued to deploy the incorrect protocol. Why?"

"Because the infestation was active. If I stopped to wait for the cooldown, the broodmother would have hatched."

"And?"

"And I got the job done."

"You violated the Cross-Contamination Statute," the older man said. He closed the notebook. "Article 7, Section B: *No Technician shall deploy a protocol against a species not currently active in their Field Guide, unless a Class VI Override is filed.*"

"I filed the override," Dale said. "Post-event. Within the twelve-hour grace period."

"Filed," the older man repeated. He smiled.

It was a thin, joyless expression.

"But not approved.

The Authority does not approve retroactive overrides when the resulting infestation spreads to adjacent dimensions.

"

Dale's grip tightened on his pliers.

"Spreads to adjacent dimensions?"

"Look at the sky."

Dale looked up. The white light was no longer just a beam. It was a crack.

A fracture in the fabric of the reality around them. Through the crack, Dale saw other skies.

Different colors.

Different stars. And things moving in those other skies. Things with too many legs.

"You didn't just kill the beetles," the older man said. "You opened a door. And you didn't document the threshold."

Dale looked at his System interface.

*Threat Level: Class X.* *New Protocol Unlocked: Temporal Variance Analysis.*

He tapped the protocol. Nothing happened. The button was greyed out.

*Locked. Prerequisite: Thoroughness Rating 9.5.*

Dale's current rating: 9.2, and he was three-tenths of a point short.

"Fix it," Dale said.

"Fix what?"

"The rating. Raise my Thoroughness. Let me use the protocol. Let me fix the door."

The other Dale shook his head.

"You cannot fix a violation with more violations. You must submit to review."

"Review by whom?"

"By me."

The older Dale stepped forward. The white lattice beneath his feet rippled. He held out his pen.

"Sign here."

Dale looked at the paper. It had floated up to his chest. It was a termination form.

*Certification Revocation.* *Reason: Gross Negligence leading to Dimensional Breach.* *Consequence: Erasure from the Registry.*

If he signed, he ceased to exist in the eyes of the Authority.

He became unlicensed. Unrecognized.

Invisible to the System.

And without the System, he had no power. No protection.

He would be just a man with a clipboard in a world full of monsters.

"No," Dale said.

The other Dale sighed.

"I expected this. You're stubborn. It's your greatest asset and your fatal flaw."

"I need time," Dale said. "I need to document the breach. That's protocol."

"Protocol is for licensed technicians."

"I am licensed."

"Not anymore. Not if you refuse to accept the citation."

Dale looked at the pen. Then he looked at his own clipboard.

His hand moved on its own.

Not to sign.

To write, and he wrote in the margin of the termination form.

*Addendum: Citation disputed. Evidence provided: Field Guide entry for Rattus profundus, updated with behavioral data suggesting cross-dimensional migration patterns. Correction required.*

The other Dale stopped. He stared at the writing.

"You're arguing with the Authority?"

"I'm filing an appeal," Dale said. "Procedurally, if I dispute a citation with evidence, the clock stops. The revocation is pending until the evidence is reviewed."

The older man's face darkened.

"You're buying time."

"I'm following procedure."

"Procedure won't save you from the things behind me."

A shadow moved in the white void. Something large. Something with chitinous plates and too many eyes. It was emerging from the crack in the sky.

Dale saw it. His Field Guide flickered.

*Unidentified Entity.* *Analysis: Pending.*

If he didn't identify it, he couldn't target its weakness. And if he couldn't target its weakness, he couldn't kill it.

"Identify it," the other Dale said. "If you can."

"I need data," Dale said.

"You have seconds."

Dale looked at the entity. It moved with jerky, spasmodic motions. It clicked its mandibles.

*Dental wear,* Dale noted. *Heavy abrasion on the lower left molar. Indicates feeding on silica or stone. Not flesh. Stone-eater.*

*Species: Lithovore Variant.*

*Weakness: Joint vulnerability to high-frequency vibration.*

Dale looked at his pliers. Then he looked at the older Dale.

"Vibration," Dale said. "Does the System support sonic protocols?"

The older Dale paused.

"Class IV Protocol: Resonance Disruption. Requires Certification Level IV."

Dale was Level II.

"Then I can't use it," Dale said.

"Correct."

"So how do I kill it?"

The older Dale smiled.

"You don't. You run."

The entity lunged. Its claws tore through the white lattice, sending shards of light scattering like glass. Dale dodged, rolling to the side.

His knees hit the ground. Pain flared in his shoulder.

"Run," the older man said again.

Dale scrambled up, and he looked at the entity. It was huge. Ten feet tall.

Its body was made of jagged rock, fused with living muscle.

It roared, a sound like grinding tectonic plates.

Dale's clipboard was gone. Lost in the scramble.

He had no documentation. No proof of identification.

If he killed it now, without the Field Guide entry, he would get zero points. And the breach would remain open.

*Unless.*

A loophole. Dale's mind raced. He remembered a clause from the licensing manual, page 402, subsection C: *Field Guide entries may be co-signed by a secondary observer if the primary observer lacks the necessary clearance to verify the identification independently.*

He looked at the older Dale.

"Sign it," Dale said.

The older man raised an eyebrow.

"Sign what?"

"The Field Guide entry. You're the Gatekeeper. You have Level IX clearance. Your signature validates the entry."

The older Dale laughed. It was a dry, raspy sound.

"You want me to help you?"

"I want you to witness the identification. It's procedure."

"And if I sign, you get the points. You get the protocol access. You might even fix the breach."

"Yes."

"And what do I get?"

"Nothing. You're just a witness."

The entity swung a claw. Dale ducked. The claw smashed the ground where he had been standing. A crater appeared.

"You're trying to trick me," the older Dale said.

"I'm trying to survive," Dale said.

"And you're trying to revoke my license. If I die, the violation goes unsolved. The Authority hates unsolved violations.

It creates a backlog.

"

The older Dale's eyes narrowed. He looked at the approaching monster. He looked at Dale.

"A backlog," he muttered. "Annoying."

He pulled out his pen.

"Dale Kowalski. Field Guide Entry: Lithovore Variant. Weakness: Sonic Resonance. Witnessed by Dale Kowalski (Temporal Variant)."

The older Dale signed.

The System pinged.

*Field Guide Updated.* *Thoroughness Rating: 9.2 -> 9.5.* *Protocol Unlocked: Resonance Disruption.*

Dale felt the power surge into him. It wasn't magic. It was mechanics. The System recognized his knowledge.

He raised his pliers. They hummed. A low, high-pitched frequency emanated from the metal tips.

The Lithovore paused. Its head tilted. It heard the vibration.

Dale stepped forward, and he didn't hesitate.

He didn't panic. He calculated the distance.

Five feet. Three feet.

"Hey," Dale said.

The creature turned.

Dale clicked the pliers.

A sonic pulse, visible as a ripple in the air, shot toward the creature. It hit the Lithovore's left knee joint. The stone plates cracked.

The creature stumbled.

"Again," Dale said.

He clicked again. This time, he aimed for the neck.

The creature shrieked. The sound was deafening. The white lattice around them shatterred.

The older Dale watched, impassive. He didn't help. He didn't interfere. He just watched.

Dale moved. He was a pest control technician.

He knew how to maneuver in tight spaces, and he knew how to flank. He used the debris as cover. He waited for the creature to overextend.

When it swung wide, Dale stepped in.

*Target: Cervical vertebrae.* *Protocol: Resonance Disruption.*

He jammed the pliers into the gap between the stone plates. He clicked.

The frequency maximized.

The Lithovore froze. Its eyes bulged. Cracks spread across its body like spiderwebs.

Then it exploded.

Not into gore. Into dust.

A cloud of fine, grey powder filled the air.

Dale stood back. He coughed. He wiped his face.

He looked at his System interface.

*Job Completed.* *Treatment Points Awarded: 500.* *Thoroughness Rating: 9.8.*

*Bonus: Certification Level III Unlocked.*

Dale looked at the older Dale.

"Did that solve the breach?"

The older Dale looked at the crack in the sky. It was still open. But it was smaller. Contained.

"The breach is contained," the older Dale said. "But not closed."

"How do I close it?"

"You don't."

Dale frowned. "Why?"

"Because the breach wasn't caused by the Lithovore," the older Dale said. "It was caused by you."

Me?

"Your hesitation," the older man said. "Your need to document. You waited too long. You let the entity stabilize the rift before you acted."

"I followed protocol."

"Protocol is a shield," the older Dale said. "Sometimes, you have to drop the shield."

He stepped closer. The white light intensified.

"You passed the test," the older Dale said. "But you failed the lesson."

"What lesson?"

"That thoroughness has a cost."

The older Dale pointed to the ground.

Where the Lithovore had exploded, the grey dust was settling. But it wasn't just dust. It was forming shapes. Small, insect-like shapes.

Hundreds of them, and they were moving. Scuttling across the white lattice.

"Cross-contamination," the older Dale said. "The Lithovore was carrying parasites. You disrupted it. You spread them."

Dale looked at the insects. They were small. No bigger than a thumbnail. But they were multiplying fast.

And they were heading toward the crack in the sky.

"If they get through," the older Dale said, "the infestation spreads to the other dimensions."

Dale felt a cold dread settle in his stomach. He looked at his pliers. The sonic protocol was cooling down. Cooldown: 30 seconds.

He couldn't stop them. Not with this tool.

"Then what do I do?" Dale asked.

The older Dale handed him the pen.

"You write the next report," he said. "And you decide whether to save the world, or save your license."

Dale took the pen. The insects were getting closer. Ten feet away. Five feet.

He looked at the crack. He looked at the insects.

He raised the pen, and he wrote:

*Incident 361: Secondary Infestation Detected.* *Priority: Immediate Containment.* *Action Required: Self-Sacrifice.*

He didn't hesitate.

He stepped forward.

He threw himself into the swarm.

The older Dale watched him go. He didn't look away. He didn't blink.

He just wrote in his own notebook.

*Case File Closed.*

*Subject Status: Missing.*

*Next Review: Chapter 362.*

The white light faded.

The alley returned.

Miller was back. She was on her knees, coughing. She looked around, confused.

"Dale?" she said.

Dale was gone.

Only the clipboard remained. And the pen. And the smell of ozone.

And in the sewer grate, a single, tiny insect scurried into the darkness.

Miller reached down and picked up the pen.
