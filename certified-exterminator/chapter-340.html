Chapter 340: Suing the Rift

# Chapter 340

The front door was unlocked. That was the first violation.

Dale's hand hovered over the biometric scanner, his thumb hovering a millimeter from the glass. He didn't touch it. Touching it meant acknowledging the breach, and acknowledging the breach meant admitting panic, and panic contaminated the workspace.

Kael stood behind him, chest heaving, sweat dripping from his nose onto the porch concrete.

"It's unlocked," Kael wheezed. "Why is it unlocked?"

"Because whoever is inside knows we're coming," Dale said. His voice was flat.

Clinical, and he pulled a pair of nitrile gloves from his utility belt. The snap of the rubber was loud in the quiet suburb.

"And because they didn't bother to lock it.

Laziness is a tell.

"

He pushed the door open with his boot.

The smell hit him before the sight. It wasn't rot.

Rot was organic, decomposing, predictable, which was ozone and copper, mixed with the sweet, cloying scent of crushed chitin. It smelled like a hive that had been smashed with a hammer, then heated in an oven.

Dale stepped inside. He didn't look for movement. He looked for damage.

The entryway rug was stained black. Not mud.

Not blood.

Frass.

Coarse, pellet-like waste, scattered in a perfect circle around the coat rack.

Dale knelt. He picked up a pellet with tweezers from his belt. He held it to the light. It was iridescent, shifting from green to purple as he tilted it.

"Chrysalis waste," Dale muttered. "High-grade metabolic byproduct. This isn't a scout. This is a breeder."

Kael gagged, turning away toward the kitchen.

"Is Linda..."

"Linda is likely in the kitchen," Dale said, not standing up. "She makes dinner at six. It is six-ten.

She is still making dinner. That means she is either unconscious, trapped, or dealing with it.

We find out in ten seconds.

"

Dale stood. He checked his clipboard. The *SYSTEM ALERT* was pulsing red in his peripheral vision.

*TARGET: SUBJECT 004 (RESURRECTED).* *STATUS: ACTIVE.* *CONTAMINATION LEVEL: 88%.* *PROTOCOL: CLASS IX ISOLATION.*

Class IX. Dale's throat tightened. He had never even seen the Class IX forms.

They were theoretical, and they were for things that didn't exist in nature, things that ate reality and pooped out void.

He walked down the hallway.

The walls were sweating. Not condensation. A thick, viscous slime oozed from the seams of the drywall, bubbling slightly.

It coated the baseboards in a layer of black mold that looked like lace.

Dale pulled a spray can from his hip.

It wasn't insecticide, which was a neutralizing agent, designed for dimensional residue. He sprayed the slime.

The bubbles hissed and popped, revealing clean white paint underneath.

*PROTOCOL APPLIED: CHEMICAL NEUTRALIZATION.* *FIELD GUIDE UPDATED: VOID-MOLD (SUBSPECIES B).* *WEAKNESS IDENTIFIED: ALKALINE BASE.*

A small green text box flashed. +15 Treatment Points.

"Keep moving," Dale said. "Don't touch the walls."

Kael nodded, pale-faced. He kept his hands in his pockets. Smart.

They reached the kitchen. The door was closed. A low hum vibrated through the wood, shaking the handle.

Dale listened. He counted the rhythm. *Thump.

Thump.

Thrum. Thump.*

It wasn't a heartbeat. It was a footfall. Heavy. Wet.

He pressed his ear to the door. He could hear Linda's voice, but it was distorted, slowed down, like audio playing at half speed.

"Dale..." the voice groaned. "...the pasta..."

"Linda is under the table," Dale said. "The thing is above her. It's using her as a nest anchor."

Kael reached for the handle.

Dale grabbed his wrist.

"Stop. If you open this door, you break the seal.

The containment field in this room is failing.

If you let the air out, the pressure differential will suck the spores into the ventilation system. You'll poison the whole house. Then you'll poison the street.

"

Kael froze. His hand trembled.

"What do I do?"

"Stand there," Dale said. "Don't breathe."

Dale looked at the door. He looked at the gap between the door and the floor.

He pulled a roll of duct tape from his belt, and he ripped a strip.

Then another. He pressed them over the gap, sealing the bottom of the door.

*ACTION: PHYSICAL SEAL.* *THOROUGHNESS BONUS: +5.*

He moved to the top of the door. He used a shim to block the latch.

*ACTION: LATCH JAM.* *THOROUGHNESS BONUS: +5.*

He stepped back. He looked at Kael.

"I'm going to use the ventilation return," Dale said. "It's on the far wall. If I block the intake, the pressure in the kitchen will rise.

The thing will have to vent its waste to equalize. Waste is the tell.

"

"That's insane," Kael whispered.

"That's physics," Dale said. "And it's code."

Dale moved to the side wall, and he found the air return vent. He pulled a small canister from his belt. It was a compressed air bomb, designed to clear clogged ducts.

He jammed the nozzle into the vent and twisted the valve.

*PROTOCOL: PRESSURE MANIPULATION.* *WARNING: UNLICENSED USE OF CIVIL EQUIPMENT IN COMBAT ZONE.* *PENALTY: 10% THOROUGHNESS DEBUFF.*

Dale ignored the warning. He held the nozzle steady.

The hum from the kitchen door changed pitch. It went from a low thrum to a high, shrieking whine. The slime on the walls boiled.

Something hit the door from the other side. Hard. The wood splintered.

"It's trying to break out," Kael said.

"Let it try," Dale said. He kept holding the valve.

"It's suffocating.

It's building up CO2. It needs oxygen.

"

Another hit. The door bowed inward. A crack appeared in the center panel.

Black dust puffed out.

Dale saw it. A fine, gray powder.

*FIELD GUIDE UPDATE: SUBJECT 004 RESPIRATORY WEAKNESS.* *IDENTIFICATION CONFIRMED: HOLLOW-WORM.*

Dale let go of the valve, and he pulled a different canister from his belt. This one was labeled *FUNGICIDE CONCENTRATE - CLASS C*.

He walked to the door. He didn't open it.

He kicked the bottom of the door.

The duct tape held, and he kicked the middle. The latch held.

He kicked the crack.

The door exploded outward.

A creature lunged.

It wasn't a worm. It was a torso. A human-shaped torso, stripped of skin, wrapped in translucent, pulsing sacs.

It had four arms, each ending in jagged chitinous spikes. The head was missing.

In its place was a gaping maw lined with teeth that rotated like a meat grinder.

It screamed. The sound was like metal tearing.

Dale didn't dodge. He sprayed.

The stream of fungicide hit the sacs on the creature's back.

*PROTOCOL MISMATCH.* *ERROR: FUNGICIDE VS. CHITIN.* *DEBUFF: 40% DAMAGE REDUCTION.*

The creature didn't slow down. It stumbled forward, blinded by the spray, but still moving. It raised a spike-arm to impale Dale.

Dale stepped left. The spike punched a hole in the drywall.

He didn't retreat. He reached into his belt and pulled out a small, silver sphere. A *SEALANT GRENADE*.

He threw it.

The sphere rolled under the creature. It detonated silently.

Foam expanded instantly, hardening into a cement-like block. It pinned the creature's legs.

The creature thrashed. The sacs on its back burst. Black ichor sprayed across the kitchen floor.

Linda was under the table. She was alive. She was shaking. Her eyes were wide, fixed on the monster.

"Get back!" Kael shouted. He pulled a knife from his boot.

"No!" Dale yelled. "Don't pierce the sacs! The fluid is corrosive. It will melt your skin."

Kael hesitated.

The creature broke free of the sealant. It lunged for Kael.

Dale moved. He didn't attack the creature. He attacked the floor.

He slammed his foot down on a tile he had loosened earlier. He had noticed the grout lines were darker near the sink. Water damage. Hidden weakness.

The tile cracked. The subfloor beneath was rotten wood, softened by the slime.

Dale stomped again. The wood gave way.

The creature's foot punched through the floor, up to the knee. It roared, off-balance.

Dale pulled a flare from his belt. Not a combat flare. A signal flare. Red smoke.

He lit it. He dropped it into the hole.

The smoke rose, thick and red, filling the creature's maw.

*PROTOCOL: VISUAL OBSCURATION.* *ACTION: DISORIENT TARGET.*

The creature flailed blindly. It swung its arms, smashing cabinets, cracking dishes.

Dale walked forward. He walked calmly. He picked up a shard of broken plate.

He walked up to the creature. He found the gap between its ribs, where the sacs were thinnest. The *Hollow-Worm* needed the sacs to breathe. They were lungs.

He thrust the shard into the gap, and he twisted.

The sacs ruptured.

A wet, sucking sound filled the room. The creature convulsed. Its legs buckled. It collapsed into the hole in the floor, its head still thrashing above the ground.

Dale stepped back. He checked his clipboard.

*ELIMINATION COMPLETE.* *VERIFICATION REQUIRED.*

He walked to the sink. He turned on the faucet. He washed his hands.

He washed them for thirty seconds.

Then he dried them on a paper towel, and he walked back to the creature. It was dead. The sacs had deflated, turning into gray, shriveled husks.

He pulled a marker from his belt. He drew a line around the body.

He wrote on the floor: *SPECIMEN ID: 004-B. CAUSE: RESPIRATORY COLLAPSE.*

He turned to Kael. Kael was staring at him.

"You killed it," Kael said.

"I processed it," Dale corrected. "There's a difference."

Dale looked at Linda. She was crying, but she was alive. She looked at the monster, then at Dale.

"The pasta," she whispered. "It's burning."

Dale walked past them, and he went to the stove. He turned off the burner. He opened the oven.

The lasagna was black on the edges, but the center was fine.

He plated two portions. He set the table.

"Sit," Dale said.

"We're not leaving until we clear the residue.

If we leave now, the Authority will send a cleanup crew. They'll tear out the floors.

They'll tear out the cabinets. They'll tear out the walls. And they'll bill us for it.

"

Kael slumped into a chair.

"They'll bill us?"

"Standard recovery fee," Dale said. "Plus penalty for unlicensed combat."

He sat down. He picked up a fork.

His hands were shaking. He looked at them. He gripped the fork tighter until the knuckles turned white.

He ate.

The food tasted like ash.

The *NOTIFICATION* window in his vision was still open. It hadn't closed.

*SYSTEM ALERT: NEW ASSIGNMENT PENDING.* *SOURCE: LICENSING AUTHORITY - SPECIAL DIVISION.* *STATUS: PENDING REVIEW.*

Dale stopped chewing.

A new text box appeared. It was not red. It was gold.

*NOTIFICATION: CERTIFICATION REVIEW.* *SUBJECT: DALE KOWALSKI.* *ASSESSMENT: THOROUGHNESS SCORE 8.5.* *STATUS: FAIL.*

*REASON: UNAUTHORIZED MODIFICATION OF RESIDENCE.* *REASON: USE OF CIVIL EQUIPMENT IN COMBAT.* *REASON: EXCESSIVE USE OF FORCE (LACK OF PREVENTATIVE MEASURES).*

*DECISION: LICENSE SUSPENDED INDENTIFINATELY.*

Dale put the fork down.

Kael saw the face.

"Dale?"

"I'm suspended," Dale said. His voice was quiet.

"I can't work.

I can't treat. I can't certify.

"

"So what do we do?"

Dale looked at the hole in the floor. He looked at the black ichor seeping into the basement stairs.

"We have a problem," Dale said. "The Authority doesn't just suspend licenses. It audits them."

Kael frowned. "Audits?"

"They send an Inspector," Dale said.

"An Auditor. They come to verify the kill.

They check the paperwork.

They check the method.

"

"And if the method is wrong?"

"They revoke the license permanently. And they classify the technician as a hazard."

Dale stood up. He walked to the basement door.

He pulled out his Field Guide. He flipped to the last page. He tore it out.

"What are you doing?" Kael asked.

"I'm removing the evidence of my error," Dale said. He folded the page. He put it in his pocket.

He opened the basement door. The darkness below was deep. And it was moving.

Something was crawling up the stairs.

Not a Hollow-Worm. Not a Vermin.

It was a suit. A gray, bureaucratic suit, with a helmet made of glass and bone.

It walked up the stairs. It held a clipboard.

It stopped at the bottom of the staircase. It looked up at Dale.

*NOTIFICATION: AUDITOR DETECTED.* *NAME: INSPECTOR VEX.* *CLASS: IX.* *INTENT: TERMINATION OF NON-COMPLIANT ENTITY.*

Dale looked at the Auditor. He looked at Linda, who was still crying at the table. He looked at Kael, who was reaching for his knife.

Dale smiled. It was a tight, painful smile.

"Good evening, Inspector," Dale said. "I was just cleaning up."

The Auditor tilted its head.

"Your license is invalid," the Auditor said. Its voice was the sound of grinding stones.

Dale pulled the folded page from his pocket. He held it up.

"I have a counter-claim," Dale said. "You violated Regulation 44-B. Entry without warrant. Failure to provide notice."

The Auditor stepped up. The stairs groaned under its weight.

"Dale," Kael said. "Don't."

Dale dropped the page, and he reached for his belt. He didn't grab a weapon. He grabbed a red stamp.

He pressed it against the Auditor's chest.

*STAMP APPLIED: VIOLATION NOTICE.*

The Auditor stopped. It looked down at the red mark on its suit.

*SYSTEM ALERT: BUREAUCRATIC COUNTER-OFFENSIVE.* *ACTION: FILE COMPLAINT.* *STATUS: ACCEPTED.* *DELAY: 24 HOURS.*

The Auditor's glass face cracked. It let out a hiss of steam.

"It's buying us time," Dale said. He grabbed Linda's hand.

"We leave.

Now.

Through the back door. Into the yard.

Into the Rift."

Kael stared at him.

"You're insane."

"I'm efficient," Dale said. He pulled Linda up. "Run."

They ran. They burst out the back door, into the night. The Rift was swirling above them, pulling at the stars.

Behind them, the Auditor stepped out onto the porch. It raised a hand. A beam of pure logic shot from its palm, dissolving the doorframe into dust.

It didn't follow. It stood there, watching.

Dale didn't look back. He dragged Linda toward the tree line, where the shadows were thick and the ground was soft.

Kael ran beside them, breathing hard.

"Dale," Kael panted. "Where are we going?"

Dale looked at his clipboard. It was blank.

No points.

No progress. Just the red *FAIL* stamp glowing in the corner.

He looked at the Rift, and he saw shapes moving inside it. Not monsters.

Men.

Men in suits.

Dale pulled out his pen. He wrote on the blank paper.

*OBJECTIVE: FILE APPEAL.* *LOCATION: RIFT CENTER.* *TIME: NOW.*

He looked at Kael.

"We're not running from it," Dale said. "We're going to sue it."
