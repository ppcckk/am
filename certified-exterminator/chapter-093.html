Chapter 93: Entry Threat Critical

# Chapter 93

The wipers slapped back and forth against the glass, a metronome counting down the seconds of Dale’s career. He kept his hands at ten and two. He kept his eyes on the road. He did not look at the rearview mirror, because looking at the rearview mirror meant acknowledging the sky, and acknowledging the sky meant acknowledging the towers.

He drove south on Route 9, pushing the van past the speed limit by exactly three miles per hour. Enough to be efficient. Not enough to be reckless. The rain was coming down in sheets, turning the asphalt into a slick black mirror that reflected the purple bruise of the Rift hanging above the horizon.

His phone buzzed again. *Run.*

Dale ignored it. He checked his fuel gauge.

Three-quarters tank.

Enough to reach the reservoir, do the job, and get out before the Authority’s auditors arrived. They wouldn’t be auditors. If Miller’s note was right, they would be something worse.

Something that didn’t care about OSHA regulations, and he reached the perimeter of the reservoir; the chain-link fence had been torn down.

Not cut.

Pulled. The metal mesh was bunched up like crushed tin foil, leading toward the water’s edge.

Dale pulled the van off the road and killed the engine. He grabbed his clipboard.

He grabbed the heavy-duty trash bags, and he grabbed the spray canister, though he knew it was useless against the Skitterers.

He needed documentation. He needed a paper trail.

He stepped into the rain. The air smelled of ozone and rotting kelp. The ground was mud.

Soft, sucking mud that threatened to pull his steel-toed boots off. He didn’t care.

He moved with the mechanical efficiency of a man who had walked into basements full of raccoon feces for fourteen years.

The Skitterers were there, and they weren’t the small, rat-sized things from the first chapter. These were the drones Miller had warned about. Humanoid in shape, but wrong.

Their limbs were too long, their joints bending backward.

They skittered across the mud, leaving deep, clawed furrows. They were building something.

Dale crouched behind a rusted dumpster near the shoreline. He raised his clipboard. He didn’t write down what he saw. He wrote down what he *didn’t* see.

*Entry 404: Dimensional Drones.* *Location: Reservoir Bank.* *Behavior: Construction.* *Structural Integrity: Compromised.*

The drones were assembling a framework.

It looked like scaffolding made of bone and wet clay, and they were stacking chunks of earth, compressing them into dense blocks. It was crude, but it was working.

They were building a foundation for a tower that hadn’t been built yet.

Dale watched them. He needed to identify the weak point. The System required it. *Identify, document, isolate, eliminate, verify.*

He looked at the drones, and they had no eyes.

They navigated by scent and vibration. He looked at their legs.

The joints were exposed, covered in a chitinous plating that was thinner near the knees.

He needed to test it, and he picked up a rock. Not too big.

Not too small.

A medium-weight projectile. He aimed for the nearest drone’s left knee. He didn’t throw it.

He rolled it.

A controlled release.

The rock hit the mud near the drone. The drone didn’t react. It kept stacking blocks.

Dale frowned. No aggression response. No territorial defense.

He tried again. This time, he threw the rock. Hard. It struck the drone’s shoulder.

The drone froze.

It turned its head. It didn’t look at Dale. It looked at the rock.

Then it looked at the ground where the rock had landed. It scuttled over, picked up the rock, and placed it in the wall it was building.

Dale blinked.

It wasn’t fighting. It was incorporating.

He checked his Field Guide. The Skitterer entry was contaminated. He couldn’t use it for damage bonuses. But he could still read the description.

*Dimensional Vermin (Rattus profundus variant: Builder Drone).* *Primary Threat: Infrastructure Expansion.* *Secondary Threat: Nesting.* *Weakness: Structural Instability.*

The drones weren’t attacking him because they weren’t threatened by him. They were workers. And he was just another source of material.

Dale felt a cold sweat break out on his neck. That was the loophole. That was the violation.

The Authority hadn’t banned him from entering the zone. They had banned him from *engaging* in combat. If he fought them, he broke protocol. If he didn’t fight them, they kept building.

But if he helped them build?

No. That was insane.

He needed to stop the construction; how do you stop a construction crew that treats you as raw materials?

He looked at the foundation. The drones were packing the earth so tightly it was becoming solid rock, and they were using the pressure of the water table to bind it.

Dale looked at the ground. The mud was soft here, but further out, near the water’s edge, the ground was firmer. The drones were working inward from the firm ground.

He needed to change the terrain. He needed to create a sinkhole.

He looked at the van. He looked at the fuel.

He couldn’t blow them up. That was combat. That was a violation.

But he could perform a service, and he got back in the van.

He drove down the embankment, tires spinning in the mud, until he was right next to the drone line.

He stepped out. The drones didn’t move.

They kept building.

Dale opened the back of the van, and he pulled out the industrial pump he used for clearing clogged drains in commercial basements. It was heavy. It ran on gasoline.

He dragged it to the edge of the construction site. He didn’t aim it at the drones. He aimed it at the ground beneath their feet.

He primed the pump. He started the engine. The pump whined, a high-pitched mechanical scream.

The drones stopped, and they turned. Not toward Dale. Toward the ground.

The pump started sucking. Not water. Mud. The loose, wet earth beneath the foundation.

The drones shrieked. A sound like grinding glass. They rushed toward the pump, trying to cover the hole, trying to plug the leak with their own bodies.

But it was too late. The structural integrity was gone. The blocks of compressed earth shifted. One by one, they slid into the void the pump was creating.

Dale kept the pump running. He checked his watch. He needed to keep this up for ten minutes. Just ten minutes to collapse the foundation.

The sky grew darker. The purple hue of the Rift pulsed.

A shadow fell over him.

Dale looked up, and a figure stood on the embankment above him. It wore a suit.

A pristine, white suit.

It held a clipboard. A golden clipboard.

The Authority.

Dale didn’t stop the pump. He couldn’t stop. If he stopped, the foundation might settle enough to hold.

The figure descended the hill. It moved with unnatural grace, sliding down the mud without slipping. It stopped five feet from Dale.

"You are in violation of Statute 44-B," the figure said. Its voice was flat, devoid of emotion. "Unauthorized alteration of dimensional infrastructure."

Dale didn’t look up, and he adjusted the angle of the pump. "I am performing emergency remediation of a structural hazard," he said.

"Code K-7.

Subsurface erosion. I am preventing a catastrophic collapse that could endanger public safety."

The figure tilted its head. "This is not a hazard. This is a project."

"Unpermitted construction," Dale said. "No zoning permits. No environmental impact study. I am acting within my jurisdiction as a Class II Technician to stop an illegal build."

The figure stepped closer. It raised a hand. The air around its fingers shimmered. Purple light crackled.

"You are contaminating the Field Guide," the figure said. "Your identification of these units as 'vermin' is incorrect. They are Architects."

Dale kept pumping. The mud swirled into the intake.

The drones were screaming now, frantic, throwing themselves into the pump’s intake tube.

They jammed the mechanism.

The engine sputtered and died.

Silence returned. The rain hammered against the van.

The drones were gone. Swallowed by the mud. Or buried under it.

Dale turned off the pump. He wiped his hands on his overalls. He picked up his clipboard.

He looked at the Authority figure. "Job complete. Foundation destabilized. Infestation cleared for this sector."

The figure stared at him. The purple light in its eyes dimmed.

"You broke the rules," the figure said. "But you followed the protocol."

"I followed the law," Dale said. "The law of gravity. The law of erosion. You can’t build a tower on quicksand."

The figure looked down at the collapsed mud. It looked at the ruined scaffolding of bone and clay.

"Thoroughness Scale: 6.5," the figure said. "Sloppy work. You used heavy machinery.

You did not isolate the individual units. You did not verify the elimination of each drone."

Dale felt a chill.

A 6.5 rating was bad. It meant fewer points. It meant his certification advancement would be delayed.

It meant he would be stuck at Level II longer than he wanted.

But it also meant he hadn’t failed.

"If the Authority is coming to arrest me," Dale said, "then arrest me. But the job is done. The site is cleared."

The figure stepped back. "The Authority is not coming to arrest you, Technician Kowalski. The Authority is coming to hire you."

Dale froze.

"Hire me?"

"The Rift is expanding," the figure said. "The Architects are not the only ones building.

There are other infestations.

Other cities. The Authority is understaffed. Your methods are... unorthodox.

But they are effective."

Dale looked at the collapsed mud. He looked at his clipboard. He had written a report. A violation report.

"You want me to work for you?" Dale asked.

"I want you to clean up the mess," the figure said. "All of it. The entire Rift."

Dale shook his head. "No."

The figure paused. "No?"

"I don't work for the Authority," Dale said.

"I work for the city.

I work for the people in their homes. If you want me to exterminate these things, you need to file the correct permits.

You need to pay the fees. And you need to respect the protocols."

The figure’s expression didn’t change. It couldn’t. It was a construct. A tool.

"Then you are fired," the figure said.

"I was never hired," Dale said.

He turned to get in his van.

The figure raised its hand again. The purple light intensified.

Dale heard a sound. Not from the figure. From the mud.

The ground shook.

Not a tremor. A heartbeat.

The mud bubbles rose to the surface. The puddles rippled.

Dale looked at the puddle at his feet. Something was moving under it. Something large.

The Authority figure looked down. "Oh," it said. "You missed a spot."

Dale looked at his Field Guide. The Skitterer entry was still contaminated. He couldn’t identify the new threat.

He looked at the mud. A claw emerged. Then another. Then a face.

It wasn’t a drone. It was a Broodmother. But it was different. It was fused with the mud, with the bone scaffolding, with the Rift itself.

It was part of the city now.

Dale backed up. He reached for his spray can. It was empty.

He looked at the Authority figure. "What is that?"

"It is the Core," the figure said. "And you just woke it up."

The figure turned and walked away. It didn’t look back. It dissolved into purple mist, leaving Dale alone with the thing in the mud.

The thing rose. It was ten feet tall. Its body was a mix of chitin and wet earth. Its eyes were glowing purple.

It looked at Dale. It didn’t screech. It spoke.

"Builder," it said.

Dale gripped his clipboard. He didn’t have a weapon. He didn’t have a plan. He didn’t have a permit.

He had a clipboard, and he wrote one line.

*Entry 405: Unknown Entity. Threat Level: Critical.*

He didn’t know what to do next.

The thing took a step forward. The ground cracked.

Dale ran.
