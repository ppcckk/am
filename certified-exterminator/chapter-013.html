Chapter 13

Dale measured the consumption rate every forty minutes. He'd set up a grid on the basement floor using strips of masking tape, each square exactly one foot across, and he'd placed a piece of untreated oak under each one. The oak was his control substrate. If the mutation consumed oak at a predictable rate, he could extrapolate reactivation timing. If it didn't, he'd need a different approach.

At hour fourteen, the oak under square seven had a half-inch bite mark. The oak under square twelve had none. The oak under square nineteen had been reduced to sawdust.

He wrote this down. Consumption was not uniform. It was directional. The mutation was eating toward something specific, and the rate of consumption was accelerating along that vector.

His phone buzzed.

AUTHORITY NOTIFICATION: FIELD GUIDE ENTRY (Rattus profundus, variant) UPDATED. DRAFT STATUS: 74% COMPLETE. NOTE: CONSUMPTION PATTERN DATA ADDED. REMAINING VERIFICATION REQUIREMENT: DIRECT COMBAT ENGAGEMENT.

Seventy-four percent. Close. He needed to see it fight, or at least see it respond to a targeted stimulus. The Authority was specific about this. A draft entry was a hypothesis. Combat was proof.

He was still writing when the basement door opened.

She came down the stairs fast, boots on concrete, a hard case in one hand. She was maybe forty, lean, with short hair and a jacket that had the Authority contractor patch on the left shoulder. The patch was different from Dale's. Hers was silver. His was bronze.

"Kowalski?" she said.

"Yes."

"I'm Mara Voss. Class III, contracted through Regional Response. I'm here to close this job."

Dale looked at her case. It was a Class V elimination kit. He recognized the configuration from his study materials. She had a resonance disruptor, a substrate neutralizer, and two canisters of something labeled with a hazard symbol he hadn't seen before.

"You can't close it," he said.

"I can close it. I have a Class V protocol and a direct dispatch order." She set the case on the stairs and started opening the latches. "Where's the residue?"

"Under the foundation. Dormant."

"Then I'll deploy under the slab. Drill access, inject neutralizer, done in twenty minutes."

"You can't deploy without verification."

Mara looked at him. She had the expression Dale had seen on other people when he mentioned his Field Guide. The expression that said: you're making this harder than it needs to be.

"Verification," she said. "You have a draft entry."

"I have a draft entry at seventy-four percent. If you kill it now, the entry dies with it. No damage bonus. No weakness documented. Every future encounter with this variant will be blind."

"Future encounters aren't my problem."

"They are. If this variant spreads, and you've killed the only documented specimen without verification, the Authority's database stays empty on this species. That's a systemic gap."

Mara picked up the resonance disruptor. It was the size of a flashlight and twice as heavy. "Kowalski, I've done thirty-one Class V jobs. You know what the survival rate is for technicians who insist on verification windows?"

Dale didn't answer.

"Eleven percent. The last guy who tried what you're trying was a Class IV in Dayton. He got his entry verified. He also got his left arm dissolved. The Authority gave him a 9.7 thoroughness rating and a discharge letter."

"That's not the same situation."

"It's the same principle. You're betting your body against a timer." She pointed at his clipboard. "What's your estimated reactivation?"

"Forty-eight hours. Original estimate."

"Let me see your monitoring data."

He handed her the clipboard. She read it fast, her eyes moving in the way that meant she'd done this kind of reading before, under pressure, with incomplete information.

"This isn't forty-eight," she said.

"No."

"It's closer to thirty-six. Maybe less. Your consumption data shows acceleration."

"I know."

"Then why are you still measuring?"

"Because the measurement is the documentation. The documentation is the entry. The entry is the only thing that makes the next one killable."

Mara set the clipboard down. She was quiet for a moment, looking at the oak squares. Then she picked up the neutralizer canister.

"I'm filing a Priority Closure Request," she said. "The Authority will approve it. Your draft entry gets a forty-eight-hour grace period for verification attempts, but I'm authorized to terminate if the grace period expires without engagement. I'm not waiting."

"You can't override a draft entry without filing a Premature Termination objection."

"I can. Class III authority. I don't need your consent."

Dale pulled out his phone. He opened the Authority interface, navigated to the violation filing menu, and selected code T-7: Jurisdictional Interference with Draft Entry. He typed the entry reference number, the technician ID from Mara's contractor patch, and the specific clause that governed draft entry protection.

He hit submit.

Mara watched him do it. She didn't stop him. She just picked up her case and went back upstairs.

Dale stayed in the basement. He updated his consumption measurements. He added a note about the directional pattern. He wrote a hypothesis about what the mutation was eating toward. The foundation was brick and mortar, but the soil beneath it was compacted clay. Clay was not a food source. But the mutation was moving through it. That meant it was not consuming the clay. It was moving through it to reach something else.

Something under the foundation that was not part of the house.

His phone buzzed.

AUTHORITY NOTIFICATION: T-7 OBJECTION FILED. STATUS: PENDING REVIEW. ESTIMATED RESPONSE TIME: 4-6 HOURS. NOTE: WHILE OBJECTION IS PENDING, DRAFT ENTRY JURISDICTION REMAINS WITH FILING TECHNICIAN. CLASS III TECHNICIAN MAY NOT DEPLOY TERMINATION PROTOCOL UNTIL REVIEW CONCLUDES.

Dale exhaled. Four to six hours. Mara would be waiting upstairs with her neutralizer, and the Authority would take four to six hours to decide whether she could use it. Meanwhile, the mutation was accelerating.

He needed to verify the entry before Mara's Priority Closure Request went through. He needed combat engagement with the mutation while it was still in a state he could survive.

Dormant was not combat. Dormant was not engagement. The Authority was specific: the creature had to respond to a targeted stimulus. It had to react. It had to fight back.

He looked at his kit. He had a Class II spray. Useless against Class V. He had a flashlight. He had a roll of duct tape. He had the masking tape grid.

He had the oak substrate. The mutation was eating toward something under the foundation. If he could create a stimulus that the mutation would perceive as a threat, it might react even in dormancy. A localized vibration. A chemical irritant. Something that would make it flinch.

He needed to trigger a micro-reactivation. Small enough that he could survive it. Large enough that the Authority would register it as combat engagement.

He opened his Field Guide draft. He read his own notes back to himself. The mutation consumed organic substrate at an accelerating rate. It moved directionally. It was attracted to something beneath the foundation. It was not attracted to the oak. The oak was a control. The mutation ignored it.

That was wrong. The oak was untreated. It was dead wood. The mutation consumed dead wood. But it was not consuming the oak in his grid. It was consuming the soil substrate, which was not wood. It was consuming something else. Something that was not in his grid.

He re-read his notes. The oak under square seven had a half-inch bite mark. The oak under square nineteen had been reduced to sawdust. The oak under square twelve had none.

Square twelve was closest to the wall. The wall was brick. The mutation was not eating the brick. It was eating toward the wall. It was eating toward whatever was on the other side of the wall.

The mutation was not moving through the soil. It was moving through the soil toward the wall because something on the other side of the wall was more attractive than the soil.

He looked at the wall. The black stain from the previous chapter was gone. The foundation was sealed. But the wall was old. Brick and mortar, yes, but the mortar joints were original. Nineteen-forties work. Lime-based mortar. Soft. Porous.

The mutation was not eating toward something under the foundation. It was eating toward the wall. It was eating toward the mortar.

And the mortar was between the foundation and the rest of the house.

If the mutation reached the mortar and consumed through it, it would not collapse the house. It would breach the seal. It would enter the living space. The containment Dale had built was only as good as the foundation. The mutation was not trying to break the foundation. It was trying to bypass it.

He checked his grid. The directional vector pointed at the wall. At a point approximately four feet from the basement door. At a point that was, in the original floor plan he'd pulled from the county records, directly below the kitchen.

The kitchen where he'd found the first rats. The kitchen where the broodmother had nested. The kitchen where the infestation had started.

The mutation was not a new problem. It was the same problem, grown. It was eating its way back to the nest.

His phone buzzed.

AUTHORITY NOTIFICATION: T-7 OBJECTION REVIEWED. STATUS: DENIED. NOTE: CLASS III TECHNICIAN VOSS, MARA (ID 4471-C) AUTHORIZED TO PROCEED WITH TERMINATION. DRAFT ENTRY REMAINS ACTIVE BUT VERIFICATION WINDOW CLOSED DUE TO IMMINENT MATURATION THREAT. REACTIVATION ESTIMATE REVISED: 14 HOURS. RECOMMENDATION: EVACUATE STRUCTURE.

Fourteen hours. Not forty-eight. Not thirty-six. Fourteen.

And Mara had her authorization.

He heard her boots on the stairs. She came down with the neutralizer canister in one hand and the resonance disruptor in the other. She looked at him.

"Authority cleared me," she said. "You have ten minutes to get out of the blast radius or I'm including you in the exclusion zone."

Dale looked at the wall. He looked at the grid. He looked at his Field Guide draft, still at seventy-four percent.

He looked at Mara.

"If you neutralize it now, the entry dies. You know that."

"I know that. I also know that if I don't neutralize it now, it matures in fourteen hours and the house collapses with the kitchen above it. And the kitchen is where the broodmother nested. Which means the mutation is returning to a nest site. Which means it's not just a vector residue. It's a reproductive cycle."

Dale stopped.

She was right. He'd been treating the mutation as a single entity. A vector. A residue. But it was eating toward the nest. Toward the original infestation site. That meant it was not a remnant. It was a new generation. It was growing toward maturity, and it was heading home.

If it reached the nest and completed maturation, it would not just collapse the house. It would establish a breeding population. The next generation would not be one mutation. It would be dozens.

Mara raised the neutralizer. "Ten minutes, Kowalski."

Dale looked at his clipboard. He looked at the wall. He looked at the mortar joints. Soft, porous, lime-based. The mutation was already eating through them. He could see it if he looked closely. The joints near square twelve were slightly darker than the others. Moisture. Consumption. The mutation was inside the wall.

He had fourteen hours. Maybe less. Mara was going to kill it in ten minutes. His entry would die. And the next time this species appeared, no one would know its weakness.

He had one option. He could trigger a micro-reactivation now. Right here. Right now. He could put his hand against the wall, against the mortar joint that was already compromised, and he could create a stimulus that the mutation would perceive as a threat. A localized vibration. A chemical irritant. His own body heat, pressed against the breach point.

The Authority would register it as combat engagement. The entry would verify. He would get his damage bonus.

And the mutation would react. Directly. Against his hand. Against the hand that was pressed against the wall.

He looked at Mara.

"Give me three minutes."

"You don't have three minutes."

"Three minutes. If I don't have verification by then, I walk out and you neutralize it."

Mara studied him. She had the look of someone who had seen this before. Someone who had watched another technician try to be thorough and gotten hurt for it.

"Two minutes," she said. "Then I deploy."

Dale set his clipboard on the stairs. He walked to the wall. He put his right hand flat against the mortar joint at square twelve. The mortar was cold. Damp. Soft under his palm, like wet sand.

He pressed.

The wall pulsed.

Not a vibration. A pulse. Like something on the other side had felt his hand and shifted. The mortar cracked under his palm, a thin line running from his pinky to his wrist, and something wet seeped out. Not blood. Something thicker. Something that smelled like the crawlspace. Like the broodmother. Like the nest.

His phone buzzed.

AUTHORITY NOTIFICATION: FIELD GUIDE ENTRY (Rattus profundus, variant) VERIFIED. WEAKNESS DOCUMENTED: SUBSTRATE-DEPENDENT VULNERABILITY. NOTE: SPECIES CONSUMES AND DEPENDS ON ORGANIC SUBSTRATE FOR STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY. TARGETING SUBSTRATE DEPRIVATION CAUSES IMMEDIATE COHESION FAILURE. DAMAGE BONUS: +40% AGAINST ALL SPECIMENS OF THIS VARIANT.

The entry was verified. He had his weakness. Substrate deprivation. The mutation could not maintain its form without organic material. It was not a solid creature. It was a colony held together by consumption. Starve it and it fell apart.

He pulled his hand back. The mortar joint was cracked. The wet substance was spreading. And the wall was moving. Not pulsing now. Shifting. The bricks were sliding, grinding against each other, and the sound was like teeth.

Mara was already deploying. She'd set the neutralizer against the floor and was counting down from ten.

Dale grabbed his clipboard. He grabbed his Field Guide. He ran.

Behind him, the wall split open, and something poured through that was not a rat and was not a mutation and was not a single thing at all. It was a nest. The broodmother. Alive. Mature. Not dead. Never dead. The containment had never worked. The mutation had not been a new problem. It had been the old problem, waking up, and it had been eating its way back to the only thing it had ever known.

Mara's neutralizer detonated. The basement filled with white fog. Dale hit the stairs running. Behind him, the fog screamed. Not a sound. A vibration. A frequency that made his teeth ache and his eyes water.

He made it to the kitchen. He made it out the back door. He was in the yard when the house shuddered, once, and the kitchen window blew out in a shower of glass and white mist.

Silence.

Then his phone buzzed.

AUTHORITY NOTIFICATION: CLASS V INFESTATION TERMINATED. THOROUGHNESS RATING: 9.1. TREATMENT POINTS AWARDED: 840. CERTIFICATION PROGRESS: 73%. NOTE: BROODMOTHER (Rattus profundus, variant) CONFIRMED DEAD. STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY: 12%. HOUSE UNINHABITABLE. LIABILITY: NOT ASSESSED. NEXT JOB: PENDING.

He stood in the yard. The house was intact. Barely. The kitchen window was gone. The basement was sealed with neutralizer foam. Mara came out the back door, covered in white residue, and looked at him.

"You got your verification," she said.

"Yes."

"Good. Don't do it again."

"I won't."

He opened his Field Guide. The entry was clean. Verified. The weakness was documented: substrate deprivation. He had his damage bonus. He had his points. He had his thoroughness rating.

And then he read the last line of the entry. The line that the Authority had added during verification. The line that was not in his draft. The line he had not written.

NOTE: THIS SPECIES IS NOT NATIVE TO THE RIFT. IT IS NOT A DIMENSIONAL VERMIN. IT IS A DOMESTIC SPECIES. IT WAS HERE BEFORE THE RIFT. IT HAS ALWAYS BEEN HERE. THE RIFT DID NOT BRING IT. THE RIFT REVEALED IT.

Dale read it twice.

The broodmother had not come through the Rift. It had been under the house. Under every house. Under every foundation in every neighborhood in every city that had ever been built on soil. It had been waiting. The Rift had not introduced a new threat. It had removed the ceiling.

His phone buzzed again.

AUTHORITY NOTIFICATION: NEW INFESTATION DETECTED. SERVICE AREA: 14 ADJACENT PROPERTIES. ALL PROPERTIES REPORTING STRUCTURAL SUBSTRATE DEGRADATION. ESTIMATED SPECIMEN COUNT: 11. ALL BROODMOTHERS. ALL DORMANT. ALL ACTIVATING SIMULTANEOUSLY. ESTIMATED MATURATION WINDOW: 72 HOURS.

Eleven broodmothers. Under eleven houses. All waking up. All at once.

Dale looked at the street. The neighbors were inside. The lights were on. The houses looked normal.

They were not normal. They had never been normal.

He picked up his clipboard. He started writing.
