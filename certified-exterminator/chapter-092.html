Chapter 92: Unlicensed Southbound

# Chapter 92

The rain on the windshield sounded like static. Dale Kowalski kept his eyes on the road, his hands at ten and two, his posture rigid. The van smelled of wet dog, old coffee grounds, and the coppery tang of dried blood that no amount of ventilation could scrub away.

He was thirty miles south of the capital, in a stretch of rural Ohio that the maps still called farmland but which the satellite feeds marked as a grey blur. The Rift had widened. The atmospheric pressure was dropping, creating a low-hum vibration that rattled the dashboards of every car on the highway.

Dale’s phone was dead. The license plate on his van was covered in mud and duct tape, and he had no backup, no Authority support, and no official right to be here.

But he had the map.

It wasn’t a digital map, and was a hand-drawn schematic of the county’s drainage system, taped to the passenger seat.

Dale had memorized it, and he knew that if the infestation was spreading via the water table, it would follow the old mill channels. He knew because he’d spent fifteen years treating for drain flies in industrial complexes. You didn’t need magic to know where the sewage pooled.

You just needed to know where the pipes broke.

A red light flashed in his peripheral vision. Not the Authority’s blue interface. This was a drone.

A quadcopter buzzed over the van’s roof, its camera lens dilating; a voice boomed from external speakers, distorted by wind and rain.

“Unregistered vehicle. Pull over. State troopers en route.”

Dale didn’t slow down. He checked his rearview mirror.

The drone hovered, maintaining distance.

It wasn’t a police drone. The casing was matte black, unmarked. Private sector.

Probably the same private security firm that had supplied the Enforcers who’d failed to contain the Governor’s Aide.

He reached under his seat and pulled out his Field Guide, and the physical book, leather-bound, thick with pages he’d filled himself. He flipped to the back. The entry he’d written for *Subject: Dale Kowalski* was still there, but the system had updated it.

**[SUBJECT: DALE KOWALSKI]** **[STATUS: UNLICENSED OPERATOR.]** **[THREAT LEVEL: VARIABLE.]** **[WEAKNESS: BUREAUCRATIC RIGIDITY.]** **[NOTE: TARGET PRIORITIZES PROTOCOL OVER SURVIVAL.]**

Dale stared at the last line. They were trying to predict his behavior. They thought he would stop for a code violation. They thought he would pull over to inspect the drone for non-compliance.

He didn’t pull over. He accelerated.

The drone followed. A second one joined it. Then a third.

They weren’t shooting. They were tracking.

Dale knew what that meant, and they weren’t trying to kill him.

They were trying to find where he was going. They wanted the nest.

They wanted to claim the credit, or worse, they wanted to bury the evidence of the Governor’s infection before it went public.

He turned off the highway, taking a gravel access road that led into the woods. The suspension groaned. The drone lights cut through the rain like searchlights.

Dale slammed the brakes. The van skidded, mud spraying against the undercarriage.

He jumped out, boots splashing in the slush, and he didn’t run; walked to the back of the van and opened the cargo doors.

Inside were his tools. Not weapons. Tools.

Sprayers with chemical tanks.

Bait stations. Sealant foam.

A high-pressure washer.

And a clipboard.

He grabbed a canister of industrial-grade insecticide, modified with a binding agent he’d synthesized from the acid beetle glands he’d harvested last week. It wasn’t approved. It was volatile. It was perfect.

The drones hovered ten feet above him, rotating to face him. The voice came again, closer now.

“Stand down. Surrender the device.”

Dale looked up. He raised his hand, holding the canister.

“Device?” he said. His voice was flat, tired.

“This is a treatment agent.

Class IV solvent.

It’s for wood-boring pests. You’re in violation of airspace regulations for a commercial operation.

I’m going to have to write you up.”

The drones didn’t speak. They just watched.

Then, the ground beneath the gravel road shifted.

A tremor. Not an earthquake.

A movement.

Something large was moving under the soil, pushing up toward the surface. The gravel cracked. Dirt boiled.

From the fissure, a claw emerged. Not a hand. A chitinous limb, serrated and black, dripping with mucus.

The drones dipped lower, confused. Their programming didn’t have a protocol for subterranean mega-fauna. They were expecting a man with a gun. They got a monster.

Dale didn’t panic. He opened his Field Guide.

He scanned the creature as it pulled itself fully out of the earth, which was the size of a minivan. Six legs.

A segmented carapace that shimmered with an oily sheen.

Eyes like cracked obsidian.

It was a *Chthonic Skitterer*.

Dale had seen sketches of them in the old Authority manuals, but never in the wild. They were classified as Class-5 infestations. They nested in deep aquifers. They ate concrete foundations and the people living in them.

The creature shrieked, a sound like grinding metal. It turned its head toward the drones.

The drones fired. Small-caliber rounds sparked off the Skitterer’s shell.

It didn’t flinch.

It lunged, swatting two of the drones out of the air.

They exploded in showers of sparks and plastic.

The third drone pulled back, hovering higher, recording the carnage.

Dale watched. He noted the weak point. The joints. The soft tissue between the plates.

He also noted something else. The Skitterer wasn’t attacking randomly. It was following the scent trail.

The scent of the infected Governor’s Aide. The pheromones of the self-feeding loop.

Dale had been exposed to that pheromone. It was on his clothes. It was in his hair.

The Skitterer turned. It looked at Dale.

For a second, they locked eyes. Dale felt the familiar spike of fear, the urge to run. He suppressed it. He checked his workspace.

The ground was uneven. The rain was making the soil slippery. He was standing in a depression. If the Skitterer lunged, he would be pinned.

Protocol.

Isolate.

Dale backed away, slowly. He moved toward the van. The Skitterer took a step forward. Its legs clicked against the gravel.

Dale didn’t fight it. He ran to the van and slammed the doors shut. He locked the handles.

The Skitterer approached the van. It tapped on the metal side with one claw. *Clang.*

It was testing the perimeter.

Dale sat in the driver’s seat. He didn’t start the engine. He pulled out his clipboard.

He started writing.

*Report 92-A: Encounter with Chthonic Skitterer. Location: Sector 4, Gravel Road. Hostile Agent: Unregistered Drone Surveillance.*

He paused. He listened. The Skitterer was sniffing the tires. It was trying to find a way in.

Dale reached for the radio in the van. It wasn’t connected to the Authority network; was connected to a shortwave frequency he’d picked up on a scavenged receiver. A local ham radio operator, a guy named Miller who lived in a bunker three miles east.

Miller was a conspiracy theorist. He believed the Rift was a hoax. But he had a transmitter powerful enough to reach the military bands.

Dale keyed the mic.

“Miller, this is Dale. Do you copy?”

Static. Then, a voice. Scratchy. Skeptical.

“Dale? You’re still alive? I saw the news. They said you were dead.”

“I’m not dead,” Dale said.

“I’m working. I have a Class-5 Skitterer on my van. It’s following the pheromone trail from the Aide.

It’s coming from the south.

It’s moving toward the reservoir.”

A long silence followed.

“The reservoir? That’s the drinking water for half the state.”

“It nests in the water table,” Dale said.

“If it reaches the intake pumps, it will contaminate the supply. The Authority knows. They’re not stopping it because they’re trying to contain the political fallout from the Aide.

They want it to die quietly.”

The Skitterer raked its claws down the side of the van. The metal screeched.

Miller’s voice came back, lower. “What do you want me to do?”

“I need you to broadcast a warning.

Code Blue.

Infestation event. Sector 4.

But I need you to add a tag. Authority Override. Class 9.”

Miller laughed, a harsh, bitter sound. “Class 9?

That’s for world-ending threats.

You can’t just fake a Class 9 tag. The system will reject it. It requires biometric confirmation from a licensed Level 9 technician.”

Dale looked at his hands. The scar on his chest was burning. He looked at the Field Guide.

He didn’t have a Level 9 license. He didn’t have a Level 8. He had a revoked Level 2.

But he had something else, and he had the Authority’s own database. He had the entry for himself.

**[WEAKNESS: BUREAUCRATIC RIGIDITY.]**

The System rewarded thoroughness. It rewarded adherence to protocol. And it punished cross-contamination.

Dale opened the Field Guide to the entry for the *Chthonic Skitterer*. He hadn’t identified it yet. He had only observed it.

But the entry for *Dale Kowalski* was already populated. And it contained a note.

*[NOTE: TARGET PRIORITIZES PROTOCOL OVER SURVIVAL.]*

Dale looked at the drone that was still hovering, recording everything.

He stepped out of the van.

The Skitterer froze. It was waiting for him.

Dale walked toward it. He didn’t spray. He didn’t attack. He held up his clipboard.

He spoke to the drone.

“Record this,” he said.

“I am initiating a Cross-Contamination Protocol. I am deploying a Class-4 Solvent against a Class-5 Entity.

I am accepting the 40% debuff penalty.

I am accepting the 24-hour Field Guide contamination.”

The drone’s camera zoomed in. It couldn’t process the statement. It was just recording.

Dale turned to the Skitterer.

“Identify,” he said.

The System pinged. A red box appeared in his vision.

**[IDENTIFICATION FAILED. CREATURE NOT IN FIELD GUIDE.]**

Dale ignored the error. He opened his pen.

He sketched the creature live, recording its leg count.

The shell pattern. The eye structure.

He didn’t stop. He drew as the creature moved. He documented as it attacked.

The Skitterer lunged. Dale sidestepped. The claw hit the van, denting the roof.

Dale wrote. *Legs: 6. Shell: Oily. Eyes: Compound.*

He drew the weakness. The joints.

As he finished the sketch, the System flickered.

**[FIELD GUIDE ENTRY CREATED: CHTHONIC SKITTERER (PROVISIONAL).]** **[WEAKNESS IDENTIFIED: ARTICULATION JOINTS.]** **[DAMAGE BONUS: +15%.]**

Dale raised his spray canister. He aimed not at the head, but at the front left leg joint.

He fired.

The solvent hit the joint. It sizzled. The creature screamed, not in pain, but in confusion. The chemical reaction caused the chitin to soften.

Dale fired again. And again.

He wasn’t trying to kill it. He was trying to disable its mobility.

The drone watched.

Then, Dale did something reckless. He took his phone, the broken one, and threw it into the air; it landed on the ground, screen facing up.

“Miller,” Dale shouted. “I’m broadcasting my Field Guide update. It’s in the public domain. Read it.”

Miller, in his bunker, was monitoring the drone feed. He saw Dale throw the phone. He saw the screen light up, displaying the sketch of the Skitterer.

The drone, designed to record incidents for the Authority, captured the image.

But the drone wasn’t just recording. It was transmitting.

To the Authority.

And the Authority’s automated audit system was watching.

Dale stood in the rain, breathing hard. The Skitterer was struggling, its leg buckling.

He looked at the drone.

“I have identified the species,” Dale said. “I have documented the weakness. I have initiated treatment. This is a valid extermination protocol.”

He paused.

“The Authority’s Thoroughness Scale requires full documentation before elimination. I have provided the documentation. Now, the Authority must act.

If it doesn’t, it violates its own bylaws regarding public safety threats. It cannot let a Class-5 infestation reach the water supply while an unlicensed technician is actively treating it.”

He was gambling. He was betting that the Authority’s bureaucracy was more powerful than its secrecy. He was betting that the automated systems, which were bound by rules, would override the human officers who wanted to cover up the Aide’s infection.

The drone hovered. It didn’t move.

Then, a new alert flashed in Dale’s vision. Not red. Gold.

**[SYSTEM ALERT: AUTOMATED AUDIT TRIGGERED.]** **[SUBJECT: CHTHONIC SKITTERER.]** **[STATUS: CONFIRMED THREAT.]** **[ACTION: INTERVENTION REQUIRED.]**

The drone’s lights turned from black to gold.

Its speaker crackled. A new voice. Not the private security guard. An automated, monotone voice.

“License status: Invalid. Operator: Dale Kowalski. Action: Valid. Thoroughness Rating: Pending.”

Disoriented by the solvent, the Skitterer dragged itself toward the tree line. It was retreating.

Dale didn’t chase it. He watched it go. He needed to verify.

He walked back to the van. He picked up his phone. The screen was cracked, but the display worked.

He looked at his Field Guide.

**[SUBJECT: DALE KOWALSKI]** **[STATUS: UNLICENSED OPERATOR.]** **[INTERIM RATING: 8.9/10.]** **[REASON: HIGH THOROUGHNESS, NON-COMPLIANT METHOD.]**

He had survived, and he had forced the System’s hand; the Authority’s automated watchdogs had seen the threat and registered his action. For the next twenty-four hours, he couldn’t use the Skitterer entry to gain damage bonuses, because it was contaminated by his unauthorized use.

But he had exposed the nest.

And he had exposed the drones.

The gold light faded from the drone. It turned and flew away, faster than before.

Dale sat in the driver’s seat. He started the engine. The van coughed, then roared to life.

He checked the map. The Skitterer was heading toward the reservoir. But it wasn’t alone.

On the back of the map, in the corner he’d been ignoring, was a note from Miller.

*“Dale. If the Authority is watching, look at the sky. Not the drones. The rift.”*

Dale looked up. Through the rain, through the trees, he could see the purple hue of the Rift in the distance.

But it wasn’t just purple anymore.

Shapes lay within it. Not monsters. Structures.

Towers.

The Rift wasn’t just leaking creatures. It was building a city.

And the Authority wasn’t just ignoring the infestation. They were letting the city grow.

Dale gripped the steering wheel. He had forced the System to acknowledge him. He made the Authority acknowledge the problem.

But he hadn’t fixed it, and he had only opened the door.

And on the other side of that door, something was waiting.

His phone buzzed. A text message. From an unknown number.

*You broke protocol. The Authority is coming. Run.*

Dale looked at the road. South. Toward the reservoir. Toward the towers.

He shifted into drive, and he didn’t run. He accelerated.

He had a job to do. And the Authority was going to have to catch him to stop it.

They were already behind him.
