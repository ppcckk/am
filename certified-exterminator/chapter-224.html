Chapter 224: Uncertified Nest

# Chapter 224

The marker cap was missing. Dale found it wedged between two ribs of the collapsed support beam, a small blue cylinder of plastic that had survived the reboot without a scratch. He twisted it onto the body of the pen with a click that sounded obscenely loud in the vacuum of the tunnel.

*WORK IN PROGRESS.*

The words glowed faintly in the dark, a chemical luminescence from the ink itself reacting to the residual rift energy. It wasn't a notification.

It wasn't a status update.

It was a claim. A physical, tangible assertion of jurisdiction in a space where the digital laws had just evaporated.

Dale stepped forward.

The floor beneath his boots was slick, coated in a viscous, translucent slime that had once been the Authority's data-streams. He didn't slip.

He tested the friction coefficient with his heel, noted the viscosity, and adjusted his gait.

Fourteen years of slip-and-fall liability training kicked in before panic could.

The silence was the worst part.

Usually, the tunnels hummed. The servers beneath the city generated a low-frequency drone that Dale had learned to ignore, like the white noise of a refrigerator.

Now, the air was dead.

No cooling fans.

No data packets rushing through fiber-optic cables. Just the drip of condensation and the distant, wet sound of something shifting in the dark.

His Field Guide was gone. The tablet in his hand was a black rectangle, inert glass. He tapped the screen anyway, a reflex from a decade of logging inspections. *Tap.

Tap. Tap.* Nothing.

He shoved the dead tablet into his vest pocket. It served as a paperweight now. A reminder of the rules that had just been repealed.

Ten feet ahead, the tunnel narrowed into a junction.

Three paths branched off. The left path sloped upward, leading toward the residential district. The right path descended into the bedrock, where the heavy machinery of the city's utility grid lay dormant.

The center path continued straight, deeper into the anomaly.

A drone lay in the center path. Not dead, exactly. It had fallen during the reboot, its rotors tangled in a web of fungal filaments that had grown overnight in the absence of sanitation protocols. It twitched, a single optical sensor flickering red, then green, then red.

Dale approached it slowly. He didn't draw a weapon.

He didn't have one, besides the chitin shard in his hand and the empty canister on his belt.

He reached into his vest and pulled out a blank inspection form. Paper. Real paper, from the emergency supply kit he carried for when the digital systems failed.

They always failed, eventually.

He held the form up. The drone's sensor focused on it.

"Inspection," Dale said. His voice was steady, flat. The voice he used with tenants who wanted to argue about lease violations. "State your species designation and current infestation status."

The drone did not speak.

It couldn't.

It was a machine, not a monster. But the fungus wrapped around it pulsed.

A vein of black mold throbbed, syncing with the drone's flickering light.

Dale wrote on the form: *Subject: Automated Surveillance Unit. Compromised by Fungal Host.*

He tore the sheet off the clipboard and tossed it onto the drone. The paper landed on the rotors. The drone's light stopped flickering. It went dark.

The fungus retracted slightly, as if satisfied.

Dale moved past it.

The air grew colder. The smell changed.

The metallic tang of ozone faded, replaced by the cloying, sweet rot of decomposition.

This was not a bug infestation. This was a necrotic bloom.

He reached the first intersection. A pile of debris blocked the left path. Chitin plates, shattered glass, and the twisted remains of a city bus. Something had come up from the depths recently.

Dale crouched.

He pulled a pen from his pocket and sketched. Not a map.

A diagram.

He drew the debris pile. He marked the entry points. He noted the direction of the drag marks.

*Violation Code: K-7 (Unsanctioned Transit Corridor).* *Severity: Critical.* *Action: Quarantine.*

He stood up. He couldn't clear the debris.

He didn't have the tools.

But he could document it.

He could create a paper trail that the Authority, if it ever came back online, would be forced to acknowledge. If the Authority returned and found no record of this blockage, it would classify the area as 'Clear.' If Dale marked it 'Blocked,' the Authority would have to send a crew.

And if the Authority sent a crew, they would bring backup.

And backup meant protection.

He moved to the right path. The descent was steep.

The walls were lined with root-like structures that pulsed with a faint, bioluminescent blue.

These were not fungal. These were neural. The city's infrastructure had been hijacked.

The power grid was now a nervous system.

A voice echoed down the shaft.

"Kowalski?"

Dale froze. He recognized the voice. It was distorted, layered with static, but it was unmistakable.

It was Miller. The junior associate who had failed his Level II practicals because he couldn't maintain a 90% Thoroughness Rating. He had been fired two days ago for falsifying a rodent count.

Miller stepped out from behind a support pillar. He was covered in a fine dust of gray ash.

His eyes were wide, dilated.

He held a wrench in one hand and a broken flashlight in the other. The flashlight was off.

"You're not licensed," Miller said. His voice cracked. "I checked the registry before the crash. Your license was suspended for... for unauthorized experimentation."

Dale straightened. He kept his hands visible. "The registry is down, Miller."

"I don't care about the registry." Miller stepped closer. He pointed the wrench at Dale's chest.

"I care about the payout.

You triggered the reboot.

You're the whistleblower. That means you get the compensation package when it comes back.

I saw the text on my screen before it died. *Liability: Assigned to Central Authority.*"

"It was a system error," Dale said. "I didn't trigger it. I documented it."

"You wrote on the wall."

"I wrote a status update. Standard procedure for ongoing hazards."

Miller laughed. It was a dry, hacking sound.

"You think you're smarter than us?

You think because you carry a clipboard you're the boss? We're off the grid, Dale. There are no points.

No levels.

Just whoever is left standing when the lights come back on."

Miller swung the wrench. It wasn't a wild swing. It was a calculated blow aimed at Dale's knee. A strike to disable, not to kill.

Dale stepped back. He didn't raise his hands. He raised his clipboard.

The wrench hit the hard plastic edge of the clipboard. The impact jarred Dale's arm, but the tool held. The metal of the wrench dented the plastic.

Dale didn't counter-attack. He stepped forward. He walked into Miller's guard.

"Stay back," Miller said.

He pulled a knife from his belt. A utility blade. Sharp.

Cheap.

"I have a question," Dale said. He kept walking. Slowly. Deliberately.

Miller faltered.

The uncertainty in Dale's movement threw him off.

Pest control technicians didn't fight. They assessed.

They contained. They didn't charge.

"What question?" Miller whispered.

"Why did you fail your Level II practicals?"

Miller blinked. The knife lowered an inch. "What?"

"The practicals," Dale said. "You failed because you killed the target before isolating it.

You didn't verify the nest.

You didn't document the entry points. You got a Thoroughness score of 4.1. Did you know that?"

"I... I had to kill it fast. It was attacking a resident."

"The resident was safe.

The drone had already secured the perimeter. You acted outside protocol.

You created a liability." Dale stopped.

He was now within arm's reach of Miller.

"I was saving lives!"

"You were creating a violation," Dale said. "And now you're violating it again.

By attacking a licensed technician.

Even if the license is suspended.

The act of assault is a Class A infraction. It carries a permanent ban from the Authority."

Miller looked at the knife. Then at Dale. Then at the dead drone behind Dale.

"You can't ban me," Miller said. "There's no Authority."

"There is," Dale said. He pointed to the wall. "Read it."

Miller looked at the wall where Dale had written *WORK IN PROGRESS.* Below it, in smaller letters, Dale had written: *INFRACTION LOG: ASSAULT ON CLASS II TECH. WITNESS: SELF.*

Miller stared at the writing. He looked at the chitin shard in Dale's hand. He looked at the empty canister.

"You didn't use a protocol," Miller said. "You used a pen."

"I used the system," Dale said. "The system is the paper, Miller. The system is the record.

You can turn off the servers. You can kill the drones.

But you can't erase the log."

Miller's face twisted. He raised the knife again. "I'll make sure there's no record."

He lunged.

Dale didn't dodge. He dropped to one knee. He swept his leg across Miller's ankles.

A low, efficient takedown.

The kind Dale used when a cockroach tried to run under a cabinet.

Miller hit the floor hard. The wrench clattered away. The knife skittered into the darkness.

Dale stood up. He didn't strike Miller. He pulled a zip-tie from his vest. It was a standard containment tie, rated for 500 pounds of force.

He knelt. He pulled Miller's wrists together. He tightened the tie.

"Stay down," Dale said.

He took out his marker again. He wrote on the back of Miller's shirt, in big block letters.

*DETAINED.* *REASON: ASSAULT.* *STATUS: AWAITING REVIEW.*

He left Miller bound and gagged with his own shirt sleeve. He didn't wait for an apology. He didn't wait for forgiveness. He walked past him, toward the deeper tunnels.

He needed to get to the Nexus. The central hub of the Authority's physical servers. If the reboot was a software glitch, it could be fixed. But if the Nexus was infected, the problem was biological.

And Dale was good at fixing biological problems.

The tunnel widened.

The walls were no longer concrete. They were organic. The stone had been replaced by a dense, web-like mesh of black fibers.

They pulsed in time with Dale's heartbeat.

He could feel them watching him. Not with eyes. With sensors.

He pulled out his clipboard. He flipped to a new page.

He wrote.

*Species: Unknown. Morphology: Neural-Net Hybrid. Behavior: Reactive. Threat Level: Pending.*

He paused. He looked at the ceiling. The green code was gone. But the black veins remained.

He realized something.

The Authority hadn't just been rebooted. It had been *inoculated*.

Something had introduced a pathogen into the system. A virus that looked like code.

And that virus was still active.

He could see it in the way the fibers moved. They were replicating. Spreading.

He had no Field Guide. He had no protocols. He had no points.

But he had a marker. And he had a clipboard.

He turned the page.

At the top, he wrote: *NEW VIOLATION: DIGITAL PATHOGEN.*

Underneath, he wrote: *PROTOCOL PROPOSAL: QUARANTINE THE SOURCE.*

He looked down the tunnel.

The darkness was no longer empty.

It was full. Full of millions of tiny, glowing threads, weaving themselves into a new shape.

A new monster. One that didn't have a certification level. One that didn't have a weakness in the Field Guide.

Because it wasn't a monster.

It was the system itself, trying to survive.

Dale clicked his pen.

He stepped into the nest.
