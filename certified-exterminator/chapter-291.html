Chapter 291: Compliance Form Submission

# Chapter 291

The arena floor wasn't stone. It was a grid of glowing amber tiles, each pulsing with a rhythm that matched Kowalski's own heartbeat. The Rift Architect sat on its throne of fused chitin, twelve feet tall, its face a smooth, featureless oval of bone. It held a staff topped with a spinning geometric shape that hurt to look at directly.

Kowalski stepped onto the nearest tile. The Authority's interface flickered in his peripheral vision.

**TRIAL PARAMETERS: COMBAT.** **TIME LIMIT: 00:00:00 (NULL).** **OBJECTIVE: SUBMIT COMPLIANCE FORM 77-B.**

"Form?" Vane's voice cracked from behind the pillar. "What form? We're fighting a god!"

Kowalski didn't answer. He reached into his jacket pocket and pulled out a crumpled receipt for a hardware store trip three days ago. He smoothed it out on his clipboard. The paper was thin, fragile, and mundane.

The Architect leaned forward. The air pressure dropped. A low hum vibrated in Kowalski's molars.

"I am initiating the inspection," Kowalski said. His voice was steady, practiced. It was the voice he used for homeowners who thought a single ant meant the end of the world.

The Architect raised a claw. A beam of violet light lanced toward Kowalski's chest.

Kowalski didn't dodge. He sidestepped, not with speed, but with the precise, economical movement of a man stepping around a pile of trash bags. The beam scorched the air where he had been a millisecond before.

"Improper discharge," Kowalski noted, scribbling on the receipt. "Unshielded energy emission. Violation of Subsection 4, Clause C."

The Architect paused. The violet light sputtered.

"It's listening," Vane whispered.

"It's logging," Kowalski corrected. He took another step forward. The grid tile beneath him flashed red. **WARNING: PROXIMITY TO UNLICENSED ENTITY.**

"Hey!" A new voice boomed across the arena. It wasn't Vane.

Kowalski turned. Standing at the edge of the grid, arms crossed, was a man in polished white armor. His helmet was sealed, but his posture screamed arrogance. A glowing badge on his chest read: *HIGH INQUISITOR MARCUS VEX.*

"Step away from the entity, pest," Vex shouted. "You are contaminating the sacred space."

Kowalski looked at Vex. Then he looked at the Architect. Then back at Vex.

"I'm in the middle of an active service call," Kowalski said. "Please hold your line. I'm in the middle of documentation."

Vex stepped onto the grid. The tiles beneath him turned a hostile, angry red. **UNAUTHORIZED PERSONNEL DETECTED.**

"You're an idiot," Vex said, drawing a sword that crackled with lightning. "That thing will flay you alive.

That's why the Authority kills you for engaging.

It's not a trial. It's a culling.

"

"The Authority accepted my appeal," Kowalski said. He pointed his clipboard at the Architect. "Look at the interface."

Vex glanced up, and he saw the **APPEAL ACCEPTED** text. His face twisted in confusion.

"A technicality.

A glitch."

"Glitches don't change reality," Kowalski said. "Codes do."

The Architect shifted. It dropped its staff. The geometric shape dissolved into a swarm of tiny, glowing insects. They buzzed around the throne, forming a defensive barrier.

"Phase two," Vex muttered. "It's escalating. Run, Dale. Now."

"No running," Kowalski said. "Running spreads the infestation."

He raised his hand again. This time, he didn't signal for a pause. He made a pinching motion with his thumb and forefinger.

**COMMAND: QUARANTINE ZONE ESTABLISHED.**

A transparent bubble of force snapped into existence around the Architect. The insect swarm bounced off the walls of the bubble, confused.

Vex stared. "You... you created a barrier? With a clipboard?"

"It's not a barrier," Kowalski said. "It's a containment unit.

And I'm not creating it.

I'm *requesting* it.

Based on the precedent set in Chapter 142 regarding Class-4 dimensional breaches.

"

Vex's eyes widened behind his visor.

"You cite precedent? Against a Rift Architect?"

"I cite *law*," Kowalski said. He wrote on the receipt. "Exhibit A.

Unauthorized containment of hostile biological material. Penalty: Immediate suspension of powers.

"

The Architect slammed a claw against the bubble. The force field rippled but held. The creature let out a shriek that sounded like grinding metal.

"It's getting annoyed," Vane called out. "Dale, what are you doing?"

"Building a case," Kowalski said. He turned to Vex. "You're standing in a restricted zone. Move."

Vex laughed.

A harsh, barking sound.

"I am an Inquisitor. I have higher clearance than you, technician.

I have Level IV protocols.

I can dissolve that bubble with a snap of my fingers.

"

"Show me the license," Kowalski said.

Vex hesitated.

"What?"

"Show me your license. If you're going to interfere with an active extermination protocol, you need to show your credentials. Otherwise, you're a civilian obstruction of justice."

Vex stepped closer to the bubble.

"I don't need to show you anything. I'm here to kill it."

"You're here to *execute* it," Kowalski corrected. "The Authority doesn't allow execution without a Thoroughness Rating of 8.0 or higher. Your rating is 4.2. I checked it when you walked in."

Vex froze. "How?"

"I looked at your badge," Kowalski said. "The color indicates your tier. The font size indicates your experience level. You're a novice with a power fantasy."

Vex's face reddened. He raised his sword. Lightning arced from the blade, striking the edge of Kowalski's containment bubble. The energy dissipated harmlessly.

"Your interference is causing a cross-contamination penalty," Kowalski said. "Look at your stats."

Vex looked down at his arm. A red warning flashed: **CROSS-CONTAMINATION ERROR. DEBUFF: 40% WEAKNESS TO DIMENSIONAL MATERIALS.**

"My sword is useless," Vex whispered. The arrogance drained from his voice, replaced by genuine panic. "It's not working."

"It's not useless," Kowalski said.

"It's misapplied. You're trying to cut a bureaucrat with a weapon designed for beasts. The Authority recognizes that as a code violation.

Hence the debuff.

"

Kowalski walked toward the throne. The Architect was pounding on the bubble, its faceless head tilting in frustration.

"Stay back," Vex warned. "It's breaking the containment."

"It's testing the perimeter," Kowalski said. "Standard behavior for a territorial entity. It's looking for a weakness in the logic."

He stopped five feet from the throne. He held out the receipt.

"I am submitting my report," Kowalski said. "It is brief. It is precise. It outlines your violations: unauthorized entry, unshielded energy discharge, and failure to adhere to dimensional sanitation protocols."

The Architect stopped pounding. It leaned forward, peering at the piece of paper.

"What happens if it eats the paper?" Vane asked.

"It can't," Kowalski said. "It's a document. Documents are immutable in the Authority's view. Unless you violate the filing order."

The Architect extended a long, spindly finger. It touched the edge of the receipt.

**DOCUMENT RECEIVED.** **REVIEWING...**

The arena went silent. Even the hum of the grid seemed to pause.

Vex lowered his sword, his hands shaking.

"What is it doing?"

"Checking for errors," Kowalski said.

"If there's a typo, the appeal is rejected.

If there's a missing signature, the appeal is rejected. I signed it in the bottom right corner.

In blue ink."

The Architect pulled its hand back. The receipt floated in the air, glowing with a soft white light.

**REVIEW COMPLETE.** **VIOLATIONS CONFIRMED: 3.** **PENALTY ASSESSED.**

A new text box appeared in Kowalski's vision.

**YOU ARE AWARDED: 1,000 TREATMENT POINTS.** **YOU ARE GRANTED: TEMPORARY AUTHORITY OVERRIDE (CLASS II).** **RIFT ARCHITECT IS NOW UNDER ADMINISTRATIVE CUSTODY.**

The Architect slumped. Its posture changed instantly.

The aggression vanished, replaced by a slouch of defeat.

It reached up and pulled off its crown. It held it out to Kowalski.

Kowalski didn't take it. He pointed at the floor.

"Drop it."

The Architect dropped the crown. It clattered onto the grid.

Vex stared at Kowalski.

"You just... you just beat it. With a receipt."

"I filed a complaint," Kowalski said. "The Authority punished it."

"That's not how combat works," Vex said, his voice hollow. "That's not how the system works."

"The system is a bureaucracy," Kowalski said. "You just forgot to read the handbook."

Vex looked at the Architect. The creature was shrinking, its massive form condensing into a small, tight ball of black smoke. It was being compressed by the Authority's will.

"What happens to it now?" Vane asked, stepping out from behind the pillar.

"Processing," Kowalski said. "It's being sent to the backlog. It won't die. It will wait."

Vex dropped his sword.

It landed with a clang.

"I have to report this.

The High Council.

They'll want to know how a Level I technician bypassed the combat protocols.

"

Kowalski picked up the crown. It was heavy, warm, and pulsing with residual energy. He placed it on his clipboard.

"Report what?" Kowalski asked.

"That you broke the rules."

"I followed them," Kowalski said. "You didn't."

Vex opened his mouth to argue, but a sound interrupted him. A deep, resonant bell tolled from the sky above the Rift.

The grid tiles turned a violent, flashing crimson.

**ALERT: SYSTEM INTEGRITY COMPROMISED.** **SOURCE: UNKNOWN.** **NEW THREAT DETECTED: CERTIFICATION LEVEL IX.**

Kowalski looked up. The sky above the arena was tearing open.

Not a rift, but a *door*.

And through the door, a hand was reaching down, and a hand made of the same bone and chitin as the Architect, but larger. Much larger.

Vex scrambled backward, tripping over his own feet.

"It's the Architect! The Adult!"

Vane grabbed Kowalski's arm.

"Dale, run. That thing is Level IX. You can't file a complaint on a Level IX."

Kowalski looked at the crown on his clipboard. He looked at the giant hand descending from the sky. He looked at his empty spray can.

"No," Kowalski said. "I can't."

"Then what do we do?" Vane screamed.

Kowalski picked up the Architect's staff. It was still warm. It still hummed.

"We don't run," Kowalski said. He turned to Vex. "Inquisitor Vex.

You have a sword. You have a license.

And you have a debt.

"

Vex stared at him.

"A debt?"

"You interfered with my work," Kowalski said. "You caused a cross-contamination. The Authority owes *me* a retribution point. But I'm not claiming it."

Kowalski held out the staff to Vex.

"You want to fight it? Take this. It's unlicensed.

It's wild.

But it recognizes me as its temporary custodian.

"

Vex looked at the staff. He looked at the giant hand. He looked at Kowalski's calm, deadpan face.

"Why?" Vex asked.

"Because I have a job to do," Kowalski said. "And I need a witness for the next violation."

The giant hand smashed through the grid.

The floor exploded. Debris flew everywhere. Vane dove to the ground.

Vex stood up, trembling, and took the staff.

The moment his hand touched the chitin, the Authority's interface flashed for both of them.

**PARTY FORMED: UNLICENSED ALLIANCE.** **STATUS: PROVISIONAL.** **OBJECTIVE: SURVIVE THE INSPECTION.**

Kowalski smiled. It wasn't a happy smile. It was the smile of a man who had just found a new client.

"Try to keep up," he said.
