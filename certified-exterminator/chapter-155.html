Chapter 155: Inspected by Hand

# Chapter 155

The heat from the shattered phone was a small, sharp sting against Dale's palm. It was nothing compared to the noise.

A grinding shriek tore through the lobby of Apex Industries' sub-basement. It sounded like a blender trying to digest a car engine. The swarm of mechanical parasites poured from the floor drains, a cascading waterfall of iron teeth and rotating gears. They hit the concrete floor with a rhythmic, metallic clatter that vibrated in Dale's molars.

Thorne stumbled back, shielding his face with a leather briefcase. A gear the size of a dinner plate skittered past Dale's boot, spinning wildly before embedding itself in the drywall.

Dust puffed out.

Gray powder. Not asbestos. Just gypsum.

Dale didn't flinch.

He wiped the blood from his palm with a handkerchief he'd pulled from his shirt pocket. It was a clean, white cloth.

He checked the stain.

One drop. Acceptable.

"Kowalski," Thorne yelled over the din. "The Authority is scanning you! They're trying to scrub the signal! Run!"

"Run where?" Dale asked. He didn't look at Thorne. He looked at the floor.

The drains were the entry point. Primary vectors.

If the source was the plant, the drains were the feeding tubes.

But the plants in this building were hydroponic.

Closed loop. Why would mechanical parasites use water systems?

Because they weren't drinking water. They were drinking electricity.

Dale's Field Guide pinged. Not a notification. A prompt.

**SYSTEM ALERT: EVIDENCE DESTRUCTION DETECTED.** **STATUS: INCOMPLETE DOCUMENTATION.** **PENALTY IMMINENT.**

The white figure on the roof hadn't fled. It was still there, watching. It had destroyed the phone to stop the broadcast, but it hadn't killed Dale.

That was a mistake. A professional would have terminated the witness.

This was a corporate liability decision.

They wanted a cover-up, not a corpse. Corpses required investigation. Cover-ups required silence.

Silence was harder to maintain.

"Thorne," Dale said. His voice was flat, cutting through the mechanical roar. "What's the amperage rating on the main junction box?"

Thorne blinked, sweat dripping from his forehead. "What? I'm an executive, Dale. I don't know electrical specs."

"Guess."

"I don't—"

"Guess, Thorne. High or low? Industrial or residential?"

Thorne looked at the spinning metal horde, then at the junction box on the far wall.

The cover was hanging open. Wires sparked.

"High.

Industrial. Three-phase."

"Good."

Dale moved. He didn't run.

He walked.

His boots clicked on the tile.

He stepped around a cluster of gnashing gears, his movements precise, efficient. He treated the swarm like a spilled bag of marbles.

Avoidance, not combat.

The cross-contamination risk was high. These were Mechanical Parasites.

His Class II license covered biological vermin.

Using a biological protocol against them would trigger a 40% debuff and contaminate his Field Guide entry. He couldn't spray them. He couldn't bait them.

He had to starve them.

He reached the junction box. The sparks were intense. The air smelled of ozone and burnt insulation.

**ACTION: ISOLATE SOURCE.** **METHOD: ELECTRICAL SEVERANCE.** **CERTIFICATION LEVEL: CLASS II (ELECTRICIAN SUB-SPECIALTY: UNLICENSED).**

A red warning flashed in his peripheral vision.

**WARNING: UNLICENSED ACTION. PENALTY: -50% TREATMENT POINTS.**

Dale ignored it. He pulled a pair of insulated gloves from his tool belt.

They were rated for 1000 volts.

The junction box here was running hot. Probably 240, maybe 480. Safe enough, if he didn't touch the wrong wire.

He opened the panel fully. Inside, a nest of copper wiring pulsed with blue light.

The mechanical parasites were already climbing the walls toward it.

Their legs hooked into the insulation, drilling into the conductors.

They weren't just eating the power; they were feeding on the frequency.

**FIELD GUIDE UPDATE: MECHANICAL PARASITE (SUB-STRATA).** **WEAKNESS: RESONANCE FREQUENCY.** **NOTE: Vibration destabilizes internal gearing.**

Dale frowned. Resonance. How did you vibrate an entire junction box without bringing down the ceiling?

He needed a tool. He looked at Thorne.

"Give me your belt."

"My belt?"

"The buckle. It's metal. Heavy."

Thorne unclasped his belt and tossed it. Dale caught it with his free hand. He jammed the heavy steel buckle into the gap between the main breaker and the casing.

Then he grabbed the handle of his clipboard. It was made of thick, laminated plastic, but the clip was spring-loaded steel.

He hooked the clipboard's clip onto the edge of the breaker lever.

"Thorne, cover your ears."

"Why?"

"Physics," Dale said.

He didn't flip the breaker.

He didn't cut the power. He used the clipboard as a lever. He pulled the clip against the casing, creating a fulcrum.

Then he kicked the base of the junction box with his steel-toed boot.

The impact rang out. A dull *thud*.

The buckle acted as a tuning fork. The vibration traveled through the metal casing, into the wires, and into the parasites clinging to them.

The effect was instantaneous.

The grinding shriek changed pitch. It became a high-pitched whine.

The gears stopped rotating.

They jammed. Teeth locked. Chains snapped.

On the floor, the swarm convulsed. Hundreds of mechanical bodies fell still, frozen in place by their own internal friction. The heat from their motors spiked, then died as the circuit failed to complete.

Smoke rose from the junction box. Not fire. Just melted insulation.

**SYSTEM NOTIFICATION:** **INFESTATION NEUTRALIZED (TEMPORARY).** **METHOD: NON-COMBATANT ISOLATION.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 10/10.** **TREATMENT POINTS AWARDED: 250.** **BONUS: +50 FOR PUBLIC DOCUMENTATION (PARTIAL).**

Dale stepped back. He checked his hands.

Still clean.

He picked up the ruined phone.

The camera lens was cracked, but the internal memory chip might have survived the physical destruction. He pocketed it.

"Did it work?" Thorne whispered. He lowered the briefcase.

"The swarm is immobilized," Dale said. "But the source is still active."

He pointed to the ceiling. The plant on the roof was still humming. A low, throbbing vibration that shook the floorboards.

"The vibration isn't coming from the plant itself," Dale said. "It's coming from beneath it. The plant is just the antenna."

Thorne stared at the frozen gears. "They're dead?"

"Stunned. Like hibernating bears. If the power comes back, they'll wake up. And they'll be angry."

Dale looked at the elevator bank. The doors were pried open. The cables hung loose. No way up.

"We have to go down," Dale said.

"Down?" Thorne's face went pale. "There's nothing down. It's the parking garage."

"No. This building has a sub-sub-level. Utility tunnels.

Maintenance access. I saw it on the blueprints when I inspected the foundation for termites three years ago."

"Termites?"

"Structural entry points," Dale corrected.

"I noted a discrepancy in the grading plans. A void space that wasn't accounted for. I wrote it up as a drainage issue.

Code violation K-4."

Thorne looked at the frozen nightmare of metal on the floor. "You're going to walk into the dark, with a clipboard, because you flagged a drainage issue?"

"I'm going to walk into the dark," Dale said, "because the Authority just sent me a new notification."

He looked at his peripheral vision. The text was different this time. It wasn't the standard blue. It was a deep, bruised purple.

**LICENSING AUTHORITY DIRECTIVE:** **SUBJECT: DALE KOWALSKI.** **STATUS: FLAGGED FOR AUDIT.** **ACTION: DESCEND TO SUB-LEVEL 3.** **REWARD: CLASS III CERTIFICATION EXAM.** **PENALTY FOR REFUSAL: REVOCATION OF ALL LICENSES.**

"Class III?" Thorne asked. "What does that mean?"

"It means I get to kill bigger things," Dale said. "And I get to write the reports on how they died."

He walked toward the open elevator shaft. A maintenance ladder hung on the side.

It was rusted.

Rusted ladders were a safety violation. He noted it mentally.

He climbed down.

"Wait," Thorne said. "What about the guy on the roof? The white figure?"

"He's still there," Dale said. He didn't look up. "He's waiting."

"Why?"

"Because he knows I'm thorough. And he knows that if I find the source, I'll document it. All of it. He wants to see how good I am."

Dale reached the bottom of the shaft. The air was colder here. It smelled of wet concrete and copper.

He clicked on his flashlight. The beam cut through the darkness.

The tunnel stretched ahead, long and narrow. Pipes ran along the ceiling, sweating condensation. The floor was covered in a thin layer of oil.

And there were tracks.

Not footprints. Wheel tracks. Heavy, industrial treads.

Dale crouched. He pulled out a measuring tape from his belt. He extended it. The track width was exactly 42 inches.

Standard freight cart width.

He followed the tracks. They led deeper into the dark.

At the end of the tunnel, a heavy steel door stood ajar. It was labeled **MAINTENANCE ACCESS: HVAC ZONE 4**.

Inside, the humming was louder. It vibrated in Dale's chest.

He stepped through the door.

The room was vast. It was the heart of the building's climate control system. Giant fans sat idle. Ductwork snaked across the ceiling.

In the center of the room, suspended from a chain hoist, was a cage.

Inside the cage was not a monster. It was a human.

A woman. She was hooked up to tubes. Her eyes were open, glazed over. Cables ran from her temples into a machine that looked like a oversized radio tower.

The machine was pulling energy from her. Brain waves. Bio-electricity.

And the machine was feeding it to the plant above.

Dale froze. His clipboard felt heavy in his hand.

**FIELD GUIDE UPDATE:** **NEW SPECIES DETECTED: NEURO-VAMPIRIC CORE.** **CLASSIFICATION: CLASS IV HAZARD.** **STATUS: ACTIVE.** **WARNING: THIS IS NOT AN INFESTATION. THIS IS AN ATROCITY.**

Dale's hand tightened on the clipboard. His knuckles turned white.

This wasn't a bug. This wasn't a rat. This was a crime scene.

The Licensing Authority didn't exterminate crimes. It exterminated pests.

If he reported this as a pest infestation, the Authority would process it. They would neutralize the threat by killing the host. They would kill the woman to stop the energy drain.

If he reported it as a crime, the Authority had no jurisdiction. They would ignore it.

Dale looked at the sleeping woman. He looked at the cameras in the corners of the room. Red lights blinked.

He opened his clipboard. He flipped to a fresh page.

He didn't write **PEST CONTROL REPORT**.

He wrote **EVIDENCE LOG: HUMAN RIGHTS VIOLATION**.

And then, below it, he wrote: **INFESTATION CONTAINMENT PROTOCOL: ALPHA-7 (SEVERANCE).**

Protocol Alpha-7 was for severing a host from a parasite. It was usually used to save the host. But it required a Class III license to authorize the use of sedatives strong enough to shut down a human nervous system without killing them.

He didn't have a Class III license.

But he had the Authority's direct order. *Descend. Pass the exam.*

The exam wasn't a multiple-choice test. It was this.

The machine whirred louder. The woman's heart rate monitor spiked. A beep, fast and frantic.

The white figure on the roof wasn't watching anymore. He was here.

The door behind Dale slid shut.

From the darkness of the ventilation ducts above, something dropped.

It wasn't a gear. It was a hand. A human hand, but made of the same black, grinding metal.

It grabbed Dale's shoulder. The grip was cold. Iron-hard.

Dale didn't scream. He didn't fight. He looked at the hand, then at the clipboard.

He wrote one word on the page.

**INSPECTED.**

The System pinged.

**EXAM BEGINS.**

The metal hand squeezed. The bone in Dale's collarbone cracked. A white-hot flash of pain blinded him.

He didn't drop the clipboard.

He raised it. And he swung.
