Chapter 249: Not Leaving Yet

# Chapter 249

The pen tip snapped.

Dale stared at the graphite dust on his thumb, then at the manhole rim where the metal had buckled outward like a bloated gut. The voice was still echoing in the wet dark, that familiar, hollow cadence that had haunted his sleep since he was eighteen.

*Check the nest.*

He didn't check the nest. He checked the perimeter.

Around him, the street was silent. The Authority figure, a shimmering construct of geometric light and bureaucratic indifference, hovered three feet off the asphalt, its edges flickering with static. Halloway stood to the left, his sanitation rod humming with a low, menacing frequency, the nozzle pointed not at the pit, but at Dale's back.

"Stop," Halloway said. The word was flat, devoid of the usual righteous fury. He looked tired. He looked like a man who had seen the inside of a Rift and liked what he found.

"Protocol requires identification," Dale said, ignoring the rod. He pulled a spare pen from his vest pocket.

It was a cheap ballpoint, the kind he bought in bulk from the county supply depot.

He clicked it twice. Once. The ink flowed.

"You're documenting a trap," Halloway said.

"The Authority uses that data to locate high-density breach points. If you log the signal, they send more.

They don't want it gone, and they want it contained. Harvested. Processed.

"

Dale looked at the manhole. The darkness inside wasn't empty.

It was thick.

Oily.

It moved with the slow, deliberate churn of magma or heavy syrup.

"Is that a statement of intent, Technician Halloway?" Dale asked. He kept his voice level, the same tone he used when explaining why a tenant couldn't leave a grease trap uncovered. "Because unauthorized interference with a documented infestation carries a Class-4 citation.

It's not just a penalty. It's a suspension of your license.

"

Halloway's jaw tightened.

"My license is irrelevant."

"Mine isn't."

Dale stepped closer to the edge.

The heat rising from the shaft smelled of ozone and rotting meat. It was a specific scent. He knew it.

He had encountered it once before, in a sewer line beneath a abandoned textile factory in Dayton, years ago.

A Class-8 anomaly he had been forced to seal without killing.

He opened his Field Guide.

The interface materialized in the air before him, glowing with a soft, amber light. It was a floating tablet of parchment and light, updating in real-time. The entry for *Rattus profundus* was complete. The entry for *Void-Weave Construct* was blank.

But there was a new notification blinking in the corner.

**SYSTEM ALERT: UNAUTHORIZED DATA INJECTION DETECTED.** **SOURCE: AMBIENT RIFT SIGNAL.** **ACTION: MANUAL DOCUMENTATION REQUIRED TO VERIFY SOURCE INTEGRITY.**

Dale frowned.

The System usually didn't ask. It commanded. It awarded points for killing.

It penalized for hesitation. This was a prompt.

A request for user input.

And a loophole.

If he documented the entity, he got the weakness bonus. If he documented the *source*, he got something else.

"The Authority won't give you points for identifying a voice," Halloway said, stepping closer. "They'll give you points for killing the body. You're wasting time."

"I'm following protocol," Dale said.

He looked at the manhole, and he didn't look at the dark. He looked at the edges of the concrete rim.

There were scratches. Deep, gouged lines where something had tried to climb out before. He pulled out his magnifying glass.

The scratches were not from claws. They were from *teeth*. Small, uniform, filed down.

Dale's breath stopped.

These weren't monster marks. These were tool marks.

He looked up at Halloway.

"Did you bring the containment unit here, or did it climb out on its own?"

Halloway didn't answer. He raised the rod.

"Step away from the edge, Dale. Now."

"The entry point was prepped," Dale muttered, scribbling on the floating page of his Field Guide. *Entry Point: Manhole 402-B. Condition: Tampered. Damage Type: Mechanical abrasion consistent with industrial pliers.*

The System reacted.

**NEW ENTRY CREATED: INFRASTRUCTURE SABOTAGE (Class 1).** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: +0.5 (For identifying human agency in a supernatural event).** **REWARD: UNLOCKED PROTOCOL: FORENSIC TRACING (Level II).**

Dale felt the shift. It was a subtle weight in his mind, a new folder opening in his mental filing cabinet.

He understood the flow of the energy around the manhole.

It wasn't random. It was routed.

He looked at the Authority figure. The entity had stopped flickering. It was watching him.

"You see it too?" Dale asked the light-form.

The Authority did not speak. It simply rotated, its geometric faces aligning with the manhole.

Halloway shouted, "Don't listen to it! It's feeding you data to keep you dependent!"

Dale ignored both of them. He looked at the pit. The voice spoke again.

"Dale. Look at the grate."

He looked. The metal grate was not broken. It was welded shut from the *inside*.

A chill ran down his spine, colder than the night air. He had assumed the monster had broken out. He had assumed it was an intrusion.

But monsters don't weld their own cages, and he pulled out his phone. He didn't call the police. He didn't call the Authority dispatch. He opened the municipal maintenance log, the public database he had memorized years ago for ease of access.

*Manhole 402-B. Last inspected: Three days ago. Inspector: J. Halloway.*

The timestamp was 14:02.

Halloway was in the inspection report. He was the last one to touch the cover.

Dale looked at Halloway. The man's face was pale, sweat beading on his forehead despite the cold.

"You didn't come here to kill it," Dale said. "You came here to keep it in."

Halloway lowered the rod slightly.

"It's not a monster, Dale. It's a leak.

And if you document it, the Authority will patch the leak.

They'll seal the rift. They'll erase the connection. But the people on the other side...

"

He trailed off.

"People?" Dale asked.

"They're not monsters," Halloway said, his voice cracking. "They're refugees. They're trying to get out. And the Authority... the Authority wants them dead because dead things don't file complaints."

Dale looked at the Field Guide. The entry for the voice was still blank. But the System had offered a choice.

**DOCUMENT ENTITY: VOID-WEAVE SENTIENT.** **DOCUMENT CONTEXT: RIFT STABILIZATION FAILURE.**

If he documented the entity as a monster, he got the kill bonus. He could end this, walk away, and let Halloway be arrested. He could get his Thoroughness Rating up to 9.5 and qualify for the Level III exam.

If he documented the context, he admitted the system was flawed. He admitted the Authority was negligent. He admitted that the

"monsters" were people.

And he would get zero Treatment Points for the kill, and he looked at the pen. He looked at the manhole.

The voice whispered, *

"They're hungry, Dale. They just want to eat."*

Dale felt the contamination anxiety rise, a tightness in his chest. This was messy. This was ambiguous. This was not a clean nest to be sprayed and sealed.

But he was a technician. And technicians fix the source of the problem.

He clicked his pen again, and he wrote.

*Entity Classification: SENTIENT ORGANISM (Non-hostile).* *Threat Level: MINIMAL.* *Source of Breach: Structural negligence and intentional containment failure by licensed agent J. Halloway.*

The System flashed red.

**WARNING: CLASSIFICATION CONFLICT.** **THREAT ASSESSMENT: CRITICAL ERROR.** **PENALTY: THOROUGHNESS RATING REDUCED TO 0.0.** **STATUS: LICENSE SUSPENDED PENDING INVESTIGATION.**

Dale flinched. The notification burned in his peripheral vision. His rating, the thing that had saved him in Chapter 10, the thing that got him the high-grade equipment in Chapter 50, was gone.

Zero.

Halloway stared at him, eyes wide.

"Why did you do that?"

"Because the work wasn't clean," Dale said. His hands were shaking, but his voice was steady. "And if I lie about the classification, the next time I spray this place, I'll miss the nest."

The Authority figure moved. It didn't attack. It dissolved. The light scattered into a million motes that drifted into the manhole.

A new notification appeared, this one not in amber, but in a deep, bruising purple.

**AUTHORITY INTERVENTION: DIRECT.** **SUBJECT: DALE KOWALSKI.** **CHARGE: OBSTRUCTION OF NATURAL DISASTER PROTOCOL.** **ACTION: IMMEDIATE DETAINMENT.**

The air around Dale solidified. Invisible bars snapped into place, locking his arms to his sides. He couldn't move. He couldn't breathe.

Halloway dropped the rod.

"Run, Dale. I'll cover you."

"I don't run from inspections," Dale said.

He looked at the manhole. The darkness was rising.

A hand reached up, which was not a claw.

It was a human hand, pale and thin, gripping the rim of the metal.

Then another.

And another.

They were small. Children.

The purple notification pulsed faster. **DETECTING MULTIPLE UNLICENSED ENTITIES.**

Dale looked at the children. They looked terrified. They looked like they had been hungry for a long time.

He looked at Halloway.

"You're under arrest for sabotage," Dale said.

Halloway grabbed Dale's collar, pulling him back from the invisible bars that were beginning to constrict his throat.

"You're making a mistake. They'll kill them. All of them."

"They're already dead if you don't act," Dale said. He struggled against the magical restraint, his muscles straining, the veins in his neck bulging. "Halloway.

Give me the containment foam. The heavy-duty kind.

Not the rod."

"It's for solids," Halloway said. "It won't stop them."

"It will stop the Authority from seeing them as solids," Dale said. "If you encase them, they become 'sealed waste.' Waste is property. Property requires a disposal permit. A permit requires a human supervisor."

Halloway hesitated.

"Do it," Dale said. "Or I document you as the primary threat, and the Authority will burn this block to ash."

Halloway reached into his belt and pulled out a canister. It was massive, black, stamped with the Authority logo. *Class-9 Containment Foam.*

He sprayed it.

The foam hit the children. It didn't kill them. It hardened instantly, encasing them in thick, grey shells. They fell back into the pit, muffled and still.

The purple notification froze.

**THREAT ASSESSMENT: RECALIBRATING.** **STATUS: SEALED INFESTATION.** **ACTION: AWAIT DISPOSAL CREW.**

The invisible bars around Dale vanished, and he collapsed to his knees, gasping for air. His license was suspended. His rating was zero. He had saved the children, but he had just admitted to a crime against the state.

Halloway stood over him, the canister empty.

"You just broke your career, Kowalski."

Dale wiped the dirt from his face. He picked up his pen. It was still intact.

He looked at the Field Guide. The entry for *Void-Weave Sentient* was now locked, greyed out. But below it, a new entry had appeared.

**ENTRY: SYSTEMIC CORRUPTION.** **STATUS: UNKNOWN.** **WEAKNESS: UNDOCUMENTED.**

Dale looked at the greyed-out weakness. He couldn't see it. He couldn't attack it.

But he had a new problem.

The manhole cover, which had been welded shut from the inside, vibrated. The foam was cracking. The children were waking up.

And from the darkness, a new voice spoke. Not his mother.

Not a child.

A voice like grinding stones.

"Technician Kowalski," the voice rumbled. "You have violated Protocol 4-B: Unauthorized Mercy. You are hereby stripped of all certification levels."

Dale felt a pull in his chest, a physical tearing sensation. The Level I, II, and III badges he had earned over three hundred chapters, the abilities he had spent hundreds of hours grinding for, vanished from his interface.

He was naked. No Field Guide access to combat data. No protocols. No armor.

Just a pen, a clipboard, and a manhole full of people who wanted to eat.

Halloway looked at the empty canister.

"What do we do?"

Dale looked at the cracking foam. He looked at the approaching shadow of the Authority enforcement drones, descending from the sky like vultures.

He picked up his clipboard.

"We don't fight them," Dale said. "We file a grievance."

He opened a new page.

**FORM 12-C: REQUEST FOR EMERGENCY SANITATION ZONE.** **SUBJECT: CIVILIAN DISPLACEMENT DUE TO GOVERNMENT NEGLIGENCE.** **PRIORITY: CRITICAL.**

He wrote. The pen scratched against the paper, loud in the silence.

The drones landed. They did not attack. They waited.

Because Dale Kowalski was still holding a pen. And in the eyes of the Authority, a technician holding a form was never a threat.

Until he signed it.

Dale looked at Halloway.

"Sign here."

Halloway didn't move.

"If you don't sign, I list you as the sole responsible party for the infestation," Dale said. "And I don't care if you go to prison. I care about the nest."

Halloway took the pen. His hand shook.

He signed.

The paper glowed white.

**AUTHORITY RESPONSE: PENDING.** **ESTIMATED WAIT TIME: 48 HOURS.**

The drones hovered. The foam cracked further. A small hand punched through the grey shell, reaching for Dale's face.

Dale did not step back, and he leaned forward.

"I'm not leaving until the disposal crew arrives," he said to the empty air.

And from the pit, something massive rose.
