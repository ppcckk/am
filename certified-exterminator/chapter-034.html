Chapter 34

The corridor was down to four feet of clearance and still shrinking.

Dale pressed his back against the wall and watched the red tissue fold over itself like a healing wound. The blue point at the core was half-buried now, a pinprick of light under a scab that was getting thicker every time he blinked. He had maybe ninety seconds before the passage sealed entirely.

His hands were shaking. Not from fear. From the cold. The organism's interior ran six degrees below ambient, and the moisture in the air had started crystallizing on his sleeves. Frostbite risk at prolonged exposure. He noted it automatically, filed it under environmental hazards, and pushed it out of his working memory.

Three protocols. No way to reach the core. And a licensing violation that would strip his certification before he could finish the job.

The System pinged again.

CLASSIFICATION DISPUTE STATUS: PENDING. ENFORCEMENT HOLD: ACTIVE. COMPLIANCE REVIEW SCHEDULED: IMMEDIATE.

Immediate. Not queued. Not next cycle. Now.

A second notification stacked below the first.

COMPLIANCE OFFICER ASSIGNED: AUDITOR-7741. AUTHORITY: REVOKE CERTIFICATION LEVEL III. GROUNDS: UNAUTHORIZED CORRECTION OF FIELD GUIDE RECORDS, PROVISIONAL CODES 0041 THROUGH 0043.

Dale read the line three times. Provisional codes 0041 and 0042 were the ones he'd filed during the initial survey. He'd flagged inconsistencies in the Authority's existing entry for the organism. He'd noted that the listed morphometrics didn't match observed tissue density, that the reproductive cycle described in the entry was incompatible with the brood structures he'd documented, that the territorial behavior pattern was wrong by a factor of three.

He hadn't corrected the entry. He'd flagged it. There was a difference. Flagging was standard procedure. Any technician could file an observation note.

The Authority was treating observation notes as corrections.

The corridor shrank to three feet. Dale could hear the tissue contracting, a wet sound like knuckles cracking inside a sealed jar. The blue point was a dot now. He had maybe sixty seconds.

He opened the Field Guide.

The entry for the organism was there, labeled under the Authority's name for it. The taxonomy was wrong. The morphometrics were wrong. The behavioral profile was wrong. But the protocols listed under the entry were keyed to the WRONG species. That meant his existing protocol toolkit, the one the Authority had given him based on his certification level, contained three protocols that were calibrated for a creature this organism was not.

He checked his protocol list.

Protocol One: Acoustic Disruption. Designed for resonance-based organisms. This thing had no resonance structures. Useless.

Protocol Two: Chemical Cascade. Designed for organisms with a chitinous exoskeleton. This thing was soft tissue. Useless.

Protocol Three: Thermal Gradient Inversion. Designed for cold-blooded organisms with a single thermal regulatory organ.

Dale stopped.

The organism ran six degrees below ambient. That wasn't cold-blooded. That was active cooling. Something was pulling heat out of the surrounding tissue and channeling it somewhere. The frost on his sleeves wasn't environmental. It was exhaust.

He looked at the thermal data he'd logged during his scan. The temperature gradient wasn't uniform. It ran from the outer wall inward, dropping in a consistent slope that pointed toward the core. The organism wasn't cold-blooded. It was running a thermal pump. It was using the same architecture a cold-blooded organism would use, just inverted.

Protocol Three wasn't designed for this organism. But it was designed for the same structural principle.

He opened the protocol deployment window. The system flagged it immediately.

CROSS-CONTAMINATION WARNING: THERMAL GRADIENT INVERSION IS CALIBRATED FOR SPECIES ENTRY HIST-0041 (COLD BLOODED). TARGET ORGANISM CLASSIFICATION: HIST-UNCL-0043 (DISPUTED). DEPLOYING THIS PROTOCOL MAY RESULT IN REDUCED EFFECTIVENESS AND FIELD GUIDE CONTAMINATION.

Dale read the warning. Then he read the subtext.

The warning said the protocol was calibrated for HIST-0041. The Authority's own entry for this organism listed it under HIST-0041 before he'd flagged it. The entry was wrong about the taxonomy, but the protocol was keyed to the taxonomy the Authority had assigned. Which meant the protocol was keyed to the wrong creature. But the wrong creature and the right creature shared a structural principle. The thermal pump architecture was the same shape, just running in reverse.

The debuff was forty percent. He'd be deploying a protocol at sixty percent effectiveness against an organism that wasn't the one it was designed for.

Sixty percent of a thermal gradient inversion against a thermal pump running in reverse.

He did the math in his head. If the protocol inverted the gradient, and the organism was already running an inverted gradient, the protocol would be fighting the organism's own system. At sixty percent effectiveness, it might not be enough to kill it. But it might be enough to stall it. To stop the scar tissue from closing long enough for him to reach the core manually.

Manual extraction. No protocol. No documentation. Just his hands and his knowledge of where the core was.

He deployed Protocol Three.

The System accepted it. No rejection. No additional warning. The debuff applied silently, a flat forty percent reduction noted in the corner of his vision.

The protocol hit the wall.

Nothing happened for two full seconds. Then the tissue nearest him went slack. The red layers loosened, lost their tension, and sagged inward like a deflating lung. The corridor widened by six inches. Then eight. The blue point reappeared, bigger now, exposed.

But it didn't stop. The tissue on the far side kept growing. The protocol was working locally, at the point of contact, but the organism was compensating. It was rerouting its thermal flow away from the affected zone.

Dale had maybe thirty seconds before it resealed around the breach he'd made.

He moved.

He pulled himself through the widened gap, scraping his shoulder against tissue that was still soft but beginning to stiffen at the edges. The blue point was ten feet ahead, embedded in a knot of red fiber. He could see the core now: a dense nodule the size of a grapefruit, pulsing with a rhythm that matched the thermal gradient he'd mapped. It was the pump. The heart of the system.

He reached it. His gloved hands found the knot of fiber and started pulling. The tissue resisted, elastic, trying to close around his fingers. He dug his thumbs in and tore.

The System pinged.

COMPLIANCE REVIEW: IN PROGRESS. CANDIDATE KOWALSKI, YOU ARE CURRENTLY ENGAGED IN UNAUTHORIZED PHYSICAL CONTACT WITH A DISPUTED ORGANISM. THIS CONSTITUTES AN ADDITIONAL VIOLATION.

Dale ignored it. He tore another strip of fiber. The core pulsed faster. The thermal output spiked, and the frost on his sleeves started to melt. The organism was dumping heat, trying to cook him out.

He kept pulling.

The knot came apart in his hands. The core rolled free, slick and warm, and dropped into his palm. It was heavier than it looked. Dense. The pulse in it stuttered, skipped, and went flat.

The organism shuddered. Every wall in the corridor contracted at once, a full-body response. Then they went still. The red tissue stopped moving. The blue point at the far end of the corridor flickered and went dark.

Dale stood in the middle of a dead organism, holding its core in his bare hand.

The System pinged.

INFESTATION NEUTRALIZED. THOROUGHNESS RATING: PENDING. CLASSIFICATION DISPUTE: RESOLVED. ORGANISM TERMINATED. FIELD GUIDE ENTRY UPDATE: PENDING REVIEW.

The dispute was resolved because the organism was dead. You couldn't dispute the classification of something that no longer existed. The compliance review was still active, but the grounds for revocation had shifted. He'd killed the thing. The question now was whether he'd killed it correctly.

The System added a line.

COMPLIANCE OFFICER AUDITOR-7741: REVIEW COMPLETE. FINDING: CANDIDATE KOWALSKI USED PROTOCOL THREE IN A MANNER CONSISTENT WITH AUTHORITY GUIDELINES. HOWEVER, PHYSICAL CONTACT WITH CORE CONSTITUTES UNDOCUMENTED EXTRACTION. THIS IS A PROTOCOL VIOLATION, NOT A LICENSING VIOLATION. PENALTY: THOROUGHNESS RATING REDUCTION. CERTIFICATION: MAINTAINED.

Dale exhaled. His hands were shaking again. The core sat in his palm, inert, already starting to cool.

Then the System added one more line.

CLASSIFICATION DISPUTE RESOLUTION: ORGANISM TERMINATED PRIOR TO FULL TAXONOMIC REVIEW. FIELD GUIDE ENTRY HIST-0041 REMAINS INCORRECT. CORRECTION REQUEST DENIED. CANDIDATE KOWALSKI, YOUR OBSERVATION NOTES HAVE BEEN FILED UNDER PENDING REVIEW. NO ACTION WILL BE TAKEN UNTIL A SECOND SPECIMEN IS SUBMITTED FOR VERIFICATION.

Dale stared at the message.

The entry was still wrong. The Authority knew it was wrong. They just didn't have a second specimen to prove it. And he'd destroyed the only one.

The System added a final line.

NOTIFICATION: A SECOND SPECIMEN HAS BEEN DETECTED. INFESTATION EVENT CLASS: UNCLASSIFIED. LOCATION: 2.3 KILOMETERS NORTHEAST. ESTIMATED MATURITY: 48 HOURS. RECOMMENDED ACTION: IMMEDIATE FIELD INTERCEPTION.

Dale looked at the dead core in his hand. He looked at the message.

The Authority was asking him to go kill another one. Before it matured. Before it did whatever the wrong entry said it would do, which was not what it would actually do.

He looked at his Field Guide. The entry for HIST-0041 was still there, still wrong. And now there was a new infestation event, 2.3 kilometers away, with a specimen that would mature in forty-eight hours.

He had to find it. He had to identify it correctly. And this time, he couldn't kill it.

Because if he killed it, the entry stayed wrong. And the next one would be wrong too. And the next one. And the one after that. The Authority would never correct the record. The protocols would stay calibrated for the wrong creature. Every technician who came after him would be fighting a war with the wrong weapons.

He needed a live specimen. He needed to capture it. He needed to document it alive, with full behavioral observation, and submit it for taxonomic review.

Dale had never captured a live specimen. In fourteen years of pest control, he'd exterminated everything. That was the job. You don't capture. You kill.

He opened the Field Guide to the entry for HIST-0041 and read the behavioral profile. It was wrong. The territorial range listed was a quarter of what he'd observed. The reproductive cycle was described as solitary when he'd documented brood structures. The aggression trigger was listed as chemical when he'd documented acoustic.

If he went after the second specimen with the wrong behavioral profile, he'd approach it wrong. He'd use the wrong containment strategy. And a live specimen with the wrong containment strategy was a specimen that got away.

Or a specimen that killed him.

The System pinged.

LIVE CAPTURE PROTOCOL: UNLOCKED. REQUIREMENT: CANDIDATE MUST FILE A CAPTURE REQUEST PRIOR TO ENGAGEMENT. CAPTURE REQUESTS REQUIRE A MINIMUM THOROUGHNESS RATING OF 9.0 ON THE PENDING TARGET SPECIES. CURRENT THOROUGHNESS RATING ON HIST-0041: 8.7.

Dale read it twice.

His thoroughness rating on this species was 8.7. He needed 9.0 to file a capture request. He'd just gotten the job done at 8.7 because of the undocumented extraction. The physical contact had cost him points.

He looked at the dead core in his hand. He looked at the notification about the second specimen.

He couldn't file a capture request. He couldn't unlock the capture protocol. He couldn't do the thing that would fix the entry.

Unless he went back to the dead organism and did it again.

The System would re-evaluate. If he could document the dead specimen to a higher standard, his thoroughness rating on the species would rise. He'd need to find something he'd missed. Something in the tissue, in the structure, in the thermal architecture. Something that would push the rating from 8.7 to 9.0.

He looked at the walls around him. Dead tissue. Cooling. Starting to break down.

He had maybe an hour before the organism's cellular structure degraded past the point of useful analysis.

Dale pulled out his flashlight. He knelt beside the nearest wall of tissue and started looking for something he'd missed the first time.

The System pinged.

WARNING: COMPLIANCE OFFICER AUDITOR-7741 HAS FLAGGED YOUR PRESENCE AT THE TERMINATION SITE. FURTHER ANALYSIS OF A TERMINATED SPECIMEN WITHOUT PRIOR AUTHORIZATION CONSTITUTES EVIDENCE TAMPERING.

Dale kept looking.
