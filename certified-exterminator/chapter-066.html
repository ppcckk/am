Chapter 66: Examination of the Examiner

# Chapter 66

The gap under the basement door was four inches wide. It had been four inches wide for four months.

Whatever lived behind it had spent that time learning the edges.

Dale did not step back. He did not step forward.

He held his clipboard at chest height, pen ready, and watched the shoulders emerge first.

They were too broad for the gap. The drywall had to tear, not stretch. The sound was wet and structural, like a finger pushed through wet cardboard from the inside.

He noted the tearing pattern.

Asymmetrical. Left shoulder first.

The creature was leading with its left side, which meant its dominant hemisphere was right.

He wrote this down. The pen moved without instruction. Fourteen years of muscle memory.

The head followed. It had the proportions of a human skull but the surface texture of chitin.

The eye sockets were too deep.

The jaw extended too far, and he counted the teeth visible in the partial grimace. Thirty-two.

Normal count. Wrong spacing. The incisors were set at a fifteen-degree angle from vertical, which meant the bite force would be distributed across the molars than concentrated at the front.

That was not a human feeding adaptation. That was a grinding adaptation.

He wrote it down.

The notification hit immediately.

TEMPLATE DIVERGENCE CONFIRMED.

SPECIMEN CLASS: Rattus profundus. DOCUMENTED PARAMETERS: RODENT MORPHOLOGY, QUADRUPEDAL LOCOMOTION, MAXIMUM MASS 14 KG. OBSERVED PARAMETERS: BIPEDAL MORPHOLOGY, ESTIMATED MASS 70-90 KG, CRANIAL STRUCTURE DIVERGENT.

TEMPLATE UPDATE IN PROGRESS.

The thing pulled itself through the gap with a sound like a boot pulled from mud. It stood in the crawlspace.

It was roughly the height of a man but the proportions were wrong.

The legs were too short for the torso. The arms were too long. It moved with a lurching gait that Dale recognized immediately.

It was the gait of something that had been told it was bipedal but had not yet learned to be.

It turned toward him.

The thing’s weight shift revealed the weakness to Dale.

The center of mass was too high. The hip joints were not fully developed.

If it charged, it would have to compensate by leaning forward, and that would expose the cervical vertebrae at a four-degree angle. A targeted strike at the C3-C4 junction would sever the spinal cord.

He knew this. He had identified it in real time. He had not written it down.

The notification appeared again.

WEAKNESS IDENTIFICATION DETECTED. FIELD GUIDE UPDATE AVAILABLE. DOCUMENT TO GRANT TARGETED DAMAGE BONUS.

He looked at his clipboard. The frass analysis was still there.

The measurements.

His handwriting. The thing stood six feet away, breathing with a wet clicking sound from its nasal passages, and Dale stared at the blank space on his clipboard where the weakness entry should go.

If he documented the weakness, the Authority would grant him the damage bonus.

He could kill the thing. Right now.

With his hands, maybe.

With the pry bar leaning against the joist.

But the template, and he understood the template now. The Authority used Field Guide entries as dimensional anchors.

The creature's morphology was not random.

It was conforming to what Dale had written.

His documentation of Rattus profundus had become a blueprint, and the Rift was using it. Every time a technician documented a species, they were not just recording what the creature was.

They were recording what it would become.

His entry said quadrupedal. The thing was trying to be bipedal because he had not documented bipedalism.

The template was incomplete, and the Rift was filling the gaps with its own logic.

The thing was not a mutation. It was a rough draft.

And if he documented the weakness, the Authority would update the template.

The next specimen would have that weakness. And the next.

And the next.

But the Rift would also see the weakness and close it. The template was not static. It was a negotiation.

He had maybe thirty seconds before the thing lunged, and he did not write the weakness.

Instead, he wrote something else. He wrote: CERVICAL VERTEBRAE C3-C4: STRUCTURALLY INCOMPLETE. LOCOMOTION DEPENDENT ON C4 STABILIZATION. SEVERANCE OF C3-C4 JUNCTION CAUSES IMMEDIATE MOTOR FAILURE.

This was not a weakness. This was a structural observation.

He was not documenting a vulnerability, and he was documenting a limitation that already existed.

The difference was semantic, but the Authority's system did not parse semantics. It parsed intent.

The notification changed.

WEAKNESS IDENTIFICATION REJECTED. STRUCTURAL OBSERVATION ACCEPTED. NO TARGETED DAMAGE BONUS GRANTED. TEMPLATE UPDATE: NON-WEAPONIZED.

He had exploited the loophole. The Authority's system distinguished between weakness documentation and structural documentation. A weakness could be exploited.

A structural observation simply existed. The template would update to include the structural limitation, but the Authority would not grant him the damage bonus because he had not framed it as exploitable.

He would have to kill the thing without the bonus.

The thing lunged.

Dale moved left, and the pry bar was in his right hand. He had picked it up during the thirty seconds of writing, which was the whole point. He had used the documentation window as a combat window.

The thing's gait carried it forward with that lurching overcompensation, and Dale saw the C3-C4 junction exposed exactly as he had predicted.

Four degrees. Maybe five.

He swung the pry bar at the neck.

The hit landed, and the sound was wrong. Not the clean crack of bone but a dense thud, like hitting a bag of wet gravel wrapped in leather. The thing did not fall.

It staggered. The cervical junction had not severed.

The structural limitation was real, but it was not a weakness.

It was a vulnerability that required more force than Dale could generate with a pry bar and his arms.

The thing recovered. It turned. The jaw opened. The grinding teeth rotated in a circular motion, which Dale had not documented because he had not seen it before.

NEW PARAMETER DETECTED. MANDIBULAR ROTATION: UNDOCUMENTED. TEMPLATE UPDATE IN PROGRESS.

He backed up. The crawlspace was eight feet by twelve.

The thing was six feet tall. The only exit was the gap it had entered through. Dale was in the crawlspace with a thing that was trying to become a man and had not yet figured out how to use its legs properly.

His phone buzzed.

DISPATCH: INSPECTION TEAM ETA 4 MINUTES. HOLD POSITION. DO NOT ENGAGE FURTHER. DO NOT DOCUMENT FURTHER.

He typed back: "If I stop documenting, the template stops updating. The thing stops conforming. You want me to let it finish mutating?"

The reply was immediate.

"Kowalski. You are in violation."

He read it twice.

"Violation of what."

"Class V. Template Contamination. Your Field Guide entry is the causal vector for specimen divergence. You are the infestation."

He looked at the thing. It was circling him now, testing the space, grinding its teeth.

It had stopped trying to be bipedal, which was crouching. Four limbs. Back to the original template.

His template.

His words on paper had pulled it back toward the rodent shape because the Authority's system was now prioritizing his documentation over the Rift's improvisation.

He had saved the specimen from becoming a man.

That was not a good thing. A rat-sized thing he could handle. A man-sized thing he could not.

But the Authority was telling him that the rat-sized thing was his fault.

He typed: "I documented what I saw."

"Your documentation is accurate. That is the problem."

He stared at the screen, and the thing had stopped circling.

It was watching him. The eye sockets were too deep, but the gaze was focused.

Deliberate. It assessed him as he assessed a nest.

His phone buzzed again.

"Kowalski. Inspection team has been rerouted. You are now the inspection team."

He looked up. The notification appeared in his peripheral vision.

CERTIFICATION ACTION REQUIRED. You have been assigned an Internal Audit. You must document the specimen's current parameters in full, including all divergent morphology, and submit your Field Guide entry for Authority review. Failure to complete this audit within 30 minutes will result in certification suspension and Field Guide confiscation.

He looked at the thing. The thing looked at him.

He looked at his clipboard, and he had to document it. All of it.

The human proportions.

The grinding teeth. The mandibular rotation. The gait.

The mass.

The everything. He had to write it all down, and the Authority would read it, and the template would update, and the next specimen would be exactly what he described.

And the Rift would see the description and try to break it, which was the cycle.

Every technician who documented a species was feeding the Rift a blueprint. Every blueprint the Rift broke produced something worse. The Authority knew this.

The Authority had always known this. The Field Guide was not a record.

It was a leash, and the leash was made of the technician's own observations, and the thing at the end of the leash was learning how to pull.

The thing took a step toward him.

Then another. It was testing the distance.

Dale watched its feet. The toes were wrong. Too many joints.

The foot was trying to be a hand, and he uncapped his pen.

He started writing.

He wrote the mass estimate first. 78 kilograms.

He could feel the numbers pulling at the air around him, could feel the template tightening like a wet rope. The thing's shoulders squared. Its center of gravity dropped.

It was getting better at being what he was writing, and he stopped writing.

The thing hesitated. It had felt the pause. It looked at him with those too-deep eyes and Dale understood something he did not want to understand.

The thing could feel the template. It knew when he was documenting.

It was waiting for him to finish so it could become what he described.

If he stopped, it would stall. If he wrote a weakness, it would develop defenses. If he wrote a strength, it would gain it.

He was not fighting the creature. He was designing it.

His phone buzzed.

DISPATCH: INSPECTION TEAM ETA 90 SECONDS. Kowalski, if you are still in the crawlspace when they arrive, you will be processed as a Class V contamination vector. You have been advised.

Ninety seconds, and he looked at the thing. The thing looked at him. It was waiting.

He looked at his clipboard. Half a page. Half a creature. The rest was blank.

He could write the weakness. The C3-C4 junction.

He could write it as a weakness this time, take the damage bonus, and kill the thing before the inspection team arrived.

The Authority would grant him the bonus, and he would swing the pry bar with system-enhanced force. The cervical junction would sever.

The thing would drop.

And then the Authority would read his entry. The template would update.

Every future specimen would have that weakness.

And the Rift would close it. And the next thing would be worse.

Or he could stop writing.

Walk out of the crawlspace. Let the inspection team handle it.

Accept the Class V violation.

Accept the certification suspension. Accept the Field Guide confiscation.

But if they confiscated his Field Guide, they would confiscate his documentation, and the Authority would use his documentation as the template for whatever came next.

His words would still be in the system, and he could not take them back.

He could not unwrite them.

The thing took another step. It was closer now. He could smell it.

Not the musk of a rodent. Something chemical.

Ammonia and copper.

The smell of a body that was not a body, and he looked at the blank half of his clipboard.

He looked at the gap in the wall.

The thing had come through it. The gap was widening.

He could see the other side now.

Not the crawlspace. Not the neighbor's duplex. Something else.

A space that was not a space. Dark and wet and vast, with shapes moving in it that were not shaped like anything he had a word for.

The inspection team was coming through that gap, and he looked at the notification one more time.

CERTIFICATION ACTION REQUIRED.

He picked up his pen. He looked at the thing. The thing looked at him.

He wrote one word.

NOT.

The notification appeared.

DOCUMENTATION INCOMPLETE. TEMPLATE UPDATE REJECTED. SPECIMEN STATUS: UNDEFINED.

The thing screamed.

Not a sound.

A pressure. A vibration that started in his molars and ended in his sternum.

The thing’s body started to change. Not toward the template. Not toward the Rift.

Toward nothing. Toward the absence of a shape.

It was becoming undefined because he had not defined it.

The gap in the wall collapsed. Not closed. Collapsed.

The space on the other side folded inward like a lung deflating.

The shapes in the dark vanished.

The thing in the crawlspace went still. Its outline blurred. Its mass estimate dropped. He could feel the numbers leaving it. 78 kilograms. 60. 40. 20.

It was becoming smaller. Less defined. Less real.

He had done this.

One word. NOT. He had refused to define it and the Authority's system had treated the refusal as documentation.

A negative entry. A declaration of non-existence.

The thing was not a rat. The thing was not a man. The thing was NOT.

It collapsed.

Not died.

Collapsed. Like a house of cards with the cards removed.

The mass hit the crawlspace floor and did not bounce. It was a pile of material with no shape. Chitin and cartilage and fluid, spread across four square feet of plywood.

His phone buzzed.

DISPATCH: INSPECTION TEAM STAND DOWN. ANOMALOUS RESOLUTION DETECTED. Kowalski, what did you do.

He looked at his clipboard. One word. NOT. In his handwriting.

He typed back: "I identified it."

The reply came in two seconds.

"That is not identification. That is negation.

You have created a new violation class.

Kowalski, do not move. Do not speak. Do not write anything else.

An Authority representative is arriving in six minutes and you are going to explain how you unmade a Class III specimen with a single word."

He looked at the pile on the floor. It was already drying. Already losing cohesion. Already becoming frass.

His Field Guide updated.

NEW ENTRY: Rattus profundus (variant). STATUS: NEGATED.

TEMPLATE: VOID.

NOTE: NEGATION DOCUMENTATION IS NOT A RECOGNIZED TREATMENT PROTOCOL. THIS ACTION HAS BEEN FLAGGED FOR EXAMINATION.

He looked at the notification. He looked at the frass.

He looked at the gap in the wall, which was now just a gap.

Drywall and joist.

Nothing on the other side.

His phone buzzed one final time.

DISPATCH: Kowalski. The Authority representative is not human. The Authority representative is not an inspection team.

The Authority representative is the Licensing Authority itself. You are being summoned for a Level IX Examination.

He stared at the screen.

Level IX, and he was Level IV. Five levels separated him from it. You could not skip levels.

You could not be summoned for an examination you were not qualified to take.

Unless the examination was not about his certification.

Unless the examination was about him.

His phone went dark. The notification in his peripheral vision remained.

LEVEL IX EXAMINATION: SUBJECT IS THE EXAMINER.
