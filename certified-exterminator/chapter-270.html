Chapter 270: Filing a Complaint

# Chapter 270

The dust did not settle. It spun in a tight, geometric spiral around Dale's ankles, defying gravity with the stubbornness of a wasp nest disturbed.

Dale didn't look at the sky. He looked at his hands.

They were grey. Not the pale ash of a house fire, but the uniform, chitinous grey of a deep-earth burrower. The skin had hardened, shedding the pores and follicles of a thirty-eight-year-old municipal worker. Fingers, once soft enough to grip a clipboard without pain, now ended in tips that could puncture drywall with a casual tap.

"Command to Unit Seven, report status," Corliss shouted, her voice cracking over the static of her earpiece. She backed toward the porch steps, her rifle trembling.

"Dale Kowalski is hostile.

Repeat, hostile. Initiate Level Omega containment.

"

Dale ignored her. He was busy.

The prompt *SWARM COMMAND* hovered in his peripheral vision, translucent and pulsing with a faint blue light.

It wasn't a button he could press. It was a state of being.

By selecting *Y*, he hadn't issued an order; he had become the conduit.

The connection to the Rift wasn't a leash; it was a nervous system. And right now, that system was overloaded with input from three hundred and twelve distinct consciousnesses.

Small minds. Simple minds.

They wanted warmth, and they wanted protein.

They wanted to eat the house.

Dale closed his eyes for a second. He needed structure. Chaos was a code violation.

*Identify,* he thought. *Document.*

The Field Guide flickered open in his mind. The page for *Dale Kowalski* was gone, replaced by a new header: *Host Vector: Localized Hive Mind.*

Below it, a list populated rapidly.

*Subject 1: Dust Mite Variant (Class 1). Weakness: Desiccation.* *Subject 2: Chitin Shard (Class 2). Weakness: High-frequency sonic disruption.* *Subject 3: Larval Drift (Class 1). Weakness: Chemical solvent (Acetone-based).*

Three hundred and twelve entries. He didn't have time to read them all. He had to triage.

"Get down!" Corliss screamed. A heavy tactical vehicle roared around the corner, its turret spinning up. The headlights blinded the neighbors huddled on their lawns.

Mrs. Gable, three doors down, was clutching her cat carrier like a shield.

The turret locked onto Dale.

Dale didn't run. Running triggered a chase response in the swarm, and the swarm was currently uncalibrated. Instead, he raised a hand. The grey fingers spread wide.

"Stop," Dale said.

The word wasn't spoken to the soldiers. It was pushed into the static of the Rift.

The dust vortex halted.

The swirling particles froze in mid-air, creating a suspended cloud of grey matter around Dale. The turret hesitated. The target signature had changed.

The heat signature of a human male had vanished, replaced by a cold, ambient temperature consistent with the surrounding environment.

"Sensor glitch," the driver's voice crackled over the loudspeaker. "Retargeting."

"Code violation," Dale muttered to himself. "Improper targeting protocol during active infestation containment."

He focused on the nearest cluster of dust motes. They were drifting toward Mrs. Gable's cat. The cat was hissing, fur standing on end, eyes dilated.

Dale reached out with his mind, not his hand. He felt the tiny minds of the dust mites. They were hungry. They sensed the warm blood beneath the cat's fur.

*Isolate,* he thought, and he didn't attack the cat. He redirected the swarm.

The grey cloud shifted, swirling away from the porch and toward the streetlight. The mites followed the light source, their instinct for phototaxis overriding their hunger for protein. It was a basic pest control maneuver. Lure them into a trap.

But there was no trap. Only the street.

"Move the civilians," Dale said. His voice was different now.

Deeper.

It resonated in his chest, vibrating through the grey skin.

"And secure the perimeter. If you shoot me, you kill the containment field.

The infestation spreads.

"

Corliss lowered her rifle slightly.

Her eyes were wide, and she was a soldier, trained to fight monsters. But Dale wasn't acting like a monster.

He was acting like a supervisor.

"Command, the entity is... it's negotiating," Corliss whispered into her mic. "It's citing OSHA regulations."

Dale ignored the absurdity of the comment. He was checking his Field Guide. A new warning blinked in red text.

*WARNING: HOST INTEGRITY CRITICAL.* *BODY REJECTION RATE: 45%.* *CROSS-CONTAMINATION RISK: HIGH.*

The transformation wasn't stable. The Authority's system rewarded thoroughness, but it did not reward hubris.

Dale was trying to control a Class-7 entity with the mind of a Class-7 technician.

The strain was tearing him apart. His left knee buckled. A hairline fracture spiderwebbed across his forearm, grey dust puffing out from the crack instead of blood.

He couldn't sustain this. He needed to verify the threat.

Dale stepped forward.

One step. Then another.

The ground beneath his feet crunched, and the concrete was soft, turned to powder by the sheer weight of the hidden swarm.

"Corliss," Dale said. "Do you have a sample kit?"

Corliss stared at him.

"A what?"

"Sample kit. Vials.

Labels.

I need to collect a specimen of the Dust Mite Variant for the Field Guide update.

If I don't document the weakness, the Authority won't authorize the next protocol. We're stuck here.

"

The tactical team advanced. The heavy vehicle's turret tracked Dale's movement, but the soldiers on foot kept a distance. They were terrified. Not of Dale, but of the grey cloud that moved with him, a living shroud that blocked out the streetlights.

"You're talking like you're still you," Corliss said, her voice shaky.

"I am," Dale said. "I'm just wearing a different uniform."

He reached into the pocket of his work shirt. The fabric had fused to his skin, but his hand slipped through the tear. He pulled out a small, empty glass vial from his utility belt.

He'd kept it for collecting fecal pellets from termites. It was clean.

Sterile.

"Stay back," Dale ordered. "I need to capture a sample."

He moved toward the streetlight. The mites were clustering there, a shimmering halo of grey. Dale held up the vial.

He couldn't suck them in, and he had to use bait.

He reached into his other pocket and pulled out a granola bar. He snapped it in half. Sugar. Simple carbohydrates.

He threw the half-bar onto the asphalt.

The swarm hesitated. Then, driven by instinct, they dove. The grey cloud collapsed onto the snack, burying it under a mound of chitin and dust.

Dale moved fast. He plunged the vial into the mound. The mites swarmed into the glass, filling it with a swirling grey slurry. He capped it quickly.

*SPECIMEN COLLECTED.* *ANALYZING...*

A progress bar appeared in his vision. It filled slowly, agonizingly.

The tactical vehicle's turret spun again.

"Fire," the driver's voice commanded. "The entity is unstable. It's leaking particulate."

"No!" Corliss shouted. She raised her rifle, aiming not at Dale, but at the ground near his feet. "Cease fire! He's collecting evidence!"

"Target is a threat to public safety," the driver replied.

The turret fired.

A single high-caliber round ripped through the air.

It didn't hit Dale. It hit the cloud of dust beside him. The explosion of kinetic energy scattered the mites.

The vial in Dale's hand cracked.

The grey slurry spilled onto the asphalt.

Dale looked at the broken glass, and he looked at the scattered mites.

They were confused. Agitated.

Without a central pheromone trail, they paniced.

The swarm exploded outward.

"Containment breach," Dale said. His voice was flat.

The dust cloud rushed toward the tactical vehicle. It clogged the air intake. The engine sputtered and died. The turret froze.

The soldiers screamed. Not in pain, but in suffocation. The dust was fine enough to enter lungs. It coated their visors, their eyes, their mouths.

Dale stood in the center of the chaos. He didn't move. He watched the soldiers stumble, coughing, clawing at their faces.

This was not his fault. It was a failure of protocol. They had fired without a containment zone. They had violated the isolation stage.

But the Authority didn't care about blame. It cared about results.

*ALERT: UNAUTHORIZED USE OF FORCE.* *THOROUGHNESS RATING DROPPING.* *CURRENT RATING: 8.4*

Dale felt a cold pit in his stomach. Not fear.

Disappointment, and he had been a 9.5. He had never dropped below a 9.0.

He needed to fix this. Fast.

He looked at the swarm.

They were attacking the soldiers now. Biting.

Not hard enough to kill, but enough to cause panic.

If the Authority classified this as a failed extermination, Dale would lose his certification. He would be demoted. Or worse, revoked.

He had to restore order.

*SWARM COMMAND.*

He couldn't use the full protocol. It was too unstable. But he could use a sub-routine. A local override.

Dale focused on the leader of the swarm. It wasn't a queen. It wasn't a king. It was the largest cluster of mites, a knot of grey density hovering near the broken vial.

He reached out with his mind. He couldn't speak to them. He had to *feel* them.

He projected an image into their simple minds. Not a word. A sensation.

*Cold.*

He imagined winter. Deep freeze. The end of the cycle.

The swarm shivered. The chaotic movement slowed. The biting stopped.

The soldiers collapsed, gasping for air, tearing off their masks. Corliss was on her knees, wiping dust from her eyes. She looked at Dale with a mixture of horror and awe.

"You controlled them," she whispered. "You... you're the hive."

"I am the technician," Dale corrected. He pointed to the broken vial.

"I failed to secure the specimen.

I failed to maintain the isolation barrier.

I accept the penalty.

"

He looked at the Field Guide.

*PENALTY APPLIED: 2 TREATMENT POINTS.* *FIELD GUIDE LOCKED FOR 24 HOURS.*

The grey skin on his arms cracked. Not from damage, but from rejection. His body was trying to expel the foreign entity. The grey flakes fell away, revealing pink, raw flesh underneath.

Dale gritted his teeth. The pain was blinding. But he kept his posture. He kept his clipboard hand steady, even though the clipboard was gone.

"Corliss," he said. His voice was raspy. "You have five minutes before the next wave.

The Rift is widening. I can feel the pressure.

"

"How do you know?" Corliss asked, struggling to her feet.

"Because I'm part of it now," Dale said. "And I know what happens when you don't treat the nest."

The ground shook. Not from an earthquake. From a heartbeat. A massive, rhythmic thud deep underground.

The dust around Dale swirled again. But this time, it wasn't chaotic. It was forming shapes.

Arms. Legs. A head.

The swarm was trying to build him a body. A new one. One that wouldn't reject the Authority's mark.

Dale looked at his hands. The grey was returning, thicker this time. Chitinous plates formed over his knuckles. His fingernails elongated, sharpening into blades.

He looked at the neighbors, and they were running. Mrs. Gable's cat was gone.

Probably eaten.

A casualty of the incident report.

He looked at Corliss. She was aiming at him again. But her hands were shaking too hard to pull the trigger.

"Don't shoot," Dale said. "If you shoot me, you kill the only thing that can stop what's coming."

"What's coming?" Corliss asked.

Dale looked up. The Rift above the house was no longer a tear in the sky. It was an eye. A massive, vertical pupil of black void, staring down at them.

And inside the eye, something was moving.

It wasn't vermin. It wasn't a beast.

It was a face, and a face made of thousands of smaller faces.

Dale's Field Guide flashed a new entry.

*NEW SPECIES DETECTED: THE OVERLORD.* *CLASS: UNKNOWN.* *PROTOCOL: IMMEDIATE EVACUATION.*

The Authority's system didn't have a protocol for this. There was no license for gods.

Dale felt the pull of the Overlord. It wasn't commanding him. It was *recognizing* him.

It knew he was the nest.

And it was hungry.

Dale looked at his new, clawed hand. He looked at the terrified soldiers. He looked at the sky.

He had a choice. He could let the Authority revoke his license and flee as a human. Or he could accept the Overlord's invitation.

The grey skin spread to his neck. His vision narrowed. The color drained from the world, replaced by the thermal map of every living thing in the county.

He could see the heat of the neighbors. The cold of the dead soldiers. The burning fire of the Overlord's gaze.

Corliss screamed.

"Dale! What are you doing?"

Dale smiled. The smile was too wide. It showed too many teeth. And now, they were sharp.

"I'm filing a complaint," Dale said.

He reached into the Rift.
