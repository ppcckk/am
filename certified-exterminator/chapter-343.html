Chapter 343: Filing Against Gods

# Chapter 343

The clipboard was wrong.

The paper was wrong. The handwriting was wrong.

Inspector Vane watched the figure drop from the ceiling of the Audit Chamber, landing in a pile of wet, black sludge that hissed against the sterile white tiles. Vane's own clipboard trembled in his grip.

He adjusted his spectacles, wiping away a smear of condensation that had formed on the left lens.

He did not look at the man on the floor. He looked at the data stream hovering in the air above the body.

*SUBJECT: DALE KOWALSKI.* *STATUS: TERMINAL.* *THREAT LEVEL: CLASS ZERO.*

Vane tapped his pen against the side of his head, and he had processed four thousand, two hundred and twelve exterminators in this sector. Most of them died screaming.

Some died trying to punch the walls.

A rare few died while trying to file the paperwork, which was the most annoying way to die because you had to wait for the forms to burn before you could clear the floor.

This one didn't scream. This one didn't move.

He lay face down in the sludge, arms splayed, fingers twitching in a rhythmic pattern. *Tap, tap, tap. Hold. Tap.*

It looked like Morse code.

It looked like someone trying to write a violation report from the inside of a corpse.

Vane stepped back. His boots squeaked on the tile.

The sludge was expanding, eating the edges of the Audit Chamber's floor, dissolving the geometric patterns of the Authority's flooring.

It wasn't just rot; it was administrative decay, and the floor was losing its structural integrity because the entity beneath it was refusing to acknowledge the grid.

"Unit Seven," Vane said into his radio. "We have a Class Zero manifestation in Sector 4. The sludge is consuming the floor plan. I need a perimeter."

Static crackled. *

"Perimeter is established. Do you need extraction?"*

"No," Vane said. He watched the twitching hand. "It's still working. Look at the fingers."

The sludge rose. It coated the man's back, thick and viscous, smelling of ozone and old blood. Vane pulled a small, yellowed manual from his belt. *Code 44-B: Interaction with Non-Compliant Entities.* He flipped to page twelve.

*Rule:* Do not engage. Do not attempt to reason. *Exception:* If the entity displays procedural adherence, observe for compliance markers.

Vane leaned in. The man's hand stopped twitching. He lifted his head.

His eyes were gone. In their place were two small, blinking red lights, like the indicator on a faulty smoke detector.

A Geiger counter clicked in the darkness. *Click. Click. Click.*

Vane's heart hammered against his ribs, and he took a step back, then another. The red lights scanned the room. They swept over Vane's clipboard.

They swept over the doorframe.

They stopped on the sludge.

The man spoke. His voice was wet, gurgling, but the cadence was familiar.

"Entry point," the man rasped.

Vane froze.

"Structural failure," the man continued. "K-7. Immediate remediation required."

The sludge shuddered.

The man pushed himself up. He didn't look like Dale Kowalski anymore. He looked like a suit of armor made of compressed waste, held together by fraying red tape.

But he moved with a terrifying, mechanical precision. He reached into the sludge and pulled out a pen.

It was a standard-issue ballpoint. Blue ink.

He uncapped it.

Vane raised his radio.

"Cancel extraction. It's... it's filing."

***

Pain was a variable. Dale had learned to isolate it.

He stood in the center of the void, the sludge clinging to his uniform like second skin. The air tasted of copper and burnt hair. The darkness pressed against his eyes, but he didn't close them. Closing eyes was a violation of observation protocol.

*Click. Click. Click.*

The Geiger counter sound was in his skull.

It wasn't radiation, which was attention. The Authority was watching.

The Audit wasn't a fight; it was an inspection. And inspections were won by documentation.

Dale looked at his hands. They were black, coated in the decay, but his fingers were steady.

He looked up.

Above him, far above, a faint glow pulsed. The Archivist's stamp. It had marked him.

*Permanent Record.*

The words burned in his mind, but not as a warning. As a citation.

*Violation:* Unlicensed Existence. *Penalty:* Erasure.

But Dale knew the loopholes. He knew that erasure required a signed order, and signing orders required a target that could be defined. If he could not be defined, he could not be erased.

He pulled the clipboard from his belt. It was warped, the metal bent, the paper singed at the edges. He held it out in front of him.

"Field Guide," he muttered.

Nothing happened. The interface was gone. The blue text, the floating stats, the familiar prompts—all gone. The Authority had stripped his license.

But the Authority had not stripped his knowledge.

Dale closed his eyes. Not to rest. To visualize.

He saw the sludge, and he saw the way it flowed. It wasn't random.

It followed the path of least resistance, pooling in corners, climbing vertical surfaces where the gravity vectors were weak.

It was a fluid. It behaved like a colony of amoebas.

*Dimensional Sludge.*

He hadn't identified it yet. The Field Guide was blank.

If he couldn't identify it, he couldn't treat it.

But he could document it.

Dale opened his eyes. He saw the Inspector watching him from the doorway. He saw the man in the suit of bone. He saw the red lights in his own reflection on the wet floor.

He wrote.

*Date: Unknown.* *Location: Sub-Basement, Authority Facility.* *Observation: Substance exhibits semi-autonomous behavior. Reacts to sound. Reacts to intent.*

The sludge pulsed. The red lights in his eyes flared brighter.

*Hypothesis:* The substance is a manifestation of administrative backlog, and he wrote it down. The act of writing made the words real. The System wasn't just a game; it was a bureaucracy. And bureaucracies ran on forms.

A new line appeared in his mind, not in blue, but in white.

**FORM SUBMITTED: SPECIMEN ANALYSIS.** **REWARD: LOCALIZED CLARITY.**

The darkness around him thinned. Just a fraction. The Geiger counter slowed. *Click... click.*

Dale stepped forward. The sludge tried to grab his ankle. He kicked it away, not with force, but with precision. He aimed for the joint where the sludge was thinnest.

"Targeted removal," Dale said.

He reached into his belt and pulled out a small vial. It was empty. Useless. But he held it up.

"Sample collection," he announced.

The Authority loved samples.

The sludge recoiled. It sensed the intent. It sensed the categorization. By naming it, by preparing to collect it, Dale had changed its status from *Hazard* to *Subject*.

The red lights in his eyes dimmed. The pressure on his chest eased.

He wasn't fighting the Audit. He was participating in it.

***

Inspector Vane watched the man stand up straight. The sludge seemed to retreat, pulling back from the man's body as if embarrassed.

The man looked at Vane.

"Inspector," Dale said. His voice was clear now. Not wet. "Your floor plan is incorrect."

Vane blinked. "Excuse me?"

"You're using the Sector 4 grid. But the sludge is following the Sector 7 drainage protocols.

That's why it's pooling here.

" Dale pointed to a crack in the floor.

"You have a cross-contamination leak.

Class II violation.

"

Vane looked down. The sludge *was* pooling in the corner. But it wasn't random.

It was forming a pattern. A spiral.

"That's impossible," Vane said. "Sector 7 is sealed."

"Sealed is a suggestion," Dale said. "Containment is a process."

Dale walked toward the crack. He didn't run. He walked with the steady, measured gait of a man walking through a hallway to a door.

Vane wanted to shoot him. He wanted to call for backup. But he was frozen. The man was talking like he was in a training seminar.

"What do you want?" Vane asked.

Dale stopped. He looked at the floating text above Vane's head.

*INSPECTOR VANE. CERTIFICATION LEVEL IV. THOROUGHNESS RATING: 6.8.*

"6.8," Dale said.

"Low. You're cutting corners. That's why the sludge got in.

You didn't seal the vents.

"

Vane's face reddened.

"I followed protocol."

"Protocol is a baseline," Dale said. "Exterminators exceed baseline."

Dale reached into his pocket and pulled out a roll of duct tape. It was black. Standard issue. He tore off a strip.

"Move," Dale said.

"I can't move. I'm the witness."

"Then you're part of the hazard zone."

Dale slapped the tape over the crack in the floor. The sludge hissed. The red lights in Dale's eyes flickered.

**ACTION LOGGED: EMERGENCY CONTAINMENT.** **PENALTY: UNAUTHORIZED USE OF MATERIALS.** **BONUS: IMPROVISATION.**

The sludge stopped moving. It solidified. The crack was sealed.

The Geiger counter stopped.

Silence.

Vane lowered his radio. He looked at Dale. The man was breathing hard. The sludge was gone from his uniform, leaving only damp patches on the fabric.

"How did you know?" Vane whispered.

Dale looked at his hands. They were shaking. He clenched them into fists.

"I didn't," Dale said. "I guessed. But the guess was good enough to count."

Vane stepped forward. He reached for his stamp. He had to certify the closure.

"I have to stamp this," Vane said. "It's procedure."

"Stamp it," Dale said.

Vane pressed the stamp to the floor, to the sealed crack. It glowed white.

**JOB CLOSED. THOROUGHNESS RATING: 3.0.**

Dale winced. "Three? That's terrible."

"It's a minor repair," Vane said. "You're not supposed to be here, Dale. The Audit doesn't end with a stamp. It begins."

Dale looked up. The ceiling of the room was cracking.

Not physically.

Metaphysically. The reality was stretching.

"The Archivist," Dale said.

"She's not the enemy," Vane said. He looked pale. "She's the auditor. And she just found a discrepancy in your file."

"What discrepancy?"

Vane pointed to Dale's chest.

Dale looked down. The suit of bone was gone.

His uniform was back.

But over his heart, a black stain remained.

It pulsed.

**STATUS UPDATE: INFECTED.** **TYPE: ADMINISTRATIVE CORRUPTION.** **PROGRESSION: 1% PER MINUTE.**

Dale looked at the stain. It wasn't sludge. It was ink.

"You're being rewritten," Vane said. "The Authority doesn't delete files. It edits them.

If you don't stop the corruption, you won't be Dale Kowalski anymore. You'll be a character in their report.

"

Dale felt the cold spread through his chest. It felt like forgetting. Forgetting the name of the first rat he ever killed. Forgetting the smell of his wife's shampoo.

"How do I stop it?" Dale asked.

"You don't," Vane said. "You outrun it. You have to file an appeal."

Dale looked at the clipboard in his hand. The paper was blank again.

"I need a new form," Dale said.

Vane shook his head.

"There is no form for this."

"There is always a form," Dale said. He looked at the door. "Where is the Appeals Office?"

"It doesn't exist," Vane said. "It's a myth."

Dale smiled. It was a thin, cracked smile.

"Then we have to build it."

He turned to the door. The handle was locked.

Dale raised his fist. He didn't punch the door. He knocked.

*Knock. Knock. Knock.*

Three times. Standard request for entry.

The door didn't open. The lock clicked.

But the lock wasn't a key. It was a question.

**WHAT IS YOUR LICENSE NUMBER?**

Dale paused. His license number was on his badge. But his badge was gone. Lost in the fall.

He looked at Vane.

"I don't have it," Dale said.

Vane stepped back.

"Then you're trespassing."

"I'm an exterminator," Dale said. "I don't need a number. I need a workspace."

The door dissolved. Not into mist. Into paper. Sheets of white paper fluttered out, creating a tunnel.

Vane stared. "That's not possible."

Dale stepped into the paper.

"Everything is possible," Dale said, "if you fill out the paperwork correctly."

He disappeared into the white.

Vane stood alone in the room. The sludge was dry. The crack was sealed. The red lights were gone.

He looked at his clipboard. The entry for Dale Kowalski was crossed out.

In its place, written in fresh, black ink, was a new line.

**SUBJECT: DALE KOWALSKI.** **STATUS: MISSING.** **NEXT AUDIT: IMMEDIATE.**

Vane felt a cold draft on his neck. He turned around.

Behind him, in the corner where the sludge had been, a single sheet of paper lay on the floor.

It was a receipt.

**PAYMENT RECEIVED: ONE MEMORY.** **COST: THE NAME OF YOUR FIRST MENTOR.**

Vane tried to remember. He tried to recall the face of the man who had taught him how to hold a spray nozzle.

He couldn't, and the face was blank.

Vane looked at the empty space where the memory had been. He felt a hole in his mind. A clean, precise cut.

He picked up the paper. His hands were shaking.

He walked out of the room. He didn't look back.

But in the darkness of the paper tunnel, Dale Kowalski was running. Not away. Toward.

He was running toward a desk. A desk that shouldn't exist, in an office that wasn't there, to file a complaint against the god that ruled the world.

And the ink on his chest was spreading.

**PROGRESSION: 1.2%.**

He had to be faster, and he had to be thorough.

He had to win.
