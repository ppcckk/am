Chapter 327: Appeal Denied

# Chapter 327

The clipboard didn't stop the first shadow.

It deflected the swing, which was the problem. Dale had expected the hazmat-suited figure to be dead, or at least inert.

Instead, the thing lunged with the fluid, jerking motion of a marionette whose strings had been pulled by a drunkard.

The impact rattled Dale's teeth. He stumbled back, boots skidding on wet asphalt, and heard Miller scream somewhere behind him.

Dale didn't look back. Looking back was for victims. Technicians faced the problem.

He raised his left hand, fingers splayed, and activated the only active protocol he had left. *Sonic Deterrent, Class I*.

It wasn't much. It emitted a low-frequency hum designed to disorient vermin with sensitive auditory canals.

These things were wearing heavy-duty respiratory masks, and they couldn't hear it.

Or could they?

The figure in front of him paused. Its head tilted. The thick rubber of its hood creaked.

Dale adjusted the frequency dial on his wrist unit. He wasn't trying to hurt it. He was trying to annoy it.

A second figure grabbed his arm. It felt cold.

Not the cold of a corpse, but the cold of a freezer door left open in a warm room.

Dale twisted his wrist, using the leverage of the clipboard to pry the fingers loose.

He slammed the metal edge of the board into the figure's knee joint. It buckled.

*Crack.*

Not bone. Not quite. The sound was brittle, like dry plastic snapping.

Miller was screaming louder now.

"They're taking my suit! They're trying to peel it off!"

Dale didn't turn. He couldn't. If he turned, he'd have to process the visual input of a former technician's skin being stripped away by a colleague.

That was outside his current operational parameters. He needed to isolate the threat.

The first shadow was back up. It raised a rusted pipe.

Dale sidestepped. The pipe swung through the air where his head had been. He drove the point of his pen into the figure's neck.

Nothing. The pen bounced off the rubber hood.

*Ineffective against Class-2 Containment Gear.*

Dale's mind raced through the Field Guide entries. He had no entry for

"Failed Technician." He had no weakness listed. That meant he was flying blind. It meant he was violating Protocol Section 4, Paragraph B: *Never engage an unidentified entity without first establishing a baseline.*

He was already violating Protocol Section 1: *Survival.*

The shadow swung again. Dale ducked. The pipe hit the brick wall behind him, sending a shower of red dust into the air.

He needed to identify them, and he needed to document them.

He needed to eliminate them.

And he was running out of clipboard.

"Dale!" Miller's voice was wet. "It's in my mouth!"

Dale spun.

Miller was on the ground, two figures pinning him down. One had its gloved hands inside Miller's helmet seal. The other was holding Miller's arms.

They weren't trying to kill him, and they were trying to *wear* him.

This wasn't combat. This was an infestation of behavior.

Dale looked at the radio in the parked car twenty feet away. The dial was still spinning. *Help.*

He looked at the shadows. Dozens of them. All wearing hazmat suits. All moving with the same synchronized, clumsy rhythm.

They weren't individuals. They were a hive.

Dale's eyes narrowed. He tapped his wrist unit. He didn't activate *Sonic Deterrent*. He activated *Field Guide Scan*.

The System interface flickered in his peripheral vision.

**SCANNING...**

**TARGETS: 34**

**IDENTIFICATION: UNKNOWN**

**RECOMMENDED ACTION: RETREAT**

Useless.

Dale ignored the recommendation, and he watched the shadows move. They didn't walk randomly. They stepped in a pattern.

Left, right, left, shuffle. Left, right, left, shuffle.

It was a dance.

No. It was a code.

Dale had spent fourteen years reading pest control manuals, and he knew that termites followed chemical trails.

He knew that ants followed pheromone gradients. These things weren't following scents.

They were following a signal.

The radio.

The frequency wasn't a word. It was a carrier wave. A command structure.

Dale looked at the car. The radio was broadcasting. The shadows were listening. They were reacting to the broadcast.

If he could change the signal, he could change their behavior.

But he couldn't reach the car. The shadows had formed a semi-circle, cutting him off.

Miller was gone from his view, and the other figures were closing in, their movements becoming more frantic, more desperate. They were starving for a host.

Dale had one trick left.

He reached into his belt pouch and pulled out a marker. A thick, permanent marker, black, industrial strength.

He wrote on the back of his clipboard.

**VIOLATION: UNAUTHORIZED BROADCASTING. SEVERITY: CRITICAL. ACTION: TERMINATE SOURCE.**

He held the clipboard up.

The shadows stopped, and they didn't understand the writing. But they understood the gesture. A technician holding up a report meant a shutdown order. It meant the job was done.

One of the figures stepped forward. It raised its hands. Not to attack. To surrender.

Dale's heart hammered against his ribs, and he kept his face blank. He kept his posture rigid.

He was the Authority.

He was the Law.

"Protocol complete," Dale said. His voice was steady. "Evacuate the zone."

The figures hesitated.

Then, one by one, they turned. They walked back into the darkness from which they had come.

Dale didn't breathe. He watched them go. He watched the last one disappear around the corner.

He turned to Miller.

Miller was lying on the asphalt. His helmet was gone. His face was pale, eyes wide, mouth open. He was gasping.

Dale walked over. He checked Miller's pulse. Strong. Fast.

"Are you intact?" Dale asked.

Miller swallowed.

"I...

I think so, and they stopped.

They just... stopped.

"

"They were following a signal," Dale said. He looked at the car. The radio was still humming. *Help.*

"We need to shut that up," Miller whispered.

"No," Dale said. "We need to answer it."

Miller stared at him.

"You're insane."

"I'm thorough."

Dale walked to the car. He opened the driver's side door. The interior smelled of stale sweat and old rubber.

He picked up the radio handset. The static was thick.

He turned the dial. The spinning number slowed. It clicked. It clicked.

It stopped.

The word *Help* faded.

Silence.

Then, a new voice. Clear. Distinct. Human.

"Unit 7-Alpha. You are out of compliance."

Dale froze. His thumb hovered over the transmit button.

"Who is this?" Dale asked.

"The Authority," the voice said.

It was calm. Bureaucratic.

"You have failed to isolate the infestation source.

You have allowed cross-contamination with localized psychic residue.

You have not submitted your post-incident report.

"

Dale gripped the radio.

"I'm in the field. I'm handling the breach."

"You are violating Protocol 12: *Do not communicate with the breach.*"

Dale's blood ran cold.

"I didn't know," Dale said.

"Ignorance is not a defense," the voice replied. "Your Thoroughness Rating has been adjusted. From 8.5 to 4.2."

The numbers flashed in Dale's mind.

**PENALTY APPLIED.**

**CERTIFICATION LEVEL: II (PROGRESS TO III: 45%)**

He lost half his progress. In seconds.

"Restore it," Dale said.

"Only upon submission of a Class-4 Containment Report," the voice said. "And the eradication of the source signal."

"Where is it?"

"You are standing next to it."

Dale looked at the radio.

"The signal isn't coming from outside," the voice said.

"It's coming from you. Your anxiety. Your need to be recognized.

Your need to be right. The Rift feeds on procedural rigidity.

You are the conduit, Technician Kowalski.

"

Dale dropped the handset, and he looked at his hands. They were shaking.

Miller sat up.

"Dale? What did he say?"

Dale didn't answer, and he looked at the shadows retreating in the distance. They hadn't left.

They had just paused. Waiting for him to break. Waiting for him to fail.

He was the weak link, and he was the nest.

He pulled out his clipboard. His hands were steady now. The fear was gone, replaced by the cold, hard clarity of a man who has been cited for a safety violation and knows exactly how to fix it.

He opened to a new page.

**SUBJECT: SELF. STATUS: COMPROMISED. ACTION: QUARANTINE.**

He uncapped the marker, and he drew a box around his own name.

Miller stood up, swaying.

"Dale, we need to move. The Authority can't track you here, but the things can. We need to get out of the city."

Dale looked at the box he had drawn.

If he left, he was abandoning the job. If he stayed, he was becoming the job.

The radio crackled again.

"Thoroughness Rating: 4.1. You are failing, Technician."

Dale looked at Miller.

"Give me your spray can," Dale said.

Miller blinked.

"My... my bug spray? It's empty."

"I know," Dale said. "I need the can. It's conductive."

Miller handed it over. Dale took it.

He walked to the car's battery, and he stripped the insulation off the jumper cables. He touched the red cable to the spray can's nozzle. He touched the black cable to the can's base.

Sparks flew.

The radio shrieked. A feedback loop, loud and painful, tore through the air.

The shadows in the distance stopped moving. They turned toward the car. Toward Dale.

But they didn't walk closer. They flinched.

Dale had created a noise. A chaotic, unstructured, non-procedural noise.

The Rift hated chaos. It loved order. It loved rules. By breaking the rules, Dale was hurting it.

He was hurting himself, too. The feedback loop was screaming in his ears. His nose bleeded.

"Dale!" Miller shouted over the noise. "Stop!"

Dale didn't stop, and he adjusted the voltage. He increased the frequency.

He was documenting the pain.

He was isolating the noise.

He was eliminating the signal.

The shadows dissolved. Not into death, but into static. They blurred. Their hazmat suits flickered.

The voice on the radio was garbled.

"Violation... detected... system... reset..."

Dale let go of the cables.

The sparks died, and the radio went silent.

The shadows were gone.

The street was empty.

Dale stood there, bleeding from the nose, holding the melted spray can.

Miller walked up to him. He looked terrified. Not of the monsters. Of Dale.

"You're going to get us killed," Miller said.

"I'm going to get us certified," Dale said.

He wiped the blood from his lip. He looked at the car. The radio was smoking.

"We need a new plan," Dale said. "And we need a new source."

Miller looked at the smoke rising from the dashboard.

"What source?"

Dale looked at the darkness beyond the streetlights.

"The Rift isn't just a hole," Dale said. "It's a filing error. And someone has to correct the record."

He looked at his Field Guide. The entry for *Self-Reflection Entity* was gone. Replaced by a new entry.

**SUBJECT: AUTHORITY PROXY. WEAKNESS: BUREAUCRATIC RIGIDITY. STATUS: ACTIVE.**

Miller looked at the screen. He read the words. He looked at Dale.

"You're hunting the Authority?"

"I'm auditing it," Dale said.

A sound came from the sky. A low, grinding rumble. Like stones being dragged across the bedrock of the world.

The clouds parted.

A face looked down.

It wasn't a monster. It was a man.

A giant, translucent man, made of paper and ink, and he was looking at them.

He was holding a stamp.

**DENIED.**

The word appeared in the air, huge and red.

Dale looked up. He didn't run. He opened his clipboard.

He wrote: **APPEAL.**

The giant man frowned. The stamp hovered.

Dale looked at Miller.

"Run."

"Where?"

"To the next violation," Dale said. "Before he processes the rejection."

The stamp came down.
