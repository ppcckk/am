Chapter 103: Documenting the Violation

# Chapter 103

The woman in the rain didn't scream. That was the first thing that struck Dale, though he was watching her through the cracked lens of his own peripheral vision, a trick of the Authority's surveillance feed that had glitched into his retina three miles ago. She stood on the shoulder of Route 9, drenched, holding a clipboard that was rapidly disintegrating in the downpour.

She looked like a ghost. A gray, waterlogged thing with black pits for eyes and teeth that caught the neon glow of a passing billboard.

She wasn't looking at the van. She was looking at Dale's reflection in the wet asphalt.

Behind her, the treeline rippled. Not with wind.

With movement.

Hundreds of small, chitinous legs scratching against mud, the sound like dry leaves skittering over pavement. The man from the radio broadcast, the one with the serrated jaw, was gone. The Authority had pulled him back.

They always did that when a Level III threat approached.

They didn't like witnesses.

Dale slammed the brakes.

The van skidded, tires hydroplaning on the slick road, before jerking to a halt. The engine sputtered, dying. The fuel light on the dashboard blinked with a frantic, red urgency. Empty tank.

Dale didn't look at the woman.

He looked at the rearview mirror. The thing that had been chasing him was no longer running. It stood at the edge of the road, twenty feet back.

Its skin was too smooth, too tight against the bone. It tilted its head, a bird-like motion that made Dale's stomach turn.

This wasn't Rattus profundus. This wasn't even a Class-1 nuisance.

The Field Guide in his pocket hummed, a vibration against his thigh. It was trying to populate. It was trying to categorize the woman.

*Identification pending,* the text scrolled in the corner of his eye. *Cross-contamination risk detected.*

Dale ignored it.

He stepped out of the van, the rain hitting his face like gravel.

He didn't reach for the flare gun. He reached for his clipboard.

It was wet, warped, but the ink held.

"Ma'am," Dale said, his voice flat. "You are standing in a designated exclusion zone."

The woman didn't blink. The rain ran down her cheeks, dark and oily.

"The nest is behind the treeline," she said.

Her voice sounded like static. "They are nesting in the storm drains."

Dale checked his watch. 2:14 AM. The Authority's servers were likely down for maintenance in this sector, which explained the lag in the notification system. If the servers were down, the automated penalties for unauthorized engagement were paused.

But manual oversight was still active.

"If I treat this without a permit, the Thoroughness Score gets voided," Dale said. He spoke to the air, to the invisible camera that was undoubtedly watching from the sky, or from the trees, or from the mind of the gray thing standing in the rain. "But if I don't treat it, the infestation spreads to the municipal water supply by morning."

The gray thing took a step forward. Its jaw unhinged.

Dale opened his Field Guide. He didn't look at the entry for the creature. He looked at the blank page where he had scrawled the note the Authority wanted erased. *Protocol K-9: Thermal Disruption of Subterranean Hives.*

It wasn't a standard protocol. It wasn't licensed. It was a workaround. A loophole in the code that stated extermination could be performed via environmental manipulation if the target was deemed 'structural' than 'biological.'

He had spent three days arguing with the Licensing Authority that a nest of dimensional vermin in a concrete storm drain was a structural integrity issue, not a monster fight. They had laughed. They had revoked his permit for three hours.

Now, with the servers down, the laugh was gone.

Dale pulled a small, silver device from his belt. It looked like a remote control for a garage door. It was a sonic emitter, modified to broadcast at 18 hertz. The frequency of structural resonance for poured concrete.

He aimed it at the treeline.

"Warning," the Authority's voice echoed in his head, distorted by static. "Unauthorized use of Class-4 Environmental Tool. Penalty: Immediate suspension of Certification."

Dale pressed the button.

The sound wasn't audible. It was a pressure change. A vacuum. The air around the treeline shimmered.

The gray woman screamed.

Not in pain, but in recognition. Her black eyes widened.

"Too fast," she hissed.

"You are too fast."

The trees didn't fall. The ground beneath them exploded.

Concrete chunks flew into the air. The storm drain, hidden under a layer of mud and ivy, shattered.

From the hole, a cloud of black insects erupted.

Not rats.

Cockroaches. But not brown-banded.

These were the size of dinner plates, their wings thick and leathery, their mandibles clicking with the sound of grinding stones.

*Dimensional Vermin (Blatta tenebris),* the Field Guide populated instantly.

Dale didn't shoot them. He didn't stab them.

He pulled a canister from his pack.

It wasn't insecticide. It was liquid nitrogen, stolen from a cryogenic storage facility in Sector 4.

He tossed it into the hole.

The canister cracked.

The steam hissed, white and violent. The temperature in the immediate area dropped forty degrees in a second. The giant cockroaches froze, their movements jerking, slowing, stopping. Their exoskeletons, brittle from the sudden cold, cracked.

Dale walked forward.

He pulled out his flashlight. He shone it on the frozen swarm. He didn't kill them. He killed them with documentation.

He held up his clipboard.

"Infestation identified," he said to the empty air. "Species: *Blatta tenebris*.

Habitat: Municipal infrastructure.

Treatment method: Cryogenic suspension followed by structural demolition. Status: Contained."

The gray woman watched him. Her face was cracking. The skin was peeling away, revealing not muscle, but wires. Copper filaments.

"You are not human," Dale said. He didn't ask. He stated. "You are an auditor."

The woman smiled. It was a horrible, jagged thing. "I am the consequence of your thoroughness, Dale Kowalski."

The radio in the van crackled. The static cleared. A calm, bureaucratic voice returned.

"Unit 4-Alpha. You have violated Protocol 7, Section C.

You have engaged an unidentified entity without verification.

You have used unauthorized environmental tools.

Your Certification is hereby suspended. You are now an unlicensed exterminator."

Dale looked at the frozen cockroaches. He looked at the gray woman.

"I completed the job," Dale said.

"You did not file the report," the voice said.

Dale pulled a pen from his pocket. He clicked it. He wrote on the wet clipboard.

*Violation Code: K-9. Status: Resolved.*

He tore the page off. He held it up.

The Authority didn't care about paper. They cared about the digital log. But the digital log was down.

For ten minutes, maybe fifteen. The only record was the one in Dale's hand.

And the one in his mind.

The gray woman lunged.

Dale didn't dodge. He let her hit him.

She slammed into his chest, her hands—now fully revealed as claws made of copper wire—digging into his tactical vest. He staggered back, the wind knocked out of him. Pain flared in his ribs.

Broken.

Maybe two.

He grabbed her wrist. It was cold. Metal cold.

"Field Guide," Dale said. "Update entry: Auditor Entity. Weakness: Structural integrity of host vessel."

The text scrolled. *Identification complete. Weakness identified. Damage bonus: 100% against metallic constructs.*

He didn't have a weapon. He had a flashlight.

He swung it.

The beam of light hit the woman's eye.

She shrieked, a sound of tearing metal. Dale swung again. He hit the joint of her shoulder.

The copper filaments snapped.

She fell.

Dale didn't stop. He kicked her. He kicked the head. The skull crunched, not like bone, but like plastic casing.

Black fluid leaked from the wound. It sizzled on the wet asphalt.

The Authority's voice was screaming now. *Cease action. Immediate termination imminent. Sector 7 quarantine enforced.*

Dale looked at the treeline. The swarm was still frozen, but the heat from the engine of the van was melting the frost. They were twitching. Waking up.

He had one minute before they thawed. One minute before the Authority sent a Level IV enforcement team. One minute before he was erased.

He looked at the van. The fuel light was still on. He couldn't drive.

He looked at the storm drain.

It was broken open.

A portal? No.

Just a hole. But inside the hole, something was pulsing. A light.

Blue and sick.

The nest wasn't just cockroaches. It was a gateway. A minor rift, sealed by concrete, now cracked by Dale's sonic pulse.

If he left, the rift would widen. The things would pour out. The city would burn.

If he stayed, he died.

Dale pulled the flare gun from the glove box.

He loaded the flare. Red phosphorus. Enough to start a fire that could burn for three days.

Enough to seal a rift with heat and ash.

He looked at the gray woman. She was trying to stand. Her legs were tangled wires.

"You think you're saving them?" she gurgled. Black fluid bubbled from her mouth. "You're just cleaning up a mess you made."

"I'm a technician," Dale said. "I clean messes."

He pointed the flare gun at the hole in the ground.

The Authority's voice went silent. The system knew what he was doing.

It knew that if he burned the nest, he would be destroying the evidence.

The Field Guide entry would be voided. The Thoroughness Rating would drop to zero.

But the entry would exist. For ten seconds.

Dale pulled the trigger.

The flare screamed into the hole.

The blue light flared. The cockroaches, trapped in the frost, caught fire. The plastic exoskeletons melted. The air turned into smoke and stench.

Dale turned to run.

The ground shook.

The gray woman wasn't moving anymore. But the shadows were.

From the smoke, a hand emerged. Not a wire hand.

A flesh hand.

Massive.

Covered in sores.

It grabbed the edge of the storm drain.

It pulled itself up.

It was ten feet tall. It had no face. Just a mouth, open, wide, and filled with rows of shark-like teeth.

The Authority's voice returned, whispering. *Level IV Infestation detected. Class: Abyssal Breach. Certification Level Required: VI.

You are Level III. You are insufficient.*

Dale stared at the monster.

He looked at his clipboard. The page was wet. The ink was running.

He looked at the monster. It didn't care about protocol. It didn't care about identification. It just wanted to eat.

Dale reached into his pocket. He pulled out the Field Guide. The cover was scorched.

He opened it.

The entry for *Blatta tenebris* was there. And below it, a new entry, written by his hand, not the system.

*Vane, Julian. Note: Do not trust the Authority. They are not licensing us. They are farming us.*

He tore the page out. He held it up to the monster.

"Identify," Dale said.

The monster paused.

It tilted its head. It sensed the intent. The focus.

The sheer, stubborn refusal of Dale Kowalski to accept an error code.

The monster screamed.

Dale didn't run. He walked toward it.

Because if he ran, he was prey. If he walked, he was an inspector.

And an inspector doesn't run from a violation. He documents it.

Even if it kills him.

He clicked his pen.
