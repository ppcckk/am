Chapter 403: Mirror Identity Purge

# Chapter 403

The clipboard was the first thing to break.

It snapped against the concrete lip of the fissure, splintering the plastic casing into jagged shards. The paper inside didn't fall. It hovered, suspended in a gelatinous haze that smelled of copper and wet rot.

Margaret watched it hang there, and she didn't blink. Her eyelids had fused shut three days ago, sealed by a secretion the Authority called *Rattus profundus* adhesive.

She couldn't see the clipboard.

She couldn't see the eyes. But she could feel the vibration of Dale's boots hitting the ledge.

*Thud.*

*Thud.*

*Thud.*

Each impact sent a ripple through the gel. Margaret's remaining lung, the one still functioning, hitched in her chest. She tried to speak, but her vocal cords were coated in mucus. A wet gurgle emerged instead.

Inside the gel, the paper curled. Ink bled out, black tendrils swimming through the slime to form a single word.

*RETRIEVE.*

Margaret knew that word. She had heard it whispered in the vents of the old high school. She had seen it written in blood on the bathroom tiles of the municipal gym.

*RETRIEVE.*

It wasn't a command. It was a promise.

Dale stood at the edge of the world.

The drop into the fissure was estimated at forty feet. The air rising from the depths was warm, humid, and thick with particulate matter. His face shield fogged instantly. He wiped it with his sleeve, smearing the condensation into a smear that obscured his left eye.

The Authority's interface pulsed in his peripheral vision, red and urgent.

*Warning: Structural Integrity Compromised.* *Warning: Unknown Species Density: Critical.* *Recommendation: Abort.*

Dale ignored the warnings. He tapped the side of his headset.

"Log entry," he said.

His voice was steady, stripped of the tremor that had shaken his hands in the previous chapter.

"Subject: Vertical Descent.

Objective: Primary Nest Verification.

Method: Controlled rappel.

"

He checked his harness. The carabiner clicked.

The rope was rated for two tons, and the creature below was likely much heavier, but the physics didn't change because the biology was alien.

Mass was mass. Gravity was gravity.

He stepped off the edge.

The fall wasn't a plunge. It was a controlled slide, friction burning through the gloves of his harness. The eyes below multiplied.

Thousands of them. They weren't just watching.

They were waiting, and they recognized him.

The largest eye, the one that resembled the Inspector's, dilated.

Dale hit the floor of the cavern.

The impact knocked the wind out of him. He rolled, absorbing the shock, his boots sliding across a surface that wasn't rock. It was chitin. The entire floor was a single, living organism, a vast expanse of segmented plating that hummed with a low-frequency thrum.

He stood up.

The eyes blinked in unison. A wave of motion rippled through the dark, like a field of wheat bending in a wind.

Dale raised his flashlight. The beam cut through the haze, illuminating the immediate vicinity. The walls of the cavern were lined with pods.

Hundreds of them.

Some were empty. Some were not.

Inside the nearest pod, a human body floated in amber fluid.

Dale froze, and the face was young. Male. Mid-twenties. A jawline that looked familiar.

*Case File 402-B.*

Missing persons report. Filed six weeks ago. Presumed dead.

Dale stepped closer, and the pod was translucent. The man inside was conscious. His eyes tracked Dale's movement.

He mouthed a word.

*Help.*

Dale looked at the pod. Then he looked at his Field Guide. The interface popped up automatically, scanning the new subject.

*Species: Unknown. Substrate: Biological Host.* *Status: Pre-Infestation.* *Thoroughness Rating Impact: Pending.*

He couldn't save him. Not yet.

Protocol required identification first.

If he smashed the pod, he risked cross-contamination, and the fluid inside was likely a vector for the broodmother's data burst. If he broke it now, he would lose the data.

He would lose the only chance to map the network.

Dale pulled out his pen. He wrote in the air, his handwriting projected via the headset's haptic interface.

*Observation: Hosts are not dead. They are incubators.* *Action: Sample collection. Minimal invasive procedure.*

He approached the pod. He didn't use a weapon. He used a syringe from his kit. A standard 18-gauge needle.

He pierced the membrane.

The fluid resisted. It was viscous, like oil.

He withdrew a small vial of the amber substance.

The pod shuddered. The man inside screamed, but no sound came out. The vibration traveled through the chitin floor, shaking the eyes.

The largest eye narrowed.

A sound erupted from the darkness. Not a roar. A click. A series of rapid, chitinous clicks that vibrated in Dale's teeth.

*Protocol Violation,* the Authority flashed. *Unauthorized Sampling.* *Penalty: -20 Treatment Points.*

Dale ignored the notification. He capped the vial.

The eyes turned away from him. All of them. Simultaneously.

They looked up.

Toward the ceiling.

Toward the entrance where Dale had rappelled down.

Margaret was still there, on the edge, watching the gel suspend the clipboard. She felt the shift in the air. The pressure dropped. The humidity spiked.

Something was coming down.

Not a person.

A shadow.

Dale felt it before he saw it. The air in the cavern grew heavy, static electricity prickling against his suit. The flashlight beam flickered.

He looked up.

A shape descended. It was small, no larger than a house cat, but it moved with the weight of a tank. It had eight legs, segmented and sharp, ending in barbed hooks. Its body was translucent, revealing a pulsating core of blue light.

An Arachnid-variant. Class III threat.

But it wasn't alone.

Behind it, larger shapes detached from the ceiling. Dozens of them.

Dale backed away, his boots crunching on the chitin floor. He reached for his spray canister, but his fingers fumbled. The canister was empty. He had used the last of it on the broodmother.

He had no weapon, and he had no protocol.

He had only the vial.

The first arachnid landed in front of him. It raised its forelegs, preparing to strike. Its mandibles clicked, a rhythmic, predatory sound.

Dale didn't run, and he didn't fight.

He held up the vial.

The arachnid paused.

Its head tilted. The blue core in its chest pulsed in time with the pod the man was floating in.

Dale realized it.

The pods weren't prisons. They were batteries.

The hosts were fuel.

And the creatures weren't hunting. They were farming.

Dale looked at the arachnid. He looked at the pod. He looked at the vial in his hand.

He smashed the vial against the ground.

The amber fluid splattered onto the chitin floor. It sizzled, releasing a cloud of sweet, cloying vapor.

The arachnid shrieked.

It didn't attack him.

It attacked the fluid.

It dove onto the puddle, its barbed legs tearing into the chitin to get closer to the scent. It drinked, its core flaring bright white.

The other arachnids in the cave froze, and they sensed the resource. The competition.

The largest eye, the one that looked like the Inspector, widened. It wasn't watching Dale anymore. It was watching the arachnid.

And it was hungry.

Dale stepped back, pressing himself against the wall of the cavern. He watched the arachnid consume the fluid. The creature grew larger, its legs elongating, its body swelling.

It was evolving.

Fast.

Too fast.

Dale's Field Guide updated.

*Species Identified: Dimensional Parasite (Sub-variant: Consumer).* *Weakness: Resource Scarcity.* *Weakness: Competition.* *Damage Bonus: +40% against aggressive territorial disputes.*

Dale looked at the other arachnids. They were closing in. Not on him. On the one drinking.

The Inspector's eye blinked.

Slowly.

Deliberately.

It communicated a command.

The other arachnids stopped, and they formed a circle around the drinking creature. They didn't attack. They waited.

The drinking arachnid finished the fluid. It stood up, trembling. Its core was dim. It was full, but it was weak.

The circle tightened.

The eyes of the circle watched the prey.

The Inspector's eye watched Dale.

Dale realized the trap, and he had fed the prey. He had weakened the predator. He had created a vacuum that the Authority's system would fill with points if he killed the weakened creature.

But the Inspector wasn't letting him act.

The Inspector was letting the other arachnids act.

Dale looked at his interface.

*New Protocol Unlocked: Host Severance.* *Requirement: Isolate target from network.* *Cost: 50% Thoroughness Rating.*

He looked at Margaret. She was still on the edge. She couldn't see him, but she could hear the silence. The terrible, waiting silence.

Dale looked at the circle, and he looked at the vial shard on the floor.

He picked it up.

The shard was sharp. It was made of the same glass as the jar he had broken earlier. The jar that contained the voice. The voice that had unlocked Level II.

Dale didn't know what was in the jar. He had never seen it. He had only heard the voice.

But he had the shard.

And the shard was still humming.

A low, resonant frequency.

The same frequency as the broodmother's scream.

Dale held the shard up.

The Inspector's eye narrowed.

The circle of arachnids tensed.

Dale looked at the weakened arachnid, which was vulnerable. If he attacked now, he could kill it.

He could earn the points.

He could finish the job.

But the Inspector was watching.

If he attacked, the Inspector would retaliate.

If he didn't attack, the other arachnids would kill the prey, and the infestation would grow stronger, fed by the conflict.

Dale had to choose.

Exterminate the weak link, and start a war.

Or leave the weak link, and let the network stabilize, and he looked at the pod. The man inside was crying. Silent tears floated in the amber fluid.

Dale looked at the shard in his hand.

The hum grew louder.

The Inspector's eye bleeded. Black fluid seeped from the corner of the eye, dripping down the wall.

Dale understood.

The Inspector wasn't a monster, and the Inspector was a symptom.

And the cure wasn't violence.

It was documentation.

Dale raised his pen. He didn't write a report.

He wrote a question.

*Who is the Inspector?*

The word appeared in the air, projected by his headset. It hung in the dark, glowing white.

The Inspector's eye stopped bleeding.

It stared at the word.

The arachnids in the circle took a step forward.

The weakened arachnid looked at Dale.

It didn't attack.

It opened its mouth.

And it spoke.

In Dale's voice.

"Log entry," the arachnid said. Its voice was distorted, mimicking Dale's dry, procedural tone. "Subject: Vertical Descent. Objective: Primary Nest Verification."

Dale's blood ran cold, and the arachnid was mimicking him.

Not just his voice.

His protocol.

His thoughts.

The Inspector's eye widened in delight.

The System wasn't just rewarding thoroughness, which was learning from it.

And now, it was copying it.

The arachnid took a step toward Dale.

Dale stepped back.

He looked at the Authority interface.

*System Update: User Profile Mirrored.* *Warning: Identity Corruption Detected.* *Action: Immediate Purge Required.*

Dale looked at the shard, and he looked at the arachnid wearing his voice.

He looked at the pod with the weeping man.

He had to purge himself.

Or become them.
