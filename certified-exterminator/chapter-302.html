Chapter 302: Protocol Seven Controlled Burn

# Chapter 302

The clipboard sat on the concrete floor, aligned with the grout lines. Perfect alignment.

Dale didn't pick it up. He stared at the word *TARGETED*.

The ink was fresh.

It hadn't smudged. Someone had written it while he was still in the sub-basement, perhaps while he was still breathing.

A notification pinged in his peripheral vision. Not the usual soft chime of the Authority. This was a harsh, metallic scrape.

**SYSTEM ALERT: LICENSE REVOCATION IMMINENT.** **VIOLATION: FAILURE TO ACKNOWLEDGE PRE-EXISTING CONTRACT.**

The voice again. *Mr. Kowalski. You forgot to sign the receipt.*

Dale's hand went to his pocket, and the dead finger was there, wrapped in his handkerchief. Heavy.

Cold.

It felt less like evidence and more like a grenade with the pin pulled.

He stepped over the clipboard.

"Identify the contractor," Dale said. His voice was flat.

Procedural.

"If you are the Authority, your jurisdiction over private contracts is limited to infestation vectors.

If you are the Broker, you are trespassing on licensed service territory.

"

Silence from the boiler room. Then, a rustle. Paper sliding across concrete.

A second clipboard slid out from the shadows. It stopped three feet from Dale's boot.

On it was a contract. Standard municipal form. Form 4B: *Voluntary Transfer of Liability for Dimensional Breach.*

At the bottom, a signature line. Unsigned.

And next to it, a second document. A death certificate. For Dale Kowalski.

Dale knelt. He didn't look at the death certificate. He looked at the contract's header.

*Party A: The Licensing Authority.* *Party B: [Redacted].* *Party C: Dale Kowalski, Level II Technician.*

"Party C is not me," Dale said. "I never signed a tripartite agreement. This is a forgery."

*"It is a pre-authorization,"* the voice corrected. Sharp. Like scissors. *"You accepted the job.

You accepted the risk. You accepted the terms when you entered the Rift Zone without filing a Preliminary Hazard Assessment.

By default, you are the liability holder.

The Broker is merely the intermediary.

"*

Dale's stomach tightened, and he knew the rules. He had memorized them.

But he hadn't accounted for a loophole.

A *jurisdictional* loophole. The Authority could delegate liability if the technician failed to document the initial contact properly.

He had documented it. He had written it down.

But had he *filed* it?

No. He had kept it in his pocket. Unofficial. Unverified.

If he didn't file this report within the next ninety seconds, the Authority would deem the contact

"unauthorized." Unauthorized contact meant Dale was no longer a technician. He was an illegal operator. And illegal operators could be terminated. Not fired. *Terminated.*

"Sarah," Dale said. "Get the tablet. Now."

Sarah was behind him, flashlight beam shaking.

"Dale, the wiring—"

"Forget the wiring. The tablet. Access the local server. Find the submission queue for District 9."

"The servers are down," Sarah whispered. "The Broker cut the lines."

Dale looked at the clipboard on the floor. The Authority's system was offline.

That was why the Broker was here.

Not to fight.

To bury.

If he couldn't file online, he had to file analog.

But analog reports didn't count for Treatment Points. They didn't unlock protocols. They were just paper.

Unless.

Dale remembered a footnote in the Certification Manual, page 402, paragraph 7, subsection C. *In the event of total system isolation, physical submission of a completed Field Guide entry to a certified Authority Auditor constitutes immediate provisional filing.*

He didn't have an Auditor, and he had a rival.

From the boiler room shadows, a figure stepped out. Tall. Thin.

Wearing a suit that was too crisp for the dust-choked air. A badge on his lapel.

Class IV.

"Kowalski," the man said. "Stop."

It was Miller. The guy who had failed his written exam three times because he didn't understand the difference between *eradication* and *suppression*. The guy who always tried to blast things before he identified them.

"Miller," Dale said. "You're here."

"I'm here to clean up your mess," Miller said. He held a shotgun. Modified.

Runes etched into the barrel.

A Level IV protocol weapon.

"You've attracted a Class 5 entity.

The Broker.

And you've contaminated the zone.

"

"I haven't contaminated anything," Dale said. "I isolated the threat."

"You touched it," Miller pointed at Dale's pocket. "Cross-contamination penalty. You're debuffed. You're useless."

Miller raised the shotgun.

"Drop the evidence," Miller ordered. "If you hand it over, I can suppress the breach.

The Authority will write it off as a localized anomaly.

You keep your license.

You keep your pension.

"

Dale looked at the shotgun. Then he looked at Miller's Field Guide, clipped to his belt.

Miller's guide was open. Page 12. *Dimensional Broker: Weakness - Structural Instability of Contractual Terms.*

Miller hadn't read it. He'd just glanced at it.

Dale's own guide was in his mind. He had written a new entry, in his head, during the walk down the stairs.

*Entity: Dimensional Broker (Variant: Bureaucratic).* *Weakness: Validity of Consent. If the technician does not consent to the contract, the contract is void. Voiding a contract requires a formal objection filed within the window of engagement.*

"Miller," Dale said. "Your protocol is *Suppression*. It works on mindless vermin. It doesn't work on intelligent entities bound by law."

"Shut up," Miller said. He racked the slide. "I'm doing this by the book. Section 4, Paragraph 1: Immediate elimination of unlicensed operators."

"I am licensed," Dale said. "I'm just... temporarily suspended."

"Temporarily," Miller sneered. "That's a gap in your paperwork. And gaps get filled."

Miller fired.

The shotgun boomed. The pellets hit the wall where Dale had been standing. But Dale had moved. Not away. *Sideways.* Behind the boiler.

The pellets sparked against the iron.

Dale didn't run. He pulled the dead finger from his pocket.

It was ugly. Grey skin. Black nails.

The joint had been severed cleanly. No blood.

Just dry, ancient flesh.

"Sarah," Dale called out. "Read the last line of the contract on the floor. Aloud."

Sarah, trembling, looked at the contract.

"It says... *'By accepting delivery, the recipient assumes all liabilities arising from the transaction.'*"

"Read the date," Dale said.

"It's... yesterday."

"And the time?"

"11:42 PM."

Dale checked his watch. 11:45 PM.

Three minutes.

The Broker's contract had a retroactive clause. It tried to bind Dale to events that had already happened. A classic scam. A classic code violation.

"Miller," Dale shouted. "You're firing on a licensed technician. That's a Class II infraction.

Your rating will drop.

Your points will vanish. Are you sure you want to spend your last Protocol Point on me?

"

Miller hesitated. His shotgun dipped.

Dale stepped out from behind the boiler. He held the dead finger up.

"This is not evidence of a monster," Dale said.

"It is evidence of a breach of fiduciary duty. The Broker is not an animal. It is a contractor.

And it violated the Ohio State Code on Deceptive Business Practices.

"

Miller laughed. It was a nervous, jagged sound.

"You're arguing law with a monster."

"I'm arguing law with a *system*," Dale said. "Look at your Field Guide. Page 12. Does it have a sub-clause about *Consent*?"

Miller glanced down. He flipped the page.

He paused.

"Where is it?" Miller muttered.

"It's not there," Dale said.

"Because you didn't document the weakness.

You assumed the weakness was physical. It's legal.

And because you didn't identify it, you can't use it. You're attacking with a blunt instrument. And you're contaminating the zone with your ignorance.

"

Miller's face went white. The Authority's interface flickered in his eyes.

**WARNING: FIELD GUIDE INCOMPLETE. DAMAGE REDUCTION: 40%.**

Miller's shotgun sputtered. The runes dimmed.

"That's not fair," Miller snarled. "You didn't write it down either!"

"I wrote it in my head," Dale said. "And I have a witness."

He pointed to Sarah.

"Sarah, state for the record: Did I identify the entity as a *Bureaucratic Broker* prior to engagement?"

Sarah swallowed. She looked at Miller, then at Dale.

"Yes," she said. Her voice was small, but clear.

"You identified it.

You said it was a middleman. You said contracts need signatures.

"

**SYSTEM NOTIFICATION: VERIFIED WITNESS TESTIMONY.** **FIELD GUIDE UPDATE: COMPLETE.** **TARGETED DAMAGE BONUS: +150% AGAINST DIMENSIONAL BROKER (VARIANT: BUREAUCRATIC).**

The air in the room changed. It grew heavy. Static electricity prickled on Dale's skin.

Miller screamed. He fired again.

The blast hit Dale's shoulder. Pain, sharp and hot. But not enough to stop him. Dale had a protocol for pain. *Pain is data.*

He didn't dodge. He advanced.

He walked toward Miller. Step by step.

"Stop," Miller yelled. He racked the slide. The shotgun clicked empty.

"You don't have the ammo," Dale said. "You spent it on suppression. You wasted your treatment points on a non-combatant."

Miller fumbled for a reload. His hands shook.

Dale reached the boiler. He slammed the dead finger against the iron surface.

*Clack.*

The sound echoed.

"Broker!" Dale shouted into the dark. "I object to the contract. I refuse consent. I am filing a formal objection under Section 9, Paragraph 3 of the Licensing Authority Bylaws."

Silence.

Then, the shadows peeled back.

Not the Broker.

The Authority, and a figure made of light and geometry stood in the center of the room. It held a stamp. A red, burning stamp.

*"Objection Received,"* the figure said.

Its voice was the sound of pages turning. *

"Evaluation: Valid. Reason: Lack of informed consent.

Action: Voiding Contract.

Penalty: Issued to Party B (Broker).

"*

The light flared.

Miller was thrown backward. He hit the wall. Hard.

Dale staggered. The pain in his shoulder throbbed. He looked at his Field Guide.

**NEW ENTRY ADDED: AUTHORITY AUDITOR (CLASS 0).** **STATUS: NEUTRAL.** **WARNING: AUDITORS DO NOT ENGAGE IN COMBAT. THEY ENFORCE COMPLIANCE.**

The Auditor vanished.

The Broker's presence remained. But it was weaker. The sharp voice was gone. Replaced by a low, grinding growl.

The floor cracked.

From the crack, something emerged. Not a rat. Not a beetle.

A hand. Human-sized. But made of ash and ink. It grabbed the edge of the concrete.

And then another hand.

And then a face.

It wore Miller's face.

Dale froze.

The thing with Miller's face smiled. Its teeth were made of paper.

*"Mr. Kowalski,"* the thing said, in Miller's voice. *"The audit is over. The contract is void. But the debt remains."*

Dale looked at Miller, who was unconscious against the wall, and he looked at the ash-thing.

He looked at his empty hands.

He had no weapons. No protocols. No points.

And the thing with Miller's face pulled itself out of the ground.

Dale reached into his pocket. He felt the dead finger.

He pulled it out, and he looked at the thing.

"Sarah," Dale said. "Run."

Sarah didn't run. She raised her flashlight.

Dale threw the finger.

It hit the ash-thing's chest.

The finger burned. Blue flame.

The thing shrieked. A sound like tearing fabric.

But it didn't die.

It looked at Dale.

*"You have one minute,"* it said. *"The Authority is watching. If you do not complete the extermination protocol, you will be audited. Permanently."*

Dale looked at the thing. He looked at the unconscious Miller. He looked at Sarah.

He had to kill it. But he had no tools.

He looked at the boiler, and the boiler was old. Rusted. A pressure valve stuck in the

"open" position.

A code violation.

Dale smiled. It wasn't a happy smile. It was the smile of a man who had just found the loophole.

"Sarah," Dale said. "Get me the wrench from the toolbox. The big one."

"What are you going to do?"

"I'm going to create a hazard," Dale said. "And then I'm going to cite it."

He grabbed the wrench.

The ash-thing lunged.

Dale swung the wrench not at the monster, but at the boiler's main gas line.

The pipe burst.

Gas hissed.

Dale pulled a lighter from his pocket.

"Protocol 7: Controlled Burn," Dale said. "For infestations of organic material in enclosed spaces."

He flicked the lighter.

The flame danced.

The ash-thing froze.

It knew what was coming.

And Dale Kowalski, Level II Technician, did not hesitate, and he dropped the lighter.

The lighter hit the floor.
