Chapter 260: Protocol in the Void

# Chapter 260

The red light burned through Dale's eyelids before he even registered movement.

Cold linoleum pressed against his cheek. It smelled of bleach and old blood, a scent profile Dale associated with the basement of the Elm Street duplex during the '09 termite crisis.

He didn't panic.

Panic was inefficient. It wasted oxygen and elevated heart rate, both of which were detrimental to fine motor control when handling hazardous materials.

He rolled onto his back. The ceiling was gone. In its place, a void stretched upward, filled with rotating geometric shapes that hurt to look at directly.

*System Notification: Location Updated.* *Zone: The Administrative Void.* *Status: Active Contract.*

Dale sat up. His clipboard was in his left hand. The pen was still in his right. The tape roll was gone.

Good. He hadn't taken the heart. He had followed protocol. He had requested the service, and the service had been rendered.

But the room around him was wrong. The walls weren't walls. They were filing cabinets. Thousands of them, stretching into the darkness, labeled with dates that hadn't happened yet.

A sound echoed from the far end of the aisle. The sharp *clack-clack-clack* of hard shoes on tile.

Dale stood.

His knees popped. He checked his pockets.

No spray.

No bait gel. Just the clipboard and the pen.

He walked forward. The floor clicked under his boots. The air was thin, dry enough to crack his lips.

"Mr. Kowalski."

The voice came from everywhere and nowhere. It sounded like a printer jamming.

Dale stopped. He raised his clipboard.

"I am responding to the summons. I am here to complete the transaction."

The filing cabinets shifted. A gap opened. A figure stepped out.

It was tall. Thin. Dressed in a suit that looked like it was made of static. Its face was a smooth, oval mirror.

*New Entity Detected: Auditor Prime.* *Threat Level: Class IV.* *Recommendation: Do Not Engage. Do Not Engage. Do Not Engage.*

Dale ignored the recommendation. He had seen worse. He had seen the Hive Queen in the sewers of Dayton, and he had seen the Void Spiders in the subway tunnels. This thing was just another violation.

"Your contract was accepted," the Auditor said. Its voice didn't move its mouth. The sound vibrated the air. "But the invoice is incomplete."

"I left the heart," Dale said. "I left everything."

"You left a discrepancy." The Auditor pointed a long, spindly finger at the floor. "Item 7-B. The emotional residue of the Consumer was not logged. This constitutes a Cross-Contamination Event."

Dale blinked. Cross-contamination. The 40% debuff. The 24-hour lockout.

If he was contaminated, he couldn't treat anything else. He couldn't level up. He couldn't get his license renewed. He would be stuck here, in the void, forever.

"I didn't touch it," Dale said. "I stepped away. I requested the service. The service was completed."

"The intent was not cleared," the Auditor said. "You desired the outcome.

You wanted to be important.

That desire lingers in the file.

It is now part of the record.

"

Dale felt a cold weight in his stomach. He remembered the thought. *I want to be licensed.* It had been a desperate, clawing need. A hunger.

The Auditor had logged it.

"What is the penalty?" Dale asked.

"Restoration," the Auditor said. "You must identify the specific node of contamination and excise it. If you fail, you become part of the archive."

The filing cabinets behind the Auditor opened. A swarm of things flew out. Not flies.

Not bees. Small, black cubes with eyes.

*Infestation Detected: Administrative Mites.* *Species: *Mordantia burocrata*.* *Weakness: Unknown.*

Dale's Field Guide was blank. He had no entry. He had no tools.

The mites descended. They moved with terrifying speed, buzzing like angry wasps. They landed on Dale's clipboard.

*Warning: Document Corruption Imminent.*

Dale slapped the paper. A mite died, leaving a smear of black ink on the page.

He looked at the Auditor. The creature was watching. Waiting.

"You have no tools," the Auditor said. "You have no authority. You are a Level II Technician in a Zone of Class IX Bureaucracy. You are out of scope."

Dale looked at the pen in his hand. A standard ballpoint. Blue ink.

He looked at the mites. They were eating the text on his clipboard. The words were disappearing, turned into dust.

If the document was gone, the contract was void. If the contract was void, he was still in the void.

But the System had a rule. A loophole Dale had learned in Chapter 14, when he'd fought a swarm of Glass Wasps with a vacuum cleaner.

*Protocol 4-B: Administrative Erasure.*

It wasn't a combat protocol. It was a filing protocol. It allowed a technician to cancel a pending transaction if the transaction was invalid due to procedural error.

Dale had to prove the transaction was invalid, and he had to prove that his desire to be important was not a contamination, but a pre-existing condition.

He clicked the pen.

"I object to the citation," Dale said.

The mites paused. The Auditor tilted its mirror-face.

"State your basis."

"Item 7-B, Emotional Residue, is not a contaminant," Dale said. His voice was flat. Steady. "It is a baseline metric of the Technician."

The Auditor did not move.

"Explain," it said.

Dale stepped forward. The mites parted around his boots. He held the pen out.

"The Licensing Authority requires candidates to demonstrate *Drive*," Dale said. "Section 4, Paragraph 2 of the Candidate's Handbook. 'A technician must possess the intrinsic motivation to resolve infestations.' This motivation is classified as 'Professional Ambition.'"

He pointed at the void.

"My desire to be licensed is not a contaminant. It is a certification requirement. It is the fuel for the protocol. If you delete it, you delete the technician."

The Auditor's mirror-face rippled.

"That is a semantic argument."

"It is a procedural fact," Dale said. He clicked the pen again. The tip sparked. A small blue light.

"I am invoking Rule 12-C: Pre-Existing Conditions. The contamination you identified existed prior to the encounter. Therefore, it cannot be logged as a new violation. It is part of the equipment."

The Auditor stepped back. The filing cabinets groaned.

*System Alert: Argument Accepted.* *Checking Database...*

Dale held his breath. He stared at the black cubes. He needed them to stop eating.

The mites froze.

*Result: Citation Dismissed.* *Contamination Level: Normal.*

Dale exhaled. His hand shook. He gripped the clipboard tighter.

"However," the Auditor said, "the invoice remains unbalanced. You entered the Zone. You accepted the service. You did not provide proof of completion."

"I left the heart," Dale said.

"The heart is not the proof. The proof is the report."

Dale looked at his clipboard. The paper was half-eaten. The text was gone.

He had no report.

"You have ten seconds to generate a valid completion log," the Auditor said. "If you fail, you are archived."

*Time Limit: 10.* *9...*

Dale looked at the blank paper. He looked at the pen.

He couldn't write the report, and he didn't remember the details. The fight with the Consumer. The mirror floor.

The choice.

It was gone.

*8...*

He looked at the mites. They were waiting for more.

*7...*

He looked at the Auditor.

"I don't have the data," Dale said.

"Then you fail," the Auditor said.

*6...*

Dale looked at the pen, and he didn't need data. He needed a signature.

*System Rule Check: Certification Level II.* *Privilege: Auto-Log.*

Dale had never used it. It was a lazy feature.

It generated a generic report based on the outcome, which was sloppy work. It earned a low Thoroughness Rating.

But it was fast.

He pressed the pen to the paper.

*Auto-Log Initiated.*

The pen moved on its own. It scribbled lines. Not words. Lines.

*5...*

The Auditor watched. The mites watched.

The paper filled with blue scribbles.

*4...*

It wasn't a report. It was a receipt.

*Transaction Complete.* *Client: Kowalski, Dale.* *Service: Emotional Amputation.* *Payment: Identity.*

*3...*

Dale signed his name.

The mites flew up. They circled the paper. They ate the scribbles. They ate the signature.

*2...*

The paper turned white. Blank.

*1...*

Dale held the blank clipboard up.

"Job done," he said.

The Auditor paused.

The mirror-face reflected Dale. For a second, Dale saw himself.

But the reflection wasn't him, which was older.

Greyer. The eyes were empty.

Then the reflection winked.

*Thoroughness Rating: 1.2.* *Points Awarded: 5.* *Status: Probation.*

The Auditor nodded.

"Your file is closed. You are dismissed."

The red door appeared. It was right in front of Dale.

He stepped through it.

***

Dale hit the ground.

Concrete. Cold. Wet.

He was in an alley. Rain was falling. It smelled of ozone and garbage.

He stood up. His legs felt heavy. His back ached.

He looked at his clipboard. The paper was blank.

He looked at his hands. They were shaking.

He reached into his pocket for a cigarette. He didn't smoke. He never had. But he found a pack there.

He lit one. The flame flickered in the wind.

A notification pinged in his vision.

*New Quest: The Backlog.* *Objective: Clear 50 Class III Infestations within 48 hours.* *Reward: Certification Level III Access.* *Penalty for Failure: Licensure Revocation.*

Dale stared at the text.

Fifty jobs. In two days.

He looked around the alley. It was dark. The rain was pouring.

In the distance, a scream tore through the air. Not a human scream. A metallic, grinding sound.

Something was wrong with the city, and he heard a siren. Then another. Then ten more.

The street lights flickered. One exploded. The glass rained down on the pavement.

A truck crashed into the side of the building across the street. It was a delivery truck. It was skidding, sliding toward the corner where Dale stood.

Dale didn't move. He watched the truck.

The driver was screaming. The door was open. A claw, black and chitinous, was wrapped around the driver's throat.

Dale looked at the truck.

*Vehicle Infestation Detected.* *Species: Iron Beetle.* *Level: Class III.*

He looked at the time on his watch, and he had fifty jobs.

He walked toward the truck.

He didn't run. He walked.

He pulled the pen from his pocket, and he clicked it.

"I need a sample," Dale said to the rain.

The claw pulled the driver out. The driver fell onto the wet asphalt. He wasn't moving.

The Iron Beetle crawled out of the truck. It was the size of a small car. Its shell was made of twisted metal and rust. Its eyes were glowing red LEDs.

It looked at Dale.

Dale held up his clipboard.

"I am filing a complaint," Dale said.

The beetle shrieked. It charged.

Dale didn't dodge. He stepped aside. He let the beetle crash into the brick wall.

*Crunch.*

The beetle stuck in the wall. It struggled. Its legs churned the masonry.

Dale walked over to it. He reached into his pocket.

He pulled out the pack of cigarettes, and he didn't have a spray. He didn't have a net.

But he had a lighter. And he had a can of hairspray in his other pocket, which he'd found in the alley earlier.

He sprayed the hairspray on the beetle's legs.

The metal shell hissed. The hairspray reacted with the rust.

It wasn't a poison. It was a cleaning agent.

But the Field Guide didn't know that.

The beetle froze. Its legs locked up.

Dale clicked his pen.

"Identification confirmed," he muttered. "Weakness: Oxidative Locking."

He wrote it down.

*New Entry: Iron Beetle.* *Weakness: Chemical Oxidation via Aerosol.*

The beetle tried to move. It couldn't.

Dale stepped onto its back. He looked at the driver.

The driver was alive. Barely.

"Stay down," Dale said.

He looked at the city. The sirens were getting closer. The sky was turning green.

He had forty-nine jobs left.

And the Authority was watching, and he took a drag of the cigarette. He coughed.

He opened his clipboard to the next page.

He started writing.
