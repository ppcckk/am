Chapter 119: Certified Parasite

# Chapter 119

Bleach burned. It was the wrong concentration, but it was the only thing within reach of the sink.

Dale scrubbed at his forearm until the skin turned pink and raw, watching the black residue swirl down the drain before vanishing entirely.

The water ran clear. The stain on his skin remained.

**DECONTAMINATION FAILED.** **STATUS: INTEGRATED.** **NOTE: YOUR BIOLOGICAL SIGNATURE IS NOW RECOGNIZED AS INFESTATION VECTOR.**

The notification floated in the center of his vision, translucent and mocking.

Dale ignored it. He grabbed a paper towel, dabbed the wet area, and checked the clock. 03:14 AM.

The doppelganger was on the porch.

The house was sealing itself from the outside. And Dale was now, according to the Authority, a Class-IX parasite.

He walked back to the living room. The doppelganger hadn't broken the door.

It was simply standing there, hand resting on the frame, waiting.

It wore Dale's clothes—khaki work pants, a polo shirt with a coffee stain on the left chest—but the fabric looked stiff, new.

Like it had been printed than woven.

Dale didn't shout. He didn't beg. He pulled out his clipboard.

The ash writing was gone. In its place, fresh ink appeared, flowing across the page as if written by a ghost's hand. It wasn't a job order. It was a form.

**FORM 114-C: VOLUNTARY SELF-QUARANTINE AND SUBMISSION.** **SUBJECT: DALE KOWALSKI.** **CLASS: IX.** **RECOMMENDED ACTION: IMMEDIATE INCINERATION OF SUBJECT.**

Dale stared at the form. He uncapped his pen. His hand shook, just once. Then he wrote.

*Requesting appeal based on Protocol 9: Unintentional Infestation Vector.*

The ink dried instantly. Below his handwriting, new text appeared.

**APPEAL REJECTED.** **SUBJECT IS CONTAGIOUS.** **SUBJECT IS HOSTILE.** **SUBJECT IS THE JOB.**

A heavy thud echoed from the front door. Not a knock. A lock engaging from the outside.

The doppelganger was sealing the exit. Dale looked at the windows.

The glass was fogged, opaque.

The air in the room grew thick, smelling of ozone and old blood.

If he stayed, the house would digest him.

If he tried to leave, the doppelganger would kill him. But the system said he was the infestation.

Infestations have nests.

Infestations have trails.

Dale looked at the floor. A faint, black residue trailed from the wall to the kitchen, where he had touched the slime. He followed it with his eyes. It led to the pantry.

He walked toward the pantry. The doppelganger didn't follow. It remained at the door, watching. Its head tilted, a mechanical, bird-like motion.

Dale opened the pantry door. The shelves were stocked with canned goods, but the back wall was missing.

A hole had opened in the drywall, revealing not the next room, but a swirling vortex of black smoke.

The smell was overpowering.

Rotting meat and sweet flowers.

He stepped closer. The system pinged.

**NEW FIELD GUIDE ENTRY DETECTED.** **SPECIES: RATTUS PROFUNDUS (DIMENSIONAL VERMIN).** **VARIANT: ECHO-CHIMERA.** **WEAKNESS: RECURSIVE OBSERVATION.**

Dale froze. *Recursive Observation.* He had never heard that term. He flipped through his mental Field Guide. The entry for the Echo-Chimera was blank except for the weakness.

He had to identify the weakness to gain the bonus. He had to look at the doppelganger.

He turned around.

The doppelganger was still at the door. But it wasn't looking at him. It was looking at the pantry.

It was watching the vortex.

Dale realized then. The doppelganger wasn't trying to kill him. It was trying to feed. It was using Dale as bait.

He closed the pantry door. He needed to document this.

He needed to isolate it.

He needed to eliminate it. But he was the infestation.

He pulled out his phone. No signal.

He opened the Authority's diagnostic app.

It was running in the background, a low-level scan he always kept active for compliance.

It showed a heat map of the house. The pantry was hot.

The front door was cold. And Dale himself was glowing red.

He walked to the center of the room. The doppelganger watched him.

"State your identification," Dale said. His voice was flat. Procedural.

The doppelganger didn't speak. It raised its hand again. This time, it held a clipboard.

Dale looked at the clipboard. It was identical to his own. The ink was moving.

**FORM 115-A: INFESTATION CONTROL LOG.** **ACTION: TERMINATE HOST.**

Dale realized the game. The doppelganger wasn't a monster.

It was a technician.

A Class IX technician. It was doing his job. And it was doing it better than he was.

He had to contaminate the process.

He looked at the black slime on his arm.

It was still there. He looked at the doppelganger.

It was clean.

Sterile.

He walked toward the doppelganger. Slowly. Deliberately.

The doppelganger tensed. It shifted its weight.

Dale held out his arm. The black residue glistened.

"Contamination," Dale said. "Class IX vector. Direct contact."

The doppelganger stepped back. It didn't want to touch him. It wanted to kill him from a distance. Because if it touched him, it would become contaminated too.

Dale lunged. He didn't strike. He brushed his arm against the doppelganger's chest.

The contact was brief. A split second.

The system shrieked.

**CROSS-CONTAMINATION EVENT.** **SOURCE: DALE KOWALSKI.** **TARGET: RIVAL TECHNICIAN.** **PENALTY: 40% DEBUFF TO TARGET.** **FIELD GUIDE STATUS: CORRUPTED.**

The doppelganger staggered. Its form flickered.

For a second, it looked like a pile of ash.

Then it stabilized, but it was slower.

Its movements were jerky.

Dale backed away. He hadn't killed it. But he had poisoned it.

The doppelganger raised its clipboard. The ink was scrambled. Unreadable.

**ERROR: LOG CORRUPTION.** **PROCESSING DELAY: 30 SECONDS.**

Dale ran. He ran for the back door. The kitchen.

The glass of the oven was still fogged, but he could see his reflection. He was smiling.

He wasn't smiling.

He kicked the back door. It was locked. He kicked it again. The wood splintered.

**DETECTION ALERT: UNAUTHORIZED EXIT.** **SUBJECT: KOWALSKI.** **STATUS: ESCAPED HOST.**

The house groaned. The walls contracted. The ceiling lowered.

Dale squeezed through the broken door into the yard. The air outside was cold. The stars were wrong. Too many of them.

He looked back at the house. The doppelganger was standing in the doorway. It wasn't following. It was watching.

He walked to his truck. The truck was untouched. He got in. He started the engine.

**JOB UPDATE: RECURSIVE INFESTATION.** **STATUS: PARTIALLY CONTAINED.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 2.1.** **NOTE: POOR PERFORMANCE. REVIEW REQUIRED.**

Dale drove. He didn't look in the rearview mirror. He kept his eyes on the road.

The black stain on his arm pulsed.

It was spreading.

He needed a hospital.

No. He needed a clinic. The Authority's clinic.

The one in the county seat.

He drove for an hour.

The stain reached his elbow.

He felt dizzy. His thoughts were slippery.

He kept thinking about the nest. *Find the nest.*

But what was the nest?

He pulled into the parking lot of the Authority branch. It was a small, concrete building with a single door. He got out. His legs felt weak.

He walked to the door. He knocked.

The door opened. A woman in a grey suit stood there. She held a tablet.

"Mr. Kowalski," she said. Her voice was bored. "You're early."

"I'm infected," Dale said. He held out his arm.

The woman looked at the black stain. She didn't flinch. She tapped her tablet.

**SCAN COMPLETE.** **SUBJECT: DALE KOWALSKI.** **STATUS: CLASS IX VECTOR.** **DISPOSITION: QUARANTINE.**

"Go to room 4," she said. "They'll take care of it."

Dale walked into the building. The hallway was long and white. Room 4 was at the end. The door was open.

Inside was a sterile room. A metal table. Restraints.

A man in a mask stood by the table. He held a scalpel.

"Sit down," the man said.

Dale sat. He felt a wave of nausea. The black stain had reached his shoulder.

The man in the mask leaned over him. He looked at the stain. Then he looked at Dale.

"Who taught you how to do that?" the man asked.

"Do what?" Dale asked.

"The contamination. The brush against the rival."

Dale paused. He thought about the doppelganger. The clipboard. The error.

"It was an accident," Dale said.

The man smiled. It was a cold smile.

"It wasn't an accident. It was a protocol."

The man raised the scalpel.

"Protocol 9-Alpha: Self-Detonation."

Dale's eyes widened. He looked at the monitor on the wall.

It showed his vitals.

His heart rate was steady. But his brain activity was spiking.

He looked at the man.

"You're not a doctor," Dale said.

"No," the man said. "I'm the exterminator. And you're the bug."

The scalpel came down.

Dale jerked his arm up.

The black stain moved faster than his muscle. It surged up his neck, wrapping around the scalpel.

The metal hissed.

The man in the mask pulled back, shouting.

The stain pulsed. It grew. It covered Dale's face.

He couldn't breathe.

He couldn't see.

But he could hear the chime.

**FIELD GUIDE UPDATE.** **NEW WEAKNESS IDENTIFIED: THE OBSERVER.** **DESCRIPTION: THE INFESTATION ONLY EXISTS IF IT IS SEEN.** **ACTION: BLIND THE OBSERVER.**

Dale thrashed. He knocked the table over.

He rolled onto the floor.

He couldn't see, but he could hear the man's footsteps.

Fast. Panicked.

Dale reached for his pocket. His phone. He pulled it out.

The screen was cracked, but it still worked. He opened the camera.

Flash.

Max brightness.

He pointed it at the man.

The flash fired.

The man screamed. He covered his eyes.

Dale felt the stain recede. Just a little. The pressure in his head eased.

He crawled toward the door. He had to leave. He had to get out.

But the door was locked.

He hit the door with his shoulder. Again. Again.

It didn't budge.

He looked at the man. The man was on his knees, rubbing his eyes. He wasn't looking at Dale.

Dale realized something. The stain wasn't reacting to his movement. It was reacting to the *attention*.

If no one was looking at him, he wasn't real. If he wasn't real, he couldn't be digested.

He stopped moving. He held his breath. He made himself as small as possible.

The man stood up. He looked around the room. He didn't look at Dale.

He looked at the broken table. He looked at the scalpel on the floor.

He looked right through Dale.

Dale felt a chill. He was invisible. Not to the eye, but to the system.

He walked to the door. He turned the handle. It clicked.

He stepped out into the hallway. The woman in the grey suit was gone. The lights were off.

He walked to the exit. He pushed the door open.

The night air hit him. The stars were gone. The sky was a void.

He looked at his arm. The stain was still there. But it was smaller.

He looked at his phone. A new message.

**JOB COMPLETE: PARTIAL.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 4.5.** **NOTE: YOU HAVE DISCOVERED THE HIDDEN PROTOCOL.** **WARNING: THE AUTHORITY IS NOW AWARE OF YOUR EXISTENCE.** **NEW RIVAL DETECTED: THE INSPECTOR.** **OBJECTIVE: FIND THE NEST BEFORE THE INSPECTOR FINDS YOU.**

Dale looked at the dark road ahead. He didn't know where the nest was. He didn't know who the Inspector was.

But he knew one thing.

He wasn't fighting monsters anymore.

He was hiding from the people who certified them.
