Chapter 211: Inspection Termination

# Chapter 211

The purple light swallowed him whole.

It wasn't warm. It wasn't cold.

It felt like being pulled through a straw made of static electricity.

Dale Kowalski didn't scream. He didn't thrash. He kept his eyes open, scanning the swirling vortex for structural weaknesses, entry points, or nesting sites.

The sensation of falling lasted three seconds. Then, gravity asserted itself with brutal suddenness.

Dale hit the floor. Concrete. Hard. Unyielding.

He rolled with the impact, a technique drilled into him during the mandatory safety module for high-altitude maintenance. His shoulder throbbed, a sharp, familiar ache that reminded him of the time he'd slipped on wet tile in a commercial kitchen. He pushed himself up. His hands scrabbled for purchase.

No water.

The air smelled of ozone and rot.

Dale blinked. His Field Guide was still blank. The suspension notice hovered in his peripheral vision, a red, pulsing warning that screamed *License Invalid*.

He ignored it. The notice was a symptom. The problem was the location.

He was in a basement.

But not a residential one. The walls were lined with pipes the size of subway tunnels.

The ceiling was lost in shadow.

The floor was slick with a viscous, black fluid that oozed from the seams in the concrete.

To his left, a rusted workbench. On it, a single, pristine clipboard.

Dale froze.

He didn't touch it. Not yet.

He scanned the room. Three exits. Two sealed with heavy blast doors. One open, leading to a narrow corridor.

Movement in the corridor.

Dale's hand drifted toward his pocket. Empty.

His spray can was gone.

His flashlight was gone.

His boots were gone. He was barefoot, covered in black slime, and wearing a t-shirt that had been shredded by the pump blades.

A figure stepped into the basement.

It wasn't the Auditor.

This one was human. Short. Stocky.

Wearing a heavy, rubberized apron that looked like it had been made from a shark's skin. A face mask hung around his neck, revealing a mouth twisted in perpetual disgust.

The man held a wrench the size of a crowbar.

"You're early," the man said. His voice was gravelly.

Dale didn't answer. He kept his eyes on the clipboard.

"Earliest," the man corrected himself.

He pointed the wrench at Dale. "And you're dead. Or you're about to be.

Which one is it, Kowalski?"

"Neither," Dale said. His voice was steady. Too steady. "I'm undocumented."

The man snorted. "Undocumented.

Right.

That's what they say. Before I crush their skulls and file the paperwork."

He took a step forward. The black fluid on the floor squelched.

Dale looked at the wrench. Then at the man's boots.

Thick-soled.

Grip-heavy.

But covered in the same black slime.

Slime.

Dale's mind clicked. The black fluid wasn't just water. It was frass.

Insect excrement. Specifically, the waste product of a Class-IV burrowing entity.

The man was walking in the nest.

"Who are you?" the man asked.

"Dale Kowalski. Class II. Suspended."

"Class II," the man mocked.

He spat on the floor. "Useless. You're in the Deep Sector.

Nothing here is Class II.

Nothing here is human. You're trespassing."

"I'm not trespassing," Dale said. "I'm investigating."

The man laughed.

It was a wet, ugly sound. "Investigating. With what?

Your eyes?"

"With my protocol," Dale said.

He pointed at the ceiling. "There's a structural failure in the support beams. You're standing on a load-bearing slab that's compromised by acidic erosion. If you move your left foot three inches to the right, you'll punch through into the void below."

The man paused. He looked down.

He looked at his left foot.

He didn't move.

"Bullshit," he said. But his weight shifted. Just an inch.

The concrete beneath his boot groaned. A hairline fracture appeared. Dust rained down.

The man jumped back. He looked at the crack. Then he looked at Dale.

"How did you know?"

"Olfactory indicators," Dale said.

"The air is heavier on the left.

That's where the rot is worst. And the color of the slime is darker.

Higher concentration of uric acid. Means higher metabolic activity. The nest is directly above your left foot."

The man's eyes narrowed. "You're bluffing."

"I don't bluff. I measure." Dale pointed to the workbench. "Give me the clipboard."

"Why?"

"Because you're in violation of Code 44-B: Unsanctioned Nesting in a Public Utility Zone. And you're blocking the emergency egress. I need to document the hazard before I address it."

The man's face twisted. "You're insane."

"I'm licensed," Dale said. "Or I was.

Until that Auditor showed up.

Which means you're now working without a supervising technician. That's a Class A felony under the Authority Statutes."

The man hesitated. His wrench lowered an inch.

"I'm not an employee," the man said. "I'm a scavenger. I take what I find. I don't work for the Authority."

"Then you're an unlicensed contractor," Dale said.

"And you're standing in a Class IV infestation zone. Which means you're a vector.

You're spreading the contamination.

Every step you take moves the spores. Look at your boots."

The man looked down. The black slime was bubbling slightly.

"Spores?"

"Fungal hybrid. Dimensional origin.

High contagion rate.

If you walk out that corridor, you'll infect the entire basement level.

Maybe the whole district."

The man's hand went to his mask. He pulled it up over his nose and mouth. The fabric was thin. Useless.

"Stop lying," the man said. But his voice was higher. Tension tightened his shoulders.

"I'm not lying. I'm identifying the risk. Now, hand me the clipboard.

I need to log the entry point. If I document the breach, the Authority will send a cleanup crew.

Not a kill squad.

A cleanup crew. You can walk away."

The man stared at him. The wrench trembled in his hand.

"You think the Authority sends cleanup crews?" the man asked. "They send Auditors. And Auditors don't clean. They erase."

"Not if the paperwork is filed," Dale said. "That's the loophole. Section 9, Paragraph 3: *In the event of active contamination, the primary objective shifts from extermination to containment. The technician's role is to secure the perimeter, not to eliminate the source.*"

The man's eyes went wide.

"Paragraph 3," he whispered. "That's… that's real?"

"It's in the manual," Dale said.

"Page 412. I memorized it during the Level II exam.

It's the only reason I survived the first Rift event.

I didn't fight. I contained."

The man looked at the crack in the floor. He looked at the dark corridor. He looked at Dale.

"If I give you the clipboard," the man said, "what do you do?"

"I document the nest location," Dale said. "I mark the perimeter. I signal the Authority for containment. And you walk away."

"And if I don't?"

"Then I document you as a hostile vector. And I trigger the self-destruct protocol on the utility main."

The man went pale. "You can't trigger that. You're not on the grid."

"I don't need to be on the grid. I need to be in the zone.

The Authority tracks location, not intent.

If I'm in a Class IV zone and I declare an emergency, the local infrastructure responds.

It floods this sector with neutralizing agents."

Dale pointed to the pipes on the wall. "These aren't just water lines. They're coolant feeds for the dimensional stabilizers.

I can reverse the flow. It'll flood the basement with inert gas.

It won't kill the monster.

But it will seal the rift. And it will kill you if you're not wearing a respirator."

The man looked at his mask. Then at the pipes. Then at Dale.

"You're bluffing again," the man said. But he didn't move.

"Am I?" Dale asked.

"Check the pressure valve. The red one.

It's hissing.

That's not steam. That's the stabilizer failing. It's been failing for ten minutes.

You didn't notice because you were too busy looking at me."

The man turned slowly. He walked to the large valve on the wall. He put his hand near it.

A low, rhythmic hissing sound emanated from the metal.

The man pulled his hand back.

He looked at Dale.

Fear was in his eyes now. Real fear.

Not the anger of a thug, but the terror of a man who realized he was standing on a ticking bomb.

"What do you want?" the man asked.

"The clipboard," Dale said.

The man walked to the workbench. He picked it up. He held it out.

Dale didn't move.

"Put it on the floor," Dale said. "Between us. Five feet away."

The man frowned. But he did it. He set the clipboard down on the slime-covered concrete. He stepped back.

Dale kept his eyes on the man. He didn't look at the clipboard. Not yet.

"Name," Dale said.

"Vargus," the man said. "Scavenger. Sector 7."

"Vargus. You're in violation.

You're a vector.

And you're standing on a collapse zone. Move your right foot. Now."

Vargus shuffled his feet. He kept his weight off the cracked slab.

"Okay," Vargus said. "Okay. I'm moving. What next?"

Dale reached for the clipboard. He grabbed it. He flipped it open.

It was blank.

Of course it was. The Authority hadn't given him a fresh form. This was Vargus's personal notebook.

Dale pulled a pen from his pocket.

It was dry. He scribbled his name.

His certification number.

The date. The time.

Then he wrote: **Active Infestation. Vector: Vargus. Status: Contained.**

He looked up. "You're contained, Vargus. You're not leaving this room until the Authority arrives."

Vargus's face hardened. "You tricked me."

"No," Dale said. "I used the system. That's what I do."

From the corridor, a sound emerged. A clicking. A chittering.

Vargus went rigid. "What is that?"

"The source," Dale said. "It heard you."

The clicking grew louder. Closer.

Dale stood up. His bare feet slid on the slime. He planted them wide. He looked at the corridor.

Something was coming.

Vargus raised his wrench. "It's a Skitterer.

Class III.

Fast.

Armored."

"Class IV," Dale corrected. "Look at the frass. It's black.

Skitterers leave brown frass. This is something else.

Something bigger."

Vargus looked at the floor. He looked at Dale.

"It's coming for you," Vargus said.

"No," Dale said. "It's coming for the breach. It's trying to widen it."

Dale looked at the workbench. There was a roll of industrial tape. Silver. Strong.

He grabbed it.

"What are you doing?" Vargus shouted. The clicking was at the edge of the light now. A shape, dark and jagged, emerged from the shadows.

Dale tore a piece of tape. He slapped it onto the cracked concrete.

He tore another.

He slapped it on the crack.

He tore a third.

He slapped it on the crack.

"What are you doing?" Vargus yelled. "That won't stop it!"

"It's not about stopping it," Dale said. "It's about marking the boundary."

He slapped a fourth piece of tape.

Then a fifth.

A line of silver tape on the black slime.

The creature in the corridor stopped.

It was huge. The size of a horse. Its legs were multiple, jointed, clicking against the concrete.

Its head was a cluster of eyes, glowing red.

Its mouth was a vertical slit, dripping black fluid.

It looked at the tape.

It tilted its head.

Dale held his breath.

The creature took a step forward. It put a leg on the tape.

Nothing happened.

Dale tore another piece. He slapped it on the floor, extending the line.

The creature stopped again. It looked at Dale. It looked at the tape.

It didn't attack.

"Why is it stopping?" Vargus whispered.

"Because I'm defining the workspace," Dale said. "The Authority recognizes territorial markers. Even improvised ones. As long as they're consistent."

He tore another piece. Slap.

Another. Slap.

The creature hissed. A sound like tearing metal. It lunged.

Dale didn't move.

The creature's claws hit the tape.

The tape glowed blue.

The creature recoiled. It shrieked. It pulled back its leg.

Dale's heart hammered against his ribs. But his hands were steady.

"Keep going," Vargus said. His voice was shaky. "Keep going. Make it bigger."

Dale tore more tape. He slapped it on the floor.

He formed a square. A containment zone.

The creature circled the square. It couldn't cross the line.

Dale looked at the empty space in the center of the square. The epicenter.

He looked at Vargus.

"Hand me the wrench," Dale said.

"What?"

"The wrench. Give it to me."

Vargus hesitated. Then he tossed the heavy tool.

Dale caught it. He didn't aim at the monster.

He aimed at the ceiling.

Above the square, directly over the center, was a crack in the concrete. A small one. But it was weeping purple light.

The rift.

Dale raised the wrench.

He didn't swing at the monster.

He swung at the crack.

He hit it. Once. Twice. Three times.

The concrete shattered.

The purple light flared.

The creature screamed. It threw itself against the blue tape line. It clawed at the air.

Dale stepped back.

He pulled out his pen. He wrote on the clipboard: **Rift Stabilized. Perimeter Secured.**

He looked at the creature. It was trapped. But it wasn't dead.

And then, the blue light of the tape faded.

The tape dissolved.

The creature's claws ripped through the air where the line had been.

Dale dodged. A claw slashed his arm. Blood sprayed.

He didn't flinch. He didn't drop the clipboard.

He ran.

Not away from the creature.

Toward Vargus.

"Run," Dale said.

Vargus ran.

They sprinted for the blast door. The one Vargus had guarded.

The creature was behind them. Clicking. Fast.

Dale slammed the door.

He spun the wheel. Locked it.

The creature slammed into the metal. The door buckled.

Dale looked at Vargus.

"You're a liability," Dale said. "You're infected. You're a vector."

Vargus backed away. "What do you mean?"

Dale looked at the door. The metal was heating up.

"I mean," Dale said, "that the Authority doesn't just suspend licenses. They revoke access."

He pointed to the red emergency lever on the wall. The one for the floodgates.

"If I pull that, the basement floods with neutralizing gas. It kills the monster."

"Does it kill us?" Vargus asked.

Dale looked at him.

"The gas is heavier than air," Dale said.

"It sinks. It fills the floor. We climb.

We go up. Through the ventilation shaft."

Vargus looked up. There was a small hatch in the ceiling.

"That's a ten-foot drop," Vargus said.

"It's a four-foot climb if you use the pipes," Dale said. "And it's the only way to avoid the cross-contamination penalty."

The door groaned. The metal was turning white.

Vargus looked at the hatch. Then at Dale.

"You're serious?"

"I'm thorough," Dale said. "Climb."

Vargus grabbed the pipes. He started up.

Dale reached for the lever.

He didn't pull it.

Not yet.

He looked at the door. It was holding.

He looked at Vargus, who was halfway up the shaft.

Dale had one more move. A final inspection.

He reached into his pocket. He pulled out a small, crumpled receipt. It was from a hardware store. Dated three days ago.

He unfolded it.

He looked at the item listed: *Industrial Adhesive, High-Viscosity, Non-Newtonian.*

He hadn't bought tape.

He'd bought glue.

And he hadn't used it on the floor.

He'd used it on his boots. Which were gone.

He'd used it on his hands.

Which were now stuck to the wrench.

Which was now stuck to the door.

Which was now melting.

The creature broke through.

It was in the room.

It was five feet away.

Dale didn't run.

He looked at the ventilation shaft. Vargus was at the top. He was reaching for the hatch.

Dale looked at the creature.

He looked at his hands. Still glued.

He looked at the lever. Still untouched.

He had one choice.

To seal the room.

Or to seal himself inside it.

The creature lunged.

Dale closed his eyes.

He reached for the lever.

And then, a voice echoed in his head. Not an Auditor. Not the Authority.

A familiar voice. Dry. Bureaucratic.

*Technician Kowalski. You have three seconds to justify your presence in a Class IV zone. Failure to justify results in immediate termination of biological functions.*

Dale opened his eyes.

He looked at the creature.

He looked at the clipboard.

He wrote one word.

**Inspection.**

The world went black.
