Chapter 380: Licensure Suspension

# Chapter 380

The ink pooled on the floor, thick and viscous, spreading like a spilled bottle of India ink across the linoleum. It wasn't just ink. It was memory. It was the Auditor's own bureaucratic identity, liquefied and leaking out of the breach Dale had just punched into its reality.

Dale didn't look at the puddle. He looked at his hands.

They were trembling, but not from fear. The vibration was mechanical, a resonant frequency shuddering through his bones. The pen in his right hand was still warm, the plastic casing fused to his palm by the heat of the violation. On the left, the stack of invoices remained intact, though the edges were now singed black.

A notification pinged in the corner of his vision. It was a sound like a cash register, but wet and distorted.

**VIOLATION DETECTED: UNAUTHORIZED IDENTITY RECLASSIFICATION.** **PENALTY: IMMEDIATE LICENSURE SUSPENSION.** **CROSS-CONTAMINATION EVENT: LEVEL VI.**

The system didn't wait for him to process it. The air pressure in the room dropped.

The overhead fluorescent lights flickered, buzzing at a frequency that made Dale's teeth ache.

The Auditor was gone, dissolved into a slurry of administrative error, but the authority it represented remained. It hung in the room, a heavy, oppressive weight that pressed against Dale's eardrums.

He dropped the pen. It clattered against the floor, rolling into the black sludge.

"Protocol violation," Dale muttered. His voice was steady, despite the shaking of his hands. "Self-defense is not a recognized exception for Level I technicians."

He kicked the pen deeper into the sludge, and he needed to document this. He needed to file the incident report before the Authority's automated systems flagged him as a rogue element.

But the paperwork was in his arms.

The invoices for Job 378-C, the one he had failed to submit because the client had been eaten.

A low growl rumbled through the walls. Not from the floor, but from the ceiling.

Dale looked up, and the plaster was cracking. Not the dry, dusty cracks of an old house, but wet, pulsing fissures that wept a clear, amber fluid. Something was pushing through from the other side of the drywall. Something that wore the shape of the Auditor but was filled with the hunger of the Rift.

**INFESTATION DETECTED: BUREAUCRATIC PARASITE (SUB-CLASS: MIMICRY).** **THREAT LEVEL: UNCLASSIFIED.** **WARNING: DO NOT ENGAGE WITHOUT CORRECT FIELD GUIDE ENTRY.**

Dale stared at the text. He didn't have an entry for this.

He had never seen a monster that mimicked an auditor, and he had dealt with rats, cockroaches, centipedes, and the occasional dimensional spider.

He had not dealt with red tape.

The fissure widened. A limb emerged. It was made of paper.

Not flesh, not bone. Layers of compacted, yellowed paper, twisted into the shape of an arm.

The fingers were sharp, jagged edges of legal documents.

Dale backed away. He bumped into the filing cabinet. The metal groaned.

"Identification required," he whispered. He pulled out his Field Guide. The leather cover was slick with sweat.

He flipped through the pages. *Rattus profundus*. *Scarabaeus acidus*. *Araneus abyssalis*.

None of them fit.

The paper-arm lunged.

It moved with jerky, stop-motion speed, snapping forward like a broken hinge. Dale ducked. The limb slashed through the air where his head had been, slicing a hole in the ceiling tile.

Dust rained down, and he scrambled behind the cabinet. The paper-arm slammed into the metal, denting it. The sound was like a car crash.

Dale's mind raced.

He couldn't kill it.

He didn't have a weapon that could cut paper without getting contaminated. And if he used a protocol for a different creature, the cross-contamination penalty would drop his effectiveness by forty percent.

Against a mimic, that could be fatal.

But there was a loophole.

The System rewarded thoroughness. It punished sloppy work.

The Auditor had been an infestation of authority itself.

Dale had identified it as an infestation. He had applied the protocol. The result was a cross-contamination event.

The paper-arm punched through the cabinet door. The metal buckled inward.

Dale slid across the floor, grabbing a roll of industrial-grade packing tape from his belt. It was meant for sealing breaches in containment zones. It was rated for Class II pests. This thing was at least Class IV, possibly higher.

He slapped the tape over the hole in the cabinet where the arm had punched through. Then he slapped another strip over the crack in the ceiling. Then another over the window.

Seal the entry points. Isolate the threat.

The paper-arm shrieked.

It wasn't a vocal sound. It was the sound of pages tearing.

It thrashed against the tape, trying to rip through the seal.

The adhesive held, but barely. The paper limb unravelled, threads of text flying off into the air.

**ISOLATION IN PROGRESS.** **NOTE: CONTAINMENT IS INCOMPLETE. SOURCE REMAINS ACTIVE.**

The source. The Auditor had been a conduit.

If Dale sealed the room, he was trapping the connection.

But he wasn't killing the parasite, and he was just containing it.

And the Authority hated containment that wasn't followed by elimination.

The ceiling exploded.

Not with fire. With ink. A torrent of black, viscous fluid poured down from the vent, flooding the room. It mixed with the paper-arm, creating a swirling vortex of bureaucratic horror.

Dale was engulfed. The ink coated his face, his clothes, his Field Guide. It seeped into his pores. He gagged, trying to breathe, but the air was thick with the taste of old libraries and stale coffee.

He was blind. He was suffocating. He was contaminated.

And then, a new notification appeared. It was bright red, flashing against the darkness of the ink.

**ALERT: IDENTITY CORRUPTION IMMINENT.** **YOUR CERTIFICATION LEVEL IS UNDER REVIEW.** **IF YOU DO NOT RESOLVE THE INFESTATION WITHIN 60 SECONDS, YOU WILL BE RECLASSIFIED AS A PEST.**

Dale stopped struggling. He stood still in the center of the ink pool. The paper-arm was still thrashing, but it was slowing down. The ink was clogging the joints.

He reached into his pocket. He felt for the only thing he had left that wasn't paper or ink.

His phone, and the screen was cracked, but it was alive. He opened the Authority's app. The interface was glitching, symbols scrambling.

He couldn't access his Field Guide. He couldn't call for backup.

But he could file a complaint.

He opened the "Report Malfunction" tab.

The form required details. *Nature of Error. Location. Suspected Cause.*

Dale typed with his ink-slicked fingers. The keys were sticky. The words smeared.

*Nature of Error: Auditor malfunction. Cause: Unauthorized biological interaction.*

He hit submit.

The app froze. Then, a single line of text appeared.

**COMPLAINT RECEIVED. AWAITING AUDIT.**

The ink stopped moving. The paper-arm went limp. The room was silent.

Dale waited. One second. Two seconds. Ten.

The ink receded. It pulled back into the floor, the walls, the ceiling. The paper-arm dissolved, leaving behind a single, crumpled receipt.

Dale picked it up. His hands were clean. The ink was gone. But his Field Guide was blank.

Every entry was wiped.

*Rattus profundus*. Gone. *Scarabaeus acidus*. Gone.

All the knowledge he had gathered over three hundred chapters. All the weaknesses he had documented. All the targeted damage bonuses. Erased.

A new notification pulsed in his vision.

**STATUS: CLEAN SLATE.** **CERTIFICATION LEVEL: I.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 0.0.**

Dale stared at the rating.

Zero. He had started at zero. He had climbed to nine.

Now he was back to zero.

But he was alive. And he had a receipt.

He unfolded it. It was from the Authority's cafeteria. A charge for one cup of black coffee. Timestamped five minutes ago.

He hadn't been in the cafeteria. He had been in a bedroom in suburban Ohio.

Someone had filed a ticket for him. Someone had authorized his existence.

The door to the bedroom opened.

Dale spun around.

Standing in the doorway was not the Auditor. It was a woman.

She wore a gray suit that fit perfectly, tailored to her sharp, angular frame.

Her hair was pulled back in a severe bun. Her eyes were human, brown and tired, with dark circles under them.

She held a clipboard. The same clipboard the Auditor had held.

"Mr. Kowalski," she said. Her voice was calm, professional. "You have been flagged for Level VI redaction."

Dale lowered the receipt. He didn't speak. He just watched her.

"I am Senior Compliance Officer Halloway," she said. She stepped into the room.

The air didn't distort around her.

It felt normal.

Too normal. "Your actions constituted a direct attack on a licensed entity.

By rights, I should have erased you from the timeline.

"

She paused. She looked at the blank Field Guide in his hands.

"But your Thoroughness Rating triggered a glitch. You documented the Auditor as an infestation. You sealed the breach. You filed a complaint."

Halloway tapped her clipboard. A line of gold light traced her finger.

"The Authority does not punish thoroughness, Mr. Kowalski. Even when it is applied to us."

She held out the clipboard.

"Sign."

Dale looked at the form. It was an employment contract. Not for a technician. For an Auditor.

**POSITION: ASSOCIATE AUDITOR.** **DUTIES: INTERNAL COMPLIANCE. PEST REMOVAL (AUTHORITY PERSONNEL).** **TERMS: INDEFINITE. NO TERMINATION WITHOUT WRITTEN CONSENT.**

"You want me to work for you," Dale said. It wasn't a question.

"You have a gift," Halloway said. "You see monsters where others see people. And you see people where others see monsters. That is useful."

"I am a pest control technician," Dale said. "I have a license."

"Your license is invalid," Halloway said. She pointed to the blank pages of his Field Guide.

"You have no records.

No history. To the System, you are a blank slate. You are no one.

"

She stepped closer. The scent of old paper and ozone was overwhelming.

"You can walk out of here," she said.

"You can go back to your apartment. You will be unemployed.

You will have no money.

And the next time a Rift opens, you will have no tools. You will have no knowledge. You will be a civilian.

"

Dale looked at the pen on the floor. The one he had used to write the violation. It was gone.

He looked at Halloway. Her eyes were hard, but there was a flicker of something else.

Fear?

No.

Interest.

"What happens if I sign?" Dale asked.

"You get your Field Guide back," Halloway said. "All of it. Every entry.

Every weakness. Every protocol.

And you get access to the Authority's database.

You can identify anything.

"

"And if I don't?"

"Then you are a civilian. And civilians get eaten."

Dale took the clipboard. The pen was real. It worked. He clicked it.

He looked at the contract. There was no expiration date. No exit clause.

He thought about the Auditor. The way it had bled ink. The way it had looked confused when Dale had identified it.

He thought about the blank pages. The silence in his head where the Field Guide used to be.

He thought about the next Rift. The next broodmother. The next thing crawling out of the ground.

Without the Field Guide, he was blind. Without the knowledge, he was vulnerable.

But with it?

He would be hunting his own kind. The people who ran the system. The people who had put him in this position.

Halloway watched him. Her face was impassive.

"The clock is running, Mr. Kowalski," she said. "The Authority is waiting."

Dale uncapped the pen. The tip was dry.

He wrote his name at the bottom of the page.

**DALE KOWALSKI.**

The ink flowed smoothly, which was black. Not gold.

Not red.

Just black.

Halloway smiled. It was a small, tight smile.

"Welcome to the Authority," she said.

The room changed. The walls were no longer drywall.

They were whiteboards.

Covered in equations, diagrams, and maps of the city, and the floor was polished concrete. The ceiling was a grid of lights.

Dale was standing in an office. A cubicle.

He was wearing a gray suit.

He looked down at his hands. They were clean.

He looked at the clipboard. The contract was gone. Instead, there was a new file.

**JOB ID: 381-A.** **TARGET: PREVIOUS TECHNICIAN (CLASS II).** **STATUS: NON-COMPLIANT.** **LOCATION: SUBURBAN OHIO, SECTOR 4.** **OBJECTIVE: REDACTION.**

Dale stared at the file, and the target was him.

He looked up. Halloway was gone. The office was empty.

On the desk, his old Field Guide lay open. The pages were full. Every entry was there. *Rattus profundus*. *Scarabaeus acidus*. *Araneus abyssalis*.

But at the bottom of the last page, a new entry had appeared.

**SUBJECT: DALE KOWALSKI (FORMER).** **WEAKNESS: CONTAMINATION ANXIETY.** **PROTOCOL: ISOLATION AND ERADICATION.**

The door to the cubicle opened.

A man walked in. He was wearing a blue jumpsuit. He had a clipboard. He had a flashlight.

He looked exactly like Dale had looked three hundred chapters ago.

"Excuse me," the man said. "I'm here to inspect your property. Do you have a pest problem?"

Dale looked at the man. He looked at the file on his desk.

He picked up his pen.

"Yes," Dale said. "I do."
