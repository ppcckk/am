Chapter 122: Twelve Smiling Reflections

# Chapter 122

The pen hovered over the clipboard.

Not because Dale was afraid. He wasn't. Fear was a variable he hadn't accounted for, and therefore irrelevant.

He was recalculating.

The voice on the sphere was Gerald. Gerald Kowalski, his former supervisor at Summit Pest Solutions. Gerald, who had fired him for "excessive adherence to protocol" during the Akron ant infestation of 2019. Gerald, who had died in a car accident three months before the Rift opened.

Gerald, who was currently dead.

Unless the Authority was running a simulation.

Dale clicked the pen. *Click.*

The sound echoed in the concrete chamber. The sphere pulsed, a low thrumming vibration that rattled Dale's teeth. The crack in the floor widened another inch. Dust fell from the ceiling, coating the shoulders of his hazmat suit in a fine gray powder.

"Gerald," Dale said. His voice was flat. "You are not here."

"I am in the break room," the voice said. It sounded exactly like him.

The slight wheeze.

The impatience. "And you're missing the deadline, Dale. The client is unhappy.

They want the rats gone.

Now. Not 'documented.' Gone."

Dale looked at the duct tape. It was peeling at the corner. The membrane beneath it rippled, a dark, viscous mass pressing against the adhesive.

He looked at the sphere.

"You're not Gerald," Dale said. "Gerald never cited OSHA 1910.132 regarding proper PPE compliance. He preferred loose ties and open-toed shoes."

The sphere flickered. The light shifted from a soft blue to a harsh white.

"Stop arguing with me," the voice snapped. "Fix the containment. Or I'll write you up."

Dale didn't answer. He turned away from the sphere and walked back to the grate.

The crack was now six inches wide. A claw, black and chitinous, pried itself through the fissure. It scraped against the concrete, sending up sparks.

**WARNING: STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY AT 40%.** **CONTAINMENT FAILURE IMMINENT.** **AVAILABLE PROTOCOLS: NONE (LICENSE SUSPENDED).**

The red text burned in his peripheral vision.

The Authority had stripped his license. He was operating as an unlicensed contractor in a high-risk zone. If he failed, he didn't just lose points.

He died.

Dale knelt.

He ignored the claw snapping at his face.

He reached into his tool belt and pulled out a can of expanding polyurethane foam. Standard issue.

Useless against Class IV threats, but effective on structural gaps.

He sprayed it into the crack.

The foam hissed, expanding rapidly. It hardened into a white, lumpy barrier. The claw pushed against it. The foam cracked.

Dale shook his head.

"Insufficient adhesion," he muttered. "Poor surface preparation."

He stood up and walked back to the sphere. The voice was silent now. Watching. Waiting.

Dale raised his clipboard.

"Violation K-7," he said. "Improper storage of hazardous materials. This sphere is not secured. It is a hazard to personnel."

He wrote it down. He signed it. He stamped it with the Authority's digital seal, even though his license was suspended. The seal glowed faintly, then dimmed.

**VIOLATION LOGGED.** **CREDIBILITY SCORE: 88%.**

The sphere hummed louder.

"Stop writing," the voice said. "It's not a real job, Dale. It's a trap."

"Everything is a job," Dale said. "If you don't document it, it didn't happen."

He walked over to the ceiling cables. They hung loosely, swaying in the air currents generated by the sphere's energy field. One cable, thick and insulated, dangled near the edge of the grate.

Dale looked at the cable. He looked at the sphere. He looked at the crack in the floor.

A new thought formed. Not a weapon. A conduit.

The Authority's system was bureaucratic. It rewarded adherence to protocol, not raw power. If he couldn't fight the thing, he could file a complaint about its presence. But the complaint needed to be actionable.

He needed to make the breach *legal*.

No, that was stupid.

He needed to make the breach *temporary*.

If he could isolate the energy signature, he could treat it like a localized leak. Not a monster. A plumbing issue.

Dale grabbed the cable. The insulation was intact. He pulled it free from its mounting bracket. Sparks showered down as it ripped away from the power grid.

He dragged the live wire to the edge of the grate.

"What are you doing?" Halloway's voice cracked over the comms in his earpiece. She was outside, safe. "Dale, don't touch that."

"Grounding," Dale said. "The sphere is emitting unshielded radiation.

The breach is amplifying it.

If I ground the sphere, the energy dissipates. The thing on the other side loses its signal."

"You'll electrocute yourself."

"I'm wearing rubber-soled boots. And I'm not holding it with my bare hands."

He used a pair of insulated pliers to strip the end of the cable. He jammed the exposed copper into the crack in the floor, right next to the claw.

The claw shrieked. A high-pitched sound that made Dale's vision blur.

The cable sparked violently. Blue arcs jumped from the wire to the dark mass below. The sphere flickered wildly.

"NO!" the voice screamed. It wasn't Gerald anymore. It was something guttural. Something that used Gerald's voice like a stolen suit.

Dale held his ground. He kept the cable pressed against the breach. The heat was intense, searing through his gloves, but he didn't let go.

**ACTION LOGGED: IMPROVISATION.** **TYPE: NON-LETHAL SUPPRESSION.** **EFFICIENCY: LOW.** **POINTS AWARDED: 5.**

Five points. For trying to electrocute a dimensional rift.

The sphere went dark. The humming stopped.

The crack in the floor sealed itself with a wet, sucking sound. The claw retracted. The foam barrier held.

Dale let go of the pliers. His hands were shaking. Not from fear. From the aftershock.

He checked his Field Guide.

**ENTRY UPDATED: Dimensional Parasite (Auditory Mimicry).** **WEAKNESS: Grounding of Energy Signature.** **NOTE: Do not engage directly. Isolate signal source.**

"Nice work," Halloway said. She was standing in the doorway now, holding a fire extinguisher. "I thought you were trying to commit suicide."

Dale rubbed his hands on his suit. "I was troubleshooting."

He walked to the sphere. It was cold now. Dead metal.

He picked up his clipboard. The paper was singed at the edges.

"Inspection concluded," he said. "No further hazards observed."

He turned to leave.

The sphere lit up again.

Not blue. Not white.

Black.

A single line of text scrolled across its surface.

**CLIENT COMPLAINT RESOLVED.** **BUT THE CONTRACT REMAINS.**

Dale froze.

"What contract?" he asked.

The sphere didn't answer. Instead, the air in the room changed. The pressure dropped. His ears popped.

A new notification appeared. Not in red. Not in blue.

In gold.

**CERTIFICATION LEVEL III UNLOCKED.** **CONDITION: SURVIVED UNLICENSED INTERVENTION.** **PENALTY: 50% REDUCTION IN ALL FUTURE EARNINGS.** **NEW MECHANIC: LIABILITY SHIELD.**

Dale stared at the text.

A Liability Shield. He had no idea what it did, but the name sounded like something an insurance adjuster would appreciate.

He looked at Halloway.

"I'm getting paid less?"

"Apparently," she said. "But you're still alive. And you just figured out how to shut it up."

Dale nodded. He walked toward the exit. His steps were measured. Each one deliberate.

He wasn't proud.

He was calculating the next move. The Authority had punished him for improvising.

But it had also rewarded him for surviving.

The system was flawed. It valued procedure, but it didn't punish deviation if the outcome was positive.

Loophole.

He could work with that.

As he reached the door, Halloway grabbed his arm.

"Dale. Look at your Field Guide."

He frowned. He pulled up the interface.

The entry for *Dimensional Parasite* was greyed out. Contaminated.

"Why?"

"Because you used a method that wasn't in your approved protocol list," Halloway said. "The system doesn't know how to handle it. So it's locked you out of using that knowledge for twenty-four hours."

Dale looked at the timer counting down. 23:59:58.

He looked back at the dark sphere.

Something inside it was moving. Not a claw. Not a voice.

A face.

Gerald's face. Pressed against the inside of the metal, screaming silently.

Dale's hand tightened on the clipboard.

He didn't speak. He didn't write.

He walked out into the hallway.

The corridor was long and dark. The lights flickered, buzzing with the same frequency as the sphere.

Halloway followed him.

"Dale, we need to go. The Authority is scanning us. They're going to see the unauthorized use of—"

"I know," Dale said.

He stopped walking.

Ahead of them, the hallway ended. But it wasn't a wall.

It was a mirror.

A perfect, floor-to-ceiling mirror.

And in the mirror, Dale wasn't alone.

Behind his reflection stood a dozen figures. They wore hazmat suits. They held clipboards. They had his face.

But their eyes were black.

**NEW OBJECTIVE:** **IDENTIFY THE ORIGINAL.** **REWARD: FULL LICENSE RESTORATION.** **PENALTY FOR FAILURE: TOTAL WIPE.**

Dale raised his pen.

He didn't look at the reflections. He looked at the pen tip.

There was ink on it.

Not black ink.

Red.

He hadn't written anything in red.

He looked at his clipboard. The page was blank.

But his fingers were stained red.

The mirror rippled.

The reflections turned to look at him.

All twelve of them.

Smiling.

Dale stepped back.

One of the reflections stepped forward.

It stepped *out* of the mirror.
