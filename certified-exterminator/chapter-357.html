Chapter 357: No Protocol for This

# Chapter 357

The first thing to go was his left eye.

Not the vision. The socket.

A wet, sickening pop echoed inside Dale's skull, followed by the cold rush of fluid draining down his cheek.

He didn't blink. He couldn't. The nerve endings had been rerouted, hijacked by the Symbiotic Integration, feeding raw sensory data directly into his motor cortex.

He saw the corridor through thermal, through vibration, through the chitinous resonance of the tunnel walls. It was ugly. It was bright. It was too much.

Miller's voice was gone, swallowed by the static that now thrummed in his ears like a high-voltage wire. Dale stepped forward. The black veins on his forearm pulsed in time with his heartbeat, a synchronized rhythm that felt less like biology and more like machinery.

The air tasted of copper and ozone.

Ahead, the infestation massed, and they were no longer rats. They were no longer beetles.

They were a shifting tide of Class VI entities, a hybrid swarm formed from the dimensional bleed.

Dale's Field Guide flashed in his peripheral vision, the text scrolling so fast it blurred.

**UNIDENTIFIED HYBRID SWARM.** **SUBSPECIES: UNKNOWN.** **IDENTIFICATION FAILED.**

Identification failed, which was the problem. Without identification, there was no bonus.

Without the bonus, the Symbiotic Integration's +50% damage modifier applied to the base damage of his tools, not his targeted weaknesses.

It was inefficient.

It was sloppy. It was a 6.5 on the Thoroughness Scale, minimum.

Dale raised his right hand. The spray nozzle, modified by the Authority's last update, hummed with a low, menacing pitch. He didn't aim at the swarm. He aimed at the wall ten feet to his left.

A blast of pressurized gel hit the stone. It wasn't poison. It was a tracer dye, a neon blue pigment that bonded with pheromone trails.

The swarm reacted instantly. Not to the damage, but to the chemical signal. They turned, a hundred eyes fixing on the blue stain.

"Come on," Dale whispered. His voice sounded distorted, layered with a harmonic undertone that wasn't his own.

He walked toward them. The monsters parted, not out of fear, but out of confusion. They sensed his signature.

They sensed the Apex Mark. They thought he was one of them, a rogue node in their hive mind.

Dale used that, and he walked into the center of the swarm. The pressure against his suit was immense, a physical weight of bodies pressing against his armor. His ribs groaned. The black veins on his arm darkened, turning from obsidian to a bruised purple.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** **INTEGRATION STABILITY: 88%.** **SANITY STABILITY: 74%.** **WARNING: NEURAL OVERLOAD IMMINENT.**

He ignored the warnings. He needed to isolate the core.

The swarm wasn't attacking, which was observing. That was the new violation type. Passive aggression.

They were letting him walk through their territory, letting him think he was safe, until they decided to consume him all at once.

It was a containment protocol of their own.

Dale stopped. He closed his good eye. He focused on the vibration in his feet.

There.

A rhythmic thumping, deep in the earth. A heartbeat. Not the swarm. The source.

He opened his eye.

The world tilted. A migraine spike drove a lance of pain through his temple. He stumbled, catching himself on a rock protrusion.

The swarm surged forward, sensing the stumble, but he raised his left hand.

Symbiotic Integration, and he didn't spray. He *projected*.

A wave of distorted sound, a frequency tuned to the resonance of the Apex Mark, blasted outward. The front line of the swarm exploded. Chitin shattered. Fluid sprayed across Dale's visor.

He wiped it away with a gloved hand, checking his Field Guide. The entry for the swarm updated instantly.

**SUBSPECIES IDENTIFIED: PHANTOM COLLECTOR.** **WEAKNESS: SONIC RESONANCE DISRUPTION.** **TARGETED DAMAGE BONUS: +15%.**

Good. But not enough.

He needed the core. He needed to eliminate the queen. And he needed to do it before his sanity stability hit 50%, at which point the Authority would revoke his license for

"unstable operator status" and deploy a cleanup crew that would incinerate the entire sector, including him.

He moved deeper. The tunnel narrowed. The walls were slick with slime that smelled of rotting fruit and old blood. Dale stepped over a pile of discarded exoskeletons, his boots squelching.

A voice in his head. Not Miller. Not the Authority.

*Join,* it whispered. *The hive is complete. The certification is eternal.*

Dale gritted his teeth.

"I don't want your certification," he muttered. "I want my deposit back."

He pulled a secondary tool from his belt. The Authority had issued it during the Level VI exam, but he'd never used it. It was a Containment Bubble Generator, designed for Class IV pests. Using it on a Class VI entity was a cross-contamination risk.

If it failed, his Field Guide entry for the Phantom Collector would be corrupted for twenty-four hours. He would lose his bonus. He would be fighting blind.

But he had the Symbiotic Integration. He had the Apex Mark. He could override the safety limits.

He activated the generator. A sphere of translucent energy expanded around him. The swarm pressed against it, their claws clicking against the barrier. The energy field flared red.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** **CROSS-CONTAMINATION RISK DETECTED.** **APPLYING OVERRIDE VIA APEX MARK.** **PENALTY: -5% MAX HEALTH PER SECOND.**

His suit's integrity alarms screamed. Dale kept walking. The bubble pushed the swarm back, creating a pocket of clear space. He was moving through the infestation like a bubble wand through soap film.

The tunnel opened up into a cavern, and the Queen sat on a throne of fused bone and rusted metal. She was massive, a grotesque amalgamation of insect and human features. Her upper body was a woman's, pale and translucent, with eyes like black glass. Her lower half was a spider's, legs thick as tree trunks, rooted into the floor.

She looked at him. She didn't roar. She spoke.

"Technician," she said. Her voice was the sound of grinding stones. "You are licensed to exterminate. You are not licensed to ascend."

Dale stopped. The bubble around him flickered. His health bar was dropping. 95%. 94%. 93%.

"I am licensed to identify, document, isolate, eliminate, and verify," Dale said. His voice was flat, procedural. "You are an unregistered structural hazard."

The Queen tilted her head. A smile, too wide for her face, stretched across her skin.

"Your certification is based on human rules," she said.

"I have evolved beyond them.

I have integrated with the Rift. I am no longer a pest.

I am a tenant."

Dale checked his clipboard. The page was missing.

He knew this, and he had accepted it. But he still reached for the phantom weight of it in his hand.

"Tenancy requires a lease," Dale said. "You do not have a lease. You have a violation. Code 404: Unauthorized Dimensional Encroachment."

He raised his spray nozzle.

The Queen laughed. It was a horrible, wet sound.

"Shoot me, little man. My carapace is hardened by the Rift itself. Your chemicals are water to me."

"Chemicals are not the protocol," Dale said. "The protocol is isolation."

He deactivated the bubble.

The swarm rushed in.

Dale didn't fight them. He ran. He sprinted toward the Queen, dodging lashing legs and snapping jaws. The Symbiotic Integration allowed him to move faster than humanly possible, his muscles burning with unnatural strength.

He reached the base of the throne. The black veins on his arm were now entirely purple, pulsing violently. His vision fractured, splitting into multiple angles.

He slammed the Containment Bubble Generator into the floor.

Then he kicked the Queen's leg.

Not a strike. A trip.

The Queen stumbled, her massive form toppling sideways. She crashed into the wall, cracking the stone.

Dale was already moving, and he pulled a pin from a grenade on his belt. Not a bomb.

A Beacon.

A Class III Signal Flare, designed to attract Authority enforcement teams.

He threw it into the Queen's mouth.

She choked, the flare igniting inside her throat. Blue light spilled from her eyes, her nose, her ears.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** **TARGET IDENTIFIED: CLASS VI QUEEN.** **STATUS: CRITICAL DAMAGE.** **VERIFY ELIMINATION?**

Dale looked at his health bar. 62%, and he looked at his Sanity Stability. 49%.

The whisper in his head was louder now. *Join. Join. Join.*

He could feel the hive mind brushing against his own, a seductive, overwhelming unity. He could feel the Queen's pain.

He could feel the swarm's hunger, which was easy.

It was warm. It was the end of the anxiety.

The end of the fear. The end of the need for paperwork.

Dale raised his clipboard, and he wrote a single line in the air, his pen scratching against the nothingness.

**VIOLATION CONFIRMED. TERMINATION REQUIRED.**

He didn't write it for the Queen. He wrote it for himself. To anchor his humanity. To prove he was still a technician, not a monster.

The flare detonated.

Not with fire. With light. A pure, blinding white explosion that vaporized the Queen's upper torso. Her legs spasmed, kicking out, shattering the floor.

The swarm shrieked, a unified wail of agony and confusion. Without the Queen's command, their coordination collapsed. They teared at each other, driven mad by the loss of the hive signal.

Dale stood in the center of the chaos, his suit smoking, his arm black and twisted.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** **ELIMINATION VERIFIED.** **TREATMENT POINTS AWARDED: 500.** **CERTIFICATION LEVEL VI CONFIRMED.** **NEXT LEVEL: VII.**

He took a step back. His legs gave out. He fell to his knees.

The static in his ears cleared. The black veins on his arm receded, turning back to a dull gray.

He gasped for air. The taste of copper was gone, replaced by the smell of burnt ozone and dead things.

A sound approached. Heavy footsteps. Mechanical.

Dale looked up.

Miller stood at the entrance of the cavern, flanked by two containment officers in heavy exo-suits. They were carrying weapons that glowed with Authority insignia.

Miller's face was pale. He stared at Dale, then at the corpse of the Queen, then at the swarm tearing itself apart.

"Dale?" Miller called out.

Dale tried to stand. His legs refused.

"Job complete," Dale said. His voice was raspy.

The containment officers raised their weapons. Not at the swarm. At Dale.

Miller stepped forward, waving his hands.

"Stand down! He's one of us! He's Level VI!"

"Level VI operators require a debrief before reintegration," the lead officer said. His voice was amplified, cold.

"And a containment check.

You're radiating Apex Mark signature, Technician Kowalski. You're contaminated.

"

"I cleaned up the mess," Dale said.

"You became part of it," the officer corrected.

He tapped his tablet.

"The Authority has flagged your biological signature.

You are no longer Class I through VI personnel.

You are flagged as a Potential Apex Host.

"

Dale felt a chill that had nothing to do with the cold cavern.

"Flagged?" Dale asked.

"Pending examination," the officer said. "If you fail the psychological evaluation, you will be terminated. As an infestation."

Miller stepped between the officer and Dale.

"He just killed a Class VI Queen. He has a 9.8 Thoroughness Rating. You can't terminate him."

"Read the rules, Officer Miller," the lead officer said. "Cross-contamination penalty.

He used a Class IV tool on a Class VI entity.

He accepted the Symbiotic Integration, and he is compromised. The Authority does not leave liability untreated.

"

Dale looked at his hands. The veins were gone, but the skin beneath was scarred, etched with faint, glowing lines.

He looked at Miller.

Miller looked back, fear in his eyes. Not for Dale. For what Dale had become.

"What happens now?" Dale asked.

The officer raised his weapon.

"Now? You're in custody. And we have to find out if you're a technician... or a monster."

Dale stood up. His legs held this time.

He didn't run. He didn't fight. He picked up his clipboard from the ground. It was scorched, but the page was intact.

He opened it.

**CURRENT STATUS:** **CERTIFICATION: LEVEL VI.** **THOROUGHNESS: 9.8.** **STATUS: UNDER REVIEW.**

He looked at the officer.

"I have a question," Dale said.

The officer hesitated. The weapon wavered.

"Who authorized the Symbiotic Integration protocol?" Dale asked.

The officer lowered his tablet slightly.

"The Authority. It was a reward for the previous chapter's completion."

"Wrong," Dale said. "The Authority doesn't reward integration. They punish it. The only entity that rewards integration is the Rift."

Silence.

The static returned to Dale's ears. But it wasn't random. It was a voice.

*They don't know,* the voice whispered. *They think they control us. But we control them.*

Dale looked at Miller.

Miller's eyes widened. He stepped back.

"Dale?"

Dale looked at the containment officers. He looked at the dead Queen. He looked at the glowing lines on his arm.

He realized why the monsters had parted for him. He realized why the Queen had called him a tenant.

He hadn't just accepted the mark. He had been invited.

And the Authority didn't know who had sent the invitation.

Dale tapped his clipboard.

"I'm not under review," Dale said. "I'm under audit."

He smiled. It was the same dry, procedural smile.

"And I have found a violation."

The officer frowned.

"What violation?"

Dale pointed to the officer's chest. To the Authority insignia glowing there.

"The Authority is leaking," Dale said.

The insignia on the officer's chest pulsed. Not with light. With movement. Something inside the metal was stirring.

The officer looked down. The metal melted, sliding off his chest like wax. Beneath it, black veins spread across his skin.

The officer screamed.

Miller drew his weapon.

Dale didn't move. He watched.

The other officer raised his gun. But his hand shook. The weapon clattered to the ground. Black veins crawled up his neck.

Dale looked at Miller.

Miller was safe. For now.

But the lead officer, the one who had come to terminate Dale, was now twitching, his eyes rolling back.

"Miller," Dale said. "Don't shoot them."

"Why? They're infected!"

"They're not infected," Dale said. "They're activated."

The lead officer looked at Dale. His voice was no longer his own. It was the static. It was the whisper.

*Kowalski,* the officer said. *The audit begins.*

Dale felt a pull in his chest. A connection. The Symbiotic Integration hadn't just bonded with him.

It had bonded with the network. And the network was waking up.

Every Authority officer in the sector. Every Level I through IX technician.

They were all connected.

And they were all being called.

Dale looked at his clipboard. The page was blank.

He needed to write. But he didn't know what to write.

Because for the first time in his life, Dale Kowalski had no protocol for this.

The officer lunged. Not at Dale. At Miller.

Dale moved.
