Chapter 362: Certified for Extermination

# Chapter 362

The pen snapped.

Not the plastic casing. The nib. It crunched between Dale's teeth like a stale cracker, spraying ink into the back of his throat. He spat the black sludge onto the concrete, gagging, his tongue burning with chemical bitterness.

He wasn't in the alley anymore. Or he was, but the alley had folded in on itself, the geometry of the sewer grate twisting into a loop of wet stone and buzzing heat.

The air tasted of copper and rot.

Around him, the swarm wasn't just moving. It was vibrating, a low-frequency hum that rattled his molars.

Dale wiped his mouth with the back of a glove, and the glove was shredded. His left hand hung uselessly at his side, fingers curled into a claw.

He didn't look at it.

Looking at it meant acknowledging the injury, and acknowledging the injury meant accepting the pain. Pain was inefficient. Pain caused tremors.

Tremors made writing illegible, and he had no pen.

The clipboard remained in his right hand, the paper blank save for the header he had started writing: *Incident 362.*

A beetle the size of a house cat skittered past his boot.

It had too many legs, and they moved in a rhythm that made Dale's eyes water.

It didn't attack. It ignored him.

It was heading for the crack in the sky, the tear in the fabric of reality that hung above the street like a jagged scar.

Dale stepped forward. The ground was slick with slime. He slid, catching himself on the clipboard. The impact sent a shockwave up his arm, but his grip held.

He needed a writing instrument, and he needed to document the infestation.

If he didn't document it, it didn't count. If it didn't count, he didn't get the points. If he didn't get the points, he didn't get the certification. And if he didn't get the certification, he couldn't close the breach.

The logic was circular, but it was the only logic that mattered.

He scanned the ground. Glass shards.

A rusted hubcap.

A puddle of motor oil. Nothing sharp enough to etch, nothing fluid enough to write.

The older Dale's voice echoed in his memory. *Save your license.*

It wasn't about heroism. It was about compliance.

Dale looked at his bleeding hand. The blood was dark, arterial. It pulsed with a rhythm that matched the swarm's hum.

An idea, cold and clinical, formed in his mind, and he raised his left hand. He bit the end of the clipboard's metal clip, tearing the plastic away to expose the steel. He jammed the raw edge into the wound on his palm.

The pain was white-hot, blinding, but Dale didn't scream. He exhaled sharply, a short, controlled burst of air. Blood welled up, thick and dark. He dipped the metal clip into the pool of his own blood.

It worked, and he touched the paper. The red ink spread, blotchy and uneven, but legible.

*Incident 362: Biological Vector Detected.* *Species: Unidentified Parasitic Insecta.* *Status: Active Migration.*

The System notification pinged in his peripheral vision, bright and intrusive.

**FIELD GUIDE ENTRY CREATED: SELF-HEMORRHAGE AS INK SOURCE.** **WARNING: CONTAMINATION RISK. BIOLOGICAL SAMPLES MUST BE VERIFIED.**

Dale ignored the warning. He kept writing.

*Action: Eliminate Vector.* *Method: Direct Contact.*

He couldn't kill them all.

There were thousands. But he didn't need to kill them all.

He needed to stop the ones going through the crack.

The ones acting as the spearhead, and the swarm was thickening near the grate. The larger insects, the Lithovore remnants, were pushing through the smaller ones, creating a tunnel of chitin and leg. They were trying to widen the passage.

Dale moved toward them.

His legs felt heavy. The sonic cooldown from the previous fight was still active, a dull ache in his temples.

He couldn't use the sonic protocol.

It would disrupt the documentation process, scrambling the Field Guide entries.

He had to do this manually, and he reached the edge of the grate. The smell was overpowering now, a mixture of sulfur and stale sweat. He could see the backs of the insects, thousands of them, pouring into the darkness.

He grabbed the first one he could reach. It was a beetle, its shell iridescent and oily. He crushed it in his right hand. The shell cracked, releasing a spray of acidic fluid that hissed against his glove.

He didn't drop it. He held the carcass over the paper on the clipboard, letting the acid etch the fibers.

*Specimen 1: Acidic Carapace. Vulnerability: Thermal Shock.*

The System flashed again.

**IDENTIFICATION CONFIRMED.** **FIELD GUIDE UPDATED: LITHOVORE PARASITE (PHASE 2).** **WEAKNESS DISCOVERED: HEAT.**

Dale smiled. A grim, tight expression that didn't reach his eyes.

He had a weakness. Now he needed a source.

The alley had a dumpster. Flammable contents. But he didn't have a lighter.

He had a clipboard. He had a metal clip.

He had blood, and he looked at the crack in the sky. The light coming through it was intense, UV-heavy. It burned.

He looked at his own hand. He could use his own body heat. No, not enough.

He looked at the electrical box on the side of the building. The wires were exposed, sparking where they touched the wet brick.

High voltage.

Dale walked toward the electrical box. The swarm parted around him, confused by his proximity. He was the anomaly. The contaminant.

He reached the box. The sparks danced in the rain. He held the metal clip of his clipboard near the live wire.

The connection was immediate. A arc of blue-white light jumped from the wire to the clip. The metal glowed red.

Dale didn't pull away. He pressed the glowing clip against the back of the nearest beetle.

The insect shrieked, a high-pitched sound that made Dale's teeth ache. Its shell blackened, cracking and peeling. It fell dead.

He moved to the next. And the next.

It was slow. It was brutal. It was inefficient.

But it was working.

**TREATMENT POINTS: +5** **TREATMENT POINTS: +5** **TREATMENT POINTS: +5**

The counter in his vision ticked up. Small numbers. Meaningless.

Then, a new notification.

**CERTIFICATION LEVEL: II (INTERMEDIATE PEST CONTROL)** **UNLOCKED PROTOCOL: THERMAL DISRUPTION (TIER 1)**

Dale paused. He looked at the notification. He looked at the glowing clip. He looked at the swarm, which was now swarming up his legs, biting through his pants.

He had unlocked a protocol. But he hadn't learned it. He had just stumbled into the prerequisite.

The Authority didn't care how you got there. It only cared that you were there.

A hand grabbed his ankle.

Dale looked down. Miller was there. She wasn't gone.

She hadn't left, and she was crawling out of the sewer, her face smeared with dirt and blood. She looked terrified, but her eyes were focused.

She wasn't attacking the bugs. She was holding a fire extinguisher.

Dale blinked. Where had she gotten it?

Miller raised the extinguisher. The nozzle was frozen.

She looked at Dale. She didn't speak. She just nodded.

Dale understood, and he stepped back, away from the electrical box. He held the glowing clip out, like a baton.

Miller pulled the pin. The extinguisher fired. A cloud of dry chemical powder exploded outward, covering the swarm in a white fog.

The insects slowed. The powder clogged their spiracles, their breathing tubes. They stumbled, their movements jerky and uncoordinated.

Dale seized the moment, and he slammed the glowing metal clip into the ground. The heat conducted through the puddle of rainwater, heating the liquid in an instant. The water boiled.

The steam rose, mixing with the chemical powder.

The resulting reaction was explosive.

A cloud of scalding vapor erupted from the grate. The insects, coated in powder and submerged in boiling water, didn't just die. They burst.

Carapaces shattered. Exoskeletons dissolved. The sound was like a thousand windshields breaking at once.

Dale shielded his face with the clipboard. The heat washed over him, singeing the hair on his arms. He didn't move. He kept writing.

*Incident 362: Containment Method: Thermal-Chemical Hybrid.* *Outcome: 100% Mortality Rate in Zone A.*

The System pinged. A loud, resonant chime.

**JOB COMPLETE.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 9.5** **CERTIFICATION PROGRESS: 12%**

Dale lowered the clipboard. The paper was ruined, soaked in blood and chemical residue. But the header remained.

The alley was silent. The swarm was dead. Piles of insect husks covered the ground, smoking and steaming.

Miller stood next to him, panting. The fire extinguisher was empty. The nozzle was cracked.

She looked at the piles. She looked at Dale.

"You wrote it down," she said. Her voice was raspy.

Dale nodded, and he looked at his hand. The bleeding had stopped. The skin was burned, blistered, and raw.

He flexed his fingers. They worked.

"It counts," Dale said. "It's in the system."

Miller shook her head, and she walked over to the crack in the sky.

It was smaller now. Closing.

But not gone.

And from it, something was emerging.

Not an insect. Not a rat.

A hand.

Large. Pale. Fingers too long. The skin translucent, showing the veins beneath like blue rivers.

It gripped the edge of the reality tear. It pulled itself through.

Dale's Field Guide flashed.

**NEW SPECIES DETECTED: VOID ARCHITECT (CLASS V).** **WARNING: LICENSING REQUIRED: CLASS III.** **YOU ARE UNDER-CERTIFIED.**

Dale froze.

Class V. He was Level II.

He couldn't fight this, and he couldn't even document it properly. The penalty for engaging an over-leveled threat was severe. Permanent revocation.

The hand pulled itself further. The arm followed. Then the shoulder.

The creature was humanoid, but distorted. Its face was a smooth, featureless oval. It wore no clothes. It radiated cold.

Miller stepped back.

"Dale. We need to run."

Dale didn't run, and he looked at the ruined clipboard. He looked at the pen, which was still broken in his teeth.

He looked at the creature.

The Authority had given him a rating of 9.5. It had given him points. It had recognized his thoroughness.

But it hadn't given him the power.

The creature looked at them. It tilted its head.

A voice echoed in Dale's mind. Not in his ears. In his bones.

*UNLICENSED ENTITY DETECTED. COMPLIANCE REQUIRED.*

Dale felt a pull. Not physical. Bureaucratic.

The System was trying to assign him the job, and he looked at his status screen.

**CURRENT STATUS: UNDER-CERTIFIED.** **PENALTY FOR ENGAGEMENT: TERMINATION OF LICENSE.** **TERMINATION MEANS: ERASURE.**

If he fought, he died.

Not just physically. Existentially.

His records would be wiped.

His certification revoked. He would cease to be a Technician. He would just be a man with a clipboard.

And then the creature would kill him.

But if he didn't fight, the infestation spread. The other dimensions got infected.

The older Dale's voice returned. *Save the world, or save your license.*

Dale looked at Miller. She was waiting. Her hand was on the empty extinguisher. She was ready to leave.

Dale looked at the creature. It was pulling itself fully into the alley now. It stood eight feet tall. Its skin shifted, mimicking the texture of the brick wall.

He took a breath, and he picked up the broken pen. He picked up the ruined clipboard.

He opened his Field Guide.

There was a loophole. He had seen it in the fine print of the Certification Manual, the one he had read during the written exam.

**CLAUSE 44-B: PROVISIONAL AUTHORIZATION.**

In cases of imminent Class V breach, a Level II Technician may invoke Provisional Authority to contain the threat, provided they accept full liability for any resulting structural damage to the dimensional fabric.

The cost wasn't his life, and the cost was his debt.

The Authority didn't care about money. They cared about balance.

If he invoked Provisional Authority, he would accrue a Debt Score.

If the Debt Score got too high, the Authority would seize his assets.

Not his house. Not his car.

His memories, and they would take the memories of his work. The satisfaction. The pride. The knowledge that he had done it right.

He would be left with only the trauma. The horror. The pain.

The creature took a step forward. The ground cracked beneath its foot.

Dale looked at Miller.

"Get behind me," he said.

Miller didn't argue. She moved behind him, crouching low.

Dale raised the clipboard. He didn't write. He held it up like a shield.

He spoke to the air. To the System.

"I invoke Clause 44-B," Dale said. His voice was steady. "Provisional Authority. Class V Containment."

The world went silent.

Then, a new text box appeared. Red. Pulsing.

**PROVISIONAL AUTHORITY GRANTED.** **DEBT ACCRUED: 10,000 POINTS.** **WARNING: INSUFFICIENT FUNDS TO COVER LIABILITY.** **ASSET SEIZURE INITIATED.**

Dale felt a sharp tug in his mind. A hook in his brain.

He remembered his daughter's face.

Then the memory was gone. Replaced by a blank space. A void where the joy had been.

He gasped, stumbling forward.

But he was still standing.

The creature paused. It looked at Dale. For the first time, it seemed uncertain.

Dale looked at his hands. They were shaking. But they were still holding the pen.

He didn't know why he was fighting. He didn't know why he loved his daughter. He knew he had a daughter.

He knew he loved her. But the feeling was gone.

The texture of it was gone, which was just data now.

**FIELD GUIDE UPDATE: VOID ARCHITECT.** **WEAKNESS: UNCERTAINTY.**

Dale looked at the creature. He smiled. It was a hollow smile. An empty smile.

"You're not certified," Dale said.

He raised the pen.

And he attacked.
