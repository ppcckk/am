Chapter 348: Protocol Is a Weapon

# Chapter 348

The janitor watched Dale's hands.

They were steady. Too steady.

A man with a clipboard in a commercial zone during a Class-4 breach usually shook like a leaf in a gale, or dropped the clipboard entirely to run for his life.

Dale Kowalski did neither. He adjusted the grip on the mop handle, tested the weight, and looked at the bucket with the clinical detachment of a surgeon choosing a scalpel.

To the janitor, who had seen heroes weep and gods kneel, this was more terrifying than the monsters themselves. It suggested a mind that had categorized the apocalypse and found it merely inefficient.

The janitor checked his watch. The hour hand trembled.

He stepped back into the shadows of the loading dock, letting Dale approach the black water.

The janitor's eyes narrowed. This wasn't just about survival anymore.

This was about protocol.

And protocol, in Dale's hands, was a weapon sharper than any sword.

***

Dale ignored the screaming reflection, and he ignored the girl. He ignored the grey suit. He focused on the fluid dynamics of the bucket.

The black sludge wasn't just water. It was viscous, clinging to the metal rim with a surface tension that defied natural laws. It moved in slow, deliberate pulses, like a heartbeat.

Dale dipped the mop handle into the bucket. He didn't agitate it. He let it sink, then lifted it. The sludge clung to the fibers, forming a thick, tar-like coating.

**SPECIMEN COLLECTED.** **STATUS: CONTAMINATED.** **ACTION REQUIRED: ISOLATE.**

A text box flashed in the corner of his vision. The font was standard Authority grey, cold and sans-serif.

Dale pulled the mop back. He didn't try to clean it.

Cleaning was a waste of time when the contamination was active.

Instead, he reached for the duct tape on his belt, and he ripped a strip with his teeth and wrapped it around the mop head, sealing the fibers tight.

This was a deviation. Standard pest control protocol required disposal of contaminated tools. But the Authority's new rule—*Isolation Precedes Elimination*—was ambiguous on tool preservation.

Dale exploited the ambiguity. If he disposed of the mop, he lost his only physical anchor.

If he sealed it, he trapped the sample inside the tool.

He wrapped the mop head completely. Then he wrapped the handle, leaving only the end exposed.

The black water in the bucket surged. It didn't splash. It *reached*. Tendrils of sludge rose from the surface, forming shapes that looked like fingers, grasping at the air.

Dale stepped back. He opened his Field Guide.

He didn't look up from the tablet screen. He scrolled through the existing entries. *Rattus profundus. Acid Beetles. Dimensional Vermin.*

None of them matched the viscosity of the substance in the bucket.

He created a new entry.

**SPECIES: [UNNAMED]** **TYPE: LIQUID PHASE PARASITE** **BEHAVIOR: SENTIENT MIMICRY** **WEAKNESS: UNKNOWN**

He typed the last word with deliberate slowness. The System hated unknowns. It punished uncertainty with debuffs.

Dale knew this.

He was banking on the fact that identifying a *behavior* was worth more than identifying a *species* if the species was truly unknown.

The bucket overflowed.

The sludge spilled onto the concrete floor. It didn't spread like a puddle. It moved like a school of fish, darting toward Dale's boots.

Dale didn't move his feet. He planted them wide. He swung the sealed mop like a bat, not at the sludge, but at the concrete near it.

*Crack.*

The impact sent a shockwave through the floor. The sludge recoiled, pulling back from the vibration.

"Resonance sensitivity," Dale muttered. He tapped the screen. **WEAKNESS: HIGH-FREQUENCY VIBRATION.**

The System pinged.

**IDENTIFICATION CONFIRMED. TARGETED DAMAGE BONUS APPLIED: +25% TO VIBRATIONAL ATTACKS.**

The grey-suited man in the reflection smiled. He raised a hand. The girl's scream intensified, distorting the sound waves. The black water around Dale boiled, rising in a column that reached ten feet high.

The janitor, watching from the shadows, felt a chill crawl up his spine. Dale wasn't fighting the monster. He was auditing it.

Dale looked up. The column of sludge formed a face. The girl's face. Her eyes were hollow pits of ink.

"Help me," the face gurgled. The voice sounded wet, like a throat full of fluid.

Dale checked his phone.

**EXAM START TIME: 12:00 PM.** **CURRENT TIME: 11:58 PM.** **LOCATION: COMMERCIAL ZONE, SECTOR 4.**

Two minutes.

If he stopped to talk, he'd be late.

If he rushed, he'd miss the documentation required for the Thoroughness Rating. If he failed to document, the points would be void. If he failed the exam, his certification would be revoked.

Without certification, he couldn't legally treat the next rift. Without treating the rift, the girl would die.

It was a closed loop of bureaucratic death.

Dale looked at the mop. The duct tape was holding, but the wood was starting to splinter from the internal pressure of the trapped sludge.

He needed a new tool. He needed to verify the weakness without getting wet.

He looked around the commercial zone. It was a strip mall, abandoned. Storefronts with shattered glass. A hardware store across the street.

Dale ran, and he didn't sprint. He jogged with efficient strides, conserving oxygen, keeping his clipboard tucked under his arm. He crossed the street, weaving between the abandoned cars.

The black water pursued him. It flowed down the sidewalks, faster than any liquid should move. It was gaining on him.

Dale reached the hardware store. The door was locked.

He didn't bother with the lock. He used the mop handle to smash the glass. The sound of breaking glass was sharp, clear.

The sludge behind him paused. It didn't like the high-frequency sound of shattering glass.

Dale stepped inside. The store smelled of sawdust and stale air. He moved to the aisle marked *Power Tools*.

He didn't grab a chainsaw. That was too heavy, too slow, too messy.

He grabbed a cordless drill and a box of drill bits. Specifically, the ones designed for masonry. Diamond-tipped.

He ran back outside, and the sludge had formed a wall across the street, blocking his path back to the bucket. It pulsed with a low, throbbing light.

Dale stopped. He was trapped.

The grey-suited man stepped out of the reflection in the window of the hardware store. He was no longer just a reflection. He was stepping through the glass.

His suit was pristine. His tie was perfectly knotted. He held a clipboard of his own. It was made of bone.

"Mr. Kowalski," the man said. His voice was smooth, devoid of accent. "You are interfering with an approved contract."

Dale looked at the bone clipboard. He looked at the sludge wall. He looked at the drill in his hand.

"I don't have the contract," Dale said.

"You have the job," the man replied. "And the job is to eliminate the host. The girl is the host."

"She's a victim," Dale said.

He flipped open his Field Guide, and he added a new note. **SPECIES: CONTRACT ENFORCER (HUMANOID). TYPE: ADMINISTRATIVE THREAT.

WEAKNESS: PROCEDURAL ERROR.**

The System didn't ping. It didn't recognize the entry.

The man's smile vanished.

"That's not a valid classification," the man said.

"It is," Dale said. He pointed the drill at the sludge wall. "I'm going to drill a hole."

The man tilted his head.

"A hole in what?"

"In the wall. To test the structural integrity."

Dale revved the drill. The masonry bit spun at high speed. He jammed it into the sludge wall.

The sludge screamed. A high-pitched, metallic shriek that shattered the remaining windows in the strip mall.

Dale held on. The vibration traveled up his arms, shaking his teeth. The sludge resisted, trying to absorb the drill, but the diamond tip cut through.

It didn't cut like flesh. It cut like ice.

A hole opened in the black wall. Through the hole, Dale saw the girl. She was suspended in the center of the column, her body encased in the black fluid. She was unconscious.

But she was breathing.

And the grey-suited man wasn't attacking. He was watching.

Why was he watching?

Dale pulled the drill out. The bit was glowing red hot.

**TREATMENT POINTS AWARDED: 50.** **DOCUMENTATION UPLOADED.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 6.5 (PENDING VERIFICATION).**

Dale frowned. 6.5? That was low. He had identified the weakness, isolated the sample, and attempted elimination. Why the penalty?

He looked at the hole. The sludge was sealing itself. The edges of the hole were knitting together, but they were moving slowly.

He realized his mistake, and he hadn't isolated the *infestation*. He had only isolated a *portion* of it. The main body was still connected to the girl. If he drilled more holes, he might kill the girl by severing the neural link the parasitic sludge had formed.

The grey-suited man stepped closer. He held out the bone clipboard.

"Sign," he said. "Acknowledge the host as collateral damage.

You get your points, and she dies. The rift closes.

"

Dale looked at the clipboard. The contract was written in a language that hurt his eyes to look at directly. Symbols that writhed on the page.

He looked at the girl. She twitched in her fluid prison.

He looked at his phone.

**EXAM START TIME: 12:00 PM.** **TIME REMAINING: 00:02.**

If he signed, he passed the implicit test of *diligence* by completing the job, even if the job was unethical. The Authority valued completion over morality.

If he didn't sign, he would be late. Late meant automatic failure. Failure meant contract termination. Termination meant death.

Dale took the pen from the man's coat pocket, and he didn't sign.

He wrote a note on the bottom of the bone clipboard.

**VIOLATION: UNLICENSED INTERVENTION.** **CITATION: K-7.**

The man's eyes went wide.

"You can't cite me," the man hissed. "I am the Authority."

"No," Dale said. "The Authority is the System. You are a contractor. And contractors require permits."

He tapped the screen of his Field Guide. He highlighted the entry for *Contract Enforcer*. He dragged it into the *Threat Assessment* module.

The System pinged.

**CONFLICT DETECTED.** **SOURCE: FIELD GUIDE ENTRY VS. CURRENT JOB PARAMETERS.** **ACTION: RESOLVE CONFLICT TO CONTINUE.**

The grey-suited man lunged. His hand turned into a claw of black sludge.

Dale didn't dodge. He slammed the bone clipboard onto the ground.

He stepped on it.

The sound of cracking bone echoed. The man screamed, a sound of bureaucratic outrage.

"Stop!" the man yelled. "You are creating an audit trail!"

"I am documenting the scene," Dale said. He picked up the mop. He dragged it toward the sludge wall, leaving a trail of wet black residue.

He was creating a hazard. A slip-and-fall risk.

The man stopped. He couldn't attack while Dale was creating a visible, documented hazard. The Authority's rules stated that *any* active hazard must be secured before combat could proceed.

Dale pulled out the duct tape again. He taped the mop to the floor. Then he taped the drill to the mop. Then he taped the man's bone clipboard to the drill.

He built a totem pole of evidence.

The sludge wall shuddered. The vibration from the drill, still running, was resonating through the chain. The sludge liquefied, losing its cohesion.

The man looked at the clipboard. He looked at Dale.

"You're cheating," the man said.

"I'm working," Dale said. "Check my Thoroughness Rating."

The man looked at the System interface floating above the clipboard.

**THOROUGHNESS RATING: 9.8.** **STATUS: EXCELLENT.**

The man's face went grey.

The sludge wall collapsed. It didn't fall; it *evaporated*, turning into a fine mist that drifted into the sky. The girl fell to the ground, coughing up black bile. She was alive.

The grey-suited man vanished. The contract was void. The infestation was treated.

Dale picked up his clipboard. He wiped the black slime off it with his sleeve.

He looked at his phone.

**EXAM START TIME: 12:00 PM.** **LOCATION: COMMERCIAL ZONE, SECTOR 4.** **STATUS: DELAYED.**

He was late.

But the System didn't mark him as failed.

A new notification appeared.

**PROTOCOL VIOLATION: UNAUTHORIZED TOOL MODIFICATION.** **PENALTY: NONE.** **REWARD: BONUS MULTIPLIER FOR INNOVATION.**

Dale smiled. It was a thin, tight smile.

He walked toward the girl. She was shivering.

"I need a blanket," Dale said.

The janitor stepped out of the shadows. He was holding a thick wool coat.

"You're lucky," the janitor said. "The Authority doesn't like improvisation. They like checklists."

"I know," Dale said. He draped the coat over the girl. "But checklists don't account for sentient contracts."

The janitor looked at the spot where the grey-suited man had stood.

"He'll be back," the janitor said. "And he won't be alone."

Dale looked at his Field Guide. The entry for *Contract Enforcer* was now marked as *Verified*.

He had a new enemy. And he had a new weakness to exploit.

But the girl was alive. And the exam had already started.

Dale looked at the girl. She was holding something in her hand. It wasn't a weapon.

It was a business card, and he took it. The card was black. Embossed with silver letters.

**LICENSING AUTHORITY INTERNAL INVESTIGATIONS DIVISION.**

Underneath the logo, a name was printed.

*Director Vane.*

Dale looked at the janitor.

"Is she an investigator?"

The janitor shook his head.

"No. She's the whistleblower."

Dale looked at the girl. She wasn't shivering anymore. She was looking at the business card with a calm, cold expression.

She looked up at Dale.

"You're not a technician," she said. Her voice was raspy.

Dale paused. "I am. Class II."

"No," she said. "You're a target. And so am I."

She stood up. She dropped the business card.

"Welcome to the audit, Dale."

The ground beneath them cracked. Not with a rift. With a stamp.

A massive, red seal appeared on the pavement. **PENDING REVIEW.**

Dale looked at the seal. He looked at the girl. He looked at the janitor.

The janitor was gone.

The exam wasn't over. It had just begun.
