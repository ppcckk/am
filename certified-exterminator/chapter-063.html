Chapter 63: Certified Infestation

# Chapter 63

The foam hit Dale in the chest and he went down. Not hard. Not dramatic. His feet left the ground for maybe half a second before his knees buckled and he sat in a spreading puddle of gray-white polymer that smelled like a hospital corridor and tasted like pennies.

The Authority notification came immediately. Not for him. For the sprayer.

PROTOCOL MISCLASSIFICATION: Class-3 Containment Foam deployed against non-creature target. Violation Code 11-C. Cross-contamination penalty applied. Field Guide entries for target species contaminated for 24 hours.

The sprayer guy looked at his own hands like they'd betrayed him, and the nozzle on his containment unit was still humming.

He hadn't meant to fire at Dale.

Or maybe he had. Dale couldn't tell. The man's face was doing something complicated that Dale didn't have time to decode.

The foam was already setting.

Dale could feel it stiffening against his coveralls, pulling his arms toward his torso, and he had maybe ninety seconds before full immobilization.

Class-3 containment was designed for burrowing creatures with exoskeletal joints.

It worked on human limbs too. Physics didn't care about your certification level.

He looked at the woman. She was walking toward him.

Clipboard in one hand, the mimic's copy of her Field Guide in the other.

Her pace was unhurried.

She'd already won the documentation phase. All she had to do was complete the filing.

"Filing what?" Dale said. His voice came out flat. Professional.

His tone matched that of a man reading a lease violation to a landlord whose sewer line had backed up into a tenant's kitchen. "You can't file me.

I'm not a target species."

"You're a licensed exterminator operating outside protocol parameters," she said. "You entered an active infestation zone without a secondary. You failed to verify workspace conditions before engagement.

You deployed a provisional entry with no weakness field.

That's four violations."

"That's four violations for me. You've got one for me that you pulled from a mimic's copy of a guide you destroyed. That's not admissible."

"It's in the guide."

"It's in the mimic's guide. Not mine."

She stopped walking.

Five feet away. Close enough to read the clipboard. She held it up so he could see.

The filing form was already half-complete. His name at the top.

His certification number.

His rating. 7.4.

And below that, a classification field that was still blank, and she needed one more thing. A species designation. Without it, the filing was incomplete.

Incomplete filings didn't trigger enforcement. That was the rule.

Dale knew the rule.

The Authority's Thoroughness Scale was built on the assumption that incomplete work was still work, but it didn't activate protocols. You couldn't exterminate what you hadn't classified.

So she needed to classify him.

And to classify him, she needed a weakness. And the weakness was in the mimic's guide.

And the mimic's guide was not his guide.

And if she used it, the Authority would flag the source.

Unless she could get him to confirm it.

"Your weakness is contamination anxiety," she said. "You won't engage until your workspace meets protocol standards.

That's a behavioral classification.

I can file you as a Class-4 behavioral anomaly.

That's a valid species designation. It doesn't require a combat weakness.

It requires a behavioral pattern. And I have it."

Dale looked at the foam on his arms. He looked at the floor.

The polymer was spreading under him, reaching toward his boots.

His workspace was a mess. Foam everywhere. The sprayer guy's containment unit was still active, dripping residue.

The mimic's guide was open on the floor where someone had dropped it.

Pages scattered. A violation of documentation integrity protocols.

And Dale noticed it, and he noticed the mess. And his hands stopped moving. Not because of the foam.

Because the room was wrong. The pages were scattered.

The foam was uncontained.

The sprayer guy was dripping residue on the floor without a spill kit.

His hands stopped moving. The foam was still setting. Ninety seconds. Maybe sixty now.

The woman smiled. She started writing.

Dale watched her pen move. He watched the classification field filling in.

Class-4 Behavioral Anomaly. Species designation: Kowalski, Dale. Certification Level IV.

Rating 9.2. Weakness field: contaminated workspace dependency.

It was almost complete.

One more line. She was writing the verification signature. The Authority would accept it.

Any licensed exterminator in the region would get the notification.

A new target. A new infestation.

And they'd come for him.

Dale's hands were still still. The foam was almost fully set. He couldn't move his arms.

His legs were locked. His torso was encased in a shell that was getting harder by the second.

But his eyes were moving. And his mouth was moving.

"Source authentication," he said.

The woman's pen stopped.

"What?"

"Source authentication.

Article 7, Section 3 of the Licensing Authority's Field Guide maintenance code.

Any entry used for combat classification must have a verified source. The source must be the exterminator's own guide.

Or a guide provided by the Authority. Or a guide that has been formally transferred through a licensing transaction."

"That's not a rule."

"It's in the code. Page forty-three of the maintenance manual. You can look it up."

"I don't need to look it up."

"You do. Because the entry you're using was written by a mimic.

A mimic is not a licensed source.

It's not an Authority-provided document. It's not a transferred guide. It's an unauthorized copy produced by a Class-2 shapeshifter.

The source is invalid."

The woman's face didn't change. But her pen didn't move.

"The Authority doesn't check sources," she said.

"The Authority checks sources when a dispute is filed. And I'm filing one."

The notification appeared in his peripheral vision. Not for him. For her.

DISPUTE FILED: Field Guide entry source challenged. Adjudication pending.

Target entry: Kowalski, Dale.

Source: unauthorized mimic copy. Status: under review.

The woman looked at the notification. Then she looked at Dale. Her face was still calm. But her pen was down.

"You can't file a dispute while immobilized," she said.

"I didn't file it with my hands. I filed it verbally.

The Authority recognizes verbal disputes when the filer is within visual range of the disputed document.

I can see the guide.

I can see your name on the cover. I can see the mimic's watermarks on the pages."

She looked at the guide in her hands. She looked at the pages. The watermarks. She'd been so focused on the content that she hadn't checked the provenance.

The Authority's adjudication was fast. Faster than Dale expected. The notification came back in under ten seconds.

ADJUDICATION COMPLETE: Source authentication failed. Entry classified as UNVERIFIED. Weakness field voided.

Combat classification based on this entry is invalid. Filing rejected.

The woman's clipboard went blank. The filing form dissolved. The classification she'd been building, gone.

She stared at the clipboard.

Then she stared at Dale. And for the first time, something moved behind her calm. Not anger.

Recognition.

Like she'd just found a code violation in her own work and was deciding whether to fix it or walk away.

She walked away.

Not fast.

Not panicked. She picked up the mimic's guide from the floor, closed it, and put it in her bag. She looked at the sprayer guy.

He was still standing there, his containment unit dripping, his Field Guide entries contaminated; was looking at the woman with an expression that was changing.

Because the Authority had done something else. While adjudicating Dale's dispute, it had scanned the woman's own Field Guide for source integrity. Standard procedure. When you challenge one entry, the Authority audits the whole guide.

The notification went to everyone in the room. Including Dale.

AUDIT COMPLETE: 14 entries in subject's Field Guide flagged as UNVERIFIED. Source: unauthorized mimic copies. Subject has been operating with contaminated documentation for an estimated 6 weeks. All combat classifications based on these entries are retroactively invalid.

The sprayer guy read it. Dale read it. The documentation specialist read it.

The woman's guide was full of mimic entries.

Fourteen species.

Fourteen weaknesses. All sourced from a creature that wasn't licensed to write them.

Which meant every fight she'd won in the last six weeks was built on unverified data. Which meant the Authority's Thoroughness Rating for those fights was about to be recalculated.

And recalculated ratings could drop, and if they dropped below the threshold for her certification level, she'd lose her license.

The sprayer guy raised his containment unit. Not at Dale. At her.

"You're unlicensed," he said. "You've been operating unlicensed."

"I'm licensed. My certification is valid."

"Your documentation isn't. That's a Level-3 violation. You know what happens to Level-3 violators."

She knew. Everyone knew.

Level-3 violations triggered mandatory field review.

The Authority would send an inspector. And inspectors didn't negotiate. They sealed.

They quarantined unlicensed operators, treating them like unlicensed infestations.

The woman looked at the sprayer guy. She looked at his nozzle. She looked at the foam still setting on Dale's arms. And then she did something Dale didn't expect.

She opened her bag, and she pulled out another guide. Not the mimic's copy. Her own.

The real one. The one she'd been carrying all along.

She opened it.

And she read a page.

The sprayer guy's name was on it. His weakness. Filled in.

"Contamination anxiety," she said. "He won't engage until his workspace meets protocol standards."

The sprayer guy froze.

His nozzle wavered, and he looked at the floor. At the foam residue dripping from his unit.

At the scattered pages. At the mess he'd made.

His hands stopped moving.

Dale watched the sprayer guy lock up. Watched him stare at his own boots like they'd committed a crime.

And Dale understood, with the cold clarity of a man reading a violation report, that this woman had been running the same play on everyone.

She'd built a network of mimic-copied guides. She'd seeded weaknesses into the guides of every exterminator she'd worked with, and when the time came, she'd use those weaknesses like a trigger.

She hadn't been trying to file Dale. She'd been testing whether the same trick worked on a man with a 7.4 rating. And it had. Almost.

She looked at Dale, and he was still encased in foam. Still unable to move.

But his eyes were on her.

And his mouth was open.

"You've got fourteen unverified entries," he said. "That's fourteen people whose weaknesses you know. Fourteen people you can freeze. Fourteen people you can walk up to and file."

She closed the guide. She put it back in her bag.

"And you've got a Level IV certification," she said. "Which means you're the only one in this region who can read the Authority's adjudication notices in full.

The other twelve people in my network can't, and they don't have the clearance level.

They just see the weakness trigger. They don't see the source flag.

They don't know their own guides are contaminated."

She stepped back. Toward the door.

"I'm going to leave," she said. "And I'm going to keep doing this.

Because the Authority's source authentication only works if someone files a dispute.

And most people can't. They don't know the code. They don't know the maintenance manual.

They don't know about page forty-three."

Dale watched her go. The foam was still setting.

He couldn't move, and the sprayer guy was still frozen at his own boots; the documentation specialist was still watching.

And in Dale's peripheral vision, the Authority's notification was still open. The audit results.

Fourteen entries.

Fourteen names, and he read them. He read the certification levels.

He read the ratings.

And the last name on the list made his stomach drop.

It was his own.

Not as a target. As a source.

His Field Guide had been copied by the mimic.

His entries had been used to build the woman's network. His identification work, his documentation, his careful methodical species classifications. All of it had been harvested.

Fed into a mimic.

Used to build a web of weaponized weaknesses across the region.

He wasn't just a target. He was the blueprint.

And the Authority's next line was worse than anything he'd expected.

RECOMMENDED ACTION: Source extermination. Subject Dale Kowalski is the origin point of unauthorized documentation network. Classification: Class-7 Vector. Weakness field: pending identification.

He was a vector. A source. The thing that had spread the infection. And the Authority wanted him exterminated.

The foam finished setting, and he couldn't move at all. And the notification was still open. And the sprayer guy was starting to thaw.

And the documentation specialist was pulling out her own clipboard.

And Dale Kowalski, Certification Level IV, Thoroughness Rating 7.4, was classified as an infestation. And he couldn't move.
