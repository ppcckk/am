Chapter 174: The Landlord Is Coming

# Chapter 174

The fluorescent tube above the filing cabinet flickered, buzzing with the low-frequency hum of a dying insect. Dale Kowalski didn't look up. He kept his eyes on the form in front of him, his pen scratching against the carbon paper with rhythmic, aggressive precision.

*Form 88-B: Preliminary Assessment of Class 9 Anomalous Entity.*

*Subject Designation: The Clerk.*

*Location: County Records Office, Sub-Level 2.*

The pen stopped. A drop of black ink pooled on the *Threat Level* box.

Dale shook his hand, watching the droplet slide down the column, obscuring the number 9.

He didn't wipe it away. It looked like a bruise. It looked like truth.

"Sign here," the junior agent said. Her voice was tight, vibrating with the kind of fear that smells like copper pennies. She held the pen out, but her hand hovered, trembling just enough to blur the tip.

Dale took the pen, and he didn't sign. He tapped the pen against the paper, three times. *Tap.

Tap.

Tap.*

"Read clause 42," Dale said.

The agent blinked. She looked at the form, then at Dale.

"Clause 42?"

"Section regarding Inter-Departmental Jurisdiction," Dale said. His voice was flat, devoid of the adrenaline that still pumped through his veins.

"If the entity is classified as a Class 9, it falls under the Bureau of Dimensional Integrity, not the Municipal Extermination Division.

I am a Level II Technician.

I do not have authority to sign off on Class 9 containment protocols.

"

"But the Agent said—"

"The Agent gave you a clipboard," Dale interrupted. "He did not give you a license endorsement."

Dale stood up. The chair scraped against the linoleum, a harsh sound in the quiet office. He looked at the junior agent, then at the two other rookies behind her.

They were young. Too young.

Their uniforms were crisp, the Authority logos still peeling off the sleeves.

They hadn't yet learned that the Authority didn't care if they lived or died, only that the paperwork was filed correctly.

"Fix the jurisdiction header," Dale said. "Change 'Municipal' to 'Federal/Interdimensional.' Add a witness line for myself, but do not list me as primary handler. List me as *Consultant on Vermin Identification*."

The agent frowned.

"That's not... that's not a standard role."

"Standard roles get you eaten," Dale said.

He walked to the door.

"Make the change.

Or don't.

But if you file it as a Municipal Job, I will refuse to provide the Field Guide entry for *Clericus infestus*. And you will be fighting a creature that feeds on bureaucratic guilt with a spray bottle of bleach.

"

The silence in the room was heavy. The agent swallowed. She picked up her own pen. She crossed out *Municipal* and wrote *Federal/Interdimensional* in shaky, blocky letters.

Dale watched her write it. He felt a tiny, cold satisfaction. It wasn't heroism. It was liability management.

He stepped out of the records office and into the hallway. The air here was stale, recycled through vents that hadn't been cleaned since the 1990s. It smelled of old paper, dust, and something else—something sweet and rotting, like fruit left too long in a jar.

*System Notification: Field Guide Entry Updated.* *Species: Clericus infestus (The Clerk).* *Weakness: Procedural Inconsistency.* *Note: Target exploits administrative loopholes to bypass physical barriers. To engage, one must introduce a paradoxical regulation into the target's immediate workspace.*

Dale read the entry three times. It was new.

He hadn't written it yet, and the Authority was feeding him data before he'd even seen the nest.

That meant the nest was close. It meant the Clerk was already aware of him.

He walked down the hall. His footsteps echoed. *Clack. Clack.

Clack.* The heels of his boots hit the tile in a steady beat. He checked his wrist monitor.

*Thoroughness Rating: 9.9.* *Pending Tasks: 1.* *Current Objective: Locate Primary Nesting Site.*

The hallway stretched out, lined with filing cabinets that reached the ceiling, and the labels were handwritten. *Divorce Proceedings 1988-1992. Tax Lien Disputes. Zoning Variances.* The Rift above the city had warped the architecture, but the bureaucracy had remained stubbornly intact.

The monsters hadn't destroyed the records; they had *become* the records.

A shadow moved behind a stack of boxes.

Dale stopped. He didn't reach for his spray. He reached for his clipboard.

"Who's there?" he called out. His voice was polite. Professional.

No answer. Just the rustle of paper.

He approached the stack. The boxes were labeled *Personnel Files: Terminated Employees.* The handwriting on the label was jagged, frantic. Dale pulled the top box open.

It was empty.

But inside the box, taped to the bottom, was a form. *Form 1099-D: Notification of Termination.*

Dale picked it up. The ink was wet.

*To: Dale Kowalski.* *From: The Clerk.* *Reason: Excessive Thoroughness.*

Dale's jaw tightened. The Clerk had noticed him.

The Clerk had targeted him.

Not because he was a threat, but because he was a *problem*. He was a glitch in the system. He didn't fight; he audited.

He didn't kill; he documented. And in a world where monsters fed on chaos, Dale was pure, ordered chaos's antithesis.

He folded the form. He put it in his pocket.

"Nice try," he muttered.

He continued down the hall. The sweet rot smell grew stronger. It mixed with the scent of ink and toner. He turned the corner into the main records room.

It was a large, open space.

Rows of metal shelves stretched into the darkness, and the lights here were broken, flickering in erratic patterns. In the center of the room, a desk sat.

A single, wooden desk.

Sitting behind it was a figure.

It looked like a man, but wrong. Its proportions were stretched.

Its arms were too long, hanging past its knees.

Its skin was pale, translucent, veined with black ink. It wore a suit that seemed to be made of shredded legal documents. When it moved, the pages rustled.

It didn't look up. It was writing.

Dale stopped ten feet away. He didn't draw his weapon. He opened his clipboard.

"Excuse me," Dale said. "I'm here to serve a notice of infestation."

The figure paused.

Its pen hovered over a blank page. Slowly, it turned its head.

Its face was smooth.

No nose. No ears. Just a mouth, wide and full of teeth that looked like little staples.

"Notice?" the figure said. Its voice was the sound of pages turning in a windstorm.

"Yes," Dale said. He held up the clipboard.

"You are in violation of Code 77-A: Unauthorized Accumulation of Soul Debt.

You are processing transactions that do not exist.

You are creating phantom liabilities. This is a Class 9 Infestation.

"

The Clerk smiled. The staples clattered against each other.

"You are a Technician," the Clerk said. "You are small. You are licensed for rats.

For beetles. For things that scurry.

"

"I am licensed for problems," Dale said.

The Clerk stood up, which was tall. Ten feet tall. The ceiling groaned.

The shelves rattled.

"I am the Clerk," the Clerk said. "I am the memory of the city. I remember every lie told, every bribe paid, every contract signed in haste. I feed on the weight of unfinished business."

The Clerk raised a hand. A sheet of paper flew from the desk, slicing through the air.

Dale didn't dodge. He didn't block. He caught the paper.

It hit his hand, but it didn't cut. It stuck to his skin. It was a subpoena.

*Subpoena for the Soul of Dale Kowalski.*

Dale looked at the paper. Then he looked at the Clerk.

"This is improper service," Dale said.

The Clerk tilted its head.

"What?"

"You didn't hand it to me," Dale said. "You threw it. Under Ohio Municipal Code, Section 442, service by throwing is invalid if the recipient is not within arm's reach."

The Clerk stared. For a second, the air in the room froze. The flickering lights stopped flickering. The hum of the vents ceased.

The System Notification flashed in Dale's peripheral vision, bright red.

*ERROR: PARADOX DETECTED.* *Target Logic Loop: If Service is Invalid, Attack is Invalid.* *Target Vulnerability Opened: 10 Seconds.*

Dale moved, and he didn't run. He walked.

He walked toward the desk, his steps deliberate.

He pulled a marker from his pocket. A thick, black permanent marker.

He drew a line through the subpoena in his hand.

*VOID.*

He held it up.

"I reject this document," Dale said. "Therefore, you cannot attack me."

The Clerk screamed. It was a sound of tearing paper. The figure lunged. Its long arms swiped at Dale.

Dale didn't flinch. He sidestepped, using the desk as cover. He slid under the desk, into the footwell. The Clerk's claw crashed onto the wood, splintering it.

Dale crawled out the other side. He came up holding a file folder he'd pulled from the bottom drawer.

*File: Property Deed - Lot 442.*

He slammed the folder onto the desk.

"Exhibit A," Dale said.

The Clerk recoiled. The black veins in its skin pulsed.

"You are bound by your own rules," Dale said. He pointed at the folder.

"This deed was filed in 1994.

It was never updated.

The city still recognizes the old lot lines. The building you are occupying is not the building you think it is.

It is a zoning violation.

"

The Clerk's mouth opened, but no sound came out. It looked at the folder. It looked at Dale.

*System Notification: Target Confusion Detected.* *Damage Bonus: +50% (Targeted Weakness: Procedural Inconsistency).*

Dale pulled his spray can. It wasn't acid. It wasn't poison. It was a bottle of correction fluid.

"Correction," Dale said.

He sprayed the desk. White fluid covered the splintered wood.

The Clerk shrieked, its form flickering.

The white fluid seemed to burn it. Because in the Clerk's logic, the error had been fixed. And if the error was fixed, the entity no longer had jurisdiction over the space.

The Clerk tried to lunge again, but its leg phased through the floor. It was losing solidity.

"You can't be here," Dale said. "You don't have the permit."

The Clerk looked at Dale with its eyeless face. There was something in that void. Not anger. Desperation.

"I need to finish the file," the Clerk whispered.

"Then file it correctly," Dale said. "Start over. From the beginning."

The Clerk shuddered. Its body dissolved into loose paper. Sheets fluttered to the ground, covering the floor in a white snow of bureaucracy.

Dale stood up. He brushed dust off his jacket. He picked up the subpoena. He tore it in half.

*System Notification: Infestation Contained.* *Thoroughness Rating: 9.9.* *Next Task: Verify Perimeter.*

Dale didn't celebrate. He walked back to the door. He needed to check the exits. He needed to ensure no other fragments had escaped into the ventilation system.

He reached the hallway. The junior agents were waiting. They looked pale.

"Is it done?" the girl asked.

"It's paused," Dale said. "The Clerk is not dead. It's in suspension. It's waiting for the next audit."

He looked at them.

"Pack up the files. All of them.

Don't leave a single page behind.

If you miss one, it will grow back.

"

The agents nodded. They moved quickly, gathering the scattered papers.

Dale walked to the window. Outside, the Rift was still open. But the black thread of ink that had led to the courthouse was gone.

In its place, a new line appeared, which was thicker. Red. Not black.

It started from the location of the former Clerk's nest and stretched upward, piercing the sky. It went straight into the Rift.

Dale squinted. He pulled out his binoculars.

The line wasn't leading *to* the Rift. It was leading *from* something inside the Rift.

And it was moving.

Something was pulling on the other end.

Dale's blood went cold. He looked at his Field Guide. The entry for *Clericus infestus* changed.

*Status: Dormant.* *New Entry Detected: Upstream Entity.* *Designation: The Auditor.*

The name appeared in blood-red letters.

Dale lowered the binoculars. He looked at the junior agents.

"Get the truck," Dale said. "We're not done."

"But the infestation—"

"The Clerk was the janitor," Dale said. "The Auditor is the landlord. And the landlord is coming."

He opened his clipboard to a fresh page. He wrote one word at the top.

*Escalation.*

The red line in the sky pulsed. It was getting closer.

And it was no longer a line, which was a rope.

And something was climbing down it.
