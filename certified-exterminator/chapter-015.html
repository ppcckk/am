Chapter 15: Certified Too Low

# Chapter 15

The broodmother was not like the others.

Dale saw it the moment his flashlight beam found it. He had killed six broodmothers in four days.

They were all the same: eight legs, a chitinous carapace the color of wet cardboard, a mouth that opened sideways.

They were fast. They were aggressive. They were Rattus profundus, Dimensional Vermin, Field Guide entry one.

He knew their weaknesses as well as he could tell a German cockroach from a brown-banded one.

Substrate deprivation. Cut them off from whatever they were feeding on and they died in ninety seconds.

This one was not fast, which was bigger.

Not dramatically bigger, maybe twenty percent, but the difference showed in how it sat. The others crouched. They were ready to move.

This one was settled. It had grown into the crawlspace, intruding like a tree root into a wall.

Its legs had spread across the joists and fused to the wood, and the carapace had thickened in patches where it pressed against the foundation.

The wood grain ran through the chitin in places, like the thing had been growing and the house had been growing around it.

It was not crouching. It was rooted.

Dale checked his Field Guide. The entry was clean. Substrate deprivation.

He had his damage bonus. He had his protocol.

He had his clipboard, and he reached for the isolation perimeter.

The Authority notification hit him.

CONFLICT OF INTEREST DETECTED. PROPERTY OWNER IS SERVICE PROVIDER. TREATMENT POINTS WITHHELD PENDING EXTERNAL VALIDATION.

He read it twice. He had seen this before. Not this exact wording, but the concept.

In municipal pest control, you could not certify your own work.

If you treated your own property, the report was void. You needed a second technician to sign off.

Or a licensed inspector.

Or a documented witness.

Section 14(b). He had read it in the Authority's licensing manual during exam prep.

A documented witness could validate a treatment if they were present for identification, application, and verification.

The witness did not need to be licensed, and they just needed to observe and confirm each step verbally.

He needed Reyes.

"Reyes." He spoke into the crawlspace access, a forty-inch square opening under the kitchen sink. "I need you down here."

A pause. The sound of her sliding through the opening, and she came in headfirst, like she had been doing this her whole life.

"Tell me what you see," Dale said.

Reyes swept her flashlight across the crawlspace. The beam caught the joists, the vapor barrier, the plumbing. It caught the broodmother.

She went still.

"That's not the same as the others," she said.

"No."

"It's stuck to the wood."

"It's not stuck. It's integrated. Look at the leg joints.

See where the chitin has grown into the joist? That's not attachment.

That's symbiosis with the substrate.

It's been here long enough to merge with the structure."

Reyes moved her flashlight along the legs. The joints were fused. She traced a leg where the chitin and the pine grain had become the same material. She pulled her flashlight back.

"How long?" she asked.

Dale looked at his inspection reports, and he had copies in his truck. He always kept copies.

Fourteen years of inspection reports on this property.

Three foundation inspections. Two crawl space inspections. Every one of them noted moisture intrusion at the slab joint.

Every one of them recommended a moisture barrier. None of them had excavated.

He pulled the reports from his back pocket. They were folded. They were coffee-stained. They were fourteen years old.

He held them up to his flashlight. The Authority processed the documents. A notification appeared.

PRIOR OBSERVATION RECORDS DETECTED. CROSS-REFERENCING WITH FIELD GUIDE.

The Field Guide entry flickered.

New text appeared.

SUBSTRATE-INTEGRATED VARIANT. The damage bonus shifted.

Substrate deprivation still applied, but the multiplier dropped from 2.0x to 1.3x. The thing was too integrated. Cutting it off from the wood would take longer.

Dale noted the change. He did not complain.

The Authority was right, and he had been treating this like a standard broodmother. It was not standard. It was a fourteen-year-old colony that had been feeding on his foundation while he wrote his inspection reports above it.

"Reyes, I need you to witness a protocol. Identification, application, verification. You'll confirm each step verbally. Say 'confirmed' when I tell you to."

"Confirmed," Reyes said.

Dale almost smiled.

"Step one.

Identification. Species: Rattus profundus.

Dimensional Vermin.

Broodmother. Variant: substrate-integrated. Entry updated."

"Confirmed."

"Step two. Isolation."

Dale laid the perimeter. Not duct tape this time.

He had upgraded, and the Authority had issued him a Class II isolation kit after node four.

It was a roll of something that looked like aluminum foil but behaved like a containment membrane. He ran it along the joists, boxing the broodmother in.

The thing did not move. It could not. It was rooted.

"Confirmed."

"Step three. Application."

Dale pulled the spray. Class II protocol.

Substrate deprivation agent, which was designed to sever the bond between the organism and whatever it was feeding on. He sprayed the joists first. The wood hissed.

The broodmother's legs twitched.

The fused joints cracked along a seam that ran from the carapace into the pine.

It was slow.

Slower than the others. The substrate integration meant the agent had to work through the wood grain before it reached the organism. Dale kept spraying.

He worked the perimeter. He did not rush.

Rushing was how you got cross-contamination penalties.

Rushing was how you got a 6 on the Thoroughness Scale.

Reyes watched. She did not flinch. She had been watching him do this for four days.

The broodmother pulled. Its legs tore free from the joists with a sound like a wet staple gun.

It moved for the first time. It was not fast. It was not aggressive.

It was disoriented. It had been stationary for so long that its proprioception was gone.

It did not know where its legs were.

It dragged one forward and it bent the wrong way.

Dale sprayed the carapace.

The agent worked. The chitin softened.

He moved to the mouth.

The thing opened it sideways, as was its habit. He sprayed inside.

The broodmother went still.

"Step four. Verification."

Dale checked the legs. No movement.

He checked the mouth.

No reflex, and he checked the carapace. The substrate bonds were severed.

The thing was dead.

"Confirmed."

The Authority notification came.

TREATMENT COMPLETE. THOROUGHNESS RATING: 9.7. TREATMENT POINTS: 34. CERTIFICATION LEVEL II CONFIRMED.

Dale sat back on his heels. Level II.

He had been Level I for four days.

Four days and eleven nodes. The written exam would come later. The written exam always came later.

Then the crawlspace shook.

Not a tremor.

A vibration. A frequency.

It came from below the slab.

From the soil. From somewhere deeper than the crawlspace.

Dale looked at his phone.

SYSTEMIC INFESTATION: 11/11 NODES ELIMINATED. INFESTATION CLEARED.

Then:

DISPERSAL EVENT DETECTED. 47 JUVENILE VERMIN DISLODGED. MIGRATION VECTOR: FOUNDATION WATER TABLE. ESTIMATED DISPERSAL RADIUS: 200 METERS.

Forty-seven juveniles. Scattered into the water table. Under the neighborhood. Under everyone's houses.

Dale looked at Reyes.

"They're going to spread."

"Can you stop them?"

"Not from here. I need to get above ground."

He was already climbing out. Reyes followed.

They came up through the kitchen sink access, into the kitchen, into the house that was still his house.

The kitchen floor was warm under his boots, which had always been warm, and he had written three reports about the heat.

He had called it a thermal anomaly. He had recommended insulation. He had not excavated.

He walked to the front door. Opened it.

The street was quiet, and the other nodes were done. Reyes's team was packing up at number 1152. Someone was sweeping the porch.

A neighbor.

Normal. The kind of thing that happened when the monsters were gone.

His phone buzzed.

NEW NOTIFICATION.

He looked at it.

CLASS III INFESTATION DETECTED.

LOCATION: 1147 ELMCREST DRIVE. DEPTH: 40 FEET. SPECIES: UNKNOWN.

FIELD GUIDE: EMPTY.

And below it, in text he had never seen before.

Text that was a different color from every other notification he had received.

The color was wrong. It was not the clean white of the Authority's standard alerts.

It was a gray that seemed to shift when he looked at it directly.

WARNING: THIS SPECIES PREDATES THE AUTHORITY. CLASSIFICATION: NATIVE. TREATMENT PROTOCOL: UNAVAILABLE AT CURRENT CERTIFICATION LEVEL. RECOMMENDED ACTION: DO NOT ENGAGE.

Dale looked at the notification. He looked at the front door.

He looked at the soil in the flower bed by the porch.

The flower bed where he had planted marigolds in April. The flower bed where the soil was now moving. Not shifting.

Not settling.

Moving. Something was turning it from below.

He looked at his phone again. The notification was still there. The gray text pulsed once, slowly, like a heartbeat.

He looked at the flower bed.

The soil split open.

Something came up, which was not a rat.

It was not a beetle. It was not anything in his Field Guide. It was pale and long and it had too many segments and it did not stop moving when it saw him.

It did not crouch. It did not flee.

It oriented toward him like a broodmother toward food.

The Authority notification flashed.

SPECIES IDENTIFICATION FAILED. FIELD GUIDE: EMPTY. NO DAMAGE BONUS AVAILABLE. RECOMMENDED ACTION: DO NOT ENGAGE.

Dale looked at the thing, which was about the size of his forearm; it had no eyes that he could see.

It had a mouth that did not open sideways. It opened forward. It opened like a door.

He looked at his phone. He looked at the thing.

He looked at the soil behind it, which was still moving.

Still splitting. Still opening.

He reached for his spray.

The Authority notification flashed one more time.

CERTIFICATION LEVEL: II. REQUIRED CERTIFICATION FOR CLASS III: LEVEL V. ATTEMPTING UNLICENSED TREATMENT WILL RESULT IN FIELD GUIDE CONTAMINATION AND CERTIFICATION REVOCATION.

Dale looked at the notification, and he looked at the thing. He looked at the soil, opening faster now. More than one was coming up, and they were not the only ones.

The whole flower bed was splitting.

The whole front yard. The sidewalk. The neighbor's driveway.

He looked at Reyes, and she was already on the phone.

Dale looked at the things coming out of the ground in front of his house, and he looked at his Field Guide.

It was empty.

He had no damage bonus, and he had no protocol. He had a clipboard and a can of spray that was rated for Class I vermin and a certification level that was three tiers too low.

He set his clipboard on the porch railing.

He picked up the spray.
