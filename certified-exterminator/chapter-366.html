Chapter 366: Unlocking the Auditor

# Chapter 366

The fluorescent tube above the reception desk flickered, buzzing with a frequency that made Dale's molars ache. He didn't look up.

He kept his eyes on the manila folder in front of him, rotating it four degrees clockwise with his left hand, then four degrees counter-clockwise with his right.

A standard inspection maneuver. Calibrate the surface. Check for contamination.

"You're not listening," Agent Vane said. He was a tall man, thin as a wire, wearing a suit that fit poorly around the shoulders. The fabric shimmered with a faint, oily residue.

Dale tapped the folder.

"I am listening to the terms. Clause 4, Section B.

Asset Seizure.

It states that physical limbs are collateral for non-payment of TP debt. It does not specify which limbs.

"

"It specifies *all*," Vane said. He smiled.

His teeth were too white, and they looked like porcelain implants, or maybe calcified bone.

"The Authority does not negotiate with delinquents, Mr. Kowalski.

You treated a Class-9 Auditor with a Class-1 containment seal. That is gross negligence. That is a violation of Code 77-A: Unauthorized Use of High-Tier Resources by Low-Certification Personnel.

"

Dale stopped rotating the folder. He pulled a pen from his inner pocket. It was the burnt one from the hallway, the ink still smeared across the barrel. He uncapped it.

"Code 77-A applies to resources *acquired* through unlicensed channels," Dale said. His voice was flat.

Deadpan.

"I acquired the Auditor's barrier through the Licensing Authority's emergency override interface. I have the timestamp. I have the log.

I have the receipt.

"

Vane leaned forward. The air in the room grew cold. Frost patterns crept across the glass partition behind the desk.

"You forged the receipt."

"I corrected an administrative error," Dale said. He slid a second piece of paper across the desk.

It was a printout, warm to the touch.

It smelled of ozone and sulfur.

"I found this in the Auditor's remains. It's a partial transaction log.

It shows the Authority authorized the expenditure under Provisional Emergency Statute 12. You just haven't processed the paperwork yet.

Bureaucracy takes time.

I know that.

I live in it."

Vane picked up the paper. He held it up to the light. The text shifted, rearranging itself as if confused by the proximity to Dale's hands.

"Provisional Statute 12 requires a witness," Vane said.

"The witness is dead," Dale said. "But the witness's Field Guide entry is still active. Jenkins.

She's standing in the lobby. She has a Level III certification.

Her testimony constitutes valid documentation under Bylaw 44, Paragraph 9: *Testimony of a Licensed Observer during Critical Infrastructure Failure.*

"

Vane dropped the paper. He looked at Dale with something resembling irritation. It wasn't anger. It was the look of a man who had found a cockroach in his clean kitchen.

"You are trying to exploit a loophole," Vane said.

"I am trying to pay my bill," Dale said. "And I am trying to keep my hands. My hands are my primary tools.

Without them, I cannot write reports.

Without reports, I cannot earn TP. Without TP, I cannot pay the debt.

It is a closed loop.

A cycle of failure. You want me to fail, Vane. But the Authority wants data.

And right now, I am the only source of data on the Auditor's fragmentation pattern.

"

Vane sat back. He steepled his fingers.

"Explain."

"Jenkins," Dale called out.

The door to the lobby opened. Jenkins walked in. She was wearing a borrowed coat, too large for her frame. Her hands were shaking, so she kept them in her pockets.

"Sit," Dale said. He pointed to the empty chair opposite him.

Jenkins sat. She looked at Vane, then at Dale.

"I don't know what to say."

"Say what happened," Dale said. "Start with the identification."

"It was... blue," Jenkins said.

Her voice cracked, and she cleared her throat.

"It was blue light.

But it had texture. Like static. Like TV snow.

"

"Dale," Vane said, ignoring Jenkins. "You are coaching the witness."

"I am prompting the witness," Dale said. He didn't look at Vane.

He looked at Jenkins.

"Jenkins. Did you see the spores before the barrier expanded?"

"Yes."

"Did you document them?"

"No. I was... scared."

Dale nodded, and he made a note on his clipboard.

"Standard human reaction.

Fear inhibits documentation.

I will note this as 'Environmental Interference' in my report. Now. Did the spores move toward the Auditor, or away from it?

"

Jenkins frowned. She closed her eyes.

"Away.

They were running.

Not fighting. Running.

"

Vane's eyes narrowed.

"Runners? Auditors do not run.

They are absolute. They are the law.

"

"These were fragments," Dale said.

"Incomplete. Unlicensed. They were scared of the barrier because they knew they were being purged.

It's not law.

It's infestation control. The barrier didn't execute justice.

It exterminated a swarm.

"

"Semantics," Vane said.

"Data," Dale corrected. "The Authority rewards thoroughness. Jenkins's observation that the spores exhibited avoidance behavior is a data point.

It suggests the Auditors are not monolithic entities. They are colonial organisms.

Like bees.

Or termites."

Vane stood up. He walked around the desk. He stopped two feet from Dale. He was close enough that Dale could smell the ozone on his skin.

"You are implying the Licensing Authority is underestimating the threat," Vane said.

"I am implying you have a classification error," Dale said. "You listed the Auditors as Class-9 Singularities.

You should list them as Class-9 Colonial Infestations. That changes the protocol. You don't need a hero.

You need an exterminator. You need me.

"

"I have a team of Level IV Hunters on standby," Vane said.

"They will kill a fragment and write a poem about it," Dale said.

"They will not document the nest. They will not seal the entry point. They will leave the spores in the walls.

And in six weeks, the problem will come back.

Twice as big. With better armor.

"

Jenkins looked up.

"Can you fix it?"

Dale looked at her.

"Can you hold the line?"

"I don't know."

"You can," Dale said.

"You identified the weakness. You saw them run. That's enough.

That's the first step of the protocol. Identification.

I need you to do the second step.

"

Vane looked at his watch.

It didn't have hands. It just had a spinning wheel of numbers.

"You have three minutes before I initiate the seizure protocol. Use them wisely."

Dale picked up the manila folder again. He opened it.

Inside was not paper, but a sheet of translucent plastic.

A Field Guide page. It was blank.

"Jenkins," Dale said. "Look at the page."

Jenkins looked. She squinted.

"It's empty."

"Focus on the memory," Dale said. "The blue light. The static. The running."

Nothing happened.

"Try harder," Dale said.

His voice remained flat, but his knuckles whitened around the pen.

"This is not magic.

This is a cognitive interface.

The Authority reads your intent. If you believe the spores are a colony, the system will update. If you believe they are demons, the system will punish you.

"

"I believe they are monsters," Jenkins whispered.

"Wrong," Dale said. "They are pests.

They invade, and they multiply.

They contaminate. Say it.

"

"They're pests," Jenkins said.

The plastic page flickered. Text appeared. It was faint, grey, like condensation.

*Species: Auditor Fragment (Incomplete).* *Type: Colonial Hive Mind.* *Weakness: Structural Integrity of Barrier.* *Vulnerability: High-frequency vibration disrupts cohesion.*

Jenkins gasped. She pulled her hand back.

"I didn't touch it."

"You didn't have to," Dale said. He wrote the last line on his clipboard. *Targeted Damage Bonus: +40% against Auditor Fragments when using sonic tools.*

He looked at Vane.

"I have a Field Guide entry. I have a weakness.

And I have a witness who corroborates the data. You cannot seize my assets.

Not yet.

Because I am currently providing value to the Authority.

"

Vane stared at the page. He picked it up. He rubbed his thumb over the text. The text faded, then returned, brighter this time.

"This is irregular," Vane said.

"This is thorough," Dale said. "And thoroughness pays."

Vane sat down heavily. He rubbed his temples.

"The debt is still outstanding. Five thousand eight hundred TP."

"I know," Dale said.

"How do you intend to pay?"

"I don't," Dale said.

"I intend to earn it. I have a new contract.

A high-priority one.

One that requires a Level II certification, which I have lost, but which I can regain if I complete the next job. The Authority has a backlog. I can see it in the queue.

Three hundred untreated infestations in Sector 4. All Class-3 or higher.

Sloppy work.

Incomplete reports.

Half-eliminated nests.

"

Vane looked at the spinning watch.

"That is a suicide mission. Those infestations are in active Rift zones."

"They are," Dale said. "But they are also untreated. That means they are vulnerable.

They are disorganized. They are confused.

And they have no license to be there.

"

"You want to go into the Rift Zone without a containment suit?" Vane asked.

"I want to go in with a clipboard," Dale said. "And a can of spray that works. Jenkins can carry the gear. I can carry the data."

Vane laughed. It was a dry, rattling sound.

"You are insane.

You owe us your soul.

You owe us your hands. And you want to go to the Rift Zone?

"

"I want to go to the Collection Agency," Dale said.

"But I'm not going to sit in the waiting room. I'm going to file a counter-claim. For unpaid hazard pay.

For overtime. For the use of my personal vehicle during the breach.

For the psychological trauma sustained during the inspection of the broodmother.

"

Jenkins stared at him.

"You're going to sue the Authority?"

"I'm going to audit them," Dale said.

"And I'm going to win. Because they are messy.

They are bureaucratic. And they are afraid of being wrong. I am not afraid of being wrong.

I am afraid of being unprofessional.

"

Vane stood up, and he walked to the door. He opened it. Two men in black suits stepped in.

They carried briefcases.

They did not look like bailiffs. They looked like accountants.

"Mr.

Kowalski," Vane said. "You have one hour to report to Collection Agency 4. If you are not there, the seizure begins.

If you are there, you will be processed. And you will be released.

But you will be marked.

"

Dale stood up. He buttoned his shirt. He checked his tie.

"Marked how?"

"Red flag," Vane said.

"Any licensed technician who sees your name in the registry will know you are a debtor. They will not work with you.

They will not trade with you. They will hunt you.

"

"Good," Dale said. "Hunters are sloppy.

They don't document, and they don't isolate. They just kill. And that means they leave nests.

And that means I have work.

"

He walked past the bailiffs. He walked past Vane. He walked out into the lobby. Jenkins followed him.

"You're serious?" she asked. "You're going to fight the whole system?"

"I'm going to work the system," Dale said. He pulled his clipboard from his belt.

It was battered, stained with ichor and dust.

"The system is broken, Jenkins. It rewards heroes. Heroes are expensive.

Heroes are inefficient. Heroes get killed.

But exterminators?

We are cheap.

We are durable. And we are thorough.

"

He pushed open the front doors. The air outside was grey. The sky was torn. In the distance, a tower of black smoke rose from the city center.

"Where are we going?" Jenkins asked.

Dale looked at the smoke. He looked at his clipboard. He wrote one word at the top of the page.

*SECTOR 4.*

"To the Rift," Dale said. "We have a backlog to clear. And I have a bill to settle.

If we clear one hundred Class-3 infestations, the penalty is waived. If we clear two hundred, we get a rebate.

"

"And if we don't?"

Dale turned to her.

His eyes were cold.

"Then we don't exist. The Authority deletes debts.

And it deletes the debtors.

"

Jenkins looked at the smoke. She looked at her hands. She reached into her pocket and pulled out a small, jagged stone. It was glowing blue.

"I found this in the Auditor's wall," she said. "It's a shard. A piece of the barrier."

"Put it in your pocket," Dale said. "Don't lose it.

That's a receipt.

That's proof. And proof is the only thing that keeps the bailiffs away.

"

He walked. His steps were rhythmic. Left, right, left, right. A march.

Behind them, the lobby doors closed. Vane watched them go through the window.

He picked up the plastic page, and he crushed it in his hand.

It didn't break. It folded.

"He's going to die," Vane said.

"He's going to audit us," the head bailiff said. "And if he finds the error in the ledger, he will own us."

Vane looked at the crushed page. The text glowed brighter.

*Vulnerability: High-frequency vibration.*

Vane frowned. He tapped his earpiece.

"Get me the sonic array schematics for the Sector 4 perimeter. And find out where Kowalski learned that trick."

The bailiff hesitated.

"He didn't learn it. He invented it."

Vane looked at the smoke in the distance.

"Then he's not a technician. He's a liability."

"No," the bailiff said. "He's a threat. And you can't audit a threat. You have to erase it."

Dale walked faster. Jenkins ran to catch up.

"Dale," she shouted. "Wait. You didn't check the nest."

"I don't need to check the nest," Dale said. "I know where it is. It's in the city center.

It's in the Authority's headquarters. The Auditor wasn't attacking us.

It was hiding something.

And now we're going to find out what.

"

Jenkins stopped.

"What are you talking about?"

Dale turned, and he held up his clipboard. The page was blank again.

But this time, there was a drawing.

A simple, crude sketch of a door. And on the door, a symbol. A key.

"The Auditor wasn't a monster," Dale said. "It was a lock. And we're the key."

He pointed to the smoke.

"Let's go unlock it."
