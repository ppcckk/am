Chapter 41

The truck fishtailed onto the gravel shoulder. Dale killed the engine before it fully stopped. His clipboard was already open, pen moving.

CLASS III. SECONDARY INFESTATION. ORIGIN: MEMBRANE RUPTURE.

He read the coordinates. A drainage culvert under Route 9, two miles east of the membrane site. Kovac was at the membrane. Kovac was Level IV and watching the lattice. That meant this was Dale's zone. That meant this was his problem.

Except it wasn't.

He'd delegated. Filed the paperwork. Handed operational authority to Kovac for the entire eastern perimeter. The delegation protocol was clean, documented, signed. He'd done it right.

And now the Authority was asking him to treat an infestation inside a zone he no longer controlled.

He stepped out of the truck. The culvert mouth gaped at him like a wound in the embankment. Concrete cracked and weeping. The smell hit first. Ammonia and something sweeter, like rotting fruit left in a sealed container. The air shimmered faintly near the culvert opening, a heat-haze effect that had nothing to do with temperature.

His clipboard buzzed.

TREATMENT AUTHORITY: REVOKED. ZONE DELEGATED TO: KOVAC, M. (LEVEL IV). DIRECT TREATMENT PROTOCOLS UNAVAILABLE.

He stared at the notification. Read it again. The words didn't change.

He could call Kovac. Wait for Kovac to drive two miles from the membrane site. Watch the membrane drop below fourteen percent while a Class III infestation spread through a drainage system that fed into three residential basements.

Or he could find a way to treat this without violating the delegation protocol.

He walked to the culvert mouth. Crouched. Shined his flashlight into the dark.

The tunnel system went back. Not a single hole. A network. Branching galleries, each one the diameter of a large pipe, walls coated in a pale waxy substance. The waxy coating was translucent. Under it, something moved. Slow. Deliberate. Like watching a time-lapse of mold spreading across bread.

He pulled out his Field Guide. Flipped to the index. Scrolled through entries he'd accumulated over the last six weeks. Nothing matched. The waxy coating, the branching galleries, the movement pattern. Nothing in his guide.

He photographed the tunnel walls. Photographed the waxy substance. Photographed the entry point where the concrete met the natural soil. Then he pulled out his phone and took a wider shot of the culvert system, the embankment, the road above.

He sat on the tailgate of his truck and thought.

Branching galleries. Waxy lining. Colony structure with a network, not a nest. That wasn't rat behavior. That wasn't beetle behavior. That wasn't anything he'd seen from the membrane before.

But he'd seen it before. Not from the membrane. From a house in Mayfield, three years ago. A crawlspace inspection. Subterranean termites. The galleries. The waxy mud tubes. The branching network that followed load paths.

He opened his Field Guide. Started a new entry.

SPECIES: UNIDENTIFIED. BEHAVIORAL ANALOGUE: Reticulitermes flavescens (subterranean termite). TUNNEL STRUCTURE: Load-bearing nodes at branch junctions. Primary galleries radiate from central node. Waxy secretion consistent with structural reinforcement. HYPOTHESIZED WEAKNESS: Destruction of primary node collapses colony infrastructure. Secondary nodes may sustain partial function.

He stared at the entry. It was a hypothesis. Not an identification. The Field Guide required confirmed identification for the targeted damage bonus. But the hypothesis was there. It was documented. It was professional.

His clipboard buzzed.

FIELD GUIDE ENTRY: PENDING CONFIRMATION. HYPOTHESIS NOTED. TARGETED DAMAGE BONUS: UNAVAILABLE UNTIL CONFIRMED.

He didn't care about the bonus yet. He cared about the protocol.

He pulled out his clipboard and started writing. Not a treatment plan. A referral.

In pest control, a referral was a standard document. You found a problem that wasn't yours to fix. You documented it, identified the responsible party, and filed the transfer. A plumbing leak causing a cockroach infestation got referred to the plumber. A structural crack letting in mice got referred to the contractor.

The membrane rupture was a structural entry point. The infestation was secondary to that entry point. The entry point was in Kovac's zone. The infestation had migrated into Dale's zone.

This was a cross-zone referral.

He wrote it fast. Standard format. Referring party: Kowalski, D. (Level III). Receiving party: Kovac, M. (Level IV). Nature of referral: Secondary infestation originating from structural compromise (membrane rupture) within receiving party's delegated zone. Requested action: Source elimination. Secondary elimination authorized under Cross-Zone Referral Protocol, Section 4.2.

He signed it. Filed it.

The clipboard flashed.

CROSS-ZONE REFERRAL: ACCEPTED. TREATMENT AUTHORITY: GRANTED (SECONDARY ELIMINATION ONLY). NOTE: SOURCE ELIMINATION REMAINS RESPONSIBILITY OF RECEIVING PARTY. NOTE: THIS REFERRAL WILL BE REVIEWED BY EXAMINER.

He stood up. Pulled his gear from the truck bed. The spray rig. The containment kit. The marking flags. The documentation folder.

He treated the culvert system like a termite job. Because it was a termite job. Same logic. Same structure. Same approach.

He found the primary node first. It sat at the junction of three main galleries, a swollen knot in the waxy lining roughly the size of a basketball. Inside it, something pulsed. Slow. Rhythmic. The colony's heart.

He didn't spray it directly. That would be sloppy. That would trigger a defensive response from the secondary nodes, scattering the colony and making full elimination impossible.

He drilled. A six-inch hole into the node, angled to hit the central cavity. He injected a slow-acting agent. Not a kill agent. A disruption agent. Something that would interfere with the waxy secretion process. The colony would lose its structural integrity from within.

Then he moved to the secondary nodes. Found them. Drilled. Injected. Documented each one with a photo and a flag.

He worked for forty minutes. The waxy substance resisted his drill bit at first, then gave way. The pulsing inside the nodes slowed as the disruption agent spread. The movement in the tunnels stopped.

He waited. Twenty minutes. The waxy lining began to dry. Crack. Flakes of it drifted down like dead skin.

He went back in. Checked each node. Confirmed collapse. Photographed the dead colony structure. Pulled samples of the waxy substance for his Field Guide entry.

The entry updated.

SPECIES CONFIRMED: Vesica perforans (tentative). WEAKNESS VERIFIED: Primary node destruction causes total colony collapse. TARGETED DAMAGE BONUS: ACTIVE.

His clipboard buzzed.

JOB COMPLETE. THOROUGHNESS RATING: 9.4 (UP FROM 9.2). TREATMENT POINTS: 340. NOTE: CROSS-ZONE REFERRAL FILED. REVIEW PENDING.

Dale packed his gear. Wiped down the drill. Bagged the samples. Cleaned the site. He was still cleaning when his clipboard buzzed again.

NOTIFICATION: EXAMINER REVIEW COMPLETE. FINDING: CROSS-ZONE REFERRAL ACCEPTED AS VALID PROTOCOL USE. NOTE: EXAMINER COMMENDS APPLICATION OF STRUCTURAL ANALOGUE METHODOLOGY.

He exhaled. That could have gone badly. The Authority might have seen the referral as a loophole. A workaround. A sign that his delegation was poorly structured. Instead, the examiner had seen it for what it was. A professional recognizing the correct protocol for the situation.

He was loading the last of his gear into the truck when the clipboard buzzed one final time.

NOTIFICATION: NEW VIOLATION DETECTED. VIOLATION TYPE: TREATMENT-INDUCED SECONDARY BREACH (TISB). CLASSIFICATION: CRITICAL. DESCRIPTION: RESIDUAL TREATMENT AGENTS APPLIED TO PRIMARY SITE (CHAPTER 14) HAVE MIGRATED THROUGH SUBSTRATE AND DEGRADED MEMBRANE INTEGRITY AT SECONDARY SITE. CAUSE: OVER-APPLICATION OF DISRUPTION PROTOCOL. EXCESSIVE THOROUGHNESS. LIABILITY: PRIMARY LICENSING HOLDER.

Dale read it. Read it again.

The membrane hadn't ruptured on its own. His treatment at the original site, the thorough, careful, nine-point-two-rated treatment he'd done at the duplex, had left residue. Residue that seeped through the ground and weakened the membrane at Kovac's site. His thoroughness had caused the breach.

His clipboard buzzed.

CERTIFICATION STATUS: UNDER REVIEW. TREATMENT POINTS: PENDING RECALCULATION. NOTE: EXAMINER REQUESTS IN-PERSON REVIEW. LOCATION: AUTHORITY OFFICE, COLUMBUS. TIME: IMMEDIATE.

Dale sat on his tailgate. His hands were steady. His face was not doing anything his hands were not doing.

He looked at the dead culvert system. The dried waxy flakes. The collapse. A job done right. A job done thoroughly. A job that had, apparently, torn a hole in the membrane between dimensions.

He started the truck. Pulled out onto Route 9. Drove west toward Columbus.

His clipboard buzzed.

NOTE: EXAMINER WILL NOT BE REDACTED. NOTE: EXAMINER WILL BE PRESENT. NOTE: YOU WILL NOT BE ALONE IN THE ROOM.

He didn't know what that meant. He drove anyway.
