Chapter 253: Total Loss

# Chapter 253

The Enforcer's optical sensors flared crimson, a mechanical pulse of judgment scanning the gap between Dale and the screaming boy. To the construct, the scene was a binary error. A licensed technician was violating containment protocol by engaging in unverified physical contact with a Class-3 biological anomaly. The logic gates within its chassis processed this deviation with cold, unforgiving precision.

*Target: Dale Kowalski. Status: Non-Compliant. Action: Neutralize threat to local stability.*

The Enforcer's armature locked. The plasma caster hummed, drawing power from the ambient ley lines crackling in the air.

It did not care about the boy, which did not care about the hand. It cared only for the integrity of the Extermination Protocol.

Dale's fingers were white-knuckled around the boy's wrist.

The pale thing in the manhole had tightened its grip. The boy's skin was stretching, bruising purple under the strain.

Dale ignored the burning in his shoulders.

He ignored the warning notifications flashing in his peripheral vision, a cascade of red text that read *VIOLATION: PHYSICAL CONTACT UNAUTHORIZED*.

He looked up at the Enforcer. He did not beg. He did not plead. He pointed a shaking finger at the manhole cover, which lay shattered on the asphalt.

"Read the site," Dale said. His voice was flat, stripped of panic.

"Look at the frass pattern.

Look at the entry vector, which is not a spawn event. That is a retrieval.

"

The Enforcer paused. Its head tilted, a servos whirring sound cutting through the wet, heavy air. The crimson light in its eyes dimmed slightly, shifting to a cautious amber.

"Analysis incomplete," the construct droned. "Biometric signature of subject matches known predatory mimicry profiles. Elimination recommended."

"Wrong profile," Dale said. He gritted his teeth as the boy let out a wet, gurgling cough. "The mimicry is on the boy.

The predator is the anchor. You fire, you miss the anchor.

You hit the boy.

You contaminate the witness. The case goes cold. The Authority docks your rating.

"

The Enforcer hesitated. Its programming prioritized efficiency over empathy, but it prioritized *accuracy* over efficiency. A failed elimination was a waste of energy. A contaminated witness was a waste of data.

Dale used the pause, and he didn't pull up. He couldn't.

The thing in the hole was anchored, likely hooked into the bedrock or a subterranean root system.

Instead, Dale changed tactics. He reached into his utility belt, not for his spray, but for his marker. Thick, industrial-grade permanent ink.

He grabbed the boy's other wrist. Not to pull. To mark.

"Hold still," Dale commanded, his tone shifting to the bored irritation of a supervisor correcting a rookie's paperwork.

The boy looked at him, eyes wide, whites showing all around the iris.

"What are you doing?" the boy screamed.

"Dampening the signal," Dale muttered. He uncapped the marker and slashed a thick, black line across the boy's forearm, right over the pulse point.

Then another.

And another.

Cross-hatching the skin.

The Enforcer's sensors zoomed in.

"Unregistered glyph application detected."

"It's a dampener field," Dale lied. He didn't have the tech. He didn't have the magic.

But he had the Authority's interface, and he had a brain that treated magic like a code violation.

"You want to know if he's a mimic?

Check his heart rate variability.

Mimics sync with the host's fear response. Humans don't have that kind of cardiac irregularity under duress without pre-existing conditions.

"

He tapped the boy's chest.

"Check."

The Enforcer scanned. The boy's heart was racing, erratic, chaotic. Human chaos. Not the synchronized, hive-mind rhythm of a Vermin brood.

"Subject: Non-Viable Host," the Enforcer stated. "Threat reassessment: External Anchor."

The amber light vanished. The crimson returned, but this time it locked onto the manhole. The plasma caster charged again, a high-pitched whine that made Dale's teeth ache.

"Clear the line of fire," Dale said.

He didn't let go of the boy. He shifted his weight, bracing his boots against the edge of the asphalt.

"I'm not dropping him.

If I drop him, he falls into the anchor. If you shoot, you have to thread the needle.

"

"Probability of success: 12%," the Enforcer noted.

"Probability of a lawsuit from the Authority: 100% if you kill the witness," Dale countered. "Do the math."

The Enforcer's internal processors churned. The logic was sound.

Sloppy work yielded lower Thoroughness Scores.

Killing the witness meant the infestation's origin remained unknown.

Unknown origins meant unverified kills meant lost points.

"Stand by," the Enforcer said. It recalibrated its aim, focusing on the pale hand protruding from the dark.

Dale watched the construct's movements. He watched the way the joints adjusted, the way the energy coils spun up. He knew this machine.

He had fought three of them in the last month. They were bureaucratic nightmares with guns.

They were slow to react, but precise, and he needed to speed them up.

Dale reached into his pocket and pulled out a vial of concentrated pheromone lure. It was meant for Class-1 roaches, but he had modified the mix. He had added a drop of his own blood.

Not because he was brave.

Because he was thorough, and he poured it directly onto the boy's neck.

The Enforcer jerked back.

"Bio-hazard detected."

"It's a bait marker," Dale said, sweating now, the physical strain of holding the boy becoming a tremor in his arms. "The anchor responds to biological proximity. By marking the host with a high-scent lure, I am forcing the anchor to expose itself further. It will reach for the source."

The pale hand in the hole twitched. The fingers, long and jointed like a spider's legs, stretched upward. They were seeking the scent. Seeking the warmth.

"Targeting lock acquired," the Enforcer announced. "Preparing to discharge."

"Now!" Dale shouted.

He didn't pull. He shoved.

Dale threw his weight backward, wrenching the boy away from the center of the hole. It wasn't a rescue. It was a displacement. A tactical repositioning.

The pale hand lunged, missing the boy's ankle by inches and grasping only air.

The Enforcer fired.

The beam of blue-white energy was silent, a distortion in the air that burned Dale's retinas. It struck the pale hand. The flesh exploded, not with blood, but with a black, viscous smoke that smelled of ozone and old copper.

The boy screamed, falling back onto the asphalt. Dale caught him, rolling them both away from the heat wave.

The hand was gone. The darkness below remained, churning.

Dale lay on his back, gasping for air. His clipboard was on the ground, cracked down the middle. He stared at the sky, or what passed for it in the Rift zone—a bruised purple swirl of clouds that looked like a hematoma.

The System notification popped up in his vision.

**TREATMENT POINTS AWARDED: 45.** **SPECIES IDENTIFIED: DIMENSIONAL ANCHOR (UNCLASSIFIED).** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 8.5 (MINUS 1.5 FOR UNAUTHORIZED BIO-HAZARD DUMPING).**

Dale closed his eyes. 8.5. Not good enough. Not for the level he needed to reach. Not for the things that were coming.

He sat up, groaning as his muscles screamed. The boy was shaking, curled in a fetal position, the ink marks on his arm already beginning to fade, the System's dampening effect kicking in.

The Enforcer stood over them, smoke rising from its caster.

"Infestation contained," the construct said. "Proceed to documentation."

Dale wiped his nose. Blood. He had bitten his tongue during the shove. He tasted the iron, sharp and metallic.

"Documentation," Dale repeated. He reached for his broken clipboard. He couldn't write. The carbon paper was shredded.

He looked at the boy. The boy was staring at the manhole. Not with fear. With recognition.

"He knows where it comes from," Dale said to the Enforcer.

"Resident is a witness," the Enforcer agreed. "Interrogate for source data."

Dale crawled toward the boy. He didn't ask if the boy was okay. He asked for data.

"Where did it come from?" Dale asked.

His voice was calm, professional. He pulled a pen from his pocket. It still worked.

He held it out to the boy.

"Write it down.

Or tell me.

I can't file the report without a location.

"

The boy looked at the pen. Then he looked at Dale.

"You wrote the report," the boy whispered. "You wrote it before you got here."

Dale froze. The pen hovered over the cracked clipboard.

"What?"

"The man in the hole," the boy said. "He said you wrote the report. He said you've already done this."

Dale's stomach dropped. He looked at the System notification again.

**DIMENSIONAL ANCHOR (UNCLASSIFIED).**

He had never seen this species before. His Field Guide was empty on it. He had identified it by context, by the behavior, by the frass. He had never *seen* it.

Unless.

Unless he had seen it in a dream. A nightmare he had dismissed as stress-induced insomnia.

The boy grabbed Dale's wrist. His grip was surprisingly strong.

"It said you're on probation," the boy said. "It said if you fail this job, they revoke your license. And if they revoke your license..."

The boy pointed at the manhole.

"...you go in there."

Dale pulled his hand away. He looked at the dark hole.

The black smoke was settling. But something else was moving in the shadows. Shapes.

Many shapes.

The Enforcer raised its weapon again.

"Multiple hostiles detected. Preparing to engage."

"Wait," Dale said.

He stood up. His legs felt like lead. He picked up the broken clipboard.

He looked at the boy.

"Stay here," Dale said. "Don't move. Don't speak. If you speak, you contaminate your own testimony."

The boy nodded, tears streaming down his face, mixing with the black ink on his skin.

Dale turned to the manhole. He didn't have a weapon. He didn't have a protocol for *Unclassified Anchors*. He didn't have the Thoroughness Rating to justify a deep dive.

But he had the Authority's attention. And he had a new violation.

**SYSTEM ALERT: TEMPORAL ANOMALY DETECTED IN FIELD GUIDE ENTRY.**

Dale blinked. He opened his Field Guide mentally. The entry for *Rattus profundus*—the first thing he had ever killed, back in Chapter 1—was flashing.

It wasn't flashing with an error. It was flashing with an update.

*Addendum: Subject shares genetic markers with Dimensional Anchor. Link confirmed.*

The rats in the duplex. The first job.

They weren't just rats, and they were scouts. Probes. Test cases.

And Dale had passed.

He had passed too well. He had documented them.

He had isolated them, and he had eliminated them.

He had verified the area.

The Authority hadn't just rewarded him. It had *used* him. It had used his obsessive need for order to map the Rift's edge. And now, the Rift was using him back.

The boy's voice echoed in his mind. *The report is already written.*

Dale looked at the pen in his hand. He looked at the blank space on the broken clipboard.

He didn't write a report. He wrote a name.

He wrote *KOWALSKI*.

And below it, he wrote *SUBJECT 1*.

**SYSTEM ALERT: SELF-IDENTIFICATION CONFIRMED.** **STATUS: INFESTATION.**

The ground shook. The manhole widened. The pale hand was gone, but the voice remained.

"Welcome to the nest, Dale," the voice said. It was his voice. But older.

Tired. Broken.

Dale took a breath. He capped the pen. He straightened his tie.

"Protocol check," Dale said to the air. "Am I allowed to exterminate myself?"

The Enforcer turned to him.

"Query invalid. Technician is licensed. Licensure precludes self-elimination."

Dale smiled. It was a thin, sharp thing.

"Then I have a loophole."

He stepped toward the hole.

"Level II Technician, initiating Protocol Omega: Full Site Decommission. Including Personnel."

The Enforcer didn't stop him. It couldn't. The rules were clear. If the technician declared the site a total loss and requested decommissioning, the Authority would collapse the zone to prevent spread.

It would kill the monster.

It would kill the boy.

It would kill Dale.

But it would be thorough.

Dale jumped.
