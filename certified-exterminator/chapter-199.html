Chapter 199: Editing Denied

# Chapter 199

The slime stopped climbing. It didn't recede. It simply halted, the viscous mass frozen mid-surge, a single drop of translucent goo suspended an inch from Yura's nose.

Time had stopped.

Yura didn't move. Her lungs burned, but the air in her chest was static.

She couldn't inhale.

She couldn't exhale. She could only look. The shadow-Vane stood rigid, its cracked face fixed in a permanent, jagged scream.

The holographic guides were gone, but the System text lingered in her vision, glowing with a cold, bureaucratic blue.

*ADMINISTRATIVE HOLD ACTIVE.* *TIME DILATION: 10:00 REMAINING.* *STATUS: PENDING REVIEW.*

Yura forced her eyes to focus on the floor. The pen was still in her hand, stabbed deep into the core of the sludge.

The silver tip vibrated, not with energy, but with the hum of paused physics.

She pulled it out. The sound was wrong. A wet *shluck* that arrived a full second after the motion.

She had ten minutes. The Authority had given her a pause button, but the cost was listed in red text at the bottom of the window: *CLASS-IX ENTITY APPROACHING.*

What was a Class-IX? Dale had mentioned levels, but he'd treated them like parking permits.

Level One was rats.

Level Five was something that ate cars.

Level Nine was the thing that ate the concept of cars.

Yura looked at the frozen shadow. It wasn't just a monster. It was a violation.

The Authority's notice had been clear: *UNAUTHORIZED INTERVENTION.* She had acted outside her certification. She had used a protocol she wasn't licensed for.

She needed to document the violation before the clock ran out. If she could prove she was acting under duress, or if she could reclassify the entity, maybe the penalty wouldn't be total eradication. Maybe it would be just... probation.

Her hands shook.

The pen felt heavy. She dipped it into the slime pool again. The liquid didn't flow.

It held its shape.

She wrote on the side of the frozen sludge mound.

*FORM 14-B: INCIDENT REPORT.* *SUBJECT: VANE, THOMAS.* *STATUS: ANOMALOUS.*

The System flickered. A new window popped up, blocking her view.

*ERROR: FORM 14-B NOT AVAILABLE AT CERTIFICATION LEVEL II.* *REQUIRED LEVEL: III.*

Yura gritted her teeth.

She couldn't use the standard paperwork. She had to use what she had. What did she have?

A pen. A clipboard, actually, though it was currently embedded in the wall behind her.

And the Field Guide.

The Guide was open in her mind.

It listed everything she had ever seen. Every bug.

Every rat. Every minor dimensional tear. But it didn't have a category for *Shadow Vane*.

It didn't have a category for *Time Freeze*.

She flipped through the mental index. *Rattus profundus.* *Acid Beetles.* *Broodmother.*

None of them fit.

But there was a loophole. Dale had taught her that, even if he'd done it by accident. *Identification precedes elimination.* If you couldn't kill it, you had to define it.

She looked at the frozen shadow-Vane. It was a construct. It was made of resin and shadow and the Authority's own code. It was a glitch.

She wrote in the air, using the frozen slime as a backdrop.

*CLASSIFICATION: ADMINISTRATIVE ARTIFACT.* *TYPE: PROTOCOL FAILURE.* *ACTION: QUARANTINE.*

The System paused. The text hovered, waiting.

*CLASSIFICATION ACCEPTED.* *NEW FIELD GUIDE ENTRY CREATED.* *ENTRY: ADMINISTRATIVE ARTIFACT (CLASS-IX PRECURSOR).* *WEAKNESS: UNSTABLE CODE STRUCTURE.* *DAMAGE BONUS: +40% AGAINST SYSTEM GLITCHES.*

Yura blinked. She had just defined the thing that was trying to kill her. And by defining it, she had made it vulnerable.

But the clock was ticking. Seven minutes left.

The lights in the sub-basement were dead, but the System text provided its own illumination.

It cast long, sharp shadows against the walls. The resin on the floor was still. The dripping had stopped.

The silence was absolute.

Then, a sound.

Not a roar. Not a scream.

A click.

Like a pen clicking.

*Click.*

Yura turned. The shadow-Vane was still frozen. The click came from behind her.

She spun around, her boots sliding on the slick floor.

There was nothing there. Just the dark. And the text.

*CLASS-IX ENTITY DETECTED.* *PROXIMITY: CLOSE.*

The air in front of her warped. It didn't bend like heat haze. It folded. Like a page being creased.

A figure stepped out of the fold. It was tall.

It wore a suit.

The suit was gray, perfectly tailored, and devoid of any texture. It looked like a photograph of a suit. The face was a smooth, white oval, identical to the shadow-Vane's, but smoother.

Perfect.

It held a clipboard.

The figure didn't speak. It raised a pen. A black pen.

*VIOLATION LOGGED.*

Yura scrambled back.

Her heel caught on a ridge of dried resin.

She fell hard, the air leaving her lungs in a painful wheeze. The figure advanced.

It didn't walk. It shifted. One frame it was ten feet away.

The next, it was five.

*PROTOCOL: ERADICATION.*

The figure raised the pen. The tip pointed at Yura's heart.

Yura looked at her own pen.

The silver tip was dull. Useless. She couldn't fight a Class-9 with a ballpoint.

She needed something else.

She looked at the Field Guide. *Administrative Artifact.* *Unstable code structure.*

What did that mean?

The figure paused. It tilted its head. The white oval face didn't change, but the angle suggested curiosity.

*QUERY: SOURCE OF CLASSIFICATION?*

Yura's mind raced. It was asking how she knew. How she had identified a glitch before it had even fully manifested.

She had to answer. If she didn't, it would complete the eradication protocol.

"The form," she said. Her voice was raspy. "I filed a Form 14-B. Incident Report."

The figure lowered the pen slightly.

*FORM 14-B IS INVALID.*

"I used a workaround," Yura said. "I classified it as an Artifact. I used the Quarantine protocol."

The figure stared at her. The white face reflected the blue light of the System text.

*UNAUTHORIZED WORKAROUND DETECTED.* *PENALTY: INCREASED.*

The air grew colder. Frost formed on the slime. The frozen shadow-Vane cracked, further apart. The fissure widened.

Yura realized she had made a mistake. She hadn't just identified the glitch.

She had *filed* it.

She had put it in the system. And the system didn't like being told what to do by an unlicensed user.

She had to fix the paperwork.

She had to update the entry.

*UPDATE: CLASSIFICATION.* *CHANGE: ADMINISTRATIVE ARTIFACT.* *TO: UNLICENSED TECHNICIAN ERROR.*

The System window flashed.

*ERROR: CANNOT RECLASSIFY SELF-REFERENTIAL ENTITY.*

Yura cursed. She was trying to use the system to fix a problem she had caused within the system. It was circular. It was a paradox.

The figure moved closer. Three feet away. It raised the pen again.

*FINAL NOTICE: ERADICATION IMMINENT.*

Yura looked at the clipboard in the figure's hand. It was blank. White. Empty.

She had a clipboard. It was buried in the wall. But she had the data.

She had the Field Guide.

She had the new entry: *Administrative Artifact.*

What was the weakness? *Unstable code structure.*

How do you stabilize code? You debug it. You rewrite it.

Yura couldn't rewrite the Authority. But she could rewrite her own entry.

She focused on the Field Guide. She focused on *her* entry. *Yura Kowalski. Certification Level II.*

She added a note.

*NOTE: OPERATOR UNDER DURESS. CONTEXTUAL OVERRIDE REQUIRED.*

It was a desperate, procedural lie. But it was the only procedural language she had.

The figure stopped. The pen hovered.

*CONTEXTUAL OVERRIDE?*

"Yes," Yura said. She pointed at the frozen shadow-Vane.

"That thing.

It's a glitch.

I fixed it. I documented it.

I'm not violating protocol. I'm performing maintenance."

The figure looked at the shadow-Vane. Then at Yura. Then at its own clipboard.

The blank page rippled. Text appeared.

*MAINTENANCE PROTOCOL: NOT FOUND IN LICENSE.*

Yura's heart hammered against her ribs. It wasn't enough. Maintenance was a Class-3 protocol. She was Level 2.

She needed a higher level. She needed Level 3.

But she didn't have it.

Unless.

The loophole.

Dale had said it. *Cross-contamination.* Deploying a protocol designed for one creature type against another inflicts a 40% debuff. But it also contaminates the Field Guide.

Contamination was a penalty. But it was also a state.

If she contaminated her own guide, she could claim she was under *emergency directive*.

She looked at the *Rattus profundus* entry. The giant rat.

She couldn't use it. It was useless here.

But she could *cite* it.

She wrote in the air.

*REFERENCE: FIELD GUIDE ENTRY 004.* *PROTOCOL: TERTIARY CONTAINMENT.* *APPLICATION: ADMINISTRATIVE ARTIFACT.* *STATUS: OFF-LABEL USE.*

The System window exploded with red warnings.

*WARNING: CROSS-CONTAMINATION PENALTY: -40% DAMAGE EFFICIENCY.* *WARNING: FIELD GUIDE CORRUPTION: 24 HOURS.* *CONFIRM?*

Yura didn't hesitate.

*CONFIRM.*

The red warnings faded. The blue text remained.

The figure lowered its pen.

*OFF-LABEL USE NOTED.* *REVIEWING.*

Time was still frozen. Five minutes left.

The figure stood still. It was processing. The clipboard filled with text, scrolling too fast to read.

Yura used the time to breathe. To check her body.

Her legs were numb.

Her chest hurt. But she was alive.

She looked at the shadow-Vane. The crack in its face was wider now. Through the crack, she saw not darkness, but code. Streams of green and black.

She looked at the figure. It was waiting.

And then, the figure spoke. Not in text. In sound.

"Provisional approval granted."

The voice was flat. Monotone.

"However. The penalty remains."

The figure raised the pen again. But this time, it didn't point at Yura. It pointed at the ceiling.

"Correction applied."

The crack in the shadow-Vane sealed.

The fissure closed. The face smoothed over.

The monster was gone.

Not dead. Just... corrected.

The figure turned to Yura.

"Your Field Guide is corrupted."

"I know," Yura said.

"You will be unable to identify vermin for twenty-four hours."

"I know."

"You have incurred a Class-IX alert."

"I know."

The figure stepped back. The fold in the air opened again.

"Report to the nearest Authority Hub for recalibration. Immediate."

It stepped into the fold.

The fold closed.

Time resumed.

The slime surged. The lights flickered back on, buzzing angrily. The dripping resumed.

Yura sat in the pool of goo, gasping. Her head pounded. The System text in her vision was gone.

She looked at her pen. It was broken. The silver tip had snapped off.

She looked at her hand. It was trembling. Not from fear. From adrenaline.

She stood up. Her legs wobbled. She leaned against the wall, pulling her clipboard free from the plaster. The paper was shredded.

She walked toward the exit. The sub-basement was quiet.

She opened the door.

The hallway was empty.

But on the floor, just outside the door, was a new window.

*NOTIFICATION: CERTIFICATION LEVEL III UNLOCKED.* *NEW PROTOCOL AVAILABLE: ADMINISTRATIVE OVERRIDE.* *WARNING: USE OF ADMINISTRATIVE OVERRIDE TRIGGERS AUTHORITY AUDIT.* *WARNING: YOU ARE NOW UNDER SURVEILLANCE.*

Yura stared at it. She had leveled up. She had survived.

But she had just used a protocol that didn't exist. And she had done it in front of a Class-9 entity.

She looked down the hallway. At the far end, near the elevator, a figure stood.

It was Dale.

He wasn't looking at her. He was looking at his clipboard. His face was pale. He was tapping his pen against the board, rhythmically.

*Tap. Tap. Tap.*

He turned. He saw her.

He didn't smile. He didn't look relieved.

He looked terrified.

"Yura," he said. "Don't move."

"Why?"

"Because," Dale said, pointing at her chest. "You have a violation code on your forehead. And it's blinking."

Yura looked in the reflection of the elevator doors.

On her skin, glowing in faint blue, was a symbol. A circle with a line through it.

*PROHIBITED ACT.*

"It's not just a code," Dale said. "It's a beacon."

"Beacon for what?"

Dale swallowed. He looked at the ceiling. Then back at her.

"For the things that enforce the rules."

The elevator dinged. The doors opened.

Inside was not the lobby.

Inside was a room filled with files. Endless rows of files. And sitting at a desk in the center of the room was a person with no face.

Dale stepped forward. He reached out and grabbed Yura's arm.

"We're not going down," he said. "We're going up. And you're going to have to explain why you broke the universe's paperwork."

Yura pulled her arm away. She looked at the faceless figure in the elevator. It raised a pen.

"I didn't break it," Yura said. "I just edited it."

The faceless figure tilted its head.

*EDITING DENIED.*

The elevator doors closed.

Yura lunged.
