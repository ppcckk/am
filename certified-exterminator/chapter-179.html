Chapter 179: Audit Complete

# Chapter 179

The cursor blinked on the terminal screen, a rhythmic pulse against the black void of the interface. Dale's fingers hovered over the keyboard, trembling slightly. Not from fear, but from the specific, low-frequency vibration of a decision that weighed more than gravity.

"You're stalling," the older Dale said. His voice was dry, like paper tearing. "The audit clock doesn't stop for hesitation. It stops for completion."

On the massive monitor, the red dots on the Ohio map had coalesced into a jagged, pulsing line. It looked like a fault line drawn in blood. The Authority's system overlay flickered, attempting to categorize the anomaly.

*ANOMALY CLASSIFICATION: PENDING.* *ERROR: NO PROTOCOL FOUND.* *ERROR: NO SPECIES IDENTIFIED.* *ERROR: REALITY INTEGRITY 42%.*

Dale stepped away from the chair. He didn't look at his older self. He looked at the clipboard that had materialized in his hand, a glitch in the rendering of the server room. The paper was blank, except for a single pre-filled header in bold, sans-serif font.

**INCIDENT REPORT: UNAUTHORIZED NARRATIVE EDIT.** **REPORTING OFFICER:** KOWALSKI, DALE. **STATUS:** CRITICAL.

He clicked the pen. It made no sound.

"I don't edit," Dale said. "I exterminate."

"Exterminate me, then." The older Dale spread his arms wide. The lab coat was stained with ink that shimmered with impossible colors.

"Delete the author.

Remove the variable. The story ends, and the infestation stops. That is the only protocol that works when the system itself is the host."

Dale looked down at the clipboard. A new line appeared on the form, auto-generated by the Authority's backend.

**PROPOSED ACTION: TERMINATE NARRATIVE THREAD.** **REQUIRED AUTHORIZATION: LEVEL IX CERTIFICATION.** **CURRENT LEVEL: VIII (PENDING AUDIT).**

A red box flashed around the authorization field. *ACCESS DENIED.*

"That's the trap," Dale muttered.

He tapped the screen with a finger. The glass felt warm, alive.

"You think I need Level IX to fix this.

But Level IX is just a higher tier of management. It doesn't change the nature of the pest."

"The pest is the plot," the older Dale snapped. He walked toward the server racks, his footsteps making no echo.

"Look at the code.

Look at the threads.

They're tangled. We have to cut them."

Dale turned to the nearest server tower. He didn't look at the lights. He looked at the cabling.

Most people saw wires. Dale saw vectors.

He saw entry points. He saw nesting material. He saw a frass trail of corrupted data packets leaking from the base of the tower into the floor vents.

*Identify.*

He pulled out his Field Guide. The holographic interface snapped open in the air beside him. He swiped past the entries for *Rattus profundus*, *Giant Acid Beetle*, and *Void Weaver*.

He needed a new entry. He needed to classify the enemy before he could target it.

*Document.*

He typed. Not commands. Notes.

*SUBJECT: META-PLOT INTERFERENCE.* *CLASS: NARRATIVE PARASITE.* *SYMMPTOMS: UNSTABLE REALITY, CHARACTER REPETITION, FORCED CONFLICT.* *WEAKNESS: PROCEDURAL RIGIDITY.*

The older Dale stopped. He frowned, tilting his head. "What are you doing?"

"Fill out the form," Dale said, not looking up. "You can't deploy a treatment protocol against an unidentified species. It's a Class A violation. Cross-contamination penalty applies."

"There is no protocol for this!" The older Dale's voice rose, cracking with static. "I am the protocol! I am the result of a thousand failed exterminations!"

Dale kept typing.

His movements were precise, mechanical. The anxiety that usually paralyzed him was gone, replaced by the cold, hard clarity of compliance. He had done this before.

He had dealt with tenants who refused to vacate.

He had dealt with landlords who ignored health codes. He had dealt with the bureaucratic inertia of the Authority itself.

This was just another code violation.

*Isolate.*

He walked to the center of the room, to the chair. He didn't sit in it. He stood behind it. He placed his hands on the backrest.

"If I accept your premise," Dale said, "then the Authority is broken. But the Authority isn't broken. It's just... misaligned."

He looked at the screen.

The red line was moving. It was approaching the edge of the map. If it crossed the border, it would spill into the neighboring states.

Into Indiana. Into Pennsylvania.

The infestation would become regional.

Then national.

Then impossible to contain.

"You're trying to reboot the server," Dale said. "That wipes the data. But the data is the evidence.

And without evidence, there is no certification. No points.

No progress."

"The progress is meaningless if the world ends!"

"Progress is everything," Dale said flatly. "Without progress, we're just bugs."

He pressed a sequence of keys. Not *Enter*. Not *Delete*.

*Ctrl+Shift+P.*

The Authority's system froze. The red dots stopped pulsing. The hum of the servers dropped to a sub-bass groan.

A new window popped up. It was small, white, and blindingly bright.

**SYSTEM QUERY: DEFINE TREATMENT SCOPE.** **OPTION A: GLOBAL PURGE (REBOOT).** **OPTION B: LOCALIZED CONTAINMENT (PATCH).**

The older Dale laughed. It was a harsh, barking sound. "You choose B?

You think a patch will hold?

The Rift is tearing the fabric of spacetime. You can't patch a hurricane with duct tape."

"I can," Dale said. "If you seal the entry points."

He pointed to the vents in the floor.

The corrupted data was leaking out of them. The server room wasn't the source. It was the nest.

The old Dale was the broodmother, laying eggs of bad narrative structure into the core code.

"You're not the result," Dale said, turning to face him. "You're the queen."

The older Dale's eyes widened. For the first time, the mask of authority slipped. He looked confused. He looked like a man who had forgotten the rules of his own game.

"Me?"

"Look at your hands," Dale said.

The older Dale looked down. His scarred hands were dissolving. Ink was leaking from his pores, dripping onto the metal floor. The ink sizzled, burning holes in the server room's anti-static tiles.

"I... I have authority," the older Dale whispered.

"You have tenure," Dale corrected. "That's not the same thing."

He tapped the clipboard again. The form had auto-filled.

**TREATMENT PROTOCOL: CONTAINMENT FIELD (CLASS IV).** **TARGET: META-NARRATIVE QUEEN.** **METHOD: ADMINISTRATIVE LOCKDOWN.** **EFFECT: FREEZE ALL EDITING CAPABILITIES FOR 72 HOURS.**

The older Dale scrambled backward, knocking over a rack of hard drives.

They clattered to the floor, sparking.

"You can't freeze me! I own the license!

I wrote the exam!"

"You failed the final," Dale said. "You stopped documenting."

He pressed *Execute*.

A wall of white light erupted from the terminal. It didn't attack the older Dale.

It enclosed him.

A perfect, transparent cube, humming with low-grade reality stabilizers. The older Dale slammed against the walls, but the force was gentle. It was administrative.

It was a cease-and-desist order made of pure law.

The red line on the map stopped. It didn't disappear, but it stopped moving. It became a static, jagged scar.

**THREAT NEUTRALIZED.** **TREATMENT POINTS AWARDED: 5,000.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 9.8.**

Dale exhaled. His shoulders dropped. The tension in his neck eased, just a fraction.

He walked over to the containment cube. The older Dale was still there, pounding on the wall, his mouth open in a silent scream. He was frozen mid-shout, his face contorted in a mix of rage and terror.

Dale stared at him. He felt nothing.

No pity.

No triumph. Just the satisfaction of a job well done.

He pulled a marker from his pocket and wrote on the side of the cube.

*DO NOT DISTURB.* *PENDING REVIEW.* *AUTHORIZED BY: D. KOWALSKI.*

Then he turned back to the main screen. The map was still damaged. The red line was still there. The infestation wasn't gone; it was just quarantined.

A new notification flashed.

**SYSTEM ALERT: SECONDARY INFESTATION DETECTED.** **LOCATION: 400 MILES NORTH.** **CLASS: UNKNOWN.** **STATUS: ACTIVE.**

Dale frowned. He hadn't finished the job. The primary breach was contained, but the secondary breach was spreading. The older Dale hadn't been the only queen.

He looked at the exit door. It was sealed. The reboot hadn't happened, but the server room was still locked down.

He checked his certification level.

**CURRENT LEVEL: VIII.** **NEXT EXAM: LEVEL IX (PRACTICAL).** **REQUIREMENT: SOLVE A CLASS-V INFESTATION WITHOUT AUTHORITY INTERVENTION.**

Dale looked at the frozen older Dale. Then at the red line on the map.

He picked up the briefcase. He picked up the clipboard.

He walked to the door. It didn't open.

He pulled out a can of industrial solvent from his belt. He sprayed the lock mechanism. He waited three seconds. He kicked the door.

It didn't budge.

He kicked it again. Harder.

The door remained closed. The Authority was watching. It was waiting for him to ask for permission.

Dale stared at the keypad. He entered his ID.

*ACCESS DENIED. CLEARANCE INSUFFICIENT.*

He cursed under his breath. He had contained the narrative parasite, but he had trapped himself in the nest.

On the screen, the red line pulsed again. Faster.

The older Dale in the cube smiled. It was a small, tight smile. He pointed a finger at the door, then at Dale.

Dale realized his mistake.

The protocol hadn't just contained the queen. It had contained the technician.

He was now part of the system. And the system was hungry.

The lights in the server room went out. The hum died. The only sound was the ticking of the cooling fans, winding down into silence.

And in the dark, a new voice spoke. Not from the cube. Not from the screen.

From inside Dale's own head.

*"Audit complete. Subject is ready for integration."*

Dale gripped the pen so hard it snapped. Ink bled into his palm, black and permanent.
