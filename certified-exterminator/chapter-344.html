Chapter 344: Signed in Bone

# Chapter 344

The ink had a smell. Not the metallic tang of blood or the sulfurous rot of the Rift, but the sharp, chemical bite of industrial solvent. It was the scent of a fresh printout.

Dale slid across the polished surface of the desk, his boots squeaking against a material that resembled marble but felt like compressed ash. The paper tunnel collapsed behind him with a sound like a book slamming shut. Dust motes danced in the stagnant air, suspended in beams of light that fell from a ceiling he couldn't see.

Ahead sat a desk. It was vast, sprawling, and covered in mountains of paperwork. Some sheets were yellowed with age, others blazed with neon red warnings. A single stamp sat in the center, heavy and iron-black.

A figure sat behind the desk. It wore a suit that shifted like oil on water. Its face was a blank slate, smooth and featureless, save for two ink stains where eyes should be.

Dale didn't slow down. He reached into his utility belt, bypassing the spray can and the containment bag.

He pulled out his clipboard, and the plastic was cracked, stained with old grime, but the clipboard itself was a Class-IV Artifact. It was the only thing he had that the Authority recognized as official.

He slammed the clipboard onto the desk.

"Form 77-B," Dale said. "Complaint of unauthorized dimensional alteration. Subsection: Reality Integrity Violations."

The figure didn't move. The ink stains on its face seemed to widen.

"Processing," the figure said. Its voice was the sound of paper tearing.

Dale checked his progression bar. The ink crawling up his chest had reached his collarbone. **PROGRESSION: 1.4%.** The rate was accelerating. The more time passed without a filed grievance, the more his existence was being rewritten into the system's data stream.

"You have no jurisdiction here," the figure stated.

"I am a licensed technician," Dale replied.

He flipped through his Field Guide. The pages were thick, leather-bound things that smelled of ozone and decay.

He found the entry for *Reality Warp Entities*, and the text was blurry, resisting focus.

He needed a weakness. He needed to identify the target before he could eliminate the threat.

"Field Guide update," Dale muttered. He pulled a stylus from his belt. It wasn't a pen; it was a Focus Tool, capable of rewriting local narrative constants if the user had sufficient clearance.

He wrote in the margin of the entry: *Vulnerable to bureaucratic paradox.*

The system flashed green. **[ENTRY UPDATED: REALITY WARP ENTITY. WEAKNESS IDENTIFIED: PROCEDURAL INCOMPATIBILITY.]**

Dale felt a surge of clarity. The ink on his chest slowed.

His breathing steadied, and he had identified the target.

Now came the isolation.

He pointed at the mountain of papers.

"Isolate."

The papers shifted. They formed a wall between Dale and the entity, creating a narrow corridor. The entity didn't react to the barrier. It simply leaned forward, its oily suit rippling.

"Your complaint is invalid," the entity said. "You are already a file. You are data. Data does not file complaints."

Dale stepped over the paper wall. He didn't run. He walked.

He checked his watch. The hands were spinning backward.

"Then I'm filing a correction," Dale said.

He pulled out a red stamp from his belt. It wasn't standard issue. It was a relic he'd won in the Level III exams, a tool that allowed a technician to reject a system process if it violated the Core Protocols.

He slammed the stamp onto the desk.

**[REJECTED: PROCESSING OF SUBJECT DALE KOWALSKI.]**

The entity's head snapped toward the stamp. The ink stains turned into voids, swirling black holes.

"You lack clearance," the entity hissed.

"I have Level III clearance," Dale said. "And I am invoking the Right to Documentation."

This was a loophole. A tiny, obscure clause in the Authority's bylaws that most technicians ignored because it required stopping combat to write a report. But the Authority rewarded thoroughness. By stopping to document the violation, Dale was forcing the system to acknowledge the crime before it could complete the erasure.

The air around them froze. The dust motes stopped dancing.

The entity raised a hand. The fingers lengthened, turning into quills. They slashed at Dale.

Dale didn't dodge. He held up his clipboard.

**[DOCUMENTING: ASSAULT ON LICENSING AGENT.]**

The quills stopped an inch from his face. The system had recorded the attack. The rules required the attacker to pause until the record was complete.

Dale pulled out a form. He filled it out. His hands moved with the practiced speed of a man who had written thousands of inspection reports.

*Date: Today.* *Location: Non-Euclidean Office 4.* *Subject: Reality Warp Entity.* *Violation: Attempted identity theft via narrative overwrite.* *Evidence: Ink on chest. Missing memory of Vane.*

He signed it. He stamped it.

The entity screamed. It was a sound of static, of servers crashing.

"Stop," the entity shrieked. "You cannot file against a God."

"I'm not filing against a God," Dale said. "I'm filing against a contractor."

The entity froze.

"Explain," the entity whispered.

"You're not the Authority," Dale said. "You're a sub-routine. A middle-management daemon left behind when the original Architects vanished. You're unauthorized."

The entity's suit flickered. For a second, Dale saw something underneath. Not skin, but code. Raw, screaming code.

"If you are a sub-routine," Dale continued, "you are subject to performance reviews."

He flipped to a new page. He drafted an audit report.

This was dangerous.

The Cross-contamination penalty was always looming. If he used a Protocol designed for physical monsters against a metaphysical entity, he would debuff himself. But an Audit wasn't a combat Protocol.

It was a Structural Adjustment, and he wrote: *Subject fails to meet the standard for Autonomous Agency. Recommendation: Termination and Re-integration.*

The entity lunged. This time, it didn't stop. The rules didn't apply to its own nature. It was breaking its own programming.

Dale dodged, sliding across the desk. The quill hand carved a groove into the marble surface.

He needed a workspace. He needed a standard environment.

Dale pulled a canister from his belt. It wasn't spray. It was a *Reality Anchor*. It looked like a heavy iron weight attached to a chain.

He slammed it onto the floor.

**[ANCHOR DEPLOYED: LOCAL REALITY STABILIZED.]**

The room shuddered. The paper walls fell away.

The shifting suit of the entity slowed, becoming rigid, and the entity was no longer fluid. It was bound by the laws of physics.

It was now vulnerable.

Dale grabbed his Focus Tool. He didn't attack the entity. He attacked the paper.

He wrote on the desk itself.

*Correction: The entity is not a contractor. It is a legacy system. It must be deprecated.*

The words glowed. The entity tried to move, but its limbs locked. The ink on Dale's chest stopped spreading. It receded, flowing back into the paper, forming new letters.

**[PROCESSING: DEPRECATION REQUEST.]**

The entity looked at its own hands. They were cracking. White light leaked from the fissures.

"You... you are killing me," the entity said.

"I am auditing you," Dale corrected.

The entity slumped. It didn't fall. It dissolved into a pile of loose-leaf paper. The black ink drained out of the suit, pooling on the desk.

Dale stood there, breathing hard. His heart hammered against his ribs.

He looked at the ink, which was pooling into a shape.

A face.

Vane's face.

No. Not Vane.

Dale's own face.

The ink formed a mirror image of Dale, staring up from the desk.

**[ERROR: IDENTITY CONFLICT DETECTED.]**

Dale stared. The system had misidentified the target. In trying to audit the sub-routine, he had flagged himself as a duplicate.

The room went silent.

A new notification appeared in his peripheral vision. It was red. Not the usual orange of warnings. Red.

**[CRITICAL ALERT: DUPLICATE TECHNICIAN DETECTED. PROTOCOL 9-ALPHA INITIATED.]**

**9-ALPHA: ELIMINATION OF REDUNDANCY.**

The floor beneath Dale turned to quicksand. Not literal sand, but a swirling vortex of shredded documents. He grabbed the edge of the desk.

The duplicate face on the desk spoke.

"Hello, Dale."

It wasn't his voice. It was Vane's voice.

"Who are you?" Dale asked.

"I am the report you didn't file," the face said. "I am the mistake in your records. And I am going to correct you."

The desk shook. The papers around Dale rose into the air, forming a cage.

Dale looked at his Field Guide. The entry for *Reality Warp Entity* was blank. The cross-contamination penalty had kicked in. He had used a Structural Adjustment on a metaphysical threat, and now his ability to understand the threat was gone for twenty-four hours.

He was blind.

The cage of paper tightened. Dale kicked at the air. He couldn't see where the walls were. He couldn't see the exit.

He reached for his clipboard. It was empty. The pages had been sucked into the vortex.

He was defenseless.

The face on the desk smiled. It was a cruel, bureaucratic smile.

"Your Thoroughness Rating is dropping," the face said. "You left the nest untreated. You left the source unverified."

Dale closed his eyes. He thought about the duplex. The kitchen infestation. The frass trail.

He remembered the smell of the solvent, and he opened his eyes.

"Show me," Dale whispered.

The paper cage parted. Not because he commanded it, but because he identified the flaw.

He looked at the face on the desk. He looked at the ink.

"It's not a duplicate," Dale said. "It's a draft."

He pulled out his Focus Tool. He didn't write a correction. He wrote a *revision*.

*Draft 1: Dale Kowalski. Status: Erased.* *Draft 2: Dale Kowalski. Status: Active.*

He signed it.

The ink on the desk screamed. The face dissolved into black sludge.

The paper cage collapsed.

Dale fell onto the marble floor. He gasped for air. The ink on his chest was gone.

But his Field Guide was still blank.

And on the desk, where the entity had been, a new file appeared. It was thick. Bound in black leather.

Dale crawled toward it. His hands shook.

He opened the first page.

It wasn't a report, which was a list.

**SUBJECTS PENDING AUDIT:**

1. **VANE, ARTHUR.** *Charge: Memory Embezzlement.* 2. **THE RIFT SOURCE.** *Charge: Unlicensed Expansion.* 3. **DALE KOWALSKI.** *Charge: Unauthorized Existence.*

Dale stared at the third line.

He wasn't the auditor, and he was the next item on the docket.

A door opened in the wall. Not a paper door. A wooden door, plain and brown, like the one in his apartment in Ohio.

A woman stood in the doorway. She wore a uniform. Not the Authority's grey, but the bright yellow of a Sanitation Crew.

She looked at Dale. She looked at the file.

"You're late," she said. "The supervisor is waiting."

Dale looked at the file. Then he looked at the woman.

"Who is the supervisor?"

She smiled. It was not a friendly smile.

"You'll find out when you sign the receipt," she said.

She held out a pen. It was made of bone.

Dale took it. He knew he should refuse. He knew that signing meant accepting jurisdiction.

But he also knew that if he didn't sign, the next line on the list wouldn't be an audit.

It would be an extermination, and he signed.

The floor dropped out from under him.
