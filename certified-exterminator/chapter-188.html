Chapter 188: Verified Contamination

# Chapter 188

The foreman of the night crew at the Apex Canery did not believe in ghosts. He believed in shift differentials, union breaks, and the specific, rhythmic hum of the conveyor belts that processed three thousand pounds of herring every hour. But when the metal gate to the loading dock buckled inward, bowing like wet cardboard under an invisible fist, he decided that physics had finally quit.

He stood with his hard hat tilted back, sweat pooling in the creases of his neck, watching the breach. It wasn't just the gate. The concrete floor rippled. A substance, thick and violet, seeped from the hairline cracks in the foundation, smelling of copper and rotting kelp.

"Call it in," the foreman shouted to the safety officer. "Call it in to the city. Tell them we got a sinkhole."

"It's not a sinkhole," the safety officer whispered, his face draining of color as he pointed to the ceiling.

Stalactites of chitin hung from the industrial rafters, dripping a viscous, black sludge that hissed when it hit the stainless steel vats below. The herring in the bins had stopped moving. They weren't dead. They were vibrating.

The foreman looked for the exit sign, but the lights flickered and died, replaced by the pulsing, bioluminescent glow of the infestation spreading across the walls.

*Technician Kowalski.*

The name echoed in his mind, unbidden. He'd seen the news.

The guy who cleaned up the subterranean rat problem in Dayton with a clipboard and a canister of industrial solvent.

A pest control tech. A bureaucrat with a spray nozzle.

The foreman grabbed his walkie-talkie, his fingers trembling against the plastic casing.

"Dispatch? We have a Code Red structural failure at Apex Canery.

We need emergency services.

We need... whatever Kowalski uses."

He didn't know if the Authority would answer. He didn't know if the man even existed. But as the first of the chitinous stalactites detached and dropped toward the processing line with a wet, heavy thud, the foreman knew that no amount of overtime pay could save this shift.

Dale Kowalski stepped out of his truck before the engine had fully cooled.

He did not run. Running elevated the heart rate, which increased oxygen consumption, which in a confined, low-ventilation environment accelerated the inhalation of airborne particulates.

Dale checked his respirator seal.

The rubber flanged against his cheekbone.

He adjusted the filter cartridge, twisting it until it clicked. The sound was dry, mechanical, and reassuring.

The canery loomed against the night sky, a brutalist block of corrugated steel and brick. Purple veins crawled up its exterior, pulsing in time with the tear in the sky. The air here tasted different.

Heavier. It carried the scent of ammonia, old fish guts, and something sharper, like ozone mixed with formaldehyde.

Dale pulled his clipboard from the cab. The paper was already damp from the humidity. He flipped to the new page, labeled *Apex Canery – Initial Assessment*.

He clicked his pen.

*Location: Loading Dock, North Side.* *Observation: Structural compromise. Biological mass adhering to exterior.* *Status: Active infestation.*

He walked toward the main entrance.

The heavy steel doors were jammed open. Inside, the emergency lights cast long, jagged shadows across the wet floor. A puddle of violet sludge reflected the flickering bulbs.

Dale noted the consistency.

Viscous. High protein content.

Likely excreted by a Class-4 or higher hive entity.

His Field Guide entry for *Dimensional Vermin* had updated automatically after the sewer incident, but this wasn't a rat. It wasn't a beetle. The texture of the slime suggested something more complex. Something that built things.

A man in a high-visibility vest emerged from the shadows near the forklift. He held a tire iron, his knuckles white.

"You're the tech," the foreman said. It wasn't a question. His voice was flat, stripped of hope.

Dale nodded, not looking up from his clipboard. "Name's Dale. I'm here to assess the contamination level."

"Contamination?" The foreman laughed, a harsh, barking sound. "Look at the walls, Dale. Look at the ceiling. We're being eaten alive."

"I see the chitin deposits," Dale said, pointing his pen at the ceiling. "High density. You have a breeding colony. Or a construction site."

"A construction site?"

"Pest control isn't just about killing," Dale said, tapping the page. "It's about understanding the architecture. If they're building, they have a goal. If they have a goal, they have a pattern."

Dale moved past the foreman, stepping into the main processing floor. The smell was overwhelming. Rotting fish, yes, but beneath it, a sweet, cloying scent that triggered a primal disgust in Dale's gut.

He suppressed it. Disgust was not data.

He approached the central processing line.

The conveyor belts were stalled.

The herring bins were overflowing, the fish twitching in unison. Dale crouched, examining the sludge pooling around the base of a vat.

He dipped a gloved finger into it, then held it up to the light.

"Protein-rich," he muttered. "Calcium carbonate. And... heavy metals."

He pulled a small vial from his belt and scraped a sample into it. He capped it. The Authority's interface blinked in his peripheral vision, but he ignored it. He needed the visual confirmation first.

*Field Guide Entry Updated: Unknown Species.* *Hypothesis: Architectural Chitin.* *Weakness: Unknown.*

"Hey," the foreman called out, hanging back by the door. "There's something in the back.

Near the autoclave.

It's big. It's moving the machinery."

Dale stood up. "Show me."

They walked deeper into the facility.

The temperature dropped. Frost coated the metal pipes, despite the industrial heat exchangers still humming in the distance.

The violet sludge was thicker here, forming intricate lattices along the support beams.

It looked like coral, or perhaps neural tissue.

In the center of the room, near the massive cylindrical autoclaves used to cook the fish, a mound of the substance had coalesced. It was roughly the size of a compact car, pulsing with a slow, rhythmic beat. Tendrils of chitin extended from it, plugging into the pipes, the electrical conduits, and the structural supports.

"It's taking the building," Dale said, his voice quiet. "It's not infesting it. It's integrating it."

He opened his notebook to the section on *Structural Parasites*. There was no entry for this yet. He had to improvise.

"If I hit it with the standard acid spray, I might damage the pipes," Dale said, more to himself than to the foreman. "And if I use the sonic disruptor, I might destabilize the foundation. The building is already compromised."

"So what do we do?" the foreman asked. "We can't just watch it grow."

Dale looked at the autoclave. The door was ajar. Steam hissed from the seam.

"The autoclave," Dale said. "It's a pressure chamber.

Sealed environment.

High heat.

High moisture."

He walked toward it. The tendrils recoiled as he approached, sensing the vibration of his boots. Dale didn't flinch. He reached into his kit and pulled out a canister labeled *Thermal Shock Agent (Industrial Grade)*.

"This is for frozen pipes," the foreman said. "It won't kill a monster."

"No," Dale agreed. "But it will change the local environment. Protocol Delta-9 requires a controlled thermal inversion to disrupt chitin bonds without releasing spores."

He wasn't sure if Protocol Delta-9 existed. He was making it up. He was applying the logic of plumbing repair to a biological horror.

Dale positioned himself between the mound and the autoclave. He unsealed the thermal canister. The cold air hissed out, creating a visible mist.

"Stay back," Dale said.

He walked to the autoclave door. He didn't open it. Instead, he placed the canister against the metal skin of the chamber and triggered the nozzle. The liquid nitrogen-based agent flooded the seam, freezing the gasket, the hinges, and the surrounding metal.

The temperature in the room plummeted. Frost spread across the floor in a rapid, crystalline web.

The mound shuddered. The tendrils stiffened. The chitin, brittle in the extreme cold, cracked.

*System Notification: Environmental Modification Detected.* *Target Vulnerability Revealed: Thermal Shock Sensitivity.* *Field Guide Entry Unlocked: Architectural Chitin (Sub-species: Ferro-Resistant).* *Damage Bonus Applied: +15% against Structural Targets.*

Dale didn't celebrate. He pulled a different tool from his belt. Not a weapon. A pry bar.

He walked to the cracked section of the mound where it connected to the main electrical conduit. He inserted the bar into the fissure.

The Authority's interface flashed red.

*Warning: Direct Engagement with Class-5 Entity Risk.* *Contamination Risk: High.*

Dale ignored it. He needed to isolate the power source. If he could cut the connection between the entity and the city's grid, he could starve it.

He twisted the pry bar. The chitin shattered, releasing a cloud of black dust. Dale held his breath, relying on the respirator.

He saw the core beneath the shell. It wasn't flesh.

It was a tangled mass of copper wire, fused with bone-like structures.

The entity had eaten the wiring. It was using the building's own nervous system to feed.

"There," Dale whispered.

He pulled the secondary battery pack from his truck. It was a heavy, lead-acid unit used for powering his spray pumps. He dragged it over to the exposed core.

"You're going to shock it?" the foreman asked, horrified.

"I'm going to short-circuit it," Dale corrected.

He placed the positive terminal of the battery against the copper mass. He grabbed the negative lead and hooked it to a grounded pipe.

The reaction was instantaneous. A bright arc of blue electricity jumped between the terminals. The mound screamed, a sound like grinding metal. The tendrils whipped back, lashing out at Dale.

He ducked, rolling behind the autoclave. A tendril slammed into the metal door, denting it inward.

*System Notification: Treatment Protocol Initiated.* *Status: In Progress.* *Estimated Completion: 4 minutes.*

Dale checked his watch. He had four minutes before the entity stabilized and rebuilt its connections. He needed to verify the kill. Verification required documentation.

He crawled out from behind the autoclave, his knees scraping against the frost-covered floor. He pulled out his phone. The camera lens was fogged. He wiped it on his sleeve.

He took a photo of the short-circuited core. Then another. He photographed the shattered chitin. He photographed the battery connection.

*Documentation Complete.* *Elimination Protocol: Pending.*

The mound was convulsing.

The black dust settled. The pulsing slowed.

But it wasn't dead.

It was retreating. Pulling its tendrils back into the floor, down into the sewer lines below.

"It's escaping," the foreman yelled. "It's going down!"

Dale looked at the floor. The grate beneath the autoclave was vibrating. The entity was draining itself, flowing into the municipal infrastructure.

Dale stood up. He looked at the foreman.

"You said the plant was already breached," Dale said.

"Who said that?"

"The radio. Before I got here."

Dale walked to the loading dock. The truck was waiting. But he didn't get in. He looked at the dark, swirling hole in the floor where the entity had disappeared.

If it went to the treatment plant, it would use the main pump station as a hub. From there, it could infect the entire city's water supply.

The Authority's voice had said *Central Processing Hub*.

Not the canery.

The *Hub*.

The canery was just a feeder.

Dale looked at his clipboard. He crossed out *Apex Canery*. He wrote *Central Processing Hub* in bold letters.

He got back in the truck.

"Where are you going?" the foreman shouted from the doorway. "You haven't finished! It's not dead!"

Dale rolled down the window.

"It's not dead," Dale said, his voice devoid of emotion. "But I have the location. And I have the guide."

He put the truck in gear.

The rearview mirror showed the purple tear in the sky widening. The sirens were closer now. But they weren't coming for the canery. They were coming for the Hub.

Dale drove. He didn't look back at the foreman's terrified face. He looked at the map on his dashboard.

The Hub was twelve miles away. Traffic was nonexistent.

The roads were empty.

Because everyone else was already dead.

He checked his Field Guide. The entry for *Architectural Chitin* listed one weakness: *Disconnection from Power Source.*

But the Hub had the city's largest backup generators.

Dale's hand tightened on the steering wheel. He had no plans for nuclear reactors. He had no plans for industrial-scale power grids.

He only had a clipboard.

And a feeling that he was already too late.

The radio crackled.

*"Technician Kowalski. Your certification is suspended. Do not approach the Hub.

This is a Level 9 Incident.

You are not qualified."*

Dale turned off the radio.

He drove faster.

He wasn't qualified. But he was the only one who knew where the nest was. And if he didn't treat it, the city would be consumed by its own infrastructure.

He reached the first intersection. The traffic lights were green.

He didn't stop.

He drove through the red.

And then, he saw it.

Not the Hub.

Himself.

In the rearview mirror, reflected in the glass, Dale saw a figure standing in the back of his truck cab. It was wearing his uniform. It was holding his clipboard.

But it had no face. Only a smooth, chitinous plate where his features should have been.

Dale slammed on the brakes. The truck skidded, tires screaming against the asphalt.

He turned around.

The back seat was empty.

But the clipboard on the dashboard was bleeding.

Black ink. Or blood.

And it spelled out a single word.

*VERIFIED.*

The Authority hadn't sent a warning.

It had sent a confirmation.

Dale looked at his own hand. His skin was turning gray. Hardening.

The contamination hadn't stopped at the canery.

It had started with him.
