Chapter 99: Inherited Auditor Protocols

# Chapter 99

The truck’s engine died with a shudder that vibrated through Dale’s molars. Miller didn’t start it again.

He just sat there, hands white-knuckled on the steering wheel, staring at the windshield.

Outside, the sky was the color of a bruised plum, torn open by jagged seams of violet light. The air smelled of ozone and rotting gardenias.

Dale looked at his phone. The text message from the unknown number glowed in the darkness of the cab. *Your father was one of us.*

He didn’t delete it, and he couldn’t. The Authority had upgraded his interface.

The notification wasn’t just a text; it was a persistent UI element, anchored to his peripheral vision like a smudge on a lens.

A new tab had appeared in his mental HUD, labeled **AUDITOR PROTOCOLS**, and it pulsed with a faint, sickly gold hue.

Miller turned to him. His face was gray, sweat beading on his upper lip. “You’re not moving.”

“I’m assessing,” Dale said. His voice came out steady, though his stomach churned with a nausea that had nothing to do with motion sickness.

He forced his hands to stop shaking, and he pulled the clipboard from his jacket.

It was empty, but the act of holding it grounded him. It was a physical anchor to his profession.

“Assess what?” Miller hissed. “We’re in the kill zone. The containment line is five miles back. We’re in the breach.”

“We’re in the service area,” Dale corrected. He opened the clipboard. The pen clicked. “And we have a code violation.”

Miller stared at him. “Are you insane?”

“Maybe. But look at the perimeter.”

Dale pointed to the tree line. Beyond the immediate chaos of fleeing civilians, the ground had been churned into a slurry of mud and black ichor. But it wasn’t random.

The churning followed a geometric pattern. Perfect circles, spaced exactly twelve feet apart.

“Those aren’t footprints,” Dale said. “Those are injection points. Someone laid down a bait station grid. Class IV density.”

Miller blinked. “What?”

“A Class IV infestation requires systemic application, not brute force. If you shoot those things now, you’ll just scatter the spores. They’ll colonize the water table by morning. The whole county dies.”

Miller looked at the approaching horde. Creatures the size of horses, with too many joints and eyes that reflected light like cat eyes, skittered out of the rift. They were fast. They were hunting.

“So what do we do?” Miller asked, his voice cracking.

Dale looked at the **AUDITOR PROTOCOLS** tab, and he didn’t know how to use it. He didn’t know if using it would trigger the “corruption” he’d just erased. But he had access.

And right now, access was the only weapon he had.

He closed his eyes. He reached for the mental interface. It felt like reaching into a pocket that wasn’t there.

*Command: View Active Infestations.*

The world changed.

A grid overlay snapped into place over his vision. Red lines crisscrossed the trees. Yellow warning icons popped up over every creature. Text scrolled rapidly.

**THREAT DETECTED: VOID-STAGGER (SPECIES: CERATOPUS ABYSSALIS)** **STATUS: UNLICENSED** **PROTOCOL REQUIRED: CLASS III CONTAINMENT + CLASS IV ERADICATION** **NOTE: USER LACKS LEVEL IV LICENSE. ATTEMPTING CLASS II SUBSTITUTE PROTOCOL. WARNING: EFFICIENCY REDUCED BY 60%. CROSS-CONTAMINATION RISK: HIGH.**

Dale opened his eyes. He felt a migraine spike behind his left eye. The system was punishing him for his lack of certification. But it had given him the data.

“We need to move,” Dale said. “To the center circle.”

“Why?”

“Because that’s the nest. And I can’t spray it. But I can seal it.”

Miller didn’t argue. He kicked the door open. “Lead.”

They stepped out into the mud. The air was thick, heavy with the scent of sulfur. The creatures were closing in. Dale could hear their chittering, a sound like wet gravel in a blender.

He moved with the purposeful, unhurried gait of a man walking toward a work order. He scanned the ground.

He identified the entry points.

Each circular disturbance in the mud was a node. He needed to block them.

“Give me your belt,” Dale said.

Miller frowned, unclasping his tactical belt. “What are you going to do? Tie them up?”

“Isolate the perimeter,” Dale said. He took the belt. It was leather and nylon, sturdy. Not ideal, but it was a barrier.

He ran to the nearest node. A Void-Stagger lunged at him, its claws tearing through the air.

Dale didn’t flinch, and he dropped to one knee, rolling under the creature’s swipe.

The beast passed over him, its momentum carrying it into the next node.

Dale slammed the belt buckle onto the edge of the muddy circle. He activated his **SEALANT SPRAY**. The canister hissed, releasing a stream of rapidly hardening foam.

It wasn’t combat grade. It was industrial grade, meant for sealing cracks in foundations.

It wasn’t enough to kill a Void-Stagger.

But it was enough to plug the hole.

The foam hardened instantly, creating a physical barrier, and the creature that had passed over him turned back. It shrieked, a sound that vibrated in Dale’s chest. It slammed into the foam barrier.

**SYSTEM ALERT: BARRIER BREACHED. STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY: 40%. TIME TO FAILURE: 10 SECONDS.**

Ten seconds.

Dale looked at the next node. Ten feet away.

“Miller! Flare gun!”

Miller, who had been cowering behind the truck, fumbled for his gear. He pulled out the emergency flare. “What do I do with it?”

“Ignite the foam!” Dale shouted. “Heat weakens the sealant but hardens the outer shell. It’s a thermal shock effect!”

Miller didn’t understand the physics, but he understood the tone. He raised the flare and fired.

The red light hit the foam barrier. The sealant hissed, turning black and brittle, and the outer layer cracked, but the core fused into a dark, glass-like substance.

The Void-Stagger slammed into it again. This time, it bounced off.

**SYSTEM ALERT: BARRIER STABILIZED. TEMPORARY SEAL ESTABLISHED.**

One down. Three to go.

Dale moved to the next node, and he was running out of spray. The canister was half-empty.

He checked his Field Guide.

The entry for **CERATOPUS ABYSSALIS** was populated. He scrolled through the data. He had identified their weakness earlier, in the archives. *Sonic resonance disrupts their equilibrium.*

He didn’t have a sonic emitter. But he had Miller’s truck.

“Miller! Start the engine! Full throttle!”

Miller scrambled back into the cab. “Why?”

“Just do it!”

Dale sprinted to the next node. He sprayed the foam. He stepped back.

Miller roared the engine. The truck’s exhaust pipes belched a cloud of smoke and noise; the sound was deafening, a low-frequency rumble that shook the ground.

The Void-Staggers froze. Their heads twitched. They looked toward the truck.

Dale sprayed the foam again. He activated the **VIBRATION DAMPING** protocol.

It was a low-level trick he’d learned for dealing with termite mounds in hollow walls.

It usually cost him a point of endurance.

Now, it cost him a fraction of his Auditor status.

The foam hardened. The truck’s rumble continued. The creatures were disoriented, stumbling around the perimeter of the sealed circle.

Two nodes sealed. One left.

The largest creature, the Alpha, emerged from the rift. It was massive, easily eight feet tall at the shoulder. Its carapace was a shifting, iridescent black, and it had no eyes, just a row of sensory pits along its neck.

It looked at Dale.

Dale looked back, and he was out of spray. He had no foam. He had no sealant.

He had the clipboard.

He looked at the Alpha. He looked at the final node. The mud was still churning. The infestation was trying to spread.

He couldn’t seal it, and he had to eliminate it.

But he didn’t have a weapon.

He looked at his hands. He looked at the clipboard.

He opened his Field Guide. The entry for **CERATOPUS ABYSSALIS** flashed red.

**WEAKNESS: CORE EXPOSURE VIA DORSAL PLATE. REQUIRES PRECISION STRIKE. UNLICENSED WEAPONRY NOT RECOMMENDED.**

Dale looked at the Alpha. It was charging.

He looked at Miller. Miller was watching, terrified, waiting for an order.

Dale stepped forward, and he didn’t run away. He ran toward the Alpha.

He held the clipboard up.

The Alpha slammed into him.

Dale twisted. The creature’s horn hit the clipboard. The wood splintered. The wire frame bent.

The impact threw Dale backward. He hit the mud. Pain exploded in his shoulder.

But the clip. The wire clip on the clipboard. It had pierced the creature’s sensory pit.

The Alpha screamed. It thrashed, clawing at its neck.

Dale scrambled to his feet. He grabbed the broken wire from the clipboard. It was a jagged, twisted piece of metal.

He lunged, and he drove the wire into the Alpha’s dorsal plate.

It was a pathetic attack. A pest control technician using office supplies against a dimensional horror.

But the system recognized it.

**TARGET IDENTIFIED: CERATOPUS ABYSSALIS (ALPHA)** **ACTION: MANUAL EXTRACTION OF CORE PARASITE** **PROTOCOL: CLASS I (UNLICENSED) - EMERGENCY OVERRIDE** **RESULT: CRITICAL DAMAGE**

The Alpha convulsed. A black, pulsating orb popped out of its neck. The creature collapsed, its legs twitching, then stilling.

Silence fell over the clearing.

The other creatures, confused by the loss of their Alpha, retreated into the rift. The violet light dimmed.

Dale stood there, breathing hard. His shoulder was dislocated. He didn’t care.

He looked at his hand. The wire was covered in black slime.

He wiped it on his pants.

Miller jumped out of the truck. He ran to Dale. “You did it. You actually did it.”

Dale didn’t answer. He was looking at his UI.

A new notification flashed. Gold letters.

**AUDIT COMPLETE.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 1.2/10** **NOTES: CRUDE METHODS. EXCESSIVE PROPERTY DAMAGE. LACK OF DOCUMENTATION. PENALTY APPLIED.**

Dale winced. A penalty. Not enough to fail, but enough to hurt.

But then another message appeared.

**NOTICE FROM THE HIGH COUNCIL:** **USER DALE KOWALSKI (CERTIFICATION LEVEL: AUDITOR / LEVEL 1)** **ACTION: LOCALIZED CONTAINMENT SUCCESSFUL.** **REWARD: ACCESS TO RESTRICTED ARCHIVE SECTOR 4.** **WARNING: YOUR PARENTS ARE STILL REGISTERED AS 'MISSING'. INQUIRY INTO STATUS IS NOW ENABLED.**

Dale froze.

*Parents. Plural.*

He had only one parent. His mother had died of cancer when he was twenty. His father had “died” when he was six.

The message said *parents*.

Miller grabbed his arm. “Dale. We need to go. More are coming.”

Dale looked at the rift. It was closing. But the text was still there.

He typed a command into the mental interface.

**QUERY: PARENTAL STATUS.**

The system hesitated. Then, it responded.

**PARENTAL STATUS: ACTIVE. STATUS: INCARCERATED. LOCATION: SECTOR 4.**

Dale felt the ground drop out from under him, and they weren’t dead.

They were in jail.

And he had just earned the key to their cell.

He looked at Miller. Miller was already in the truck, engine running.

Dale climbed in. He didn’t say a word.

Miller looked at him. “Where to?”

Dale looked at the town. The soldiers were still setting up perimeters. The civilians were being rounded up.

He looked at the rift. It was gone.

He looked at the gold badge in his pocket, and he pulled it out.

It was warm.

Miller saw it. “What is that?”

Dale closed his hand around it.

“We’re going to the Authority’s main branch,” Dale said.

Miller’s face went pale. “Are you joking?”

“No,” Dale said. “I have an appointment.”

He leaned back in the seat. He closed his eyes.

For the first time, he didn’t feel like a technician.

He felt like a suspect.

And he was going to turn himself in.

Because if he didn’t, they would come for him.

And he knew, with the cold certainty of a man who had seen the inside of a crawlspace, that they were already watching.

The truck pulled away.

In his mind, the **AUDITOR PROTOCOLS** tab flickered. A new sub-menu appeared.

**INCOMING TRANSMISSION: ENCRYPTED.**

Dale ignored it, and he knew what it was.

It was his mother’s voice.
