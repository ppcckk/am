Chapter 216: The Baby Is the Nest

# Chapter 216

The fluorescent lights of Mercy General's sub-basement flickered with a rhythmic, dying buzz.

Sodium vapor. Old wiring. A code violation waiting to be written.

Dale Kowalski stood before the heavy steel door of Ward 9, his clipboard held at chest height like a shield. The brass key from the suitcase was cold in his left hand. It fit the lock with a precision that suggested the Authority had been maintaining this door for centuries.

The lock clicked.

The door groaned open.

A smell hit him first. Not rot.

Not blood.

It smelled like ozone and wet copper. It smelled like a server room on fire.

Inside, the room was vast.

Hospital beds lined the walls, but they were empty. The sheets were pulled tight, taut as drum skins.

In the center of the room, suspended from the ceiling by thick, pulsating cables of black organic matter, hung a mass of flesh.

It was roughly the size of a compact car. It twitched. Veins the width of garden hoses pulsed along its surface, glowing with a faint, sickly blue light.

Two figures stood beneath the mass. One was a woman in a tailored grey suit, holding a tablet. The other was a man in scrubs, his hands shaking.

Dale stepped inside. The air pressure dropped. His ears popped.

"Mr. Kowalski." The woman did not look up from her tablet.

"You are late.

By four minutes.

We calculated a margin of error, but you exceeded it."

"The highway was congested," Dale said. His voice was flat, professional. "And I stopped to inspect the intake filters on my van. Cross-contamination risk."

The man in scrubs looked up. His face was pale, sweating. "Is that it? Is he going to kill it?"

The woman raised a hand. "Silence, Dr. Aris.

Mr. Kowalski is here to assess.

Not to assault."

Dale walked toward the hanging mass. He kept his eyes on the ceiling fixtures. Structural integrity first.

Secondary hazard assessment second.

The cables were anchored into the concrete, but the concrete was cracked. Hairline fractures spiderwebbed out from the bolts.

"Identification," Dale said.

The woman tapped her tablet.

"Class IV Dimensional Parasite. Species: *Ostium vorax*. Common name: The Gatekeeper.

It feeds on dimensional resonance. It's trying to widen the Rift in the adjacent room."

"It's not widening," Dale corrected.

He pointed his pen at the ceiling.

"It's stabilizing. Look at the tension in the cables.

It's acting as a load-bearing structure. If you cut it, the ceiling collapses. If you ignore it, the walls breach."

The doctor stepped forward. "We tried to sedate it.

The tranquilizers made it swell.

It started screaming. Not with its mouth. With the walls."

"Because you didn't identify the vector," Dale said. He pulled his pen cap off. He wrote on his clipboard. *Vector: Acoustic resonance. Host: Structural concrete.*

The woman looked at him.

Her eyes were dark, unreadable. "You see the system, Mr.

Kowalski.

Most do not."

"Most people see a monster," Dale said. "I see a maintenance issue."

He walked closer to the mass. The blue light intensified. The veins pulsed faster.

"Stay back," the doctor warned. "It reacts to movement."

"It reacts to *unauthorized* movement," Dale said. He raised his clipboard. The paper glowed with a faint white outline. The Field Guide was active.

*Target: Ostium vorax.* *Status: Dormant/Active Hybrid.* *Weakness: Disruption of harmonic frequency.*

"Dr. Aris," Dale said, not turning around. "What is the ambient noise level in this room?"

"Standard hospital hum," Aris stammered. "Sixty decibels."

"Too low. The parasite is feeding on silence.

It's amplifying the quiet." Dale turned to the woman.

"You need a sound source.

Something constant. Something non-organic."

"The ventilation system?" the woman suggested.

"Insufficient decibel floor. You need white noise. High frequency.

Industrial grade." Dale looked at the doctor. "Do you have a backup generator?"

"Down the hall. Diesel."

"Start it. Run the exhaust into the room. The vibration will disrupt its cohesion."

Aris blinked. "That's... that's insane. The fumes—"

"Carbon monoxide is a chemical hazard.

Dimensional instability is a structural hazard. I am managing the structural hazard. You manage the chemical one.

Ventilation fans on high.

Doors open."

The woman tapped her tablet.

"He has issued a verbal protocol. Acceptable. Dr.

Aris, comply. If the walls shatter, your medical license is irrelevant.

You will be buried."

Aris ran.

Dale watched the hanging mass.

He could see the frass trails. Not insect droppings.

Crystallized silica. The parasite was sweating glass dust. It was shedding the concrete of the building.

"Ms. Vane," Dale said.

The woman's name was on her badge.

"Why was this assigned to me? I am Class II. This is Class IV."

Vane looked at him.

"Because you are thorough. The previous technician was a combatant.

He tried to shoot it.

The bullets turned into snakes. The next technician was a mage. He tried to banish it.

The banishment spell inverted the gravity in the ICU. Forty patients died."

Dale nodded slowly. "Bad documentation."

"Abysmal," Vane agreed. "The Authority requires a Thoroughness Rating of 8.5 or higher for Class IV containment protocols. You scored a 9.2 on your residential review."

"9.4," Dale corrected. "I found the entry point in the crawlspace. The previous inspector missed the gap under the siding."

Vane smiled.

It was a small, sharp thing.

"Precisely. That is why you are here.

Not to fight. To fix."

The door at the far end of the room crashed open. Dr.

Aris returned, dragging a heavy cable from the corridor.

A low, rumbling drone filled the space. The diesel generator roared to life outside.

The hanging mass shuddered. The blue veins flickered.

"Vibration increasing," Aris shouted over the noise. "The cables are loosening!"

"They are not loosening," Dale said.

He stepped back, his eyes scanning the ceiling. "They are detaching.

The parasite is releasing its hold on the structure because the noise is interfering with its sensory input.

It is confused. It is trying to re-anchor."

"How do we stop it from re-anchoring?" Vane asked. She was moving now, positioning herself near the door. Ready to seal the room if things went wrong.

Dale looked at his Field Guide. The entry for *Ostium vorax* was incomplete. He had identified the weakness, but he hadn't documented the secondary vulnerability.

He needed a loophole.

"Cross-contamination," Dale muttered.

"What?" Vane asked.

"The debuff," Dale said. "If I use a protocol designed for a different creature, I get a forty percent penalty. But the debuff applies to *me*. Not the target."

Vane frowned. "That makes no sense. Why would you want a penalty?"

"Because the penalty creates a vacuum," Dale said. He closed his Field Guide.

He opened his toolkit.

He pulled out a canister of aerosol spray.

Standard residential insecticide. Pyrethroid-based.

Useless against dimensional matter.

"Mr. Kowalski," Vane said, her voice tight. "What are you doing?"

"I am declaring this a Class I infestation," Dale said. He sprayed the air. A cloud of chemical fog drifted toward the hanging mass.

The effect was instantaneous.

The parasite shrieked. Not a sound, but a pressure wave that shattered the glass on the observation window. The blue light turned violent red. The cables snapped taut.

"You have triggered a Contamination Alert," Vane shouted. "Your Field Guide entry is compromised! You have lost your targeted damage bonus!"

"I don't need the bonus," Dale said. He walked toward the mass.

The system notification flashed in his peripheral vision.

*CROSS-CONTAMINATION PENALTY ACTIVATED.* *Debuff: -40% Efficiency.* *Field Guide: OOSTIUM VORAX LOCKED (24 HOURS).*

Dale felt the weakness hit him. His steps became heavy. His vision blurred at the edges. The air tasted like ash.

But the parasite was screaming. It was trying to reject the foreign substance.

The pyrethroids were binding to the crystallized silica on its surface.

It wasn't killing it. It was making it *sticky*.

"Dr. Aris!" Dale yelled. "The suction unit!"

Aris was cowering behind a bed. "What?"

"The vacuum system for the laundry chute! Turn it on! Full power!"

"It's not connected to this room!"

"Connect it!" Dale pointed to the floor vent.

The grate was vibrating. "The parasite is shedding silica.

It's clogging the air return.

If you reverse the airflow, you create a pressure differential. The parasite will be pulled toward the vent. It's lighter than the cables."

Vane stared at him. "That is... theoretically possible. But the structural load—"

"Is manageable if you brace the door," Dale said. "If it's pulled down, it hits the floor, not the ceiling.

The ceiling holds.

The floor breaks.

I can fix a floor."

Aris scrambled to the panel. He flipped switches. A roar built up in the walls.

The hanging mass jerked downward.

The cables sang like guitar strings.

"Hold the door!" Vane commanded. She grabbed the handle, bracing her feet against the frame.

Dale watched the mass descend. Inches. Feet.

The blue light was dying, replaced by a dull, angry orange. The silica dust clouded the air, choking him.

He coughed, wiping his mouth with his sleeve.

The mass slammed into the floor.

Concrete cracked.

The room shook.

Silence.

Dale stood there, breathing hard. The debuff was fading, but his head still throbbed. He looked at the creature.

It was flattened.

Stunned. The cables were dangling, useless.

"Is it dead?" Aris whispered.

Dale walked over to the mass. He kicked it.

It didn't move.

"It is immobilized," Dale said. "Not dead. Killing it would release the dimensional energy back into the room. We need to bag it."

He reached into his van, which he had left parked in the loading dock. He pulled out a heavy-duty biohazard bag. It was oversized. Intended for large vermin nests.

"Help me lift this," Dale said to Aris.

Aris hesitated.

"Lift.

It," Dale said. His voice was ice. "Or I will write you up for Obstruction of Licensed Personnel.

And I will make sure it gets sent to the Board."

Aris moved.

Together, they wrestled the heavy, rubbery mass into the bag. It was warm. It pulsed once, then went still.

Dale zipped the bag.

He wrote on the tag. *Specimen 4. Contained. Awaiting incineration.*

He walked back to Vane.

She was staring at the cracked floor. Then she looked at Dale.

"You used a Class I protocol on a Class IV threat," she said. "You violated the protocol hierarchy."

"I exploited a loophole in the interaction rules," Dale corrected.

"The cross-contamination penalty applies to the technician's efficiency.

It does not prevent the physical reaction of the chemical agents. The pyrethroids bonded to the silica.

The vacuum removed the mass. The system penalized me for 'wasting' the action, but the action was effective."

Vane looked at her tablet. The numbers were scrolling.

"Thoroughness Rating: 9.8," she read.

Dale felt a flicker of something in his chest. Not pride. Satisfaction. A job well done.

"You are now eligible for Class V assignments," Vane said.

"I am tired," Dale said. "I want to go home."

"You cannot go home," Vane said.

Dale stopped. He looked at her. "Why?"

"Because you are not the only one here."

The lights in the room died.

Total darkness.

Then, a single red light flashed on Vane's tablet.

"Your Field Guide," Vane said. Her voice was different.

Calmer.

Colder. "It is locked for twenty-four hours. Correct?"

"Yes," Dale said. He couldn't see her, but he could hear the click of her pen.

"And during that lock period, you cannot identify species. Correct?"

"Yes."

"Then how did you know it was an *Ostium vorax* before you engaged?"

Dale froze. The question hit him harder than the debuff.

He hadn't looked at his Field Guide until he was already in the room.

He had assessed the environment first. The cables.

The smell.

The cracks.

He had *inferred* the species.

"I used environmental indicators," Dale said slowly.

"No," Vane said. "You used memory. You have encountered this species before."

Dale's hand tightened on his clipboard. "I have not."

"The Authority has records," Vane said. "Three years ago.

A residential inspection in Dayton, Ohio.

A similar infestation.

You treated it. You documented it.

You did not report it to the Authority."

Dale said nothing.

"You hid it," Vane continued. "You cleaned it up. You sprayed it. You wrote a report about 'structural rot.' You erased the evidence to protect your client from stigma."

The room was silent.

"That is a felony under the Licensing Act," Vane said. "Unreported Infestation. Cover-up. Malpractice."

Dale looked at the door. The handle was locked. Vane's hand was on the keypad.

"I was protecting a family," Dale said. His voice was quiet.

"You were protecting your Thoroughness Rating," Vane corrected. "And now, because you are on probation, that cover-up makes you a liability."

A sound came from the hallway.

Heavy footsteps. Not one person. Many.

"Who is that?" Aris whispered.

Vane did not answer. She looked at Dale.

"The Authority does not tolerate hidden files, Mr. Kowalski. But it does tolerate useful assets."

The door handle turned.

Vane unlocked it.

The door swung open.

Standing in the corridor was a figure in black armor. It was six feet tall. It had no face, just a smooth, black visor. In its hands, it held a rifle that looked like it was made of bone.

Behind it, three more figures.

"Mr. Kowalski," Vane said. "Step aside."

"What is that?" Dale asked. He didn't move.

"That is an Auditor," Vane said. "They do not exterminate. They erase."

The lead Auditor raised its rifle.

It wasn't pointing at Dale.

It was pointing at the bag on the floor. The bag containing the *Ostium vorax*.

"Wait," Dale said. "If you destroy it here, the dimensional residue will—"

"Too late," Vane said.

The Auditor fired.

A beam of white light hit the bag.

The bag exploded.

Not into pieces. Into *nothing*.

The bag, the creature, the silica dust, the smell—it all vanished. The floor where the bag had been was clean. Sterile. Blank.

Dale stared at the empty spot. His Field Guide was still locked. He had no entry for what had just happened.

But he had a memory.

And he had a new problem.

Vane stepped out of the room. The Auditors followed her.

"Come, Mr. Kowalski," Vane said from the hallway. "You have a new assignment."

Dale looked at the empty floor. He looked at Aris, who was shaking violently.

He looked at his clipboard.

He wrote one line.

*Loss of Evidence. Chain of Custody Broken.*

He walked out of Ward 9.

The hallway was lined with black-armored figures.

Vane was waiting for him at the elevator.

"The cover-up," she said. "You did it for a family in Dayton. Who were they, Mr. Kowalski?"

Dale said nothing.

"Because they are still alive," Vane said. "And they are infested again."

The elevator doors opened.

Inside, standing in the corner, was a woman. She was holding a baby carrier.

Dale recognized her.

It was Mrs. Gable. From Chapter 1. The tenant who had screamed about demons.

She looked at Dale. Her eyes were hollow.

"He's back," she whispered.

Dale looked at Vane.

"He didn't leave," Dale said. "I never saw him leave."

"You didn't," Vane said. "Because you didn't check the perimeter. You didn't check the roof. You didn't check the children's bedrooms."

The baby in the carrier cried.

But it wasn't a human cry.

It was a hiss.

A long, wet, chitinous hiss.

Dale looked at the carrier.

The blanket shifted.

Something with too many legs pushed against the fabric.

Vane smiled.

"Your probation starts now, Mr. Kowalski. And you have no Field Guide."

She pointed to the elevator.

"Go home. Or don't. It doesn't matter. You are already compromised."

Dale stood in the hallway.

The baby carrier was vibrating.

He gripped his clipboard.

He turned to Vane.

"Is the baby infected?"

"The baby is the nest," Vane said.

Dale closed his eyes.

He opened them.

He looked at the Auditor.

"I need a new bag," Dale said.

The Auditor tilted its head.

Vane laughed.

It was a dry, rasping sound.

"Get in the elevator, Mr. Kowalski. The ride is long."
