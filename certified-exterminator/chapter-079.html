Chapter 79: Structural Entry Point

# Chapter 79

"Structural entry point," Dale said.

Yuen's finger stopped. Not released.

Stopped.

The nozzle didn't waver. Her eyes stayed fixed on the gap between him and Voss. Six feet of linoleum.

The distance the system had measured, quantified, and converted into a thirty percent damage bonus against both of them.

"Repeat," Yuen said.

"Structural entry point. Not a weakness." Dale's voice came out flat.

He'd said it four hundred times in his career.

He said it now with the tone he used for homeowners dealing with gaps under their door sweeps. Diagnostic. Matter-of-fact.

"The system misclassified. Proximity to partner is a connection vector.

A trail.

It's not a biological vulnerability.

It's a structural gap between two infestation sites."

Yuen didn't lower the nozzle. "The system populated that field as a weakness."

"The system populated it as a weakness because it doesn't know the difference between a trail and a trait. I do." Dale reached for his clipboard. Not the one he'd been holding.

The one on the floor. He'd dropped it when Yuen came in.

He picked it up.

His hands weren't shaking. They hadn't shaken in fourteen years. Not when the rats came.

Not when the broodmother came.

Not when the things from the Rift came. His hands shook when he couldn't find the right form.

He pulled his pen.

"Field Guide amendment," he said. "Entry 47-C. MUTUAL VECTOR. I'm reclassifying the weakness field from biological vulnerability to structural entry point."

The notification came up in his peripheral vision.

He'd learned to read these without looking. The system had a specific font for amendments. Smaller.

Thinner. Like it expected you to ignore them.

AMENDMENT REQUEST: Entry 47-C, weakness field reclassification. Pending Authority review.

"You can't just," Voss started.

"I can." Dale didn't look up.

He was writing, and the amendment form was simple. Current classification: WEAKNESS.

Proposed classification: STRUCTURAL ENTRY POINT. Justification: Connection between two discrete infestation sites constitutes a structural entry point per Protocol 3.1, Isolation of Inter-Colony Vectors. Correction action: Physical separation of connected sites.

Protocol 3.1. The one that said: when two colonies are connected by a shared trail, you don't spray both colonies.

You seal the trail.

You break the connection. The colonies then become isolated and can be treated independently.

He'd used Protocol 3.1 forty-three times.

Ant trails between two nests. Cockroach harborage connected to a drain line.

The logic was identical.

Two infestations, one connection, one structural fix.

The notification changed.

AMENDMENT PENDING. CORRECTION ACTION REQUIRED BEFORE REVIEW COMPLETES.

"You have to seal it," Yuen said. Her voice had shifted.

Not softer.

Flatter.

The voice of someone watching a procedure unfold correctly and recognizing that it was correct. "The system won't accept the reclassification unless you physically correct the entry point."

"Six feet," Dale said. "I need to break it."

"You need to leave." Voss's voice was tight. "The system needs you to leave this room."

Dale looked at Voss. Voss looked at Dale. Six feet.

The number the system had used to calculate damage, and the number that, if it became zero, would mean the entry point was sealed.

The number that, if it became large enough, would mean the same thing.

"Protocol 3.1 doesn't specify distance," Dale said. "It specifies disconnection. The trail has to be broken. A physical barrier between the two sites is sufficient."

"There's no barrier in this room."

"There is." Dale pointed at the doorway. "The doorframe. If I step through it and close the door, that's a physical barrier. The trail is broken."

Voss stared at him. "You're leaving."

"I'm sealing the entry point." Dale stood. His knees popped. He was thirty-eight and his knees popped.

"The amendment needs a correction action.

The correction action is separation. That's it.

That's all it is."

He walked toward the door. One step. Two.

Three. The linoleum squeaked under his boots.

Four.

Five, and the gap was closing from the system's perspective. Or opening.

He wasn't sure which direction the math went.

Six. He was at the door.

"Kowalski." Yuen's voice. Still holding the nozzle.

Still aimed at the space where he'd been standing.

"If you go through that door, your Field Guide entry goes into review. You lose the entry. You lose the weakness field.

You lose the damage bonus."

"I know."

"For twenty-four hours."

"I know."

"You're walking into whatever's outside this house with no weakness data. No damage bonuses. No targeted protocols. Nothing."

Dale put his hand on the doorknob. "I've done jobs with no data before.

It's called an inspection.

You look, you identify, you treat. The data comes after."

He turned the knob.

The notification hit.

AMENDMENT ACCEPTED. ENTRY 47-C RECLASSIFIED: STRUCTURAL ENTRY POINT. CORRECTION ACTION LOGGED. FIELD GUIDE ENTRY 47-C: LOCKED FOR REVIEW (24:00:00).

Twenty-four hours. His entry was gone.

He could feel it.

Not physically, and the system had a specific sensation associated with locked entries; a lightness in the back of his skull.

Like a drawer pulled open and left empty.

He stepped through the doorway. Pulled the door closed. The latch clicked.

He stood in the hallway. The door was a physical barrier. The trail was broken. The entry point was sealed.

Through the door, he heard Yuen lower the nozzle. A small mechanical click. Safety catch re-engaged.

Then silence.

Dale checked his Field Guide. Entry 47-C was greyed out.

The weakness field was blank.

Not pending. Blank. Locked.

He scrolled through his other entries.

Dimensional Vermin. Acid Beetles.

Broodmother variants.

All still active. All still populated. But 47-C, the entry that had described the thing he and Voss were, the thing the system had classified as a mutual vector, was gone.

He looked at his hands. Steady.

His phone buzzed. Voss. A message.

You good?

Dale typed back: Entry sealed. 24-hour lockout.

A pause. Then: I know. I'm locked too.

Dale stared at the screen. Of course.

The amendment reclassified the entry for both of them, and they shared the entry.

They shared the lockout. Voss had no weakness data either.

Not against 47-C. Not against whatever came next that was classified under that entry.

He put the phone away, and the hallway was empty. The house was quiet.

The front door was twenty feet away.

Beyond it, the yard. Beyond the yard, the street. Beyond the street, whatever had been crawling out of the ground for the past six weeks.

Dale walked to the front door. His clipboard was still in his hand. He'd been holding it the whole time. He hadn't noticed.

He opened the front door, and the notification came before he stepped outside.

NEW INFESTATION DETECTED. PERIMETER BREACH.

Dale stopped. Read it again.

PERIMETER BREACH. CLASSIFICATION: STRUCTURAL ENTRY POINT. SOURCE: UNIDENTIFIED.

He looked at the yard, and the lawn was intact. The fence was intact.

The Rift, the thing that had opened over the neighborhood six weeks ago, was still there.

A vertical tear in the air, maybe forty feet from the house; it looked like a seam in a pair of jeans someone had worn too tight. A dark line.

Not wide. Not yet.

But the system was calling it a structural entry point, which was new.

The Rift had always been classified as an environmental hazard.

A background condition. Like weather.

You didn't treat the weather. You treated what the weather brought in.

Now the system was calling the Rift itself a structural entry point, which meant it could be sealed.

That meant someone was supposed to seal it.

And that meant the Authority was looking for a technician to do it.

Dale looked at his Field Guide. Entry 47-C was locked.

His other entries were active.

But none of them described the Rift. None of them described whatever was on the other side of it, and he had no weakness data for the source.

He had no protocol for sealing a dimensional breach, and he had a clipboard. He had a pen. He had a doorknob in his hand.

His phone buzzed again. Not Voss.

Unknown number.

The Authority's direct line. He'd never gotten a call from it. Only notifications.

Only the thin-font amendments and the bold-font violation alerts.

He answered.

"Kowalski." The voice was flat.

Genderless.

The same voice that had read his written exam answers back to him at Certification Level III. "Your amendment was accepted.

Your Thoroughness Rating has been adjusted."

"Adjusted how?"

"Up. 9.4."

Dale said nothing. 9.4 was the highest he'd ever gotten. The Authority's threshold for Class IV threats was 9.0. He'd been above it for weeks.

"You're being reassigned."

Dale's hand tightened on the clipboard. "To what?"

"The Rift."

Silence. The kind of silence that has weight.

"The Authority has identified the Rift as a structural entry point," the voice said. "Protocol 3.1 applies. Isolation of Inter-Colony Vectors. You've used this protocol forty-three times in your career."

"I have."

"The Rift is a trail. It connects two sites. The correction action is the same as it's always been."

"Seal the trail."

"Seal the trail."

Dale looked at the dark line in the air. Forty feet away.

Maybe less, and he could see something on the other side. Not. Not yet.

But something was moving behind it.

Something pressing against the seam. Like a hand pushing against a closed door.

"The Authority is authorizing a Class IV treatment," the voice said. "You will need a partner."

Dale looked back at the house.

Voss was inside. Locked entry. No weakness data.

Twenty-four hours of blindness.

"I don't have a partner," Dale said.

"You do. You just sealed the entry point."

The line went dead.

Dale stood in the doorway.

The Rift pulsed.

Something behind it pushed. The seam widened by a fraction.

A sliver of dark. Then wider. Then wider still.

He looked at his clipboard. The amendment form was still on it. The correction action was logged. The entry was sealed.

He could go back inside. Open the door.

Break the seal.

Re-open the entry point. Re-link with

Voss.

Re-link with the sealed entry point. Standard re-entry protocol.

He'd done it forty-three times.

You don't sever a trail permanently unless the Authority authorizes a full structural closure, and that required a second licensed technician on-site for verification.

He turned back toward the house.

Three steps. The porch boards were wet with something that wasn't rain.

He didn't look down.

Protocol 3.1 didn't require surface analysis during active breach response, and he reached for the doorknob.

The Rift behind him made a sound. Not a tear. Not a rip.

A *click*. Like a latch releasing.

He stopped. Didn't turn around. Protocol 1.2: when an uncontrolled vector changes state, freeze and reassess.

The click came again.

Closer. Then a wet, sliding sound, like something pulling itself through a gap that was too small for it. Something that didn't want to fit.

Something that was *choosing* to fit.

Dale kept his hand on the doorknob. His knuckles were white.

His breathing was steady, and he counted. One, two, three. He counted during the written exam.

He counted as the acid beetles entered through the crawlspace vent in March.

Behind him, the sliding stopped.

The silence that followed was worse.

He turned, and the Rift was open.

Not a seam anymore.

A gap. Two feet wide.

Maybe three. The edges of it were frayed and dark, like the inside of a mouth that had been chewed open from the outside. Something was standing in it.

Not through it. *In* it. Occupying the gap like a body in a doorway.

It was tall.

Too tall. The proportions were wrong, causing Dale's eyes to slide off it just as they had off the Voss entry data. His Field Guide didn't populate.

No entry.

No identification. No weakness data.

It had too many joints in its arms. It had no face. It had a clipboard.

The clipboard was wrong, and the shape was wrong. The paper on it was wrong. But it was *holding* it as Dale held his.

A technician holding a clipboard during active treatment.

The thing tilted its head. The joints in its neck moved in sequence, one after another, like vertebrae being tested for range of motion.

Dale's clipboard hand was still raised. The thing's clipboard hand was still raised.

He looked at the gap. He looked at the thing. He looked at the clipboard in its hands.

The paper on it had his name on it, and he looked down at his own clipboard. The amendment form was gone. The page was blank.

The thing extended its arm toward him. The clipboard extended with it. An offer. A handoff.

Dale's mouth opened.

Closed, and he looked at the house. At the doorknob.

At the gap between his hand and the knob. Three inches.

The thing's hand was closer than that.

It was already reaching.
