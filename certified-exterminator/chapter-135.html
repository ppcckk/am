Chapter 135: Analog Intent

# Chapter 135

The ink on the page was still wet.

Dale stared at the single word he had scrawled in the blue ballpoint. *Threat*.

It wasn't a report. It wasn't a citation.

It was a confession of intent, recorded in a medium the Authority couldn't legally monitor because it predated their digital licensing framework.

Miller's smile didn't widen. It tightened, the corners of his mouth pulling back like old skin over bone. The sword in his hand hummed, a low, vibrating note that made Dale's teeth ache.

"Smart move," Miller said.

He didn't step forward. "The Authority monitors intent through the System.

But you just bypassed the sensor by using analog media.

That's a Class-4 evasion. You know the penalty for that."

Dale looked up. His heart hammered against his ribs, but his hands were steady. He capped the pen. He closed the Field Guide.

"I know the penalty," Dale said. "I also know you're standing on treated wood."

Miller glanced down at his boots.

The crawlspace floor was covered in a fine, grey powder. Dale had scattered it three minutes ago, during the initial assessment of the wall cavity's structural integrity. It wasn't dust. It was desiccant-based silica gel, spiked with a low-concentration neurotoxin derived from the acid beetles of Chapter 12.

"You didn't document the application," Miller said. "No Treatment Points. No authorization."

"I didn't need authorization for a hazard control measure," Dale replied. "I needed containment."

The sword's hum changed pitch. It dropped an octave, sounding less like a weapon and more like a warning siren. Miller shifted his weight. A cloud of grey powder kicked up around his ankles.

"You're contaminating the evidence," Miller said. His voice was flat.

Dangerous.

"This infestation is under Authority jurisdiction.

You're interfering with an investigation."

"An investigation?" Dale tapped the clipboard against his thigh. "The notification said *Unlicensed Collaboration*. It didn't say *Investigation*. It said *Termination*."

"Exactly." Miller took a step forward. "Because I'm not here to fight the monster, Dale. I'm here to fight you.

The Authority has a blind spot. A certification loophole.

Level VII technicians can request a 'Sanctioned Intervention' if they identify a rogue entity operating outside the licensing grid.

Who do you think filed the request that put me in this basement?"

Dale froze.

The room seemed to shrink.

The shadows in the corners of the basement weren't just dark; they were moving. The scratching noise had returned, but it wasn't coming from the walls anymore.

It was coming from the ceiling.

From the floor. From the spaces between the floorboards where the silica gel didn't reach.

Miller wasn't the only thing in the room.

"You're a bait," Dale said. The realization hit him with the cold clarity of a winter morning. "You're not the threat. You're the lure."

Miller's smile vanished. "Don't be dramatic.

I'm a Level VII exterminator.

Like you.

I just have better tools."

"Tools that don't follow protocol," Dale said. He looked at the sword. It was pristine.

Too pristine. No wear marks.

No residue of previous kills.

It was a new issue. "You're not certified. You're a contractor.

An unlicensed mercenary hired by the Authority to clean up messes that don't look good on the paperwork."

The sword swung.

Dale didn't dodge. He dropped to a crouch, sliding behind the overturned workbench. The blade cleaved the air where his neck had been, slicing through a stack of old paint cans. Sparks showered the concrete floor.

*System Alert: Physical Impact Detected. Evasion Successful. No Points Awarded.*

"You can't run from this, Dale," Miller called out. He walked around the workbench, sword raised.

"The Authority wants this infestation buried.

Literally. They don't want the public to know the Rift is expanding. They want it contained, contained, contained.

And if you write a report that says otherwise, you're a liability."

Dale looked at the Field Guide in his hand. The page with *Threat* was still visible.

He flipped to the next page. The blank one.

He wrote.

*Entry: Contracted Enforcer.* *Type: Human.* *Weakness: Over-reliance on lethal force.* *Countermeasure: Environmental manipulation.*

The System flickered.

**NEW ENTRY DETECTED: HOMO SAPIENS (ROGUE).** **Field Guide Updated.** **Damage Bonus: 0%.** **Note: Non-monster entity. Treatment Protocols do not apply.**

A zero percent bonus. A non-monster. The system refused to categorize him.

Miller laughed.

"You're trying to field-guide me?

I'm not a rat, Dale. I'm not a beetle.

I'm a man."

"That's the problem," Dale said. He stood up.

He didn't hold the clipboard as a weapon.

He held it like a shield. "The Authority doesn't have a protocol for men who break protocol. That's a gap in the system.

And gaps get filled."

He reached into his pocket. Not for a pen.

For a canister.

A small, pressurized vial of aerosolized fogging agent. High-concentration pyrethroid. Intended for cockroach nests.

Useless against humans, generally. Unless they were already compromised.

Miller's eyes narrowed. He looked at the grey powder on his boots. He looked at Dale's hand.

"Put that away," Miller said. "Or I cut you down."

"You're already breathing it," Dale said.

"The silica.

It's not poison. It's a respiratory irritant.

It causes coughing. It causes tearing. It causes distraction."

Miller coughed. Just once. A small, involuntary spasm.

His eyes watered.

For a second, his sword hand wavered.

That second was all Dale needed.

He didn't attack Miller. He attacked the room.

He threw the fogging agent at the ceiling. Not at Miller.

At the junction where the support beams met the joists.

The canister burst. White fog exploded outward, thick and choking.

Miller shouted, swinging his sword blindly into the smoke. The blade sparked against a metal pipe. Water sprayed from the broken line, mixing with the fog.

Dale moved.

He didn't run for the stairs. He ran for the furnace.

The basement's old gas furnace was rusted, shut down for years. But the pilot light assembly was still there. And the gas line, though capped, had been weakened by the vibration of the sword's impact against the pipe. A tiny leak.

Dale knew this because he had inspected the furnace six months ago. He had written a citation for "Improper Ventilation Clearance." He had taped off the area. He had left a tag.

The tag was still there.

Miller was coughing now. coughing. The fog was heavy, obscuring his vision. He swung the sword again, slicing through a wooden support post. The house groaned.

"Stop!" Miller yelled. "You'll bring the whole thing down!"

Dale struck the pilot assembly with his clipboard. Hard. Metal on metal.

A spark.

The gas didn't explode. Not yet. The mixture wasn't right. It was too lean.

But the spark caught the residual fumes from the fogging agent. A small flare-up. A *whoosh* of orange flame.

Miller stumbled back, shielding his face. "Fire? You're starting a fire?"

"I'm starting a ventilation cycle," Dale said. He kicked the furnace's emergency shutoff valve. It was rusted shut. He kicked it again.

The valve gave way with a screech of metal.

The gas line fully opened.

The flame from the fogger ignited the gas.

The fire didn't burn the house. It burned the air. The oxygen in the basement, already thinning from the combustion, was sucked toward the flame. The backdraft pulled the fog, the gas, and the heat toward the open stairs.

Miller screamed.

Not in pain. In anger.

He charged through the flame, sword raised.

The magical blade cut through the fire. It was enchanted to withstand heat.

He reached Dale.

He grabbed Dale's throat.

"You stupid technician," Miller hissed. His face was red, skin blistering from the heat.

"You think you're clever?

You think the rules protect you?

The Authority doesn't care about your rules. They care about results."

Dale couldn't breathe. His vision blurred. The blue ballpoint pen was still in his hand.

He looked at Miller's sword.

It was glowing brighter now, fueled by the ambient magical energy of the Rift. But the hilt...

Dale looked at the hilt. There was a small, jagged chip in the leather grip. A piece of dark wood was exposed.

*Weakness: Structural integrity.*

Dale had identified a weakness in the house. He had identified a weakness in Miller.

He brought the pen down. Not on Miller. On the sword's hilt.

The cheap plastic casing of the pen cracked. The ink cartridge inside, filled with dense, viscous fluid, burst open.

The ink sprayed onto the exposed wood of the sword's grip.

The wood was cursed. Or enchanted. Or both.

But it was wood. And ink is a solvent.

The grip softened. The enchantment faltered.

Miller's hand slipped.

The sword flew from his grip, embedding itself in the concrete floor.

Miller staggered back, clutching his burned hand. He looked at the sword, then at Dale.

"What did you do?"

"Contaminated your tool," Dale wheezed. He pointed to the floor. "Check your Field Guide. See if you have a protocol for ink-stained wood."

Miller looked at the sword. The glow faded. The magic drained out of it, sputtering like a dying bulb.

The System notification flashed in Dale's vision.

**INFRACTION: DESTRUCTION OF AUTHORITY-ISSUED EQUIPMENT.** **PENALTY: Level Demotion Pending.** **STATUS: CRITICAL.**

Dale didn't care. He was on his knees, gasping for air.

The fire was dying. The gas was gone.

Miller picked up the sword. He looked at it with disgust. He looked at Dale with hatred.

"This isn't over," Miller said. He walked to the stairs. His steps were heavy. He didn't look back.

Dale waited until the footsteps faded.

Then he crawled to the wall.

The scratching had stopped.

The swarm was gone.

But the wall was still bulging.

Dale pulled out his clipboard. He opened it to a fresh page.

He wrote.

*Entry: The Authority.* *Type: Institutional.* *Behavior: Self-preservation.* *Weakness: Bureaucratic inertia.*

He underlined the last word twice.

His phone buzzed.

Not a System notification. A regular call.

The ID said: *Licensing Authority - Audit Division.*

Dale answered.

"Technician Kowalski," a cold, synthesized voice said.

"You are hereby placed on administrative leave pending investigation of unauthorized modification of certified assets. Do not attempt further treatments. Do not leave your service area.

A recovery team will be dispatched to your location in four minutes."

The line went dead.

Dale looked at the sword's empty scabbard on the floor. He looked at the hole in the wall.

He walked to the crawlspace.

The broodmother was gone. But something else remained.

A single egg. Large. Pulsing. Warm.

It wasn't a vermin egg. It wasn't a beetle egg.

It was an insect egg. But the shell was transparent. Inside, a tiny, human-shaped larva curled in fetal position.

Dale stared at it.

The System popped up.

**NEW SPECIES IDENTIFIED: HOMO SAPIENS (DIMENSIONAL VARIANT).** **CLASSIFICATION: PEST.** **TREATMENT PROTOCOL: TERMINATION.**

Dale looked at the egg. He looked at the phone in his hand.

The recovery team was coming.

He picked up the egg.

He put it in his pocket.

He didn't write a report.

He started walking toward the stairs.
