Chapter 239: Under Direct Command

# Chapter 239

The floor of the Authority's Intake Chamber did not tilt. It vibrated. A low, sub-bass hum rattled the fillings in Dale's molars and made the fluorescent lights flicker in a rhythmic, nauseating pattern.

Dale stood at the edge of the abyss. The hole in the floor was no longer a hole. It had become a jurisdiction.

Above him, the ceiling of the chamber had dissolved into a grid of scrolling text. Red text. Thousands of lines of red text.

**VIOLATION LOG: CLASS-5 CLEARANCE REQUIRED.** **PENDING COMPLAINTS: 1.** **PENDING COMPLAINTS: 2.** **PENDING COMPLAINTS: 3...**

The counter spun upward. Fast.

Dale didn't look at the counter. He looked at his clipboard.

He had found the clipboard. It was resting on a pedestal of obsidian that had risen from the floor when he initiated the audit. The paper was blank, but the pen was heavy. It felt like a loaded weapon.

"Stop counting," Dale said. His voice was flat. He didn't shout. Shouting wasted oxygen.

The eyes in the abyss blinked. All of them. A thousand distinct, chitinous lids snapping shut in unison.

A figure materialized from the smoke. Not a monster. A bureaucrat.

He was tall. Too tall. His suit was pressed so the crease could cut skin. His face was a mask of bureaucratic indifference, gray and smooth, with eyes that held no pupils, only the reflection of Dale's clipboard.

"Mr. Kowalski," the figure said. The voice sounded like dry leaves skittering over pavement. "You are invoking the Grievance Protocol."

"I am invoking the Audit," Dale corrected. He clicked his pen. *Click.* "There is a difference.

Grievance is emotional.

Audit is structural. I have identified a structural failure in your supervision of Dimensional Rift K-7."

The figure's head tilted. The motion was jerky, mechanical.

"K-7 is a minor infestation," the figure said. "It has been resolved."

"Resolved?" Dale stepped forward.

He didn't cross the line of the obsidian pedestal. He stayed behind the safety barrier of the audit zone.

"The broodmother was eliminated.

The nest was treated. The frass trails were documented. But the entry point remains active.

The dimensional shear is still bleeding into the crawlspace of 422 Maple Street."

Dale pointed his pen at the scrolling red text.

"You failed to seal the breach. You treated the symptom. You left the vector open. That is a Class-3 Code Violation: *Negligent Containment.*"

The figure paused. The scrolling text slowed.

**SYSTEM NOTICE: AUDIT INITIATED.** **AUDITOR: DALE KOWALSKI (CLASS II).** **SUBJECT: AUTHORITY LOCAL BRANCH 4.** **STATUS: UNDER REVIEW.**

The air in the room changed. The humidity dropped. The smell of ozone and old paper replaced the stench of the abyss.

Arthur, who had been cowering in the corner behind a pillar of support, peeked out. His face was still wet with tears, but his eyes were wide.

"Dale," Arthur whispered. "What are you doing?"

"Dale is doing his job," Dale said without turning around.

"Go to the waiting room, Arthur.

Fill out Form 88-B. *Statement of Witness Regarding Dimensional Instability.* Use black ink. No blue.

They reject blue."

Arthur scrambled away, his sneakers squeaking on the polished floor.

The figure in the suit stepped closer. The obsidian pedestal lowered. Dale had to step down to meet him at eye level.

"An audit requires evidence," the figure said. "You have a clipboard."

"I have a Field Guide," Dale said. He flipped the page.

"Entry 42: *Rattus profundus.* I noted the nest location.

I noted the entry vector. I noted the lack of post-treatment verification from the Authority."

He held up the page. The text on it glowed faintly green.

**EVIDENCE LOGGED: FIELD NOTE 42-A.** **VALIDITY: CONFIRMED.**

The figure's mask twitched.

"That is not sufficient," the figure said. "The Authority's records indicate the breach was sealed upon termination of the host organism."

"Your records are wrong," Dale said.

He reached into his pocket. He pulled out a small, clear plastic bag.

Inside was a single shard of chitin.

It was iridescent, shifting colors as it caught the light. It was still warm.

"This is shed carapace," Dale said. "Collected forty-eight hours post-extermination. From the exact coordinates you claim are sealed. If the breach was sealed, where did this come from?"

He dropped the bag on the obsidian pedestal.

The sound was a dull thud.

The figure stared at the bag. The scrolling text on the ceiling froze.

**EVIDENCE LOGGED: PHYSICAL PROOF.** **VALIDITY: CONFIRMED.**

A chime sounded. It was a harsh, dissonant note.

**VIOLATION: NEGLIGENT CONTAINMENT. CONFIRMED.** **PENALTY: LOCAL BRANCH 4 LICENSE SUSPENDED.** **REMEDY: IMMEDIATE SEIZURE OF JURISDICTION BY AUTHORITY LEVEL 2.**

The floor beneath the figure cracked. The suit-wearing bureaucrat dissolved into smoke. He didn't scream. He just unraveled, like a loose thread.

Dale didn't celebrate. He didn't smile. He pulled a fresh form from his pocket.

*Form 114-C: Transfer of Jurisdiction.*

He wrote.

The abyss was shrinking. The eyes were closing one by one, retreating into the darkness. The vibration stopped. The lights steadied.

But the ceiling was still scrolling.

**NEW STATUS: PROVISIONAL AUTHORITY GRANTED TO DALE KOWALSKI.** **SCOPE: LOCAL BRANCH 4 (SUBURBAN OHIO SECTOR).** **LIMITS: 72 HOURS.** **OBJECTIVE: STABILIZE BREACH K-7 OR FACE LICENSE REVOCATION.**

Dale finished writing his signature. He stamped the form with the stamp that had appeared in his breast pocket, a heavy brass thing he hadn't noticed until just now.

**ACCEPTED.**

The obsidian pedestal sank into the floor. The hole in the floor sealed itself. The concrete knit together with a wet, snapping sound.

Silence returned.

Arthur walked back, holding a crumpled piece of paper.

"Did you fix it?" Arthur asked. He looked terrified.

"I filed a complaint," Dale said. He capped his pen. "The Authority is now watching me."

"That's good, right?"

"It means they are waiting for me to fail," Dale said. "If I fail, they revoke my license.

If they revoke my license, I lose my protection.

Without protection, the things in the Rift don't just crawl out.

They hunt."

Dale looked at his Field Guide. The page for *Rattus profundus* was no longer green. It was blinking yellow.

**WARNING: CROSS-CONTAMINATION RISK DETECTED.** **NEW SPECIES DETECTED IN SECTOR 4: *Vesperus gluttonus.* (The Screaming Moth).**

Dale frowned. He hadn't seen a moth.

He looked at Arthur.

"Did you hear any buzzing?" Dale asked.

Arthur shook his head. "Just crying. I was crying."

"Listen," Dale said. He closed his eyes.

He listened.

Not with his ears. With his instincts. The same instincts that let him hear a termite queen tapping in the walls of a Victorian duplex.

A sound. Low. Wet. Coming from the ventilation ducts.

*Thrum. Thrum. Thrum.*

It wasn't mechanical. It was biological.

Dale opened his eyes. He looked at the vent cover above Arthur's head.

The screws were missing.

"Get back," Dale said. He pushed Arthur behind the pillar.

The vent cover popped off.

It didn't fall. It was pushed. Outward. By something soft and heavy.

A creature poured out. It wasn't a bug. It was a mass of pale, translucent wings and too many legs. It had no head, just a mouth that widened vertically, splitting the creature's body in half.

The mouth opened.

**SYSTEM NOTICE: NEW ENTRY DETECTED.** **IDENTIFY: [UNKNOWN]**

Dale didn't run. He didn't raise a weapon. He raised his clipboard.

"Identify," Dale whispered.

He stared at the creature. The wings were membranous, veined with black blood. The legs were jointed, spindly, ending in hooked claws that dug into the plaster.

Dale's mind raced through the taxonomy.

*Order: Lepidoptera.*

*Family: Noctuidae.*

*Genus:?*

He looked at the mouth. It wasn't for eating. It was for screaming.

The creature let out a sound. A high-pitched shriek that shattered the glass in the ceiling lights.

**ENTRY CREATED: *Vesperus gluttonus*.** **WEAKNESS: UNIDENTIFIED.** **PENALTY: 40% DEBUFF IF PROTOCOL MISAPPLIED.**

The creature lunged.

Dale sidestepped. The creature's claws tore a gash in his coat.

**DAMAGE TAKEN: 15 HP.**

Dale didn't recoil. He pulled a canister from his belt.

Not spray. Not poison.

A pheromone trap.

He smashed the canister against the floor.

A cloud of sweet, cloying scent exploded outward.

The creature stopped. Its many legs froze. Its vertical mouth quivered.

It turned toward Dale.

It didn't attack. It hovered.

Dale stepped back. He watched the creature's antennae twitch.

"It's not hungry," Dale said. "It's looking for a mate."

The creature shuddered. It spun in place. Its wings beat faster, creating a vortex of dust and debris.

Dale reached into his pocket. He pulled out a small vial.

*Synthetic Queen Pheromone.*

He had synthesized it himself, in his garage, using chemicals he bought from the hardware store and formulas he decoded from the Field Guide. It was illegal. It was dangerous. It was his only chance.

He uncapped the vial.

The creature lunged.

Dale threw the vial.

It didn't hit the creature. It hit the wall.

The vial shattered.

The scent hit the air.

The creature screeched. It wasn't a sound of rage. It was a sound of desperation.

It flew toward the shattered vial. It collided with the wall, its wings crumpling against the plaster. It teared at the wet spot where the pheromone had landed.

Dale pulled out his pen.

**DOCUMENTATION: BEHAVIORAL RESPONSE TO SYNTHETIC STIMULANT CONFIRMED.** **WEAKNESS IDENTIFIED: SEXUAL IMPULSE / PHEROMONE OVERLOAD.** **TARGETED DAMAGE BONUS: +50% AGAINST *VESPERUS GLUTTONUS*.**

Dale grabbed a heavy, steel-rimmed clipboard from the pedestal.

He swung it.

The edge caught the creature's wing joint.

*Crack.*

The wing snapped.

The creature screamed. It fell, thrashing on the floor, unable to fly.

Dale stepped on it. His boot pinned the torso. He raised his boot.

He didn't stomp. He pressed. He waited.

The System notification popped up in his peripheral vision.

**THOROUGHNESS CHECK: VERIFICATION REQUIRED.** **ACTION: CONFIRM DEATH.**

Dale looked at the creature. It was still moving. The mouth was still open.

He pulled a scalpel from his tool belt.

He cut the throat.

The creature went still.

**ELIMINATION CONFIRMED.** **TREATMENT POINTS AWARDED: 12.** **CURRENT THOROUGHNESS RATING: 9.1.**

Dale wiped the blood off his boot. He looked at the ceiling.

The scrolling text was gone.

In its place, a new message appeared.

**AUDIT COMPLETE.** **LOCAL BRANCH 4 LICENSE RESTORED.** **PENALTY WAIVED.**

**NOTE: DALE KOWALSKI HAS IDENTIFIED A NEW PROTOCOL LOOPHOLE.** **REWARD: UNLOCKED PROTOCOL 4.2 - *CHEMICAL DISRUPTION*.**

Dale exhaled. His shoulders dropped an inch.

Arthur stepped out from behind the pillar. He was shaking.

"Is it over?" Arthur asked.

"The job is over," Dale said. He picked up the clipboard. He inspected the bloodstain on the corner.

He pulled a rag from his pocket and wiped it clean. "The shift is over."

He turned to walk toward the exit.

But the door was gone.

Where the door had been, there was now a wall. And on the wall, a single, large screen.

It displayed a live feed.

It was a view from above. From the sky.

The feed showed the suburban neighborhood. 422 Maple Street. The crawlspace.

But the crawlspace was open.

And something was crawling out.

It wasn't a rat. It wasn't a moth.

It was a hand.

A hand made of chitin and shadow, dragging itself out of the hole in the earth. It was enormous. It covered the entire front yard.

Then another hand. Then a torso. Then a head.

The head had no eyes. It had a mouth that spanned the width of the house.

The System notification flashed red. Not yellow. Red.

**NEW ENTRY DETECTED: CLASS-5 THREAT.** **DESIGNATION: *THE ARCHITECT*.** **STATUS: ACTIVE.** **THREAT LEVEL: EXTERMINATION PROTOCOL INSUFFICIENT.**

Dale stopped walking.

He looked at the screen.

He looked at his clipboard.

He looked at his pen.

**ERROR: CLASS-5 CLEARANCE REQUIRED FOR ENGAGEMENT.**

Dale stared at the message.

He had just regained his license. He had just saved the branch. He had just identified a new weakness.

And now the Authority was telling him he wasn't allowed to fight the thing that had just crawled out of the hole he'd just saved.

A new notification appeared.

**INVITATION: JOINT TASK FORCE FORMATION.** **RECRUIT: DALE KOWALSKI.** **ROLE: SUPPORT SPECIALIST.** **COMMANDER: [UNNAMED] (CLASS IX).**

**ACCEPT? [Y/N]**

Dale's thumb hovered over the pen.

He didn't want to be support. He was an exterminator. He didn't take orders from Class IXs. He didn't need a commander.

But he needed to deal with the Architect. And the Authority would not let him.

The hand on the screen twitched. It crushed the mailbox.

Dale clicked *Y*.

The screen changed.

**ACCEPTED.** **TRANSFER INITIATING.** **WARNING: YOU ARE NOW UNDER DIRECT COMMAND.** **WARNING: INDEPENDENT ACTION PENALTY: 50% TP LOSS.**

Dale pocketed his pen.

He walked toward the wall.

He didn't have a door anymore. He had a transfer beam.

He stepped into the light.
