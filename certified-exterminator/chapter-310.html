Chapter 310: Administrative Error

# Chapter 310

The graphite tip of the mechanical pencil snapped.

Dale didn't flinch. He didn't curse.

He simply rotated the pencil in his fingers, advancing the lead by two millimeters, and resumed writing on the back of the jammed clipboard.

The darkness was absolute, a thick, suffocating blanket that swallowed the room whole. But the notification hung in the air, burning a neon blue against the blackness.

**SPECIES: UNKNOWN.** **WEAKNESS: ADMINISTRATIVE ERROR.**

The door handle rattled violently.

The clipboard wedge held, but the wood was groaning under the pressure of something far heavier than a man. Dale could hear the wet, chitinous scraping of limbs against the drywall.

It wasn't the clumsy thumping of a rat, which was precise. Calculated.

*Click-click-click.*

The Morse code continued, but the rhythm had changed. It was faster now. Urgent.

Dale ignored it. He focused on the System prompt.

Administrative error, which was absurd. It was bureaucratic. It was the only thing that made sense in a world where eldritch horrors wore suits.

He pulled the pen from his shirt pocket and clicked it open. The soft *click* was loud in the silence. Outside, the scratching stopped. The presence on the other side of the door seemed to lean closer, listening.

Dale wrote the date. Then the location. Then the time.

Standard inspection protocol.

*03:42 AM. Substation Delta-Nine.

Unauthorized entry by Class V entity.

Environmental conditions: Total blackout.

Atmospheric pressure: Elevated.*

The door buckled. A splinter of wood flew inward, embedding itself in the floorboards.

Dale didn't look up. He kept writing.

*Observation 1: Entity exhibits humanoid morphology but lacks defined facial features. Audio signature suggests telepathic or acoustic communication via structured repetition.*

He was documenting. He was identifying. He was doing the one thing the Authority rewarded more than blood.

The notification pulsed again.

**DOCUMENTATION IN PROGRESS. POINTS ACCUMULATING.**

A faint, warm glow emanated from the clipboard. It wasn't light, exactly. It was the sensation of order being imposed on chaos. The paper felt heavy, dense, like lead.

Dale smiled. It was a thin, tight thing, devoid of joy.

He knew the loophole.

The Authority didn't care if you killed the monster. It cared if you *filed* the monster. If you completed the paperwork, the creature ceased to be an anomaly and became a record. And records could be amended.

If the weakness was an administrative error, then the fix was a correction.

But you couldn't correct a record that didn't exist. You had to create it first.

The door gave a shudder. The latch snapped.

Dale kicked the desk forward, shoving it against the door to reinforce the wedge. The furniture screeched across the linoleum, a harsh, grinding sound that echoed in the dark.

He didn't have a weapon. He had a pencil. He had a pen. He had a clipboard.

He had the Field Guide, and he closed his eyes and visualized the interface. The mental map of his current certifications flickered in his mind's eye. Level IV.

A respectable rank. He had protocols for acid beetles, for spore clouds, for dimensional rifts under ten meters wide.

But not for this. Not for the thing that spoke in Morse code.

He reached for the new entry. It was blank. Unfilled. A void in the data structure of his professional soul.

*Identify,* he told himself. *Isolate. Eliminate. Verify.*

He couldn't eliminate it. He couldn't verify it without seeing it. But he could isolate it.

Dale grabbed the roll of duct tape from his belt. It was a habit, a reflex born of fourteen years of sealing cracks. He tore off a strip. The sound was sharp, final.

He slapped the tape over the keyhole of the door. Then another. Then another.

He was creating a barrier. Not physical. Symbolic.

In the eyes of the Authority, a sealed entry point was a treated entry point.

**PROTOCOL INITIATED: CONTAINMENT VIA OBSTRUCTION.** **COST: 15 TREATMENT POINTS.** **STATUS: ACTIVE.**

The points deducted from his reserve. Dale felt a sudden hollowness in his chest, a metaphysical drain. He was running low. He needed those points for the next tier of threats, the ones that required more than duct tape.

But the darkness outside the door didn't move, and the chittering had stopped.

Dale waited. His hand hovered over the pencil.

A voice spoke. Not from the door. From the ceiling.

"You are writing," the voice said. It sounded like dry leaves skittering across pavement.

Dale didn't answer. He wrote: *Observation 2: Entity possesses vocal mimicry capabilities.*

"You are wasting time," the voice continued.

Closer now. Above him.

"The ink is finite.

The page is finite.

The man is finite.

"

Dale looked up. The ceiling was gone.

Where the plaster should have been, there was a void.

A swirling, inky mass that defied perspective. From within the mass, eyes opened. Hundreds of them.

No pupils. Just white, unblinking spheres that reflected the blue glow of his notification.

Dale's breath hitched. His fingers tightened around the pencil until the plastic cracked.

This was it. The source of the infestation. The breeder.

It wasn't looking at him. It was looking at the clipboard.

"Show me," the entity said.

Dale raised the clipboard. The paper was still blank below his initial notes. He had filled the top half with procedure. He had left the bottom half empty.

He understood then.

The Administrative Error wasn't a mistake in the file. It was the *absence* of the file.

The entity existed because it was unclassified. It thrived in the gap between knowledge and reality. As long as it remained a mystery, it was powerful. If Dale named it, he trapped it.

But he couldn't name it. The Field Guide didn't have an entry for it. The System didn't know what it was.

Unless he lied.

Unless he fabricated a classification, and the risk was immense.

The cross-contamination penalty for deploying a protocol designed for one creature type against another was severe.

Forty percent debuff. Twenty-four hours of guide corruption.

If he misidentified this Class V threat, he might lose everything. He might be unlicensed. He might be dead.

But if he did nothing, the entity would peel the skin off his bones and wear it like a suit.

Dale looked at the eyes in the ceiling. They blinked in unison.

*Click-click-click.*

They were waiting for a classification.

Dale bit the inside of his cheek. The sharp pain grounded him.

He thought about the last job.

The broodmother in the crawlspace. He had called it *Rattus profundus*. He had been wrong, technically.

It was a variant.

But the label had worked because the *structure* of the label was correct. Genus.

Species.

Habitat, and he needed to force the structure.

He wrote in the empty space.

*Species Classification: Bureaucratic Parasite.* *Common Name: Red Tape.* *Habitat: Limbo.* *Diet: Uncertainty.*

The words burned on the page. The System resisted. Red text flashed in his peripheral vision.

**WARNING: UNVERIFIED CLASSIFICATION. PENALTY IMMINENT.**

Dale kept writing.

*Behavior: Mimics authority figures. Exploits procedural gaps. Weakness: Finality.*

The entity recoiled. The ceiling of eyes widened. The inky mass rippled, turning a violent shade of purple.

"No," the voice hissed. "That is not the name. That is not the truth."

"It's the only name I have," Dale said. His voice was steady.

Deadpan, and he sounded like he was reading a violation code.

"And I'm filing it."

He slammed the pen onto the paper.

**CLASSIFICATION ACCEPTED (PROVISIONAL).** **SPECIES: RED TAPE (SUBJECT TO REVIEW).** **TREATMENT POINTS AWARDED: 50.** **FIELD GUIDE UPDATE: COMPLETE.**

The blue light of the notification exploded outward, filling the room.

The ceiling dissolved. The eyes vanished. The inky mass screamed, a sound like tearing metal, and then it was sucked into the paper.

The suction was violent. Dale's shoes slid across the floor. His clipboard flew out of his hand, pinned against the wall by an invisible force.

He grabbed the edge of the desk, his knuckles white, his muscles straining against the pull of the void.

The entity was being archived, which was being *filed*.

And as it was filed, it was stripped of its power. The darkness receded. The lights flickered, buzzed, and returned to a dull, humming fluorescent glow.

Silence rushed back into the room.

Dale fell to his knees. His heart hammered against his ribs, a frantic, irregular bird. He gasped for air, the taste of copper in his mouth.

The clipboard lay on the floor. The paper was intact. The words were there.

*Red Tape.*

He had named it. He had trapped it.

And then the notification changed.

**STATUS: PENDING REVIEW.** **REVIEWER ASSIGNED: INSPECTOR VANE.** **TIME TO REVIEW: 00:00:01.**

Dale froze.

Inspector Vane, and he knew the name. Every technician in the Authority knew the name. Vane was a Level VII.

A Senior Adjudicator. The kind of bureaucrat who could revoke a license for a misplaced decimal point.

And Vane had just arrived.

The door to the office didn't open. It simply ceased to be jammed. The splinters fell away. The desk slid back an inch.

The door swung open, and a figure stood in the doorway. It was tall, impossibly thin, wearing a suit that seemed to be made of gray ash. Its face was smooth, featureless, save for a pair of spectacles that sat on empty air where a nose should be.

It held a stamp in one hand. A heavy, brass thing, etched with runes that hurt to look at.

"Mr.

Kowalski," the figure said. Its voice was polite. Terrifyingly polite.

"Your provisional classification is... irregular."

Dale stood up. His legs shook. He picked up the clipboard.

"It fits the behavior profile," Dale said. He didn't apologize. He didn't explain. He presented the evidence.

Vane stepped into the room. The air grew cold. Frost crept along the floorboards from the figure's feet.

"The behavior profile is insufficient for a Class V entity," Vane said. "You have created a placeholder. A temporary label. This constitutes a violation of Section 4, Paragraph 9: Misrepresentation of Threat Level."

Dale looked at the notification.

**THREAT LEVEL: CLASS V (CONFIRMED).** **PENALTY FOR MISREPRESENTATION: LICENSE SUSPENSION.**

He had lost it.

Not his license. Not yet. But his access.

"I documented it," Dale said. "I contained it. I eliminated the immediate threat."

"You fabricated a taxonomy," Vane said. The stamp hovered near Dale's forehead.

"The Authority does not permit fabrication.

It permits inference. You inferred. You did not infer.

You invented."

Dale stared at the stamp. He could feel the cold radiating from it.

"If you revoke my license," Dale said, "who files the rest of them?"

Vane paused. The spectacles tilted.

"Explain."

"There are twelve more rifts in this district," Dale said.

"You know it.

The backlog is growing. You have no other Level II technicians in the zone.

The others are dead, or unlicensed, or insane. I am the only one who knows how to write a report that sticks.

"

He held up the clipboard. The paper glowed faintly.

"I named it *Red Tape*. Because that's what it feeds on.

Ambiguity.

Delay. Procedure. You're a man of procedure, Inspector.

You understand the value of a well-ordered file.

"

Vane looked at the paper. The runes on the stamp dimmed.

"Red Tape," Vane murmured. "A derogatory term. Unprofessional."

"Accurate," Dale said.

Vane stepped closer. The frost on the floor melted.

"The Authority values thoroughness," Vane said. "But it values obedience more."

"I followed the protocol," Dale said. "Identify.

Document.

Eliminate. Verify. I did all four.

"

"You skipped 'Verify' with a standard method. You used a novel method."

"Effective method," Dale corrected.

Vane raised the stamp.

Dale didn't flinch. He watched the brass metal descend.

The stamp touched the paper.

It didn't print a rejection.

It printed a red, jagged symbol. An asterisk.

**PROVISIONAL ACCEPTANCE.** **REVIEW REQUIRED: 24 HOURS.** **NOTE: MONITOR FOR RECLASSIFICATION.**

Vane pulled the stamp away.

"You have twenty-four hours," Vane said. "If the entity re-emerges, or if another technician identifies it differently, your license will be revoked. And you will be held liable for the breach."

Dale exhaled. The tension in his shoulders dropped, but his hands remained clenched.

"And if it doesn't?"

"Then you may proceed to the next file."

Vane turned. The figure dissolved into a cloud of gray ash and drifted out the door.

Dale stood alone in the office. The lights hummed. The smell of ozone lingered.

He looked at the clipboard. The word *Red Tape* sat there, bold and black, surrounded by the red asterisk.

He had bought himself a day.

But the Morse code was still in his head. *Click-click-click.*

It wasn't coming from the ceiling anymore, which was coming from his own wrist.

Dale looked down. His skin was rippling. A faint, translucent outline of an insect was shifting beneath the dermis.

**INTERNAL INFESTATION DETECTED.** **SOURCE: EXTERNAL ENTITY RESIDUAL.** **ACTION REQUIRED: IMMEDIATE SURGICAL INTERVENTION OR CHEMICAL FLUSH.**

He had named the monster. But he hadn't killed it.

He had trapped it in a box. And the box was leaking.

Dale opened his desk drawer. His hand trembled as he reached for the scalpel he kept for dissections.

The clock on the wall ticked, and he had twenty-four hours.

He had no anesthesia.

And the thing inside him was starting to click.
