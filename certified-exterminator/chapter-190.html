Chapter 190: Candidacy of Violation

# Chapter 190

The concrete was wet.

Dale tasted copper and sulfur as he hit the bottom. His spine compressed, vertebrae stacking like wet cardboard.

He didn't scream.

Screaming wasted oxygen. He curled into a fetal position, knees to chest, protecting the clipboard tucked inside his vest. The impact killed his momentum but not his consciousness.

Pain registered late, a dull throb in the lower lumbar region that he filed under *Physical Trauma – Non-Critical*.

He lay still. Counted to ten. Checked his limbs.

Ten fingers. Ten toes.

Breathing steady, and the space was dark. Not the comforting, dusty darkness of a crawlspace, but a void that swallowed the beam of his flashlight.

The light hit a wall three feet away and died.

No reflection. No texture. Just absorption.

His HUD flickered.

**LOCATION: UNKNOWN.** **STATUS: ANOMALOUS.** **INTEGRATION PAUSE: ACTIVE.**

The text hovered in his peripheral vision, grayed out. *Integration Pause.* He had thirty-six hours left on his clock. The appeal was pending.

But *Unknown Location* was a problem.

You couldn't document what you couldn't see.

You couldn't isolate what you couldn't locate.

Dale tapped the side of his helmet. Audio sensors engaged. The silence was absolute.

Not even the hum of the city above. Just the wet drip of his own blood hitting the floor. *Plip.

Plip.*

He stood up. Slowly. Testing the weight.

Something moved in the dark.

Not a step. A shift in air pressure. A displacement of volume.

Dale switched his flashlight to UV mode. The beam cut through the dust.

The walls weren't concrete. They were chitin. Translucent, layered chitin, pulsing with a faint, bioluminescent blue vein. The floor was a lattice of fibrous strands, vibrating under his boots.

*Infestation.*

He pulled his clipboard. The paper was crumpled but legible. He clicked his pen.

**SUBJECT:** Structural Integration Site. **OBSERVATION:** Living architecture, and he walked forward. His boot sank into the fibrous floor. It resisted, then released.

A trap?

Or merely soft tissue? He stepped carefully, testing each footfall.

This was a Class-4 hazard zone.

High-risk biological environment.

A sound echoed from deeper in the tunnel. Wet, clicking mandibles.

Dale didn't run. He raised his flashlight. The beam swept across the ceiling.

Suspended from the roof were hundreds of sacs. White, translucent, dripping. Inside, dark shapes curled. Larval forms.

His Field Guide updated automatically.

**NEW ENTRY DETECTED: ARCHITECTURAL PARASITE (UNIDENTIFIED).** **IDENTIFICATION CONFIDENCE: 15%.** **WARNING: DO NOT ENGAGE WITHOUT PROPER PROTOCOL.**

Low confidence. That meant he was blind.

But he had a tool.

Dale reached into his belt. He didn't pull a weapon.

He pulled a sample vial.

Glass.

Sterile, and he approached the nearest sac. The vibration of his boots made the sacs tremble. One burst. A spray of viscous fluid hit the floor, hissing where it touched the chitin.

Dale held the vial steady. He scooped a sample of the fluid.

The fluid glowed. It reacted to the UV light.

He capped the vial. Shook it.

*Chemical analysis required.*

He didn't have a lab. But he had the System.

He focused on the vial in his inventory.

**ITEM:** Biological Sample (Unknown). **ACTION:** Analyze. **COST:** 50 Treatment Points, and he paid it.

The results flashed in his mind.

**COMPONENTS:** 40% Enzymatic Mucus, 30% Chitin Dust, 20% Residual Rift Energy, 10% Human DNA.

Human DNA.

Dale's jaw tightened.

This wasn't just an infestation. It was a harvesting ground. The

"Architectural Parasite" wasn't just building a structure; it was using people as raw material.

He looked at the sacs again. The dark shapes inside weren't larvae.

They were hands.

Fingers. Partial faces.

A scream tore through the silence.

Not a monster's scream. A human voice. Distorted, gargled, coming from one of the sacs directly above him.

Dale froze. His protocol required isolation. But the victim was active.

*Section 12, Clause 7: Immediate Mitigation of Hostile Environmental Factors.*

He couldn't wait for the inspection team. He had to stabilize the zone.

He raised his spray canister. Standard Issue Class-II Repellent. Designed for Class-1 vermin. Useless against Class-4 biological threats.

Unless he modified it.

Dale uncapped the nozzle. He pulled a small ampoule from his utility belt: *Rift-Solvent.* Expensive. Restricted.

He'd bought it on the black market in Sector 4. It was meant for cleaning equipment, not combat.

But it dissolved chitin, and he mixed the solvent into the repellent canister. The liquid turned milky white.

He sprayed.

The mist hit the nearest sac.

The chitin dissolved. The sacs collapsed, spilling their contents onto the floor. Screams erupted from the debris.

Bodies.

Dozens of them. Half-fused with the walls.

Dale didn't look at them. He looked at the ceiling.

The structure was reacting to the damage. The blue veins pulsed faster. The floor rose.

*Aggressive structural response.*

He needed to map the exit, and he turned, flashlight sweeping the tunnel.

A figure stood at the far end.

Not a monster.

A man. Wearing a suit. Pristine.

White. Unmarked.

The man held a clipboard. Identical to Dale's.

Dale lowered his flashlight. His hand went to his sidearm. Then stopped.

*Hostile Intent Check.*

The man didn't move. He just watched.

"Inspector," Dale said. His voice was flat.

The man smiled. It was a tight, practiced expression.

"Technician."

"You're early," Dale said. "The review panel doesn't arrive for thirty-six hours."

"The Authority expedited my ticket," the man said. He stepped forward. His shoes made no sound. "I am the third party."

Dale's eyes narrowed.

"Your ID."

The man tapped his chest. A badge appeared. Shiny. Gold. *Certification Level IX.*

Level Nine.

Dale was Level Five.

"You're not an inspector," Dale said. "Inspectors wear gray. You're wearing white."

"White is for correction," the man said. "Gray is for assessment. I am here to correct."

He raised his hand. The air around him warped.

"You're unauthorized," Dale said. "Section 12, Clause 4 requires a *panel*. Not a single entity. A single entity lacks jurisdiction."

The man tilted his head.

"Jurisdiction is a construct. Power is absolute."

He snapped his fingers.

The floor beneath Dale dissolved.

Dale jumped, and he landed on a floating fragment of chitin.

The tunnel was collapsing. The walls were contracting.

The sacs were bursting, releasing a cloud of acidic mist.

Dale coughed. His eyes watered. His lungs burned.

He checked his HUD.

**STATUS:** CRITICAL. **OXYGEN:** 80%. **INTEGRATION PAUSE:** FAILED, and the pause was broken. The man had forced a violation of the appeal process.

Dale looked at his Field Guide.

**ARCHITECTURAL PARASITE.** **WEAKNESS:** Structural Dependency on Host Biomass. **NOTE:** If host biomass is removed, structure collapses.

He had the sample. He had the DNA.

He needed a distraction.

Dale pulled his flare gun. Not a weapon. A signaling device. He aimed it at the ceiling, at the thickest cluster of sacs.

He fired.

The flare hit. The sacs ignited.

The chitin caught fire.

The blue veins turned black. The structure groaned.

The man in white frowned. He looked up.

Dale used the distraction. He sprinted toward the man.

Not to attack. To *document*.

He pulled out his phone. He took a photo.

**ACTION:** DOCUMENT VIOLATION. **TARGET:** UNLICENSED INTERVENTION. **EVIDENCE:** PHOTOGRAPHIC.

The System pinged.

**VIOLATION LOGGED.** **OFFENSE:** Unauthorized Modification of Infestation Site. **SEVERITY:** CLASS 1. **PENALTY:** Immediate Termination of Certification.

Dale stopped. He pointed the phone at the man.

"You're not inspecting," Dale said. "You're destroying evidence."

The man's smile vanished.

"You think you can out-bureaucrat me?"

"I think," Dale said, "that you're contaminating my Field Guide."

He showed the phone screen. The photo was blurry, but the man's face was clear. And the System tag.

**UNLICENSED ENTITY.**

The man lunged.

Dale didn't dodge. He activated his last protocol.

**PROTOCOL: CONTAINMENT FIELD.** **COST:** 200 Treatment Points. **DURATION:** 3 Seconds.

A dome of energy snapped around Dale. The man's hand hit the barrier and shattered. Black glass fragments flew into the air.

The man screamed. A high, thin sound.

Dale stepped back. The containment field held. But the man was regenerating. The black glass flowed back together.

Dale had three seconds, and he looked at the burning sacs. The human biomass was failing. The structure was dying.

He had to get out.

He looked at the tunnel entrance. It was closing.

He looked at the man.

Dale raised his clipboard.

"I am filing a grievance," he said.

He slammed the clipboard against the containment field.

The System registered the action.

**ACTION:** SUBMIT COMPLAINT. **STATUS:** PENDING. **NOTIFICATION:** INSPECTION TEAM EN ROUTE. ETA: 10 MINUTES.

The man froze.

"They're coming," Dale said. "And they're bringing the sledgehammers."

The man's face twisted.

"You're stalling."

"I'm following procedure," Dale said. "It's the only way I know."

The containment field flickered. It was failing.

The man smiled again.

"Then let's see how long your procedure lasts."

He reached out. His hand passed through the barrier.

Dale stumbled back. The barrier dissolved.

The man grabbed Dale's throat.

Black fingers closed around his windpipe.

Dale couldn't breathe. His vision tunneled.

He couldn't speak. He couldn't fight.

But he could think.

*Cross-contamination penalty.*

Dale activated his Field Guide.

**ENTRY:** ARCHITECTURAL PARASITE. **ACTION:** WEAKNESS EXPLOITATION. **TARGET:** UNLICENSED ENTITY.

The System flashed a warning.

**WARNING:** TARGET IS NOT A VERMIN. PROTOCOL MISUSE. **PENALTY:** 40% DEBUFF. **CONSEQUENCE:** FIELD GUIDE CORRUPTION.

Dale didn't care, and he focused his mind on the weakness. *Structural Dependency.*

The man was part of the structure. He was wearing the uniform of the Authority. He was connected to the site.

Dale projected the weakness onto the man.

The man gasped. His black glass skin cracked.

Dale coughed. Air rushed into his lungs.

The man pulled his hand back, and he looked at his own chest. A hole was forming.

Not flesh.

A void.

"What did you do?" the man whispered.

"I identified you," Dale said. His voice was raspy. "You're an entry point. And I'm sealing it."

He pulled out a strip of duct tape.

Standard issue, and he slapped it over the hole in the man's chest.

The man screamed. The sound was like grinding metal.

The structure around them collapsed in earnest. The ceiling came down.

Dale ran, and he ran into the darkness. He didn't look back.

He climbed. He scrambled over debris. He cut his hands on sharp chitin. He didn't stop.

He reached the surface, and the lobby was gone. The floor was a crater.

The city outside was silent.

No sirens.

No cars.

Just dust.

Dale lay on the pavement. He checked his HUD.

**INTEGRATION PAUSE:** RESUMED. **TIME REMAINING:** 00:00:05, and he had five seconds.

Then he looked up.

The sky was cracking. Not with light. With text.

Huge, floating letters.

**CERTIFICATION LEVEL VI EXAM: IN PROGRESS.** **SUBJECT: ETHICAL VIOLATION.** **CANDIDATE: DALE KOWALSKI.**

Dale closed his eyes, and he hadn't passed the exam.

He had become the exam.
