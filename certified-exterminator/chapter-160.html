Chapter 160: Trapped Inside

# Chapter 160

Dale’s knuckles whitened against the steel crowbar as he stared into the hovering gap. It hovered in the gap, trembling with a violence that had nothing to do with fear. The fingers, too numerous and jointed for any human anatomy, curled inward, grasping at the air as if feeling for the structural integrity of the reality that held them.

Dale did not back away, and he adjusted his grip on the crowbar. The metal was cold, biting into his palms, but his fingers remained steady.

His heart rate spiked, but his breathing remained controlled.

In for four counts. Hold for four. Out for six.

A standard panic mitigation technique from the technician's manual, page 14, paragraph 3.

"Show me your license," the darkness whispered again.

The voice didn't come from the doorway. It vibrated in Dale's molars. It resonated in the fillings of his back teeth.

Dale looked at the hand. Then he looked at the clipboard.

The Field Guide was blank. The entry for *Rattus profundus* had been wiped, but the physical book remained. He flipped it open. The pages were empty, waiting.

He pulled a pen from his breast pocket.

The creature in the doorway was not an animal. It was not a monster in the traditional sense. It was a structural failure. A violation of biological code so severe it had manifested as physical matter.

"Subject exhibits poly-dactyly with hyper-articulated phalanges," Dale muttered, writing the observation. "Dermal pallor consistent with rapid cellular apoptosis. No chitinous plating. No exoskeletal features."

The hand twitched. A drop of black fluid fell from the fingertips onto the porch step. It sizzled, eating a small crater into the wood.

Acidic secretions.

But not the type produced by beetles. Not the type produced by rats.

Dale leaned in, just an inch. He sniffed the air.

Copper.

Rot. And something else. Ozone.

Like the air after a lightning strike.

Electrical discharge, and he looked at the plywood covering the door. It was warped. Blackened at the edges. Scorch marks radiated from the seams.

Not a burrow. A short circuit.

The darkness laughed. It was a dry, crackling sound, like static on a dead channel.

"You have no protocol for me," the voice said. "You have no license."

Dale didn't answer. He looked at the system notification hovering in his peripheral vision. It was faint, glitching, but legible.

**ERROR: CLASSIFICATION AMBIGUOUS.** **TREATMENT POINTS SUSPENDED PENDING MANUAL OVERRIDE.**

The Authority was confused.

Good.

Confusion meant hesitation. Hesitation meant opportunity.

Dale shifted his weight. He planted his feet shoulder-width apart. The crowbar was still jammed in the doorframe. He used it as a fulcrum.

"Identify," Dale said aloud.

The voice paused.

"What?"

"Identify yourself," Dale said, his voice flat, professional. "If you are a mutation, you are a host. If you are a host, you have a vector. If you have a vector, you have an entry point."

The hand clenched. The joints popped with the sound of snapping twigs.

"I am the end," the voice hissed.

"You are a symptom," Dale corrected.

He stepped forward. One step.

The darkness recoiled. Not physically, but psychically. A wave of nausea hit Dale, but he didn't let it break his posture. He forced himself to look at the details, not the horror.

The scorch marks on the plywood.

The black fluid.

The lack of organic waste. No frass.

No droppings. No nest debris.

This wasn't a biological infestation. It was an electrical one.

A thought struck him. A loophole in the certification system he had never tested.

The system rewarded documentation of biological threats. But it also penalized cross-contamination. What if the threat wasn't biological at all? What if it was environmental?

The Authority recognized *infestations*. Did it recognize *malfunctions*?

Dale withdrew the crowbar.

The darkness surged forward, a tendril of shadow lashing out to grab his throat.

Dale sidestepped. The shadow struck the doorframe, charring the wood further.

He didn't attack. He inspected.

He ran his hand along the edge of the plywood, careful to avoid the black fluid. His glove came away stained, but he ignored it.

He looked at the nails, and they were bent. Twisted.

Not by force. By expansion.

The house wasn't being invaded. It was being overloaded.

The woman on the curb was screaming again, but she had retreated behind her car. She was watching Dale. Her eyes were wide, terrified, but fixed on him.

Dale looked at her.

"Is your breaker box on the north wall?"

She blinked. "What?"

"The north wall. Exterior. Near the garage."

She pointed a shaking finger.

"Yes. But don't—"

"Go to the basement," Dale said.

"Shut off the main water valve. Do not touch the breaker box.

Do not enter the house.

If the lights flicker, leave the premises. Immediately.

"

"Why?" she shouted.

"Because this isn't a monster," Dale said. "It's a fire hazard."

He turned back to the door, and the shadow was coalescing. The hand was trying to pull itself through the gap.

More fingers emerged.

Then a wrist.

Then a forearm, elongated and wrong, bending backward at the elbow.

Dale looked at his clipboard, and he wrote: **SUBJECT: NON-BIOLOGICAL. VECTOR: ELECTRICAL/ARCANIC OVERLOAD. SOURCE: UNKNOWN.**

The system flashed red.

**WARNING: INVALID TARGET TYPE. CROSS-CONTAMINATION PENALTY IMMINENT.**

Dale ignored the warning, and he reached into his utility belt. He bypassed the spray cans. He bypassed the traps.

He pulled out a heavy-duty rubber mat.

It was used for standing on during long inspections to prevent back strain. It was thick. Insulated. Rated for high-voltage environments, though never tested against eldritch energy.

He laid the mat on the porch, directly in front of the door.

Then he took his flashlight.

Not the standard LED model. The high-lumen, floodlight attachment he used for inspecting attic insulation.

He switched it to strobe mode.

The light flashed. Rapid, disorienting pulses.

The shadow shrieked. It wasn't a scream of pain. It was a sound of interference.

The strobe light disrupted the frequency of the darkness. It broke the cohesion of the entity.

Dale saw it then.

The darkness wasn't solid. It was composed of millions of tiny, fast-moving particles. Like dust in a sunbeam, but black and electric.

It was a swarm.

A swarm of static.

A Class-1 Vermin swarm, but composed of energy than matter.

Dale's eyes narrowed.

If it was a swarm, it had a hive.

If it had a hive, it had a nest, and the nest was the source of the overload.

He looked at the scorch marks on the plywood. They radiated from the center of the door. But the door wasn't the source. The door was the vent.

The source was inside.

And it was drawing power from the house.

Dale realized his mistake. He had been treating the manifestation, not the cause.

He needed to cut the power.

But he couldn't go to the basement. The entity was blocking the door.

And if he cut the power, the entity might disperse. Or it might coalesce into something worse. A solid mass of ungrounded energy.

He needed a ground, and he looked at the rubber mat.

He looked at the crowbar.

He looked at the woman's car.

The car battery.

If he could create a circuit, a path for the energy to travel, he could drain it. He could starve it.

It was a stupid idea. It was a dangerous idea. It was a violation of at least six OSHA safety codes regarding electrical grounding near uncontrolled energy sources.

But it was also a treatment protocol.

*Isolate.*

*Eliminate.*

*Verify.*

Dale grabbed the rubber mat. He dragged it toward the car.

"What are you doing?" the woman yelled.

"Grounding the nest," Dale said.

He pulled the jumper cables from the trunk of his work truck. He had them for emergency starts, not for exorcisms.

He connected one clamp to the negative terminal of the car battery.

He kept the other clamp in his hand, and he walked back to the porch.

The entity was pulling itself through the gap now. The head was visible. A face made of smoke and static, with eyes that were nothing but holes in the fabric of space.

It looked at Dale.

It looked at the cables.

It hesitated.

"Show me your license," it whispered.

Dale held up the clipboard.

"I am a Certified Exterminator, Class II," he said. "And I am citing you for code violation K-12: Unauthorized Energy Discharge."

He threw the rubber mat over his hand holding the cable.

He lunged.

Not at the creature, and he lunged at the puddle of black, acidic fluid on the step.

He slammed the positive clamp into the fluid.

The circuit closed.

The house lit up.

Every bulb in the neighborhood burst.

The shadow screamed, a sound of tearing metal. The energy drained from the doorway, flowing into the puddle, into the cable, into the car battery.

The car battery exploded.

It didn't just pop. It detonated. A shockwave of flame and lead shards ripped through the street.

Dale was thrown backward. He hit the asphalt hard. His vision whited out.

He lay there, staring at the sky. The stars were visible. Too bright. Too sharp.

Silence returned, and the house was dark. The door was open.

The hand was gone.

The darkness was gone.

Dale coughed. Blood filled his mouth. He spat it onto the asphalt.

He checked his clipboard. It was cracked, but the writing was legible.

**SUBJECT: NON-BIOLOGICAL. VECTOR: ELECTRICAL/ARCANIC OVERLOAD. SOURCE: UNKNOWN.** **STATUS: DISSIPATED.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 4.1**

Dale frowned.

4.1?

That was low.

Sloppy work, and he had destroyed the evidence. He had not verified the nest was dead. He had only dispersed the manifestation.

The problem was still inside.

He sat up. His ribs ached. His hands were burned.

He looked at the house, and the door was still open.

But now, something else was visible in the darkness.

A figure.

Standing in the hallway.

It was human-sized. It was wearing a technician's uniform.

The same uniform Dale wore.

The figure stepped forward. Into the light of the streetlamp that had survived the explosion.

It had Dale's face.

But the eyes were black holes.

The figure held a clipboard.

On the clipboard was a single word.

**REVISED.**

The figure smiled. It was Dale's smile, but stretched too wide.

"You passed," the figure said. "But you failed the inspection."

Dale scrambled to his feet. His legs shook.

The Authority's notification appeared in his vision.

**NEW ENTRY CREATED: DALE KOWALSKI (CLASS IX).** **STATUS: ACTIVE.** **TREATMENT POINTS: -500.** **CROSS-CONTAMINATION PENALTY: ACTIVE.** **FIELD GUIDE LOCKED.**

Dale stared at the other Dale.

The other Dale raised a crowbar, which was the same crowbar Dale had dropped in the explosion.

"You don't get to exterminate me," the other Dale said. "I have a better license."

Dale looked at his own clipboard. The page was blank. The Field Guide was empty.

He had no tools. No protocols. No knowledge.

The other Dale swung the crowbar.

Dale didn't block, and he ducked.

The crowbar smashed into the asphalt next to his head.

Dale rolled. He grabbed a shard of broken glass from the street.

He looked at the other Dale, and he looked at the house.

He looked at the sky.

The Rift was closing. Or it was opening wider. It was hard to tell.

The other Dale raised the crowbar again.

"Who are you?" the other Dale asked.

Dale tightened his grip on the glass.

"I'm the technician," Dale said.

"And I'm the pest," the other Dale whispered.

He lunged.

Dale didn't run, and he stepped into the house.

Into the darkness.

The door slammed shut behind him.

The lock clicked.

And for the first time in his career, Dale Kowalski was trapped inside with the thing he was supposed to kill.

He heard something shift in the darkness.
