Chapter 26

The proboscis was two inches from Webb's left eye.

Dale moved the clipboard into the gap. The filament touched the laminated surface and recoiled, curling back on itself like a burned wire. Webb gasped but held position. Good. The host didn't fight back because they thought they'd already won, but Webb wasn't fighting. Webb was frozen, which was functionally identical to compliance.

Dale stepped left. The mimic's legs tracked him, but the proboscis stayed anchored to Webb's face, tasting. That was the tell. The shell was a distraction. The real organism was the filament, and it couldn't detach once it had a host candidate. It was committed.

He raised his pen. Not as a weapon. As a probe. He touched the tip to the carapace seam, the line where the shell met the thorax. The Field Guide entry pulsed in his peripheral vision: Ostracodermis mendax. Weakness: dorsal suture line. Structural integrity compromised at the junction of the third and fourth abdominal plates.

The +40% bonus was already applied. He didn't need to think about it. He needed to find the seam.

The mimic shifted its weight. The proboscis twitched toward Webb's ear.

"Webb. Chin up."

"What?"

"Chin up. You're exposing the submandibular gland. If it gets under your jaw, the seal is permanent."

Webb lifted his chin. The proboscis followed, and in the moment it stretched, the carapace pulled taut across the dorsal plates. Dale saw the seam. A hairline gap between the third and fourth plates, barely visible, weeping a clear fluid that smelled like vinegar and copper.

He drove the pen into the gap.

The mimic's legs folded. The shell cracked along the suture line with a sound like a zipper being pulled too fast. Clear fluid sprayed across Dale's clipboard. The proboscis detached from Webb's face and retracted into the thorax, curling into a tight spiral.

The Authority flashed:

ELIMINATION COMPLETE. SPECIES: OSTRACODERMIS MENDAX. THOROUGHNESS: 8.7. TREATMENT POINTS: 34.

Eight point seven. Not great. He'd skipped the documentation step because Webb was about to be sealed alive. He'd also used a pen instead of a proper tool. The Authority docked him for improvised implements.

But 34 points was 34 points.

"Move," Dale said. "Slowly. Away from the body."

Webb stumbled backward. The mimic's shell lay split open on the asphalt, revealing the actual organism inside: a pale, segmented thing no bigger than a thumb, its proboscis still curling and uncurling. Dale stepped on it. The Authority didn't register it as a separate elimination. The organism was part of the mimic. One species, one kill.

He was already walking. The Authority's distance marker pulsed in his vision like a countdown. Thirty-eight meters. Thirty-six. The parent organism was moving.

"Where are you going?" Webb said.

"Forty meters that way." Dale pointed north. "There's a storm drain. The parent is host-seeking, which means it's moving toward people. The parking lot has a group of about twenty survivors huddled near the loading dock. If it reaches them, it'll start spawning mimics. You saw what one does. Multiply by twenty."

"Then we should run."

"We should treat."

Webb stared at him. Dale didn't look back. He was already scanning the ground, looking for the entry point. Not the creature. The entry point. The crack in the world where the thing had come through.

Twenty-eight meters. Twenty-six.

He found it. A seam in the asphalt, three inches wide, running parallel to the storm drain grate. Not a crack. A seam. Clean edges, like something had been unzipped. The edges were warm to the touch, which meant it was active. Still open. Still breathing.

The Authority flashed:

STRUCTURAL ANOMALY DETECTED. CLASSIFICATION REQUIRED.

Dale crouched. He ran his fingers along the seam. The edges were smooth, almost oily. No frass. No organic residue. This wasn't a biological entry point. It was a dimensional one. The distinction mattered.

He pulled out his clipboard. Started writing.

Entry point type: non-organic. Dimensional seam. Active. Width: 7.6 centimeters. Length: 1.4 meters. Depth: indeterminate. Surrounding substrate: asphalt, polymer-modified. Substrate shows no thermal degradation. No combustion. No biological vectors.

He paused. The Authority was waiting.

He wrote the classification.

Structural anomaly. Dimensional entry point. Violation category: K-7. Structural breach.

The Authority flashed:

CLASSIFICATION ACCEPTED. VIOLATION LOGGED. STRUCTURAL BREACH, DIMENSIONAL. SEVERITY: CLASS 3.

Class 3. That was above his certification level. Class 3 work required Level IV. He was Level II.

But the Authority had accepted the classification. And that meant the protocol was open.

Dale pulled the roll of duct tape from his belt. Not because it would work. Because it was what he had. He'd been in this business for fourteen years. He'd sealed entry points with expanding foam, with caulk, with silicone, with epoxy, with a combination of hardware cloth and construction adhesive. He'd sealed a crack in a foundation with a tube of toothpaste because it was March and the homeowner's house was freezing and the furnace was broken and the family had two kids and Dale had said, "I'll be back Thursday with the real stuff," and he had been.

This was the same job. Different substrate. Different scale. Same principle.

He pressed the duct tape over the seam. It adhered to the oily edges. The Authority flashed:

CONTAINMENT PROTOCOL INITIATED. STRUCTURAL SEAL. PROGRESS: 12%.

Twelve percent. The tape was holding. The seam was narrowing. On the other side, something pushed. The tape bulged outward, then held. Dale pressed another strip.

Twenty-two percent.

"Help me," he said.

Webb knelt. Pressed a hand over the tape. The thing on the other side pushed again. Webb's hand slid. Dale slapped another strip down.

Thirty-one percent.

The Authority flashed:

PARENT ORGANISM APPROACHING. DISTANCE: 14 METERS. HOST-SEEKING BEHAVIOR INTENSIFIED.

Fourteen meters. It was getting closer because the entry point was closing. It couldn't go back. It needed a host.

Forty percent.

The seam was shrinking. The parent organism's legs appeared at the edge of the narrowing gap. They were wrong. Too many joints. Each one bent backward at an angle that made Dale's stomach turn, but he didn't look. He was looking at the tape. At the adhesion. At the edges.

Fifty-five percent.

"Keep pressing."

"It's moving my hand."

"Keep pressing."

Sixty-three percent. The legs retracted. The thing was trying to push through before the seal closed. Dale added another strip. The Authority registered it.

Seventy-one percent.

A sound from the north. Wet. Rhythmic. Like a boot pulled out of mud, repeated. Getting closer.

Seventy-eight percent.

The Authority flashed:

SEAL INTEGRITY: STABLE. STRUCTURAL BREACH CONTAINED. VIOLATION RESOLVED.

Dale exhaled. He pressed the last strip down. Eighty-four percent. It was holding. The seam was sealed. The parent organism was on this side. Trapped.

He stood up. Turned.

The Authority flashed:

NOTICE: STRUCTURAL BREACH, DIMENSIONAL. THIS CLASSIFICATION REQUIRES CERTIFICATION LEVEL IV. CURRENT CERTIFICATION: LEVEL II.

Dale stopped.

VIOLATION LOGGED. UNLICENSED STRUCTURAL WORK PERFORMED ON CLASS 3 BREACH. THOROUGHNESS RATING SUSPENDED PENDING AUTHORITY REVIEW.

The notification hung in his vision. Red text. He read it twice.

His rating was suspended. Ninety-two percent, gone. The number that had made him the only person in the county qualified for the next tier of threats, gone. Because he'd done the job correctly and the job had been above his pay grade.

"Shit," he said.

Webb was staring at him. "What?"

"I sealed it wrong."

"You just said it was stable."

"I sealed it right. That's the problem." Dale pulled his clipboard close. The Authority's suspension notice was still there, pulsing. Below it, a new line:

THOROUGHNESS RATING SUSPENDED. TREATMENT POINTS HELD. CERTIFICATION PROGRESS FROZEN.

His points. All of them. Held. He'd earned 34 points for the mimic. That 34 points was now in escrow. His Level II progress, which had been at 78% toward Level III, was frozen at 78%. He couldn't advance. He couldn't do Class 3 work without the Authority's permission, and he couldn't get permission without a rating, and he couldn't get a rating because he'd done the work too well.

The wet sound was closer. Eight meters. The parent organism was in the parking lot. It had lost its way back through the sealed seam, and it was looking for a host.

Dale could hear it now. The legs. The wrong joints. The thing was bigger than the mimics. Much bigger. He could feel the vibration through the asphalt.

"Webb."

"Yeah."

"Go to the group. Tell them to move east. Away from the storm drain."

"What about you?"

"I need to document the parent organism. I can't treat it. I'm not certified. But I can document it. Documentation doesn't require certification."

"That's your plan? You're going to take notes while a monster walks toward you?"

"That's my plan."
