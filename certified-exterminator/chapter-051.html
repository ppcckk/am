Chapter 51: The Blank Page

# Chapter 51

The operator's Field Guide was already open. Dale saw it from twenty meters out, the cover angled toward them like a shield. Protocol deployment stance. Left foot forward, guide at chest height, right hand already reaching for the treatment tool.

Dale stopped walking. Tanaka bumped into his shoulder.

"Category Nine," Dale said.

"What?"

"The protocol. Look at the cover angle. That's a Category Nine deployment. They're treating us as a nest-level infestation."

Tanaka squinted at the approaching figure. "What's a Category Nine?"

"Total eradication. No containment.

No isolation.

You just kill everything in the treatment zone. " Dale's jaw tightened. " Including the documentation. Including the entry."

The operator was closer now.

Mid-thirties, maybe. Female.

Carrying a treatment kit on her back that looked heavier than Dale's.

Her Field Guide was open to the page that pulsed with Authority ink. The species name. Self-treating entity.

She'd already identified them. She was already in protocol.

"Stop," Dale called out.

He raised both hands.

Empty. Clipboard in the left.

"Licensed technician, Class II, Dale Kowalski. I'm invoking Primary Documentation Rights under Section 4, Subsection 11."

The operator didn't stop. Her right hand closed around the treatment tool.

A canister, pressurized.

Dale recognized the model. Fumigation-grade. Not a targeted agent.

A broadcast treatment, and she was going to flood the entire commercial strip with a Category Nine agent.

She didn't stop.

Dale stepped in front of Tanaka.

Not heroically. Procedurally. He was the primary identifier on the file.

Section 4, Subsection 11 said the primary identifier could refuse treatment if the deploying technician had not verified chain of custody. She hadn't verified.

She'd read the entry and gone straight to protocol, which was a procedural violation.

The operator stopped. Three meters away.

Her eyes moved from Dale's face to the clipboard in his hand. She looked at the duct tape. Two pages bound together.

"You're the primary identifier," she said.

"I am."

"That's why I'm here. You created the entry. You triggered the classification."

"And you're deploying a Category Nine against a single-species file with two individual hosts. That's a protocol mismatch. You need a targeted agent. You need to identify the nest before you broadcast."

"The Authority classified you as a nest."

"The Authority classified us as a species. Not a nest.

There's a difference." Dale held up the clipboard.

"I documented two separate encounters. Two separate hosts. The system merged them because the documentation was bound together.

That's a filing error, not a biological finding."

The operator's Field Guide flickered. The Authority, reading.

Checking.

Dale watched the page. The species name stayed. Self-treating entity.

But the classification tag beneath it shifted. Nest-level became Individual-level.

The operator's hand relaxed on the canister. She looked at her Field Guide. Looked at Dale.

"Fine," she said.

"Individual-level.

I'll need to deploy targeted agents. One for each host.

Standard protocol."

"You'll need chain of custody transfer first. I'm the primary identifier. You can't deploy against my file without a signed transfer."

She reached into her kit. Pulled out a form.

Standard Authority documentation.

Dale recognized it. Custody transfer. Two signatures.

Primary identifier releases, deploying technician accepts, and she held it out.

Dale didn't take it.

"Read the species name again," he said.

She looked down at her Field Guide. Self-treating entity. The entry was still there. Still pulsing.

"You're going to document the treatment," Dale said. "Standard protocol.

You identify, you document, you isolate, you eliminate, you verify.

The documentation step. When you write your treatment report, you'll be documenting a self-treating entity. You'll be adding your own observations to the file."

"So?"

"Your Field Guide will populate. You'll have a new entry. Your own encounter with a self-treating entity. You'll be the primary identifier on your own documentation."

The operator stared at him. Her hand went still on the form.

"You'll become a self-treating entity," Dale said. "Because you'll be documenting your own treatment of a self-treating entity. The Authority will classify you the same way it classified us."

Silence. The parking lot. The fluorescent buzz of the strip mall sign behind them. Tanaka breathing hard behind Dale's shoulder.

The operator looked at her Field Guide. The entry pulsed. She looked at the form in her hand. She looked at Dale.

"That's not how it works," she said.

"It's how it works. The Authority classifies based on documentation.

You document, you're in.

I documented my own encounter.

That's what made us a species. You document your encounter with us, you're in the same file."

The operator's jaw worked. She looked at the canister on her back. A Category Nine broadcast agent.

Against two individual hosts. Against herself, if the classification spread.

"I need to verify," she said.

She opened her Field Guide to the index. Flipped pages. Fast.

Her lips moved.

Reading.

Dale waited, and he knew what she was looking for. Section 7. Self-referential classification.

The Authority's own rules about entities that document themselves. It was buried in the middle of the index because nobody had ever triggered it before.

She found it. Her face changed.

"There's a provision," she said.

Slow.

"Self-referential entries require a tertiary identifier. A third technician who is not part of the documented encounter.

They verify the classification before it becomes permanent."

"That's us," Dale said. "We're the tertiary identifier for you.

And you're the tertiary identifier for us.

Circular. The Authority can't resolve it without a fourth operator."

"Which would become a fifth."

"Which would become a sixth."

The operator closed her Field Guide.

Opened it again. Closed it.

She was thinking.

Dale watched her weight shift from one foot to the other. Technician's thinking. Running the protocol.

Looking for the exit.

"There's a kill switch," she said.

"What?"

"Section 12. If a self-referential classification cannot be resolved through tertiary identification, the Authority escalates to the next certification level. A Class V operator handles it."

Dale felt cold.

Class V, and he was Class II. The jump from II to V was three exams.

Three practicals. Three years of work, minimum. Unless the Authority waived the prerequisites.

"Which they will," the operator said. "Because the Authority doesn't have a Class V operator in this district. They'll send one. And that operator will arrive with a Category Nine protocol."

"Because they'll treat the whole cluster as a nest."

"Yes."

Dale looked at Tanaka. Tanaka looked back. Their face was the same color as the strip mall paint.

"So what do we do?" Tanaka said.

Dale looked at the operator. She was waiting.

She knew what came next, and she was a technician. She'd already run the protocol. She'd already found the dead end.

She was standing in it, same as them.

"We don't let her document," Dale said.

The operator's head snapped up. "What?"

"If you don't document, you don't become a self-treating entity. The chain stops.

You leave.

We stay classified. The Authority sends a Class V operator. They treat us.

We get exterminated."

"That's the alternative."

"The alternative is you don't document.

You walk away.

We're still a file. Still an infestation.

But the file stays closed. No new entries. No cascade."

"And the Authority?"

"The Authority doesn't care about us. The Authority cares about the file. A closed file is a closed file. They'll move on."

The operator looked at her Field Guide. The entry.

Still pulsing, and she could close the file. Just close it. Walk away.

Let the Authority's system mark the treatment as incomplete.

Incomplete treatments got flagged. Flagged treatments got re-assigned.

Re-assigned to someone else.

Someone who would open the file. Document. Cascade.

Unless the file was closed permanently.

"Deletion," Dale said. "You delete the entry.

Not the treatment.

The entry.

You remove the species classification. We go back to being unclassified.

Unlicensed infestations. The Authority doesn't track those."

"That's a protocol violation."

"Section 3, Subsection 2. Primary identifier can request deletion of an entry created through documentation error." Dale held up the duct-taped pages.

"This is the error.

Two files bound together. The species was an artifact of the binding. Not a real finding."

The operator looked at the pages, and she reached for them. Dale let her take them.

She held them up.

Read both. Tanaka's entry. Dale's entry.

Two separate encounters. Two separate hosts.

Bound together by a strip of gray duct tape, and she pulled the tape off. The pages separated. She held them apart. Looked at her Field Guide.

The entry flickered. The Authority, reading the separation. Re-evaluating.

The species name wavered. Self-treating entity. The letters thinned. The ink pulled back.

It didn't disappear.

It changed.

New name.

New entry. The Authority had a different classification now.

Not self-treating entity. Something else. Dale read it.

His stomach dropped.

Symbiotic treatment agent.

The operator read it too. Her face went worse than Tanaka's.

"That's not us," she said.

"No," Dale said.

"That's you. You're not a self-treating entity. You're the thing that treats self-treating entities.

The Authority reclassified you."

She looked at her Field Guide. The new entry.

Symbiotic treatment agent.

A species that exists only in proximity to self-treating entities, and a species that cannot be separated from the thing it treats. A species that IS the treatment.

"The Authority didn't close the file," Dale said. "It reclassified the treatment vector. You're not coming here to exterminate us. You're part of the infestation now."

The operator's hand moved to her treatment kit. Her fingers found the canister.

She pulled it off her back.

Held it in front of her.

Pressurized. Category Nine.

Broadcast agent, and she looked at Dale. Looked at Tanaka. Looked at the canister.

"If I'm a symbiotic treatment agent," she said. "Then I can't leave. The Authority won't let me leave. I'm part of the file."

"Yes."

"Then I have to treat you."

"Yes."

"Which means I document. Which means I become a self-treating entity. Which means the cascade starts."

"Yes."

The operator set the canister down. She sat on the curb.

She put her Field Guide in her lap, and she opened it to the new entry. Symbiotic treatment agent. She read her own classification.

Dale stood over her.

He had his clipboard. He had the two separated pages.

He had no protocol that worked, and he had no certification high enough to override the Authority's classification. He had one thing.

He had the file.

He sat down next to her. He opened his own Field Guide.

His entry.

Dimensional Vermin.

Rattus profundus. The first thing he'd ever identified.

Fourteen years of work, and this was the page that mattered.

"I'm going to document something," Dale said.

The operator looked at him.

"I'm going to document you. Right now.

As a symbiotic treatment agent.

I'm going to write the full protocol. Identify, document, isolate, eliminate, verify. All five steps.

And when I document you, I'm going to identify the nest."

"What nest?"

"The Authority's nest. The thing that made the classification possible.

The thing that created the self-referential loop." Dale picked up his pen.

"The Authority doesn't have a certification level. It doesn't have a Field Guide entry. It's not in the system.

It IS the system. And the system just told me that the treatment vector is part of the infestation."

He put the pen to paper.

"If the Authority is the nest," he said, "then the nest is unlicensed."

The operator stared at him. Her Field Guide pulsed. Tanaka stared at him. The parking lot went quiet.

Dale wrote the first line.

Infestation type: Unlicensed regulatory entity.

His Field Guide opened to a blank page. The Authority ink started to form. Letters. A species name.

The operator grabbed his wrist.

"Stop.

If you document the Authority as an infestation, every licensed technician in the system becomes a symbiotic treatment agent. Every operator.

Every technician. Every person who's ever held a Field Guide."

"I know."

"That's not two infestations. That's every licensed technician on the planet."

"I know."

"Then the Authority will send a Class V operator."

"The Authority will send a Class V operator to treat every licensed technician on the planet."

The operator let go of his wrist. She looked at the pen.

She looked at the blank page in Dale's Field Guide, and the Authority ink was still forming. Still waiting. The system hadn't committed yet.

It was reading.

Deciding.

Tanaka spoke. Quiet.

"Dale. If you write that, what happens to us?"

Dale looked at Tanaka. Looked at the operator. Looked at the pen.

"We get treated," he said. "By everyone."

The Authority ink stopped forming. The page went blank. The system was waiting. The decision was his.

Dale held the pen above the page.

His hand didn't shake. His hand never shook. Fourteen years of writing reports.

Fourteen years of identifying what was wrong and naming it, and he pressed the pen down.

He did not name this one.
