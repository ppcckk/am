Chapter 262: Protocol Survival

# Chapter 262

White heat.

The shockwave punched the air out of Dale's lungs. He didn't fly backward; he was pinned against the bakery's far wall, the plaster crumbling into grit around his shoulders. His ears rang with a high-pitched whine that drowned out the sirens outside.

Debris rained down. Flour bags. Glass shards. Chunks of iron carapace, blackened and curled like burnt leaves.

Dale coughed. Ash coated his tongue. He wiped it with the back of his sleeve, smearing gray soot across his face.

The room was gone. The ceiling was gone. The ovens were twisted slag.

But the swarm?

Gone.

Not killed. Disintegrated.

A pile of smoking ash lay on the floor, shaped roughly like a mound of disturbed soil.

Dale checked his clipboard. The paper was singed at the edges, the top corner curled black. But the entry remained.

*Iron Beetle Swarm. Eliminated.*

*Status: Total Eradication.*

*Points Awarded: 450.*

*Thoroughness Rating: Pending Assessment.*

He exhaled. The breath came out shaky.

Commander Vander's voice cracked through the ringing in his ears.

"Clear?"

Dale didn't look up. He tapped the clipboard twice.

"Clear," he said. "Though I'd advise caution on the structural integrity. The thermal bloom compromised the load-bearing beams."

Vander stepped through the ruined doorway. His uniform was dusted with white powder. He looked at the ash pile, then at Dale.

"You just blew up a city block," Vander said.

"I vented a contained infestation," Dale corrected. He walked toward the exit, stepping over a twisted oven rack.

"Protocol requires isolation of the breeding source.

The flour dust was the vector. The gas was the accelerator. I neutralized the hazard.

"

Vander stared at him.

"You killed them instantly."

"Efficiency is a metric, Commander."

Dale stepped out into the night air. The street was silent, save for the crackle of cooling metal and the distant wail of sirens. Fire crews were already pulling up, hoses unspooling.

Vander followed him out.

"You're under arrest," Vander said. Not angry. Just tired.

Dale stopped. He turned.

"On what charges?"

"Reckless endangerment. Use of explosives in a residential zone."

"It wasn't an explosive," Dale said. "It was a thermal ignition event induced by natural gas and combustible particulate matter. I did not use an explosive. I used the environment."

Vander rubbed his face.

"You could have waited for the fire department. They have protocols."

"Fire departments treat smoke," Dale said.

"They don't treat nests. If I had waited, the beetles would have dispersed.

They have flight capability, and they would have entered the sewer lines. They would have nested in the foundations of the next three blocks. The infestation would have been uncontainable.

"

Vander opened his mouth. Closed it.

Dale checked his watch.

"I have a court date for the previous citation," Dale said. "I suggest you write the report as 'Authorized Hazardous Material Containment.' It's cleaner."

Vander looked at the ash pile again. He looked at the ruined bakery.

"I don't think the city sees it that way."

"The Authority sees it that way." Dale pointed to the air above his head. A faint, golden notification hovered there.

*Job Completed.* *Certification Level VI.* *Next Tier Unlocked: Subterranean Infestations.*

Vander didn't see it. He saw a man standing in the rubble, holding a clipboard like a shield.

"Go home, Kowalski," Vander said. "And turn yourself in tomorrow."

Dale nodded. He walked away.

He didn't go home, and he went to the sewer grate three blocks down.

The smell hit him first. Rot.

Old grease.

And something else.

Something metallic and sweet.

Dale pulled a gas mask from his belt. He clicked it into place. The seal hissed.

He dropped into the dark, and the ladder was slick. Rats scattered, their eyes reflecting his headlamp. Small, normal rats.

Dale ignored them.

He followed the trail of ash. It didn't stay on the surface. It had been flushed down by the heat wave, pushed into the storm drain system by the pressure of the explosion.

*Cross-contamination risk,* his mind noted. *Using surface-level thermal protocols in a subterranean environment.*

He slowed down.

The System rules flashed in his peripheral vision.

*Warning: Protocol Mismatch.* *Deploying Class-3 Thermal Ignition in Class-4 Wet Environment.* *Debuff: -40% Efficiency.* *Field Guide Entry for Iron Beetle: Contaminated. Access Restricted for 24 Hours.*

Dale stopped, and he looked at the black water flowing in the trench.

He couldn't use fire down here. Methane pockets.

He needed a different tool.

He pulled out a canister. Not gas. Foam. Polyurethane expansion foam, industrial grade.

He sprayed it into the mouth of the side tunnel where the ash trail disappeared.

The foam expanded, sealing the opening, and he sprayed again. And again.

He was building a plug.

*Identification: Iron Beetle Larvae.* *Habitat: Subterranean Moisture Zones.* *Weakness: Hypoxia.*

The Field Guide updated.

*New Entry: Iron Beetle Larva.* *Weakness: Oxygen Deprivation.*

Dale checked his points.

*Points Awarded: 0.*

He hadn't killed anything. He had sealed a hole.

But he had isolated the infestation, and he moved deeper.

The tunnel narrowed. The air grew thick.

He saw them.

Not adults. Larvae.

They were the size of dogs. Legless, segmented, pale and translucent. They writhed in the black water, their mandibles snapping at the foam he had sprayed.

They were slow. They were blind. They relied on vibration.

Dale walked softly, and he pulled a small device from his belt. A sonic emitter. Borrowed from the police tactical unit, confiscated as evidence.

He activated it.

A low hum.

The larvae froze.

Dale watched them. He counted.

Twelve, and he adjusted the frequency.

The larvae vibrated. Their soft tissues rippled.

He cranked it up.

One larva burst.

Fluid sprayed across the walls.

Dale didn't flinch. He kept the emitter steady.

*Targeted Damage Bonus: +15% against Larval Stage.*

The others twitched. They tried to burrow into the mud.

Dale sprayed more foam. He clogged their escape routes. He trapped them in a pocket of expanding chemical.

He watched them suffocate, which took four minutes.

Four minutes of holding a vibrating box while monsters screamed in silence.

When the last one stopped moving, Dale switched off the emitter.

He pulled out his pen.

*Job Complete.* *Protocol: Containment and Asphyxiation.* *Points Awarded: 300.* *Thoroughness Rating: 8.8.*

*Reason for Deduction: Use of unauthorized sonic equipment. Failure to document species count prior to elimination.*

Dale wrote a note on his clipboard.

*Recommendation: Install sonic deterrents in all major sewer junctions.*

He climbed back up, and the city was awake now. Police tape was up. News vans were circling.

Vander was waiting at the manhole.

"You went down there," Vander said.

Dale pulled off his mask. He wiped his face with a rag.

"Documentation," Dale said. "You said arrest me. I wanted to clear my name first."

Vander looked at the clipboard. He saw the entry.

"Iron Beetle Larva."

"You found them?"

"I found the survivors," Dale said. "I didn't kill them. I suffocated them."

Vander shook his head.

"You're insane."

"I'm thorough."

Dale walked past him.

"I'll see you at the station," Dale said.

He didn't go to the station, and he went to his truck.

He got in. He started the engine.

He checked his rearview mirror.

Nothing behind him, and he drove.

He drove to the edge of town. To the old industrial park. The place where the Rift had first touched the earth.

His phone buzzed.

Unknown Number, and he answered.

"Kowalski," a voice said. Not Vander. Not the Authority.

"Who is this?"

"Someone who read your report," the voice said. Smooth. Calm.

"The Thoroughness Rating. 9.2. Then 8.8.

It's inconsistent.

You're hiding something.

"

Dale gripped the steering wheel.

"I'm following protocol."

"Protocol is a cage," the voice said. "The Authority wants you to clean up the mess. They don't want you to understand it."

"I understand my job," Dale said.

"Do you?"

The voice paused.

"Did you notice the ash pattern?"

Dale frowned.

"What ash pattern?"

"The Iron Beetles," the voice said.

"They didn't scatter when you lit the lighter. They waited.

You said they froze.

You said they looked at you.

"

"I noted that."

"Beetles don't look," the voice said. "Beetles don't wait. They react to threat. Unless they're being controlled."

Dale felt a cold knot in his stomach.

"Controlled by whom?"

"Look at the entry," the voice said. "Look at the weakness.

Thermal Ignition.

Combustible Dust.

It's too specific. It's too easy.

Who gave you that weakness?

"

Dale pulled over, and he opened his Field Guide.

*Iron Beetle Swarm.* *Weakness: Thermal Ignition.*

He looked at the timestamp.

He had written it in the air. With his lighter.

He hadn't looked it up. He hadn't researched it.

He had guessed.

And the System had accepted it.

"Who are you?" Dale asked.

"I'm the one who wrote the manual," the voice said. "And someone is rewriting it. Someone is teaching the monsters how to be weak to you. Why?"

Dale hung up.

His hands were shaking, and he looked at the Field Guide entry again.

*New Entry: Iron Beetle Swarm.* *Weakness: Thermal Ignition.*

He tried to delete it.

*Error: Entry Locked.* *Reason: Active Infestation Link Detected.*

He tried to refresh it.

*Error: Connection Lost.*

A new notification popped up. Red this time.

*System Alert:* *Unauthorized Modification Detected.* *Investigating...*

Dale stared at the red text.

His phone buzzed again.

Vander.

"Kowalski," Vander said. "Get to the station. Now."

"I can't," Dale said. "There's a problem with the—"

"The Authority just issued a warrant," Vander said. "For your head. They say you're compromising the integrity of the Field Guide."

Dale looked at the red alert.

*Unauthorized Modification.*

He hadn't modified it. He had discovered it.

Or had he?

He thought about the voice. *Someone is teaching the monsters.*

If the weakness was real, why was it locked?

If the weakness was fake, why had it worked?

Dale started the truck, and he didn't go to the station.

He went to the library.

He needed a hard copy. A physical book on entomology.

He parked. He walked in.

The librarian looked at his soot-stained clothes, and she didn't say anything. She pointed to the science section.

Dale pulled out a heavy volume. *Coleoptera: The Beetles.*

He flipped through the pages.

Iron Beetles.

They weren't in the book. They were a dimensional construct. A mutation.

He looked for thermal properties.

Nothing, and he looked for combustion.

Nothing.

He looked for behavior.

*Many species exhibit thanatosis, playing dead when threatened.*

Thanatosis.

Playing dead.

Dale's blood ran cold.

The beetles hadn't been waiting for him to light the flame.

They had been feigning death.

Or rather, they had been mimicking a state that *he* expected them to be in.

He had projected the weakness, and the System had rewarded the projection.

But the projection was wrong.

The beetles were susceptible to heat, yes. But not because of their biology. Because of the environment.

The gas. The flour.

He had used the environment.

But the Field Guide said *Thermal Ignition*, which was a simplification. A lie.

A lie that the System had accepted.

And now the System was hunting him for lying.

Dale closed the book.

He walked out.

His phone rang.

Unknown Number.

"You see?" the voice said.

"I see," Dale said.

"Someone is feeding you false data," the voice said. "Or someone is feeding the monsters true data. Either way, you're a pawn."

"Who is it?"

"Check your last kill," the voice said. "The Larva."

Dale looked at his clipboard.

*Larva.* *Weakness: Oxygen Deprivation.*

He had suffocated them.

But the sonic emitter.., and he hadn't killed them with sound.

He had used the sound to freeze them. Then the foam.

The foam had killed them.

The System had awarded points for *Asphyxiation*.

But the weakness listed was *Oxygen Deprivation*, which was true.

But was it the *only* way?

"Why are you telling me this?" Dale asked.

"Because you're good," the voice said. "And because the Authority is going to revoke your license tonight. They think you're hacking the System."

Dale looked at the red alert on his phone.

*Revocation Pending.*

"I need to prove it wasn't a hack," Dale said.

"You can't," the voice said. "The data is gone. You only have the memory."

Dale looked up.

Across the street, a black SUV pulled up.

Men in suits got out. They didn't have badges. They had briefcases.

"Run," the voice said.

Dale didn't run, and he stood on the sidewalk.

The men approached.

"Dale Kowalski," the lead agent said. "You are suspended from the Licensing Authority. Surrender your Field Guide."

Dale held up the clipboard.

"It's not mine," Dale said. "It's theirs."

He pointed at the sky.

At the Rift.

"The bugs are coming back," Dale said. "And this time, they won't freeze when you light a match."

The agent reached for the clipboard.

Dale stepped back, and he pulled a flare from his pocket.

He lit it.

The red light flared in the dark street.

The agents hesitated.

Not because of the flare.

Because of the sound.

A low rumble. From the sewers. From the ground.

Thousands of wings.

Not Iron Beetles.

Something bigger.

The ground cracked open.

A hand, made of chitin and bone, reached up.

Dale looked at the agent.

"Identification," Dale said. "Required."

The agent looked at the hand, and he didn't have a clipboard.

Dale smiled.

A small, dry smile.

He clicked his pen.

*New Entry Pending.*

*Threat Level: Unknown.*

*Protocol: Survival.*

The hand grabbed the agent.

Dale ran.
