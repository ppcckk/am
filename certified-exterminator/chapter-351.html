Chapter 351: Trial by Infestation

# Chapter 351

The pen tip dug into the paper, tearing the fiber.

Dale didn't write a plan. He wrote a classification.

*Specimen: Auditor-Host Hybrid. Status: Active Infestation. Vector: Biological.*

The black vein pulsed against his wrist, hot and thick. It wasn't just ink spreading under his skin; it was data. It was the Authority's code, rewriting his biology in real-time. He could feel the syntax of his own cells being edited, lines of DNA deleted and replaced with something rigid and geometric.

Vane tilted his head. The geometric mask rippled, settling briefly into Dale's own tired, thirty-eight-year-old face before shifting back to abstract polygons.

"You're documenting," Vane said. The voice was wet, like gravel grinding in oil. "Typical. You think if you name the demon, you can file it away."

"Identification precedes elimination," Dale said. His voice was flat.

Controlled, and he didn't look at Vane. He looked at the sludge bubbling at the edge of the stone corridor. It was rising.

"Protocol 7-A.

Isolate the source before neutralizing the host. You're telling me the Authority is the infestation.

That's a claim, not evidence.

"

"Evidence?" Vane laughed. The sound shattered against the damp walls. "I am the evidence.

I am the root system you spent three hundred chapters pruning. You cut the branches.

You killed the mites.

You leveled up.

And now? Now you're standing in the root cellar, holding a clipboard while the tree eats you.

"

Dale finished writing. He capped the pen. The click echoed in the silence.

"Your classification is wrong," Dale said. He held up the clipboard. The paper was glowing faintly.

"You're not a root. You're a misidentified entry.

Section 4, Subsection C: Bureaucratic Parasite.

I've seen your kind before. They hide in the paperwork.

"

Vane stepped closer.

The floor didn't liquefy under him. He walked on the sludge as if it were solid ground.

The black veins on Dale's arm were reaching his elbow now.

Numbness was spreading through his left hand.

"Parasite," Vane repeated. "Cute.

But you don't have a Class IX protocol.

You're Class IV.

You have a spray can and a prayer. I have the entire licensing infrastructure.

I *am* the infrastructure.

"

"Then you should know the rules," Dale said.

He tapped the clipboard. A holographic window snapped open in the air, not in his vision, but in the physical space between them. It was ugly, jagged, and flickering. The Authority's interface was struggling against Vane's corruption.

**FIELD GUIDE ENTRY: AUDITOR-CLASS ENTITY.** **WEAKNESS: CONTRADICTION IN LEGAL STANDING.**

Vane paused. The geometric face froze.

"What is that?" Vane asked.

"Rule 12," Dale said. "Cross-contamination penalty.

Deploying a protocol designed for one creature type against another inflicts a 40% debuff.

But here's the loophole you missed, Vane. I didn't deploy a protocol. I updated the Field Guide.

"

"You can't update the Guide in combat," Vane said. The polygons on his face sharpened into spikes. "It's locked during active engagement."

"Not if the engagement is a dispute," Dale said.

He pointed at the hologram.

"I'm challenging your classification. You're claiming to be the Authority.

But the Authority is a licensing body.

It doesn't have a biological form. It doesn't have a face. Therefore, you are not the Authority.

You are an imposter. An unauthorized entity masquerading as a regulatory agency.

"

Vane lunged.

Dale didn't dodge, and he stepped back, pulling a small, silver device from his belt.

It wasn't a weapon. It was a stamp.

The Authority's official Notary Seal, which he'd looted from a previous high-level contractor's corpse in Chapter 290.

He slammed the stamp onto the holographic window.

**CLASSIFICATION CORRECTED.** **TARGET TYPE: UNLICENSED IMPERSONATION OF REGULATORY AUTHORITY.** **PENALTY: IMMEDIATE REVOCATION OF PRETENSE.**

The effect was instant.

Vane screamed. It wasn't a human scream.

It was the sound of a filing cabinet being crushed by a hydraulic press.

The geometric shapes on his face didn't just shift; they unraveled. Threads of black light pulled away from his features, dissolving into the air.

The black vein on Dale's arm stopped pulsing. It receded, inch by inch, retreating from his elbow back toward his wrist.

Vane fell to his knees. The sludge beneath him turned to clear water, then to dry stone. The corruption was being stripped away, not by violence, but by bureaucracy.

"You..." Vane gasped. The voice was smaller now. Human again, but broken. "You used a legal argument as a weapon."

"I used a protocol," Dale said.

He checked his wrist. The black mark was gone.

Just a faint bruise remained.

"You claimed to be the Authority. That gave you power. But power requires legitimacy.

I revoked your legitimacy.

"

Vane looked up. His face was pale, sweaty, and human. He looked like a middle-aged accountant who had just been fired.

"The Authority... it's not gone," Vane whispered.

"You didn't kill it.

You just... disqualified me. The real Authority is still there.

It's in the walls. It's in the system. You can't stamp it out.

"

"Watch me," Dale said.

He didn't raise his voice. He didn't smile. He just looked at Vane with the detached interest of a technician inspecting a faulty circuit board.

"Why?" Vane asked. Tears streaked his face.

"Why do you care?

You're a pest control guy. You spray for ants. Why fight the god of the system?

"

"Because the ants don't pay their taxes," Dale said.

"And when the system is corrupt, the infestations get worse. You let the mites get bad.

You let the rats get big.

Because you were busy playing games with your own ego.

"

Vane shook his head.

"It's not ego.

It's maintenance.

The Authority needs... chaos.

To test the licensees. You were the best test.

The most thorough. And now you're the one who can't be pruned.

"

"I'm not unprunable," Dale said. "I'm just better at paperwork than you."

A new notification pinged in Dale's peripheral vision. It wasn't the usual blue.

It was red.

Bold. Urgent.

**ALERT: SYSTEM INTEGRITY COMPROMISED.** **UNLICENSED ENTITY DETECTED: ROOT ACCESS.** **THREAT LEVEL: CATACLYSMIC.**

Dale ignored it. He looked at Vane.

"You said I invited it into the mainframe. What mainframe?"

Vane smiled, which was a sad, terrifying smile.

"The world, Dale.

The world is the mainframe.

And you just rebooted it.

"

The stone corridor shook. Dust rained down from the ceiling. The holographic window flickered and died.

"The Authority is waking up," Vane said. "And it's angry.

It didn't like being challenged.

It didn't like being audited.

You broke the fourth wall of the bureaucracy, Dale. And now the walls are coming down.

"

Dale picked up his clipboard. The paper was blank again. The correction had been temporary. The system had reset.

"What does that mean?" Dale asked.

"It means the next exam isn't written," Vane said. "It's practical. And you're not the examiner anymore. You're the subject."

A crack appeared in the wall behind Dale. Not stone. Glass.

Through the glass, Dale saw a city. Not Ohio. Not any city he knew.

It was a sprawling, infinite metropolis of spires made of bone and light. And crawling through the streets, filling the sky, were things that looked like Dale's old Field Guide entries, but scaled up to the size of skyscrapers.

Giant rats with wings. Beetles the size of buses. Cockroaches with human faces.

The infestation wasn't local. It was global. It was dimensional. And it was leaking through the cracks Vane had made.

"Stop it," Dale said. He raised the Notary Seal. "I can revoke the dimension. I can stamp it out."

"You can't," Vane said. "Because you're still in the system. You're still a licensee. And licensees don't get to close the store."

The glass shattered.

A hand reached through. It was made of black sludge, but it wore a suit. A white shirt. A tie.

It grabbed Dale's arm, and the grip was cold. Hard. The black vein returned instantly, but this time it didn't pulse. It locked.

**STATUS: LOCKED.** **PROTOCOLS DISABLED.** **CERTIFICATION SUSPENDED.**

Dale tried to pull away. His muscles didn't respond. The System wasn't responding to his commands. It was overriding him.

"See?" Vane said, standing up. He brushed dust off his torn suit. "You're not the exterminator anymore, Dale. You're the pest."

The thing with the suit pulled Dale through the shattered window.

Dale didn't scream. He looked at his clipboard one last time. The blank page was gone.

In its place, a single line of text appeared, written in the hand of the Auditor.

*NEXT STEP: TERMINATE LICENSEE.*

Dale hit the ground of the bone-city. The air smelled of ozone and old paper. Thousands of monsters turned to look at him.

He didn't have a weapon. He didn't have a protocol. He didn't have his certification.

He had a pen, and he clicked the cap off.

And he started writing.

Not a report. Not a plan.

He wrote a complaint.

*To: The Licensing Authority.* *From: Dale Kowalski, Level IV Technician.* *Subject: Unlawful Detention and Improper Protocol Application.*

The monsters didn't attack. They paused. They waited.

Because in this world, even the monsters respected the paperwork.

But the thing in the suit was already moving. It raised a hand, and the air around it twisted into sharp, geometric blades.

Dale looked at the blades. He looked at the pen.

He had ten seconds before the blade hit his throat, and he had one sentence left to write.

He didn't write a defense.

He wrote an appeal.

*I demand arbitration.*

The blades stopped.

The world froze.

And from the sky, a voice spoke. Not Vane's. Not the Auditor's.

It was the voice of the Authority itself. And it sounded bored.

**ARBITRATION REQUEST ACCEPTED.** **BUT BE WARNED, LICENSEE.** **YOU ARE NOW ON TRIAL.** **AND THE JUDGE IS THE INFESTATION.**

The courtroom opened beneath Dale’s feet.
