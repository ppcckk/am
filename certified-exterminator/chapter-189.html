Chapter 189: Verified Contamination Protocol

# Chapter 189

Gray spread across Dale's knuckles. The texture was wrong. It felt like cured leather, smooth and cold, devoid of the fine lines and pores that defined human skin.

He didn't scream. Screaming consumed oxygen he couldn't spare.

Dale kicked the brake pedal. The truck shuddered to a halt in the middle of the intersection, blocking the flow of emergency vehicles that were already converging on the city center. Horns blared from behind, a discordant symphony of panic, but Dale ignored the noise. He looked at the clipboard.

The word *VERIFIED* pulsed in wet, black ink.

His Field Guide flickered open in his peripheral vision. The entry for *Architectural Chitin* was no longer just text. It was a live feed. The Authority had linked his biometric data to the infestation's growth metrics.

**STATUS: CONTAMINATION PROTOCOL ACTIVE.** **SUBJECT: DALE KOWALSKI (CERT LEVEL II).** **DIAGNOSIS: SYMPATHETIC RESONANCE DETECTED.** **ACTION REQUIRED: IMMEDIATE ISOLATION AND DOCUMENTATION.**

Dale's jaw tightened. The gray skin crept up his wrist, stopping at the cuff of his uniform.

It wasn't a transformation, which was a tagging. The Authority had marked him as part of the infestation because he had failed to maintain a sterile perimeter during the previous encounter.

He wasn't infected. He was *filed*.

The passenger door flew open. A woman in tactical gear slid into the seat, her rifle leveled at Dale's chest. Her visor reflected the gray patches on his face.

"Step out of the vehicle, Technician," she said. Her voice was filtered through the helmet's comms unit, flat and metallic. "You are a biohazard."

Dale didn't move, and he kept his hands on the wheel. The gray skin on his right hand had hardened into a plate, fused to the steering wheel.

He couldn't let go.

Not physically. But he could control the pressure.

"Officer," Dale said. His voice was raspy, the vocal cords stiffening under the chitinous pressure. "I am not the threat. I am the diagnostic tool."

"The Authority issued a suspension," the woman said. She shifted her weight, adjusting her aim.

"Level 9 Incident.

You are a contaminant.

I have orders to terminate if you resist.

"

"Termination yields zero Treatment Points," Dale said. "And zero clearance."

The woman hesitated. Her finger hovered over the trigger.

"What?"

"Read the protocol," Dale said. He pointed with his left hand, the only one not yet fully encased in gray. "Cross-contamination penalty.

If you deploy Class-4 incendiary rounds on a Class-9 bio-mass that is currently resonating with a licensed technician, you trigger a systemic backlash. The Authority will fine your entire unit.

They will revoke your squad's license.

"

Silence in the cab. The woman stared at the gray plate on Dale's face.

"You're bluffing," she said.

"Check your HUD," Dale said. "Look at the Thoroughness Scale. Look at the penalty warning."

The woman blinked. She glanced at her heads-up display. A red box had appeared, flashing rapidly.

**WARNING: CROSS-CONTAMINATION RISK.** **TARGET IDENTIFICATION: FAILED.** **PENALTY ESTIMATE: -40% EFFECTIVENESS.** **ADMINISTRATIVE RECALL LIKELY.**

She lowered the rifle, an inch. Then two.

"How did you know?" she asked.

"I didn't," Dale said. "I read the fine print. Section 4, Paragraph C. 'Inadvertent collateral damage to licensed personnel during active certification tracks is classified as an Administrative Error, not a Combat Loss.'"

The woman looked at Dale, then at the city center. Smoke rose from the Hub, thick and black. The streetlights were flickering, pulsing in a rhythm that matched the heartbeat Dale felt in his own chest.

The building was alive, which was breathing.

"The Hub is the nest," the woman said. "But the Authority says you're compromised."

"I am verified," Dale corrected. "That is different from compromised. Verification means the Authority recognizes my connection to the source. It means I can see what they cannot."

The woman pulled out a datapad.

Her fingers flew across the screen.

"I'm pulling your file. If this doesn't hold up, I shoot you.

If it does, you walk in. But you do not engage.

You observe."

"I require a Form 11-B," Dale said. "Standard Containment Waiver. Signed by the incident commander."

The woman stared at him.

"You want a waiver? While your face is turning into armor?"

"I want a waiver," Dale said.

"Or I don't go in. And if I don't go in, the Hub consumes the grid.

And then the Authority fines everyone for infrastructure failure. Which is a higher penalty than administrative error.

"

The woman swore under her breath. She tapped the screen. A notification pinged on Dale's vision.

**FORM 11-B APPROVED.** **SIGNATORY: CAPTAIN E. VANCE.** **CONDITIONS: OBSERVATION ONLY. NO DIRECT COMBAT. NO TREATMENT PROTOCOLS.** **PENALTY FOR VIOLATION: IMMEDIATE REVOCATION.**

Dale exhaled. The gray plate on his cheek flexed.

"Go," the woman said. "Get out. Move fast."

Dale opened the door. His right leg felt heavy.

The chitin had spread to his boot, fusing the rubber sole to the metal pedal assembly.

He had to rip the boot free, leaving a jagged hole in the leather. He stepped onto the asphalt, balancing on one foot.

The air outside smelled of ozone and rot.

The pavement beneath his feet was soft. He looked down.

The asphalt was bubbling, rising in slow, rhythmic waves, and the city was digesting the road.

Dale walked toward the Hub. He didn't run.

Running increased heart rate, which accelerated the spread of the gray skin.

He had calculated the rate of conversion, and he had approximately forty minutes before his vocal cords failed. Sixty before his legs locked.

He passed a squad of soldiers setting up barricades. They watched him with a mixture of awe and revulsion. He ignored them. He was focused on the Field Guide.

A new entry had appeared.

**SPECIES: URBAN PARASITE (SUBSTRATE: CONCRETE/STEEL).** **WEAKNESS: IDENTIFICATION FAILURE.** **NOTE: THIS SPECIES FEEDS ON UNDOCUMENTED THREATS.**

Dale stopped. He looked at the soldiers. They were armed, ready to fight.

But they hadn't scanned the environment. They hadn't filled out the intake forms.

They were just standing there, waiting for orders.

To the *Urban Parasite*, they were invisible. Which meant they were vulnerable.

Dale pulled out his clipboard. The paper was still wet with black ink, but he found a clean section. He wrote.

**DATE:** October 14. **LOCATION:** Sector 4, Main Ave. **SUBJECT:** Unauthorized personnel presence. **OBSERVATION:** Squad Alpha fails to maintain perimeter documentation. Risk of substrate absorption increased by 80%. **RECOMMENDATION:** Immediate withdrawal.

He held the clipboard up, not to show the soldiers, but to show the air. The gray skin on his arm twitched. The *Urban Parasite* responded to documentation.

It fed on chaos.

It starved on order.

One of the soldiers, a young man with a terrified expression, looked at Dale's clipboard.

"Sir?" he asked. "What are you doing?"

"Documenting," Dale said.

"You're in a blind spot. The building is watching you because you haven't registered your presence with the Authority. You're just a body.

No file."

The young man paled. He checked his own HUD.

**ERROR: LOCATION NOT LOGGED.** **WARNING: BIO-HAZARD PROXIMITY.**

"Move back!" the young man shouted. "Form up! Check your logs!"

The squad scrambled. They pulled out their datapads, frantically entering their coordinates. As they did, the bubbling asphalt around them slowed. The rhythmic pulsing of the ground decreased.

Dale nodded. It worked.

The loophole was in the definition of

"active engagement." The Authority considered documentation a form of combat. By forcing the soldiers to file reports, Dale was engaging the threat. He was starving it of the unrecorded anxiety it fed on.

But the Hub was still growing. The gray on Dale's face reached his ear. He couldn't hear the sirens anymore. He could only feel the vibration of the city's heartbeat.

He reached the perimeter of the Hub. The building was a monolith of black concrete, but it was wrong. The windows were too many, shifting and rearranging themselves. The doors were not doors; they were orifices.

Dale stood before the main entrance. A barrier of police tape fluttered in the wind. He stepped over it.

A voice boomed from the building's speakers. It was not a human voice. It was the grinding of tectonic plates.

**"TECHNICIAN. YOU ARE VERIFIED. ENTER."**

Dale stepped inside, and the lobby was vast.

The floor was made of the same black chitin as his skin.

It pulsed. In the center of the room stood a figure.

It was tall, wearing a suit that looked like it was woven from fiber-optic cables. It had no face, just a smooth, gray plate similar to Dale's.

It held a clipboard.

Dale stopped. The figure raised its hand.

**"YOU ARE LATE,"** the figure said. Its voice was Dale's own, filtered through static. **"THE NEST IS COMPLETE. THE INFESTATION IS TOTAL. YOU ARE NOW PART OF THE STRUCTURE."**

Dale looked at the figure. He looked at his own clipboard. He looked at the gray skin on his arm.

He realized the trap.

The Authority hadn't sent a confirmation. It had sent a *template*.

By identifying himself as the technician who could see the nest, Dale had accepted the role of the *Anchor*. The *Architectural Chitin* didn't need a power source.

It needed a witness.

A certified observer to validate its existence. Without an observer, the infestation was theoretically non-existent, and thus, unlocatable.

But with Dale, it was real. And because it was real, it could be treated.

Or it could be *promoted*.

The figure stepped forward.

"Your certification is Level II. This infestation is Level 9.

To treat it, you must ascend.

To ascend, you must integrate.

"

Dale looked at the Field Guide. The entry for *Architectural Chitin* had changed.

**NEW WEAKNESS: OBSERVER SATURATION.** **NOTE: THE INFESTATION CANNOT SUSTAIN MORE THAN ONE CERTIFIED OBSERVER.**

Dale's eyes widened, and he wasn't the victim. He was the battery.

If he stayed, he would become the Hub. The city would be consumed, but the infestation would be stable. Dale would live, as a node in the network.

But if he died, the observer was lost. The infestation would lose its anchor. It would collapse into a non-localized cloud, spreading uncontrollably to every unmonitored square foot of the city.

The Authority had set a paradox.

Kill the observer, and the city dies of neglect. Keep the observer, and the city dies of integration.

Dale looked at the figure. The figure looked back with its faceless plate.

"Make your choice, Technician," the figure said. "Document the end. Or become it."

Dale raised his clipboard. His hand trembled. The gray skin had reached his elbow.

He opened the report. He wrote.

**DATE:** October 14. **SUBJECT:** Self-Reported Certification Upgrade. **STATUS:** PENDING, and he didn't choose to die. He didn't choose to integrate.

He chose to *appeal*.

The Authority hated appeals. They were slow.

They were bureaucratic nightmares, and they required a human review panel.

A panel that had to physically come to the site to inspect the anomaly.

Dale wrote: *I am invoking Section 12, Clause 4: Disputed Identity. I request a third-party verification of my certification status before final integration.*

The figure paused. The grinding voice stopped.

**"REQUEST LOGGED,"** the figure said. **"PROCESSING TIME: 72 HOURS. DURING THIS PERIOD, INFESTATION GROWTH IS PAUSED. YOU REMAIN ANCHORED."**

Dale felt the gray skin stop spreading. It froze at his shoulder.

He had bought time.

But the figure stepped closer. It reached out a hand made of black glass.

"Three days," the figure said. "The city will hold its breath. And when the inspectors arrive, they will not find a technician. They will find a monster wearing a uniform."

Dale looked at his clipboard. The ink was drying. The word *PENDING* was fixed in place.

He looked up at the figure.

"I'm not a monster," Dale said. "I'm an employee."

He tapped the side of his head, activating the emergency recording function on his uniform.

"Say that again," Dale said. "For the record."

The figure tilted its head. The air in the lobby grew heavy, pressing against Dale's eardrums. The floor beneath him cracked, a spiderweb of black lines spreading out from his feet.

**"YOU ARE THE ERROR,"** the figure said. **"AND ERRORS ARE CORRECTED."**

The floor gave way.

Dale fell.
