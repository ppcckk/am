Chapter 406: Found by the Swarm

# Chapter 406

"Move your left foot," Dale said, not looking up from the clipboard. His pen hovered over a blank field labeled *Structural Integrity: Substrate B*.

"You're standing on a load-bearing root.

If it snaps, the ceiling collapses on us. I don't have the budget for a rescue operation.

"

Gary Miller shifted his weight, wincing.

The black veins crawling up his neck pulsed in time with his heartbeat, a slow, viscous rhythm that reminded Dale of a dripping faucet.

"It hurts," Gary whispered.

His voice was thin, scraped raw by smoke and screaming.

"It feels like something is rewriting my bones."

"Standard reaction to Phase II integration," Dale replied, scribbling a note. *Subject exhibits acute biological rejection. Recommend immediate isolation.

Cost: High.* "The Authority doesn't care about comfort.

It cares about containment.

You're a vector. Vectors don't get to complain about the plumbing.

"

From the shadows of the tunnel exit, a voice crackled, distorted by static and authority.

"Kowalski. Stop writing. Report status."

Dale didn't flinch. He capped his pen. The sound of the click echoed in the damp concrete corridor.

"Status is clear. Primary nest neutralized.

Secondary brood contained in Sector 4.

But the client is compromised. I need a hazmat extraction team. Class IV minimum.

My current loadout is Class II.

If I touch him, I contaminate my Field Guide, and you know what that does to my Thoroughness Rating.

"

The drone descended, hovering three feet above Dale's head.

Its single red eye scanned Gary, then Dale.

"Extraction is denied. The surface breach is active.

You are the only licensed personnel within a ten-mile radius. Complete the protocol.

Or we terminate the contract.

"

"Terminate the contract," Dale repeated, his voice flat. "You mean kill us."

"I mean close the file. Sloppy work results in zero points. But termination results in zero points *and* zero existence. Choose."

Gary looked up, eyes wide and watery.

"Dale, don't listen to it. Just walk.

We can make it to the street. My truck is parked there.

"

Dale shook his head slowly. He pointed the clipboard at the wall behind them, where a faint, shimmering rift still hung in the air, leaking gray mist.

"Look at the perimeter. See the residue?"

Gary squinted.

"It's just fog."

"It's dimensional excretion," Dale corrected. "If we cross that threshold without a neutralizing agent, the Authority registers it as an unsealed entry. You know the penalty for unsealed entries."

Gary swallowed.

"Decontamination fees.

They take everything.

My house. My car. My name.

"

"Exactly.

And since you're infected, the fee calculation includes your biological assets. Which means they harvest you.

" Dale turned to face the drone.

"I need a neutralizer.

I don't have one. My kit is for arthropods, not human-carrying symbiotes. I'm a pest control tech, not a biochemist.

"

The drone's eye blinked.

"Improvisation is permitted. Section 4, Paragraph C: *Resourcefulness overrides inventory.*"

Dale stared at the machine. Then he looked at Gary. He looked at the dead monster's corpse in the corner. He looked at the puddle of black blood pooling near the exit.

"I need to speak to the client," Dale said.

The drone recoiled slightly, a subtle tilt of its chassis.

"The client is the Authority."

"The *real* client," Dale said. "Gary Miller.

He's the one paying the bill, even if he doesn't know the price.

And he's the one holding the infection.

If I can't stabilize him, I can't seal the breach. And if I can't seal the breach, I can't file the report.

If I can't file the report, the Authority can't certify the job. And if the Authority can't certify the job, the system fails.

"

Dale walked toward Gary. The arachnids that had been trailing them skittered back, their legs clicking on the wet floor.

They recognized the shift in Dale's posture, and he wasn't attacking. He was managing.

"Gary," Dale said, crouching down. He pulled a small notebook from his vest. "When you went into the tunnel, did you see anything before the spiders?"

Gary shook his head, then stopped.

"Wait. Yes.

A light.

Blue. Coming from the center. It wasn't a fire.

It was... structured. Like a lattice.

"

Dale's eyes narrowed. He wrote it down. *Source: Blue Lattice. Likely mana-dense substrate.*

"Did you try to touch it?"

"No.

I pulled my hand back.

But I felt it. It felt like... static electricity.

But warmer. It made my teeth ache.

"

Dale flipped through his Field Guide. The pages were thick, filled with sketches of chitinous legs, mandibles, and weak points. He found the page for *Arachnida Profundus*. He traced the diagram of the central nervous cluster.

"The lattice," Dale muttered. "It's not a power source. It's a relay."

He stood up, turning back to the drone.

"Query: Does the Authority recognize *Resonant Mana Lattices* as a form of structural contamination?"

The drone paused. For a full second, the silence was absolute. Then, a chime sounded.

*Query Recognized. Category: Environmental Hazard. Class: III.*

Dale smiled. It was a small, tight expression, devoid of joy.

"So it's a violation.

It's not just a monster problem. It's a zoning violation. The rift is leaking energy into the local grid.

"

"Correct," the drone said. "And?"

"And if it's a zoning violation, it requires an environmental remediation protocol. Not a combat protocol." Dale held up his clipboard. "I'm declaring this site a *Hazardous Material Zone*. Under Protocol 7-B, any personnel entering a HAZMAT zone without proper shielding must be treated as contaminated evidence."

Gary looked confused.

"What does that mean?"

"It means I don't have to cure you," Dale said. "I have to contain you. There's a difference."

Dale walked over to the dead monster. He grabbed one of its severed legs. It was long, segmented, and still twitching with residual bio-electricity. The black blood on it glowed faintly blue.

"Gary, hold still."

"Are you insane?" Gary yelled, backing away.

"Hold still, or I'm not covering you. If I don't cover you, the Authority harvests you. If I cover you, we both get to walk out with our licenses intact. Pick a side."

Gary froze. The black veins under his skin flared brighter.

"Don't touch me with that thing."

"I'm not touching you.

I'm bridging the circuit.

" Dale knelt and placed the severed leg on the floor, aligning it with the blue residue on the ground. "The lattice reacts to bio-electricity.

Your infection is bio-electric.

The monster's nervous system is a conductor. If I connect them, I create a feedback loop. A short circuit.

"

"That's not a treatment," the drone said. "That's destruction."

"It's a *containment field*," Dale corrected. "Look."

He dragged the leg across the blue line on the floor. A spark jumped.

The air smelled of ozone and rot.

Gary screamed as his veins lit up, the blue light traveling from the floor, up the leg, and into his body.

But instead of spreading, the light concentrated. The black veins hardened, turning into brittle, glass-like structures.

The drone's eye widened.

"System Error. Unregistered Protocol."

"It's registered," Dale said, breathless. "It's called *Improvised Grounding*. Section 4, Paragraph C.

I'm not killing the monster. I'm grounding the charge.

The infection is trapped in the physical substrate.

It can't move. It can't spread. It's a statue.

"

Gary gasped, his body rigid. He looked like he was encased in clear ice. His eyes were still moving, terrified, but alive.

"Is it stable?" Dale asked the drone.

The drone scanned Gary.

"Vital signs: Critical. Status: Contained.

Biological movement: Zero.

Threat Level: Reduced to Class I.

"

"Then the job is done," Dale said, pulling a pen from his vest. He wrote, his hand shaking slightly.

"Infestation neutralized via environmental grounding.

Secondary vector stabilized.

No further treatment required. Cost: High.

Reward: Full Certification Credit.

"

The drone hovered closer.

"You exploited the ambiguity of the 'Hazardous Material' clause."

"I read the manual," Dale said, not looking up. "Page 402. Footnote 3. *'In the absence of specific containment tools, local materials may be used to facilitate energy dissipation, provided the operator assumes liability for structural damage.'*"

"You caused structural damage," the drone noted, gesturing to the cracked wall where the spark had hit.

"I noted it," Dale said. "I took the penalty.

My Thoroughness Rating drops by 0.5.

I don't care. A 9.0 is better than a 0.

"

The drone was silent. Then, a new message projected onto the wall.

*Job Closed. Thoroughness Rating: 8.5. Certification Level: II Confirmed.*

*Dale Kowalski. You have passed the practical exam.*

Dale exhaled, and he straightened his back. His hands were trembling, but he kept them steady by gripping the clipboard.

"Good.

Now, where is the extraction team?

"

"There is no extraction team," the drone said.

Dale froze. "What?"

"The Authority does not provide services. The Authority certifies. You are now a licensed professional. You are responsible for post-job cleanup and client recovery."

Gary's glass-encased body groaned, a low, grinding sound. The blue light was fading. The black veins were returning, but slower. Slower, but present.

"He won't last," Dale said. "The grounding is temporary.

The lattice is still active.

If I leave him here, he dies.

If I take him to the surface, the rift spreads.

"

"Then you have a choice," the drone said. "You have your license. You have your reputation.

You have your career. But you have a carrier.

A carrier is a liability.

"

Dale looked at Gary. Gary couldn't speak. He could only blink.

"I need to close the rift," Dale said.

"To close the rift, you need a catalyst," the drone said. "The catalyst is usually the source. But the source is gone.

The alternative is a sink.

A vessel that can absorb the dimensional energy and hold it.

"

Dale understood. The System was cold. It was bureaucratic. And it was cruel.

"If I absorb it," Dale said, his voice hollow, "I become the carrier."

"If you absorb it," the drone said, "the job is fully closed.

The rating becomes 10.0. You advance to Level III immediately. You gain access to Class III protocols.

You become a Certified Exterminator.

"

"And if I don't?"

"Then the rift remains open. The infestation continues. And you fail the final phase. Your license is revoked."

Dale looked at the clipboard, and he looked at the score. 8.5.

It was good. It was excellent.

It was the difference between a man who cleaned up messes and a man who ended them.

But it wasn't the end.

"Gary," Dale said softly. "I'm sorry."

Gary's eyes widened. He knew. He had known since the tunnel. He had known he was fuel.

Dale stepped forward. He placed his hand on Gary's chest. The glass casing was warm.

"System," Dale said. "Initiate Protocol: Voluntary Absorption."

*Warning: Voluntary Absorption is irreversible. Side effects include: Permanent dimensional instability, loss of human physiological norms, and accelerated aging. Do you wish to proceed?*

Dale looked at the arachnids. They were watching him. Waiting.

He looked at Gary.

"I'm a professional," Dale said. "I don't leave a job unfinished."

*Proceeding.*

Pain, white and absolute, ripped through Dale's nerves. It wasn't like the monsters. It wasn't like the cuts or the stings. It was like being erased.

The blue light from the floor rushed into his arm. It traveled up his shoulder, into his chest, into his heart.

He fell to his knees.

The rift in the wall collapsed. The gray mist vanished. The tunnel was just a tunnel.

Dale gasped, spitting black blood onto the concrete. He looked at his hands.

The veins were black.

And they were spreading.

The drone hovered directly in front of his face.

*Job Complete. Thoroughness Rating: 10.0.*

*New Status: Certified Exterminator.*

*Next Phase: The Authority requires a witness for the Level III Exam.*

*Subject: Dale Kowalski.*

*Task: Hunt the one who opened the rift.*

Dale stood up. His body felt heavy, wrong. He could hear the heartbeats of the rats in the walls. He could taste the electricity in the air.

He looked at Gary. Gary was unconscious, but breathing. The infection had receded.

Dale picked up his clipboard. His hand was shaking, but he wrote one word.

*Found.*

He walked toward the exit. The arachnids followed, but they didn't wait for a command anymore. They moved with him.

Because he was one of them now.
