Chapter 16

The spray did nothing.

Dale knew it would do nothing the moment the nozzle left his thumb. The mist hit the creature's carapace and beaded off like water on wax. The thing didn't flinch. It didn't slow. It kept coming up out of the soil with that forward-opening mouth and the segmented legs working the dirt like a drill.

He sprayed again. Three seconds. The Authority logged it.

TREATMENT ATTEMPT: INEFFECTIVE. CLASS I AGENT AGAINST CLASS III TARGET. NO DAMAGE APPLIED. FIELD GUIDE REMAINS EMPTY.

He set the can down.

The thing was four feet from the porch steps. Behind it, the flower bed had split into three distinct fissures, each one widening by the inch. The neighbor's driveway was cracking in a spiderweb pattern that Dale recognized instantly. He'd seen that pattern before. Not in a monster context. In a concrete context. Root intrusion. Subgrade shifting. The kind of thing that preceded a slab heave.

The soil wasn't just opening. It was failing.

He looked at the things coming up. Four now. Maybe five. They moved with the same orientation toward him, toward the house, toward whatever thermal signature they were tracking. No fear response. No territorial display. Pure resource acquisition behavior.

Reyes was still on the phone. She said something. He didn't hear it.

He was looking at the soil.

The Authority notification changed.

STRUCTURAL ENTRY POINT IDENTIFIED. TREATMENT CATEGORY: PRE-TREATMENT. CERTIFICATION REQUIRED: LEVEL II. STATUS: ELIGIBLE.

Dale read it twice.

Pre-treatment. Not elimination. Not a protocol for the creatures. A protocol for the entry point. The same protocol he used when a duplex had a gap under the back door and German cockroaches were moving through the subfloor. You didn't spray the roaches. You sealed the gap.

He grabbed the clipboard.

The things were at the steps now. The first one pulled itself onto the porch with a sound like wet cardboard tearing. Its mouth opened forward and Dale saw the inside of it. Layered. Like a lamprey. Like a woodworm's frass trail turned inside out.

He stepped back. Not running. Repositioning. He'd done this a hundred times with wasp nests. Back up, get the angle, get the seal.

He looked at the fissures. The largest was six inches wide at the top, tapering down into darkness. The soil around it was loose, granular, freshly displaced. He could see the edges of the split were clean. No root intrusion. No settling pattern. This was a fresh cut. A dimensional cut, sure, but structurally it behaved like any other entry point.

He needed a sealant.

He looked at his truck. He looked at the porch. He looked at the roll of duct tape in his work belt. Not enough. Not for a six-inch gap that was still widening.

The first thing on the porch turned toward him. Its mouth opened. A sound came out. Not a scream. A vibration. Low frequency. Dale felt it in his molars.

He grabbed the trash bag from the porch hook. Heavy-duty contractor grade. Three mil. He'd used these for crawlspace vapor barriers. Not a sealant, but a physical barrier. Temporary.

He threw it over the fissure.

The Authority flashed.

PRE-TREATMENT ATTEMPT: PHYSICAL BARRIER. CLASS III ENTRY POINT. BARRIER RATING: INSUFFICIENT. SEAL INTEGRITY: 12%. RECOMMENDED ACTION: UPGRADE SEAL MATERIAL.

Twelve percent. The thing under the bag was pushing up. The plastic stretched.

Dale looked at his truck again. In the bed, under the tarp: expanding foam. Two cans. The polyurethane kind he used for gap sealing around pipe penetrations. Not rated for dimensional breaches. Not rated for anything above Class I.

But the Authority didn't care about the rating on the can. It cared about the protocol.

He grabbed both cans.

The second thing was on the porch now. The third was climbing the railing. Reyes shouted something. A car alarm started two houses down.

Dale shook the first can. Cold. Half-full. He pressed the nozzle against the edge of the fissure and pulled the trigger. The foam hit the soil and expanded. It filled the gap. It pushed against the plastic bag from underneath and the bag bulged upward like a blister.

The Authority logged it.

PRE-TREATMENT: POLYURETHANE FOAM. CLASS I MATERIAL. CLASS III ENTRY POINT. SEAL INTEGRITY: 34%.

Thirty-four. Not enough. But the foam was still expanding. He hit the edges. The sides. The smaller fissures. Each one he sealed added a percentage.

The second thing on the porch lunged. Dale sidestepped. It hit the railing and the wood cracked. He didn't look at it. He was on his knees, foam in the soil, working the nozzle along the largest fissure like he was caulking a window frame.

The foam expanded around the thing that was trying to climb through. It didn't kill it. It trapped it. The segmented legs splayed against the expanding polyurethane and the thing thrashed and the foam hardened around its midsection and it stopped moving.

The Authority flashed.

ENTRY POINT SEAL: 61%. RECOMMENDED: COMPLETE SEAL TO 90%+ FOR STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY.

He grabbed the second can. Full. He worked the remaining fissures. The one in the driveway. The one by the sidewalk. Each one he sealed, the Authority updated the number. Seventy-three. Eighty-one. Eighty-eight.

The things that had already emerged were agitated. They'd lost their connection to whatever was below. They moved differently now. Faster. More erratic. The one that had been climbing the railing dropped and came at him.

Reyes hit it with a broom.

The Authority flashed one more time.

SEAL INTEGRITY: 91%. STRUCTURAL ENTRY POINT: SEALED. TREATMENT STATUS: PRE-TREATMENT COMPLETE. TREATMENT POINTS AWARDED: 8. THOROUGHNESS SUB-RATING: 6.4.

Eight points. Less than he'd gotten for the broodmother. But the seal held. The soil stopped moving. The fissures were closed. The foam had hardened into a pale yellow crust across the front yard.

The orphaned creatures were still out there. Five of them. No nest. No reinforcement. No way back.

And they knew it.

They were all oriented toward the house now. Toward the heat. Toward the only food source left.

Dale stood up. His knees ached. The foam had gotten on his boots and it was already curing.

Reyes was breathing hard. The broom was splintered.

"How many are left?" she said.

"Five."

"Can you kill them?"

He looked at the Authority notification.

CLASS III TARGETS: 5. CERTIFICATION REQUIRED: LEVEL V. STATUS: UNLICENSED. ATTEMPTING ELIMINATION: FIELD GUIDE CONTAMINATION. CERTIFICATION REVOCATION.

"No," he said.

A truck pulled up. Not Reyes's car. Not a police car. A white utility truck with a county seal on the door and a rack on top that held equipment Dale recognized. Sprayers. Bait stations. Monitoring traps.

A man got out. Mid-forties. Sunburned neck. Class IV patch on his shoulder. The Authority's certification insignia, four tiers above Dale's.

The man looked at the foam. Looked at the sealed ground. Looked at the five creatures that were now circling the house in a pattern that Dale recognized as a search behavior loop.

"You sealed the entry point," the man said.

"Yes."

"That's not protocol."

"It's in the Authority's system. Pre-treatment category. Level II eligible."

The man looked at his own notification. Dale could see the glow of it in the man's eyes.

"That's not protocol for Class III," the man said. "You're supposed to identify, document, isolate, eliminate, verify. In that order. You skipped to structural containment without completing identification."

"The Field Guide was empty. I couldn't identify."

"Then you document. You observe. You wait for the Authority to populate the entry."

"The Authority said DO NOT ENGAGE. I engaged. I sealed the entry point. The system accepted it."

The man was quiet for a moment. He looked at the sealed ground. He looked at the creatures.

"You created a residual population," he said.

Dale looked at the Authority notification.

RESIDUAL POPULATION: 5. CLASS III. ORPHANED. NO NEST ACCESS. AGGRESSION LEVEL: ELEVATED. TREATMENT REQUIRED: CLASS V CERTIFICATION. DEADLINE: 72 HOURS.

The man read the same notification. Dale could see it in his face.

"Seventy-two hours," the man said. "You've got seventy-two hours before these things start breeding in the residual population and you've got a secondary infestation on your hands that requires Level VII."

"I sealed the entry point."

"The entry point is sealed. The population is not eliminated. You sealed the door and left the roaches inside the walls. Now they're going to find a new entry point. Or they're going to reproduce in the walls."

Dale looked at the five creatures. They were still circling. Still searching. The one with the broom splinter in its carapace had stopped moving. The others were adapting. Changing their search pattern. Expanding it.

"What's your name?" the man said.

"Kowalski."

"Kowalski, you just dropped your Thoroughness Rating from 9.2 to 6.4 on a single job. Do you know what that means?"

Dale knew what it meant. He'd seen the scale. He knew where 6.4 sat. It was below the threshold for autonomous job assignment. It meant the Authority would assign him a supervisor. It meant his next three jobs would be audited. It meant if he dropped below 5.0, he'd be pulled from the certification track entirely.

"It means I'm on review," Dale said.

"It means you've got seventy-two hours to eliminate five Class III creatures without a Field Guide entry, without a damage bonus, and without a protocol. And if you don't, the secondary infestation gets classified as a Level VII event and the Authority sends someone who's going to solve it the way the Authority wants it solved."

"Which is?"

The man looked at the sealed ground. He looked at the foam. He looked at Dale like he was looking at a code violation.

"By killing everything in the house."

The man walked to his truck. He opened the back. He pulled out a spray rig that Dale recognized. Professional grade. Class III rated. The nozzle was twice the size of Dale's can. The tank was forty gallons.

He walked back toward the house.

"I'm taking over," he said.

Dale looked at the Authority notification.

JOB STATUS: TRANSFERRED. NEW LEAD TECHNICIAN: MARTINEZ, R. (CLASS IV). ORIGINAL TECHNICIAN: KOWALSKI, D. (CLASS II). ROLE: SUPPORT.

Support.

Dale looked at his clipboard. He looked at the sealed ground. He looked at the five creatures that were now pressing against the side of the house, testing the foundation, looking for another way in.

He looked at Martinez's spray rig. Class III rated. The right tool for the job. The certification to use it. The Authority's blessing.
