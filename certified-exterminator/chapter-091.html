Chapter 91: Contagious Truth

# Chapter 91

The revolving door at the Statehouse entrance was broken. Specifically, it was jammed with a mass of translucent, gelatinous tendrils that pulsed with a low, subsonic hum. Dale Kowalski stepped up to the brass housing, inspected the friction point, and pulled a small notepad from his vest.

He did not draw a weapon. He did not cast a spell. He wrote down the dimensions of the obstruction.

*Obstruction: Biological. Non-euclidean geometry. Adheres to vertical axis.*

Behind him, the crowd of protesters and journalists surged forward, held back by a line of state troopers who looked increasingly ill. Their uniforms were stiff, but their eyes were wide. They had been told to maintain order, and had not been told how to fight things that looked like melted clocks.

Dale tapped the glass of the revolving door with his knuckles. The tendrils recoiled, hissing like steam.

"Hey!" a trooper shouted, stepping out from behind a police barrier. He pointed a baton at Dale.

"You can’t be here.

This is a crime scene. You’re interfering with an active investigation."

Dale looked up.

His expression was flat. He held up his clipboard.

"I’m here for an emergency audit.

Assignment ID 99-Alpha. Governor’s office. Infestation type: Political."

The trooper blinked. He looked at the gelatinous horror clogging the door. He looked at Dale’s clipboard. He looked back at the horror.

"Get lost, man," the trooper said, his voice cracking. "Whatever that thing is, it’s eating the hinges. You need to go."

"I need entry," Dale said. "And you’re creating a safety hazard.

That slime is corrosive to the brass finish.

If it reaches the motor assembly, the whole system seizes.

That’s a code violation. K-7 equivalent."

The trooper hesitated. The bureaucratic reflexes of the state were still stronger than the panic, even if only barely. He glanced at the jammed door, then at the clipboard, and didn't understand the paperwork, but he understood the fear of liability.

"Wait here," the trooper said. He walked back to the command post.

Dale did not wait. He knew he wouldn't get in through the front. The Authority notification had been specific: *Emergency Audit.* Not *Secure the Building.* Not *Kill the Monsters.* *Audit.*

Audits required access. And access, in a government building, required a bypass.

Dale walked to the side of the building, where the service entrance for maintenance staff was located. It was chained. The lock was a standard padlock.

Dale did not pick it. He didn't need to.

He applied a thin layer of solvent to the chain links, then used a small hammer to tap the padlock body.

Not to break it, but to align the tumblers through vibration. It was a trick he’d learned in high school from a kid who liked stealing bicycles. It worked.

The chain fell away. Dale slipped inside.

The interior of the Statehouse was silent.

Too silent. The air tasted of copper and dust.

The marble floors were slick with condensation.

Dale moved carefully, his boots making no sound. He checked his Field Guide. The entry for *Licensing Authority Enforcers* glowed in his peripheral vision.

*[WEAKNESS: PROCEDURAL RIGIDITY.]* *[NOTE: THEY CANNOT ACT WITHOUT A VALID CITATION. ALWAYS ASK FOR THE STATUTE.]*

Dale adjusted his grip on his spray tank. It was empty. He didn't need chemicals for this job. He needed words.

He took the service elevator up. The buttons were smeared with a black residue. He pressed *G*. *Governor’s Floor.*

The elevator lurched, then stalled. The lights flickered. A shadow detached itself from the corner of the elevator car.

It wasn't a rat. It wasn't a bug. It was a humanoid shape, wearing a suit that was too large for its frame. The face was a smooth, white mask with no features except a single, vertical slit where the mouth should be.

**[SUBJECT: AUTHORITY ENFORCER (CLASS V)]** **[STATUS: OBSTRUCTING ACCESS.]** **[THREAT LEVEL: HIGH.]**

The Enforcer raised a hand. A beam of red light shot out, scorching the metal wall beside Dale’s head.

Dale didn't flinch. He didn't dodge. He stood perfectly still, his clipboard held tight against his chest.

"Identify yourself," Dale said. His voice was steady, professional.

The Enforcer tilted its head. The slit widened. A voice came out, not from the mouth, but from the air itself. It sounded like grinding gears.

**"UNLICENSED ENTITY DETECTED. CEASE MOVEMENT. SUBMIT TO TERMINATION PROTOCOL."**

"I am here on official Authority assignment," Dale said. He held up his phone.

The screen displayed the assignment notification.

"Emergency Audit.

Governor’s Office. I am exercising my right to inspect."

The Enforcer paused. The red light in its hand dimmed slightly.

**"INSPECTION REQUIRES VALIDATION. PRESENT YOUR LICENSE."**

Dale tapped his screen. A digital badge appeared. It was the Level IX certification he had earned in the previous arc. But the assignment required Level X.

**"LICENSE INADEQUATE. CERTIFICATION LEVEL INSUFFICIENT FOR THIS JURISDICTION. DENIAL OF ENTRY."**

The Enforcer raised its hand again. The red light intensified.

Dale felt the heat on his face. He knew he couldn't fight this thing. Not physically.

Its armor was immune to kinetic damage. His previous protocols were useless.

He needed a loophole, and he needed to exploit the *procedural rigidity* noted in his Field Guide.

He looked at the Enforcer. He looked at the assignment notification.

There was no Level X. The system stopped at IX. That was the bug. But every bug had a feature.

"Wait," Dale said. "I am not claiming to be Level X. I am claiming to be a delegate of the Authority. I am here to audit the *Enforcers*."

The Enforcer froze.

**"CLARIFY."**

"Your Thoroughness Rating," Dale said.

He remembered the leader from the last chapter. He remembered the scar on his own chest.

"You failed your last audit.

Your rating is 2.1. That is below the minimum threshold for independent operation. According to Protocol 14-B, any Enforcer with a rating below 3.0 must be supervised by a Level IX technician during active field deployments."

The Enforcer’s mask twitched. The gears in its voice slowed.

**"SUPERVISORY REVIEW IS NOT CURRENTLY SCHEDULED."**

"It’s mandatory," Dale said. He pointed at the ceiling.

"The Authority has issued an Emergency Audit.

That triggers an automatic supervisory review of all active Enforcers in the vicinity.

I am here to conduct that review. You cannot obstruct a supervisory inspection."

The Enforcer stood motionless. The red light in its hand flickered. It was processing the logic.

It was trapped by its own programming. It could not attack a supervisor.

It could not ignore an audit.

But it could not verify the audit either, because it didn't have the clearance to see the assignment details.

"VERIFY AUTHORITY,"** the Enforcer said. ** "PROVIDE STATUTE."**

Dale took a deep breath, and he didn't have the statute. He didn't know if Protocol 14-B even existed.

He was bluffing.

He was using the one thing that worked on monsters: the assumption that rules were universal.

"Statute 9-4-1," Dale said, reciting numbers he had made up on the spot, hoping they sounded real. "Supervisory Override. Immediate compliance required."

The Enforcer’s mask cracked. A thin line of smoke rose from its head.

**"ERROR. STATUTE NOT FOUND IN LOCAL CACHE."**

"It’s in the remote server," Dale said. "You’re off-network.

That’s a violation.

That’s why I’m here.

To fix your connection. Move, or I cite you for unauthorized disconnection from the Authority Grid."

The Enforcer lowered its hand. The red light vanished. It stepped aside.

Dale exhaled. His hands were shaking. He hid them behind the clipboard.

He walked past the Enforcer. The elevator doors opened.

The Governor’s office was empty. The desk was overturned. Papers were scattered across the floor. But the walls were moving.

The wallpaper was peeling. Beneath it, a dark, viscous substance pulsed. It spread like mold, but it moved with intent. It crawled along the crown molding, down the curtains, and pooled on the floor in front of the Governor’s chair.

Dale stepped into the room. The air was thick. It tasted like copper and regret.

He looked at the puddle. It wasn't just slime. It was data.

He could see numbers floating in the black liquid. Credits.

Favor ratings.

Bribe amounts.

**[SUBJECT: POLITICAL INFESTATION (CORRUPTION)]** **[STATUS: ACTIVE.]** **[WEAKNESS: EXPOSURE.]** **[NOTE: FEEDS ON SECRETS. DENY IT DATA.]**

Dale stared at the weakness. *Deny it data.*

He walked to the desk. He picked up a piece of paper. It was a transcript of a phone call. The Governor was discussing zoning laws with a developer.

The black puddle surged toward him. It touched the paper. The paper dissolved. The puddle grew larger.

Dale dropped the paper. He didn't fight it. He didn't attack it. He opened his Field Guide.

He started writing, but the monster was not the subject. He wrote about the environment.

*Observation: Infestation thrives on opacity. It uses hidden information as a nutrient source. To eliminate, one must illuminate.*

Dale pulled out his flashlight. He didn't turn it on. He looked at the puddle.

"You want secrets?" Dale said.

The puddle rippled.

Dale opened his mouth. He didn't scream. He didn't curse. He started reading.

He read the phone transcript aloud. He read every name. Every dollar amount. Every dirty trick.

The puddle shrank.

It hated the light.

It hated the truth. Not because truth was moral, but because truth was public.

Public knowledge diluted the power of the secret.

The infestation fed on the *uncertainty*, the *whisper*, the *unspoken*. By speaking it,, and loudly, Dale was poisoning it.

He kept reading. His voice was monotone, professional. He treated the corruption like a code violation.

"Clause 4, Section B. Conflict of interest. Failure to disclose."

The puddle bubbled. It started to evaporate. Smoke rose from the black liquid.

Dale felt a surge of power. Treatment Points were ticking up.

**[TREATMENT POINTS: +50]** **[TREATMENT POINTS: +50]** **[TREATMENT POINTS: +100]**

But then, the door burst open.

The Enforcer from the elevator rushed in. Behind it came the trooper from the lobby.

And behind them, a man in a sharp gray suit, and he didn't look like a monster.

He looked like a lawyer. He looked like a politician.

He smiled. It was a cold, thin smile.

"Stop," the man said.

Dale stopped reading. He looked at the man.

"You’re not an auditor," the man said. "You’re a liability."

The Enforcer pointed at Dale. ** "THREAT LEVEL: EXTREME. AUTHORITY REVOKED." **

Dale looked at his phone. The assignment notification had changed.

**[STATUS: INVALID. USER: DALE KOWALSKI. REASON: UNAUTHORIZED EXPOSURE OF CLASSIFIED MATERIAL.]**

He hadn't just killed the infestation. He had broken the rules. He had exposed secrets that the Authority itself had sanctioned.

The Authority wasn't just a game master. It was a regulator.

And regulators protected their interests.

The black puddle wasn't gone. It had retreated into the walls. It was hiding.

The man in the gray suit walked forward. He held out a hand.

"Give me the Field Guide," the man said. "And the phone. Or I will have you terminated for Contamination of State Secrets. That’s a capital offense in this jurisdiction."

Dale looked at the Enforcer. The Enforcer looked back. Its mask was cracked, but it was still standing. Waiting for an order.

Dale looked at the scar on his chest. It was burning hot.

He realized then that the Authority didn't reward thoroughness if the thoroughness threatened the Authority; the system had a ceiling. And he had just hit it.

He had one move left.

Dale smiled. It was the first time he had smiled in this chapter.

"You can't take my Field Guide," Dale said. "It’s property of the Authority. And you just lost your license."

The man in the suit frowned. "What?"

"I cited you," Dale said. He held up his clipboard. "Statute 9-4-1.

You obstructed a supervisory inspection.

You are now subject to audit."

The man laughed. "You can't audit me. I’m not a monster."

"You're an infestation," Dale said. "Type: Political. Weakness: Exposure."

Dale pulled out his phone. He didn't unlock it. He smashed it against the desk.

The screen shattered. But the notification remained. It glowed through the cracked glass.

**[BROADCAST INITIATED: ALL CHANNELS.]**

The room went dark. The lights exploded. The Enforcer screamed. The man in the suit screamed.

Dale didn't scream. He stepped back into the shadow.

The black puddle surged from the walls. It didn't attack Dale. It attacked the man.

Because the man was the source.

Dale watched as the infestation consumed its host. He didn't help. He didn't run. He pulled out a fresh sheet of paper and a pen.

He started writing.

*Report 91-A: Containment Failure.

Host: Governor’s Aide. Cause: Self-feeding loop. Solution: Isolation.

Next step: Identify the next node.*

He looked at the door. The troopers were still there. They were looking at the screaming man, then at Dale.

Dale walked out, and he passed the troopers. He passed the Enforcer, which was lying on the floor, its mask broken, its red light dying.

He walked to the elevator.

His phone was broken. His license was revoked. His certification was suspended.

But his Field Guide was open, and a new entry had populated.

**[SUBJECT: DALE KOWALSKI]** **[STATUS: ROGUE.]** **[WEAKNESS: NONE.]** **[NOTE: DO NOT ENGAGE. REPORT TO SUPERIOR.]**

Dale looked at the entry. He crossed out *ROGUE*.

He wrote *INDEPENDENT CONTRACTOR*.

Then he hit the button for the ground floor.

The elevator doors closed.

Dale was alone. He had no license. He had no Authority. He had no backup.

But he had a job to do, and the Rift was still open. The infestation was still growing. And now, the only thing between the world and the dark was a man who refused to stop writing reports.

He looked at his hands. The scar on his chest pulsed.

*CASE OPEN.*

Dale Kowalski started the van, and he drove south.

He didn't know where he was going. But he knew he had to find the next nest.

And this time, he wasn't working for the Authority.

He was working for the truth.

And the truth was contagious.
