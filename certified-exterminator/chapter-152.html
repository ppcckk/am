Chapter 152: Recruited by the Authority

# Chapter 152

The hand closing around his throat was cold, dry, and rigid. Not flesh. Carapace.

Dale couldn't breathe. The white oval on Sarah's face pulsed in rhythm with his fading heartbeat. He clawed at her wrist, his fingernails scraping uselessly against the chitin forming under her skin. Behind her, the faceless figure in the white suit held its clipboard steady, waiting.

*Thoroughness Rating dropping.*

The notification burned in the corner of his vision. *Current Rating: 8.9. Penalty for failure to identify target: -0.1 per second.*

Sarah's jaw unhinged. A wet, clicking sound erupted from her throat, vibrating through Dale's ribs.

She wasn't trying to strangle him, and she was trying to feed. The System had protected her, yes, but protection didn't mean safety. It meant she was a liability.

A biohazard.

Dale's eyes watered. His pen, still clutched in his right hand, hovered inches from his own neck.

*Legal Protections Active.*

He couldn't kill her. But he could process her.

His left hand fumbled in his vest pocket. His fingers brushed the emergency sealant tube.

Not a weapon.

A tool. Class II protocol required isolation before elimination. If he couldn't eliminate, he had to isolate.

Dale didn't try to push Sarah away. He didn't try to scream for help. He twisted his body, slamming his head back against the concrete floor of the alleyway. The impact jarred his teeth, but it broke the seal of her grip just enough for a gasp of air to rush into his lungs.

He rolled.

Sarah shrieked, a sound like tearing metal, and lunged again. Dale scrambled onto his knees, spitting blood. He didn't look at the faceless figure. He looked at the puddle of red fluid pooling around Sarah's feet.

**SPECIES IDENTIFICATION: SARAH KOWALSKI (INFECTED).** **STATUS: ACTIVE INFESTATION VECTOR.** **ACTION: CONTAINMENT.**

The text flashed green. Then red. Then green again.

**ERROR: SUBJECT CLASS AMBIGUOUS.** **RETRY REQUIRED.**

"Identify the vector!" Dale gasped, his voice a ragged rasp. He pointed his pen not at Sarah, but at the slime trailing from her mouth onto the asphalt.

The System hesitated. The faceless figure tilted its head.

It was testing him.

This wasn't a monster fight, which was an audit.

Dale's mind raced, bypassing fear, bypassing grief. He saw the pattern. The white oval on Sarah's face wasn't just a mask.

It was a sealant breach. The infection wasn't in her blood.

It was on her surface.

The "Sarah" that remained was a husk, a host shell. The true infestation was the colony forming *on* her.

He didn't try to write

"Sarah Kowalski" again. That name triggered the legal protection. He needed to describe the *thing* wearing the name.

He wrote fast, his handwriting jagged and shaky.

**TARGET: PARASITIC OUTFIT (GENUS: PSEUDO-SARAH).** **VECTOR: DERMAL LAYER.** **STATUS: NON-SENTIENT HUSK.**

The System paused. The red warning vanished.

**IDENTIFICATION ACCEPTED (CONDITIONAL).** **NOTE: HUMAN HOST DETECTED. COLLATERAL DAMAGE PENALTY APPLIED.** **TREATMENT POINTS: 0 (UNTIL VERIFIED).**

The legal protection dropped. The

"Sarah" tag disappeared from the UI. In its place, a new label: **INFESTATION NODE: LIVING.**

Sarah screamed. It wasn't a scream of pain. It was a scream of confusion. The human part of her was still trapped inside the chrysalis, aware enough to be horrified by what her body was becoming.

Dale didn't hesitate. He ripped the cap off the sealant tube with his teeth and squeezed the entire contents onto Sarah's chest.

The foam hit her midsection and hardened instantly into a white, expanding block. Sarah's limbs seized. The chitin cracking along her arms slowed. The white oval on her face flickered, trying to expand against the chemical barrier.

The faceless figure stepped forward. Its clipboard raised.

"Efficient," the figure said. Its voice was smooth, lacking any gender or inflection. "But inefficient."

Dale wiped blood from his chin.

"She's contained."

"She is *paused*," the figure corrected. "Containment requires structural isolation. You have applied a topical agent to a mobile vector. This is a Class-1 violation of Protocol 44-B: *Stabilization of High-Value Hosts*."

The System flashed a new notification.

**VIOLATION LOGGED.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 8.4.** **PENALTY: FIELD GUIDE LOCKED FOR 24 HOURS.**

Dale's stomach dropped.

His Field Guide. The source of his damage bonuses.

The source of his knowledge.

Locked. He couldn't identify the weak points of the creatures behind him. He couldn't exploit the weaknesses of the tall, shadowy thing that had been watching from the darkness.

He looked past Sarah. The tall creature stepped out of the shadows.

It wasn't a monster, which was a man, or something that used to be a man.

Tall. Thin.

Wearing a hazmat suit that looked like it had been stitched together from hospital sheets and duct tape.

Behind him, three more figures emerged. They moved with the jerky, disjointed gait of the infected.

"Dale Kowalski," the figure said. "I am Auditor Vane. I am here to certify your termination."

Dale stood up. His legs shook. His throat throbbed. He looked at the faceless figure, then at Auditor Vane.

"You're not the Authority," Dale said. "The Authority sends inspectors. They carry badges. They have serial numbers."

"The Authority is busy," Auditor Vane said. "The backlog is critical. We are subcontractors."

The System flashed.

**NEW ENTITY DETECTED: SUBCONTRACTOR (UNLICENSED).** **WARNING: THESE ENTITIES DO NOT ADHERE TO STANDARD TREATMENT PROTOCOLS.** **TREATMENT POINTS: 0.5 PER ELIMINATION (FLAT RATE).**

Dale laughed. A dry, barking sound.

"Half a point?" he said. "That's a rounding error. That's not enough to level up. That's not enough to get back my Field Guide."

Auditor Vane smiled. The faceless figure beside him nodded.

"Precision is expensive, Dale," Vane said. "Speed is cheap. We offer speed."

Vane raised a hand. The three infected figures behind him lunged. They weren't attacking Sarah. They were attacking *Dale*.

Dale didn't have his Field Guide. He didn't know their weaknesses. He didn't know if they were fungal, bacterial, or dimensional. He was flying blind.

He had one tool left. The clipboard.

He swung it like a shield, catching the first infected creature in the chest. The clipboard cracked. The creature didn't stop. It swatted the debris aside and continued its charge.

Dale stumbled back. He looked at the faceless figure.

"If I kill them," Dale said, breathing hard, "do I get points?"

"You get points," the faceless figure said. "But you lose rating. Killing without documentation is sloppy. Sloppy work is contaminating."

"Then I document," Dale said.

He turned his back on Vane. He walked toward Sarah.

The infected creatures hesitated. They were confused. Why was the technician walking toward the containment unit?

Dale reached into his belt and pulled out a marker. He uncapped it. He walked right up to the frozen, foam-encased Sarah. He wrote on the white foam.

**SPECIMEN ID: SK-001.** **VECTOR: DERMAL.** **STATUS: DORMANT.**

The System flashed.

**DOCUMENTATION COMPLETE.** **TREATMENT POINTS: 15.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING RESTORED: 8.6.** **FIELD GUIDE LOCK LIFTED (EARLY RELEASE DUE TO CORRECTIVE ACTION).**

Dale's vision cleared. The map of the alley returned to his mind.

The weaknesses of the infected creatures flooded back into his memory. *Joint decay.

Sensory overload. Structural fragility in the skull.*

He spun around.

The first infected creature was two feet away. Its arm was raised to strike.

Dale didn't dodge. He stepped *into* the strike.

He jammed his thumb into the creature's eye socket.

Not a punch. A precision insertion.

He twisted.

The creature shrieked, a wet, gurgling sound, and collapsed.

**ELIMINATION VERIFIED.** **TREATMENT POINTS: 20.**

The second creature lunged. Dale sidestepped, grabbing its wrist.

He pulled, and he used the creature's momentum to slam it into the brick wall.

The skull cracked. The brain matter sprayed against the brick.

**ELIMINATION VERIFIED.** **TREATMENT POINTS: 20.**

Auditor Vane watched, unmoved. The faceless figure scribbled on its clipboard.

"Two kills," Vane said. "Fast. Sloppy. You didn't isolate the third."

The third creature paused, confused by the sudden violence. It looked at its fallen comrades. It looked at Dale.

Dale didn't attack it. He walked toward it.

"I'm not killing you," Dale said. "I'm processing you."

He reached out and touched the creature's shoulder. The skin was gray, flaking.

He spoke into the air, loud and clear.

"Third specimen. Male. Approximately thirty years post-infection.

No visible chitin. Neural pathways intact.

Potential for rehabilitation?

"

The System flashed.

**QUERY: UNKNOWN STATUS.** **RECOMMENDATION: TERMINATE.**

"No," Dale said. "Query: Isolate."

The faceless figure stopped writing. It looked at Dale.

"You are attempting to quarantine an active combatant," the figure said. "This is not protocol."

"It's *my* protocol," Dale said. "Level II technicians are authorized to assess viability. If this one is viable, I don't waste resources killing it. I contain it."

Auditor Vane frowned. For the first time, the smooth mask of the subcontractor slipped.

"Viable?" Vane said. "Look at him, Dale. He's a corpse.

He's walking because the fungus is piloting the nerves.

He's not alive.

"

"Show me the diagnostic," Dale demanded.

The System hesitated.

**DIAGNOSTIC REQUEST: HIGH COST.** **COST: -1.0 THOROUGHNESS RATING.**

Dale didn't hesitate.

"Confirm."

**THOROUGHNESS RATING: 7.6.**

A beam of blue light scanned the third creature. The creature screamed, dropping to its knees.

**DIAGNOSTIC RESULT:** **HOST VIABILITY: 12%.** **NEURAL INTEGRITY: CRITICAL.** **RECOMMENDATION: TERMINATE IMMEDIATELY.**

12%.

Dale stared at the number. It wasn't zero.

"He's still in there," Dale whispered.

Auditor Vane stepped forward. The air in the alley grew cold. The shadows lengthened.

"Viability is irrelevant," Vane said. "If it is less than 50%, it is waste. Waste is destroyed. This is the law of the Exterminator."

Dale looked at the kneeling creature. It was crying. Actual tears, black and viscous, running down its cheeks.

"It's not waste," Dale said. "It's a patient."

Vane shook his head.

"You are failing, Dale. Your rating is dropping. The Authority does not tolerate sentimentality."

The faceless figure raised its pen.

"Termination order," the figure said. "Execute."

Dale looked at the third creature. He looked at Sarah, frozen in her foam prison. He looked at the faceless figure.

He looked at the crack in the wall behind Vane. A crack that hadn't been there before. A crack that was pulsing with purple light.

**NEW INFESTATION DETECTED.** **CLASS: V.** **NAME: VOID-BREACH.**

Dale's eyes widened. Class V. A Rift event. A dimensional tear.

Vane didn't see it. Or he didn't care.

"Execute," Vane repeated.

Dale raised his clipboard. He didn't aim at the third creature. He aimed at the crack in the wall.

He wrote.

**ACTION: SEAL ENTRY POINT.**

The System flashed.

**ERROR: CLASS V THREAT EXCEEDS CLASS II CAPACITY.** **CANNOT SEAL.**

"Then I redirect," Dale said.

He slammed his palm against the brick wall next to the crack. He didn't try to close it. He tried to *tag* it.

**MARK: ENTRY POINT.** **STATUS: ACTIVE.** **DANGER LEVEL: EXTREME.**

The crack widened. A tentacle of black smoke shot out. It didn't go for Dale. It went for Auditor Vane.

Vane's eyes went wide. The faceless figure stepped back, dropping its clipboard.

"You marked it?" Vane hissed. "You marked a Class V breach?"

"I documented it," Dale said. "And I pointed it at you."

The smoke wrapped around Vane's legs. The subcontractor screamed. Not in pain. In fear.

The faceless figure looked at Dale. Its faceless head tilted.

"Interesting," it said. "You are using the environment as a weapon. This is... unorthodox."

The smoke pulled Vane into the crack. The Auditor was swallowed whole. The three infected creatures, freed from Vane's command, collapsed into heaps of twitching flesh.

Dale stood alone in the alley.

The faceless figure picked up its clipboard. It walked toward Dale.

"Your Thoroughness Rating is 7.4," the figure said. "You have eliminated two threats, contained one, and redirected a third. You have also violated three protocols and incurred a debt of one full rating point."

Dale wiped his hands on his pants.

"I'm still standing."

"For now," the figure said. "But the Authority has noticed your actions. You are flagged."

The figure handed Dale a new card, which was black. Not white. Not blue.

Black.

**NOTIFICATION:** **LICENSE SUSPENDED.** **REASON: UNAUTHORIZED USE OF ENVIRONMENTAL HAZARDS.** **PENALTY: DEMOTION TO CLASS I.** **EXAMINATION: MANDATORY. IMMEDIATE.**

Dale stared at the card.

Class I, and he was back to square one. No protocols.

No special tools. Just a pen and a clipboard.

"Go back to your office," the figure said. "The examination is tomorrow morning. If you fail, you are terminated. If you pass, you are reinstated."

The figure turned and walked into the shadows. The crack in the wall sealed itself, the purple light fading.

Dale stood there. Sarah was still frozen in her foam. The third creature was still kneeling, weeping black tears.

He walked over to the creature. He didn't kill it. He didn't quarantine it.

He sat down next to it.

"12%," Dale whispered.

The creature looked at him. Its eyes were milky, but they focused.

"Help," it croaked.

Dale looked at his black card. He looked at the System interface.

**THOROUGHNESS RATING: 7.4.** **NEXT LEVEL: 10.0.** **REQUIRED POINTS: 500.**

He had 55 points, and he needed 445 more. And he had no Field Guide. And he was demoted.

And tomorrow, he had to pass an exam that determined if he lived or died.

Dale pulled out his pen. He wrote on the ground, in the dust.

**PLAN: REBUILD.**

The creature watched him.

"Can you write?" Dale asked.

The creature shook its head.

Dale nodded. He stood up. He looked at Sarah. He looked at the alley.

He started walking toward the exit. He didn't look back.

Because if he looked back, he might see the faceless figure still watching. And if he saw it, he might have to admit that the Authority hadn't just flagged him.

They had recruited him.
