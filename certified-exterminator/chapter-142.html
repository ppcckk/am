Chapter 142: Faceless Performance Review

# Chapter 142

The pen snapped.

Plastic shards scattered across the linoleum. Dale didn't look down. His eyes remained fixed on the faceless figure, which had closed the distance in a blur of gray fabric and impossible physics. The air around it rippled, distorting the light of the overhead lamp into jagged, bleeding streaks.

Vane chewed. Slowly. Deliberately.

"Documentation," Dale said. The word came out flat, stripped of panic. "You filed a false report, Vane. That's a Class Four infraction."

The figure stopped three feet from the coffee table. It didn't have hands, just tapered nubs of gray matter that ended in sharp, geometric points. A low hum emanated from its torso, vibrating the loose change on the counter.

*Thrum. Thrum. Thrum.*

Dale's clipboard slipped from his sweaty palm. He caught it with his left hand, fingers white-knuckled.

His right hand hovered over the vial of frass.

The powder was inert now, useless against something that wasn't biological. Or was it?

*Is it?*

He looked at the frass. Brown. Fine-grained. *Rattus profundus*. Dimensional rat droppings.

He looked at the figure. No eyes. No mouth. Just a smooth, featureless mask that reflected his own terrified, angry face back at him.

The System notification burned in the corner of his vision.

**ACTION: DOCUMENT OR FLEE.** **STATUS: STALLED.**

Stalled. The System was waiting. It didn't know what to do with a faceless bureaucrat from a non-human dimension. It didn't have a protocol for *Bureaucratic Horror*.

Dale stepped forward. One step.

The figure lunged again. Faster this time.

Dale didn't dodge. He raised the clipboard.

The gray nubs slammed into the plastic board.

The clipboard cracked down the middle. Dale grunted, absorbing the impact through his forearm.

Pain shot up his elbow, hot and sharp, but he held his ground.

He jammed the broken halves of the clipboard against the figure's chest, creating a barrier.

The figure shrieked. A sound like tearing metal.

Dale kicked the coffee table. It slid across the floor, colliding with the figure's legs. The creature stumbled. Its form flickered, losing cohesion for a split second.

"Vane!" Dale shouted. "Your employee is violating workplace safety standards!"

Vane swallowed his bite of ham and cheese. He wiped his mouth with a napkin.

"It's not my employee.

It's a sub-contractor.

You know how the licensing authority works. They outsource the hard stuff."

Dale grabbed the vial. He uncapped it with his teeth.

The frass spilled onto the cracked clipboard. A cloud of brown dust rose.

Dale threw the dust.

It wasn't an attack. It was a test.

The particles hit the faceless figure. They didn't pass through. They stuck.

The brown powder clung to the gray surface, outlining the shape of the entity. A head.

Two shoulders.

Arms. Legs.

And then, the System pinged.

**IDENTIFICATION PROTOCOL TRIGGERED.** **SOURCE: CROSS-CONTAMINATION ANALYSIS.** **SUBSTANCE: RATTUS PROFUNDUS FRASS.** **RESULT: RESIDUAL DIMENSIONAL SIGNATURE DETECTED.**

A new entry flashed in Dale's mental Field Guide. It wasn't a monster name. It was a category.

**SUBSTANTIA OFFICIA (OFFICE OF THE VOID)** **WEAKNESS: BUREAUCRATIC RIGIDITY.** **VULNERABILITY: INCOMPLETE FORMS.**

Dale stared at the words. His heart hammered against his ribs. He understood.

The figure wasn't an animal.

It wasn't even a magical construct in the traditional sense. It was a *process*.

A walking, breathing set of rules.

And rules could be broken. Or exploited.

"Vane," Dale said, his voice dropping to a whisper. "You didn't just send a monster. You sent an auditor."

Vane smiled. It wasn't a nice smile.

It was the smile of a man who had already sold the deed to the house.

"Auditors are expensive, Dale.

But they're thorough."

The faceless figure shook itself. The frass fell away, dissolving into the air. It regained its form.

The hum grew louder. The walls of the living room bleeded black ichor, seeping from the corners where the reality of the rift pressed against the suburban drywall.

Dale backed up. His heels hit the baseboard.

Behind him, the kitchen. Behind the kitchen, the door to the backyard.

But the door was locked. And the lock was jammed with something thick and yellow. Sap? Resin?

He looked at the figure.

It was advancing again. Step by careful step. It didn't rush.

It *processed*.

Dale's hand went to his belt. He didn't have a weapon.

He had a utility knife.

He had a roll of duct tape. He had a spray bottle of concentrated pyrethrin mix, diluted to a safe percentage.

He grabbed the pyrethrin.

The figure stopped. It tilted its head.

**WARNING: INCOMPATIBLE AGENT DETECTED.** **PENALTY: 40% EFFICIENCY REDUCTION.**

Dale ignored the warning. He sprayed.

A mist of chemical death washed over the figure.

Nothing happened. The mist passed through the gray mass like smoke. No damage. No reaction.

"Useless," Vane said. "You can't kill a concept with poison, Dale. You have to *file* against it."

The figure raised a nub. The air pressure dropped. Dale's ears popped. The windows rattled in their frames.

Dale looked at the clipboard. Broken in two pieces. He looked at the pen, snapped in half.

He looked at Vane.

"You want a complaint," Dale said.

"Yes," Vane said. "And I want it to stick. Make it hurt."

Dale dropped the spray bottle. It clattered on the floor.

He reached into his pocket. He pulled out his license.

His Class II certification card.

Plastic.

Durable. Official.

He held it up.

**CERTIFICATION LEVEL II: ACTIVE.** **STATUS: GOOD STANDING.**

The figure paused. It looked at the card. The gray surface of its chest rippled.

**SCAN INITIATED.**

Dale didn't move. He let the System do the work.

The card wasn't just an ID. It was a token of authority. In the hierarchy of the Licensing Authority, a Class II technician had jurisdiction over Class I and lower infestations.

He had no jurisdiction over Class VIII entities. But he had the *right of entry*.

The figure's hum changed pitch. Lower. Slower.

It was checking permissions.

Dale's mind raced. He couldn't fight it. He couldn't kill it. But he could *challenge its jurisdiction*.

He stepped forward. One step. Two.

He held the license out, not at the figure, but at Vane.

"Vane," Dale said. "You're operating without a permit."

Vane blinked. "What?"

"Unlicensed dimensional manipulation. Section 4, Paragraph B. You're not a certified handler. You're a civilian."

Vane's smile faltered. Just a fraction. "I have connections."

"Connections don't override protocol." Dale pointed the license at the figure. "I am invoking my right to inspect the site for unauthorized modifications to the structural integrity of the timeline."

The figure froze.

The air in the room went still. The black ichor stopped seeping. The vibration in the floor ceased.

Dale waited.

This was a gamble.

A massive, stupid, potentially fatal gamble. The System had rewarded him for thoroughness. It punished him for sloppiness.

But did it reward him for *bureaucratic warfare*?

The figure turned its head. Slowly.

It looked at Vane.

Then it looked at Dale.

Then it looked at the license.

A sound echoed in the room. Not a scream. Not a growl.

A *ding*.

Like a microwave finishing its cycle.

**JURISDICTIONAL CONFLICT DETECTED.** **PARTY A: LICENSED TECH (CLASS II).** **PARTY B: UNLICENSED ENTITY (CLASS UNKNOWN).** **RESOLUTION: PENDING REVIEW.**

The figure lowered its nubs. It didn't attack. It didn't flee. It stood there, rigid.

Vane stood up. The chair scraped against the floor.

"You're bluffing," Vane said. His voice was tight. "It won't stop."

"It's stalled," Dale said. "Like me. The System doesn't know who wins. So it waits."

Vane stepped around the table. He moved closer to the figure. "Kill him."

The figure didn't move.

Vane walked up to the faceless entity. He reached out and touched its arm.

His hand passed through the gray flesh.

He pulled back, shaking his fingers. "It's not bound by my command anymore. You broke the link."

Dale's chest heaved. Sweat stung his eyes.

He had bought time. Seconds, maybe minutes. Not a solution. A pause.

He needed to escalate. He needed to make the conflict *concrete*.

He looked at the frass on the floor. The brown powder was still there, untouched by the chemical spray.

He looked at Vane.

"You said you specialize in cleanups," Dale said.

"I did."

"I'm not cleaning up," Dale said. "I'm *quarantining*."

Dale grabbed the roll of duct tape from his belt. He tore off a strip with his teeth.

He walked toward the figure.

Vane tensed. "Don't."

Dale ignored him. He approached the faceless entity. The hum returned, weak and uncertain.

He wrapped the duct tape around the entity's midsection. Just one strip. Then another.

The figure resisted. It struggled against the tape. The gray matter buckled, deforming under the physical pressure.

Dale wrapped it tight. He used the cracked halves of the clipboard to brace the tape, creating a makeshift restraint.

He wasn't trying to contain it. He was trying to *label* it.

He grabbed his marker. He wrote on the tape.

**CONTAINED: SUSPECTED JURISDICTIONAL ERROR.** **TAG: 44-B.**

He stepped back. Panting.

The figure jerked. The tape held. Barely.

Vane stared at him. "You're insane. You're taping up a rift entity."

"I'm tagging a violation," Dale said.

**FIELD GUIDE UPDATE.** **NEW ENTRY: SUBSTANTIA OFFICIA.** **STATUS: TEMPORARY CONTAINMENT.** **TREATMENT POINTS: 0.** **NOTE: INCOMPLETE PROTOCOL.**

Zero points.

Dale felt a surge of frustration. He had done everything right, and the System gave him nothing.

No XP.

No level up.

Just a note saying *Incomplete*.

Because he hadn't eliminated the threat. He had just delayed it.

But the delay mattered.

The front door burst open.

Not the back door. The front.

Light flooded in from the porch. Not sunlight. A blinding, sterile white light.

Silas Vane spun around. He dropped his sandwich.

A group of figures stood on the porch. They wore identical gray suits. They had identical faceless masks.

There were six of them.

Vane's face went pale.

"Oh," Vane said. "Oh, no."

Dale looked at the figures on the porch. Then at the figure in the living room. Then at Vane.

The figure in the living room stopped struggling. It turned its head toward the porch.

The six figures on the porch raised their hands in unison.

A beam of white light shot from their palms. It hit the living room entity.

The entity screamed. A sound like a hard drive failing.

It exploded into a cloud of gray dust and static.

Dale threw his arms over his head. The blast wave knocked him backward. He crashed into the coffee table, which was now shattered.

Pain bloomed in his shoulder. His vision blurred.

He rolled onto his back. He coughed. Dust filled his lungs.

He opened his eyes.

The six figures on the porch were walking into the house. They stepped over the threshold without hesitation. The rift behind them closed with a soft *pop*.

Vane was on his knees. He was shaking.

One of the faceless figures walked up to Vane. It placed a hand on Vane's head.

Vane's eyes rolled back. He slumped to the floor.

The figure looked at Dale.

Dale scrambled to his feet. His legs felt like jelly. He grabbed his clipboard. The broken halves were still in his hand.

He held them up.

"I have a report," Dale said. His voice was raspy.

The figure stopped.

It looked at the clipboard. It looked at the frass on the floor. It looked at the duct tape shreds.

It didn't attack.

It walked over to Vane's unconscious body. It picked him up. Carried him like a sack of potatoes.

Then it looked at Dale.

It raised a nub. Pointed at Dale's chest.

**NEW MESSAGE:** **SUBJECT: DALE KOWALSKI.** **STATUS: UNDER REVIEW.** **CHARGES: UNAUTHORIZED INTERFERENCE, FALSE FILING, JUMPING JURISDICTION.** **SENTENCE: PROBATIONARY AUDIT.**

The figure turned and walked out the front door. The other five followed.

Dale stood alone in the ruined living room.

The house was silent.

His hands were trembling. He looked at the clipboard. The ink was smeared. The paper was torn.

He had survived.

But the Authority wasn't rewarding him. It was *investigating* him.

He walked to the window. The street outside was empty. No monsters.

No rifts. Just the quiet suburban night.

But in his peripheral vision, a new icon appeared.

A red stamp.

**PROBATION.**

He had gained no levels. He had lost his reputation. And now, every job he took would be watched. Judged.

He looked down at his feet.

The frass was still there. A small pile of brown powder.

He walked over to it. He took out a fresh bag from his pocket. He sealed it.

He didn't throw it away.

He put it in his pocket.

Evidence.

Because if the Authority wanted to audit him, he needed proof. And he needed answers.

Vane was gone. Taken by the Authority. But the faceless figures hadn't killed him. They had *processed* him.

Dale realized then that Vane wasn't just a rival. He was a file. And files could be reopened.

He walked to the door. He opened it.

The porch light flickered.

On the step, there was a card.

It wasn't a business card. It was a citation.

**VIOLATION: UNLICENSED MONSTER HANDLING (ATTEMPTED).** **FINE: 500 TREATMENT POINTS.** **DUE: IMMEDIATELY.**

Dale looked at the fine. He had less than fifty points in his total balance.

He looked up at the sky. The rift was gone. But the air tasted like copper.

He closed the door. He locked it.

He sat on the couch. He picked up his pen.

He had a new problem. Not a monster. Not a rat.

Debt.

And in the Authority's world, debt was a kind of monster too.

He opened his notebook. He wrote down the date.

Then he wrote a new entry in his Field Guide.

**SPECIES: SUBSTANTIA OFFICIA (AUDITOR VARIANT).** **NOTES:** **1. DO NOT ENGAGE IN COMBAT.** **2. ENGAGE IN LITIGATION.** **3. NEVER LOSE YOUR LICENSE.**

He underlined the last point three times.

The floor beneath him didn't vibrate this time.

It just settled.

But the silence was heavier than the noise had been.

Dale sat in the dark, listening to the hum of the refrigerator. He thought about the vial of frass in his pocket.

He thought about Vane's face when he said, *I told you I'd be watching.*

He thought about the faceless figure's hand on Vane's head.

And he realized something terrible.

The Authority hadn't saved him.

The Authority had *harvested* Vane.

And Dale was next.

He stood up. He went to the kitchen. He opened the fridge. He took out a beer.

He cracked it open.

He drank it in one swallow.

He looked at the citation on the floor.

He picked it up.

He walked to the phone. He dialed the number listed on the back of his license.

It was a local number. A man-hours-only line.

It rang once.

It rang twice.

A voice picked up. Dry. Bored.

"Licensing Authority, Customer Service."

Dale's throat was dry. He swallowed.

"I need to dispute a fine," Dale said.

"Please state your certification number," the voice said.

Dale said it.

The voice paused.

"Kowalski, Dale?" the voice asked. "The one who filed the false report?"

"Yes."

A long silence.

"You're on probation, Mr.

Kowalski," the voice said. "You don't dispute fines. You pay them.

Or you terminate your license."

"I didn't file a false report," Dale said. "I filed a complaint against an auditor."

The voice sighed. It sounded tired.

"Auditors don't exist, Mr. Kowalski."

Dale froze.

"What?"

"There are no auditors in the Authority. There are only Inspectors.

And Inspectors don't take bribes.

They don't take sandwiches. And they certainly don't come to your living room to kill a Class II technician."

Dale looked at the citation.

It didn't have an authority seal.

It had a logo.

A stylized hourglass.

He didn't know the logo.

"Who sent this?" Dale asked.

"We didn't," the voice said. "Check your sender address."

Dale looked at the citation.

At the bottom, in tiny print:

**ISSUED BY: THE VOID BUREAU OF HUMAN RESOURCES.**

He hung up the phone.

He looked at the logo.

It wasn't the Authority.

It was something else. Something older. Something that dealt with *people*, not pests.

And it was coming for him.

Dale grabbed his toolbox. He grabbed his flashlight.

He went to the door.

He had to find the source. Before they sent the next auditor.

He opened the door.

The street was still empty.

But on the driveway, a single black car was idling.

Its headlights were off.

Its engine was on.

Dale stepped out.

The car door opened.

A woman got out. She was wearing a gray suit.

She had a face.

But her eyes were black. Void-black.

No whites.

No pupils.

Just endless, dark depth.

She smiled at him.

"Mr. Kowalski," she said. "We need to discuss your performance review."
