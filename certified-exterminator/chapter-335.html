Chapter 335: License Suspended

# Chapter 335

The flashlight beam jittered across wet concrete. A chittering sound erupted from the darkness, sharp and wet, like nails scraping against a chalkboard.

Dale didn't flinch. He angled the light low. The beam caught the edge of a rusted ventilation grate, half-buried in sludge.

Behind the grate, something moved.

It wasn't a rat. The proportions were wrong. The limbs were too long, the joints articulating in directions biology shouldn't allow.

Dale reached for his belt. Empty.

He tapped the side of his head. The Field Guide interface flickered in his peripheral vision, blue text against the black of the tunnel.

*STATUS: UNLICENSED PROTOCOL.* *WARNING: CROSS-CONTAMINATION RISK.* *CURRENT LEVEL: CLASS IV.*

He had left his primary kit in the truck. The truck was three miles back, surrounded by police tape and confused officers who didn't understand why a man in a gray jumpsuit was screaming at them about ventilation codes.

He had a flashlight. He had a clipboard. He had a can of industrial sealant in his back pocket, intended for the small cracks he'd found in the subway walls.

Not enough.

The thing behind the grate hissed. It sounded like steam escaping a broken pipe.

"Identification incomplete," Dale muttered. He clicked his pen. The sound was loud in the enclosed space.

He didn't retreat. Retreating wasted time. Time was the enemy. Time allowed the infestation to spread, to dig deeper, to corrupt the structural integrity of the city's foundation.

He checked his watch. 11:42 PM.

The Authority's clock was ticking. Every minute spent without documentation was a minute deducted from his Thoroughness Rating. A rating below 8.5 would trigger an automatic audit. A rating below 7.0 meant license revocation.

He couldn't afford a revocation. Not now. Not with the Architect moving through the walls.

"Come out," Dale said. His voice was flat. Professional. "I am conducting a routine inspection."

The chittering stopped.

Silence stretched. Rain dripped from the tunnel ceiling, hitting the puddles with a rhythmic *plink, plink, plink*.

Then, a claw punched through the rusted metal of the grate. It wasn't metal. It was bone, white and slick, shaped like a hand but with too many fingers.

Dale stepped back. Not in fear. In calculation.

He needed to see the full form. Partial views yielded partial points. He needed a 10/10 identification to unlock the targeted damage bonus for this new species.

He walked forward.

The flashlight beam widened. He adjusted the focus knob. The light became a tight, burning circle.

The creature pulled itself through the grate. It was tall.

Thin.

Its skin was translucent, revealing the dark organs pulsing beneath. Its face was a void, no eyes, just a vertical slit that opened to reveal rows of needle-teeth.

*FIELD GUIDE: NEW ENTRY DETECTED.* *SUBJECT: VOID-SKELETON (Osteus nihil).* *CLASS: 3.* *WEAKNESS: UNIDENTIFIED.*

Dale wrote the name down. His handwriting was neat. *Osteus nihil.* He underlined it twice.

The creature lunged.

Dale didn't raise a weapon. He raised his clipboard.

The bone-claws shattered the plastic cover. Wood splintered. Dale twisted his body, letting the momentum carry him sideways. The creature's other hand swept through the air where his head had been.

A cut opened along Dale's forearm. Blood welled, dark and quick.

He ignored it.

Pain was data. He noted the location of the wound.

Left forearm, distal third.

Depth: shallow. Bleeding: moderate.

He backed against the tunnel wall. The creature circled. It moved with a jerky, stop-start motion, like a broken animation frame.

"Structural vulnerability?" Dale asked the air.

The Authority's text flashed. *NO STRUCTURAL VULNERABILITIES DETECTED IN LOCAL ENVIRONMENT.*

That wasn't helpful.

Dale looked at the grate. The metal was bent, twisted outward. The creature had used its strength to deform the barrier.

But the barrier was anchored to the wall. The wall was brick. Old brick. Mortar.

Dale ran his hand along the wet brick. His fingers traced the gaps.

One gap was wider than the others. The mortar had crumbled away, leaving a hollow space behind the brick.

He kicked the brick.

It didn't move, and he kicked it again, harder. His boot connected with a dull thud. The brick shifted, half an inch.

The creature shrieked. It recognized the threat. It charged.

Dale dove. He slid under the creature's reaching arm, rolling through the sludge. Mud splashed into his eyes. He blinked it away, blinking rapidly to clear his vision.

He came up behind the brick, and he didn't try to break the creature. He tried to break the containment.

He pulled the sealant can from his pocket. He shook it. The rattle was loud.

He aimed the nozzle at the hollow space behind the loose brick.

He sprayed.

The sealant hit the void. It wasn't meant for this.

It was meant for small cracks in residential drywall.

But it was thick.

It expanded on contact with air.

It expanded inside the hollow space behind the brick.

The pressure built. The brick bulged outward.

The creature turned. It saw the bulge. It hesitated.

For a Void-Skeleton, physical barriers were irrelevant. But pressure was different. Pressure implied confinement. Confinement triggered their instinct to burrow.

The creature lunged at the brick.

It didn't attack Dale. It attacked the expanding foam.

It clawed at the sealant. The foam hardened instantly, gluing the creature's claws to the brick.

The creature pulled. The brick held. The foam held.

The creature shrieked, a sound like tearing metal. It pulled harder. The brick cracked.

Dale watched. He clicked his pen again.

*PROTOCOL: ISOLATION.* *METHOD: CONTAINMENT VIA PRESSURE.* *STATUS: IN PROGRESS.*

The brick broke.

The sealant didn't stop the creature. It slowed it. That was all Dale needed.

He reached into his other pocket. He had a small bag of calcium pellets. Standard pest control deterrent for rodents. Useless against eldritch horrors.

But the Authority was bureaucratic. It looked for *intent*.

Dale threw the bag at the creature's face.

The pellets scattered. They hit the creature's skin. They didn't burn. They didn't explode.

But the creature paused. It touched the white powder. It brought its hand to its nose-slit.

It sniffed.

*SYSTEM ALERT: NEW MECHANIC TRIGGERED.* *ACTION: CHEMICAL CONFUSION.* *EFFECT: SENSORY OVERLOAD (10 SECONDS).* *NOTES: CALCITRATE INTERACTION WITH DIMENSIONAL ORGANIC MATTER.*

The creature staggered. It rubbed at its face. It was confused.

Its sensory input was conflicting. The smell of calcium was triggering a repulsion response, but the physical sensation was null.

The disconnect caused a neurological stutter.

Ten seconds.

Dale didn't waste them, and he stepped forward. He placed his boot on the creature's leg. He pushed.

The creature tripped. It fell into the shallow water.

Dale raised his flashlight. He didn't hit it. He shone it directly into the vertical slit of its face.

Light. Intense, concentrated LED light.

Void-Skeletons were adapted for total darkness. They hunted via echolocation and thermal sensitivity. Bright light didn't blind them.

It overloaded their auditory sensors.

The light carried heat. Heat confused their thermal mapping.

The creature thrashed. It screamed, a high-pitched whine that made Dale's teeth ache.

He wrote in his notebook. *WEAKNESS: SENSORY OVERLOAD. LIGHT + CHEMICAL DETERRENT.*

He had the weakness. He had the identification.

Now he needed to eliminate it, and he had no weapon. He had no spray.

He had the sealant can.

He sprayed the creature's face.

The foam expanded.

It covered the slit. It hardened over the nose. The creature couldn't breathe.

It couldn't smell. It couldn't hear.

It clawed at its own face. It tore at the foam.

Dale stepped back. He watched.

The creature convulsed. It slammed its head against the tunnel wall. Again. Again.

Dale checked his watch. 11:43 PM.

One minute had passed.

The creature stopped moving. It lay in the water, twitching.

Dale didn't go near it. He waited. Protocol required verification.

He clicked on his radio. Static. He tried again.

"Unit 7-G. Requesting backup for termination verification."

Nothing, and he couldn't wait for backup. The Architect was moving. He could feel the pull in his chest, stronger now. A hook in his gut.

He approached the body. He kicked it. It was cold. Rigor mortis was setting in, unnatural and fast.

He wrote *TERMINATED* in his notebook.

*SYSTEM ALERT: JOB COMPLETE.* *THOROUGHNESS RATING: 9.8.* *POINTS AWARDED: 45.* *FIELD GUIDE UPDATED.* *NEW SKILL UNLOCKED: SENSORY DISRUPTION (CLASS IV).*

Dale exhaled. His hands were shaking. He clenched them into fists to stop it.

He turned away from the corpse.

The tunnel stretched ahead. Dark. Wet. Endless.

He walked, and he didn't look back. He didn't care about the points. He cared about the pattern.

Why was this creature here?

Void-Skeletons were deep-Rift entities. They shouldn't be in Sector 7. They shouldn't be in the subway. They belonged in the Void itself.

Unless the Rift was expanding.

Unless the Architect was pulling things in.

Dale reached a junction. Two tunnels branched off. Left. Right.

The left tunnel smelled of ozone. The right tunnel smelled of rot.

He chose the right, and he walked faster. The rain was heavier now, pouring down from a crack in the ceiling far above. It drenched his jumpsuit. It made his clothes heavy.

He felt the pull again. Stronger. Closer.

It wasn't just a hook. It was a leash.

The Authority was leading him, and he realized it then. The notification hadn't appeared because he found the job. The notification appeared because the Authority *wanted* him to find the job.

The Architect wasn't hiding. It was being baited.

Dale stopped.

He looked at his clipboard.

The page for *Osteus nihil* was wet. The ink was running.

He looked up.

At the end of the tunnel, standing in the light of a flickering emergency bulb, was a figure, which was tall. It wore a suit. A perfect, tailored suit.

It had no face. Just a smooth, white mask.

It held a clipboard.

Dale froze.

The figure raised its hand. It pointed at Dale.

Then it pointed down.

The floor beneath Dale cracked.

A hand erupted from the concrete. Not bone. Flesh. Human flesh.

It grabbed Dale's ankle.

Dale looked down.

A face pushed up through the floor. It was Sarah's face.

Her eyes were open. Her mouth was moving. She wasn't screaming. She was whispering.

"Don't let them in."

Dale tried to pull away. The grip was iron.

Sarah's body pulled down. The floor swallowed her.

The figure in the suit tilted its head.

It wrote something on its clipboard.

Dale couldn't see what it wrote.

But the System flashed red.

*SYSTEM ALERT: CRITICAL VIOLATION.* *STATUS: LICENSE SUSPENDED.* *REASON: UNAUTHORIZED ASSISTANCE DETECTED.*

The pull in Dale's chest snapped, and he was alone.

And the figure in the suit was walking toward him.

The figure stopped, reached for his face, and spoke his name.
