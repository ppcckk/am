Chapter 258: Filing the Claim

# Chapter 258

Kappa stared at the telemetry feed, her knuckles white against the console. The biometric readout for Unit 404—Dale Kowalski—was screaming in red.

His heart rate wasn't just elevated; it was syncing with the ambient energy spikes of the Rift Core.

Most exterminators panicked when their internal storage hit thirty percent. Dale Kowalski ran toward the source of the leak with the calm of a man checking his watch before a bus arrives.

"He's not fleeing," Kappa said, her voice tight. "He's filing a claim."

She watched the thermal imaging. A single, cold heat signature moved through the superheated plasma corridor. It wasn't dodging the falling debris. It was stepping over it, preserving the integrity of the clipboard in his left hand while his right hand, now glowing with a sickly, verdant luminescence, trailed sparks.

"He's going to overload," the junior technician beside her said. "If he detonates that much dimensional energy in the Mainframe, the sector collapses. We lose the whole county."

"Then stop him," Kappa ordered.

"I can't override his protocols remotely. His Certification Level is too high. The Authority won't let us interfere with an active field survey without a Class-IX warrant."

Kappa slammed her palm on the desk.

"He's a pest control guy. He's not a god. Why is he walking into the fire?"

The screen flickered.

Dale's vitals stabilized for a microsecond, then dropped into a pattern that shouldn't be possible. His respiration slowed.

His pupils dilated until the iris was gone, and he wasn't fighting the corruption. He was cataloging it.

***

The heat in the central processing chamber was not hot enough to burn skin, but it was hot enough to warp logic. Dale Kowalski stepped through the threshold of the Mainframe, his boots crunching on glass and melted circuitry. The air smelled of ozone and rotting lilies.

His right arm throbbed. The green veins had reached his shoulder, pulsing in time with the low hum of the servers.

Ten minutes.

Maybe eight, and he didn't check his watch. He knew the count by the tightness in his chest.

Before him stood the Source.

It wasn't a monster. It was a geometry error. A knot of reality tied so tightly it had snapped, leaving a gaping hole in the fabric of the room.

Tendrils of black static whipped from the center, lashing against the server racks. Where they touched, the metal turned to dust.

*System Notification: Infestation Type - Void Parasite (Larval Stage). Threat Level: Class VI.*

Dale raised his clipboard. The paper was blank. His pen hovered, trembling slightly.

*Objective: Document. Isolate. Eliminate.*

He couldn't isolate it. The room was too large. The containment protocols for Class VI void entities required a vacuum-sealed chamber, not a server farm.

*System Error: Protocol Mismatch. Cross-Contamination Risk High.*

Dale ignored the red text, and he stepped closer. The static tendrils lashed out, striking the ground three feet from his left boot. He didn't flinch.

He noted the timing.

*Observation: Attack pattern repeats every 4.7 seconds. Static discharge follows a Fibonacci sequence.*

He pulled out his spray can. It was empty.

He had used it all on the spider, and he pulled out his flashlight. Useless. He pulled out his duct tape.

The Authority would have laughed. Or revoked his license.

Dale didn't laugh. He looked at the server rack nearest the anomaly. It was a standard commercial unit, bolted to the floor. The cabling was exposed, stripped by the static.

He didn't attack the monster. He attacked the environment.

He sprinted to the rack, sliding on his knees. The green light in his hand flared, burning his skin, but he ignored the pain. He ripped the main power cable from the wall. Sparks showered down on his shoulders.

*System Notification: Unauthorized modification of infrastructure. Penalty: -5 Thoroughness.*

"Code violation," Dale muttered, his voice raspy. "Improper grounding."

He wrapped the severed cable around his left hand—the one still human, the one still cold. The copper bit into his palm. He jammed the other end into a junction box on the server rack, bypassing the fuse.

The static tendrils paused. The Fibonacci rhythm broke.

Dale gritted his teeth. The green veins in his arm pulsed faster. *Time to Overload: 4 Minutes.*

He wasn't trying to kill the Void Parasite. He was trying to feed it.

The Authority's system rewarded elimination, but it also rewarded containment. And containment required a vessel. A void parasite wanted to consume energy. It wanted to eat the grid.

Dale became the conduit, and he slammed his glowing hand against the metal rack. The connection screamed. The server fans spun up to maximum RPM, shrieking like dying birds. The Void Parasite surged forward, drawn to the massive dump of electrical potential Dale was offering it.

*System Notification: Bait deployed. Efficiency: 12%.*

"Too low," Dale said.

He needed higher conductivity. He needed a better ground.

He looked at his own body. The green light was a battery.

The servers were a sink.

But the resistance was too high, and the energy was bouncing off the metal, scattering into the room.

He ripped his shirt open. The green veins were visible now, branching like lightning maps across his chest.

He pressed his bare skin against the server rack.

The pain was absolute. It felt like being fed into a wood chipper made of ice.

*System Notification: Biological Interface Detected. Warning: Organ Failure Imminent.*

Dale ignored it. He twisted the wire tighter, creating a short circuit that arced directly into his palm.

The Void Parasite shrieked. It wasn't a sound; it was a vibration that rattled Dale's teeth. The black static lashed out, wrapping around Dale's torso.

It tried to enter him. It tried to eat the light.

But Dale was already full.

*Internal Storage Capacity: 98%.*

The parasite hesitated. It recognized the density of the host. It was trying to digest a star.

Dale smiled. It was a grimace, mostly.

"Contamination," he whispered. "You're contaminating yourself."

He pushed his will into the connection, and he didn't fight the parasite. He forced it to drink everything. The servers drained.

The lights in the room died.

The emergency generators kicked in, sputtering, then failed.

The darkness was total, except for the green glow of Dale's arm and the black void of the parasite.

*System Notification: Parasite saturation detected. Rejection protocol initiated.*

The parasite tried to pull back. It couldn't. Dale had locked the circuit. He was holding the door open.

*Time to Overload: 0 Minutes.*

Dale closed his eyes. He waited for the end. He waited for the Authority to strip his license for gross negligence and structural sabotage.

Instead, the world went white.

***

Dale woke up on his back, and the ceiling was tiled. White. Sterile.

He sat up too fast. Dizziness washed over him, but it wasn't the dizzy feeling of blood loss. It was the dizzy feeling of too much information.

He looked at his hands.

His right hand was normal. Pale. Scarred. Human.

His left hand held a clipboard.

*System Notification: Infestation Eliminated. Thoroughness Rating: 9.8.*

Dale blinked. He checked the score. It was perfect.

*Penalty: 0.*

*Bonus: +500 Treatment Points for Innovative Grounding Technique.*

He looked around, and the server room was gone. He was in a waiting area. Beige walls.

Plastic chairs. A water cooler.

A receptionist looked up from her computer. She wore a headset. Her eyes were wide.

"Mr. Kowalski," she said. Her voice was flat, professional. "The Authority wishes to speak with you."

"Am I fired?" Dale asked. His voice sounded wrong. Deeper. Resonant.

"No. You are being audited."

She pointed to a door. It was heavy, iron, with no handle.

Dale stood up. He walked to the door. He pushed it open.

Inside was not a room. It was a hallway that stretched infinitely in both directions. The floor was covered in ash.

Standing in the middle of the hallway was a figure. It wore a suit made of shadows. Its face was a blank mask of porcelain.

"Dale Kowalski," the figure said. "Certification Level III."

Dale stopped. He hadn't leveled up. He had absorbed a Class VI entity. He should have died.

"You violated Protocol 74," the figure said. "You used your own biology as a grounding wire for a Void Parasite."

"It worked," Dale said.

"It worked because you are no longer entirely human."

Dale looked down at his hands. There were no green veins. But when he flexed his fingers, the air rippled.

"What do you mean?"

The figure stepped forward. It didn't walk. It glided.

"The Rift Core was not just an infestation. It was a test."

"A test for what?"

"For a Class IX Handler."

Dale laughed. It was a dry, barking sound.

"I'm a Class III. I fix sinks and kill rats."

"You killed a Void Parasite by becoming its nest," the figure said. "That is not pest control. That is symbiosis."

The figure raised a hand. A document materialized in the air.

"The Authority has updated your Field Guide."

Dale looked at the paper. It wasn't a list of monsters. It was a list of people.

*Entry 1: The Inspector.* *Entry 2: The Auditor.* *Entry 3: The Consumer.*

"Your job has changed, Mr. Kowalski," the figure said. "The monsters are no longer outside the house."

The figure pointed to Dale's chest.

"They are inside you. And you have been hired to evict them."

Dale looked at the list. He looked at his hands. He felt the hum in his bones, a low vibration that wasn't there before.

He looked at the figure.

"I need a clipboard," Dale said.

The figure smiled. It was a terrible, jagged thing.

"You already have it."

The document dissolved into Dale's skin.

*System Notification: New Quest Unlocked: The Inner House.*

*Warning: Subject contains dormant Class-9 Entity.*

Dale turned around. The hallway behind him was gone. The waiting room was gone.

There was only a door. A single, white door.

It was the door to his childhood bedroom.

Dale reached for the handle. His hand passed through it.

He was already inside.
