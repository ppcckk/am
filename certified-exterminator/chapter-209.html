Chapter 209: Foundation Inspection

# Chapter 209

The valve handle was frozen. Not with ice, but with something thicker, darker, and smelling of copper pennies and old rain. Dale Kowalski adjusted his grip on the wrench, his gloved fingers whitening against the rusted metal. The municipal water plant loomed behind him, a concrete monolith against the bruised purple sky.

He didn't look at the sky. He looked at the pipe.

**SYSTEM NOTICE:** *SUB-OPTIMAL ENVIRONMENT. MOISTURE LEVELS EXCEEDING 85%. RISK OF SLIPPAGE HIGH.*

Dale tapped the notification away. It was just a suggestion. The Authority liked to nag. He preferred the safety of a hard hat and steel-toed boots.

"Come on," he muttered, his voice flat. "Turn."

The wrench groaned. The pipe didn't.

The substance oozing from the seam wasn't water.

It was a viscous, black slurry that pulsed once, then stopped. Dale's Field Guide flickered in his peripheral vision. A new entry tried to form, but it was incomplete.

**SPECIES: UNKNOWN (HYDRIC PARASITE?)** **WEAKNESS: [REDACTED]**

The redaction bar was thick.

It meant the Authority hadn't verified the sample. Or it meant the sample was too dangerous to log without a Level IV clearance.

Dale was currently Level III.

He was out of his depth, but not out of his jurisdiction.

He pulled out his clipboard. It was battered, held together with duct tape and hope. He clicked his pen.

*Inspection Log, Entry 409-B.* *Location: North District Water Reclamation Plant, Sector 4.* *Issue: Structural breach in main distribution line.* *Observation: Non-Euclidean fluid discharge. Viscosity inconsistent with standard sewage or biological waste. Suspected dimensional leakage.*

He wrote it down. Writing it down made it real. And if it was real, it was his problem.

A car door slammed. Heavy footsteps crunched on the gravel.

Dale didn't turn.

He knew who it was.

The air around the approaching figure hummed with static electricity.

"You're contaminating the evidence," Vex said. Her voice was like grinding gears.

Dale continued writing. "Evidence implies a crime. This is a maintenance ticket.

Code violation. K-9: Unauthorized Fluid Discharge."

Vex stepped into his peripheral vision. She was tall, her scales shifting from a defensive obsidian to a warning amber. She wore a coat that seemed to absorb the dim light of the streetlamps. Behind her, two junior analysts held containment seals, their hands shaking.

"You're filing a report on a dimensional breach," Vex said.

"I'm filing a report on a leaking pipe," Dale corrected. "The leak happens to be from another dimension. That's a zoning issue, not an apocalyptic event. Read the municipal code, section 12.4.2."

Vex's eye twitched.

"The Auditor is coming, Dale. If you keep treating this like a plumbing issue, he's going to redact the entire district. Erase the block.

Delete the people."

Dale paused. His pen hovered over the paper.

Erasure.

That was a real threat. Not death, but deletion. Like a typo in a database.

"If I treat it like a monster," Dale said slowly, "the system flags it as a Combat Event. Combat Events require a certified exterminator.

I have one.

But I don't have a license for *aquatic* infestations.

Section 4 states that aquatic protocols require Level IV certification. I'm Level III."

Vex leaned in. "So what are you doing?"

"I'm exploiting a loophole," Dale said. "If I document the leak as a structural failure of the *pipe*, not an infestation of the *water*, the Authority classifies it under Infrastructure Maintenance. Maintenance doesn't require a hunter's license. It requires a technician's license."

He turned back to the pipe. "I'm not fighting the monster, Vex. I'm fixing the house so the monster has no place to live."

Vex stared at him. For a second, her scales flattened. Respect?

Fear? It was hard to tell with reptilian physiology.

"That's insane," she said.

"It's compliant," Dale replied.

He kicked the wrench again. The metal shrieked. The slurry bubbled. A tendril of blackness shot out, aiming for his face.

Dale didn't dodge. He sidestepped. The tendril slapped against the concrete, leaving a steaming stain.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** *HOSTILE MANEUVER DETECTED. COMBAT PROTOCOL SUGGESTED.*

Dale ignored the alert. He pulled out a can of industrial degreaser. Not a weapon. A cleaner.

He sprayed the concrete stain. The black tendril recoiled, hissing. The chemical reaction was immediate. The slurry breaked down, turning into a gray, inert foam.

"Water pressure is building," Vex warned. "If that line bursts, the contamination spreads through the residential grid in six minutes."

"Then we have six minutes," Dale said.

He looked at the valve again.

It wouldn't turn. The corrosion was too deep. He needed leverage.

He looked around the plant.

There was a hydraulic lift used for moving heavy filtration units. It was idle. Power was cut, but the manual override was usually mechanical.

Dale walked over. The analysts watched him, confused. Vex watched him, tense.

He hooked the wrench into the lift's arm. He engaged the hydraulic pump. The lift arm rose. He wedged the wrench under the valve handle again, using the lift as a fulcrum.

"Come on," he whispered.

The pump whined. The lift arm strained. The metal groaned.

**SYSTEM NOTICE:** *EQUIPMENT FAILURE IMMINENT. RECOMMEND ABANDONMENT OF TASK.*

Dale ignored it. He put his weight on the lever.

His muscles screamed.

His contamination anxiety spiked. The slurry was dripping onto his boots. He could feel the wrongness of it, the way it seemed to look back at him.

But he kept pushing.

The valve gave way.

A screech of metal tore through the air. The handle spun. The pipe shuddered. The flow of black slurry stopped.

Silence rushed back in, deafening and absolute.

Dale exhaled. He wiped his hands on his rags. He clicked his pen.

*Issue Resolved: Main valve secured. Fluid containment achieved via manual override and chemical neutralization.*

He looked up. Vex was staring at him.

"You're insane," she said.

"I'm thorough," Dale corrected. "Thoroughness rating: 9.8."

**SYSTEM NOTIFICATION:** *THOROUGHNESS RATING UPDATED.* *REWARD: +50 TREATMENT POINTS.* *NEW MECHANIC UNLOCKED: INFRASTRUCTURE AUDIT.* *DESCRIPTION: YOU MAY NOW TAG STRUCTURAL WEAKNESSES AS INFESTATION SOURCES. TARGETING THESE WEAKNESSES YIELDS 200% DAMAGE BONUS AGAINST ENTITIES DEPENDENT ON LOCAL GEOMETRY.*

Dale read the text. Infrastructure Audit. He smiled, a small, dry stretching of the lips.

The ground shook.

Not the pipe. The ground.

A crack split the pavement ten feet away. From the crack, a sound emerged.

Not a roar.

A chittering.

Thousands of legs clicking against stone.

Vex's eyes widened. "What is that?"

Dale looked at the crack. His Field Guide updated.

**SPECIES: SUBTERRANEAN HYDRAULIC MITE (COLONIAL)** **HABITAT: MOIST, CONFINED SPACES.** **WEAKNESS: VIBRATION.**

The mites poured out. They were the size of rats, armored in chitin that reflected the streetlights. They moved in a swarm, a river of legs flowing toward the water plant.

Vex raised her hands. Energy crackled around her scales. "I'm going to burn them."

"No," Dale said. "Fire creates heat. Heat increases moisture evaporation.

That makes the habitat more hospitable for the next wave. You're making it worse."

"Then what do we do?" Vex snapped.

Dale looked at the hydraulic lift. The pump was still running, vibrating the air.

"Vibration," he said.

He grabbed the emergency stop lever for the lift. He slammed it down. The pump died. The vibration stopped.

The mites froze.

They stood there, thousands of them, legs clicking in unison.

Then, they turned.

They looked at Dale.

Not with eyes.

They didn't have eyes. They looked at him with *intent*. The air pressure dropped.

The temperature plummeted.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** *PREDATOR-PREY DYNAMIC REVERSED. HOSTILE ENTITY AWARENESS: MAXIMUM.*

"They know I'm the threat," Dale realized. "Because I changed the environment."

Vex lowered her hands. Her scales were rigid. "Dale. Run."

"No," Dale said. "I haven't filed the incident report yet."

He pulled out his clipboard. His hands were shaking, but only slightly. He clicked the pen.

*Incident Report: Unlicensed Vermin Swarm.* *Location: North District Water Plant.* *Action Taken: Environmental manipulation via vibration cessation.* *Status: Threat Assessment Pending.*

He wrote it down. As he wrote, the system responded.

**SYSTEM MECHANIC TRIGGERED: DOCUMENTATION DURING COMBAT.** *EFFECT: TARGET CONFUSION.* *ENEMIES ARE CONFUSED BY THE ABSENCE OF COMBAT LOGIC.*

The mites hesitated. Their chittering slowed. They seemed... stalled. Like a program waiting for input.

But the confusion was temporary. Dale could see it.

The leader of the swarm, a massive mite with a carapace scarred by old claws, stepped forward.

It opened its mandibles. A sound like grinding glass echoed.

Vex looked at Dale. "You have three seconds before they rush you. Why are you writing?"

"Protocol," Dale said. "I need to identify the species before I eliminate it. If I skip the documentation, I get zero points. And if I get zero points, I don't get the resources to clean up the rest of the city."

"That's not how survival works," Vex hissed.

"That's how the *job* works," Dale said. He finished the entry.

He looked up. The leader mite was charging.

Dale didn't move. He didn't raise a weapon. He pointed at the ground.

"Hydraulic lift," he said to Vex. "Full power. Reverse thrust."

Vex blinked. "What?"

"The lift. Push the valve open again. Create a vacuum. Suck them back into the pipe."

"That will pull the water pressure in," Vex shouted. "It'll flood the plant!"

"Better the plant than the streets," Dale said.

Vex hesitated. The mites were ten feet away. Five feet.

Dale held her gaze. "Trust the protocol, Vex."

Vex screamed a command. The analysts scrambled to the lift controls. They slammed the reverse lever.

The hydraulic arm slammed down. The valve handle was wrenched open again.

A suction force erupted from the pipe. The black slurry reversed flow. The mites nearest the pipe were dragged backward, their legs scrabbling for purchase.

The swarm panicked. They trampled each other. The suction was strong, pulling them into the dark throat of the water plant.

Dale watched them go. He noted the time on his clipboard.

*03:14 AM.*

He looked at the pipe. The suction had stopped. The pipe was empty.

Vex walked over, breathing hard. Her scales were dull. "You almost got killed."

"No," Dale said. "I almost got a lower rating."

He turned to Vex. "There's more. Look at the map."

Vex pulled up her screen. The red dots were moving. They weren't static. They were flowing.

The water plant wasn't the source. It was a drain.

Something was pushing the infestation *into* the water supply. The mites were just the symptom. The cause was upstream.

Dale's Field Guide pinged. A new notification.

**NOTICE: AUTHORITY AUDIT SCHEDULED.** **LOCATION: MUNICIPAL SEWER MAINS, JUNCTION 4.** **REASON: ANOMALOUS FLOW PATTERNS.** **WARNING: LOCAL GEOMETRY UNSTABLE. DO NOT ENTER WITHOUT CLASS V CLEARANCE.**

Class V. Dale was Class III.

Vex looked at the notification. Then at Dale.

"That's a death sentence," she said. "Junction 4 is where the Rift started. If you go there, you're walking into the heart of the breach."

Dale closed his clipboard. He clicked the pen one last time.

"I need the points, Vex. The Authority doesn't care about my survival. It cares about the form."

He started walking toward the dark mouth of the sewer grate, which Vex had opened.

"Where are you going?" she called out.

"To fix the source," Dale said.

He stepped into the darkness.

The grate closed behind him.

Inside, the air was thick and wet. The smell of rot was overpowering. But Dale didn't gag. He adjusted his respirator.

He pulled out his flashlight. The beam cut through the fog.

At the end of the tunnel, something large was waiting. It wasn't a mite. It wasn't a rat.

It was a hand. A hand the size of a car, made of black water and bone.

Dale raised his clipboard.

"Hello," he said. "I'm here to inspect the foundation."

The hand grabbed him.
