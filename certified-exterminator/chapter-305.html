Chapter 305: New Infestation

# Chapter 305

The countdown digits burned a cold blue in Dale's left eye.

**00:00:09.**

Silence hung heavy over the suburban lawn. The twelve figures in suits stood motionless, their faces smooth and featureless, like mannequins pulled from a department store clearance bin.

The man in the tuxedo, the Auditor, hovered a foot above the grass.

He didn't blink. He didn't breathe. His existence was a violation of biological norms, but his paperwork was immaculate.

Dale tightened his grip on the Ziploc bag. The plastic crinkled under his gloves.

"Process the sample," Dale repeated.

His voice sounded flat, stripped of inflection. He kept his eyes on the bag, not on the Auditor.

Looking at the man invited psychological contamination.

Looking at the evidence maintained protocol integrity.

**00:00:08.**

The Auditor tilted his head. A joint cracked in his neck. It sounded like a dry branch snapping.

"You misunderstand the nature of the summons," the Auditor said. His voice didn't come from his mouth.

It vibrated in Dale's molars.

"This is not a court of law.

This is an audit. And audits do not pause for discovery requests.

"

Dale checked his Field Guide. The entry for *Auditor Construct* pulsed with a dull red light.

*Weakness: Bureaucratic Inertia.* *Note: Constructs of this class cannot act outside defined procedural windows. They must wait for the current transaction to close before initiating a new one.*

The timer wasn't counting down to death. It was counting down to the end of the current interaction phase. If Dale forced a new legal motion into the queue, the system had to acknowledge it.

That acknowledgment created a stall. A hiccup.

A gap in the continuity of the attack.

**00:00:07.**

"Motion to dismiss for lack of jurisdiction," Dale said. He spoke into the air, addressing the invisible Authority interface that hovered in his peripheral vision. "The dimensional debt accrued in this sector falls under Class-3 Residential Zoning, not Class-5 Corporate Liability.

My service area is residential, and the debt is residential. Therefore, the Auditor has no standing.

"

The tuxedoed man stepped forward. The dozen figures stepped with him, moving in perfect lockstep. They raised their hands. Fingers elongated, turning into sharp, translucent spikes.

"Jurisdiction is irrelevant to the debt," the Auditor said. "The debt is absolute."

**00:00:06.**

Dale didn't back up. He stepped into the Auditor's space.

That was a mistake. Or it was a tactic. It depended on the angle.

The spike-hand lunged. It moved faster than human sight, a blur of white light aimed at Dale's throat.

Dale didn't dodge. He didn't block. He pointed his clipboard at the spike.

"Objection," Dale said. "Improper form."

The Authority interface flashed.

**SYSTEM ALERT: INTERACTION STALL DETECTED.** **REASON: CONTRADICTION IN LEGAL FRAMEWORK.** **TIME EXTENSION: +00:00:15.**

The spike froze an inch from Dale's jugular.

The Auditor's smooth face twisted. A vein bulged in the man's forehead, though he had no blood to bleed. He was struggling against his own programming.

The logic loop was grinding.

Dale held his breath. He didn't look at the spike. He looked at the clipboard.

"Submit the sample for laboratory analysis," Dale said.

He held out the bag.

"Proper chain of custody requires a neutral third party. You are not neutral.

You are the plaintiff. You cannot test your own evidence.

"

**00:00:20.**

The timer restarted. It ticked upward now. The extension had worked.

The Auditor lowered his hand slowly. The spikes retracted, folding back into fingers.

"You are exploiting a loophole."

"I am utilizing standard operating procedure," Dale corrected. "Section 4, Paragraph 12: *Evidence Handling in Cross-Dimensional Disputes.* I'm requesting a transfer of custody to the Municipal Sanitation Authority. We have a certified lab three miles north. It's under my jurisdiction."

The man in the tuxedo looked around the yard. The other figures stood still, waiting for input.

"The lab is closed," the Auditor said.

"It's not," Dale said. He checked his phone.

The screen was cracked, but the map app still worked.

"Open 24/7 for emergency bio-hazards. I filed the incident report ten minutes ago. The lab is prepping the containment unit."

It was a lie.

Dale hadn't filed anything. He was bluffing.

But the Authority's system didn't check external reality.

It checked the *status* of the ticket. And Dale had created a ticket by filing the motion. If he claimed the lab was ready, the system had to account for that possibility until disproven.

Disproving it would require the Auditor to visit the lab.

And the lab was full of Class-2 Acid Beetles. Dale had left them there this morning. Uncontained.

**00:00:30.**

The Auditor's smile vanished.

"You would invite a Class-2 infestation into a sterile zone?"

"I would follow protocol," Dale said. "If you dispute the readiness of the lab, you must send a representative to verify.

I will not escort you.

I am a technician.

Not a courier."

The silence stretched. The crickets had stopped chirping. The wind had died.

The Auditor looked at the dozen figures. They looked back. Then they looked at Dale.

One of the figures stepped forward. It was shorter than the others, wearing a grey suit that hung loosely on its frame. It approached Dale slowly.

"I will inspect the facility," the figure said. Its voice was softer, raspy. Like sandpaper on wood.

Dale felt a drop of sweat run down his spine. He hated being separated from his primary workspace. He hated leaving his tools.

"Take Sarah with you," Dale said suddenly.

The Auditor jerked his head toward the woman. Sarah stood on the porch, her hands clamped over her mouth. Her eyes were wide, fixed on Dale.

"Why?" the Auditor asked.

"Chain of custody," Dale said. "She witnessed the creation of the sample. She must verify the integrity of the transfer.

If she stays, she becomes a witness in a pending litigation. If she goes, she becomes part of the audit trail.

You can't have both.

Pick one."

The Auditor's face hardened. The air around him grew colder. Frost formed on the grass blades.

"You are playing a dangerous game, Mr. Kowalski."

"I am doing my job," Dale said.

"Send her. Or send the sample.

But you cannot leave this interaction in limbo.

My shift ends in four hours. I have other appointments.

"

The grey-suited figure nodded. It walked past Dale, toward the house. Sarah didn't move. She looked at Dale, her eyes pleading.

Dale looked away. He looked at the bag in his hand. The ash swirled inside, forming tiny, jagged shapes.

*Go,* he thought. *Just go.*

Sarah stepped off the porch. Her legs shook.

She walked toward the grey figure, and the figure took her arm gently.

It felt like a machine handling glass.

As they walked away, the Auditor remained. He and Dale stood alone in the yard.

"You bought time," the Auditor said. "But time is not a resource you own."

"Time is currency," Dale said. "And you're spending it poorly."

The Auditor raised a hand. A black cube materialized in his palm. It pulsed with a low, rhythmic hum.

**SYSTEM ALERT: UNAUTHORIZED CONTAINMENT FIELD INITIATED.** **PROTOCOL VIOLATION: LEVEL 3.**

Dale's eyes widened. This was new.

The cube expanded. A dome of black energy swept over the yard, sealing Dale inside. The edges of the dome sizzled where they touched the grass.

"You are now in quarantine," the Auditor said. "No motions. No appeals. You will be processed here."

Dale looked at the dome. He looked at the cube. He looked at his clipboard.

The Field Guide popped open.

*New Entry: Quarantine Dome (Type: Static).* *Weakness: Thermal Differential.* *Note: Constructs of this type are powered by ambient magical flux. High heat sources disrupt the frequency.*

Dale had no flamethrower. He had no fire.

He had a can of hairspray, which was in his pocket. He kept it for the static in his hair. He kept it because he hated looking unkempt.

He pulled it out. The can was cold. The nozzle was clogged.

The Auditor walked toward him.

"Surrender the sample."

Dale popped the nozzle clear with his thumbnail.

"If I surrender the sample, the debt remains," Dale said. "If I destroy the sample, the evidence is lost. But if I compromise the container, the audit fails."

The Auditor stopped.

"You wouldn't."

"Try me."

Dale raised the can. He didn't spray it at the Auditor. He sprayed it at the ground between his feet and the dome's edge.

A mist of chemical solvent hung in the air.

Then, Dale pulled out his lighter. He flicked it.

The flame danced.

"OSHA Regulation 1910.110," Dale recited. "Flammable liquids require adequate ventilation. This zone is sealed.

Therefore, combustion will cause pressure buildup. The dome will rupture.

"

The Auditor's eyes narrowed.

"You will burn yourself."

"Personal protective equipment," Dale muttered. He pulled his collar up over his nose. He put on his safety goggles. The ones with the yellow tint.

He lit the mist.

A small fireball bloomed. It wasn't a large explosion. It was a controlled burst. But the effect was immediate.

The black energy of the dome flickered. It turned purple. Then it shattered.

Shards of light rained down on the grass. The Auditor stumbled back, his suit torn by the static discharge.

Dale coughed. His lungs burned. His eyes watered. But he was standing.

He was still standing.

The Auditor looked at the broken dome. He looked at Dale. For the first time, the man looked afraid.

Not of Dale. Of the consequence.

**SYSTEM ALERT: STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY COMPROMISED.** **THREAT LEVEL: ESCALATED.** **AUTHORITY INTERVENTION REQUIRED.**

A sound tore through the air. A deep, resonant horn blast that vibrated in Dale's chest.

The sky above the Rift changed.

The purple clouds swirled, forming a shape. A giant, translucent eye opened in the heavens. It looked down at the suburb.

It looked down at Dale.

**NOTICE: LICENSING AUTHORITY DISPATCHING AUDIT TEAM.** **ETA: 00:02:00.**

The Auditor looked at the sky. He looked at Dale.

"You summoned them," the Auditor whispered. "You brought the heavy machinery."

"I filed a motion," Dale said. He wiped soot from his forehead. "And I requested expedited processing."

The Auditor smiled. It was a thin, cruel smile.

"They will not like your methodology, Dale. They do not like technicians who break their tools."

The sky darkened. The eye closed. But the pressure remained.

Dale looked at the bag. The ash was gone. The fire had consumed the sample.

He had no evidence.

But he had a deadline.

And he had a new problem.

The ground beneath his feet shook. Not a tremor. A rhythmic pounding.

*Thump. Thump. Thump.*

Something was digging up from below.

Dale looked at the spot where the Ash Pile had been. The dirt was churning.

A claw broke the surface. It was made of bone and wire.

Dale raised his clipboard.

"New infestation," he said.

The claw snapped.
