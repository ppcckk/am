Chapter 396: The Pen That Vanished

# Chapter 396

The pen swung.

Dale didn't dodge. He couldn't.

The weight of the hammer was an anchor dragging his shoulder down, a phantom ache from the debuff that still clung to his muscles like wet wool.

Instead, he dropped to one knee. The pen sliced through the air where his head had been, carving a shallow trench into the drywall. Plaster dust snowed onto his boots.

"Stand up," the Auditor said. His voice was flat, devoid of the theatrical malice Dale expected. "Get up. I want to see your face when you break."

Dale kept his knee down. He used the leverage to drive the hammer head-first into the floorboards. Not at the man. At the floor.

*Thud.*

Vance scrambled back, slipping on the scattered paperwork of the previous inspection.

"Dale! Get away from him!"

"Stay down, Vance." Dale's voice was gravel, stripped of its usual procedural calm. "Don't move unless I tell you to."

The Auditor paused.

The pale light at the tip of his pen dimmed, then flared again.

"You think this is a physical contest?

I am an Auditor of the Third Tier.

I rewrite reality's ledger. You are a technician. A glorified janitor with a license to kill.

"

"Protocol requires containment," Dale said. He shifted his weight, feeling the give of the subfloor beneath his boot.

Old pine.

Rotted near the joists, and he knew this house. He'd inspected it six months ago for a suspected termite issue that turned out to be water damage.

He remembered the soft spot three feet from the north wall.

"Containment is irrelevant," the Auditor snapped. He stepped forward, the pen trailing a line of black ink that sizzled against the carpet. "I am the exception. I am the loophole."

"No," Dale said. "Loopholes are just mistakes that haven't been audited yet."

He swung the hammer sideways, not at the Auditor, but at the load-bearing pillar next to them. The wood splintered. The house groaned, a deep, structural complaint that vibrated in Dale's teeth.

The Auditor stopped.

"Stop destroying the asset."

"Asset is compromised," Dale said. He pulled a roll of heavy-duty duct tape from his belt.

It was useless against the Auditor's magic, but the tape had other properties.

Static. Stickiness. Mass.

"I'm filing a hazard report."

The Auditor's eyes narrowed. The pen hovered.

"You are stalling."

"Checking for secondary infestation," Dale lied.

"There is no secondary infestation. There is only me."

"Then why are you looking at the ceiling?"

The Auditor glanced up. A single, long strand of black hair hung from the crack in the plaster where the pen had struck.

It wasn't human hair, which was thick, fibrous, and pulsing with a faint, rhythmic throb. It was attached to nothing, yet it moved.

Dale didn't look at it. He looked at Vance. Vance was frozen, hands over his mouth, eyes wide.

"Vance," Dale said, keeping his eyes on the Auditor. "The breaker box."

"What?"

"The breaker box. In the utility closet.

Turn off the main.

Not the circuits.

The main."

The Auditor turned toward the closet.

"Do not listen to him. The electricity is irrelevant. My power comes from the Void."

"Void doesn't trip breakers," Dale said. "Grid does."

The Auditor laughed. It was a dry, rasping sound.

"You think cutting the power stops me? I am not bound by copper and rubber."

"Maybe not," Dale said. He stepped back, putting the broken pillar between them. "But the *house* is. And you're standing on it."

The Auditor took a step forward. The floorboards beneath his expensive leather shoes groaned. The house was shifting.

The structural integrity Dale had noted earlier was failing, accelerated by his own previous strikes. But Dale needed more than just a collapse.

He needed a distraction.

"You are afraid," the Auditor said. "I can smell it. Your heart rate is one hundred and sixty.

Your pupils are dilated.

You are a Level II technician. You have killed rats.

You have sprayed for wasps.

You have never faced an entity that can unmake you.

"

"I've faced things that don't have eyes," Dale said. "They're worse."

"I have eyes," the Auditor said. He raised the pen. "And I have the truth."

The pen lunged. It wasn't a physical strike.

It was a conceptual one.

The tip touched Dale's forearm.

Pain didn't register as heat or sharpness, but as *absence*. For a second, Dale's left arm ceased to exist in his sensory map.

He couldn't feel the hammer. He couldn't feel his fingers.

He stumbled back, dropping the hammer. It clattered on the tile.

"Give it up," the Auditor said. He advanced, the ink trailing behind him like a snake.

"Surrender your Field Guide.

Even offline, the data remains in your neural pathway. I will extract it.

"

Dale cradled his numb arm against his chest. The sensation was returning, tingling, painful. But he could move. He could stand.

"You don't have my guide," Dale said.

He reached into his pocket. Not for a weapon.

For a small, laminated card.

His Level I ID.

"Look at me," the Auditor said. "Look at what you are losing."

"I know what I am," Dale said. "I'm the guy who cleans up."

He threw the card. It wasn't an attack.

It was a distraction.

The card fluttered through the air, catching the light of the pen.

The Auditor's eyes tracked it, instinctively, a reflex honed by centuries of bureaucracy. He reached out to catch the discarded ID, treating it like a filing error.

In that half-second of attention, Dale moved, and he didn't run. He slid. Low, under the broken pillar, into the space between the joists.

The gap was narrow, barely enough for his shoulders, but he squeezed through, tearing his uniform, scraping skin. The house shuddered.

"Vance!" Dale shouted from the dark space. "Now!"

From the utility closet, a loud *clack* echoed. The hum of the refrigerator died. The lights went out.

Darkness swallowed the living room.

But the Auditor didn't go dark. The pen still glowed, a sickly, pale beacon in the void. And the black hair on the ceiling unspooled, dropping down like a pendulum.

The Auditor stood in the center of the room, alone.

"You cut the power," he said, his voice echoing in the silence. "Did you think I would stop?"

"No," Dale's voice came from the shadows, muffled by the walls.

"I cut the power so the *lights* would go out. You rely on visual confirmation. You're an Auditor.

You document what you see.

If you can't see, you can't audit.

"

"I have other senses," the Auditor said. He spun, the pen sweeping the dark. "I can hear your breathing."

"Can you?" Dale asked.

He was still in the crawlspace, behind the drywall. He pressed his ear against the wood. He could hear the Auditor's breathing.

It was steady. Controlled.

But Dale could also hear something else. A faint, buzzing sound.

It came from the ceiling. From the crack Dale had sealed with foam earlier. The foam was cracking. The pressure inside was building.

The Auditor moved toward the hole in the wall.

"Your trickery is noted. Violation: Obstruction of Authority. Penalty: Immediate termination."

He raised the pen to strike the foam.

"Wait," Dale said. His voice was closer now. He was crawling through the wall cavity, parallel to the room. He could see the Auditor through the gap in the studs.

The Auditor paused.

"Come out."

"I can't," Dale said.

"I'm behind the insulation.

Fiberglass. It's itchy.

You wouldn't like it.

"

"Come out, or I will burn the house down with you inside it."

"You won't," Dale said. "You need the ledger.

The house is the ledger.

Every nail, every board, every violation is recorded here. If you burn it, you destroy the evidence of your own corruption.

"

The Auditor froze. The pen dipped.

"How do you know that?" the Auditor whispered.

"Because you're not here to kill me," Dale said.

"You're here to fix a bookkeeping error. The error is me.

The infestation is me.

You want to erase me from the record. But if you destroy the building, the record is gone. And if the record is gone, you can't prove you fixed it.

You'll be an Auditor with no audit trail.

"

Silence.

The buzzing grew louder.

"You are clever," the Auditor said. "For a pest."

"I'm thorough," Dale said. "And I noticed something. You're wearing a tie."

"What?"

"A Windsor knot.

Symmetrical.

Perfect. You care about presentation.

About order."

"And?"

"And you're standing on a rug that's uneven. One inch higher on the left side.

You've been compensating for it since you walked in.

You've been leaning slightly right to keep your balance. You're tired. You're not just angry.

You're fatigued.

"

The Auditor's hand trembled. The pen light flickered.

"I am not tired," the Auditor said.

"You are," Dale said. "And you're scared.

Because the foam in that wall?

It's not just foam. It's expanding. It's curing.

And it's mixed with something else. Something I brought from the Rift.

"

The Auditor looked at the wall. The white foam bulged. It was no longer smooth. It was rippling.

"What did you put in there?" the Auditor asked.

"Antifungal spray," Dale said.

"High concentration.

Combined with the dimensional energy leaking from your pen. It creates a reaction.

A rapid expansion polymer.

"

The foam burst.

It didn't explode with fire. It exploded with pressure.

A massive glob of white, expanding substance erupted from the crack, slapping across the room.

It caught the Auditor in the chest. He stumbled back, the pen falling from his grip.

The substance hardened instantly, encasing the Auditor's torso in a white shell.

The Auditor screamed, which was a high, thin sound.

"Let me out!

This is illegal!

This is a containment breach!

"

Dale crawled out of the wall. He was covered in fiberglass dust and sweat. He walked to the center of the room. Vance was still on the floor, peeking out from behind the sofa.

Dale looked at the white statue in the middle of the room. The Auditor was trapped, his arms pinned to his sides, his legs fused together. The pen lay on the floor, dark and dead.

Dale picked up the pen, which was cold. Heavier than it looked.

"Is he dead?" Vance whispered.

Dale examined the white shell. He could hear the Auditor breathing. Rapid, panicked breaths.

"No," Dale said. "He's quarantined."

**SYSTEM ALERT:** *Connection Restored.* *Field Guide Online.*

The amber eye blinked back into existence. The numbers returned.

Dale ignored them. He looked at the Auditor's trapped form. Then he looked at the pen in his hand.

**ITEM ACQUIRED:** *Auditor's Stylus (Corrupted)* *Condition: Unlicensed* *Warning: Cross-Contamination Risk. Do not use on Class I or II entities.*

Dale pocketed the pen. He pulled his clipboard from his belt. The paper was wrinkled, stained with ink and dust. He uncapped his pen.

"Vance," Dale said. "Start writing."

"What? What do I write?"

"Report 396-B," Dale said, dictating as he walked toward the front door. "Subject: Dimensional Auditor.

Status: Contained.

Violations: Unauthorized entry, obstruction of protocol, failure to maintain structural integrity.

Recommendation: Immediate referral to Level IV containment team.

"

He stopped at the door. He looked back at the white statue.

The Auditor's eyes were visible through a gap in the foam. They were wide. Terrified.

Dale raised his hand and pointed at him.

"You violated code 14," Dale said. "Improper storage of hazardous materials."

He opened the door. The night air rushed in, cool and clean.

Vance scrambled to his feet, grabbing the clipboard from the floor.

"Wait, Dale! What about the pen? What do we do with it?"

Dale paused in the doorway. He didn't turn around.

"We don't use it," Dale said. "We log it."

"Log it where?"

Dale looked at the System notification blinking in the corner of his vision.

**NOTIFICATION:** *Unusual Item Detected. Authority Review Required.* *Location: Field Station 9.* *Deadline: 12 Hours.*

He looked at Vance.

"Where the Authority is," Dale said.

Vance paled. "You want to go *back* in?"

"I want to file the report," Dale said. "And I want to find out who sent him."

He stepped out into the dark.

Vance followed, hesitating only a second before running to keep up.

Behind them, in the silent house, the white foam cracked. A single, black hair curled out from a fissure, reaching for the fallen pen.

The pen was gone.
