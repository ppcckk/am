Chapter 166: No Pen, No Evidence

# Chapter 166

The clipboard clicked.

Dale's thumb pressed the pen cap down, then up, then down again. The rhythmic snapping echoed in the silent suburban street.

It was a nervous tic, or perhaps a counting mechanism, and he didn't know which. The blue sky above was aggressively normal. Birds chirped with irritating cheerfulness.

Marla stood beside him, her eyes wide. She wore her coveralls, stained with the green ichor of the Rift. Her breathing was shallow.

"You can't appeal to the Authority," Marla whispered. "They don't have a complaints department. They have a deletion queue."

"Everything has a procedure," Dale said. He didn't look at her. He looked at the faceless woman.

She stood on the sidewalk, perfectly still. The briefcase in her hand was black leather, unblemished. No face meant no expression, but the tilt of her head suggested confusion. Or maybe boredom.

"Procedural error," Dale muttered, writing in his notebook. "Citation K-99 issued without Form 14-B notification. Violation of Section 4, Subsection C of the Licensing Code."

The Inspector General did not move.

Marla grabbed his arm.

"Dale. Stop.

If you provoke it, it erases you.

Not kills. Erases. You never existed.

"

"I am currently existing," Dale said flatly. "And I am currently filing."

The woman's head snapped toward him. The air around her shimmered, like heat rising off asphalt.

**[EVIDENCE REQUESTED.]**

The text floated in Dale's vision, sharp and white.

Dale raised his clipboard.

"Exhibit A.

The Auditor's presence.

You cited me for unauthorized dimensional traversal.

But the Auditor was inside my workspace before I entered. I did not initiate the breach.

I documented it.

"

Marla stared at him.

"You're arguing semantics with a god-like entity."

"I'm arguing jurisdiction," Dale corrected.

The Inspector General's hand moved. Not quickly.

Slowly.

Deliberately. The briefcase opened. Inside was not a weapon, but a stack of papers.

Infinite pages, scrolling inward into a black void.

**[SUBMIT DOCUMENTATION.]**

"I need a witness," Dale said.

Marla blinked.

"What?"

"A witness. To the citation. To the lack of prior notice. Section 4 requires two signatures for immediate termination protocols unless the threat is Class IX or higher."

The Inspector paused. The papers in the briefcase rustled.

"Class X," Dale pointed out. "But the Auditor was not Class X when he cited me. He was Class V. I have the logs."

Marla looked at the empty street.

"There are no logs. The Rift closed. The evidence is gone."

"The evidence is in my Field Guide." Dale tapped the leather-bound book at his belt. "Entry: *Rattus Profundus*.

And Entry: *Auditor Prime*.

I identified them. I documented them. I did not engage until protocol was met.

The citation claims I engaged before isolation. False.

"

The Inspector tilted her head again. The motion was jerky, like a broken doll.

**[CROSS-EXAMINATION INITIATED.]**

Marla stepped back.

"Run, Dale."

"No." Dale stepped forward. "If I run, I admit guilt. If I stand, I maintain the record."

"What record?" Marla hissed.

"The record that I am a professional."

The Inspector raised a hand. A finger, long and featureless, pointed at Marla.

**[WITNESS REQUIRED. CONFIRM OR DENY: DID DALE KOWALSKI FOLLOW PROTOCOL 7-ALPHA?]**

Marla swallowed. Her eyes darted to the faceless figure, then to Dale.

"He... he followed it," Marla said, her voice trembling. "He sealed the crawlspace before he sprayed. He wore the PPE. He logged the frass."

**[CONFIRMATION LOGGED.]**

Dale let out a breath he hadn't realized he was holding. His knuckles were white around the pen.

"Next," Dale said.

"The citation claims I failed to verify elimination.

I did verify. I used the thermal scanner.

The broodmother was dead. The citation is invalid.

"

The Inspector's head snapped to the left. A sharp, cracking sound emitted from her neck.

**[INSUFFICIENT.]**

The word hung in the air, red and jagged.

"Insufficient?" Dale frowned. "Why?"

**[THE AUDITOR WAS NOT ELIMINATED.]**

Dale stiffened.

"The Auditor fled. I did not pursue. Pursuit of a Class X entity violates Protocol 12: Do not chase what you cannot contain."

**[PURSUIT NOT REQUIRED. VERIFICATION REQUIRED.]**

"Verification of what?"

**[OF THE BREACH.]**

Dale looked at Marla.

"She's right.

The Auditor isn't dead, and the breach is still open. Somewhere.

"

Marla shook her head.

"I don't know. I don't know where he went."

"I do," Dale said.

The Inspector turned to him.

**[STATE LOCATION.]**

Dale closed his eyes, and he thought about the crawlspace. The smell of ozone.

The way the green light had pulsed.

It hadn't come from the floor. It had come from the wall.

"Exhibit B," Dale said. "The structural integrity report."

He flipped through his clipboard. A page fluttered in the wind.

"The duplex has a load-bearing wall. The Auditor entered through a void space in the foundation.

A crack.

K-7 violation.

I cited the homeowner for it three days ago.

"

Marla's eyes widened.

"You cited the house?"

"I cited the structure. The Auditor used the same entry point. The citation proves the vulnerability existed prior to the Rift event.

Therefore, the breach was not created by my actions. It was pre-existing.

"

The Inspector lowered the briefcase.

**[RELEVANT.]**

"Then the citation is void," Dale said. "I did not create the infestation. I managed it."

**[CITATION VOIDED.]**

The red text vanished.

Marla slumped against the fence.

"You did it."

Dale didn't smile. He watched the Inspector.

"Don't celebrate. I still have thirty days."

"To do what?"

"To prove I didn't just miss him. To prove I can contain him."

The Inspector nodded. A slow, terrifying nod.

**[NEXT PHASE: FIELD AUDIT.]**

Dale frowned. "Field audit? That's for licensed technicians. I'm Level I."

**[YOU ARE UNDER REVIEW. THE AUDITOR IS UNDER REVIEW. YOU WILL BE PAIRED.]**

Dale's stomach dropped.

"Paired with the Auditor?"

**[AFFIRMATIVE. YOU WILL WORK TOGETHER TO CLOSE THE BREACH. FAILURE TO CLOSE: DELETION.]**

Marla grabbed his shirt.

"Together? That's insane. It's a Class X.

He's not going to help.

He's going to kill us.

"

"He's an exterminator," Dale said, his voice hollow. "He's just bad at paperwork."

**[APPROACH DETECTED.]**

The air behind the Inspector rippled. The faceless woman stepped aside.

From the shadow of the house next door, a figure emerged, which was human-shaped.

Tall. Thin. Dressed in a suit that looked like it was made of static electricity.

The face was there, but it was wrong. Too many eyes.

Too many teeth, and they shifted when it blinked.

The Auditor.

Dale felt his heart hammer against his ribs. But his hand remained steady. He clicked the pen.

The Auditor smiled. It was a wet, tearing sound.

"Mr. Kowalski," the Auditor said. The voice sounded like grinding stones. "You filed an appeal."

"I did."

"You claimed procedural error."

"I did."

The Auditor walked closer. The grass wilted under its feet.

"You cited me for using an unapproved method of entry."

"I cited the structure," Dale said. "You used the structure."

The Auditor tilted its head.

"Clever. Very clever. You think you can out-bureaucrat me?"

"I think I can document you," Dale said.

The Auditor laughed. It was a horrible, bubbling sound.

"Document this, then."

The Auditor raised a hand. The air around it twisted.

Reality bent. The suburban street warped, stretching like taffy. Houses twisted into impossible angles.

The sky turned purple.

Marla screamed. She covered her ears.

Dale didn't scream. He watched the distortion.

He looked at his Field Guide. He looked at the Auditor.

**[NEW SPECIES ENTRY: REALITY WRAITH.]**

**[WEAKNESS: OBSERVATION.]**

Dale narrowed his eyes.

"Observation?"

He pulled out his flashlight. He didn't turn it on. He held it like a wand.

"You're unstable," Dale said. "Your form relies on the observer's assumption. You are a narrative construct."

The Auditor's face flickered. The eyes widened.

"Shut up," the Auditor hissed.

"I am observing you," Dale said. "I am documenting your instability. This is a Class II anomaly. Protocol requires stabilization, not combat."

Dale reached into his pocket. He pulled out a roll of duct tape.

The Auditor shrieked.

"What are you doing?"

"Sealing the leak," Dale said.

He threw the tape.

It wasn't magic. It was just tape. But it hit the distortion field.

The field snagged on the adhesive.

The reality warp stuttered.

The Auditor stumbled. The static suit flickered.

"You're interfering with the narrative," the Auditor growled.

"I'm interfering with the infestation," Dale corrected.

He walked forward. The pavement cracked under his boots.

Marla was frozen. She couldn't look away.

Dale reached the Auditor. The creature was taller than him now. Its limbs elongated. The teeth multiplied.

"Stop," the Auditor commanded. "I am the Law."

"You are a pest," Dale said.

He opened his Field Guide. He pointed to the entry for *Rattus Profundus*.

"Same genus," Dale said. "Different size. Same weakness."

The Auditor paused.

"What?"

"The rats," Dale said.

"They hate the smell of citrus. You don't have a nose. But you have a scent trail.

It smells like ozone and old paper.

"

Dale pulled a small bottle from his belt. It was a sample vial. Lemon oil. Cheap stuff from a hardware store.

He uncapped it.

The Auditor recoiled. The static suit crackled with pain.

"Get away," the Auditor wheezed.

Dale stepped closer. He didn't spray it. He just held it under the creature's shifting nose.

"Identify," Dale said.

The Auditor's form dissolved. The eyes popped out of place. The teeth fell into a slurry of static.

"Isolate," Dale said.

He pointed to the warped patch of sky.

"Containment zone. Now."

The Auditor tried to strike. Its hand formed a blade of light. Dale didn't flinch. He clicked the pen.

**[CROSS-CONTAMINATION WARNING.]**

Dale ignored it.

"Eliminate," Dale said.

He squeezed the bottle. A few drops of lemon oil hit the Auditor's chest.

The creature screamed. It was a sound of pure existential dread. The static suit disintegrated. The body collapsed into a pile of gray dust and broken lenses.

Marla gasped. "You killed it?"

Dale looked at the dust. He looked at the sky. The purple faded. The houses straightened.

"No," Dale said. "I treated it."

He wrote in his notebook.

*Subject neutralized via chemical irritant. Thoroughness Rating: Pending.*

The Inspector General stepped forward. The briefcase closed.

**[AUDIT COMPLETE.]**

**[CITATION WITHDRAWN.]**

**[TREATMENT POINTS AWARDED: 50.]**

**[CERTIFICATION LEVEL: I.]**

Dale exhaled. His shoulders dropped.

"You won," Marla said, her voice shaky. "You actually won."

Dale looked at the Inspector.

"The Auditor is dead?"

**[AFFIRMATIVE. BUT THE BREACH REMAINS.]**

Dale frowned. "I sealed the entry point."

**[THE BREACH IS NOT A DOOR. IT IS A WOUND.]**

The Inspector pointed to the ground.

Dale looked down. The pavement was cracked. A single drop of black liquid seeped from the crack.

It pulsed.

**[NEW THREAT DETECTED.]**

**[SUBJECT: THE SOURCE.]**

**[CLASS: UNKNOWN.]**

**[ACTION: IMMEDIATE TERMINATION OF REALITY.]**

Dale stared at the black drop.

"This isn't an audit," Dale said. "This is an eviction notice."

The Inspector nodded.

**[YOU HAVE 29 DAYS.]**

Marla grabbed his arm.

"Dale. We need to leave. Now."

"We can't leave," Dale said. "If we leave, the Authority deletes us for abandonment."

"Then what do we do?"

Dale looked at the black drop. It was spreading. The cracks in the pavement were widening.

"We need more tools," Dale said. "And we need a witness."

"Who?"

Dale looked at the Inspector.

"Her," Dale said. "She's the only one who can authenticate the new violation."

The Inspector tilted her head.

**[I DO NOT WITNESS. I JUDGE.]**

"Then judge," Dale said. "Right now. Before it spreads."

The Inspector stepped forward. The briefcase opened.

But the black liquid didn't attack.

It smiled.

A face formed in the sludge. A tiny, grotesque face, made of oil and malice.

"Hello, Dale," the face gurgled.

Dale stepped back. His pen clicked.

"I know you," Dale said.

The face laughed.

"Do you? Check your Field Guide, Dale. Page 142."

Dale froze, and he opened the book. He flipped to page 142.

There was no entry.

The page was blank.

Then, words appeared. Written in handwriting that was Dale's own.

*I wrote this.*

Dale's blood ran cold, and he had never been to page 142.

The face in the sludge grinned.

"You didn't write it, Dale. You *will* write it. Tonight."

The black liquid surged. It engulfed the Inspector's briefcase.

The Inspector vanished.

Marla screamed.

Dale stood alone on the sidewalk. The suburban street was empty.

His clipboard fell.

The pen rolled away, and he looked at the black puddle. It was growing. It was forming a door.

A door with his name on it.

Dale reached for his pen.

It was gone.

He had no pen. He had no evidence.

He had 29 days.

And he had already failed.
