Chapter 202: Archival Mite Weakness

# Chapter 202

The concrete groaned. It was not a sound of settling, but of grinding, tectonic plates shifting in a localized pocket of reality.

Dale Kowalski wiped his glasses on his sleeve, smearing the grime of the collapsed lobby across his lenses. He blinked.

The world remained blurry, distorted by water and fear, and he didn't care. The structural integrity of the foundation was at forty percent. Forty percent was a failing grade.

Forty percent meant the building would become a tomb.

"Marcus," Dale said. His voice was flat, stripped of the panic that usually accompanied a Class-9 breach. "Stop shooting the walls."

Marcus lowered his shotgun. The barrel smoked.

He looked at the fissures spiderwebbing across the reinforced concrete, and they pulsed with a faint, violet luminescence. The Rift energy bleeding into the physical space.

"They're hitting us," Marcus said. "I have to hit them back."

"You're hitting the load-bearing supports," Dale said. "Look at the stress fractures.

You're inducing a shear failure.

If the west wall goes, the elevator shaft collapses.

We lose vertical escape routes.

"

Marcus swallowed. His hand trembled.

"What do we do?"

"We treat the root cause." Dale turned away from the elevator. He walked toward the central atrium. The water was rising.

Knee-deep now. Cold and oily.

It smelled of sulfur and ozone, and he pulled out his Field Guide. The pages were swollen with moisture. The entry for *Lithic Construct* was already there, annotated with notes on joint weakness and silica composition. He flipped to a fresh page.

*SUBJECT: DIMENSIONAL BREACH (ACTIVE).* *STATUS: UNSTABLE.* *PROTOCOL: CONTAINMENT.*

There was no entry for

"Purge." The Authority hadn't given him the protocol for a Suicide Mission yet. That was the trick. The Auditor had told him to eliminate the source, but the System required documentation before execution.

Without a valid Field Guide entry, Dale had no targeted damage bonus.

He would be fighting blind.

And worse, the cross-contamination penalty loomed. Using a Class I extermination tool on a Class IX Rift entity would result in a 40% debuff. He would be weakened, slow, and his Field Guide would lock up for twenty-four hours.

He needed to classify the Rift itself.

"Marcus, hand me the clipboard."

Marcus handed it over. The plastic was warped from the heat.

Dale wrote. Not about the monsters. About the environment.

*OBSERVATION: Violet luminescence correlates with high-pressure gas pockets in the foundation.* *INFERENCE: The Rift is not merely opening; it is inflating. It is acting as a pneumatic wedge, forcing the concrete apart.* *WEAKNESS: Pressure differential.*

He stopped. The pen hovered.

This wasn't a monster. It was a structural fault. A code violation on a cosmic scale.

The System pinged.

*ENTRY UPDATED: DIMENSIONAL BREACH (TYPE: PNEUMATIC WEDGE).* *BONUS: +15% EFFECTIVENESS AGAINST STRUCTURAL INSTABILITIES.*

Dale exhaled.

The lockout avoided. He had found a loophole. The Rift wasn't an entity to be killed; it was a pressure leak.

To stop it, he didn't need to fight the thing inside. He needed to equalize the pressure.

"Marcus," Dale said. "Do we have any industrial vacuums?"

Marcus stared at him.

"What?"

"HVAC systems. Large intake fans. Anything that can pull air."

"The basement maintenance unit," Marcus said. "It's old. It's barely functional."

"Good enough. Bring it up. Now."

Marcus ran.

Dale stayed, and he watched the water ripple. Something moved beneath the surface.

A shape, vast and indistinct. The source of the Rift.

It wasn't a creature. It was a tear. A hole in the fabric of the city's plumbing.

Dale walked to the edge of the floodwater. He dipped his hand in. The cold bit into his skin. He pulled it back, shaking water from his fingers.

He needed to see the intake, and the basement stairs were submerged. Dale waded down.

The water climbed to his waist.

His hazmat suit, patched with duct tape and hope, held. He counted the steps. One.

Two.

Three, and the darkness below was absolute.

He clicked on his headlamp. The beam cut through the murk.

There, in the center of the pump room, sat the Breach.

It was not a door, which was a wound. A jagged slit in the air, hovering inches above the sludge. Violet light poured out of it, distorting the water.

The air around it shimmered, hot and dry despite the flooding.

Dale approached it. His boots sank into the muck.

*Field Guide Update Required.* *Action: Identify Primary Vector.*

He circled the slit. He looked for the anchor. Every infestation had a nest. Every infestation had a source.

The violet light wasn't random. It pulsed. Rhythmic. Like a heartbeat.

*Identify: Heartbeat.*

Dale pulled out his stethoscope, and he had carried it for fourteen years.

Used mostly for checking boiler pumps, but the principle was the same. Listen to the machine.

Find the irregularity.

He pressed the diaphragm against the air near the slit, and the sound was deafening. A roar. A scream. The sound of a city screaming into a void.

He pulled the stethoscope away. His ears rang.

*Vulnerability Detected: Harmonic Resonance.*

The Rift was vibrating at a specific frequency. If he could match that frequency with a counter-wave, he could collapse the tear. Not by force. By interference.

But he had no sonic emitter.

"Marcus!" Dale shouted. His voice echoed in the pump room.

Marcus arrived on the stairs, dragging a massive, rusted HVAC unit. The intake fan was broken, blades bent. The motor hummed with a dying whine.

"This is it?" Dale asked.

"It's all we got," Marcus said.

Dale looked at the unit. Then at the Breach.

The fan wasn't pushing air out. It was sucking it in.

If he reversed the polarity, he could create a vacuum. But the Breach was already a vacuum. It was pulling everything into itself.

*System Note: Cross-Contamination Risk High.*

Using a mechanical tool on a metaphysical anomaly. Dangerous.

But Dale had no choice.

"Turn it on," Dale said. "Max power."

Marcus plugged it into a surge-protected outlet. The fan roared to life. The bent blades wobbled, creating a chaotic, dissonant hum.

Dale walked toward the Breach, and he held up his clipboard. Not as a shield. As a mirror.

The paper was white. Clean.

He placed it in front of the slit.

The violet light hit the paper. The paper didn't burn. It didn't vanish. It reflected.

Dale adjusted the angle. He tilted the clipboard five degrees. Then ten.

The hum of the fan changed pitch. The dissonance increased.

The Breach twitched.

*Status: Unstable.*

Dale adjusted again.

The fan screamed. The motor sparks flew.

The Breach shuddered. The violet light dimmed.

"It's working," Marcus yelled over the noise.

"Don't touch the fan," Dale said. "Let the resonance do the work."

He watched the paper. The reflection of the Breach on the clipboard was shifting. The slit was narrowing.

But the water, and the water was being sucked into the vacuum. The HVAC unit was pulling water, air, and Rift energy into the basement.

The water level dropped. Fast.

The floor beneath Dale's feet buckled. The concrete tiles cracked.

*Warning: Structural Integrity Critical.*

The vacuum was too strong. It was tearing the floor apart.

Dale looked at the Breach. It was half the size it had been. But the foundation was failing.

If he stopped, the Breach would re-expand.

If he continued, the building would sink, and he needed a different approach.

He looked at the water. The oily, dirty sludge.

He looked at the fan. The intake.

He pulled a canister of foaming sealant from his belt. Class II Pesticide. Designed for hollow walls.

He threw it into the fan's intake.

The blades shredded the canister. Foam erupted. Thick, white, expanding foam mixed with the air being sucked in.

The foam hit the Breach.

It didn't seal it. It froze it.

The Rift energy reacted with the chemical foam, creating a crystalline structure. A lattice of frozen light.

The Breach was encased in ice.

The pull stopped.

The water rushed back in, filling the vacuum.

Dale stepped back. His heart hammered against his ribs.

The lattice held. The violet light was trapped inside a prison of foam and ice.

Silence.

The fan sputtered and died.

Marcus slid down the stairs.

"Did we... did we kill it?"

Dale looked at the lattice. It was cracking. Tiny fissures spiderwebbed through the frozen light.

"No," Dale said. "We contained it. Temporarily."

He pulled out his pen.

*Entry: PNEUMATIC WEDGE (CONTAINED).* *STATUS: UNSTABLE.* *NEXT STEP: STABILIZATION.*

He looked at Marcus.

"We need to reinforce the lattice before it shatters. If it breaks, the pressure release will blow this entire building apart."

Marcus looked at the cracks. They were growing.

"How do we reinforce ice?" Marcus asked.

Dale looked at his Field Guide. He looked at the system notifications.

*New Protocol Unlocked: STABILIZATION FIELD (Class III).* *Requirement: 50 Treatment Points.*

Dale checked his points. He had 12 from the rat. He had gotten points for the Lithic Construct. He had gotten points for the containment action just now.

He had 48 points.

Two points short, and he looked around the room.

The dead Lithic Construct from the lobby had been dragged down here by Marcus during the retreat. Its rocky husk sat in the corner, half-submerged.

*Target: Inert Mineral Mass.* *Action: Harvest Material.*

Dale walked to the rock. He pulled out his multi-tool. A screwdriver and a pry bar.

He chipped away at the creature's shoulder.

"Are you looting the corpse?" Marcus asked, horrified.

"I am sourcing materials for stabilization protocol," Dale said. "The Authority rates thoroughness. Leaving a resource unused is a code violation."

He pried a shard of stone loose. It was dense, heavy.

He dragged it to the lattice. He placed it against the largest crack.

*System: Material Used.* *Points: +2.*

*Protocol: STABILIZATION FIELD UNLOCKED.*

Dale activated it. A shimmering, transparent barrier wrapped around the lattice. It held. The cracks stopped growing.

The room was quiet, and the water was still.

Marcus slumped against the wall.

"We did it."

Dale didn't move. He stared at the lattice.

The violet light inside was pulsing again. Faster.

*Alert: Breach Reactivation Detected.*

The Stabilization Field was holding, but the energy inside was building pressure. It was not dead. It was waking up.

Dale looked at his clipboard.

*SUBJECT: SUICIDE MISSION.* *STATUS: UNLICENSED.*

He had failed to eliminate the source. He had only delayed it.

The Auditor's voice echoed in his mind. *No return protocol. No backup.*

Dale looked at Marcus.

"Get to the surface," Dale said.

"What?"

"Go. Now."

Marcus hesitated.

"Dale..."

"Go!"

Marcus ran. The stairs shook.

Dale stayed, and he watched the lattice. The cracks were spreading under the Stabilization Field. The field was draining his stamina, his focus, his will.

He could feel the strain in his teeth.

The lattice shattered.

A shockwave blasted outward.

Dale was thrown back. He hit the wall. His vision whited out.

When he opened his eyes, the water was gone, and the basement was empty. Dry.

The Breach was gone.

In its place stood a figure.

It was not a monster. It was not a rat. It was not a rock.

It was a man, and he wore a suit. A grey, tailored suit. Perfectly pressed.

He looked at Dale.

"Mr. Kowalski," the man said. "Your performance review is incomplete."

Dale tried to stand. His legs wouldn't work.

"Who are you?" Dale whispered.

"The Auditor," the man said. "But not the one you met."

Dale's breath hitched.

"There are many Auditors?"

"There are many infestations," the man said. "And many levels of oversight."

He smiled. It was the same dangerous smile.

"You passed the local inspection, Mr. Kowalski. But you failed to report the secondary breach."

Dale looked around. The water was gone, but the air smelled wrong. It smelled like ozone and old paper.

"Secondary breach?" Dale asked.

The man pointed to the ceiling.

Dale looked up.

The ceiling of the basement was gone. Above him was not the first floor.

It was a vast, infinite library. Shelves stretched into the darkness, filled with books that whispered.

And something was crawling on the shelves.

Something with too many legs.

*New Infestation Detected: Archival Mites (Class V).*

Dale closed his eyes, and he opened them.

He uncapped his pen.

He wrote: *SUBJECT: SECONDARY BREACH.* *STATUS: ACTIVE.* *PROTOCOL: ESCALATION.*

The man in the suit bowed.

"Good luck, Technician."

He vanished.

Dale stood in the dry basement. The library loomed above.

He looked at his Field Guide.

*ENTRY: ARCHIVAL MITE.* *WEAKNESS: UNKNOWN.*

He looked at his hands. They were shaking.

He took a breath, and he picked up his clipboard.

And he climbed.
