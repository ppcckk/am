Chapter 331: Twisted Door Handle

# Chapter 331

The door handle clicked.

Dale's right hand twisted it, fingers locking with a force that didn't belong to him. The beige calcification had reached his elbow. It felt less like skin and more like cured leather, stiff and unyielding against the wet plastic of the interior.

Marcus screamed, a high, jagged sound that shattered the hum vibrating through the chassis. He yanked the steering wheel hard to the left. The sedan fishtailed on the slick asphalt, tires shrieking in protest.

"Let go!" Marcus yelled, reaching over the center console. His hand closed around Dale's wrist. He pulled.

Dale didn't move. The arm was an anchor. The pen tip in his pocket pulsed against his thigh, a rhythmic throb that matched the erratic beating of his heart. The beige stain crept another inch up the forearm, visible even under the sleeve of his work shirt.

"Drive," Dale said. His voice was flat, procedural. "Stop fighting the vehicle dynamics. You're overcorrecting."

Marcus stared at him, eyes wide and white-rimmed.

"Your arm! It's trying to open the door!"

"Protocol violation detected," Dale muttered, more to himself than to Marcus. "Unauthorized exit from containment zone."

The rear window shattered inward.

Glass sprayed across the back seat, sparkling in the dashboard lights. A clawed hand, long and translucent, punched through the frame. It grasped the headrest, squeezing until the leather popped.

Marcus slammed the brakes. The car lurched, throwing them both forward against their seatbelts. The clawed thing shrieked, a sound like grinding metal, and retracted.

"Why did you stop?" Dale asked, not looking at the window. He was looking at his hand. The beige had reached his shoulder.

"They're on us," Marcus whispered, trembling. "What do we do? What do we do?"

Dale reached into his pocket with his left hand. His right hand remained fused to the door handle, twitching, trying to pull the latch. He pulled out his clipboard. The paper was damp, but the ink held.

"Open the window," Dale commanded.

"What?"

"The passenger window. Roll it down. Now."

Marcus fumbled with the switch. The window lowered an inch. Then two.

"Stop," Dale said.

The car was dead still. Rain hammered the roof. Outside, the streetlights flickered, casting strobing shadows on the wet pavement. Something moved in the peripheral vision, a blur of gray fur and too many limbs.

Dale raised his left hand. He held a small, silver canister labeled *Class-IV Aerosolized Neutralizer (Restricted)*. He hadn't acquired this item in the last hour. It had manifested in his pocket the moment the System flagged him as 'Third Attempt.'

"Who put this there?" Marcus asked, his voice cracking.

"The Authority," Dale said. He shook the canister.

It didn't rattle.

It felt heavy, dense.

"They know we're running. They're testing our compliance."

"Compliance with what?"

"Containment."

Dale uncapped the canister.

The smell hit him immediately—ozone and burnt sugar. It wasn't a smell of death.

It was the smell of a hospital corridor after a mass casualty event.

Sterile. Too clean.

"Use it on the car," Dale said.

"The car?"

"Spray the interior. Every surface. Seats, dash, door panels. Do it now, before the infestation vector stabilizes."

Marcus stared at him.

"That's poison! You're poisoning us!"

"It's a fumigant," Dale corrected, his tone dropping into the dry, lecturing register he used for homeowners who asked why their roaches kept coming back. "It targets chitinous exoskeletons and dimensional membrane anchors.

It will not harm human tissue unless inhaled in concentrated quantities for more than four minutes.

Spray.

Move quickly. Ventilate immediately after.

"

Marcus swallowed hard. He grabbed the canister from Dale's left hand. The beige stain on Dale's arm pulsed, brightening momentarily.

Marcus sprayed. The mist hung in the air, thick and yellow. He moved frantically, coating the leather seats, the steering wheel, the dashboard. The clawed creature outside scratched at the metal door, the sound like nails on a chalkboard.

"Close the window," Dale ordered.

Marcus hit the button. The window rose, sealing the cabin.

For ten seconds, silence.

Then, a wet, crunching sound from outside. Not a scream. A collapse.

The clawed hand fell away from the door frame. The scratching stopped.

Marcus slumped back in his seat, gasping.

"Is it dead?"

"Neutralized," Dale said. He checked his clipboard. New text was forming in the margin, scrawled in a handwriting that wasn't his.

**ATTEMPT 3: CONTAINMENT MEASURE APPLIED.** **VECTOR SUPPRESSED.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 4.1 (INSUFFICIENT).**

"Four point one?" Marcus read over Dale's shoulder. "That's low."

"Because we didn't document the kill," Dale said.

He tapped the pen against the paper.

"We didn't identify the species. We didn't isolate the nest.

We just sprayed the room.

That's amateur hour, Marcus. That's why the Authority gave us the canister.

They're mocking us.

"

The beige stain on Dale's arm stopped spreading. The pulsing slowed. The hand remained stuck to the door handle, but the tension had eased.

"Your hand," Marcus said, pointing.

"It's not infected," Dale said, though the word felt heavy on his tongue. "It's certified."

He looked at the System interface floating in his peripheral vision. The text was smaller now, tucked into the corner of his eye.

**SUBJECT 3 INTEGRATION: 12%.** **SKILL UNLOCKED: BUREAUCRATIC RESISTANCE (PASSIVE).** **EFFECT: REDUCES CONTAMINATION SPREAD BY 5% PER COMPLETED REPORT FILED UNDER DURESS.**

Dale blinked. He hadn't filed a report. He had filed a *violation*.

"Marcus," Dale said. "Hand me the pen tip."

Marcus looked at Dale's pocket.

"You're joking."

"I'm not. Give it to me."

Marcus hesitated, then reached into Dale's pocket and pulled out the pen tip. It was warm. It vibrated with a low frequency that made Marcus's teeth ache.

Dale took it with his left hand. He looked at the pen tip, then at his beige-locked right hand. He moved the left hand to the clipboard, positioning the pen over the blank space at the bottom of the page.

His right hand, still attached to the door, jerked. The beige skin rippled. The fingers uncurled, slowly, agonizingly. The door handle clicked open.

Rain poured in.

Dale ignored it. He pressed the pen tip to the paper.

The ink didn't flow. The paper remained blank.

"It won't write," Marcus said.

"Signature required," Dale said. He looked at his right hand. The beige reached his neck now, a collar of hardened flesh.

"The System needs a biological signature. It needs proof that I am still human, or proof that I am becoming something else."

"What does that mean?"

"It means I have to choose." Dale looked at Marcus.

"The Authority doesn't license heroes.

It licenses technicians. A technician doesn't fight the infestation with brute force.

A technician identifies the root cause and applies the correct protocol. Right now, the root cause is me.

"

"You're the cause?"

"I'm the vector. I acquired the fragment.

I'm carrying it, and the System is trying to integrate me because I'm the perfect vessel for the next tier of monsters. High compliance. High thoroughness.

Low resistance.

"

Marcus turned pale.

"How do we stop it?"

"We don't stop it. We audit it."

Dale raised his left hand again. He placed the pen tip against his own left palm. He pressed.

The pen tip pierced the skin.

Marcus gagged.

"Dale!"

Dale didn't flinch. He dragged the pen across his palm, drawing a line of blood. Then he pressed the bleeding hand to the paper.

The blood soaked into the fiber. The System text flashed gold.

**BIOMETRIC SIGNATURE ACCEPTED.** **SUBJECT 3 INTEGRATION PAUSED.** **NEW VIOLATION DETECTED: UNAUTHORIZED SELF-CONTAINMENT.** **PENALTY: 500 TREATMENT POINTS DEDUCTED.** **BONUS: FIELD GUIDE UPDATE UNLOCKED.**

Dale pulled his hand away. The cut on his palm stopped bleeding instantly, the edges sealing with a faint, gray glow. The beige on his neck receded, retreating down to his elbow.

"Are you insane?" Marcus shouted, gripping the steering wheel until his knuckles turned white. "You cut yourself to update the guide?"

"I signed the document," Dale said, wiping the blood from the paper. "The Authority rewards documentation. Self-harm is a form of documentation. It proves intent."

A new window opened in Dale's vision.

**FIELD GUIDE ENTRY: DIMENSIONAL BUREAUCRAT (CLASS VI).** **WEAKNESS: EGO.** **PROTOCOL: APPEAL TO AUTHORITY.**

Dale read the entry. He frowned.

"Ego?"

"What?" Marcus asked.

"The weakness. It's not fire. It's not ice. It's bureaucracy."

Before Marcus could respond, the radio in the dashboard crackled. Static hissed, then resolved into a voice. It was smooth, baritone, and unnervingly calm.

"Unit 4-Alpha, this is Control. You are off-route. Return to the service area immediately."

Dale looked at the radio.

"Who is this?"

"Control," the voice said. "You have deviated from your assigned inspection zone. This is a Level 4 violation. Please confirm your location."

Marcus slammed his hand on the dashboard.

"Who is talking? Is that the police?"

Dale stared at the radio. The voice wasn't coming from the speaker. It was resonating directly in his skull, layered over his own thoughts.

"This is Dale Kowalski," he said aloud. "I am currently in transit. There is an active infestation in the cabin."

"Infestation confirmed," the voice said. "You are carrying a prohibited asset. Surrender the asset to the nearest collection point. Failure to comply will result in immediate termination of your license."

"Where is the collection point?" Dale asked.

"Three miles north. The old mill site."

Marcus turned on him.

"The mill? That's where we came from! That's where the first subject died!"

Dale ignored him. He looked at the Field Guide entry again. *Appeal to Authority.*

"I am contesting the citation," Dale said.

Silence on the line. The static hissed louder.

"Explain your grounds for contest," the voice said.

"The asset was acquired during an active engagement with a Class-IV entity," Dale said, his voice steady, dropping into the rhythm of a deposition. "According to Subsection 9, Paragraph B of the Municipal Extermination Code, assets acquired during combat do not require immediate surrender if the technician is in the process of documenting the kill. I have not completed the documentation."

The voice paused. The hum in the car dropped an octave.

"Documentation incomplete," the voice said. "However, your Thoroughness Rating is 4.1. This is below the threshold for combat immunity."

"I am filing an appeal," Dale said. "I request a manual review of my recent actions.

I have evidence of successful containment protocols.

I have evidence of cross-contamination avoidance. I have evidence of...

"

Dale looked at his hand. The beige was gone from his neck. It sat dormant on his arm, a gray tattoo of infection.

"...of successful adaptation."

The radio clicked off.

Marcus stared at Dale.

"You just talked to God."

"I talked to the Licensing Authority," Dale corrected. "And I bought us time. Maybe ten minutes. Maybe less."

"What did you say? What did you do?"

"I used the loophole in the certification exam," Dale said. He stood up in the passenger seat, balancing against the vibrating car.

"The exam isn't just about killing monsters.

It's about following procedure.

I challenged the procedure. If they want to strip my license, they have to admit I followed the rules.

If they admit I followed the rules, they have to let me keep the asset.

"

Marcus shook his head.

"You're insane. You're actually insane."

"Sanity is not a prerequisite for licensure," Dale said. He sat back down. "Start driving. Head north."

"North? To the mill?"

"To the collection point," Dale said. "If they want the pen tip, they're going to have to take it off me. And according to the code, they can't confiscate a technician's tools without a witnessed inspection."

Dale looked at the clock on the dashboard. 11:58 PM.

"We have two minutes," Dale said. "If we don't reach the mill by midnight, the appeal window closes. And if the appeal window closes, the termination clause kicks in."

Marcus put the car in gear. His hands were shaking so hard he missed the shift twice. He finally got it into Drive.

"Why the mill?" Marcus asked, pulling back onto the road. "Why go back there?"

Dale looked out the window. The rain was heavier now, blurring the streetlights into watery streaks.

In the distance, the silhouette of the mill loomed against the sky.

It looked different than it had before. The smokestacks were twisted, spiraling into shapes that defied geometry.

"Because," Dale said, checking his Field Guide.

"The previous two subjects didn't just die. They were processed.

The Authority doesn't waste resources.

They recycle failed attempts.

"

Dale looked at Marcus.

"We're not just going to the collection point. We're going to the assembly line."

The car sped toward the mill. The beige stain on Dale's arm pulsed once, bright and blue.
