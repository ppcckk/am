Chapter 192: Symptom Not Patient

# Chapter 192

The needle was moving.

Not up. Not down. Sideways.

Dale watched the pressure gauge on his canister, the needle trembling against the red zone. He hadn't fired yet.

The canister wasn't empty, which was locked. A small, digital padlock had materialized on the nozzle, blinking a steady, angry amber.

*Protocol Lockout: Species Identification Required.*

He stared at the thing in front of him.

It wasn't a rat. It wasn't a beetle.

It was a knot of wet, pulsating muscle and chitin, roughly the size of a washing machine, anchored to the asphalt by six limbs that looked like human fingers fused together.

It didn't have a face. It had a mouth that opened vertically, revealing rows of translucent teeth that vibrated.

Vane was behind him, her rifle raised. Her breathing was shallow. Too fast.

"Dale," she said. Her voice was tight. "It's looking at us."

"It's not looking at you," Dale said, not turning around. He kept his eyes on the creature's dorsal ridge.

"It's scanning for thermal signatures.

Don't move.

Don't breathe."

"It's a Class IV, isn't it?" Vane asked.

"Unlicensed."

"That's worse than unlicensed. That's an anomaly. The Authority doesn't have a protocol for anomalies."

"They have a protocol for everything." Dale pulled his clipboard from his belt. The paper was blank. He needed a new entry.

But his Field Guide was still grayed out, contaminated by the cross-contamination penalty from the last fight. He couldn't read the species.

He could only see the shape.

And the shape was wrong.

The creature shifted. The finger-limbs curled. A low hum emanated from its core.

"Dale, shoot it."

"I can't shoot what I can't identify. If I fire a Class II acid spray at a Class IV anomaly, the feedback loop will detonate the canister. And me."

"Then what do we do?"

Dale tapped his pen against his clipboard. *Identify. Document. Isolate.*

He had to identify it. But the Guide was blind. He had to do it manually.

He stepped forward.

"Dale!" Vane shouted.

He ignored her. He walked toward the creature. The asphalt was slick with rain.

His boots made sucking sounds.

The creature recoiled, its vertical mouth widening. A sound like tearing plastic filled the air.

Dale stopped ten feet away. He raised his flashlight. Not to blind it. To illuminate the frass.

Where the creature stood, the asphalt wasn't just cracked. It was dissolved. A black, viscous slime pooled around the base of the limbs. It smelled of sulfur and old copper.

Dale squatted. He pulled a sample kit from his vest. He didn't touch the slime. He used a long-handled scraper to chip away a piece of the asphalt beneath it.

The Authority notification flickered in his peripheral vision, grayed out and useless.

*Analysis Pending.*

Dale scraped harder.

The asphalt crumbled. Underneath, the slime had eaten into the aggregate. It wasn't just acid.

It was enzymatic. Digestive.

"This isn't a monster," Dale said, his voice flat. "It's a digestive tract."

Vane lowered her rifle slightly.

"What?"

"Look at the structure.

Six limbs for anchoring.

One oral cavity for intake. No limbs for manipulation.

No sensory organs on the front.

" Dale pointed his flashlight at the creature's back. "The only external features are the pores along the spine."

"It's a leech," Vane said.

"A *dimensional* leech," Dale corrected. "It's not attacking us because it's aggressive. It's attacking us because we're standing on its feeding ground."

The creature shrieked. The sound was wet and high-pitched. It lunged.

Dale didn't dodge. He dropped to one knee.

Vane screamed and fired. The bullet struck the creature's side.

Black fluid sprayed.

The creature didn't flinch. It kept coming.

Dale watched the impact site. The fluid wasn't pooling. It was sizzling. Reacting with the air.

"Oxidizing," Dale muttered.

He pulled a secondary canister from his belt.

Not the acid spray. The neutralizing foam.

Class I.

For mold and mildew.

"Vane, cover me. Aim for the spine."

"That won't kill it!"

"It doesn't have to kill it. It has to suffocate it."

Dale waited. The creature was three feet away.

He could smell the rot, which was sweet.

Sickly sweet.

He pressed the nozzle.

Foam erupted. White, thick, expanding rapidly. It hit the creature's vertical mouth.

The creature thrashed, its finger-limbs clawing at the asphalt. The foam coated the oral cavity, sealing it.

The creature stopped moving.

Silence returned to the street. Only the rain and the hiss of the foam remained.

The digital padlock on Dale's primary canister turned green.

*Species Identified: Dimensional Hemophage (Anomalous Strain).* *Weakness Found: Aerobic Dependency.* *Bonus Applied: +40% Suffocation Damage.*

Dale exhaled. His hands were shaking. He hid them in his pockets.

"Is it dead?" Vane asked, her rifle still raised.

"It's asphyxiating. Give it ten minutes. Then we need to isolate it."

"Isolate it? With what? We don't have a containment unit."

Dale looked at the creature. It was twitching. The foam was dissolving, reacting with the enzymes in its mouth. They had maybe five minutes before it broke free.

"We don't need a unit," Dale said. "We need a trap."

He walked to the edge of the crater, and he looked at the surrounding buildings. Broken windows. Exposed pipes.

A collapsed awning.

"Vane, take your rifle. Shoot the water main above us."

Vane stared at him.

"What?"

"The main. It's exposed.

Shoot it.

We need water. Lots of water.

"

"You want to wash it away?"

"No. I want to dilute its enzymes. If it's aerobic, water blocks the oxygen exchange faster than foam. But I need it submerged."

"That's insane. It could wash it into the sewers."

"It's a Class IV. It won't wash away. It'll anchor. And when it's underwater, it can't breathe."

Vane hesitated. Then she nodded. She raised her rifle and fired at the overhead pipe.

The pipe burst.

Water gushed down, hitting the creature. It screamed. The sound was gurgling now. The creature tried to pull itself up, but the water pressure pinned it to the ground.

Dale moved. He didn't retreat.

He advanced, and he pulled a net from his pack.

A heavy, weighted mesh used for catching large feral dogs.

He threw it over the creature.

The mesh sank. The creature struggled, its limbs pushing against the current. The net caught on its finger-limbs.

"Secure it," Dale said.

Vane ran to the other side. She held the other end of the net. They pulled together, dragging the heavy, thrashing mass toward the center of the crater.

It was heavy. Wet. Alive.

They hauled it up the slope. The creature's mouth was still open, but the foam had hardened, blocking the airway. It was suffocating properly now.

They dragged it to the edge of the service area. The boundary line.

Dale pulled out his pen. He wrote in his notebook.

*Date: October 14.* *Location: Sector 4, Grid 9.* *Incident: Unauthorized dimensional intrusion. Species: Hemophage.* *Action taken: Chemical suffocation followed by hydraulic submersion.* *Status: Contained.*

He underlined *Contained*.

The Authority notification flashed.

*Treatment Points: 150.* *Thoroughness Rating: 8.7.* *Note: Efficient, but messy. Structural damage to public utilities noted. Fine applied.*

Dale didn't care about the fine. He cared about the rating. It was lower than last time.

He had rushed the isolation. He had used water instead of proper containment gel.

He looked at Vane. She was soaked. Her hair was plastered to her face.

"We need to move," Dale said. "The Authority just fined us. That means they're watching. They're waiting for the next slip."

"The next slip?" Vane wiped her face. "Dale, we just killed a god's pet. What's next?"

Dale looked at the Rift. The green light was pulsing faster now. Closer.

He checked his watch. Two hours since the suspension warning.

"Look at the sky," Dale said.

Vane looked up.

The clouds weren't moving. They were forming. Spiraling.

A new notification appeared in Dale's vision. It wasn't gray. It was bright, burning red.

*ALERT: RECURSIVE INFESTATION DETECTED.* *SOURCE: LOCALIZED.* *HOST: KOWALSKI, DALE.*

Dale froze.

"Vane," he said. His voice was quiet. "Get your rifle ready."

"Why? Did something else come out?"

"No." Dale looked down at his clipboard.

The paper was blank. But the ink in his pen had changed. It was no longer black.

It was green, and he tried to write. The pen wouldn't move. It was stuck.

He pulled it from his hand. The tip was fused.

He looked at his hand. His veins were visible through his skin. They were green.

"The suspension," Dale whispered. "It's not just a warning. It's a quarantine."

"What does that mean?"

Dale looked at Vane. His eyes met hers. He saw the fear in them.

"It means the Authority doesn't trust me to identify the threat anymore. So they're becoming the threat."

The ground beneath Dale's feet cracked. Not from the outside. From below.

Something was growing inside the pavement. Something that matched the green light in the sky.

Dale realized then that the creature they had just killed hadn't been the infestation, which had been the symptom.

And the patient was him.
