Chapter 116: Termination Protocol

# Chapter 116

Dale counted six cracked eggs before he reached the intersection. Dale didn't stop.

He didn't slow down.

His boots slid on the wet oil-slicked concrete, but he caught himself before he fell. The eggs in his jacket pocket felt heavy, warm, and wrong. They pulsed against his ribs like a second heartbeat.

Ahead, the sedan's doors swung open.

Two figures stepped out into the downpour. They weren't wearing rain gear.

They were wearing the crisp, dark gray suits of the Authority's Enforcement Division.

Their umbrellas were black, reinforced with sigils that hummed with low-frequency static, repelling the water in a perfect, unnatural dome.

Dale skidded to a halt. He raised his hands. Not in surrender. In documentation.

"Halt," the first agent said. His voice was flat, bored, and devoid of empathy. "Subject Kowalski. You are flagged for Unauthorized Extraction of Class-IV Biomass."

Dale blinked. Water dripped from his nose.

He looked at the agent, then at the second agent, who was already pulling a clipboard from his belt.

Not a tablet.

A physical clipboard. The Authority loved paper.

It left a better audit trail.

"I didn't extract," Dale said. His voice was steady, though his lungs still burned from the sprint. "I relocated."

The first agent tilted his head.

"Relocation is not a recognized protocol for removal. Removal requires a Disposal Order. You have no Disposal Order."

"I have a Field Guide entry," Dale countered. He reached into his jacket, slowly, telegraphing his movement so they wouldn't shoot him.

"Entry 402. *Chitin-Backed Subterranean Borer*.

I documented the infestation. I identified the nest. I isolated the specimen.

"

"You dragged it out of a quarantine zone," the second agent said, not looking up from his clipboard.

He was writing furiously.

"That's a Code 7 Violation.

Contamination risk.

If that beetle had a parasite, you just seeded the surface grid.

"

"It wasn't parasitized," Dale said. "I checked the spiracles. Clean air exchange. No spore trails."

The first agent narrowed his eyes.

"You checked? With what? Your bare hands?"

"I used a swab from my kit." Dale pointed to his belt. The kit was soaked, but the seals held.

"I have the log.

Timestamped.

Pre-extraction.

"

The second agent stopped writing. He looked up. For the first time, he seemed interested.

"Show me."

Dale pulled out his phone. The battery was dead, but he had printed the log from the mobile printer attached to his belt. He handed the slip of thermal paper to the agent.

The agent scanned it. His eyebrows raised, just a fraction of an inch.

"This is a Level Two Protocol. You're running a Level One certification."

"Technically, I'm between levels," Dale said. "I submitted my exam papers an hour ago. Pending approval."

"Pending is not approved," the first agent said sharply. "You don't get to use advanced protocols on a whim, Kowalski. The System rewards thoroughness, not improvisation. You're contaminating your own rating."

Dale felt a cold knot tighten in his stomach. Not fear. Annoyance.

It was a paperwork error. A bureaucratic hiccup.

He could fix this, and he always fixed this.

"I need to return the biomass," Dale said. "The message said fifteen minutes. I'm at minute twelve."

The first agent glanced at his wristwatch.

"You're in violation. The window is closed.

The asset is now considered lost.

You are liable for recovery costs.

"

"I recovered it," Dale said. He pointed to the drain grate. "It's right there."

The agents looked at the grate. Then they looked at Dale.

"The beetle is gone," the second agent said.

Dale froze. He turned.

The grate was empty.

The storm drain swallowed all light, all sound, and the beetle had crawled back into the dark, or the current had taken it.

Dale stared at the black hole in the pavement. His mind raced. The System rules were clear: if you lose the asset, you don't get the points.

You don't get the closure. And without closure, the infestation remains active.

But the message said *theft*. If the beetle was back in the drain, was it theft? Or was it still in his custody?

The first agent stepped forward.

"Kowalski, you have a choice. You can turn yourself in for license suspension. Or you can attempt a Re-Capture Protocol within ten minutes.

If you fail, the penalty doubles.

"

"Re-Capture?" Dale asked. "I don't have a protocol for re-capture. It's not in my toolkit."

"It's a loophole," the second agent said, his voice dropping. He leaned in, lowering his umbrella slightly.

The rain hit his shoulder, and he didn't seem to care.

"Section 4, Subsection B. If a technician loses an asset due to environmental interference, they can invoke a 'Residual Trace' search.

But it costs points. A lot of points.

"

Dale calculated.

He had 45 points.

A Residual Trace cost 30. That would leave him with 15.

If he failed, he'd be in the negative. Negative points meant probation. Probation meant he couldn't take the Level II exam.

His career would stall.

"Do it," Dale said.

The first agent frowned.

"You're spending points on a search you're likely to fail? The drain is complex. Multiple chambers.

High flow rate.

"

"I know the layout," Dale said. "I inspected this sector last week.

It's a legacy system.

Pre-Rift plumbing. There's a secondary valve at the junction box. If the beetle went downstream, it hit the valve.

It's a bottleneck.

"

The first agent hesitated.

"You're guessing."

"I'm extrapolating," Dale corrected.

"Based on flow dynamics and the creature's leg span.

It's six inches wide. The valve is four.

It's stuck. Or it's buffering.

"

The second agent tapped his pen against the clipboard.

"He's right about the valve.

I've seen this pattern before.

In the '98 outbreaks. The big borers always got snagged on the old infrastructure.

"

The first agent sighed.

"Fine. Invoke the Trace.

But if you spend those points and miss, I'm revoking your license tonight.

No appeal."

Dale nodded. He closed his eyes. He focused on the System interface, which hovered in the darkness behind his eyelids.

*Activate: Residual Trace.*

*Cost: 30 Treatment Points.*

*Warning: This action reveals the last known location of the target asset within a 50-meter radius. It does not guarantee recovery.*

*Confirm?*

Dale confirmed.

A map bloomed in his mind. A schematic of the sewers, overlaid on the street above.

A red dot pulsed.

Not in the main drain.

But in a maintenance access shaft, three blocks east.

The dot wasn't moving.

"It's stationary," Dale said, opening his eyes. "Three blocks east. Maintenance Shaft 4B. It's stuck on the valve."

The first agent looked at the second.

"That's a restricted access point. Unlicensed civilians aren't allowed."

"I'm licensed," Dale said. "Class I. Pending II. That's still a license."

"It's pending," the first agent snapped.

"Pending is valid until revoked," Dale said. "And you haven't revoked it. You're just threatening to."

The second agent smiled. It was a thin, cruel smile.

"He's got you there, Miller.

The System doesn't recognize threats. It recognizes status.

Kowalski is still active.

"

Miller grunted.

"Go. But I'm watching.

If you don't get the biomass in six minutes, I'm pulling the plug on your account.

Permanently."

Dale ran, and he didn't look back. He sprinted down the wet sidewalk, his lungs screaming. The rain lashed at his face, blinding him. He kept his eyes on the mental map, the red dot pulsing in his mind like a beacon.

Two blocks.

One block.

The access shaft was a rusted iron ladder leading down into a concrete pit.

The door was locked with a padlock. Dale didn't hesitate. He pulled his multi-tool from his belt.

It was a pest control tool, designed for cutting wire mesh and opening tight cabinets. But the jaws were strong.

He clamped it onto the padlock shackle, and he twisted.

The metal shrieked. Once.

Twice. Three times.

The lock snapped.

Dale yanked the door open. He descended.

The air below smelled of rot, sulfur, and wet stone. He climbed down the ladder, his boots slipping on the rungs. The red dot was ten meters down.

He reached the bottom. The tunnel was narrow, barely wide enough for his shoulders. Water ankle-deep.

And there it was.

The beetle. *Rattus profundus*, variant B. The *Chitin-Backed Borer*.

It was jammed against the iron valve, its legs scrabbling uselessly against the rusted wheel, which was alive. Its antennae twitched as Dale approached.

Dale didn't attack. He didn't spray. He pulled out a heavy-duty trash bag from his belt. He unfolded it.

He approached the beetle. It snapped its mandibles. Click-click-click.

"Easy," Dale muttered. "I'm just bagging you. Standard containment."

He worked with the precision of a surgeon. He looped the bag around the beetle's midsection, avoiding the legs. He tied the top shut with a knot that wouldn't slip. Then he grabbed the bag and dragged it toward the ladder.

The beetle kicked. The bag tore.

Dale flinched. A tear in the containment bag was a Code 3 violation. It meant the biomass was compromised. It meant the evidence could leak.

He pulled out a roll of duct tape. He wrapped the tear. Then he wrapped the whole bag. Layer upon layer of silver tape.

He climbed the ladder. The beetle writhed inside the bag, the sound muffled but frantic.

When he emerged, the rain was lighter. But the two agents were still there. Miller held a pen, poised over the clipboard.

"Time?" Dale asked.

"Four minutes left," Miller said.

Dale held up the taped bag.

"Specimen secured. No leakage. No contamination."

Miller looked at the second agent.

"Inspect it."

The second agent stepped forward, and he pulled on a pair of latex gloves. He examined the tape.

He checked the seals.

He held the bag up to the light.

"It's intact," the agent said. "But you used a non-standard containment material. Duct tape is not listed in the Approved Materials List for Class IV biomass."

"It's airtight," Dale said. "Water-resistant. Adhesive-backed. It meets the functional requirements of the protocol, even if it doesn't meet the brand requirements."

Miller stepped forward. He looked at Dale. His face was unreadable.

"You're trying to game the System, Kowalski."

"I'm trying to survive the System," Dale said.

Miller shook his head.

"The Authority doesn't care about survival.

It cares about compliance.

You used tape.

That's a deviation. Deviations incur a penalty.

"

Dale felt the blood drain from his face.

"How much?"

"Five points," Miller said.

Dale had 15 points. Minus 5. Ten left.

"Acceptable," Dale said. "As long as the job is closed."

Miller tapped his pen.

"The job isn't closed. You stole an asset.

You violated the quarantine zone. And you used unauthorized materials.

Your Thoroughness Rating is dropping.

"

Dale looked at his phone. It was still dead, but he could feel the System's judgment pressing against his skin. A notification pinged in his head.

*Thoroughness Rating Adjusted: 9.2 -> 8.7.*

*Notice: Rating below 9.0 disqualifies candidates for the Regional Oversight Board.*

Dale stared at Miller.

"You set me up."

Miller didn't blink.

"I provided the rules. You chose the path.

You chose the shortcut.

Shortcuts degrade quality.

"

The second agent cleared his throat.

"Miller, the rating is still high. He's still in the top ten percent of technicians in this sector. The Oversight Board is just a formality.

We need him for the Rift.

"

Miller frowned.

"He's a loose cannon. He breaks protocol. He improvises."

"He's effective," the second agent said.

"And he's the only one who documented the broodmother.

Who else has a Field Guide entry for *Rattus profundus*? Nobody.

The others just killed them. They didn't identify them. They didn't learn.

"

Miller looked at Dale.

"You're lucky, Kowalski.

Very lucky.

But this is the last time I'll let you slide. Next time, I'll revoke your license before you even open your mouth.

"

Dale nodded, and he didn't argue. He knew Miller was right.

He had cut corners.

He had risked contamination. He had almost lost the asset.

But he had the points. He had the entry. And he was still licensed.

"Where do I dispose of the biomass?" Dale asked.

Miller pointed to a black van parked at the curb. It had no windows.

Just a vented roof and a heavy steel door.

"The Mobile Incinerator Unit.

Drive it to the disposal site. You have ten minutes to deliver the package.

If you're late, we process the violation as completed theft. And you pay the fine.

"

Dale looked at the van. He looked at the bag. He looked at the rain.

"I don't have a vehicle," Dale said.

Miller smiled. It was the first time he had smiled.

It was cold.

"That's a problem. The Authority does not provide vehicles. You must transport the hazardous material yourself.

By any means necessary.

"

Dale felt the weight of the bag. It was heavy. And the beetle was still kicking.

"Any means," Dale repeated.

He looked at the street. At the empty roads. At the black van.

He didn't have a car. He didn't have a bike.

But he had a license. And he had a job.

Dale walked toward the van. He opened the side door. It was empty. Just a metal floor, stained with old fluids.

He got in. He sat in the driver's seat. There was no key. No ignition.

But on the dashboard, a screen lit up.

*Vehicle Unlocked. Destination: Disposal Site 7.*

*Warning: Unauthorized Drivers May Be Penalized.*

Dale put the vehicle in gear. The engine roared to life, though no one had started it. The System was driving.

He drove.

As the van pulled away, Dale looked in the rearview mirror. Miller and the other agent were standing in the rain, watching him go.

Miller was writing something on his clipboard, and he wasn't looking at Dale. He was looking at the sky.

Dale looked up.

The Rift was closing. The purple light was fading. But something else was happening.

The clouds were moving. Not with the wind.

Against it, and they were forming a spiral.

A vortex.

And inside the vortex, something large was descending.

It wasn't a beetle. It wasn't a rat.

It was a hand. A hand made of smoke and shadow, reaching down from the heavens.

Dale gripped the steering wheel. The van swerved, but the System corrected it. The vehicle locked onto the path.

But the hand kept coming.

And it was pointing at Dale.

Not the van.

At *him*.

A notification flashed on the dashboard screen.

*Alert: High-Value Target Detected.*

*Subject: Dale Kowalski.*

*Reason: Knowledge of the Deep Breach.*

*Action: Termination Protocol Initiated.*

Dale slammed on the brakes. The van skidded, tires screaming on the wet pavement.

He turned around.

Miller was gone, and the street was empty.

But the hand was closer. And it was holding a clipboard.
