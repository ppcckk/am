Chapter 289: License Invalid

# Chapter 289

The red warning strobe cut through the sterile white of the archive room, painting Vane's frantic movements in pulses of crimson. Kowalski stood perfectly still, holding the two halves of his Level II license like a broken talisman. The hum in his bones had shifted pitch. It wasn't a roar anymore; it was a high-frequency shriek, the sound of a microwave magnetron failing.

**ERROR: LICENSE INVALID.** **STATUS: UNLICENSED ENTITY DETECTED.** **ACTION: AUTOMATIC PURGE INITIATED.**

Vane slammed his shoulder against the steel door. The bar didn't budge. He turned, eyes wide, scanning the room for an exit that wasn't there.

"Kowalski! Do something!"

Kowalski didn't look at Vane. He looked at the printer. The paper tray was jammed. A single sheet of thermal paper was ejecting, inch by inch, trailing off the machine and pooling on the floor like a white snake.

The floor beneath them vibrated again, harder this time. The shelves of metal boxes rattled, then slid across their metal rails. The gravitational pull of the Rift was already bleeding through the walls, warping the local physics of the basement.

"Move the shelves," Kowalski said. His voice was flat, devoid of panic. It was the voice he used when telling a tenant that their infestation was a structural issue, not a cleaning issue.

"What?"

"The shelves. Push them.

Create a perimeter.

The Authority's purge protocol is spatially aware. It targets the centroid of the infestation. That's me.

"

Vane stared at him. The red light washed over his sweat-slicked face.

"You're saying the building is trying to kill you because you're the bug?"

"I'm saying the building is trying to exterminate the source of the infestation. And since I am now unlicensed, the system no longer recognizes me as a technician. It recognizes me as a contaminant."

Kowalski walked toward the printer. He didn't touch the machine. He stepped back, keeping his hands in his pockets.

"Vane. The form. On the floor. The one the machine just printed."

Vane scrambled to the pool of paper. He grabbed the trailing end and pulled. The paper tore with a sound like a gunshot. He held up a single, crumpled page.

It wasn't data. It was a form.

**FORM 77-B: TEMPORARY STATUS CHANGE REQUEST.** **SUBMITTING ENTITY: DALE KOWALSKI.** **PREVIOUS CLASS: II.** **REQUESTED CLASS: NULL.** **JUSTIFICATION: SELF-CONTAINMENT FAILURE.**

"Read it," Kowalski said.

"I can't read this! It's gibberish! Runes and barcodes!"

"It's not gibberish. It's a liability waiver." Kowalski pointed a gloved finger at the bottom of the page. "Sign it."

Vane looked at the door, then at Kowalski. The floor tilted ten degrees to the left. A metal shelf crashed down from the ceiling, crushing a desk where they had been standing seconds ago. Dust choked the air.

"If I sign this, what happens to you?"

"I cease to exist as a licensed entity in the eyes of the Authority. The purge protocol targets the *role*, not the *person*. If I am not a technician, I am not a certified threat. I am just meat."

"That's insane."

"It's procedural."

Kowalski pulled a pen from his vest. It was a cheap ballpoint, blue ink, chewed at the cap. He handed it to Vane.

"Sign it.

Use the date. Use your name.

It doesn't matter if it's legally binding in Ohio.

The Authority doesn't care about Ohio law. It cares about paperwork.

"

Vane's hands shook. He uncapped the pen. The tip hovered over the dotted line labeled *Witness*.

**WARNING: PURGE PROTOCOL OMEGA: T-MINUS 30 SECONDS.**

The text floated in the air, large and jagged, superimposed over Kowalski's vision.

"Do it, Vane."

Vane scribbled his name. *Vane. J.*

Kowalski snatched the form. He didn't look at the signature. He walked to the printer and fed the paper back into the tray. The machine whirred, a harsh, grinding sound.

The progress bar on the screen reset.

**PROCESSING... 10%... 40%... 80%...**

The red strobes stopped. The hum in Kowalski's bones vanished.

Silence rushed back into the room, heavy and suffocating. The shelves stopped sliding. The floor leveled out.

Then, the screen flashed green.

**STATUS: LICENSE REVOKED.** **ENTITY CLASS: NULL.** **TREATMENT POINTS: 0.** **FIELD GUIDE: LOCKED.**

Kowalski slumped. He didn't fall, but his knees buckled.

He caught himself on the edge of the desk.

His face was gray.

The veins in his neck stood out, blue and prominent.

"You did it," Vane whispered. He walked to the door. He pulled on the handle. It clicked open.

"Get out," Kowalski said.

"No. We go together."

Kowalski looked at his hands. They were trembling. Not from fear.

From withdrawal. The connection to the System, the constant trickle of data and points and validation, had been severed.

He felt hollowed out.

"Go," Kowalski said. "The door is open. The Authority won't purge a Null-class entity. But they will monitor it."

Vane grabbed Kowalski's arm.

"You're still coming."

"I'm not licensed. I have no Field Guide. I have no protocols.

If we run into a Class-3 or higher, I can't even identify it, let alone fight it.

I'm just a man with a clipboard and no authority.

"

"Then we find a way to get you re-certified."

Kowalski laughed, which was a dry, rasping sound.

"You don't understand. Revocation is permanent unless you pass the Appeals Board.

And the Appeals Board is located in the Deep Rift.

"

Vane froze. "The Deep Rift? That's where the Source is."

"Exactly.

The Authority doesn't punish mistakes, and they punish incompetence. And revoking your license is the ultimate punishment.

It forces you to earn it back from the bottom, in the worst possible conditions.

"

Kowalski straightened his vest. He smoothed the front of it, a reflexive gesture. He reached into his pocket and pulled out a small, spiral-bound notebook. It was old, the cover stained with coffee and blood.

"I still have my notes," he said. "My personal notes. Not the System's data. My observations."

Vane stared at the notebook.

"What good are they?"

"They're evidence."

"Of what?"

Kowalski opened the notebook. The pages were filled with cramped handwriting, sketches of anatomy, notes on behavior, lists of entry points.

"Of the fact that the Authority is lying," Kowalski said.

Vane looked up.

"What?"

"Look at Form 77-B. Look at the justification."

Vane looked at the screen, which had reverted to a blank green interface.

"It said *Self-Containment Failure*," Vane read.

"It's a standard code," Kowalski said. "But look at the header. It's from the *Department of Internal Audit*."

Vane frowned. "So?"

"The Authority doesn't have an Internal Audit department. They have a Licensing Bureau.

They have a Certification Board, and they have an Enforcement Division. Audit is for checking compliance. Not for revoking licenses.

"

Kowalski tapped the notebook.

"I didn't lose my license because I was incompetent. I lost it because I got too close to the truth. The machine printed that form before I tore the license. It was waiting for me."

Vane stepped back.

"Waiting for you?"

"The Source isn't just a monster, Vane.

It's a breach in the system. And the Authority knows it.

They know that if they let a Level II technician document it properly, the whole structure collapses.

So they rigged the purge. They made sure I would trigger a violation if I tried to upload the data. They wanted me to self-revoke.

"

"That's... that's a conspiracy."

"It's a cover-up. And now that I'm Null-class, I'm invisible to them. I can move without triggering their sensors."

Vane looked at the open door. The hallway beyond was dark, lit only by emergency lights.

"Where do we go?"

Kowalski closed the notebook and tucked it into his inner pocket. He picked up his flashlight.

It was dead, and he shook it.

Nothing.

"The basement," Kowalski said. "There's a maintenance tunnel under the archive. It leads to the sewer main."

"Why the sewer?"

"Because that's where the infestation is actually starting. The Rift isn't opening in the sky. It's seeping up from the groundwater.

The Authority's sensors are calibrated for aerial incursions. They're blind to the sub-basement.

"

Vane hesitated.

"You're risking your life on a hunch."

"I'm risking my life on a code violation," Kowalski corrected. "Section 4, Paragraph B: *Unauthorized access to restricted infrastructure is permissible if it prevents a greater public health hazard.*"

Vane shook his head.

"You're still talking like a technician."

"I'm talking like an exterminator. We treat the source."

They moved into the hallway.

The air smelled of ozone and rot. Kowalski moved carefully, testing each step. His professional caution was the only thing keeping him upright.

Without the System's HUD to warn him of structural weaknesses, he had to rely on his instincts.

At the end of the hall, a staircase spiraled down into darkness.

"Lead the way," Vane said.

Kowalski paused at the top of the stairs. He looked back at the archive room. The door was still open. Inside, the printer was still humming.

**NEW ALERT: UNLICENSED ENTITY MOVEMENT DETECTED.** **LOCATION: SECTOR 4, BASEMENT LEVEL.** **RECOMMENDED ACTION: DEPLOY PURGE DRONES.**

Kowalski didn't run. He walked down the stairs. One step. Then another.

"Vane. How many drones do you think they'll send?"

"I don't know. Hundreds?"

"Probably forty. They'll sweep the main corridors first. We have maybe three minutes before they lock down the lower levels."

"Then we better be fast."

"We don't need to be fast," Kowalski said. "We need to be thorough."

He reached the bottom of the stairs. The tunnel was narrow, damp, and lined with concrete. Pipes ran along the ceiling, dripping condensation.

Kowalski pulled out his flashlight. He clicked it. It flickered. The battery was dying.

"Here," Vane said, holding up his own torch. "Use mine."

Kowalski took it. He shone the beam into the tunnel.

The light caught something wet on the wall.

It wasn't water, which was slime. Thick, translucent, and pulsing.

Kowalski leaned in. He didn't touch it. He used the tip of his flashlight to scrape a sample. The slime stretched, sticky and fibrous.

"Chitin residue," Kowalski muttered. "High concentration."

"What is it?"

"A trail. Something big crawled through here recently. And it left pheromones."

Vane gagged. "Can you identify the species?"

Kowalski closed his eyes. He tried to access his Field Guide.

Nothing, and the mental interface was gone. The silence in his head was deafening.

"I can't access the guide," Kowalski said.

"So guess."

"I don't guess. I document."

Kowalski pulled out his notebook. He wrote: *Unknown Species.

Slime consistency: viscous.

Color: clear. Pheromone scent: sulfur and ammonia. Size estimate: 2-3 meters.*

He sniffed the air. He felt a chill on the back of his neck.

"Vane. Shut off your torch."

"What?"

"The light attracts them. If this is a subterranean ambush predator, phototaxis is likely negative. They hunt by vibration and scent."

Vane flicked the switch. Darkness swallowed them.

Kowalski stood still. He listened.

There was a sound. A wet, clicking noise. Coming from ahead. And behind.

"They're already here," Kowalski whispered.

"How do we fight them?"

"We don't fight. We contain."

Kowalski reached into his vest. He didn't have any protocols. No stun batons. No spray.

He had a roll of duct tape and a plastic trash bag.

"Vane. Hold this."

He handed Vane the trash bag.

"Unfold it. Slowly. Don't make a sound."

"What are you going to do?"

"I'm going to create a temporary barrier. The pheromones indicate a territorial mark. If we cross it, they attack. If we disrupt the mark, they hesitate."

"That's not a protocol."

"It's biology."

Kowalski moved forward, into the dark. He could feel the creatures now. The air pressure changed. The temperature dropped.

Something brushed against his leg. Soft. Furry.

He didn't flinch. He kept moving.

He reached the center of the tunnel. He could hear breathing. Multiple sources.

He unfolded the trash bag. He held it open.

"Vane. Now."

Vane threw the bag forward. It landed on the wet floor, billowing out in the dark.

Kowalski slammed his foot onto the bag. He pinned it down.

Then, he pulled the duct tape from his belt. He sealed the edges of the bag to the concrete floor.

The clicking sounds grew louder. Closer.

"Go faster," Vane hissed.

"Patience," Kowalski said. He was tearing the tape.

One strip.

Two strips.

Three.

A shape emerged from the darkness. It was low to the ground. Long. Multi-limbed.

It paused at the edge of the bag.

Kowalski didn't look at it. He looked at his hands. He smoothed the tape down.

The creature clicked. It circled the perimeter of the bag.

Kowalski finished sealing the last edge, and he stepped back.

The creature lunged at the bag. Its claws scraped against the plastic. It couldn't penetrate it. The plastic was tough, and Kowalski had secured it tight.

But the creature didn't stop. It vibrated.

The ground shook.

"It's not trapped," Vane said. "It's calling for help."

More clicking sounds. Dozens of them. From both ends of the tunnel.

Kowalski looked at the sealed bag. He looked at his notebook.

He had documented the threat. He had isolated it.

He had not eliminated it.

And now, with no license, no points, and no field guide, he had no way to verify the kill.

The Authority wasn't watching.

The real problem wasn't the monsters.

It was that Kowalski had just become a criminal in the eyes of the only entity that could keep him alive.

**ALERT: LOCAL SECURITY BREACH DETECTED.** **ENTITY: DALE KOWALSKI.** **CRIMINAL CLASS: FELONY INFESTATION NESTING.**

The text didn't appear in his vision. It appeared on a tablet Vane was holding, which had lit up with a police report.

"You just committed a crime," Vane said.

"I prevented a public health hazard," Kowalski said.

"The Authority doesn't care. Look at the timestamp. You're now flagged as a rogue agent."

Kowalski looked at the dark tunnel. The clicking was getting louder.

"We need to move," Kowalski said.

"Where?"

"To the only place they can't follow us."

"Which is?"

Kowalski pointed his flashlight at the far end of the tunnel. There was a grate. A sewer exit.

"The Rift," Kowalski said. "We're going into the source."

Vane looked at the grate. It was rusted. Heavy.

"That's suicide."

"It's the only way to get my license back."

Vane looked at Kowalski. He saw the dead eyes. The absolute certainty.

"You're not going to get it back," Vane said.

Kowalski smiled. It was a thin, cruel smile.

"No. I'm going to destroy it."

He kicked the grate. It groaned, then gave way.

He dropped into the darkness below.

Vane followed.

Behind them, the clicking stopped.

The silence that followed was worse.

Then, a new sound.

A single, sharp *crack*.

The trash bag burst.

Kowalski didn't look back. He knew what it meant.

He had contained the threat, and he had not eliminated it.

And now, the nest was awake.
