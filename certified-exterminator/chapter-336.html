Chapter 336: The Hook in His Chest

# Chapter 336

The white mask tilted.

"Mr. Kowalski."

The voice sounded like dry leaves skittering across pavement. No vibration in the air. No breath behind the words. Just the idea of speech, pressed directly into Dale's auditory nerve.

Dale didn't answer. His right hand drifted to the small of his back, fingers brushing the handle of his retractable telescopic baton.

Not a weapon.

A tool. In a suspension zone, a weapon escalated liability. A tool was defensible.

"Your license is suspended," the Suit said.

It didn't raise its voice. It didn't need to.

The Authority's decree was already ringing in Dale's skull, a high-pitched tinnitus of shame.

"You attempted unauthorized collaboration with a Class-IV anomalous entity. This is a Code 4 breach."

Dale kept his eyes on the crack in the concrete. Sarah's face had vanished. The flesh had retracted, leaving a jagged hole that smelled of ozone and old copper.

"I was performing an extraction," Dale said. His voice was flat.

Procedural.

"The entity was in distress.

Protocol 12-B allows for temporary stabilization of non-hostile targets to preserve evidence integrity.

"

The Suit stepped closer. The emergency bulb above flickered, casting long, jerking shadows against the tunnel walls. The figure's suit was immaculate.

No dust. No blood.

No wrinkles.

It moved without friction, gliding over the debris like oil on water.

"Protocol 12-B requires a signed waiver from the target," the Suit said. "You did not obtain a waiver. You did not log the interaction. You contaminated your Field Guide entry for *Osteus nihil* with unverified sensory data."

Dale's thumb hovered over the baton's release clip, and he didn't draw it. Drawing a weapon against an Authority representative was a Code 9 violation.

Immediate termination.

But standing still was Code 5: Passive Non-Compliance.

"I logged it," Dale lied.

The Suit stopped three feet away. It was close enough that Dale could see the lack of pores in the mask. Smooth. Seamless.

"Show me your clipboard."

Dale held up his clipboard. The *Osteus nihil* page was a mess. Water damage had blurred the ink. The sketch of the bone-construct's jaw hinge was smeared into a gray smudge.

The Suit reached out. Its fingers were long, articulated, and devoid of fingerprints.

It touched the paper.

The smudge vanished.

The original data reappeared, crisp and black.

"Fabrication," the Suit said. "You are attempting to bypass the cross-contamination penalty. This is a Code 6 attempt. Fraudulent reporting."

Dale felt his stomach drop. The suspension wasn't just a pause. It was a purge.

If they erased his current progress, he was back to Level I. Fourteen years of experience, gone.

"What do you want?" Dale asked.

"The Authority does not want," the Suit said.

"The Authority requires. You will surrender your Field Guide. Your current certification tier will be revoked.

You will be reassigned to sanitation duties in Sector 4, and the Rift backlog is growing. We need bodies to clear the low-level waste.

"

"Sector 4 is a dead zone," Dale said. "No one survives a month there. It's just slurry and acid mist."

"It is necessary."

"It's suicide."

"It is compliant."

The Suit held out its hand. The gesture was polite. Bureaucratic. It expected compliance because the system was absolute.

Dale looked at the clipboard in his own hands. Then he looked at the hole in the floor.

"If I refuse," Dale said, "what's the penalty?"

"Immediate termination of employment," the Suit said. "Erasure of all records. You will cease to exist in the registry."

"And if I accept the reassignment?"

"You will live. Briefly."

Dale turned his back on the Suit. He walked to the edge of the hole.

"Mr. Kowalski. Turn around."

Dale didn't turn, and he crouched down. He pulled a small vial of luminous green powder from his utility belt. Phosphor-laced silica.

Used for tracing ant trails in light-sensitive environments.

He poured a line of the powder into the crack. It glowed faintly in the dark.

"What are you doing?" The Suit's voice tightened. The dry-leaf texture sharpened into something brittle.

"Documenting the exit vector," Dale said.

"You said I contaminated the entry with Sarah.

That means the entity *Osteus nihil* was still active inside her. If it was active, it left a residue.

A chemical signature.

"

"That is irrelevant. Your license is suspended."

"Look at the powder."

The Suit did not move. But the air around it rippled. A subtle distortion, like heat haze.

"The powder is reacting," Dale said. "It's turning blue."

Blue meant necrotic tissue. Dead matter. But it also meant something else in the Authority's color-coding system. It meant *residual intent*.

"That's a glitch," the Suit said. "The suspension should have halted all protocol functions."

"Glitches don't happen in Section 8," Dale said. He stood up.

He turned to face the Suit.

"You're not an Authority agent. You're an Auditor."

The Suit remained silent.

"An Auditor doesn't enforce laws," Dale continued.

"An Auditor finds holes. You didn't suspend me because I broke a rule.

You suspended me because you were testing if I would break the rule to save a life.

I failed the test. So you're trying to erase me because I'm a risk.

"

"I am an Agent of the Licensing Authority," the Suit corrected.

"Then why are you checking my clipboard manually?" Dale pointed at the Suit's own clipboard. "Real Agents don't verify data on-site. They rely on the System. You're using a secondary verification because the System is wrong."

The Suit's hand twitched. A microscopic adjustment in posture.

Dale pressed on.

"The *Osteus nihil* entry," Dale said. "You said I contaminated it with unverified data.

But the System didn't flag it as contamination.

It flagged it as *unauthorized assistance*.

That's a different category. It means I helped the monster.

Which means the monster wasn't a threat. It was a client.

"

The Suit tilted its head. The mask remained still, but the shadow behind it shifted.

"Explain."

"Sarah wasn't screaming," Dale said. "She was whispering. *Don't let them in.* She wasn't attacking me.

She was warning me. *Osteus nihil* isn't a parasite.

It's a carrier. And it wasn't infesting her. It was shielding her.

"

Dale tapped his clipboard.

"Look at the Frass analysis from the previous job.

The one in the duplex. You gave me a 9.2 Thoroughness Rating.

You praised my attention to detail.

So why is this one rated as a violation?

"

"The rating was based on technical compliance," the Suit said.

"Technical compliance doesn't cover intent," Dale said. "And the Authority doesn't care about intent.

That's the loophole, and the Authority is bureaucratic, not moral.

It doesn't judge *why* you kill things, only *how* you document it. By suspending me, you admitted that the documentation was valid.

You just didn't like the conclusion.

"

Dale took a step forward. The Suit didn't retreat.

"If *Osteus nihil* is a carrier," Dale said, "then the Rift isn't an infestation. It's a migration path. And the things coming through aren't pests. They're refugees."

"That is a dangerous hypothesis," the Suit said.

"It's a valid identification. And if it's valid, then my Field Guide is correct. Which means my suspension is invalid. Because you can't suspend a technician for correctly identifying a species, even if that species is technically 'non-hostile.'"

The Suit stared at him. For a long moment, the tunnel was silent. The emergency bulb buzzed. The green powder glowed blue in the crack.

Then, the System flashed in Dale's vision.

*SYSTEM ALERT: COMPLIANCE REVIEW INITIATED.* *SUBJECT: DALE KOWALSKI.* *ISSUE: SEMANTIC AMBIGUITY IN SPECIES CLASSIFICATION.* *STATUS: PENDING.*

The Suit's hand lowered.

"You are exploiting a definition error," the Suit said.

"I am exploiting a bureaucratic oversight," Dale corrected. "If you classify *Osteus nihil* as a threat, you have to exterminate it.

If you exterminate it, you have to document the kill.

If you document the kill, you have to admit you killed a sentient being that was trying to protect a human. That's a Code 10 violation. Genocide.

Unlicensed extermination of a protected class.

"

The Suit's mask tilted again.

"Protected classes are defined by the Authority."

"Then the Authority is wrong. Or you are.

And if you're wrong, I win.

If the Authority is wrong, I win. Because either way, my Field Guide is the only accurate record of the event.

"

Dale held out his clipboard.

"Reinstate my license. Or charge me with Genocide.

Your choice.

But you have to pick one.

And you have to do it now, before the next inspection cycle. Because if I walk away with this data, I can submit it to the Oversight Committee.

And they will audit you, not me.

"

The Suit stood still.

"You are bluffing," the Suit said.

"I am a Level III Technician," Dale said. "I don't bluff.

I file reports.

And I just filed a mental report with you. It's in your head now. You can't delete it.

You're the witness.

"

The Suit raised its clipboard. It wrote something. Fast. Aggressive strokes.

Then it looked at Dale.

"Your license is reinstated. Conditional."

Dale exhaled. His knees felt watery. He gripped the clipboard to stop his hands from shaking.

"Conditions?" Dale asked.

"You will be assigned to the High-Containment Unit," the Suit said. "You will not work alone.

You will be paired with an Auditor.

You will document everything. And you will answer to me.

"

Dale looked at the Suit. The mask gave nothing away.

"Name?" Dale asked.

"Unit 7," the Suit said. "Call me Seven."

Seven turned and walked toward the tunnel exit. Its footsteps made no sound.

"Come, Mr. Kowalski. The infestation in Sector 4 is worsening. We need to see if your definition of 'refugee' holds up under fire."

Dale looked at the hole in the floor. The blue powder was fading. Sarah was gone.

He followed Seven.

But as he walked, he checked his Field Guide. The entry for *Osteus nihil* had changed.

*STATUS: PROTECTED (CONDITIONAL).* *WARNING: HIGH-VOLATILITY CARRIER.* *NOTE: HOSTILE TO UNLICENSED OBSERVERS.*

Dale frowned. Unlicensed observers.

He looked at Seven's back. The suit was perfect. But as they moved deeper into the dark, Dale noticed something.

Seven didn't cast a shadow.

And on Seven's clipboard, the ink wasn't black. It was red.

*SYSTEM ALERT: NEW OBJECTIVE DETECTED.* *TARGET: UNIDENTIFIED HIGH-LEVEL ANOMALY.* *CLASSIFICATION:?* *ACTION: OBSERVE AND DOCUMENT.*

Dale stopped.

"Seven," Dale said. "What is the target?"

Seven didn't turn around.

"It is the thing that made the mask," Seven said.

Dale felt the pull in his chest again. But this time, it wasn't a leash.

It was a hook.
