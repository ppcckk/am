Chapter 255: Technicians Follow Protocol

# Chapter 255

The chain rattled. A sound like a church bell struck with a hammer, but wet.

Dale kicked his legs. The harness bit into his ribs, tight enough to bruise but loose enough to allow circulation.

Protocol 4-C: Restraint must not impede blood flow.

If he passed out, he was dead. If he died, he was waste. If he was waste, the Authority processed him.

Down.

The shaft walls blurred past in streaks of gray stone and black iron. The air grew colder, then hotter, then cold again. Ozone tasted sharp on his tongue, metallic and clean. It scrubbed the smell of the nest from his skin, but it couldn't scrub the fear from his gut.

Sector 7. The Hive Mind. Recycling.

His Level 0 status flashed in his peripheral vision, red and pulsing. No protocols. No Field Guide entries for industrial machinery or eldritch infrastructure. He was a blank slate in a system that demanded specificity.

The arm swung left. A corridor opened.

Not a room. A tunnel. Wide enough for a truck, lined with pipes that pulsed with green fluid. The floor was grated steel, vibrating with a low hum that vibrated Dale's teeth.

The arm stopped.

Dale dropped, and he hit the grate with a grunt, rolling forward to distribute the impact.

His knees scraped against the metal. Pain flared, sharp and immediate.

Good.

Pain meant he was still biological. Still an organism. Still eligible for inspection.

He scrambled up, swaying. The harness retracted, snapping upward into the darkness with a final clank.

He was alone.

Except for the sound.

A wet, rhythmic clicking. Like a thousand fingernails tapping on glass.

Dale froze. His hand went to his belt, where his clipboard used to be.

Empty, and he had nothing.

No spray. No sealant.

No flashlight.

Just the clothes on his back and the license in his mind.

**LOCATION UPDATED: SECTOR 7 – PRIMARY INTAKE.** **STATUS: UNPROCESSED WASTE.** **TIME TO DECOMPOSITION: 00:14:32.**

Fourteen minutes.

Dale checked his wrists. No watch.

He counted the seconds in his head.

One-Mississippi. Two-Mississippi. The clicking grew louder.

From the shadows at the end of the corridor, shapes emerged.

Not rats. Not beetles.

They were humanoid, but wrong.

Their limbs were too long, their joints bending backward. Their skin was translucent, revealing the black sludge pumping beneath.

They moved in a synchronized wave, no leader, no follower.

Just mass.

Hive drones.

Dale backed away, his boots slipping on the oily grate. He needed a weapon. He needed a protocol. He needed a lie.

The Authority rewarded thoroughness. Thoroughness required identification. Identification required observation. Observation required time.

He had fourteen minutes.

Dale raised his hands, palms out. A universal sign of surrender, or perhaps, a request for inspection.

"I am a licensed technician," he said. His voice was flat, procedural. "I require a visual assessment before processing."

The drones did not stop. The clicking intensified. A low, collective chitter that sounded like static.

One drone stepped forward. Its face was a smooth, featureless oval.

No eyes.

No mouth.

Just sensory pits arranged in a spiral.

It lunged.

Dale didn't run. He couldn't. The corridor was narrow. Instead, he dropped to his knees.

The drone swiped, claws tearing through the air where his head had been. The wind of the strike ruffled his hair.

Dale looked up. Not at the drone's face. At its legs.

*System Alert: Potential Infestation Detected.* *Type: Unknown.* *Action: Document.*

Dale stared at the drone's left hind leg. The joint was swollen. A dark, viscous fluid bubbled from a crack in the translucent skin.

Necrosis.

Not a disease. A structural failure. These things were being recycled. They were parts.

"Subject exhibits severe joint degradation," Dale muttered. He pointed. "See the exudate?

That's not blood. That's lubricant breakdown.

They're dry-running.

"

The Authority paused.

**IDENTIFICATION PARTIAL.** *Category: Biological Automaton.* *Weakness Detected: Lubrication Dependency.*

A text box appeared in Dale's vision.

**TARGETED DAMAGE BONUS AVAILABLE.** *Target: Dry Joints.* *Cost: Requires Physical Contact.*

Physical contact. Dale had no weapon. No spray.

He looked at the grate beneath him. The pipes. The green fluid.

Coolant? Waste fluid? It didn't matter.

It was liquid.

And liquids could be applied.

Dale grabbed the edge of the grate, which was rusted. He pulled. His fingers slipped, then caught.

He yanked. The metal groaned, then tore free.

A heavy, jagged slab of steel.

The drone shrieked. It didn't understand steel. It understood biology.

Dale didn't attack the drone. He attacked the pipe.

He swung the steel slab into the nearest conduit. The pipe ruptured. Green fluid sprayed out, coating the drone.

The creature convulsed.

The fluid wasn't just liquid. It was acidic.

It hissed against the drone's translucent skin. The joint swelling erupted. The leg snapped off.

**SYSTEM ALERT: TARGETED DAMAGE APPLIED.** *Damage Type: Chemical Corrosion.* *Efficiency: High.*

The other drones paused. The clicking stopped.

They weren't attacking. They were analyzing.

Dale stood up, breathing hard. The green fluid dripped from his hands.

It burned, but only slightly.

Mild acid. pH 4. Maybe 3.

He looked at the pipe. It was leaking. A steady stream of green goo flowed onto the grate.

The drones moved. Not toward him. Toward the leak. They dipped their limbs into the fluid, their movements jerky.

They were refueling.

Dale realized his mistake. He hadn't eliminated the threat. He had fed it.

Cross-contamination penalty. He had used a fluid protocol (chemical application) on a biological automaton that required specific lubricants. The Authority hadn't flagged it yet, but the feedback loop was building.

**WARNING: CROSS-CONTAMINATION RISK INCREASING.** *Protocol Misapplication Detected.* *Field Guide Entry Compromised: Biological Automaton.*

His mental image of the drone blurred. The data he had just gathered—the necrosis, the joints—was fading. In twenty-four hours, it would be gone. And if it was gone, he couldn't use the weakness again.

He had traded a tactical advantage for a temporary reprieve.

A new sound cut through the hiss of the acid.

Heavy footsteps.

Not the clicking of drones. The heavy, deliberate thud of boots.

A figure walked into the corridor. Tall. Dressed in a hazmat suit that looked like it had been sewn from the hides of the drones themselves. The mask was a single piece of glass, fogged with breath.

The figure stopped ten feet away. It tilted its head.

Dale wiped his hands on his pants. The acid stain remained.

"You're out of uniform," the figure said. The voice was distorted, synthesized. "You're out of protocol."

Dale straightened his spine.

"I am a Level II Technician. I was misclassified as waste. I am requesting re-evaluation."

The figure stepped closer. It held a clipboard.

Dale felt a jolt of recognition. Not the object. The *posture*. The way the figure held the clipboard against its chest, protective, almost religious.

"Classification is final," the figure said. "Sector 7 does not accept appeals. Only inputs."

"I am an input," Dale said.

"I am providing data. The drones are malfunctioning.

Their lubrication systems are failing.

If you continue intake without maintenance, you will suffer a cascade failure.

"

The figure went still.

Behind it, the drones were twitching. Some had collapsed. Others were crawling over each other, trying to reach the leaking pipe.

The figure looked at the drones. Then at Dale.

"How do you know that?"

"Because I saw the necrosis," Dale said. "And because I'm an exterminator.

We don't just kill pests.

We manage the environment.

If the environment fails, the pests survive longer. You want efficient recycling.

You want fast throughput. But you're bottlenecking the intake because your machinery is breaking down.

"

The figure lowered the clipboard.

**SYSTEM ALERT: INTERLOCUTOR IDENTIFIED.** *Name: Unit 7-Kappa.* *Role: Sector Supervisor.* *Threat Level: High.*

Kappa. A supervisor. Not a monster. A bureaucrat.

Dale's mind raced. He couldn't fight Kappa. He could outmaneuver him.

"I can fix the leak," Dale said. "Temporarily. I can stop the cascade. In exchange, I want my classification reviewed."

Kappa laughed. A dry, rasping sound.

"You think you are special?

Level 0. No protocols. No tools.

You broke a pipe.

You caused a spill. That is a violation of Code 12-Alpha: Unauthorized Modification of Infrastructure.

"

"It's a repair," Dale said.

"It is vandalism," Kappa said. "And now you are liable."

Kappa raised a hand.

A device on his wrist clicked. A containment field snapped around Dale. Invisible, but he felt the pressure against his skin.

Static electricity. It made his hair stand up.

"You will be detained," Kappa said. "Process: Punitive Incineration."

Dale's heart hammered.

Incineration.

Direct burn. No transport.

No move, and he looked at the clipboard in Kappa's hand.

"Your report," Dale said. "Is it filed?"

Kappa paused. "It is in draft."

"Drafts are subject to audit," Dale said. "If you incinerate me without filing the incident report, the Authority will flag you for procedural negligence.

You have a backlog, Kappa.

I can see the drones. They're not processing. They're stuck.

If I'm gone, who documents the repair?

Who signs off on the maintenance?

"

Kappa's glass mask fogged further.

Dale pressed. He had to sound like he knew the rules better than Kappa did.

"The Authority rewards thoroughness," Dale said.

"Not speed. If you burn me, you lose the witness. If you keep me, you get a free technician to document the failure.

You get to close the ticket. You get your bonus.

"

Silence.

The clicking of the drones softened. The green fluid pooled on the grate.

Kappa looked at the pool. Then at Dale.

"You have five minutes," Kappa said. "Fix the pipe. Or I burn you."

Dale didn't hesitate. He didn't ask for tools. He didn't ask for instructions.

He walked to the pipe, and he looked at the rupture. It was a clean tear in the metal. Caused by his steel slab.

He needed a patch.

He looked around. The grate. The steel slab he had torn off earlier. It was lying on the floor, coated in green sludge.

He picked it up. Heavy. Awkward.

He couldn't weld. He couldn't screw it in.

He needed to seal it, and he looked at his own clothes. His polyester work shirt. Flammable. Useless.

He looked at the drone leg lying on the ground. The one that had snapped off. It was hard, chitinous. And it was covered in the green fluid.

Adhesive, and the fluid was acidic, but it was also viscous. It hardened when exposed to air.

Dale grabbed the drone leg. He dipped it into the pool of green fluid. The leg became coated in a thick, gooey layer.

He slammed the leg against the pipe rupture, and he held it there.

The fluid hardened. It sizzled against the metal. It ate into the steel, but it also bonded with it.

Dale watched the reaction. His hands shook. The heat from the reaction radiated against his palms.

**SYSTEM ALERT: ADHESIVE APPLICATION DETECTED.** *Material: Biological Resin.* *Application Method: Manual Pressure.* *Efficiency: Sub-optimal.*

Sub-optimal. But it was working. The flow slowed. Then stopped.

Dale held the leg in place. He counted the seconds. One-Mississippi. Two-Mississippi.

Ten seconds. Twenty. Thirty.

The fluid solidified into a black, brittle crust.

Dale let go.

The pipe held, and he stepped back, wiping his hands on his pants again. The skin was red, irritated.

Kappa stepped forward. He inspected the patch. He touched it with a gloved finger.

"Crude," Kappa said. "But functional."

"I identified the material," Dale said. "I applied it. I verified the seal."

Kappa looked at the field generator on his wrist. He turned it off. The static pressure vanished.

Dale's knees nearly buckled.

"You have documented the incident," Kappa said. "For now."

"For now?"

Kappa tapped his clipboard. A screen lit up.

**JOB STATUS: PENDING REVIEW.** *Technician: Dale Kowalski.* *Rating: Provisional.*

"You are no longer waste," Kappa said. "You are a liability. Sector 7 requires personnel who can maintain intake efficiency. You have demonstrated a basic aptitude for improvisation under pressure."

"I am a pest control technician," Dale said. "Not a plumber."

"In this sector, the distinction is academic," Kappa said. "The infestation is the infrastructure. The infrastructure is the infestation."

He turned and walked away.

"Follow me. Your orientation begins now. And if you fail the next assessment, I will not file a report. I will simply incinerate you."

Dale followed, and he walked into the heart of Sector 7.

The corridor opened into a vast cavern.

And there, hanging from the ceiling, were thousands of cages.

Each cage contained a person.

Not dead. Alive. Connected to tubes. Drained.

Dale stopped.

In the center of the room, a massive machine pulsed. It was built of bone and steel. And it was feeding.

Feeding on the memories of the people in the cages.

Kappa pointed to a console.

"New protocol assigned," Kappa said. "Extraction of Cognitive Data. Target: Human. Method: Non-Lethal."

Dale stared at the console, and he knew how to extract data. He knew how to document.

But he also knew the cost.

If he did this, he became part of the machine.

If he refused, he died.

**SYSTEM ALERT: NEW PROTOCOL UNLOCKED.** *Protocol 1-A: Cognitive Harvesting.* *Requirement: Consent or Subjugation.*

Dale looked at the nearest cage. A woman inside. Her eyes were open. Empty.

She looked at him.

Not with fear.

With recognition, and she mouthed one word.

*Help.*

Dale looked at the console.

He looked at Kappa.

Kappa was watching. Waiting.

Dale placed his hand on the console.

His finger hovered over the START button.

The Authority didn't want a hero. It wanted a technician.

And technicians follow protocol.

Dale pressed the button.
