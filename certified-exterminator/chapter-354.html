Chapter 354: Certification Level Sacrifice

# Chapter 354

The darkness above the warehouse stairs smelled of sulfur and rotting hair.

Dale clicked his pen. The sound was sharp, final, a tiny mechanical chirp against the wet slithering rising from the floor below.

He didn't look up, and he couldn't see the ceiling in the blackness, but his nose knew the altitude. The stench of ozone and pheromones thinned as he climbed.

Below, the worms churned. A low, grinding friction like tectonic plates rubbing together.

*System Notification:* **FIELD GUIDE UPDATE PENDING.** **SUBJECT:** *Annelida profundus* (Unidentified Variant) **STATUS:** UNVERIFIED. **THREAT LEVEL:** UNKNOWN.

Dale ignored the red text blinking in his peripheral vision, and he kept climbing. One step.

Two.

The stairs were metal, rusted through in places, groaning under his weight. His boots squeaked against the iron.

He reached the top landing. The door to the upper rafters was padlocked.

Not a good sign. A padlock implied human presence. Or at least, human intent.

He pulled a bolt cutter from his belt. The jaws snapped open with a metallic *clack* that echoed too in the confined space.

He didn't care about the noise.

Noise was data.

If the colony reacted to vibration, the reaction pattern would confirm their sensory range.

He clipped the hasp. The lock fell away, clattering down the stairwell. It didn't hit the ground. Something below caught it.

Dale stepped through the doorway, and the space above was a cathedral of hair. Thick, black ropes hung from the exposed beams, dripping viscous fluid onto the metal grating. The air was thick, humid, warm. It felt like the inside of a throat.

He raised his flashlight. The beam cut through the haze, illuminating thousands of writhing strands. Not just hanging.

Moving. They shifted in unison, a slow, rhythmic undulation that matched the breathing of the building itself.

*System Prompt:* **INSPECTION REQUIRED.** **DOCUMENT STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY.** **IDENTIFY COLONY CORE.**

Dale clicked on his headlamp.

The white light flooded the immediate area. He adjusted the lens to maximum spread. He needed to see the layout.

The geometry of the infestation.

If they were a colony, they had a hierarchy. Workers.

Soldiers.

Brood. And a Queen.

He walked forward. The floor was a grid of steel mesh. Through the holes, he could see the worms below, a writhing carpet of muscle and chitin. Up here, the hair bundles were anchored to the ceiling joists with what looked like resinous nodules.

He stopped at the center of the room. The density of the hair changed here.

Thicker.

Darker.

The strands pulsed with a faint, bioluminescent red glow, and he pulled out his clipboard.

His hands were steady. They were always steady when he was writing. That was the flaw, the anxiety, the thing that kept him alive.

The paperwork was the shield. If he wrote it down, it was real.

If it was real, he could control it, and he uncapped his pen.

*Entry 354-A:* **LOCATION:** Upper Rafter Zone, Sector 4. **SUBJECT:** *Annelida profundus* (Colony Variant). **OBSERVATION:** Biomass anchored to structural steel. No soil substrate. Nutrient source unclear. **HYPOTHESIS:** Parasitic colonization of infrastructure.

He paused. The pen hovered.

Nutrient source. They weren't eating the metal. They weren't eating the rats.

They were eating the *building*.

The electrical wiring. The concrete dust.

The heat, and he looked closer at the resinous nodules. They weren't just glue. They were feeding tubes.

The worms below were pumping slurry up through these hair-roots. The colony wasn't an infestation of the space.

It was an infestation of the *energy grid*.

*System Notification:* **NEW FIELD GUIDE ENTRY GENERATED:** *Annelida profundus* (Symbiotic Variant). **WEAKNESS IDENTIFIED:** Thermal Instability. **DAMAGE BONUS:** +15% against Fire-Based Protocols.

Dale stared at the blue text.

Fire.

Vane had a flare gun.

But Vane was downstairs. And Dale was alone in the throat of the beast.

He needed to prove it, and he needed to verify the weakness. He couldn't just guess.

The Authority didn't pay for guesses. It paid for verified extermination protocols.

He reached into his tool belt and pulled out a small canister of compressed propane. A spot treatment tool. Meant for killing wasp nests in wall voids.

It wasn't enough to kill the colony. But it was enough to test the reaction.

He aimed the nozzle at the nearest cluster of red-glowing strands. He squeezed the trigger.

A jet of blue flame hissed out.

The hair didn't burn. It *recoiled*.

The strands snapped back, whipping away from the heat, revealing a pale, translucent membrane beneath the black outer layer.

The membrane ruptured. A cloud of spores erupted from the wound, drifting down toward the floor.

Dale coughed, turning his head away, but he kept his eyes on the spot.

The spores weren't just smoke, and they were solid. Microscopic eggs?

No.

Waste products.

The reaction was immediate. The worms below surged upward.

The noise changed from a grind to a shriek.

High-pitched.

Violent.

The colony knew he was there.

And it knew what he had done.

*System Alert:* **CROSS-CONTAMINATION WARNING.** **PROTOCOL MISMATCH.** **YOU HAVE USED A WASP TREATMENT ON A VERMIN COLONY.** **PENALTY APPLIED:** 40% DEBUFF TO ALL FUTURE ACTIONS FOR 24 HOURS. **FIELD GUIDE ENTRY CORRUPTED.**

Dale's heart skipped a beat. Not from fear. From anger.

A debuff. A penalty. Because he had used a Class II tool on a Class IV threat. Sloppy work.

He couldn't fight with a debuff. His reflexes would be sluggish. His aim off.

He needed to fix it. He needed to document the *correct* protocol before he used it.

He grabbed his radio. Static hissed in his ear.

"Vane," Dale said. His voice was flat, controlled. "Do not fire the flare gun."

A pause. Then Vane's voice, tinny and distant.

"They're coming up the stairs.

I can't hold them back, Dale. They're fast.

"

"I know," Dale said.

"Listen to me. The weakness is thermal. But not direct flame.

The spores are the threat.

If you burn them raw, the spores go airborne. You get infected.

I get infected.

The whole city gets infected.

"

Silence.

"What are you doing up there?" Vane asked.

"Adjusting the report," Dale said.

He looked at the glowing strands. The red pulse was faster now. Agitated.

He pulled out a second tool. A UV sterilization wand. Meant for mold and bacteria. It emitted high-intensity ultraviolet light.

*System Check:* **PROTOCOL:** UV STERILIZATION. **TARGET:** MICROORGANISM. **MISMATCH:** HIGH. **PENALTY:** INCREASED.

He didn't use it. He held it up, letting the light illuminate the translucent membranes.

The light didn't hurt the worms. But it revealed something else.

The membranes were thin.

Paper-thin.

And behind them, a rhythmic pulsing.

Not a heart. A valve.

They were pumping heat.

Dale realized the error in his first hypothesis. They weren't just eating energy. They were *converting* it.

The colony was a biological radiator. It was cooling the Rift below by absorbing the thermal energy and dispersing it through the hair.

If he cut the hair, the heat had nowhere to go.

If he burned the hair, the spores spread.

The correct protocol wasn't fire. It was *asphyxiation*.

He needed to seal the vents, and he looked around the room. The roof was open to the rafters. There was no way to seal it from here.

Unless he brought the roof down.

He looked at the structural beams.

The ones supporting the hair. They were steel. But the resin nodules holding the hair were organic.

Hard.

Like amber, and he could't cut them. He could't burn them.

He could freeze them.

He pulled out his last emergency flare. Not the gun. A handheld flare. Designed for signaling.

He lit it. The red flame sputtered in the dark.

He didn't throw it. He held it under the nearest cluster of resin nodules.

The heat from the flare touched the resin. It didn't burn. It softened. The amber-like substance dripped.

Dale watched the consistency change. It went from solid to viscous.

He moved to the next node. And the next.

He was not killing them. He was loosening them.

He was preparing for demolition.

Below, the shrieking grew louder. The worms were climbing the walls. He could hear their claws scraping against the metal stairs.

"Vane," Dale said. "I need you to do something illegal."

"I'm already doing a lot of illegal things," Vane replied. "What?"

"Cut the main power breaker to this building. Cut it now. Don't let the Authority stop you. Just cut it."

"Why?"

"Because they're feeding on the heat," Dale said. "And I'm about to make them cold."

"You're going to freeze them?"

"I'm going to make them *static*," Dale said. "And then I'm going to drop the ceiling."

He reached for the central beam. The largest cluster. The Queen's perch.

He held the flare directly against the resin. The heat was intense. His glove smoked. The smell of burning rubber mixed with the sulfur.

The resin softened completely. The hair bundle detached.

It didn't fall. It floated.

For a second, Dale thought he had failed.

Then, the gravity of the detached mass took hold. It dropped.

It hit the floor of the upper level with a wet *slap*.

The worms below stopped moving, and the silence was sudden. Deafening.

Dale looked up. The remaining hair bundles were vibrating. Not in unison. In chaos.

The colony was losing its connection, and he had broken the network.

*System Notification:* **COLONY DISRUPTED.** **TREATMENT POINTS AWARDED:** 50. **THOROUGHNESS RATING:** 4.2 (INCOMPLETE). **NOTE:** YOU HAVE CREATED A HAZARD. THE SPORES ARE NOW UNCONTAINED.

Dale looked at the cloud of dust rising from the detached bundle.

He had saved the building. But he had released the infection.

The spores drifted down through the mesh floor. Into the main warehouse. Into Vane's face.

"Vane," Dale said. "Get out. Now."

"I can't," Vane said.

His voice was different. Wet. Choked.

"I'm stuck. The door is jammed."

Dale ran to the edge of the platform. He looked down.

Vane was standing in the center of the warehouse. The worms were surrounding him. But they weren't attacking. They were circling.

And Vane was coughing, and he was pulling at his neck.

Dale saw the skin beneath Vane's collar. It was bubbling. Red veins spreading across his throat.

The spores had found a host.

Dale grabbed the bolt cutters. He started cutting the secondary supports. The ones holding the rest of the hair.

If he dropped the rest, the weight might crush Vane.

But if he didn't, the colony would regroup. And the Queen would wake up.

He had to choose, and he looked at the system prompt.

**THOROUGHNESS SCALE UPDATED.** **CURRENT RATING:** 3.8. **ACTION REQUIRED:** RESTORE STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY OR ACCEPT CONTAINMENT FAILURE.

Dale looked at the Queen. The massive bundle hanging above the center of the room. It was still attached.

He couldn't cut it. Not yet.

He needed to get Vane out, and he looked at the stairs. The worms were blocking them.

He looked at the roof.

There was a skylight. Ten feet up.

Dale climbed onto the railing. He balanced on the thin metal bar. Below him, the void. Above him, the glass.

He pulled out his multi-tool. He extended the glass cutter.

He scored the skylight, and the glass was thick. Reinforced.

He scored it. Once. Twice. Three times.

The glass didn't break. It held.

He needed force, and he looked at Vane. The man was on his knees. The red veins had reached his eyes.

Dale made a decision.

He didn't break the glass. He broke the railing.

He swung his body weight against the support beam. The metal groaned. The rusted joint gave way.

The platform tilted.

Dale slid toward the edge. He caught himself with one hand. The other held the glass cutter.

He looked down at Vane.

"Duck," Dale said.

He kicked the broken railing section. It fell.

It didn't hit Vane. It hit the worms.

The impact shattered the chitinous shells of the frontline soldiers. The worms recoiled.

A path opened.

Vane didn't move. He was frozen. The infection was taking hold.

Dale had to go down there. He had to administer the treatment.

He looked at his hand. His glove was torn. His skin was exposed.

If he went down, he was exposed to the spores.

If he stayed up, Vane died.

And if Vane died, the Authority would classify the zone as a total loss. They would burn the building. With Dale inside.

He looked at the Queen. The bundle was stirring. The red glow was fading. The connection was broken.

Dale pulled the flare from his belt. The flame was dying.

He threw it.

Not at the Queen. Not at the worms.

At the skylight.

The flare struck the glass. It didn't break it.

But the heat from the flare met the cold air of the outside.

The glass cracked.

A spiderweb fracture spread across the pane.

Dale looked at the fracture, and he looked at Vane.

He looked at the Queen.

He closed his eyes.

He remembered the report. *Identify.

Document.

Isolate. Eliminate.

Verify.*

He had isolated the network.

Now he had to eliminate the vector, and he stepped off the platform.

He fell.

Ten feet.

He hit the mesh floor of the upper level, rolling, coming up in a crouch. The air was thick with spores.

He didn't hold his breath. That was a rookie mistake.

He activated his respirator. The mask sealed over his face. The hiss of filtered air was comforting.

He walked toward Vane.

The worms parted for him. They knew he was not prey. He was the auditor.

Vane looked up. His eyes were entirely red. Black veins pulsed in his sclera.

"Dale," Vane whispered. The voice was wet. "It's inside me."

"I know," Dale said.

He pulled a syringe from his belt. Not a weapon. A treatment sample.

He had prepared it an hour ago. A counter-agent for *Rattus profundus* venom.

It was the wrong protocol, and he knew it was the wrong protocol.

But it was the only one he had.

He grabbed Vane's arm. Vane didn't resist. He was too far gone.

Dale injected the sample into Vane's neck, and he watched the veins.

They stopped pulsing.

For three seconds, nothing happened.

Then, Vane's back arched. A sound came out of him. Not a scream. A crack.

Something inside Vane's chest snapped.

Vane collapsed.

Dale stood over him. He waited.

He checked his Field Guide.

**SUBJECT:** *Silas Vane.* **STATUS:** CONTAINED. **NOTE:** HOST REJECTION IMMINENT.

Dale looked at the worms. They were closing in.

He looked at the Queen above, and he looked at the broken skylight.

Rain was starting to fall through the cracks. Cold, Ohio rain.

He had one minute before the Authority's containment team arrived.

He had to finish the report, and he pulled out his clipboard. His hands were shaking.

He wrote: *Protocol Failed. Subject Compromised. Recommend Immediate Incineration.*

Then he looked at Vane's body, and he looked at the black veins.

He looked at the system prompt.

**NEW MECHANIC UNLOCKED:** **HOSTILE Symbiosis.** **You may now merge with an infected subject to gain temporary access to their Field Guide entry.** **COST:** Permanent loss of Certification Level 2.

Dale stared at the text.

He looked at Vane, and he looked at the approaching Authority trucks, their headlights cutting through the rain outside.

He made his choice.
