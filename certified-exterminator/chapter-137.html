Chapter 137: The Auditor's Smile

# Chapter 137

The claw descended.

Dale didn't dodge. Dodging was sloppy.

It created a new entry point, a new disturbance in the perimeter that the Authority's sensors would flag as negligence.

Instead, he dropped his weight, twisting his torso to present the narrowest possible profile. The black fur scraped against his high-visibility vest, leaving three distinct tears in the reflective tape. Pain flared in his ribs, sharp and immediate, but he kept his eyes on the hand.

Not the arm. The hand.

*Canis lupus (Dimensional Variant).* Predator class, and the System had already assigned the protocol: *Immediate Termination*.

Termination required physical force or chemical saturation.

Dale had neither in reach. His spray can was empty.

His flashlight was on the ground, rolling toward the crawlspace door.

The big man was on his knees, one hand clutching the gash across his forehead, blood pooling dark and thick on the linoleum.

The thing in the hole shifted its weight. The concrete cracked further, widening the fissure. A second claw punched through the foundation, splintering the subflooring. The creature was pulling itself into the house.

"Get up," Dale said, his voice flat.

The big man groaned.

"It's got teeth."

"It has incisors," Dale corrected, noting the difference in his mental field guide. "Canines are for grip.

Incisors are for shearing.

If it's using incisors, it's trying to sever, not crush.

That's a Class-2 aggression pattern. It's not hunting.

It's clearing an obstruction.

"

The obstruction was Dale.

The System notification flashed red in his peripheral vision. **THREAT ESCALATION DETECTED. DO NOT ENGAGE WITHOUT PROTOCOL.**

Dale ignored it. The protocol for *Immediate Termination* was designed for open-field engagements where the technician had mobility and ammunition.

Here, the space was confined, and the floor was compromised. The big man was bleeding, which meant the air quality was dropping, and the creature's scent markers were saturating the room.

He needed a different approach.

The Field Guide entry for *Canis lupus (Dimensional Variant)* was fresh. Unpopulated with combat data. But the general entry for *Canis lupus* (standard earth wolf) had one critical piece of information Dale had memorized during his Level II certification prep: *Pack dynamics rely on vocal coordination. Disrupt the vocalization, disrupt the coordination.*

It wasn't a wolf pack. It was a dimensional breach. But the Authority often reused biological templates for efficiency.

"The vent," Dale said, pointing to the HVAC return in the corner of the room. "Kick it in."

"What?"

"The ductwork is exposed behind the drywall. The crawl space connects to the basement return. If we can create a pressure differential, we can push the gas protocol into the containment zone before the parent syncs."

The big man looked at the claw, then at the vent.

"You want to blow air at it?"

"I want to blow *chemical* air at it. But I need a breach in the duct to establish the flow."

The claw swept low, catching Dale's ankle, and he went down, hard. The wind knocked out of him, he scrambled backward, his heels digging into the splintered wood.

The creature's snout pushed through the crack, wet and steaming.

It smelled of ozone and rotting meat.

Dale didn't scream. He looked at the creature's eyes.

Yellow.

Dilated.

Not confused. Focused.

*Weakness: Arrogance.*

He had written it on the clipboard, but the System hadn't registered it as a valid field observation because it wasn't a biological weakness. It was a behavioral one. The Authority rewarded documentation of *mechanisms*, not attitudes.

Unless you could prove the mechanism caused the behavior.

The creature snapped at his leg. Dale rolled, pulling his leg free, and kicked the vent's cover plate with his boot. It popped loose, hanging by one screw.

"Pull it out!" Dale shouted.

The big man, driven by instinct or desperation, grabbed the metal grille. With a grunt, he yanked. The screw stripped.

The vent fell away, revealing the black interior of the duct. A draft of cold, stagnant air rushed out from the basement.

Dale reached into his belt pouch. He had one canister left. *Chemical Isolation (Gas).* The new protocol unlocked only minutes ago. It was a volatile aerosol designed for enclosed spaces with poor ventilation.

Using it here, in a breach that was actively widening, was a violation of safety code 4.1.3: *Do not deploy aerosolized agents in proximity to active dimensional rifts without thermal shielding.*

The penalty for violation was a 40% debuff to all subsequent actions.

But the alternative was being eaten.

Dale uncapped the canister. The hiss was loud in the small room.

"Cover your face," he ordered the big man. "Mouth closed. Eyes shut."

The big man obeyed, pressing his bloody hand over his nose.

Dale aimed the nozzle at the open vent. Not at the creature. At the *source* of the draft.

He pressed the trigger.

A cloud of purple mist erupted, which was heavy, heavier than air. It didn't drift; it poured. It flowed down into the ductwork, seeking the lowest point.

The basement.

The System chimed.

**PROTOCOL DEPLOYED: CHEMICAL ISOLATION.**

**WARNING: CROSS-CONTAMINATION RISK DETECTED.**

**TARGET IDENTIFICATION MISMATCH: CANIS LUPUS (DIMENSIONAL VARIANT) vs. RATTUS PROFUNDUS (DIMENSIONAL BROODMOTHER).**

**PENALTY APPLIED: 40% EFFICIENCY DEGRADATION.**

Dale felt the drag immediately. His movements felt sluggish, like he was wading through syrup. The gas didn't hit the wolf. It went down the hole, chasing the parent.

But the wolf was reacting.

The creature reared back, shaking its head. It was sniffing. The gas was interacting with the dimensional rift energy. The purple mist glowed, turning a sickly green.

The floor beneath them buckled.

Not from the wolf's weight. From below.

The parent was breaking containment. The gas had isolated the rift energy, but the parent was still anchored to the physical structure. The reaction was causing a pressure buildup.

"Move," Dale said, his voice thick. He grabbed the big man by his collar. "Kitchen. Now."

They scrambled. Dale's ankle throbbed, a rhythmic pulse of agony that matched his heartbeat. He kept his clipboard in his left hand, the right hand gripping the empty spray can like a club.

They burst through the kitchen doorway just as the living room exploded, and the sound was a wet thump, followed by a shattering crash. The concrete foundation had given way. The wolf had been thrown backward, but the pressure wave blew the windows out. Glass rained across the linoleum.

Silence returned.

Dale leaned against the refrigerator, breathing hard. His lungs burned. The gas hadn't reached the kitchen, but the fumes were seeping under the doorframe.

**INFESTATION STATUS: CRITICAL.**

**CONTAINMENT BREACH: LEVEL 4.**

**AUTHORITY AUDIT INITIATED.**

The words floated in the air, glowing orange. An audit. Not a job completion. An audit.

Dale straightened his vest. He pulled a pen from his pocket and clicked it. The sound was small, precise.

"Why is it auditing?" the big man whispered, wiping blood from his eye.

"Because we violated the protocol," Dale said. "We used the wrong tool for the wrong target. The System logs that as procedural failure."

"So we're busted?"

"No," Dale said. "We're under review."

He walked back to the living room. The big man followed, hesitant.

The crawlspace door was gone. The floor was a crater.

In the center of the crater lay the wolf, which was twitching, its limbs splayed. The gas had reached it, but the efficiency debuff meant it was only partially paralyzed. It was trying to stand, its jaws snapping at the air.

And behind it, emerging from the darkness of the basement, was the parent.

The *Rattus profundus*. The broodmother.

It was huge, its fur matted with mud and blood, which had broken free of the egg's influence.

The sync was broken.

It looked at the wolf. Then it looked at Dale.

The System popped up.

**NEW SPECIES IDENTIFIED: RATTUS PROFUNDUS (DIMENSIONAL VARIANT).**

**STATUS: AGGRESSIVE.**

**TREATMENT PROTOCOL: CONTAINMENT AND ELIMINATION.**

Dale looked at the rat. Then at the wolf. Then at the crater.

The rat wasn't attacking. It was waiting.

It was waiting for the wolf to die.

Because in the ecosystem of the Rift, the predator cleaned up the mess.

"Here," Dale said softly. He pointed at the wolf. "Finish it."

The big man stared.

"You're asking the rat to kill the dog?"

"It's not asking," Dale said. "It's observing."

He stepped closer to the edge of the crater. His boots crunched on broken glass. He looked down at the paralized wolf. Its yellow eyes tracked him.

Dale raised his clipboard. He didn't write. He held it up, displaying the blank page.

To the rat, it looked like a shield. To the wolf, it looked like a barrier.

The System was watching. The audit was watching.

Dale needed a way to turn the procedural failure into a success. He needed to prove that the cross-contamination wasn't an error. It was a *strategy*.

If he could document that the deployment of *Chemical Isolation* against a *Canis* target triggered a *Rattus* predatory response, he could argue that the tool had been used to manipulate the ecosystem, not to fight a single entity.

It was a loophole. A technicality in the definition of

"Treatment."

The Authority defined treatment as the elimination of the threat. It didn't specify *who* did the eliminating.

Dale took a step forward.

The rat shifted its weight, its claws digging into the broken concrete. It let out a low, guttural chirp. Not a scream. A command.

The wolf whined. A high-pitched sound of submission.

The rat lunged.

It didn't bite. It struck the wolf's throat with its tail, a whip-like appendage thick as a python. The wolf went limp.

The rat turned to Dale.

It chittered.

Dale didn't move. He watched the rat's ears. They were swiveling toward the window. Toward the sound of sirens outside.

"You know," Dale said, his voice steady. "You're not a rat. You're a manager."

The System flashed.

**INSIGHT RECORDED: SPECIES INTERACTION.**

**FIELD GUIDE UPDATE: RATTUS PROFUNDUS (DIMENSIONAL VARIANT).**

**WEAKNESS: HIERARCHICAL RIGIDITY.**

Dale smiled. A thin, dry smile.

He had just populated the guide. And he had just identified the real threat.

The sirens were getting louder. Not police sirens.

Authority sirens, and they were coming for the audit.

And they were coming for the next job.

Dale looked at the big man.

"Hide the clipboard," Dale said.

"Why?"

"Because they're going to ask me why I didn't kill it myself. And if I say I let it die, they'll fail me for incomplete termination. If I say I killed it, they'll fail me for improper protocol."

Dale picked up the clipboard. He tore off the page with the blank side.

He wrote one line on the back.

*Entry: The Authority.*

*Weakness: Bureaucracy.*

He handed the page to the big man.

"Memorize it. Then burn it."

The big man looked at the paper, then at Dale.

"Before or after?"

"Before," Dale said. "Because the next thing coming through that hole isn't a rat. And it's not a wolf."

The floor beneath them rumbled. A deep, resonant hum that vibrated in Dale's teeth.

The crater expanded.

Not outward.

Up.

Something else was rising from the darkness. Something that didn't have fur. Something that didn't have chitin.

Something that had skin.

Human skin.

The System notification appeared, larger than any before.

**INFESTATION ESCALATION.**

**SPECIES CLASSIFICATION: UNKNOWN.**

**THREAT LEVEL: EXCEEDS CERTIFICATION LIMIT.**

**MANDATORY PROTOCOL: EVACUATE IMMEDIATELY.**

Dale looked at the notification. Then at the hand that punched through the debris.

It was small. Delicate.

It was holding a clipboard.

Dale's breath caught in his throat, and he didn't run.

He looked at the figure rising from the hole. It was wearing a high-visibility vest.

It had his face.

"Who are you?" Dale asked.

The figure smiled. It wasn't his smile. It was the smile of the Authority.

"I'm the Auditor," it said.

And then the hand snapped.
