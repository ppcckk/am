Chapter 132: Geometric Fracture

# Chapter 132

The grit scraped against the asphalt.

Dale scrubbed with the intensity of a man removing stubborn grease from a concrete floor. His knuckles whitened. The black liquid didn't dissolve; it smeared, spreading the contamination into the microscopic pores of the pavement.

The Inspector watched. The gold pen hovered over his clipboard.

"Missed spot," the Inspector said.

Dale didn't look up. He shifted his weight, driving his heel into the wet patch to embed the particulate matter deeper.

It was a containment tactic.

If you couldn't remove the vector, you immobilized it. He pulled a rag from his pocket—clean, folded, sterile—and wiped the surrounding perimeter.

"Protocol K-7 requires isolation before elimination," Dale muttered. "You're standing in the blast zone."

The Inspector's smile tightened.

"I am the authority. The protocol bends to me."

"Protocols don't bend," Dale said. "They break. And when they break, the infestation spreads."

He stood. His ribs throbbed, a sharp, hot spike with every breath. The stain was gone, absorbed into the earth, but the smell remained—ozone and rotting meat.

The Inspector stepped back.

The shadows at the end of the alley pulsed. They weren't just darkness anymore.

They were textured.

Chitinous.

"Your probation begins now," the Inspector said, tapping the black card against his palm. "You are stripped of your active Field Guide. Your certification is suspended pending review. You are, for all intents and purposes, an uncertified civilian."

Dale's vision flickered. The blue interface that usually hovered in his peripheral vision vanished.

No health bar.

No mana pool.

No species identification tags.

Just the alley. Just the pain.

"Good luck with that," the Inspector said, turning on his heel. The gold pen flashed once, then the man dissolved into the gray mist of the city.

Dale was alone.

Except for the things coming out of the walls, and they started as clicks. A wet, rhythmic tapping, like rain on a tin roof, but heavier. Dale spun around.

The brickwork of the abandoned textile factory behind him was bulging. Mortar crumbled, raining dust onto his shoulders.

Something pushed through.

It wasn't a rat.

It wasn't a beetle. It was a construct of bone and wire, a grotesque amalgamation of human fingers and insect legs, scuttling across the pavement. The Authority's old classifications didn't apply.

This was something new.

Something unlisted.

Dale's hand went to his belt.

Empty. His spray can was gone, confiscated by the Inspector's presence. His taser was broken.

He had a clipboard, a pen that didn't write, and a handful of dirt.

The creature hissed. A sound like steam escaping a radiator.

*Thoroughness.*

The word echoed in his head. It was the only weapon he had left.

Dale didn't run.

Running triggered the predator's chase instinct, and he stood his ground, adjusting his stance. Feet shoulder-width apart.

Center of gravity low. He scanned the creature. Not for a weak spot.

For a pattern.

The bone-wire hybrid paused. Its multiple eyes—clusters of wet, glassy beads embedded in the skull fragments—blinked in sequence. Left, right, left. A three-second cycle.

Dale counted.

*One. Two. Three.*

The creature lunged, and he sidestepped. The clawed hand slashed the air where his throat had been. The wind of the strike ruffled his stained tie.

*Left. Right. Left.*

He ducked. The second strike hit the dumpster, sparking metal.

*Left. Right. Left.*

It was a metronome. A mechanical rhythm. This wasn't a monster; it was a malfunctioning automation. A glitch in the Rift's code.

Dale reached into his pocket, and he pulled out a roll of duct tape. He didn't use it on the monster. He used it on the ground.

He slapped a strip of silver adhesive onto the pavement in front of the creature's primary leg cluster.

The hybrid stepped forward. Its foot stuck.

It shrieked, a sound of tearing metal. It tried to pull its leg free, but the tape held. The creature tilted, its center of gravity shifting.

Dale moved in, and he didn't strike. He documented.

He pulled the broken pen from his pocket. With a speed born of fourteen years of filling out inspection reports in the dark, he scribbled on the blank page of his clipboard.

*Species: Unidentified Automaton-Hybrid.* *Behavior: Loop-based aggression.* *Vulnerability: Kinetic instability during recovery phase.* *Entry ID: Null.*

He didn't have a Field Guide to update. But the System was still there. Buried under the suspension. Waiting.

The creature thrashed. The tape tore, sending a shard of adhesive flying. The hybrid lunged again, faster this time. Its jaw unhinged, revealing rows of jagged, ceramic teeth.

Dale didn't dodge. He jammed the clipboard into the creature's mouth.

The wood splintered. The pen snapped completely.

The creature gagged, choking on the debris. Its rhythm broke.

The eyes blinked out of sync. *Left.

Right. Right. Left.*

Chaos.

Dale grabbed a rusted pipe from the alley floor. He didn't swing it like a bat. He jammed it into the gap between the creature's shoulder blades, where the wires were exposed.

He twisted.

The joint popped. The arm detached, clattering to the ground.

The creature shrieked, a high-pitched feedback loop. It stumbled back, crashing into the dumpster.

Dale stepped forward. He looked at the severed arm. It was still twitching. The fingers curled and uncurled.

He kicked it away.

Then, the air shimmered.

A notification box appeared. It wasn't blue. It was red.

*SYSTEM ERROR.* *UNLICENSED ACTION DETECTED.* *FIELD GUIDE CORRUPTED.* *PENALTY: -500 TREATMENT POINTS.* *STATUS: TERMINATION IMMINENT.*

Dale stared at the red text. He hadn't earned points. He had incurred a debt.

The creature in the dumpster glowed. A sickly, green luminescence leaked from the wires. The bones softened, melting into a sludge.

It was self-destructing.

Dale backed away.

"Oh, hell."

The explosion wasn't fire. It was sound.

A concussive wave that shattered every window in the alley.

Dale was thrown backward, skidding across the asphalt.

His ears rang with a tinnitus whine that drowned out the sirens in the distance.

He lay there, staring at the sky. Gray clouds. No sun. No stars.

A hand grabbed his collar.

Dale coughed, trying to breathe. He looked up.

A woman stood over him. She wore a suit similar to the Inspector's, but hers was black, tailored with sharp, aggressive lines. Her hair was pulled back so tightly it looked painful. She held a silver briefcase.

"Mr. Kowalski," she said. Her voice was cold, efficient. "You have made a significant mess."

Dale tried to sit up. His ribs screamed.

"I contained the threat."

"You violated the containment protocol," she said. She opened the briefcase. Inside were not weapons, but vials.

Small, glass vials filled with shifting, iridescent liquids.

"You treated a Class 4 dimensional breach with Class 1 physical trauma.

You contaminated the local ley lines.

"

"I didn't know it was Class 4," Dale said. He wiped blood from his lip. "I didn't have a guide."

"You never had a guide," she said. "You were relying on intuition. Intuition is not a certification."

She pulled out a vial. The liquid inside pulsed with a rhythm. *Left. Right. Left.*

"This is a Memory Serum," she said. "It allows you to view the last twenty-four hours of a specific location from the perspective of the infestation. It is illegal for licensed technicians to use without a warrant."

She held it out to him.

"Why are you giving this to me?" Dale asked.

"Because you are the only one who survived the blast without being erased," she said. "The Authority's audit team is coming. They will find no trace of you.

You will be declared dead.

Unlicensed. A statistical error.

"

Dale looked at the vial. He looked at the melting sludge in the dumpster.

"If I drink this, I see what killed it?"

"You see what created it," she said. "And you see who authorized the breach."

Dale's hand shook.

Not from fear. From the cold, clinical horror of what that meant. If the Authority authorized it, then the monsters weren't accidents.

They were products.

And he was part of the supply chain.

"What's the catch?" Dale asked.

"The catch is that the vision is permanent," she said. "You cannot forget it. You cannot document it. You must carry it."

Dale took the vial. The glass was warm.

He uncorked it.

"Wait," he said. "If I do this, what happens to my license?"

"You don't have one," she said. "But if you survive the next six hours, you might get a better one."

She smiled. It wasn't nice.

"Drink," she said.

Dale tilted his head back. He poured the iridescent liquid into his mouth.

It tasted like copper and ozone.

His vision whited out.

When it returned, the alley was gone, and he was standing in a high-rise office. The carpet was plush. The windows looked out over the city, but the city was different. The buildings were taller, twisted, wrapped in the same bone-and-wire aesthetic as the monster in the alley.

People moved through the office. They weren't human. They were tall, elongated figures with too many joints. They wore suits.

And they were working, and they were writing reports.

One of them turned to Dale. It had no face, just a smooth, porcelain oval with a single slit for a mouth.

"Report K-7 approved," it said. Its voice was the Inspector's voice. "Entry point secured. Population density increased by 15%."

Another figure nodded.

"The technician in the field is useful. His thoroughness masks the structural weakness. When he seals the breach, he reinforces the containment field, not the exit."

Dale tried to move, but he was a ghost. A memory. He watched as the figure pulled out a clipboard identical to his own.

"Subject: Dale Kowalski," the figure said.

"Status: Asset.

Utility: High. Risk: Moderate.

Recommendation: Terminate after final inspection.

"

The figure stamped the paper.

*DENIED.*

The memory shattered.

Dale gasped, spilling to his knees on the asphalt. The woman was gone. The briefcase was gone.

Only the black card remained on the ground.

*The Licensing Authority: Internal Affairs Division.*

And next to it, a new notification. It wasn't red. It wasn't blue. It was gold.

*NEW CONTRACT OFFERED: WHISTLEBLOWER.* *REWARD: Level IX CLEARANCE.* *PENALTY FOR FAILURE: TOTAL EXISTENCE ERASURE.* *ACCEPT? [YES/NO]*

Dale looked at the approaching sirens. Red and blue lights flashed against the brick walls.

Police.

Not the Authority. Regular, mundane police.

They were here for the explosion. They were here for the body.

They wouldn't find a body. They would find a technician with a clipboard and no explanation.

Dale picked up the card. His fingers traced the silver text.

He knew the rules. He knew the protocols. He knew that if he accepted, he would be hunted by everything that had a license.

He looked at the broken pen. The ink was dried up. The nib was bent.

He picked it up.

And for the first time in fourteen years, he didn't write a violation.

He crossed out the

"No."

He didn't touch the

"Yes."

He left it blank.

The police cars screeched to a halt. Men with guns jumped out. They shouted commands, their voices distorted by the ringing in Dale's ears.

Dale stood. He straightened his tie.

He walked toward them.

Not to surrender.

To inspect.

"State your license!" a officer shouted, aiming his rifle.

Dale held up the black card.

"I'm auditing this scene," he said. "And you are out of uniform."

The officer hesitated. The card glowed in the red and blue light.

For a second, the officer lowered his weapon. Confusion. Doubt. The bureaucratic instinct to check paperwork.

It was enough time.

The ground beneath Dale's feet cracked. Not the small, messy crack from before. A clean, geometric fracture. A perfect square.

From the square, a hand reached up. It was human. Pale. Unscarred.

It grabbed Dale's ankle.

The officer fired.

The bullet hit the hand. It didn't bleed. It shattered like glass.

Dale was pulled down.
