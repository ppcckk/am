Chapter 217: Not a Human Hand

# Chapter 217

The elevator did not move. It shuddered, a violent metallic lurch that threw Dale against the brushed steel wall.

The baby carrier in the corner snapped its latch.

Mrs. Gable screamed, a high, thin sound that cut through the hum of the stalled lift. She tried to pull the blanket tighter, but the fabric tore. The sound of ripping cotton was obscenely loud in the small space.

Dale moved.

He didn't think about Vane. He didn't think about the black-armored figures lining the hallway outside, frozen in their rigid, authoritative silence. He thought about the structural integrity of the carrier's frame and the tensile strength of the nylon straps.

He stepped forward, blocking Mrs. Gable from the child.

"Clear the area," Dale said.

Vane stood in the corner of the elevator, her hand resting on the control panel. She wasn't pressing buttons. She was watching Dale.

"Mr. Kowalski," Vane said, her voice smooth as oil.

"You have no license.

You have no Field Guide. You cannot treat what you cannot identify."

Dale ignored her. He reached into his cargo pants, bypassing the empty holsters where his standard aerosol cans should have been. His hand found the emergency tool kit. It was a relic from the pre-Rift days, a canvas roll of heavy-duty zip ties, a multi-tool, and a roll of industrial-grade duct tape.

The thing inside the carrier erupted.

It wasn't a baby. It wasn't even a rat.

A mass of segmented, translucent limbs burst through the torn blanket. The carapace was wet, glistening with a viscous, amber fluid. It smelled of ozone and rot. The legs were long, spindly, and ended in hooked barbs that caught the light.

Mrs. Gable fell back, sliding down the wall.

The creature hissed. It was not a sound of aggression. It was a sound of hunger.

Dale dropped to one knee. He didn't reach for a weapon. He reached for the zip ties.

"Containment," Dale muttered.

The creature lunged.

It moved faster than the Rattus profundus he had killed in the crawlspace. This thing was agile, its center of gravity low and shifting. It swiped a clawed limb at Dale's face.

Dale ducked. The claw sparked against the metal wall.

He threw the first zip tie. It wrapped around the creature's left foreleg. He yanked it tight, pinning the limb against the carrier's base.

The creature shrieked, a sound like grinding glass.

"Stop that," Vane said. She sounded bored. "You are violating Protocol 4. Unlicensed intervention in a live nest results in immediate termination of the technician."

Dale threw the second zip tie. He aimed for the right foreleg. He missed. The creature twisted, its body undulating, and slammed into Dale's chest.

The impact knocked the wind out of him. Dale slid backward, his boots squeaking on the elevator floor. He scrambled up, gasping.

He looked at his clipboard.

It was blank. The Authority had wiped it clean.

No entries.

No data.

"Identify," Dale whispered.

He stared at the creature. It was huddled in the corner of the carrier, trying to protect its underbelly.

The legs were too long for a mammal.

The carapace was too segmented for an insect.

The eyes were multiple, clustered, and black.

*Arachnida?* No. The leg joints were wrong. *Insecta?* No. Too many legs, but not six. *Mammalia?* No.

Dale's mind raced. He remembered the smell. Ozone.

Rot. And the specific pattern of the leg joints.

*Dimorphus*.

The word popped into his head, unbidden. It wasn't in his memory. It was in the System's residual buffer. A ghost entry.

*Dimorphus juvenile. Class II threat. Vulnerability: Joint separation at the second tarsal segment.*

The knowledge hit him with the force of a physical blow. His vision blurred for a second. A red warning flashed across his peripheral vision.

**CRITICAL ERROR: UNLICENSED DATA RECOVERY.** **PENALTY: 50% EFFICIENCY LOSS FOR 60 SECONDS.**

Dale felt his muscles turn to lead. The zip ties in his hand felt heavier. The elevator seemed to tilt.

Vane smiled.

"You see? You are guessing. You are hoping.

That is not pest control, Dale.

That is suicide."

The creature lunged again. It scrambled over Mrs. Gable's legs. She curled into a ball, covering her face.

Dale forced his legs to move. He couldn't sprint. He couldn't dodge. He had to use the environment.

He grabbed the emergency stop lever on the side of the elevator. He yanked it down.

The car dropped.

It wasn't a freefall, but it was a sudden, sickening lurch. The creature lost its traction. Its claws skittered on the metal floor.

Dale didn't hesitate. He threw his body weight forward, tackling the creature.

They rolled across the floor.

The zip ties snapped. The creature pinned Dale's arm. The claws dug into his forearm.

The pain was sharp, immediate. He felt the skin tear.

He didn't pull away. He used the pain.

With his free hand, he grabbed the creature's hind leg. He twisted.

He didn't try to kill it. He tried to break it.

The joint snapped.

A wet crack echoed in the small space. The leg separated from the body. The creature screamed, a high-pitched wail that vibrated in Dale's teeth.

Amber blood sprayed across Dale's face.

He kept twisting. He pulled the leg away, tearing the muscle. The creature flailed, its remaining legs kicking wildly.

Dale grabbed the roll of duct tape. He didn't wrap the creature. He wrapped the *gap*.

He pressed the tape over the open wound where the leg had been severed. He used the tape's adhesive to bind the stump, effectively cauterizing it with pressure and plastic.

The creature convulsed. Its movements slowed.

Dale stepped back.

He was breathing hard.

His arm was bleeding, the claws having punctured the muscle. He didn't look at the wound.

He looked at the creature.

It lay still. Not dead. Stunned.

Vane stepped forward. She looked at the severed leg. She looked at the tape.

"Improvisation," she said. "Dirty. Inefficient. But effective."

She reached into her coat and pulled out a small, silver device. She pressed it against the creature's forehead.

The creature dissolved.

It didn't die. It simply ceased to exist. The flesh, the carapace, the blood—it all vanished, leaving only the torn blanket and the empty carrier.

Mrs. Gable sobbed.

Vane looked at Dale. "You violated three protocols. You used unauthorized materials. You caused unnecessary tissue damage to the host."

Dale wiped the amber blood from his cheek. He looked at his arm. The punctures were deep. He could feel the heat of the infection starting to bloom.

"The host," Dale said. His voice was flat. "Mrs. Gable is the host."

Vane nodded. "Correct.

The Dimorphus is a parasitic symbiote.

It requires a warm-blooded host to incubate. The baby was not infected. The baby was the incubator."

Dale looked at Mrs. Gable. She was shaking. She looked hollowed out, drained.

"You removed the parasite," Dale said.

"I removed the threat," Vane corrected. "The host remains. And the host is still infested."

Dale looked at the empty carrier. Then he looked at Mrs. Gable's abdomen.

Her shirt was stained with dark, wet patches.

"She's bleeding," Dale said.

"She is leaking," Vane said. "The removal of the primary nest triggered a cascade response. Secondary eggs are hatching."

Dale's stomach dropped.

He looked at the floor.

Tiny, translucent things were emerging from the seam of Mrs. Gable's pants.

They were the size of marbles.

They had too many legs.

**WARNING: SECONDARY INFESTATION DETECTED.** **CLASS: II.** **STATUS: UNLICENSED.**

Dale stood up. His legs felt like water. The 50% efficiency debuff was still active.

He looked at Vane.

"I need a bag," Dale said.

Vane raised an eyebrow. "You have no bag. You have no license. You have a bleeding arm and a contaminated workspace."

Dale looked at the black-armored figures outside the elevator. They hadn't moved. They were waiting for a signal.

"Open the doors," Dale said.

"No," Vane said. "You stay here.

You clean this up.

With your bare hands.

If you leave, you forfeit your probationary status. You become an Exterminator."

"An Exterminator?" Dale asked.

"The Authority's term for those who fail certification but continue to operate," Vane said. "They are not licensed. They are not protected. They are considered part of the infestation."

Dale looked at the tiny creatures crawling on the floor. One of them reached his boot.

He stomped on it.

It crunched.

He stomped again. And again.

He was moving slowly. The debuff made his steps clumsy. But he was thorough.

Vane watched him. Her expression didn't change.

"You're making a mess, Mr. Kowalski."

Dale stopped. He was breathing hard. He looked at the pile of crushed chitin.

"I'm documenting," Dale said.

He reached into his pocket. He pulled out a small, folded piece of paper. It was a receipt from a gas station. He unfolded it.

He wrote down: *Dimorphus. Secondary. Size: 2mm.

Location: Floor. Status: Eliminated.*

He held the paper up.

The System flashed.

**ENTRY RECORDED.** **THOROUGHNESS: 2/10.** **POINTS: 0.**

It wasn't enough.

But it was something.

Vane's eyes narrowed.

"You are playing with fire, Dale. The Authority sees this. They see you bypassing the Guide.

They see you using intuition over data."

"It's the only data I have," Dale said.

He looked at Mrs. Gable. She was trying to stand up. Her legs buckled.

"Help her," Vane said. "Or don't. It's your choice."

Dale walked over to her. He supported her weight. She was light. Too light.

"Where are we going?" Mrs. Gable whispered.

"Home," Dale said.

"Home is gone," she said. "The neighbors... they're not neighbors anymore. They're waiting."

Dale looked at the elevator doors. They opened.

The black-armored figures stepped aside.

But they didn't let them out.

They formed a corridor. A path.

At the end of the corridor stood a man. He was wearing a pristine white suit. He held a clipboard that glowed with a soft blue light.

He was not Vane. He was not an Auditor.

He was a Class IX Examiner.

The man looked at Dale. He looked at the blood on Dale's arm. He looked at the paper in Dale's hand.

"Mr. Kowalski," the Examiner said. His voice was amplified, echoing in the hallway. "You have failed your probationary assessment."

Dale tightened his grip on Mrs. Gable.

"You have," the Examiner continued. "However, you have also created a new violation code. Code Z-9. Unauthorized Use of Intuition."

The Examiner stepped forward.

"We do not punish failure, Mr. Kowalski. We punish inefficiency. You have proven that you can operate outside the parameters of the System."

Vane stepped out of the elevator. She looked at the Examiner. She looked at Dale.

"What is the offer?" Dale asked.

The Examiner smiled. It was a cold, administrative smile.

"The Authority has a backlog of Class IV infestations. The Rift is expanding. The licensed technicians are overwhelmed. They are burning out."

The Examiner held up his glowing clipboard.

"We need specialists who do not follow the book. Who do not wait for the Field Guide to populate. Who will enter a nest with no data and walk out with a body count."

Dale looked at his bleeding arm. He looked at the tiny creatures still crawling near his feet.

He looked at Mrs. Gable.

She was asleep, her head resting on his shoulder.

Her skin was gray. The veins in her neck were black.

She was still infested.

The Examiner extended the clipboard.

"Join the Unlicensed Division, Mr. Kowalski.

Your license is revoked.

Your certification is void.

But your tools remain. Your instincts remain."

"And the penalty for failure?" Dale asked.

"Death," the Examiner said. "But it will be a quiet death. Not in the walls.

Not in the halls. In the field.

Where you can do what you were born to do."

Dale looked at Vane. She was smiling. A real smile this time.

He looked at the Examiner.

He took the clipboard.

It was heavy. Heavier than his old one.

He signed it.

The System flashed.

**STATUS CHANGE: UNLICENSED OPERATIVE.** **CERTIFICATION LEVEL: NULL.** **ACCESS TO FIELD GUIDE: RESTRICTED.** **NEW MECHANIC UNLOCKED: INTUITIVE TRACKING.**

Dale looked at the text.

*Intuitive Tracking: Automatically highlights weaknesses in unseen creatures based on biological proximity to known species. Accuracy varies based on stress levels.*

He looked at Mrs. Gable.

He looked at the Examiner.

"I need a new bag," Dale said.

The Examiner turned and walked away. The black-armored figures parted for him.

Vane walked toward Dale. She stopped inches from his face.

"Welcome to the bottom of the pile, Mr. Kowalski," she whispered. "Try not to die in the first week."

Dale looked down at the baby carrier.

The blanket shifted again.

Something moved underneath.

He looked at Mrs. Gable's stomach. It was rippling.

He looked at Vane.

"She's not done," Dale said.

Vane's smile faded.

"No," she said. "She never was."

The rippling stopped.

A hand burst from Mrs. Gable's shirt.

It was not a human hand.

It was a claw.

Dale dropped the clipboard.
