Chapter 191: Filing Complaint Against God

# Chapter 191

The dust hadn't settled. It hung in the air like a suspended fog, gray and choking, turning the fractured skyline of the suburban sprawl into a jagged silhouette against the bruised purple of the sky.

Dale Kowalski wiped a smear of black ichor from his cheek with the back of a hand that was already trembling.

His lungs burned. The air tasted of ozone and burnt hair.

Five seconds. The countdown had expired while he was climbing out of the crater. The sky was still screaming text.

**CERTIFICATION LEVEL VI EXAM: IN PROGRESS.** **SUBJECT: ETHICAL VIOLATION.**

The letters were massive, drifting through the atmosphere like clouds made of neon light. They obscured the sun. They obscured the stars. Every survivor within a ten-mile radius had to be seeing them.

Dale sat up. His knees popped. The noise was loud in the silence of the destroyed lobby.

A shadow detached itself from the rubble pile to his left.

"Stay down," a voice said. "You're in the kill zone."

Dale didn't move.

He reached for his clipboard. It was missing.

He patted his belt.

The holster was empty. He looked up at the approaching figure.

The woman wore a high-visibility vest over tactical armor. Her helmet was scuffed, the visor cracked down the middle. She held a rifle that looked like it had been modified with chitin plating.

"I'm not in the kill zone," Dale said. His voice was flat. "I'm in the post-incident verification phase."

The woman stepped closer. She didn't lower the rifle. "You just killed an Authority Agent."

"I sealed an entry point."

"That's a euphemism for murder."

"It's a technical term for contamination control." Dale pulled a pen from his pocket. It was bent. He straightened it against his knee. "You're the cleanup crew?"

"I'm Inspector Vane. Level IV." She gestured at the sky.

"You see that?

That's not a glitch.

That's a tribunal. You're on trial, Dale.

And you're running out of time to build a defense."

Dale looked at the floating text. The words **ETHICAL VIOLATION** pulsed with a red hue. Below it, smaller text scrolled rapidly.

**ALLEGATION: UNAUTHORIZED USE OF STRUCTURAL WEAKNESS ON SENTIENT ENTITY.** **PRECEDENT: NONE.** **PENALTY: REVOCATION OF LICENSE.** **COST: TERMINAL.**

"Terminal," Dale muttered. He clicked his pen twice.

Click.

Click. "That seems disproportionate for a Class-2 pest."

Vane's eyes narrowed behind the cracked visor. "He wasn't a pest. He was a liaison. He had clearance."

"He had a hole in his chest," Dale said. "I plugged it. That's standard protocol."

"He was *alive*."

"He was non-compliant.

He was blocking the flow of dimensional energy. He was acting as a dam." Dale stood up.

His legs felt like water, but he kept them straight.

"Did you read the manual, Inspector? Section 4, Paragraph 12. 'Any obstruction to the natural flow of infestation is to be treated as a blockage, not an inhabitant.'"

Vane lowered the rifle slightly. Just an inch. "You're trying to quote the code at me? While you're standing in the corpse of a man you turned into a void?"

"I'm quoting the code because it's the only thing that saves you when the Rift opens again. And it will."

"You're arrogant."

"I'm thorough."

A rumble shook the ground. Debris shifted.

From the center of the crater, a low growl emerged.

It wasn't animalistic.

It sounded like stone grinding on stone.

Vane raised her rifle. "Hold position."

"Wait," Dale said. He didn't look at the crater. He looked at his HUD.

**SYSTEM ALERT: CROSS-CONTAMINATION DETECTED.** **SOURCE: DALE KOWALSKI.** **STATUS: FIELD GUIDE CORRUPTED (23:59:40 REMAINING).**

The corruption timer was ticking. Twenty-four hours. If he didn't fix the guide, he couldn't identify anything else for a day.

"Something's coming," Vane said. Her voice dropped. "It's not the agent. It's the thing he was guarding."

Dale turned toward the hole in the ground. The darkness inside was absolute. A tendril of black slime oozed over the lip of the crater. It moved with purpose.

"Identify it," Vane said. "If you can."

"I can't," Dale said. "My guide is corrupted. You put me in this mess by letting him get close enough to touch me."

"I didn't let him do anything. You provoked him."

"He provoked the environment. I responded." Dale stepped toward the crater.

Vane stepped in front of him. "Stop. That thing is a Class-5 Rift Spitter. It'll dissolve you in seconds."

"It's not a Spitter." Dale pointed at the slime. "Look at the viscosity. It's too thick.

It's not secreting for digestion. It's secreting for adhesion."

"How do you know that? Your guide is broken."

"I'm looking at the physical properties." Dale crouched. He reached into his pocket and pulled out a small vial of industrial solvent. "Vane. Cover me."

"No."

"If that thing gets into the municipal water table, the whole county is dead.

Not just us. Everyone." Dale held up the vial. "I need to neutralize the pH level.

It's acidic.

But it's not stomach acid. It's structural adhesive."

Vane hesitated. She looked at the sky. The **ETHICAL VIOLATION** text had grown larger. A new line appeared.

**VERDICT PENDING.** **DEFENSE WINDOW: 00:14:30.**

"Fourteen minutes," Vane said. "If you're wrong, you die. If you're right, you might still die."

"I'm never wrong about infestations." Dale uncapped the vial.

"Fine. But if I see you do anything stupid, I shoot you."

"Understood."

Dale walked toward the slime.

The creature emerged further. It had no eyes. It had no face.

It was a mass of wet, black fibers, roughly the size of a car. It sensed them.

It turned its bulk toward Dale.

Vane fired.

Three rounds. The bullets hit the slime and splashed, dissolving instantly.

The creature didn't flinch. It kept moving.

"Rounds are useless," Vane shouted. "Use the protocol!"

"I am using the protocol." Dale pulled a flare gun from his belt. "It's not about damage. It's about isolation."

He fired the flare into the crater wall, near the base of the slime. The magnesium burned white-hot. The heat caused the slime to recoil, pulling back from the wall.

"Move left!" Dale shouted.

Vane adjusted her aim. She fired again. This time, she hit the flare's reflection in the slime. The creature screamed—a sound like tearing metal.

"It's blind," Dale said. "It navigates by heat and vibration. The flare confuses its sonar."

"So what? You blinded it. Now what?"

"Now we seal the source." Dale pointed to the crater wall. "See that crack?

The slime is flowing out of it.

That's the entry point. The agent was trying to patch it with his own body. That's why he died.

He was doing manual labor."

Dale walked closer to the slime. It was lashing out now, blind and angry.

A tendril whipped out, catching Dale's shoulder.

He didn't run. He didn't scream.

He injected the solvent directly into the tendril's contact point.

The slime sizzled. It turned a pale, chalky white. The creature shrieked, a high-pitched frequency that made Dale's teeth ache.

"It's working," Dale said. "Neutralizing the bond."

"Get away from it!" Vane yelled. "It's going to explode!"

"No. It's going to solidify."

Dale pulled out his duct tape. Standard issue.

Silver.

Heavy-duty.

He slapped it over the white-stiffened tendril, then slapped another strip over the crack in the wall where the slime had originated.

The creature thrashed. The white patches spread. The black fibers hardened, turning into a rigid, glass-like substance.

The screaming stopped. The creature froze, encased in its own neutralized adhesive.

Dale stepped back. He wiped his hands on his pants.

"Done," he said.

Vane stared at the statue. "You... you fixed it."

"I stabilized it," Dale corrected. "Now we need to excavate it properly. If we leave it there, it'll crack and leak again in a week."

"You're insane."

"I'm a technician."

The sky shifted. The red text faded. New text appeared.

**INCIDENT RESOLVED: PARTIAL.** **ETHICAL VIOLATION: UNDER REVIEW.** **ADJUSTER: INSPECTOR VANE.** **TESTIMONY REQUIRED.**

Vane looked at the sky, then at Dale. Her face was pale.

"They want my testimony," she said. "They're asking if you followed protocol."

"I did," Dale said. "I isolated. I neutralized. I verified."

"You killed a man."

"I sealed a breach."

Vane walked over to him. She looked at his hands. They were shaking again. He hid them in his pockets.

"You need to understand something, Dale," Vane said.

Her voice was quiet. "The Authority isn't a government. It's not a company.

It's a machine.

It runs on rules. But it also runs on precedent."

"I know."

"You set a new precedent today. You treated a sentient being as a pest."

"Because he was acting like a pest."

"He was acting like a martyr," Vane said. "He was trying to hold the door shut. You pried it open."

"I closed it."

"No.

You changed the definition of what can be closed." Vane looked up at the sky. "The exam isn't over. The tribunal isn't judging your actions.

They're judging your philosophy."

"Philosophy is for people who don't have work to do," Dale said. He looked at his HUD. The corruption timer was still ticking. **23:45:12**.

"Your guide is broken," Vane said. "You can't identify anything else. If that creature wasn't a pest, what is it now? You have no entry for it."

"I know what it is."

"You don't know how to fight it. You don't know its weaknesses. You just know how to plug it."

Dale looked at the statue of the creature. It was ugly. It was wrong. But it was static.

"Give me a new one," Dale said.

"What?"

"A new entry. For the agent. The human."

Vane frowned. "You want to add the dead agent to your Field Guide?"

"Everything is in the guide. That's the rule. If I'm going to be licensed, I need to know what the other side is."

"That's... that's not how it works."

"It is how it works if you're thorough." Dale pulled out his pen. He wrote on the air. Not on paper. On the HUD.

**ADD ENTRY: AUTHORITY LIAISON.** **CLASS: SENTIENT OBSTRUCTION.** **WEAKNESS: STRUCTURAL DEPENDENCY.** **NOTE: DO NOT ENGAGE WITHOUT SEALING PERIMETER FIRST.**

The system flickered.

**ERROR: ENTRY REJECTED. SOURCE INVALID.**

Dale stared at the error message. He tried again.

**ADD ENTRY: AUTHORITY LIAISON.** **CLASS: CLASS-7 HUMAN.** **WEAKNESS: MORAL HESITATION.**

The system hesitated. The text glowed gold.

**ACCEPTED.** **TREATMENT POINTS: +50.** **NOTE: MORAL HESITATION IS A UNIVERSAL WEAKNESS FOR CLASS-7 ENTITIES.**

Vane laughed. It was a short, sharp bark. "You just gamed the system."

"I identified a weakness. It's true. Every one of you freezes before you shoot. I don't."

"That's not a weakness. That's a pathology."

"It's a professional trait."

The sky changed again. The text vanished.

**CERTIFICATION LEVEL VI EXAM: SUSPENDED.** **REASON: CANDIDATE DEMONSTRATED UNORTHODOX THOROUGHNESS.** **PENALTY WAIVED: TEMPORARY.**

Vane lowered her rifle completely. She looked at Dale with a mix of horror and awe.

"Suspended?" she whispered.

"They're waiting for me to mess up," Dale said. "They're waiting for me to get sloppy."

"You just almost got yourself revoked."

"I didn't get revoked. I got a warning." Dale walked toward the edge of the crater. "We have twenty-four hours before my guide fixes itself. Until then, I'm blind to any new species."

"So we're sitting ducks," Vane said.

"No," Dale said. "We're going to hunt."

"Hunt what?"

"Whatever is coming next. Before the Authority decides I'm too dangerous to keep." Dale looked at the horizon. The Rift was still open. A faint, green light pulsed in the distance.

Vane followed his gaze. "That's not a Rift event. That's a summon."

"Is it licensed?"

"No."

Dale smiled. It was a thin, dry expression. "Then it's illegal. And I'm going to fine it."

He walked away from the crater, his boots crunching on the glass-like remains of the creature. Vane watched him go. She looked at the sky, where the words **SUSPENDED** hung in the air like a guillotine blade.

She raised her radio.

"Command.

This is Vane. I need an extraction team.

And I need a legal brief. Immediately."

"Copy, Vane. What's the situation?"

"The situation is a technician who thinks he can file a noise complaint against a god."

Vane looked at Dale's back. He was walking steadily, despite the blood on his shirt. He was writing something in a small notebook he had pulled from his pocket.

He was documenting the walk.

He was logging the debris.

He was building a case.

And the sky was watching.
