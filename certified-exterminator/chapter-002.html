Chapter 2

The pen moved. It always moved. That was the thing about paperwork. You could be bleeding, you could be standing in something that used to be a person, you could have a number in your vision telling you that you had a three percent chance of surviving the week, and your hand still knew what to do. Dale Kowalski wrote the date. He wrote the address. He wrote the client name, which was Mrs. Alda Fenske, because the screaming man outside had told him so between the second and third time he'd thrown up.

He wrote the findings. Structural entry point, K-7. Brood activity, confirmed. Containment method, improvised. He noted the trash bag's failure rate and the fact that duct tape was not an acceptable sealant for biological containment. He noted that the crawlspace humidity had exceeded acceptable parameters. He noted that the kitchen sink's P-trap was dry, which meant the broodmother had been entering through the drain line.

He was halfway through the recommendations section when the notification pinged.

TREATMENT POINTS AWARDED: 12. FIELD GUIDE ENTRY CONFIRMED: Dimensional Vermin (Rattus profundus). THOROUGHNESS RATING: 9.2/10. CERTIFICATION LEVEL: I. NEXT EXAM: PENDING.

He didn't celebrate. He underlined the P-trap note twice, because he'd seen it once and needed to be sure, and the Authority's grading algorithm gave bonus points for redundant documentation when the primary observation was made under duress. Fourteen years of code violations had taught him that.

The pen kept moving.

Outside, the screaming had changed character. It was no longer the high thin wail of a person in immediate mortal danger. It was the ragged, furious yelling of a person who had decided to fight back and was losing. Dale could hear the thump of something heavy hitting a wall. Something else scuttled. The scuttle was wrong. Too many legs, too fast, the rhythm all wrong for anything with a spine.

He finished the report. He signed it. He set the clipboard on the kitchen counter and walked to the front door.

The man with the broom was gone. The man who had been screaming was gone. There were two other people on the street now, a woman in a bathrobe and a teenager with a lacrosse stick, and they were both watching the young man fight.

The young man was maybe twenty-two. He had a provisional license notification floating around his head like a halo made of bad decisions. He was swinging a baseball bat at a cluster of acid beetles that had emerged from the crack in the sidewalk. The beetles were the size of house cats, their carapaces a sickly yellow-green, their mandibles dripping something that hissed when it hit the asphalt.

The young man was doing it wrong. Not wrong in the way that mattered for his survival, which was already a coin flip. Wrong in the way that mattered for everything after him.

He was killing them one at a time.

Dale watched a beetle split open under the bat's arc. Its hemolymph sprayed across the young man's forearm. The young man didn't flinch. He swung again. Another beetle. Another spray. The hemolymph was pooling on the sidewalk, mixing with the rainwater that had started to fall, and it was running toward the storm drain.

The young man had a notification above his head that read: CLASS-1 VERMIN ELIMINATED (x3). TREATMENT POINTS: 0. REASON: NO DOCUMENTATION. NO FIELD GUIDE ENTRY. CROSS-CONTAMINATION RISK: ELEVATED.

He didn't have a Field Guide entry. He was fighting a species he hadn't identified. He was creating a chemical runoff that would attract secondary infestations within the hour. And he was standing in a puddle of his own kill, which meant his scent profile now matched the species he was supposed to be eliminating.

The young man swung again. Missed. The bat hit the sidewalk. A beetle lunged. The young man kicked it away and ran.

Dale stepped off the porch.

"Hey."

The young man stopped. Turned. His eyes were wide and wet and he was breathing like he'd run a mile. Above his head, the provisional license notification flickered.

"You're making it worse," Dale said.

The young man stared at him. "What?"

"The runoff. You're contaminating the drainage system. Those beetles are attracted to their own hemolymph. You just marked the whole block."

The young man looked at the puddle. Looked at the drain. Looked at Dale. "I killed three of them."

"You killed three of them without identifying them. Without documenting them. Without sealing the kill site. You're not exterminating. You're just making bodies."

The young man's mouth opened. Closed. The notification above his head changed: CROSS-CONTAMINATION PENALTY IMMINENT. SECONDARY INFESTATION PROBABILITY: 67% WITHIN 60 MINUTES.

"What does that mean?" the young man asked.

"It means you have about an hour before something bigger comes to eat what you killed. And it'll find you first, because you're covered in the bait."

The young man looked down at his arm. The hemolymph had dried to a crust. He looked at Dale with the expression of a person who had just realized that the thing he thought was a video game was actually a job.

"Help me," he said.

Dale looked at him. He looked at the beetles still crawling around the crack in the sidewalk. He looked at the drain. He looked at the young man's lacrosse stick, which was still in the teenager's hand, and the teenager's face, which was doing the thing faces do when they're trying not to cry.

"Name," Dale said.

"Marcus. Marcus Webb."

"Marcus, I need you to go inside. Lock your door. Do not open it until I come get you."

"What about the beetles?"

"I'll handle the beetles."

Marcus went inside. The teenager went inside. The woman in the bathrobe went inside. Dale was alone on the street with six acid beetles and a growing puddle of chemical attractant.

He went back to the duplex. He went to his truck. He went to the back of his truck, where his kit was, and he took out what he needed. A pair of nitrile gloves. A canister of foam sealant rated for structural gaps. A roll of plastic sheeting. A pair of steel-toed boots he'd been meaning to replace for two years. A headlamp. A marker.

He came back to the street. He set the clipboard on the hood of his truck. He opened to a fresh page. He wrote: SITE: 4400 block, Maple Ridge Drive. PRIMARY SPECIES: Acid Beetles (species unidentified, pending). SECONDARY RISK: Drain contamination. TERTIARY RISK: Attracted secondary infestation.

He put on the gloves. He put on the boots. He unrolled the plastic sheeting and laid it over the puddle, weighting the edges with cinder blocks from the neighbor's yard. He sealed the storm drain with foam, packing it tight, checking the seal twice.

The beetles moved around the plastic sheeting like it was a wall. Which, to them, it was. Their antennae tapped the plastic and retracted. They turned. They went back toward the crack.

Dale followed.

The crack in the sidewalk was maybe eight inches wide and three feet long, running parallel to the curb. It wasn't just a crack. It was a seam. The edges were smooth, cut clean, like something had pushed up from below and the concrete had split along a predetermined line. The Authority had been right. This wasn't damage. This was installation.

He crouched. He put his headlamp on. He looked down.

The tunnel went down about four feet before angling into the storm drain. The walls of the tunnel were coated in a substance that looked like dried egg sac material. Translucent. Layered. The kind of thing he'd seen in wasp nests, except this was threaded with something that pulsed faintly, a slow rhythm, like a heartbeat measured in minutes.

He pulled out his marker. He drew a circle around the crack on the sidewalk. He wrote: PRIMARY ENTRY POINT. He wrote: DO NOT FILL.

He went back to his truck. He got his Field Guide, which was just a clipboard with a fresh page that had a System notification on it. The notification read: FIELD GUIDE ANNOTATION AVAILABLE. You may add one weakness observation per species. Weakness observations grant targeted damage bonus during elimination.

He looked at the beetles. They were clustered around the crack now, their antennae moving in synchronized patterns. He watched them for thirty seconds. He watched them for sixty. He watched them for ninety.

They didn't go into the tunnel. They hovered at the edge. They touched the tunnel walls with their front legs and pulled back. They did this repeatedly. They were testing it. They were afraid of it.

Something in the tunnel was keeping them out.

Dale wrote in his Field Guide: ACID BEETLES (UNIDENTIFIED SPECIES) AVOID TUNNEL WALLS. WALL MATERIAL REPELS THEM. POSSIBLE CHEMICAL DETERRENT.

The notification changed: FIELD GUIDE ANNOTATION CONFIRMED. WEAKNESS IDENTIFIED: TUNNEL WALL CHEMICAL. TARGETED DAMAGE BONUS: +15% AGAINST THIS SPECIES WHEN USING WALL-DERIVED AGENT.

He looked at the tunnel wall material. He looked at the beetles. He looked at his hands.

He didn't want to touch it. He did not want to touch the thing that pulsed. Every instinct he had, fourteen years of instinct, told him that the thing that pulsed was not a thing you touched. The thing that pulsed was the thing that got you.

But the System had given him a bonus. Fifteen percent. And he needed every point he could get, because the Authority had said three percent and three percent was not a number you argued with. Three percent was a number you improved.

He put on a second pair of gloves. He reached into the tunnel. He touched the wall material. It was warm. It was soft. It gave under his fingers like wet clay, and it was not sticky, and it did not smell like anything, which was worse than if it had smelled like something, because smell was information and this gave him none.

He pulled his hand back. He looked at the gloves. They were intact. He looked at his fingers. They were fine.

He scraped a sample onto a piece of plastic sheeting. He labeled it. He wrote: SAMPLE 1. SOURCE: TUNNEL WALL. UNKNOWN COMPOSITION. HANDLE AS BIOHAZARD.

He went back to the beetles. He smeared a thin line of the tunnel wall material on the sidewalk, forming a barrier around the crack. The beetles touched it with their antennae. They recoiled. They scattered. They ran in the opposite direction, away from the crack, away from the house, away from the block.

They were running toward the next street.

Dale watched them go. He wrote in his report: CONTAINMENT METHOD: CHEMICAL DETERRENT (UNIDENTIFIED). EFFECT: RELOCATION. NOT ELIMINATION. RELOCATION TO ADJACENT BLOCK.

He underlined NOT ELIMINATION.

The notification came: TREATMENT POINTS AWARDED: 4. REASON: CONTAINMENT WITHOUT ELIMINATION. THOROUGHNESS RATING: 7.1/10. REASON FOR REDUCTION: PRIMARY THREAT RELOCATED, NOT NEUTRALIZED.

Four points. He'd gotten twelve for the rat. Four for this. The difference was the word relocate. He'd moved the problem instead of solving it, and the Authority knew it, and the Authority had docked him for it.

He was writing the entry for the Field Guide, trying to fill in the species name from the physical characteristics he'd observed, when the ground shook.

Not the subtle vibration of a truck passing. A real shake. The kind that came from below, from a depth that the cracks on the surface couldn't account for. The kind that meant something large had moved underground.

The notification came:

CLASS-1 INFESTATION STATUS: ACTIVE. SECONDARY INFESTATION DETECTED. SPECIES: UNKNOWN. CLASSIFICATION: CLASS-1. PROXIMITY: 40 METERS. DIRECTION: NORTH. NOTE: SECONDARY INFESTATION IS NOT ATTRACTED TO ACID BEETLE HEMOLYMPH. SECONDARY INFESTATION IS ATTRACTED TO ACID BEETLE TUNNELS. YOU HAVE CREATED A PATHWAY.

Dale read it twice. He looked at the crack. He looked at the barrier he'd made, which was still intact, which was still keeping the beetles away from the tunnel. Which meant the tunnel was still open. Which meant something else could come up through it.

He looked north.

The street was empty. The houses were dark. The rain was falling. And from the direction of the next block, from the direction where the beetles had run, there was a sound. Not a scream. Not a crash. A sound like something dragging itself across asphalt, slow and heavy and patient, the way a body moves when it has no reason to hurry.

The notification changed:

SECONDARY INFESTATION PROXIMITY: 30 METERS. FIELD GUIDE ENTRY: EMPTY. RECOMMENDED ACTION: IDENTIFY BEFORE ENGAGEMENT. WARNING: YOU HAVE NOT DOCUMENTED THIS SPECIES. ENGAGEMENT WITHOUT IDENTIFICATION: TREATMENT POINTS = 0. CROSS-CONTAMINATION RISK: CRITICAL. DEPLOYING ACID BEETLE PROTOCOL AGAINST UNKNOWN SPECIES: 40% DEBUFF.

Thirty meters. Twenty-five. Twenty.

Dale looked at his Field Guide. It had one entry. Rattus profundus. One annotation. He had no information about what was coming. He had no weakness to exploit. He had no protocol that would work. He had a clipboard and a marker and a pair of gloves that smelled like something that pulsed.

He looked at the crack. He looked at the tunnel. He looked at the direction the sound was coming from.

He could run. He could go back to his truck, drive away, and let whatever was coming do whatever it did. The Authority's warning said three percent. Running might be better than three percent. Running might be a hundred percent, for a while, until the thing found him somewhere else.

Or he could stay. He could identify the thing. He could document it. He could write its name in his Field Guide and find its weakness and use that weakness to kill it and get points and level up and get closer to the certification level that would let him survive what was coming in seventy-two hours.

Twenty meters.

He put on his headlamp. He walked toward the sound.

The notification changed one more time:

SECONDARY INFESTATION PROXIMITY: 15 METERS. FIELD GUIDE ENTRY: EMPTY. IDENTIFICATION WINDOW: OPENING. NOTE: FIRST IDENTIFICATION GRANTS BONUS TREATMENT POINTS. NOTE: YOU HAVE NEVER IDENTIFIED A SPECIES IN THE FIELD WITHOUT FIRST OBSERVING IT FOR A MINIMUM OF TEN SECONDS. NOTE: THIS SPECIES IS MOVING FASTER THAN TEN SECONDS.

Dale walked faster.

The sound got louder. It was not one thing dragging itself. It was many things, moving together, a collective sound, like a thousand small legs on a thousand small surfaces, and the rhythm was wrong, too fast, too coordinated, and the smell hit him before the shape did. Ammonia. Rotting sweetness. The smell of a nest that had been left too long in a wall cavity, the kind of smell that meant the problem had been there for months and no one had looked.

He rounded the corner.

The thing was on the sidewalk. It was not one thing. It was a mass. A cluster of dozens of smaller organisms, each one the size of a large mouse, moving together as a single unit, a living carpet that flowed across the concrete with a direction and a purpose. Each individual had too many legs. Each individual had a mouth that opened in the wrong place, on the underside, where the legs met the body, and the mouth was full of something that was not teeth but was close enough.

They were moving toward the crack. They were following the tunnel. They were following the path Dale had cleared.

His Field Guide notification blinked: IDENTIFICATION WINDOW: OPEN. YOU HAVE 8 SECONDS.

He looked at the mass. He looked at the individual organisms. He looked at their legs. He counted them. Six. No. Eight. No. They were different. Each one had a different number. They were not a species. They were a colony. They were a composite organism made of different species working together, and that was not something that happened in nature, and that was not something that happened in pest control, and that was not something he had a protocol for.

His pen moved. He wrote what he saw. He wrote the leg count variation. He wrote the colony structure. He wrote the mouth placement. He wrote the movement pattern. He wrote it fast, faster than he'd ever written, and the pen kept moving, because the pen always moved, and the identification window was closing, and he was running out of seconds, and the mass was ten meters away, and he could see the individual organisms now, and one of them had turned toward him, and its mouth was opening, and it was making a sound that was not a sound, it was a frequency, a vibration that he felt in his teeth.

The notification came:

FIELD GUIDE ENTRY: PARTIAL. SPECIES NAME: UNRESOLVED. ANNOTATION: COLONY COMPOSITE. INDIVIDUAL SPECIES VARY. WEAKNESS: UNKNOWN. TARGETED DAMAGE BONUS: NONE. IDENTIFICATION WINDOW: CLOSED.

The mass was five meters away.

Dale Kowalski looked at his Field Guide. He looked at his pen. He looked at the thing that was made of other things, the thing that had no weakness he could find, the thing that was moving toward the crack he had sealed, the crack that was the only thing between it and the tunnel that led under the entire block.

He looked at the plastic sheeting on the sidewalk. He looked at the foam sealant in his truck. He looked at the marker in his hand.

He wrote one more line in his Field Guide.

WEAKNESS: UNKNOWN. CANDIDATE HYPOTHESIS: COLONY REQUIRES CHEMICAL COHESION AGENT. SOURCE OF COHESION: UNKNOWN. TEST REQUIRED: SEPARATION.

He ran back to his truck. He grabbed the foam sealant. He grabbed the plastic sheeting. He grabbed the cinder blocks.

He came back.

The mass was at the crack. It was pouring over the edge. It was going into the tunnel.

Dale sprayed the foam across the crack. Not to seal it. To cut the mass in half.

The foam expanded instantly, a white hardening wall, and it split the colony. The ones on the far side went into the tunnel. The ones on this side were trapped, separated from their group, and they stopped moving. All of them. At once. They froze.

The notification came:

COLONY DISRUPTION DETECTED. COHESION AGENT: CONFIRMED. FIELD GUIDE ANNOTATION UPDATED. WEAKNESS IDENTIFIED: CHEMICAL COHESION. TARGETED DAMAGE BONUS: +25% AGAINST THIS SPECIES WHEN COHESION IS DISRUPTED. TREATMENT POINTS AWARDED: 0. REASON: NO ELIMINATION. ONLY DISRUPTION.

He looked at the frozen mass. Twenty or thirty organisms, still, silent, waiting. He looked at the tunnel, where the other half had gone. He looked at his clipboard.

He started writing.

He did not know how to kill them. He did not know their name. He did not know what would happen when the half that went into the tunnel reached whatever was at the bottom of it, whatever was Class-4, whatever had a three percent chance of killing him.

But he knew this: he had found the weakness. He had written it down. And the thing that was frozen on the sidewalk was not dead, and it was not gone, and it was not the problem.

The problem was what was already in the tunnel.

The notification came:

SECONDARY INFESTATION (TUNNEL ENTRY): 30 ORGANISMS. DIRECTION: DOWNWARD. DESTINATION: PRIMARY TUNNEL SYSTEM. ESTIMATED ARRIVAL AT NEST COMPLEX: 4 MINUTES. NOTE: NEST COMPLEX IS LOCATED BENEATH 4400 BLOCK MAPLE RIDGE DRIVE. NOTE: NEST COMPLEX IS LOCATED BENEATH THE DUPLEX YOU ARE STANDING IN. NOTE: YOU ARE STANDING ON TOP OF IT.

Dale looked down.

The ground was warm.
