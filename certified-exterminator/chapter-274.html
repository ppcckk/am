Chapter 274: License Class One

# Chapter 274

The concrete was still soft.

Dale didn't run. He reversed.

The tires screamed against the asphalt, but he didn't peel away.

He angled the bumper toward the creature that had just unfolded from the wall. It stood on four legs, its skin a translucent grey chitin stretched over a humanoid skeleton. The security badge clipped to its chest swung wildly.

The truck's front end slammed into the asphalt, not the monster. Dale slammed the gearshift into park.

Silence.

The creature tilted its head.

Its jaw unhinged. A sound came out.

Not a roar.

A whisper.

"Dale Kowalski. License Class II. Current Status: Active."

The other creatures mimicked it. A chorus of stolen voices, all pitching to Dale's name.

He rolled down the window two inches.

"Who authorized this deployment?" Dale asked. His voice was flat. Bored, even.

The lead creature blinked. Its human teeth clicked together.

"The Authority does not issue verbal authorization for unauthorized dimensional breaches. You are the breach, Dale."

That was wrong.

Dale knew the bylaws. Section 4, Paragraph C: *Unauthorized entities are classified as invasive species until proven sentient.*

He pulled his clipboard from the passenger seat. The clip was bent.

The paper was damp, and he ignored it.

He uncapped his pen.

*Violation Code Z-9: Unregistered Mimicry in a Public Access Zone.*

He wrote it down.

The creatures stopped whispering. They leaned forward. The one with the badge raised a hand.

It had six fingers. They twitched, like antennae.

"You're documenting us," the creature said. It sounded pleased.

"I'm identifying you," Dale corrected.

He looked at the Field Guide entry for *Rattus profundus*, and the entry was blank. Grayed out. Contaminated.

He'd used a rodent protocol on them, which meant he had twenty-four hours before he could use a rodent protocol again.

But these weren't rats. They were wearing rats like suits.

He flipped to a fresh page.

*Subject: Mimicry Construct. Sub-class: Humanoid.*

He didn't have a protocol for that. He had a protocol for *Homo sapiens*. But those weren't human. They were empty shells filled with intent.

The creature with the badge took a step forward. Its foot left a wet print on the pavement.

"Your Thoroughness Rating is 9.4," the creature said. "It is insufficient for this level of infestation."

Dale didn't look up.

"Insufficient for what?"

"For containment. For extermination. You are out of scope."

"Out of scope is my job," Dale said. He wrote: *Observed behavior: Verbal mimicry. Weakness: Reliance on vocal cord vibration for cohesion. If the vocal cord is severed, the construct loses structural integrity.*

He hadn't seen that.

He'd deduced it. The creature had stopped speaking when Dale had reversed the truck. The noise of the engine had drowned it out.

The construct had frozen, and he capped his pen.

"Get out of my service area," Dale said.

The creature smiled. It showed too many teeth.

"We are the service area now."

A notification flashed in his peripheral vision.

**FIELD GUIDE UPDATE: Mimicry Construct (Class IV).** **NEW PROTOCOL UNLOCKED: VOCAL DISRUPTION.** **WARNING: CROSS-CONTAMINATION RISK IF USED ON NATURAL HUMAN POPULATIONS.**

Dale stared at the text, and the Authority was playing games.

A Class IV threat in a Class II zone? That wasn't a breach.

That was a promotion. Or a trap.

He opened the truck door.

The creatures didn't attack. They watched.

They waited, and they wanted him to do something heroic. Something stupid.

Dale walked to the rear of the truck.

He opened the toolbox. He didn't take a weapon.

He took a high-frequency sonic emitter, which was designed for clearing bat roosts. It operated at 40,000 Hertz. Inaudible to humans.

Devastating to echolocating pests.

He aimed it at the lead creature.

The creature's head snapped to the side.

Its eyes rolled back.

A high-pitched squeal erupted from its throat. It wasn't a scream.

It was a feedback loop.

The other creatures stumbled. Their chitinous skin rippled. The badge fell off the leader. It clattered to the ground.

Dale turned the dial. 42,000 Hertz.

The leader's jaw locked. The bones in its neck cracked. It tried to speak again. It couldn't.

Without speech, the mimicry failed. The shell collapsed. The creature fell apart into wet, grey sludge. The security badge landed in the puddle.

The remaining three creatures screamed. Not in pain.

In confusion, and they looked at each other. They looked at Dale.

They spoke, all at once.

"Dale.

Dale. Dale.

License.

Class. II."

They were trying to coordinate. They were using sound to hold their forms together.

Dale turned the emitter to 45,000 Hertz.

The sludge on the ground boiled, and he didn't kill them. He disrupted them. He broke the signal.

The creatures dissolved. They didn't die. They dispersed. The grey matter scattered across the parking lot, seeping into the cracks in the asphalt.

Dale shut off the emitter, and he looked at the mess.

*Treatment Points: 0.*

He frowned. He hadn't eliminated them.

He'd dispersed them.

The protocol required elimination.

Identification, documentation, isolation, elimination, verification, and he had done the first three. He had failed the fourth.

A new notification appeared.

**THOROUGHNESS PENALTY: -1.0** **CURRENT RATING: 8.4**

His rating dropped. He could feel it. A tightness in his chest.

A pressure behind his eyes. The Authority punished sloppy work.

Dispersal was not extermination, which was cleanup.

Dale pulled out his phone. He dialed the Licensing Authority's emergency line. No one answered.

He looked at the sludge. It was moving. Slowly. Re-forming in the shadows.

They weren't dead. They were retreating.

He got back in the truck. He didn't start the engine. He watched the sludge pool in the cracks.

His phone buzzed.

*They're not just rats anymore, Dale. Look at the walls.*

The text was from the same unknown number.

Dale looked at the building again, and the chitinous wall was gone. The concrete was hard.

But there was a new mark.

A stamp.

It looked like a seal.

He stepped out of the truck. He walked to the wall. He touched the seal. It was warm.

It was a stamp of approval.

**INFESTATION ACCEPTED FOR REMEDIATION.** **AUTHORIZED CONTRACTOR: DALE KOWALSKI.** **SCOPE: LOCALIZED. TIMELINE: 48 HOURS.**

He hadn't applied for the job. The Authority had given it to him.

They were forcing him to stay. They were forcing him to fix this.

Dale looked at the sludge in the cracks. It was waiting for him to leave.

If he left, it would re-form.

If he stayed, he had to exterminate it.

Properly, and he pulled out his clipboard.

He wrote: *Violation Code Z-10: Unauthorized Contract Assignment.*

He underlined it.

Then he walked back to the truck. He didn't drive away. He drove around the building.

The sirens were louder now. Closer. Police cars were lining the street.

But they weren't looking at Dale. They were looking at the sky.

The purple clouds were forming a shape. A mouth.

Dale parked his truck in front of the police barricade. He got out.

An officer walked over. She looked tired. Her uniform was stained with grey sludge.

"Mr. Kowalski," she said. "We need you to clear this sector."

"I'm not licensed for Class IV," Dale said.

"You are now," she said. "The Authority updated your file twenty minutes ago. You're the only one who can interact with the constructs without triggering a dimensional cascade."

Dale looked at her.

"How do you know about the cascade?"

The officer's eyes were blank.

"The Authority told me."

She wasn't acting. She was being used.

Dale felt a cold spike in his gut.

The Authority wasn't just a system. It was a presence. It was in the walls.

It was in the air, which was in the officer.

He looked at the sludge in the cracks again. It was gone.

He looked at the sky. The mouth was closing.

"If I don't treat this," Dale said, "what happens?"

"The rift widens," the officer said.

"More come through. Then you die. Then we die.

Then everyone dies.

"

It was a simple equation.

Dale uncapped his pen.

"Give me a form," he said.

The officer handed him a pad of paper. It was blank.

"Fill it out," she said.

"In triplicate.

One for the Authority. One for me.

One for the body.

"

Dale stared at the paper, and he wrote his name.

He wrote the date.

He wrote the violation code.

Then he wrote: *Contingency Plan B: Use the Mimicry against the Source.*

He didn't know how. But the Field Guide was updating.

New entries were appearing.

Not from observation. From deduction.

*Subject: Rift Mouth. Weakness: Harmonic Resonance. Frequency: 18 Hertz.*

18 Hertz. Infrasound. Felt more than heard.

Dale looked at his truck. The sonic emitter was in the toolbox. It went up to 45,000 Hertz. It couldn't do 18 Hertz.

But the truck's subwoofer could, and he looked at the officer.

"Is there a sound system here?"

"There's a PA system on the police cruiser," she said.

Dale walked to the cruiser. He opened the door. He ripped out the radio. He wired the sonic emitter directly to the PA speaker.

The officer watched him. She didn't stop him.

The Authority was watching.

Dale turned the emitter to 18 Hertz, and he pressed the transmit button.

The air vibrated. The ground shook. The purple clouds rippled.

The rift mouth opened wider, and a sound came out. A low, guttural groan.

Dale turned the dial down. 15 Hertz.

The sound changed. It became a scream.

The creatures in the walls vibrated. They couldn't handle the resonance.

But the rift wasn't closing. It was pulling harder.

Dale felt his teeth rattle. His vision blurred.

He looked at the Field Guide.

**PROTOCOL: HARMONIC DISRUPTION.** **COST: CERTIFICATION LEVEL DOWNGRADE.** **PENALTY: LOSS OF ALL PREVIOUS TREATMENT POINTS.**

He hesitated.

If he did this, he'd lose his progress. He'd be back to Class I.

But if he didn't, the rift would swallow the town, and he looked at the officer. She was bleeding from her ears.

He looked at the sky. The mouth was huge now. It covered the horizon.

He turned the dial to 12 Hertz.

The ground cracked.

Dale stepped back. He didn't run. He stood his ground. He held the transmitter.

He waited for the signal to hit the rift.

The sound wave hit.

The rift didn't close.

It inverted.

The purple clouds turned black. The air sucked inward.

The officer was pulled forward. She screamed.

Dale grabbed the door frame. He held on.

The Field Guide flashed red.

**ERROR: TARGET NOT IDENTIFIED.** **SYSTEM FAILURE: CASCADING CORRUPTION.**

The Authority was crashing.

Dale looked at his clipboard. The paper was gone.

He looked at his hands. They were shaking.

Then, from the center of the rift, something stepped out.

It wasn't a rat. It wasn't a mimic.

It was wearing a suit. A blue uniform.

It looked exactly like Dale.

But its eyes were black. And it was holding a clipboard.

The other Dale smiled.

"License Class I," it said.

It raised a hand.

Dale felt his own hand rise. He couldn't control it.

The Authority had lost control.

And the thing in the rift was taking over.
