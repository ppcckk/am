Chapter 127: Void Report Defendant

# Chapter 127

The truck hit a pothole hard enough to rattle Dale's teeth. He didn't brace.

He adjusted his grip on the clipboard instead, checking the pen tip for smudges.

If the ink bled, the report was void. Void reports meant zero points. Zero points meant he stayed Level II.

And he couldn't afford to stay Level II.

Marcus drove with one hand, the other white-knuckled on the wheel. The dashboard clock blinked 00:45:12. Forty-five minutes to the public library. Forty-five minutes to walk into the dark and do something Dale didn't have a protocol for.

"You said you found something in the basement," Dale said, not looking up from the form he was pre-filling. *Service Ticket: Emergency Structural Remediation.*

"I said I saw shadows," Marcus corrected. "I said the termites were moving in patterns that looked like writing. I said the walls were breathing."

Dale clicked his pen. "Did you file a variance request?"

"I told my supervisor. He laughed."

"Unprofessional," Dale muttered. "And dangerous."

The windshield wipers slapped rhythmically against the sleet. Outside, Toledo was a smear of gray and orange streetlights. The city felt heavy, pressurized, like the air before a tornado touches down.

Dale's Field Guide hummed in his peripheral vision.

It wasn't a sound, exactly. It was a pressure change in the inner ear, a subtle shift in air density that the Authority's interface imposed on licensed professionals.

He ignored it.

He focused on the *Internal Affairs* note. *They exist in the paperwork.*

If the enemy was bureaucratic, Dale had an advantage. He knew how to fill out a form. He knew how to file a complaint. He knew how to make a thing disappear by drowning it in red tape.

But he also knew that some things required bleach.

"Pull over," Dale said.

Marcus didn't slow down. "We're ten minutes out."

"The truck's contaminated."

Dale pointed to the rearview mirror. The static shadow from the warehouse was still there, clinging to the back seat headrest. It wasn't gone. It was riding along.

Marcus slammed the brakes. The seatbelt locked against Dale's collarbone.

"That's not possible," Marcus hissed. "We left it."

"It's a residual marker," Dale said, unbuckling. "Class IX entities don't travel physically.

They attach to signatures.

You signed off on the warehouse inspection, didn't you?

You signed the closure document."

Marcus stared at him, mouth open. "I... I checked the box. To close the ticket."

"Then you invited it in." Dale opened the door. The wind howled, cold and wet. "Get out.

Do not sign anything else. Do not acknowledge anything else."

Marcus scrambled out, stumbling into the sleet. Dale followed, slamming the door. The truck groaned, the engine idling roughly. The static figure in the mirror flickered, then vanished.

Dale straightened his jacket.

He checked his boots. Clean. He checked his gloves.

Leather, double-layered, certified for Class 4 biohazards.

"Walk," Dale ordered.

They walked toward the library. The building loomed ahead, a brutalist concrete block wrapped in chain-link fencing. *Sealed. Condemned. Hazardous.*

The gate was locked with a heavy padlock and a chain that looked like it belonged on a prison yard. Dale pulled a pair of bolt cutters from his belt.

He didn't hesitate.

The Authority recognized property management rights during active infestations. Cutting the lock was a necessary containment measure.

The steel snapped with a loud *clack*.

Marcus flinched. "We shouldn't be here."

"Protocol 9-Alpha allows entry for verified structural threats," Dale said, stepping over the chain. "You're the witness. If we die, you say you were coerced."

"I wasn't coerced. I drove you here."

"Then you're an accomplice. Try to keep your Thoroughness Rating above a 5. It helps with insurance."

Dale moved toward the main doors. They were boarded up with plywood, but the wood was wrong. It wasn't new. It was old, gray, and slick with a black sludge that seeped from the cracks in the masonry.

Dale paused. He pulled out his flashlight.

The beam cut through the rain, illuminating the sludge.

It wasn't water.

It was thick, viscous, and it moved. Not with the wind.

It moved against it, creeping upward toward the lock.

*Fungal residue,* Dale noted. *Myco-infestation. Class 3.*

He flipped open his Field Guide. The entry for *Dimensional Vermin* was there. He scrolled past it, searching for *Fungal* entries. There were none.

He frowned.

"Marcus. You said the termites were moving in patterns."

"Yes."

"Termites don't move in patterns. They move in trails. If they're moving in patterns, they aren't termites. They're being directed."

Dale stepped closer to the door. The black sludge pulsed.

A low vibration hummed through the soles of his boots.

It wasn't a sound; it was a frequency. A sub-audible drone that made his fillings ache.

He raised his radio. "Authority Dispatch. This is Dale Kowalski, License 44-B, Class II. Requesting remote scan of the following coordinate."

Static.

Then, a voice, dry and distant. *Scan initiated. Warning: Unregistered entity detected.

Classification ambiguous.

Proceed with caution.*

"Ambiguous," Dale muttered. "That never means anything good."

He reached for the door handle. The metal was ice-cold. The black sludge recoiled from his gloved hand, sizzling slightly.

"Bio-reactive," Dale said. "Acidic. Don't touch it."

Marcus hung back, watching the tree line. The static shadow was gone, but the feeling of being watched was intense. Prickling at the back of the neck.

Dale kicked the door.

It didn't budge. The plywood held, but the frame cracked.

The wood wasn't rotten.

It was calcified.

Turned to stone.

Dale stared at the crack. He reached into his belt and pulled out a small vial of white powder. *Calcium-dissolver compound.* Standard for masonry pests, repurposed for dimensional residue.

He dusted the crack. The powder hissed, turning yellow. The stone softened. The wood returned to wood, but it was brittle, black, and covered in tiny, wriggling filaments.

"Spores," Dale said. "Active spores."

He didn't hesitate. He kicked the door again. This time, the wood splintered inward.

The smell hit him first. Not rot. Not decay.

It smelled like old paper. Like dust and vanilla and the metallic tang of dried blood.

He stepped inside.

The lobby was dark. The air was still. Rain hammered against the boarded-up windows outside, but inside, it was silent. Too silent.

Dale clicked his flashlight to full beam.

The floor was covered in paper.

Not scattered. Piled. Stacked in neat, towering columns that reached the ceiling.

Thousands of books, manuscripts, and loose-leaf pages formed a labyrinth.

And in the center of the room, standing perfectly still, was a figure.

It wore a suit. Gray, ill-fitting. Its face was a blur of shifting text. Words scrolled across its skin like a ticker tape.

*VIOLATION. NON-COMPLIANCE. CLOSER.*

Marcus froze in the doorway. "What is that?"

Dale didn't answer. He raised his clipboard.

The figure turned. The scrolling text accelerated.

**CREATURE: AUDITOR.** **CLASS: IX.** **STATUS: ACTIVE.** **ACTION: INITIATE AUDIT.**

A wave of pressure hit Dale. Not physical.

Mental.

His vision blurred. Numbers swam in his eyes. Dates.

Costs. Penalties.

*You failed to document the entry point,* the voice echoed in his skull. *Penalty: 500 points.*

*You failed to notify the homeowner,* the voice continued. *Penalty: 1000 points.*

*You are unauthorized,* the voice boomed. *Penalty: Erasure.*

Dale's knees buckled. The weight of the accusation was crushing. He felt the urge to fall, to admit fault, to sign away his license, his name, his existence.

But he didn't fall.

He bit his tongue. Hard. The pain grounded him.

"I have a ticket," Dale gasped.

The Auditor paused. The text on its face slowed.

*Show me.*

Dale held up the clipboard. His hand shook. He couldn't let it see the blank page. He couldn't let it see the void.

He needed a loophole. A system rule he could exploit.

*Cross-contamination penalty: deploying a protocol designed for one creature type against another inflicts a 40% debuff.*

The Auditor was Class IX. Dale was Class II. Direct combat was suicide. Documentation was the only weapon.

But you couldn't document a non-physical entity. Not directly.

Unless you documented its environment.

Dale looked at the paper piles. The books. The sludge.

*The library is a repository of information,* Dale thought. *Information is data. Data requires storage. Storage requires maintenance.*

The Auditor was the maintenance crew gone wrong. It was a glitch in the system. A virus in the archives.

Dale ripped a page from his clipboard. Not a report. A complaint form. *Form 14-B: Noise and Nuisance Violation.*

He scribbled furiously. Not with his hand. With his mind. He poured his intent into the ink.

*Subject: Unregistered Entity. Location: Public Library, Basement Sector 4. Violation: Excessive Bureaucratic Noise. Remediation: Immediate Silencing.*

He slammed the page onto the floor.

The Auditor shrieked. A sound like grinding gears.

*INVALID FORM,* it screamed. *INCORRECT CATEGORY.*

"It's a nuisance violation!" Dale shouted. "You are loud. You are disruptive. You are out of compliance with the peace ordinance!"

The Auditor lurched forward. The text on its face turned red.

*YOU CANNOT FILE AGAINST THE LAW. I AM THE LAW.*

Dale grabbed Marcus by the collar and shoved him backward. "Run!"

Marcus didn't move.

He was staring at the floor.

The paper piles were rising. The books were opening.

Pages fluttered into the air, forming a whirlwind.

Dale looked down. The sludge was spreading.

It was forming shapes.

Hands. Fingers. Reaching for his boots.

*Contamination alert,* the System whispered. *Field Guide corrupted.*

Dale felt a spike of panic. If his Guide was corrupted, he couldn't identify the weakness. He was fighting blind.

But he remembered the note. *They exist in the paperwork.*

Paperwork has a shelf life. Paper rots.

Dale pulled a flare from his belt.

Not a standard flare. A *chemical* flare.

Used for marking treated zones.

It burned with a phosphorescent green light that was specifically designed to be invisible to organic eyes but blinding to spectral ones.

He lit it.

The Auditor recoiled. The text on its face scrambled.

*LIGHT. LIGHT. UNAUTHORIZED ILLUMINATION.*

Dale threw the flare into the center of the paper pile.

The flame caught. Books don't burn easily when they're wet, but these weren't wet.

They were dry.

Desiccated.

Ancient.

The fire spread fast. Not with orange flames, but with white, cold fire. The Auditor screamed, a sound like tearing canvas.

*YOU ARE DESTROYING THE RECORDS,* the Auditor howled.

"I am sanitizing the site!" Dale yelled back. "That is protocol!"

He grabbed Marcus. "Move! Now!"

They ran. The library floor was buckling under the heat. The paper walls twisted and curled.

The Auditor didn't chase them. It stood in the center of the inferno, arms spread, watching the destruction.

*You have created a new infestation,* the voice said, calm now. *Fire is a Class 2 hazard. You are now responsible for the cleanup.*

Dale slammed through the back exit, bursting into the alleyway. Rain drenched him. The heat from the library radiated against his back.

Sirens wailed in the distance. Not police. *Authority* sirens. Low, humming pulses that shook the ground.

Marcus slumped against the brick wall, gasping. "We burned it down. We killed it."

Dale shook his head, wiping ash from his face. He checked his clipboard. The page was gone. Burned.

"No," Dale said. "We didn't kill it. We just moved the problem."

He looked at his System interface.

**TREATMENT COMPLETE: PARTIAL.** **POINTS AWARDED: 0.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 2.1.** **STATUS: PROBATIONARY.**

Dale stared at the number. 2.1. He had been a 9.2 for months.

*Why?*

Then he saw it. A new notification.

**VIOLATION: ARSON.** **CIVIL LIABILITY: $50,000.** **CERTIFICATION SUSPENDED PENDING INVESTIGATION.**

Dale's blood ran cold.

The Auditor hadn't just been a monster. It had been a trap.

By burning the library, Dale had destroyed the evidence of the Auditor's existence. He had destroyed the records that proved the Rift was active. He had destroyed the only thing that could clear his name.

And in doing so, he had made himself the primary suspect.

Marcus looked at him, eyes wide. "Dale. Your license."

Dale looked at the burning building. The fire wasn't orange. It was black. It was consuming the light, not creating it.

And in the shadows of the alley, three figures stepped out.

They wore suits. Gray. Ill-fitting.

Their faces were blurred.

The first one raised a hand.

*Mr. Kowalski,* it said. *Please come with us.*

Dale didn't run. He picked up his clipboard. It was empty.

He had one hour left. And now, he was the defendant.
