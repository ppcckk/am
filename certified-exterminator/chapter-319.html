Chapter 319: Nine Seconds to Loophole

# Chapter 319

The pen scratched against the clipboard. A rhythmic, dry sound, louder than the dripping of blue fluid from the cracked resin seal.

Dale Kowalski adjusted his grip.

His fingers were blackened, the skin blistered and tight over the knuckles, but the movement was precise. He did not tremble. Trembling was a liability in high-pressure documentation environments.

**FORM 114-B: PRE-INSPECTION ASSESSMENT** **SUBJECT:** UNIDENTIFIED DIMENSIONAL ENTITY (CLASS: UNKNOWN) **LOCATION:** SUB-SURFACE CAVERN, SECTOR 7-G **INSPECTOR:** DALE KOWALSKI (CERT LEVEL II) **TIME:** 03:42 AM

He looked up from the form. The figure in the tactical gear—Unit 7, according to the chest plate—had lowered the rifle. Not completely. The muzzle still tracked Dale's center of mass, a lazy figure-eight pattern that suggested the shooter was nervous.

"You're writing," Unit 7 said. The voice was filtered through a respirator, stripped of humanity, leaving only the mechanical rasp of air filters. "We're under a containment breach order. You're writing a form."

"Protocol requires documentation before engagement," Dale said.

He didn't look at the rifle. He looked at the crack in the black resin wall.

The blue drop had spread.

It formed a small puddle, shimmering with a viscosity that suggested mucus than blood.

"If I engage without a baseline assessment, and this thing is a mimic variant, my field guide entry gets corrupted. I lose my bonus damage against it.

I lose my efficiency.

"

"Efficiency," Unit 7 repeated. The word sounded foreign in their mouth. "There's a thing in there that ate three of my squad. You're worried about efficiency."

"I'm worried about redundancy." Dale turned the page. "Let me see the entry point. The crack. Is it structural or organic?"

Unit 7 hesitated. The rifle dipped an inch.

"Why?"

"Because if it's organic, I need to treat the spores before they aerosolize.

If I shoot first and ask questions later, I contaminate the sample.

And if I contaminate the sample, the Authority deducts points. I have a Thoroughness Rating of 9.2.

I don't intend to drop it because you panicked.

"

The figure stared at him. Behind the visor, the eyes widened.

Not with fear, exactly.

With confusion. The Authority did not punish fear. It punished sloppiness.

Unit 7 stepped back.

"Go. But I'm watching you. One wrong move, I put a round through your knee."

Dale nodded, and he walked toward the crack. The air here smelled of ozone and rotting kelp.

The floor was slick with condensation.

His boots squeaked on the stone.

He pulled a small vial from his utility belt. Standard issue.

Empty, and he uncapped it, held it beneath the leak, and waited.

The blue fluid dripped into the glass. It glowed faintly, pulsing with a low, rhythmic thrum.

**FIELD GUIDE UPDATE:** **SPECIMEN SAMPLE COLLECTED.** **ANALYSIS INITIATED.** **WAITING FOR ENVIRONMENTAL CONTEXT.**

Dale held his breath. He watched the fluid. It didn't mix with the residue on the floor.

It beaded up, rolling away from the stone like mercury. Hydrophobic.

High surface tension.

"Not a mimic," Dale muttered. "Mimics bond to surfaces. This thing repels them."

He pulled a pen from behind his ear and scribbled a note in the margin of Form 114-B. *Subject exhibits hydrophobic cellular structure. Likely aquatic or semi-aquatic origin. Not terrestrial vermin.*

The pen snapped in his hand. The plastic casing fractured along the stress line he'd been ignoring for weeks. Dale stared at the broken pen.

He felt a cold knot in his stomach, and he had used that pen for three inspections in a row. He hadn't replaced it.

A small oversight.

A tiny crack in his own protocol.

Unit 7 saw the broken pen.

"You okay, tech?"

"Equipment failure," Dale said. He capped the vial.

"I need to log this.

Equipment failure constitutes a safety hazard.

If I drop the sample, I lose the identification bonus. If I lose the bonus, I'm fighting blind.

Do you want me fighting blind?

"

"No."

"Then give me a pen."

Unit 7 didn't have one. They fumbled with a utility pouch, pulling out a tactical knife instead.

"I have a blade."

"A blade is for severing limbs, not signing forms." Dale sighed. He reached into his other pocket and produced a grease pencil.

He used it to write the rest of the assessment.

The graphite smeared on the paper, but the Authority accepted it. The System did not care about legibility, only completeness.

**DOCUMENTATION INCOMPLETE. MINOR DEDUCTION: -5 POINTS.** **REASON: IMPROPER INSTRUMENT USE.** **TOTAL POINTS: 145.**

Dale grimaced. Fourteen years of perfection, and he lost five points for a broken ballpoint. He taped the grease pencil back into his kit. He wouldn't make that mistake twice.

He looked at the crack again. The blue puddle had grown to the size of a dinner plate. The shape shifting in the darkness beyond the resin wall was still there, but it had stopped moving. It was waiting.

"Unit 7," Dale said. "What's the radio chatter saying?"

Static.

Then, a voice. Not the radio operator.

Something deeper, which came from the cavern itself, vibrating through the floor soles of their boots.

*"Let him in."*

The command wasn't loud. It was inevitable. Like gravity.

Unit 7 raised the rifle again.

"It's talking. Did you hear that?"

"I heard it," Dale said. "It's not speaking English. It's speaking Authority code."

"Authority code?"

"Standard summons." Dale checked his wrist monitor. The screen flickered.

**NEW QUEST: THE AUDITOR.** **OBJECTIVE: SURVIVE THE INSPECTION.** **REWARD: UNLOCK CLASS III PROTOCOLS.** **FAILURE: TERMINATION.**

Dale tapped the screen.

"It's an invitation.

A formal audit.

The Authority doesn't send auditors to kill pests.

They send them to evaluate the pest control technician.

"

Unit 7 lowered the rifle slightly.

"You're saying this monster is an inspector?"

"I'm saying we're in a workplace violation scenario." Dale straightened his back. The pain in his burned hands flared, white-hot, but he ignored it. Pain was subjective.

Protocol was objective.

"If we treat this as a combat encounter, we fail the audit.

If we treat it as a compliance check, we pass.

"

"How do you pass a compliance check against a monster?"

"You answer the questions."

"The monster asks questions?"

"The Authority asks questions. The monster is just the medium." Dale looked at the crack. "I need to enter. Alone."

Unit 7 stepped forward, blocking the path.

"No. That's suicide.

My orders are to contain the breach.

Not let you walk into it.

"

"Your orders are from the tactical command," Dale said.

"The Authority is older than your command. If I don't go, the infestation spreads. If the infestation spreads, your containment is worthless.

Do you want a containment breach, or do you want a closed case file?

"

Unit 7 looked at the rifle. Then at Dale. Then at the darkness.

"If you die, I shoot you," Unit 7 said. "To prevent infection."

"Understood," Dale said. "Please note that in your report."

He stepped through the crack, and the resin wall did not feel like stone.

It felt like skin.

Warm, yielding, pulsing. The air inside was thick, humid, and smelled of copper.

Dale's flashlight beam cut through the gloom, illuminating a vast chamber. The ceiling was lost in shadow. The floor was covered in a lattice of black, chitinous structures.

And in the center of the room, standing on a platform of fused bone and stone, was a figure.

It was humanoid. Tall.

Thin.

Its head was a smooth, featureless oval of white bone. It wore a suit that looked like it was made of pressed insect wings, shimmering with iridescence. In its hand, it held a clipboard.

A real clipboard. Wood and metal.

**INSPECTOR IDENTITY CONFIRMED.** **NAME: [REDACTED]** **RANK: AUDITOR FIRST CLASS.** **STATUS: ACTIVE.**

Dale stopped, and he adjusted his tie. He checked his pocket for a spare pen.

He didn't have one.

He pulled out the grease pencil.

The Auditor tilted its head. The motion was sharp, like a bird's.

"Technician Kowalski," the Auditor said. The voice echoed in Dale's skull, bypassing his ears. "You have accumulated a Thoroughness Rating of 9.2. This is statistically improbable for a Level II licensed technician."

"Thank you," Dale said. "I try to document everything."

"You document nothing," the Auditor said. It raised the clipboard.

"Your file is incomplete.

You have identified *Rattus profundus*.

You have identified acid beetles. You have identified broodmothers.

But you have not identified the vector.

"

Dale frowned. "The vector? The Rift?"

"No," the Auditor said. "The source.

You treat the symptoms.

You spray the ants. You seal the cracks. But you have not asked where the ants come from.

You have not filed Form 909-A: *Origin Tracing Report*.

"

Dale felt a chill. He hadn't filed Form 909-A.

It was an optional form, and he had skipped it on the last three jobs because he was short on time. He had prioritized speed over depth. A calculated risk.

A minor infraction.

"I prioritized containment," Dale said.

"Containment is a temporary measure," the Auditor said. "Eradication requires understanding. You failed to understand. Therefore, you failed the inspection."

The Auditor pointed a long, spindly finger at Dale.

**AUDIT RESULT: NON-COMPLIANT.** **PENALTY: LICENSE REVOCATION PENDING.** **COUNTER-PENALTY OPTION AVAILABLE: SUBMIT MISSING DOCUMENTATION WITHIN 60 SECONDS.**

Dale's heart hammered against his ribs.

License revocation, which meant zero points. It meant he could never advance.

It meant he was just a man with a clipboard and dead hands.

Sixty seconds.

He looked around the chamber. The chitinous lattice. The blue fluid dripping from the ceiling. The shape of the floor tiles.

He didn't need to fight. He needed to see.

He dropped to his knees. He scraped the floor with his grease pencil.

The black dust came up easily.

It wasn't dirt. It was frass. Insect waste.

He ran his fingers through the dust, which was coarse. Angular.

Not chewed wood.

Not plant matter.

Mineral frass.

He looked up. The ceiling wasn't rock. It was layers of compressed sediment, laced with veins of quartz.

The "monster" wasn't an animal. It was a geological entity. A living mineral formation that consumed organic matter to fuel its growth. The blue fluid was a byproduct of silica dissolution.

The species wasn't in his Field Guide. He had to create the entry.

**NEW ENTRY: LITHIC PARASITE (Genus: Petrosoma)** **WEAKNESS: ACIDIC DISSOLUTION (pH < 2.0).** **WEAKNESS: FREQUENCY RESONANCE (440Hz).** **WEAKNESS: STRUCTURAL FRACTURE.**

Dale wrote furiously. The grease pencil squeaked on the clipboard.

His hand shook, but the writing was legible, and he listed the physical characteristics.

The habitat. The diet.

Thirty seconds.

He added the behavior patterns. The way it sealed itself off. The way it called to the technician.

It wasn't hunting. It was mating.

The "command" he heard was a mating call.

It wanted a mate. It wanted *him*.

Twenty seconds, and he added the control methods. Acid. Sonic emitters. Explosives to break the lattice.

Ten seconds.

He signed the form. He stamped it with his digital ID.

**DOCUMENTATION COMPLETE.** **ENTRY ADDED TO FIELD GUIDE.** **AUDIT RESULT: COMPLIANT.** **BONUS: +200 POINTS FOR RAPID SPECIES IDENTIFICATION.**

The Auditor lowered its clipboard. The featureless face didn't change, but the posture softened. The iridescent wings rustled.

"Acceptable," the Auditor said. "You have identified the vector. The Lithic Parasite is the source of the dimensional bleed. It consumes the rift's energy and excretes the vermin."

Dale stood up. His knees popped.

"So, what now?"

"Now," the Auditor said, "you must submit a Remediation Plan. Or you must submit your resignation."

Dale looked at the Auditor. Then at the crack behind him, where Unit 7 was still waiting with a rifle.

"I don't resign," Dale said.

"Then you must exterminate it."

"It's a mountain," Dale said. "You can't exterminate a mountain."

"You can if you know where to drill," the Auditor said.

It handed him a small, black device. It looked like a detonator, but the wires were made of bone.

"This is a Class III Protocol Emitter.

It emits a sonic frequency that shatters the Lithic Parasite's core. But it requires a direct connection to the host's nervous system.

"

Dale looked at the device. Then at his burned hands. Then at the Auditor.

"Direct connection?"

"You must insert it into the core. You must touch the heart of the mountain."

The Auditor stepped back. The shadows in the room lengthened. The blue fluid flowed faster, pooling around Dale's boots.

"If you fail," the Auditor said, "the Authority will deem you a contaminant. We will remove you. Permanently."

Dale held the bone-wire device. It hummed against his palm, a vibration that traveled up his arm and settled in his chest.

He looked at the crack. Unit 7 was shouting something. The radio was screaming.

Dale looked at the Auditor.

"I need a pen," Dale said.

The Auditor tilted its head.

"Why?"

"Because I need to sign the waiver," Dale said. "And you don't have one."

The Auditor paused. For the first time, the entity seemed to consider a human complaint. It reached into its winged suit and produced a fountain pen. It was black ink, but the liquid glowed blue.

Dale took the pen. He didn't look at the waiver.

He didn't read the fine print, and he knew the fine print.

The Authority always wanted more. More data.

More risk. More control.

He signed his name.

**WAIVER ACCEPTED.** **PROTOCOL: LITHIC DISRUPTION.** **STATUS: ACTIVE.**

The Auditor vanished. The shadows swallowed the space where it had stood.

Dale was alone in the cavern. The blue fluid was at his ankles. The lattice around him vibrated. A low, deep hum, rising in pitch.

He looked at the device in his hand. He looked at the darkness ahead, where the core of the mountain waited.

He took a step forward. Then another. The mud sucked at his boots.

From the radio on his belt, Unit 7's voice broke through the static.

"Dale! Get out of there! The seismic sensors are spiking! It's moving!"

Dale didn't answer. He kept walking.

He held the device tight, and he thought about his broken pen. He thought about the five points he had lost.

He reached the center of the chamber. A pulsating mass of quartz and flesh rose from the floor. It was beautiful, in a terrible way. Crystals grew from it like teeth.

Dale raised the device, and he didn't hesitate. He plunged it into the crystal mass.

The world went white.

And then, silence.

Dale opened his eyes. He was standing in the cavern. The crystal mass was cracked. The blue fluid had stopped flowing.

A notification flashed in his vision.

**JOB COMPLETE.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 10.0.** **CERTIFICATION LEVEL: II (MAINTAINED).**

Then, a second notification.

**ERROR: CROSS-CONTAMINATION DETECTED.** **REASON: PROTOCOL USED (SONIC) DOES NOT MATCH IDENTIFIED SPECIES (LITHIC).** **CORRECT PROTOCOL: ACIDIC DISSOLUTION.** **PENALTY: FIELD GUIDE ENTRY CORRUPTED FOR 24 HOURS.** **STATUS: INCOMPLETE.**

Dale stared at the text.

The acid. He had identified the weakness as acid.

He had used sonic resonance, and he had followed the Auditor's instruction, but the Auditor had lied. Or the System was stricter than he thought.

He looked at his Field Guide. The entry for *Petrosoma* was blurred. He couldn't read it. He couldn't use the bonus damage against it.

And from the darkness, a sound. Not a hum. A growl. The crack in the crystal mass glowed red.

The mountain was not dead. It was awake. And it was angry.

Dale looked at his hands. The burns were turning blacker. He couldn't feel his fingers.

He looked at the radio. Unit 7 was screaming.

He looked at the new notification that just appeared.

**NEW VIOLATION: UNLICENSED COMBAT ENGAGEMENT.** **PENALTY: IMMEDIATE TERMINATION IN 10 SECONDS.**

Dale Kowalski looked at the System. He looked at the red glow. He looked at the pen in his pocket.

He took a breath, and he reached for the grease pencil.

Nine seconds left to write a loophole.
