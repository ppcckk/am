Chapter 236: Fired in the Dark

# Chapter 236

The countdown clock in Dale's vision ticked down from nine.

The giant's hand hovered, the nullifier sphere pulsing with a rhythm that matched the dripping water. Dale didn't look at the weapon.

He looked at the floor.

The water was rising past his ankles, brown and thick with dissolved insulation foam. It wasn't just water. It was a conductive medium.

"Stop," Dale said. His voice was flat, stripped of any urgency that might betray panic. "You're creating a Class 4 Electrical Hazard. If I invoke Emergency Shutdown, the resulting arc flash will vaporize everything in this room, including your boss."

The man in the chair, the one who had ordered the attack, wiped a smear of black sludge from his cheek. He didn't look angry. He looked bored.

"You're bluffing," the man said.

"I'm a technician," Dale replied. "I don't bluff. I document."

The giant hesitated.

His armored boots shifted in the slurry. The nullifier sphere dimmed slightly.

It was a defensive reaction.

The Authority's system recognized the threat of collateral damage and paused the aggression timer.

Dale seized the second. He didn't reach for the next cable. He reached for his clipboard.

It was soaked. The paper had turned to pulp.

The ink had run, turning his previous reports into blue smudges.

He didn't care.

He needed the physical anchor. The act of writing, even if the ink was bleeding, grounded him.

It signaled to the System that he was entering Protocol Phase Two: Containment.

"Who are you?" the man in the chair asked. He stepped off the raised platform, walking through the water without care. "You're not a hero. Heroes don't carry clipboards."

"I'm Dale Kowalski," Dale said. "Certification Level VI. Specializing in dimensional breaches and high-voltage containment."

"Level VI?" The man laughed. It was a dry, rasping sound. "We have Level IXs on retainer. You're a middle manager."

"And you're an unlicensed operator," Dale countered. He pointed the pen at the sparking server rack.

"Look at that.

The primary feed isn't just disconnected. It's arcing. You're feeding the Rift raw, unstable mana.

That's not a power source.

That's an infestation vector."

The man stopped walking. He looked at the rack. He looked at Dale. For the first time, the boredom slipped, replaced by a sharp, calculating interest.

"Show me," the man said.

Dale didn't move toward the man.

He moved toward the server. The water soaked through his boots, cold and biting. He ignored it.

He ignored the giant looming in the doorway, ignoring the way the floorboards groaned under the armored weight. He focused on the cables.

There were three main power conduits feeding the central server. Two were severed. The third was intact, but the insulation had melted away, exposing copper wire that hovered inches above the puddle.

"If you touch it," the giant rumbled, his voice like grinding stones, "you die."

"If I touch it," Dale said, "I can reroute the surge."

"You don't have tools."

"I have the environment."

Dale crouched.

The water lapped at his knees.

He pulled a roll of industrial tape from his belt. It was dry.

He'd kept it in a waterproof pouch. Standard protocol. You never trusted the field conditions.

He peeled a strip of tape. He didn't tape the wire. He taped the gap between the wire and the nearest metal support beam.

"What are you doing?" the man in the chair asked.

"Grounding it," Dale said.

"That's stupid. The ground is wet."

"The ground is *conductive*," Dale corrected. "The ground isn't the issue. The potential difference is."

He pressed the tape down. The System pinged.

**ACTION: TEMPORARY GROUNDING VIA ADHESIVE INSULATION.** **RISK: HIGH.** **EFFECT: NEUTRALIZES ARC FLASH FOR 10 SECONDS.**

Dale looked up. The giant's nullifier sphere was fading. The threat had passed.

"You created a violation," Dale said to the man in the chair. "Not of physics. Of licensing."

The man raised an eyebrow. "Explain."

"You're using unauthorized dimensional siphons," Dale said. "The Authority monitors Rift activity.

You think you're hiding this because you're in a basement?

The Authority sees the thermal signature. They see the mana drain. You're not just a criminal.

You're an unregistered pest."

The man's face hardened. "We're not pests. We're pioneers."

"Pests are defined by their inability to coexist with the host environment," Dale said. "You're breaking the environment. That makes you a hazard. And hazards get exterminated."

The giant stepped forward. The floor cracked. "I'll take him down."

"No," the man in the chair said. "Wait."

He walked closer to Dale. The water swirled around his expensive shoes. He didn't care about the cost. He cared about the information.

"Your System," the man said. "It's reacting to me."

"It's reacting to the violation," Dale said.

"Show me the violation."

Dale hesitated. This was the trap.

If he showed the menu, he revealed his status.

If he revealed his status, they could target his license. But if he didn't show it, the man would assume Dale was lying, and the giant would attack.

Dale made a choice. He didn't open the menu. He opened his mouth.

"Violation Code 402," Dale said. "Unlicensed Mana Extraction. Penalty: Immediate suspension of all active protocols."

The man frowned. "And what do you gain?"

"I gain the right to detain you for inspection."

The man laughed again. "You think you can detain me?

I own this building.

I own the city block.

I own the police force."

"And I own the license to kill things that don't belong here," Dale said.

He stood up. His knees popped. The water weighed a ton.

He felt the cold seeping into his bones. He ignored it.

He looked at the giant.

"You're not licensed for combat protocols in a residential zone," Dale said. "Your nullifier is a Class IX device. Using it in a confined space with unshielded electronics violates the Cross-Contamination Statute.

If you fire that, your own license gets flagged.

You'll be blacklisted. No more contracts.

No more pay."

The giant froze. The black sphere sputtered and died. The light vanished from his palm.

"He's right," the giant muttered. The voice was no longer a rumble. It was a complaint. "I don't want the paperwork."

The man in the chair looked at the giant. Then he looked at Dale. The boredom was gone.

Now there was something else. Respect, perhaps.

Or fear.

"You're a technician," the man said. "Not a fighter."

"I'm a problem solver," Dale said. "You have a problem. I have the solution."

"And if I don't want the solution?"

"Then the Rift widens," Dale said.

He pointed to the ceiling. "Look at the cracks.

They're spreading. The structural integrity of this building is compromised. If the membrane fails, this whole block goes into the void.

You'll lose your investment."

The man looked up. He saw the fissures in the concrete. He saw the purple light leaking through.

He turned back to Dale. "Fix it."

"No," Dale said.

The giant tensed. The man waved a hand. "Let him speak."

"I can't fix it," Dale said. "I can only mitigate it. And mitigation requires access."

"Access to what?"

"To the primary feed," Dale said.

"The one you disconnected. I need to reconnect it. Properly.

With shielding.

With grounding. With a containment field I can build from what's in this room."

The man studied him. "And if you reconnect it, the Rift stabilizes?"

"Yes."

"And you get your points."

"Yes."

"And we get our power back."

"Yes."

The man smiled.

It was a thin, cruel smile. "You're negotiating. That's not what exterminators do.

They act."

"They act after they assess the risk," Dale said. "You didn't assess. You just drilled. That's amateur hour."

The man pulled a chair from the desk. It was heavy, wooden, absurd in the flooded server room. He sat down. The water lapped at the legs.

"Okay, Dale Kowalski," the man said. "You have five minutes. Build your containment field. Use what you have."

Dale looked around. The room was a mess. Servers, cables, chairs, water.

He didn't have much.

But he had the water.

He had the copper.

He had the tape.

And he had the System.

Dale closed his eyes.

He didn't look at the menu.

He didn't need to. He knew what he could do.

He had done it a thousand times with ants. He had done it a hundred times with bedbugs. He had done it ten times with things that screamed in colors that didn't exist.

This was just another nest.

He opened his eyes.

"Bring me the server rack," Dale said.

The giant didn't move.

The man in the chair snapped his fingers. "Do it."

The giant grumbled. He walked over to the sparking rack.

He didn't touch the cables.

He grabbed the metal frame. He lifted it. The water splashed.

He set it down in front of Dale.

Dale knelt again.

He took a screwdriver from his pocket. He didn't use it to pry.

He used it to poke.

He tested the voltage.

**SYSTEM ALERT: VOLTAGE SPIKE DETECTED.** **SUGGESTED ACTION: INSULATE.**

Dale ignored the suggestion. He did the opposite.

He pulled a piece of exposed wire from his belt. It was a spare ground wire, always carried for emergencies. He twisted the end around the screwdriver.

He jammed the screwdriver into the base of the server rack. He connected the wire to the metal frame.

The metal frame became the ground.

The sparking wire became the source.

The water became the conductor.

It was a circuit. A dangerous, unstable, beautiful circuit.

The giant stepped back. "You're shorting it."

"I'm balancing it," Dale said.

He looked at the man in the chair. "Close the door."

"What?"

"The door," Dale said. "It's open.

The airflow is feeding the fire in the servers.

Close it.

Create pressure."

The man stood up. He walked to the door. He pulled it shut. The latch clicked.

The room became a pressure cooker.

The sound changed. The humming grew louder. The sparks intensified. The purple light from the ceiling pulsed in time with the electrical surge.

Dale stood up. He wiped his hands on his pants. They were soaked, but the tape on his gloves held.

"It's stable," Dale said.

The man looked at the monitors. They were flickering back to life. The purple light in the ceiling faded, replaced by a steady, rhythmic glow.

The Rift wasn't closing. It was being held open. Controlled.

"You're feeding it," the man said.

"I'm filtering it," Dale said. "Raw mana is poison. Filtered mana is energy. You were poisoning yourself."

The man stared at the screens. He stared at Dale.

"You're good," the man said.

"I'm thorough," Dale said.

The System pinged.

**INFRACTION RESOLVED: UNSTABLE DIMENSIONAL FLUX.** **TREATMENT POINTS AWARDED: 500.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 8.5.** **STATUS: LEVEL VI CONFIRMED.**

Dale felt the surge of points. It was a warm feeling, like stepping out of a cold bath.

But the System wasn't done.

A new notification appeared. Red. Bold. Pulsing.

**ALERT: HIGH-VALUE TARGET DETECTED IN IMMEDIATE PROXIMITY.** **IDENTIFY: ARCH-HERETIC, CLASS IX.** **ACTION REQUIRED: IMMEDIATE CONTAINMENT OR ELIMINATION.**

Dale looked at the man in the chair.

The man looked back. He saw the notification in Dale's eyes. He saw the shift in posture.

"You knew," Dale said.

"I suspected," the man said.

He stood up. He adjusted his tie.

"You have five hundred points," the man said. "Do you know what that buys you in the Authority?"

Dale didn't answer. He was already moving.

He didn't go for his wrench. He went for the water.

He kicked the puddle. The wave hit the man's shoes.

The man didn't flinch.

"You can't kill me," the man said. "I have protection."

"You have a license," Dale said. "But you broke the rules. That means your protection is void."

The man smiled. "And what rules did I break?"

Dale looked at the giant. The giant was watching the door. Waiting for orders.

Dale looked at the server rack. It was humming. It was alive.

He looked at the ceiling. The crack was wider now.

"You're not just a pest," Dale said. "You're a vector."

The man's smile vanished.

"Get out," the man said.

"No," Dale said. "I'm just getting started."

He reached into his belt. He pulled out a small, silver canister.

It wasn't a weapon.

It was a sample vial.

He uncapped it.

The air in the room froze.

The giant took a step back.

The man in the chair went pale.

"That's not possible," the man whispered. "That's Level IX containment fluid. You're a Level VI."

"I am," Dale said. "But I borrowed it from a Level IX."

He threw the canister.

It didn't hit the man.

It hit the server rack.

The liquid hit the hot copper.

It exploded.

Not with fire.

With silence.

A sphere of absolute zero expanded from the rack. It consumed the water. It consumed the sparks. It consumed the sound.

The man in the chair was caught in the edge of it. His shoe turned to ice. His leg turned to ice. He screamed, but no sound came out.

The giant lunged forward. He tried to grab Dale.

Dale didn't move. He stood in the center of the room, surrounded by the expanding circle of frozen reality.

He looked at the man.

The man looked at Dale.

The ice climbed. It reached the man's waist. His face was a mask of terror.

Dale pulled out his clipboard. The paper was still wet, but the ink held.

He wrote.

**SUBJECT: UNLICENSED VECTOR.** **STATUS: IN CAPTIVITY.** **NEXT STEP: INTERROGATION.**

The ice stopped. It held the man in place. Suspended.

Alive. But trapped.

The giant stared at Dale. His hand hovered, empty. The nullifier was gone. The power was gone.

Dale finished writing. He tore the page off.

He held it out to the giant.

"Sign it," Dale said.

The giant didn't move.

Dale looked at the ceiling. The Rift was open. The purple light was back.

But this time, it wasn't leaking.

It was watching.

Something on the other side looked down.

It saw Dale.

It saw the clipboard.

It tilted its head.

Dale didn't look up. He looked at the man in the ice.

"You have a guarantor," Dale said. "But he's not here to help you."

The man's eyes moved. He looked past Dale. At the ceiling. At the thing that was looking down.

The man's mouth opened.

He tried to speak.

No sound.

The ice crackled.

A hairline fracture appeared in the ice block.

Then another.

Then a spiderweb of cracks spread across the surface.

The man in the ice smiled.

It was a terrible smile.

The System flashed red.

**WARNING: CONTAINMENT BREACH IMMINENT.** **SOURCE: INTERNAL.** **ACTION: ABANDON PROTOCOL.**

Dale looked at the message.

He looked at the giant.

The giant raised his hands. Not in attack. In surrender.

Dale looked at the ceiling.

The thing on the other side reached down.

Its hand was made of stars.

It touched the crack in the ice.

The ice shattered.

The man fell to the floor. He was alive. He was free.

He looked up at Dale.

"Hello, Dale," the man said.

He stood up. He shook water off his coat.

"Welcome to the Authority," the man said.

Dale's clipboard fell from his hands.

It hit the water with a splash.

The man picked it up.

He looked at the report.

He looked at Dale.

"You're fired," the man said.

The lights in the room went out.

In the darkness, Dale heard the click of a gun.
