Chapter 377: Unidentified Entity

# Chapter 377

The seam in the air tore open like wet paper.

Dale slammed the brakes. The truck fishtailed on the damp asphalt, tires screeching against the grit. The engine coughed, then died. Silence rushed back in, heavy and suffocating.

He didn't wait for the dust to settle. He grabbed the clipboard from the passenger seat.

The metal edge dug into his palm.

Good pain. Grounding.

Outside the windshield, the shimmer coalesced. It wasn't just a glitch anymore. It was a doorway. And something was pushing through it.

The thing that emerged looked like a man, but wrong.

Too tall. Joints bending backward.

Its skin was a pale, translucent gray, stretched tight over ribcages that pulsed with black fluid.

No eyes. Just smooth, blank faces where a face should be.

Dale's Field Guide flashed in his peripheral vision.

**Unidentified Entity.** **Status: Unknown.** **Recommendation: Isolate and Contain.**

He didn't have a protocol for this. He hadn't studied this. The Authority's database was silent on

"Time-Eater Mimics" or whatever nightmare had just crawled out of a temporal paradox.

The creature stumbled onto the street. It let out a sound like grinding glass.

A woman across the street screamed, and she pointed at the monster, then at Dale's truck.

"Help! Call the police!"

Dale ignored her. He stepped out of the truck. His boots hit the pavement. The air tasted like ozone and rotting eggs.

*Protocol Step One: Identify.*

He couldn't identify it. Not by species. But he could identify the behavior.

It was disoriented. Staggering.

Its movements were jerky, skipping frames like a bad video file.

*It's unstable.*

The creature lunged toward the screaming woman. Dale moved. He didn't run.

He didn't sprint, and he walked with the brisk, efficient pace of a technician late for a lunch break. He pulled the canister from his belt.

Not the standard pesticide.

The experimental canister he'd been saving for Level IV threats.

He sprayed.

The mist hit the creature's chest. The gray skin rippled.

The black fluid beneath bubbled.

The creature shrieked, a high-pitched frequency that made Dale's teeth ache.

But it didn't die. It didn't even slow down.

**Cross-Contamination Warning: Protocol Mismatch.** **Effect: 40% Debuff to Next Action.**

Dale's hands trembled. The debuff was physical, a sudden weakness in his muscles that felt like he'd been drained of blood. He stumbled, catching himself on the truck's bumper.

"Stop!" he shouted. "Do not engage! Evacuate the sector!"

The woman ignored him. She froze, rooted by fear. The creature turned its blank face toward Dale.

It sensed the threat. It sensed the *protocol*.

It lunged.

Dale dodged, and he didn't have the speed of a hero. He had the spatial awareness of a man who had spent fourteen years navigating cluttered attics and narrow crawlspaces. He knew where the obstacles were.

He knew where the exit was.

The creature's clawed hand smashed into the side of his truck. Metal crumpled.

Glass shattered.

Dale rolled under the chassis, scraping his elbows raw. The debuff made his movements sluggish, clumsy.

*Why is it not dying?*

He checked the Field Guide again. The entry was still blank. No weakness listed. No vulnerability highlighted.

*Because I haven't documented it yet.*

The system rewarded thoroughness. Killing without identifying yielded zero points. And now, with the debuff, he was likely to fail entirely.

The creature pulled itself free from the truck's frame. It looked around. Confused. It tilted its head, listening to the wind.

*It's looking for the anchor.*

Dale realized it then. The creature wasn't hunting the woman.

It was hunting the *source* of the rift.

The glitch.

The seam in reality. It was trying to get back.

Or bring more through.

Dale looked at the shimmering air above the road. It was widening. Another shape was forming within the haze.

*If another one comes through, I'm dead.*

He needed a loophole, and he looked at the destroyed truck. The front tire was flat. The engine was dead. But the battery was still intact.

*Electromagnetic interference disrupts dimensional coherence.*

He hadn't tested that. Not on a Class-IV entity.

But he had tested it on Class-III Acid Beetles, and they hated high-voltage arcs. It confused their sensory organs.

Was this thing like an insect?

The creature took a step toward him.

Its legs bent unnaturally, the knees knocking together. It was trying to move, but the timeline was rejecting it.

It was a foreign body.

An invader.

Dale crawled out from under the truck. The creature noticed him. It raised a hand. The air around its fingers twisted, warping into a vortex of gray light.

*Temporal Discharge.*

Dale didn't have time to think. He reached into his tool belt.

He pulled out the jumper cables.

Thick, heavy, copper-core cables.

The kind used to jump-start a dead battery, and he ran toward the creature.

Not away. Toward it.

The debuff made his legs feel like lead. He stumbled. The creature's hand lashed out.

The vortex of gray light grazed his shoulder. Pain exploded in his arm.

Not a cut.

A sensation of *loss*. As if a piece of his memory had been erased.

He ignored it. He kept moving.

He reached the creature's foot. He threw the cable at the ground. He kicked the end of it into the shimmering seam of the rift.

"Clear!" Dale yelled.

He didn't know if it would work. He didn't know if the Authority would count this as extermination. It wasn't killing. It was *jamming*.

But the system didn't care about intent. It cared about *effect*.

Dale grabbed the other end of the cable, and he wrapped it around the creature's ankle. The skin felt cold, like dead meat.

He tied a quick knot.

A technician's knot. Secure. Redundant.

Then he ran back to the truck.

The creature roared. It tried to pull away. The cable held. The tension snapped taut.

Dale opened the hood. He bypassed the safety switches. He grabbed the positive terminal of the battery. He grabbed the negative.

He didn't connect them to the engine. He connected them to the cable.

He closed the circuit.

A spark jumped. Then another. Then a arc of blue-white lightning screamed from the battery, through the copper cable, and into the creature's leg.

The creature froze.

The gray light in its body flickered. The black fluid stopped bubbling. The creature's form pixelated. Break apart.

The rift above the road shuddered. The second shape inside the haze vanished.

The creature screamed. It wasn't a sound. It was a sensation in Dale's skull. A white-hot spike of static.

Then, it collapsed.

It didn't fall like a body. It dissolved.

Like sugar in water.

Like smoke in a draft.

The gray skin melted into the asphalt. The black fluid evaporated into steam.

The bones turned to dust.

The cable snapped. The battery exploded.

Dale was thrown backward. He hit the pavement hard.

His vision blurred.

Smoke filled his lungs. He coughed, gagging on the smell of ozone and burnt plastic.

Silence returned.

The shimmer in the air was gone. The street was empty. The woman across the road was gone. Vanished.

Dale lay there, staring at the sky.

His shoulder throbbed. The memory loss was real.

He couldn't remember his mother's name, and he couldn't remember his first car. Small pieces. Gone.

*Cost of using non-standard protocols.*

He sat up. He checked his clipboard. It was cracked. The paper was singed.

**Treatment Complete.** **Entity: Temporal Echo (Class IV).** **Method: Electromagnetic Disruption.** **Thoroughness Rating: 8.9.**

**Penalty: Memory Fragmentation.**

8.9. Not bad. Not great. But it counted.

Dale stood up. His legs shook.

He walked to the truck, and the hood was melted.

The engine block was a smoking ruin. He had no way to drive home.

He looked down the street. No one was coming. No police.

No Authority agents. Just the morning dew and the smell of burnt wiring.

He had to walk, and he started down the road.

His shoulder ached. His mind felt hollow, scraped clean. He tried to recall the name of his previous supervisor.

Blank.

*Don't think about it. Focus on the next task.*

His phone buzzed in his pocket. He pulled it out. The screen was cracked, but it worked.

A message from the Authority.

**Incoming Job.** **Location: Suburban Ohio, Sector 4.** **Threat Level: Class V.** **Client: The Municipal Library.** **Issue: Infestation.**

Dale frowned. Class V.

That was above his current certification, and he was Level II. Maybe III, if he counted the paradox bonus.

He typed back: *Cannot accept. Vehicle disabled.*

The response was immediate.

**Transport Provided. Authority Van. En route.**

Dale stared at the screen. An Authority Van?

They never sent vans, and they sent notifications.

They sent points. They didn't do logistics.

A vehicle rounded the corner. It wasn't a police car. It wasn't an ambulance.

It was a matte-black van. No markings. No license plates. The windows were tinted so dark they looked like holes in the world.

It stopped in front of Dale. The side door slid open.

Inside, the van was empty. Except for a single chair bolted to the floor. And a briefcase on the seat.

A voice came from the speaker system. It was Halloway.

"Get in, Kowalski."

Dale looked at the open door. The interior was cold. He could feel the chill from ten feet away.

"I need a clipboard," Dale said.

"Take the one in the briefcase," Halloway's voice crackled. "And hurry. The library infestation isn't rats. It's not bugs."

"What is it?"

"Stories."

Dale stepped into the van. The door slammed shut. The locks engaged with a heavy *thunk*.

The van pulled away. Dale sat in the chair. He opened the briefcase.

Inside was a new Field Guide. Leather-bound. Heavy. The pages were blank.

And on the front cover, in gold leaf that seemed to shift under the dim light, were the words:

**CLASS V: NARRATIVE PARASITES.**

Dale looked out the window. The suburban houses were blurring past. The sun was rising. The day was starting.

But the world felt different. Thinner.

He looked at his hands. They were trembling. Not from fear.

From the emptiness in his head. The missing memories.

He had traded pieces of himself for a job.

And the Authority wasn't paying him in points anymore. They were paying him in *truth*.

What was in the library?

What kind of monster ate stories?

Dale closed his eyes. He tried to remember the plot of the last book he'd read.

He couldn't.

The van sped up. The road stretched out ahead, straight and endless.

Dale gripped the briefcase. He didn't let go.

He was going to open it, and he was going to learn what he had lost.

And he was going to exterminate it.
