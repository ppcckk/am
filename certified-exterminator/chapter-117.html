Chapter 117: Workspace Compromised

# Chapter 117

Dale dropped the burning clipboard onto the floor mat and stomped the accelerator. Not with actual flame. With black smoke that smelled like ozone and rotting kelp. The smoke curled around the hand, thick and viscous, coating the plastic surface in a layer of tar-like residue.

Dale slammed the van into reverse. Tires shrieked on the wet asphalt, spinning uselessly against the slick surface of the parking lot.

The rearview mirror showed the street empty.

The street behind him empty. The sky above him empty of everything but that descending limb.

It wasn't reaching anymore. It was waiting.

The System screen flickered.

Red text scrolled across the dash, too fast to read. Dale ignored it.

He looked at his own hands, and they were shaking. He pressed them flat against the steering wheel, forcing the tremor to stop. Muscle memory.

Control. Protocol.

*Do not engage unprepared.*

The thought was automatic. It had kept him alive for thirty-eight years, through three divorces, two layoffs, and the first time a Class IV acid beetle had melted through his boot.

But protocol required a workspace, and he needed to isolate the threat.

Dale popped the gear shifter into park.

The engine growled, a low, idle rumble that vibrated through the floorboards.

He grabbed the emergency brake. His fingers brushed the leather sleeve of his work jacket.

He didn't hesitate. He ripped the jacket off, tossing it onto the passenger seat.

Underneath, he wore the standard-issue Authority uniform. Grey, fire-retardant, covered in pockets.

He reached into the left breast pocket.

Empty, and he checked the right. A pen. A half-used roll of duct tape. A granular bait packet for German cockroaches.

Not enough.

The hand in the rearview mirror twitched. The smoke thickened, extending tendrils toward the van's rear window.

*Alert: High-Value Target Detected.*

*Termination Imminent.*

Dale looked at the bait packet. He looked at the duct tape. He looked at the mirror.

"Insufficient resources," he muttered.

The words weren't panic. They were a status report.

He threw the van into drive again. Not to flee. To move.

The vehicle lurched forward, tires gripping the wet pavement. Dale swung the wheel hard to the right, aiming for the loading dock of the municipal waste facility. The doors were closed, but the sensor panel glowed green.

*Access Denied. Clearance Level: IV.*

Dale had a Level VII clearance. But the van didn't. The van was a Class I transport unit. It didn't know who he was.

The system screen flashed a new error.

*Unauthorized Access Attempt. Vehicle Lockdown Initiated.*

The steering wheel locked. The accelerator pedal went dead.

Dale watched the RPMs drop. The van slowed to a crawl.

The hand in the mirror was closer now.

It filled the entire glass. The fingers were long, articulated with joints that bent in directions human anatomy didn't allow.

The clipboard in its grip was burning.

Dale unbuckled his seatbelt, and he didn't look at the mirror anymore. He looked at the dashboard. Specifically, at the small, circular vent near the radio display.

It was a ventilation port for the cabin air recirculation system.

Standard municipal van.

Standard filter.

He reached under the dashboard, feeling for the release latch. His fingers found the plastic tab. He pulled.

The filter housing popped open.

Inside sat a rectangular mesh screen. Clogged with dust, pollen, and something that looked like dried blood.

Dale reached into his pocket and pulled out the granular bait packet, which was for German cockroaches. *Blattella germanica.*

The entity outside was not a cockroach.

Using cockroach bait against a dimensional hand would trigger a Cross-Contamination penalty. A 40% debuff to all stats. A 24-hour corruption of his Field Guide entries.

It was a terrible idea, which was also the only tool he had.

Dale ripped the packet open. The powder spilled out, grey and fine. He poured it directly into the air intake vent.

Then, he slammed the vent closed, and he hit the button for the cabin recirculation mode.

The fan whirred, louder than before.

Dale watched the rearview mirror.

The smoke from the hand was thick. It was pressing against the glass, seeking entry. Seeking him.

The ventilation system sucked air in from the outside.

Through the intake vents.

Past the filter.

Past the clogging dust.

Into the cabin.

And through the fresh pile of grey powder Dale had dumped into the housing.

The fan pushed the air out of the vents into the driver's seat.

Dale held his breath.

The powder didn't disperse into the air. It reacted.

The chemical compound in the bait, designed to disrupt the nervous systems of small, rapid-replicating insects, interacted with the metaphysical residue of the dimensional entity.

The smoke in the cabin turned yellow.

It hissed.

Dale coughed, a wet, violent hack that bent him over. He tasted copper and ash.

But the smoke didn't burn him.

It recoiled.

The tendrils pressing against the rear window dissolved. The hand in the mirror shuddered. The black smoke surrounding it fractured, breaking apart into tiny, distinct particles that swirled in the airflow.

The System screen changed.

*Contamination Detected.*

*Cross-Contamination Penalty Applied: -40% Efficiency.*

*Status: Target Neutralized (Local).*

*Note: This action constitutes a Class 3 Regulatory Violation.*

*Fine: 500 Credits.*

Dale spat blood into his hand. He wiped it on his pants.

He unbuckled. He opened the door.

Rain hit his face. Cold, hard, real.

He stepped out. The van was still idling, though the engine sounded rougher now. The dashboard lights flickered in a rhythm that felt like a heartbeat.

The street was silent, and the clouds above had stopped spiraling. They hung low, grey and heavy, blocking out the few stars that remained.

Dale walked to the back of the van. He didn't look up. He looked at the ground.

There were no footprints. No scorch marks. Just the wet pavement reflecting the streetlights.

He pulled out his clipboard. It was still there, under the passenger seat. He had grabbed it instinctively when he exited the vehicle.

He clicked his pen.

*Incident Report: 10/14/2042.*

*Location: Municipal Waste Transfer Station, Sector 4.*

*Subject: Unregistered Dimensional Manifestation (Type: Unclassified).*

*Action Taken: Chemical mitigation via off-label use of Class 1 insecticide.*

*Result: Manifestation dispersed. No physical evidence remaining.*

*Contamination Warning: Field Guide Entry 'Rattus profundus' corrupted for 24 hours.*

He paused. The pen hovered over the paper.

He looked up at the sky, and the Rift was gone. The purple light had vanished.

But the feeling remained. The pressure in the air. The static on his skin.

Miller was gone. The other agent was gone.

Dale was alone, and he walked back to the driver's side. He didn't get in. He stood by the open door, staring at the dashboard.

A new notification pulsed on the screen.

*Violation Fine Paid.*

*Penalty Applied: License Suspension (Temporary).*

*Reason: Repeated Use of Incorrect Protocols.*

*Duration: 72 Hours.*

Dale read it twice.

Three days.

Three days without access to advanced protocols. Three days without the targeted damage bonuses that came with a clean Field Guide. Three days without the ability to properly isolate threats.

It was a handicap. A massive one.

But it was also a shield.

The Authority didn't punish incompetence. It punished deviation. By using the wrong tool, Dale had flagged himself as a liability. A risk.

He looked at the empty street.

Miller had been watching the sky. Not Dale.

Miller knew what was coming. Or maybe, Miller had been feeding it to him.

The hand had pointed at Dale.

*Reason: Knowledge of the Deep Breach.*

Dale's jaw tightened, and he had no knowledge of the Deep Breach. He knew about termites.

He knew about bed bugs.

He knew that if you didn't treat the nest, the problem came back in six weeks.

Someone wanted him dead.

Or someone wanted him to act, and he climbed into the van. The seat was cold. The smell of ozone and burnt sugar was still thick in the air.

He started the engine. It coughed, then roared.

He drove.

He didn't know where he was going. Disposal Site 7 was the destination the system had provided, but the system was compromised. The vehicle's GPS was glitching, the map showing purple static where roads should be.

Dale drove by landmarks. The gas station. The pharmacy. The cracked sidewalk in front of the library.

He drove until the van ran out of fuel.

It stopped in the middle of a residential neighborhood. Silent. Dark.

Dale checked the gauge. Empty.

He checked the map. No signal.

He got out, and the neighborhood was dead. Not destroyed. Empty.

Windows were open. Porch lights were on. But no cars. No people.

A child's bicycle lay on its side in the driveway of a white colonial-style house.

Dale walked toward it, and he picked up the bike. The chain was broken.

He looked at the house. The front door was unlocked.

He pushed it open.

The inside was pristine. Vacuumed carpet. Fresh flowers on the table.

And on the counter, a single item.

A clipboard.

Not his, which was black. Heavy. Made of a material that looked like obsidian but felt warm to the touch.

On it, a single page.

Written in handwriting that matched Miller's.

*Report: Subject Dale Kowalski.*

*Status: Non-Compliant.*

*Recommendation: Immediate Extermination.*

*Authorized by: Licensing Authority, Sector 9.*

Dale stared at the word *Extermination*.

It wasn't a request. It wasn't a warning.

It was a job order.

Someone had hired a professional. Someone with high-level clearance.

He heard a sound behind him.

The front door closing.

Dale spun around, and the door was locked.

The windows slammed shut.

The house was sealed.

From the shadows of the living room, a figure stepped forward.

It wore the grey uniform of the Authority. But the insignia on the chest was inverted.

The figure held a clipboard of its own.

It wasn't a monster. It wasn't a demon.

It was a person.

A woman. Mid-40s. Hair pulled back in a severe bun. Glasses that reflected the light of the single lamp in the room.

She looked at Dale. Her expression was bored.

"Mr. Kowalski," she said. Her voice was flat, professional. "You're late for your inspection."

Dale didn't move. He watched her hands. She held the black clipboard with one hand. The other hand rested on her hip.

She wasn't holding a weapon, and she was holding a pen.

"I didn't sign up for this," Dale said.

"You didn't have to," she replied. "You accepted the license. You accepted the job. You just forgot that the Authority doesn't just exterminate pests."

She tapped the pen against the clipboard.

"It exterminates liabilities."

The floor beneath Dale's feet vibrated.

Not the ground. The house itself.

The walls were breathing, and the wallpaper was rippling.

Dale looked at the Field Guide icon in his peripheral vision. It was greyed out. Corrupted.

He had no data on this woman. No data on this house.

He was blind.

The woman smiled. It was a small, thin movement.

"Protocol 9," she said. "Isolation of the Technician."

Dale stepped back. His hand went to his pocket. Empty.

He looked at the black clipboard in her hand, and he looked at the windows. Sealed.

He looked at the door. Locked.

He looked at the vibrating floor.

The house wasn't a house, which was a container.

A nest.

And she was the broodmother.

Dale's hand twitched. He reached for the flashlight in his belt.

The woman raised her pen.

"Don't bother," she said. "Your workspace is compromised."

The floor opened up.
