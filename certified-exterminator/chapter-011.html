Chapter 11

The thing took another step. Its feet made no sound on the linoleum. That was wrong. Dale noted it. The absence of sound meant the creature was either very light or very deliberate. Both possibilities were bad.

He stepped back. His boot heel caught the edge of the hallway runner and he nearly went down. He didn't. He corrected, kept his clipboard up, kept the canister low. Not a weapon. A tool. The distinction mattered to the system even if it didn't matter to the thing smiling at him.

IDENTIFY. The first step in any protocol. No authorization required.

Dale's eyes moved the way they always moved. Fast, systematic, looking for the tells. The creature was bipedal, roughly human height, but the proportions were off. Arms too long. Shoulders too narrow for the torso. The face was the worst part. Not a face. A surface. Smooth and featureless except for the mouth, which was wrong because it was too wide and the smile didn't correspond to any facial muscle arrangement Dale had ever seen on a human skull.

He pulled the pen from behind his ear. His hand was shaking. He made it write anyway.

"Face: smooth, non-dermal texture. No pores, no follicles, no expression lines. Mouth: too wide. Likely a structural mimicry rather than a biological face. Bipedal. Arms disproportionate. No vocalization observed. No sound on footfall."

The Field Guide flickered. A new entry forming. Partial. Incomplete. But there.

FIELD GUIDE ENTRY IN PROGRESS: UNKNOWN SPECIES (COGNITIVE OVERRIDE EVENT, INTELLIGENCE CLASS 3). IDENTIFICATION: 40% COMPLETE. WEAKNESS ANALYSIS: PENDING.

Forty percent. Not enough for a damage bonus. Not enough for anything except the knowledge that he was looking at something that shouldn't have a face.

The thing tilted its head. The smile stayed fixed. It was studying him the way Dale studied a nest site. Assessing. Deciding whether the structure was worth exploiting.

He backed up another step. The hallway ended at the kitchen. Behind him, the duplex kitchen with its code violation and its German cockroach trail. He could feel the linoleum under his boots, could smell the spray residue from his own canister. Familiar ground.

"Alright," he said. Quiet. Professional. Like talking to a homeowner who wanted to argue about a moisture reading. "I can't treat you. I can't even touch you. But I can write you up."

He turned to the door frame. The one the thing had opened. The one whose latch had been overridden by a cognitive event that wasn't physical.

He ran his fingers along the frame. Wood, standard construction-grade pine. No damage visible. The latch mechanism was intact. The strike plate was fine. The door itself was fine.

The problem wasn't the door. The problem was the wall around it.

Dale pressed his palm flat against the drywall beside the frame. It was warm. Not warm like a pipe running through it. Warm like something was generating heat inside the cavity.

He pulled his pen again.

"Violation type: dimensional structural compromise. The entry point is not the door. The entry point is the wall cavity itself. The door is a secondary access point. Primary breach is internal, within the wall assembly, likely at the junction of the framing and the sheathing. The structure has been compromised from the inside."

The notification came fast.

VIOLATION FILED: DIMENSIONAL STRUCTURAL COMPROMISE. CATEGORY: NEW. AUTHORITY RECOGNITION: CONFIRMED. TREATMENT AUTHORIZATION: N/A (STRUCTURAL VIOLATION, NOT ORGANISMAL). CONTAINMENT PROTOCOL AVAILABLE: STRUCTURAL HOLD (RESTRICTED).

Dale read it twice. Structural hold. Not a creature protocol. A building protocol. The Authority was treating the wall like a cracked foundation.

He had used structural holds before. They were for termite damage, for water intrusion, for anything that compromised a building's integrity. You filed the violation, the Authority imposed a hold on the structure, and the compromised area became inert. Not repaired. Just... paused.

He didn't know if it would work on a wall that had a creature living in it.

He filed the containment request anyway.

STRUCTURAL HOLD: REQUESTED. AUTHORIZATION: GRANTED (TEMPORARY, 10 MINUTES). NOTE: HOLD WILL SUPPRESS ALL NON-PHYSICAL MANIPULATION WITHIN DESIGNATED STRUCTURAL ENVELOPE.

The wall went cold. Dale felt it through his palm. The warmth bled out of the drywall in seconds, replaced by something flat and neutral.

Behind him, the thing stopped smiling.

Its mouth closed. Not a smile fading. A mouth closing. Like a drawer shutting.

Dale turned. The creature was standing in the same spot. Its face was still wrong, still smooth, but the expression was gone. The intelligence was still there. He could see it in the way it held its arms. Deliberate. Controlled. But the cognitive override was suppressed. It couldn't reach into his head anymore.

It looked at the door. It looked at him. It stepped back toward the wall.

Not away. Toward.

It pressed its hand flat against the drywall. The surface didn't move. The structural hold was active. The wall was inert.

The thing pushed. Nothing happened. It pushed again. Nothing.

Then it turned back to Dale and did something worse than smile. It pointed at him. One long finger, aimed directly at his chest.

A notification appeared.

SUBJECT ATTEMPTING COGNITIVE OVERRIDE: SUPPRESSED BY STRUCTURAL HOLD. ATTEMPT LOGGED. SUBJECT AWARENESS OF SYSTEM MECHANICS: CONFIRMED. SUBJECT IS AWARE OF YOUR TOOLS.

Dale stared at that line. The thing knew what he was doing. It understood the protocol. It was adapting to it.

The structural hold had ten minutes. He had forty percent of a Field Guide entry. He had no treatment authorization. And the thing in front of him was smart enough to know that his containment was temporary.

He started writing. Fast. The pen moved like it always did when he was documenting. Not elegant. Legible.

"Bipedal. Bimanual. Intelligence Class 3. Capable of non-physical manipulation (cognitive override). Capable of system awareness. Face is non-dermal, likely a structural mimicry. No sound on footfall suggests either weight distribution anomaly or active dampening. Pointing gesture indicates intent communication. Possibly territorial. Possibly assessing."

The Field Guide updated.

FIELD GUIDE ENTRY: 65% COMPLETE. WEAKNESS ANALYSIS: PARTIAL. IDENTIFIED VULNERABILITY: FACE IS A STRUCTURAL MIMICRY. LIKELY VULNERABLE TO PHYSICAL DISRUPTION OF SURFACE LAYER. DAMAGE BONUS: +15% (PARTIAL).

Fifteen percent. Useless if he couldn't touch it. But it was something.

The thing tilted its head again. It was watching him write. It knew he was documenting. It knew what documentation meant.

Then it walked toward the kitchen. Not toward Dale. Toward the kitchen. Toward the wall where Dale had noted the German cockroach trail.

Dale's stomach dropped.

The cockroach trail. The entry point. The kitchen infestation that had been there before the Rift opened. The thing was looking at the entry point. The one Dale had already documented. The one that was already in his Field Guide.

If it used that entry point, it wasn't using the wall cavity. It was using a different breach. One that wasn't under the structural hold.

"Hey," Dale said. "Hey. That's a separate violation. You can't use that."

The thing looked at him. It didn't stop walking.

Dale ran. Not away. Around. He cut through the living room, grabbed the roll of duct tape from his belt, and hit the kitchen doorway. The thing was already at the kitchen wall, its long fingers working at the baseboard where the cockroach trail started.

He slapped duct tape across the baseboard gap. Not enough. Not a real seal. But it was a physical barrier.

The thing's fingers found the tape. It peeled it off. Slowly. Like it was taking its time. Like it wanted him to see.

Then it pushed the baseboard in. The wall behind it was dark. Warm. The same warmth as the hallway wall.

A new notification.

CONTAINMENT BREACH: SECONDARY ENTRY POINT ACTIVE. STRUCTURAL HOLD: INSUFFICIENT COVERAGE. NEW VIOLATION REQUIRED.

Dale was already writing.

"Secondary entry point: kitchen baseboard, west wall. Pre-existing structural compromise (German cockroach entry point, code violation K-7). Dimensional breach has exploited pre-existing weakness. Violation type: dimensional exploitation of pre-existing structural deficiency."

The Authority responded.

VIOLATION FILED: DIMENSIONAL EXPLOITATION OF PRE-EXISTING DEFICIENCY. CATEGORY: NEW. AUTHORITY RECOGNITION: CONFIRMED. NOTE: THIS VIOLATION TYPE IMPLIES THAT ALL PRE-EXISTING STRUCTURAL DEFICIENCIES IN YOUR SERVICE AREA ARE POTENTIAL ENTRY POINTS. INSPECTION REQUIRED: ALL STRUCTURES.

Dale read that line and felt something cold move through his chest. All structures. Every building in his service area. Every crack, every gap, every code violation he'd ever written up. They were all potential doors.

The thing was through the baseboard. Half of it was in the wall cavity. The other half was still in the kitchen, its face inches from Dale's clipboard.

It was close enough that he could see the surface of its face up close. Not smooth. Textured. Like skin that had been stretched over something with too many edges.

He could hit it. He could put his fist through that face and maybe the damage bonus would be enough to hurt it. But he couldn't treat it. He couldn't earn a point. And if he hit it and missed, it would be in the wall with him.

He stepped back.

The thing pulled itself through the baseboard and was gone. Into the wall. Into the cavity between the kitchen and the hallway. Into the spaces between the studs where no protocol reached.

Dale stood in the kitchen. The baseboard was pushed in. The wall behind it was dark.

He checked his watch. Four minutes left on the structural hold. After that, the hallway wall would be active again. The thing could come back through either entry point.

He needed help. He needed a Level III. He needed someone who could treat it.

He pulled out his phone. The Authority's interface was still active. He navigated to the dispatch screen. His hands were steady now. The shaking had stopped somewhere in the writing.

DISPATCH REQUEST: LEVEL III+ TECHNICIAN REQUIRED. LOCATION: 4412 MAPLE CREST DRIVE. INFESTATION: INTELLIGENCE CLASS 3, UNKNOWN SPECIES. CURRENT STATUS: UNCONTAINED, STRUCTURAL HOLD ACTIVE (4 MINUTES REMAINING).

The system processed.

DISPATCH: NO AVAILABLE TECHNICIANS WITHIN 50 MILES. NEAREST AVAILABLE: LEVEL IV, ETA 11 MINUTES.

Eleven minutes. He had four.

He filed another violation. The kitchen. The baseboard. He documented it fully, photographed it with his phone, noted the pre-existing deficiency, noted the dimensional exploitation. Full protocol. Identify, document, isolate. He couldn't eliminate or verify, but he could complete the first three steps.

AUTHORITY NOTIFICATION: JOB PROGRESS RECORDED. STEPS COMPLETED: 3/5. STEPS REMAINING: ELIMINATE, VERIFY. NOTE: JOB REMAINS OPEN. RESPONSIBILITY ASSIGNED: DALE KOWALSKI.

Responsibility assigned. His job. His problem. His to finish.

The structural hold expired. He felt it go. The warmth came back in the hallway wall. Faint, but there.

He backed out of the kitchen. Went to the front door. Opened it. The neighborhood was still there. Still quiet. A few people had gathered at the end of the street, looking at the house. They saw him come out. They saw his clipboard. They saw the duct tape on his belt.

One of them shouted something. He didn't hear it.

He was watching the windows. Every window on the house. Every crack. Every gap.

His phone buzzed.

DISPATCH UPDATE: TECHNICIAN ARRIVED. LEVEL IV. NAME: R. VASQUEZ. STATUS: ON SITE. TREATMENT INITIATED.

Dale looked up. A truck was pulling into the driveway. Unmarked. Government plates. A woman got out. She was maybe forty, wearing a field jacket with four chevrons on the sleeve. Level IV. She had a canister on her back that was twice the size of his.

She looked at him. She looked at the house. She looked at the clipboard.

"You're the one who filed the violation?" she said.

"Yeah."

"Structural hold?"

"Two of them. Both expired."

She nodded. She didn't seem impressed. She seemed impatient.

"I'm going in. You stay out. You're not cleared for this."

"The job's assigned to me."

She looked at him again. Something flickered across her face. Not contempt. Worse. Recognition. The look of someone who's seen a Level II technician die because they thought their documentation mattered.

"Job's reassigned," she said. "I'm taking it."

She went inside.

Dale stood in the driveway. He watched her go through the front door. He heard the canister activate. A sound like a pressure washer aimed at the inside of a wall.

Then he heard the thing scream. Not a vocalization. A sound like the walls themselves were being torn.

A notification appeared.

TREATMENT INITIATED BY: R. VASQUEZ (LEVEL IV). PROTOCOL: MAMMALIAN NEURAL DISRUPTION (CLASS III). TARGET: UNKNOWN SPECIES (INTELLIGENCE CLASS 3).

Dale read that line. Mammalian neural disruption. She was treating it like a mammal. The thing wasn't a mammal. He'd documented that. He'd written it down.

CROSS-CONTAMINATION EVENT DETECTED. PROTOCOL MISMATCH: MAMMALIAN PROTOCOL APPLIED TO NON-MAMMALIAN TARGET. DEBUFF APPLIED TO TARGET: -40% EFFECTIVENESS. FIELD GUIDE CONTAMINATION: ALL ENTRIES RELATED TO UNKNOWN SPECIES (INTELLIGENCE CLASS 3) LOCKED FOR 24 HOURS.

His Field Guide. Locked. All of it. The forty percent entry. The sixty-five percent entry. The weakness analysis. Gone. Twenty-four hours.

And the debuff was on the creature. Forty percent less effective against it. She was making it harder to kill with every spray.

The screaming stopped. The canister stopped. Silence.

Then the front door opened. Vasquez walked out. She had something on her jacket. Black fluid. It was moving.

She looked at Dale.

"It's dead," she said.

Dale looked at the house. The windows. The walls.

"You used a mammalian protocol," he said.

"It worked."

"It's not a mammal."

She wiped the black fluid off her jacket with her thumb. She looked at the fluid. It moved against her skin like something alive.

She looked at Dale.

"Did you file the job report?" she asked.

"Three steps. Identify, document, isolate. I didn't eliminate or verify."

"Then it's not your problem anymore." She walked to her truck. "It's mine. And I'm filing it as complete."

Dale watched her get in the truck. He watched her drive away.

His phone buzzed.

AUTHORITY NOTIFICATION: JOB 4412-MAPLE-CREST CLOSED. THOROUGHNESS RATING: 6.1. POINTS AWARDED: 0 (REASSIGNED). NOTE: SECONDARY INFESTATION DETECTED. SOURCE: TREATMENT RESIDUE. CATEGORY: CLASS V. RESPONSIBLE PARTY: R. VASQUEZ.

Class V. The residue from her treatment had created something new. Something worse.

Dale looked at the black fluid stain on his driveway. It was spreading. Moving against the grain of the concrete. Crawling toward the foundation.

His phone buzzed again.

AUTHORITY NOTIFICATION: SECONDARY INFESTATION LOCATION: 4412 MAPLE CREST DRIVE. CURRENT STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY: COMPROMISED. RESPONSIBLE PARTY (PRIMARY JOB): DALE KOWALSKI. NOTE: SECONDARY INFESTATION IS A CONSEQUENCE OF TREATMENT APPLIED TO YOUR DOCUMENTED AREA. INSPECTION AND CONTAINMENT RESPONSIBILITY REMAINS WITH YOU.

He was still responsible. The job was reassigned, the kill was hers, but the area was his. The documentation was his. The entry point was his. And now there was a Class V infestation crawling into the foundation of the house he'd been hired to inspect.

He looked at the black stain. It was at the foundation line. It was going under the house.

He looked at his clipboard. He looked at his pen.

He started walking toward the stain.
