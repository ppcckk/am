Chapter 131: Scrubbing the Stain

# Chapter 131

The impact didn't knock him down. It pinned him.

Dale felt the weight of the shifting mass press against his chest, a wet, chitinous heaviness that smelled of old copper and stagnant water. The broken pen in his hand had snapped entirely, the nib digging into the clipboard's surface, but the paper held. *Violation*. The word sat there, stark and black, the only defined thing in a world that had turned to gray static.

His vision blurred. Not from injury, but from the sensory overload.

Without the Field Guide to filter the data, the monster wasn't a collection of stats or weak points, which was just noise. A screaming, undifferentiated blob of teeth and limbs that defied Euclidean geometry.

"Get off him!" Marcus screamed.

A pipe wrench swung into the void. It connected with something solid, producing a sound like a wet sack of gravel hitting pavement. The mass recoiled slightly, one of its many limbs jerking back, dripping a viscous, black fluid that sizzled where it touched the asphalt.

Dale blinked. The pain was sharp, localized in his left ribcage, but his mind was elsewhere. His mind was on the protocol.

*Identify.

Document. Isolate.

Eliminate.

Verify.*

Step one was impossible. Step two was the only thing keeping him alive.

"Marcus," Dale wheezed. The air tasted metallic. "Stop swinging. You're contaminating the sample."

"What?" Marcus yelled, his voice cracking. He stood over Dale, wrench raised, eyes wide with terror and adrenaline. "I'm trying to save your life!"

"You're creating debris," Dale said. He pushed the pen harder against the paper.

The ink was drying too fast, and he needed to write more.

He needed to describe the thing that was currently trying to digest his lungs.

"If you hit it in a way that isn't in the guide, the cross-contamination penalty kicks in.

It's already 40 percent. If we mess up the documentation, we lose the whole job.

"

"The job?" Marcus stared at him. "Dale, there's a demon eating you."

"It's an infestation," Dale corrected. He coughed, spitting blood onto his shirt. "And I'm filing the initial complaint."

The mass shifted. A cluster of eyes, small and black and multifaceted, rotated to focus on Dale.

They didn't blink, and they just watched. A limb, thick as a thigh but segmented like a centipede's, curled around Dale's waist. It tightened.

Ribs groaned.

"Isolate," Dale whispered.

He reached into his pocket. His fingers brushed the cold metal of his multi-tool, then the crumpled receipt for his lunch, and finally, the small bottle of industrial disinfectant he kept for emergencies. It was mostly empty. Maybe three ounces left.

"Marcus, the drain," Dale said. "The storm drain to our left. Is it open?"

Marcus looked back. The manhole cover had been ripped off, twisted metal hanging by one hinge.

"Yes. Why?"

"Kick it. Hard. Toward us."

"Are you insane?"

"Do it. Protocol requires containment before extraction. If we can't kill it, we have to trap it in a localized hazard zone."

Marcus hesitated, looking at the writhing horror. Then, with a grunt of exertion, he kicked the twisted metal cover. It clattered across the asphalt, landing with a clang three feet from Dale's boots.

The mass hesitated. It seemed confused by the sudden change in terrain.

Dale uncapped the disinfectant. He didn't spray the monster. He sprayed the clipboard.

He sprayed the pen. He sprayed his hand.

"Subject exhibits high resistance to standard chemical agents," Dale wrote. The ink smeared slightly, but the words remained legible. "Susceptibility unknown. Requires environmental containment."

The Authority notification flashed in the corner of his vision, dim and glitchy. *ENTRY INCOMPLETE. THOROUGHNESS RATING: 2.1.*

"Two point one?" Dale muttered. "That's not enough for a Class II hazard."

"Help me!" Dale shouted. "Push it toward the drain!"

Marcus grabbed Dale's belt.

"I can't lift it!"

"Don't lift it.

Nudge it. Use the wrench as a lever.

Jam it under the main body.

The big lump in the center. That's the thorax. Probably.

"

"How do you know?"

"Because it's where the weight is. Physics doesn't lie. Even eldritch physics."

Marcus jammed the wrench under the pulsating mass. He heaved. The creature shrieked, a sound that wasn't sound but a pressure wave that popped Dale's ears. The mass slid two inches to the left.

Closer to the open manhole.

"Again!" Dale commanded. He was trembling, not from fear, but from the effort of holding his body rigid enough to continue writing. The pen was slipping. He had to keep the tip pressed against the paper.

*Secondary characteristic: Exoskeletal plating. High density. Low thermal conductivity.*

The entry was filling up. He was guessing, mostly.

He was using the universal rules of biology.

Chitin was chitin.

Carbon was carbon. If it had armor, it had joints.

If it had joints, it had vulnerabilities.

The Authority's algorithm, even corrupted, could recognize patterns. It didn't need the name. It needed the *shape*.

A new line appeared in his peripheral vision, faint and stuttering. *PATTERN MATCH: ARTHROPOD-BASED. PROTOCOAL: EXOSKELETAL FRACTURE.*

"It's an arthropod," Dale said aloud. "It's big, but it's an arthopod."

Marcus looked at him, sweat dripping down his nose.

"So what do we do?"

"Break the joints," Dale said. "Kick it in the knees."

"It doesn't have knees!"

"It has articulation points! Look at where the legs meet the body.

That's the weakest link.

Every exoskeleton has to flex. Use that.

"

Marcus looked at the writhing limb attached to Dale's waist. It was segmented. There were gaps between the plates. Dark, soft tissue exposed between the hard shells.

Marcus swung the wrench. Not at the center. At the joint.

The impact was clean. The wrench bit into the soft tissue. Black ichor sprayed. The limb convulsed.

Dale felt the pressure on his ribs ease slightly. The mass shifted, trying to compensate.

"More," Dale said.

He was writing faster now. His hand was shaking, but the words were flowing. *Articulation points located.

Chemical vulnerability: Acidic secretions.

Note: Secretions neutralize standard alkaline cleaners. Do not use bleach.*

*Violation: Improper disposal of hazardous biological waste.*

The Authority pinged louder. *THOROUGHNESS RATING INCREASING. 3.5.*

"Keep going," Dale said. "Identify the head. Where is the sensory cluster?"

"The eyes," Marcus said. "The bunch of them on the left."

"Wrong. Those are defensive.

Look for the larger, forward-facing optic.

Or the mouthparts.

It's trying to eat me. That means there's a feeding structure.

"

Dale looked. The mass had reoriented. A row of jagged, translucent teeth had emerged from a slit in the center of the mass. They were dripping saliva that steamed in the cold air.

"Mandibles," Dale said. "Frontal. Central."

Marcus adjusted his stance. He swung the wrench again, higher, aiming for the mouth.

The creature dodged. Not with reflex, but with intent. It knew where the threat was. It anticipated the swing.

"It's smart," Dale said.

"Since when do bugs think?"

"Since they're from the Rift. They're not just animals. They're engineered.

Or evolved. Either way, they adapt.

"

The mass lunged again. This time, it didn't go for Dale. It went for Marcus.

A limb shot out, faster than sight. It caught Marcus in the shoulder, sending him sprawling backward. He hit the pavement hard, the wrench clattering away.

"Marcus!" Dale yelled.

The creature loomed over Marcus. It raised a limb, tipped with a serrated blade of bone.

Dale's ribs screamed. He couldn't stand. He couldn't run. He was pinned, bleeding, and blind to the monster's true nature.

But he had the clipboard, and he had the protocol.

He had a rule that no one had used in this fight.

*Cross-contamination penalty.*

The creature was attacking Marcus. Marcus was not a pest control technician. He was a civilian. An unlicensed entity.

If the creature attacked an unlicensed entity, it was no longer an infestation. It was a hostile act. A breach of the municipal code.

Dale looked at his blank page. Then he looked at the broken pen.

He didn't try to write a new entry. He tried to amend the old one.

He crossed out *Violation*.

Below it, he wrote: *Complaint.*

Then, he wrote: *Unlicensed Aggression.*

The air around them shifted. The oily shadow seemed to pause. The Authority's presence grew heavier, more oppressive. The glitchy text in Dale's vision stabilized.

*SYSTEM ALERT: UNLICENSED ENTITY EXPOSURE DETECTED. IMMEDIATE SANCTION IMMINENT.*

The creature froze. It wasn't confused. It was being suppressed.

A force, invisible and absolute, pressed down on the mass.

It wasn't physical. It was bureaucratic. The Authority didn't like loose ends.

It didn't like unsanctioned violence in a service zone.

The creature shrieked, a high-pitched whine that shattered nearby car windows. It tried to retreat, pulling its limbs back into the mass.

"It's being regulated," Dale said. He coughed, wiping blood from his chin. "We're getting a fine."

Marcus scrambled to his feet, clutching his shoulder.

"What is that?"

"The Authority," Dale said. "It doesn't like freelancers.

You're not certified.

You're not on the payroll. By attacking you, it's violating the non-interference clause of the Pest Control Act.

"

The creature dissolved. Not into death, but into retreat. It sank into the shadows, melting away like oil in water, leaving only a stain on the asphalt and the smell of ozone.

Silence returned to the avenue.

Marcus slumped against a parked car, breathing hard.

"Did we win?"

Dale looked at his clipboard. The entry was incomplete.

The Thoroughness Rating hovered at 4.8.

Low.

Dangerously low.

"No," Dale said. He picked up the broken pen. The plastic shaft was cracked.

The nib was gone.

"We didn't win.

We just filed a report.

"

He looked at the stain on the ground. It was bubbling.

"Look at that," Marcus said. "It's... growing."

Dale stared. The black stain was spreading. It wasn't just oil.

It was ink.

Black, viscous ink, pooling on the asphalt. And it was forming letters.

Dale leaned forward, squinting. The Authority's system was trying to rewrite the Field Guide. It was trying to fill in the blanks using the data it had gathered from the combat.

But it was doing it wrong.

The letters scrambled. They formed a name. Not a species name. A name of a person.

*Kowalski.*

Then, beneath it, a number.

*Certification Level: Revoked.*

Dale felt a cold spike of adrenaline pierce through his pain.

"What does it say?" Marcus asked.

Dale didn't answer. He picked up the clipboard. His hands were shaking now, not from injury, but from realization.

He had written *Violation*. The Authority had accepted it. But by invoking the penalty for cross-contamination, he had triggered a secondary clause.

The clause that applied when a technician failed to maintain their own documentation integrity.

He had used a broken pen. He had used a corrupted guide. He had improvised.

And the Authority hated improvisation.

*THOROUGHNESS PENALTY: 20%.*

*STATUS: PROBATIONARY.*

*NEXT JOB: MANDATORY AUDIT.*

Dale looked at Marcus. Marcus looked at the stain.

"They know who we are," Marcus whispered.

"No," Dale said.

He pulled a fresh sheet of paper from his back pocket. It was blank. Pristine.

"They know what I am."

He looked at the fading stain, which was now just a puddle of black water.

"And they know I'm cutting corners."

A new notification pinged. Sharp. Clear. No glitch.

*NEW CONTRACT OFFERED: INSPECTOR CLASS I.*

Dale stared at the words.

"Marcus," he said.

"Yeah?"

"Put down the wrench."

"Why?"

"Because I have a new boss."

From the shadows of a nearby alley, a figure stepped out. It wasn't a monster. It wasn't a survivor.

It was a man in a suit. A gray suit. He held a clipboard that looked identical to Dale's, but the pen was gold.

"Mr. Kowalski," the man said. His voice was smooth, devoid of accent. "You've been flagged for review."

Dale straightened his spine, ignoring the scream of his ribs. He adjusted his tie, which was stained with blood and soot.

"I'm on a job," Dale said.

"You're on probation," the man corrected. "And you're late for your appointment."

He held out a card. It was black, with silver text.

*The Licensing Authority: Internal Affairs Division.*

"Who sent you?" Dale asked.

The man smiled. It wasn't a nice smile.

"Someone who noticed you didn't verify the kill," the man said. "You left a stain. Stains attract other pests."

Dale looked at the puddle. It was spreading faster now. Toward the man's feet.

The man didn't move. He just watched the black liquid climb his shoes.

"Clean it up," the man said. "Before I write you up for unsanitary conditions."

Dale looked at the broken pen. He looked at the blank page. He looked at the approaching shadows in the distance, where the air was rippling with oily darkness.

He had no Field Guide. He had no protection. He had a broken pen and a reputation for thoroughness that was currently being dismantled.

He picked up a handful of dirt, and he scrubbed the stain.

Three more cards slid under the door.
