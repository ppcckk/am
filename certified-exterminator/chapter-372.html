Chapter 372: Clean the Mess

# Chapter 372

The black leg twitched again.

It wasn't dead. It was merely compressed.

Dale Kowalski didn't step back. He stepped forward, his steel-toed boots crunching over the fresh debris. The air smelled of ozone and wet concrete, a cocktail of industrial disaster that usually signaled a code violation worth two hundred dollars in overtime.

Here, it signaled a Class-IV Structural Breach.

The red-stamped man stood in the shadows, arms crossed. He hadn't moved. He watched Dale with the detached curiosity of a health inspector finding a rat hole in a clean kitchen.

"Watch it?" Dale asked, his voice flat. "I'm a technician. I treat. I don't watch."

The man tilted his head.

"You are now a Witness, Kowalski.

The Authority has flagged this event as *Unresolved*.

You lack a verified kill. Therefore, you lack the right to engage further threats in this sector until the primary infestation is confirmed dead.

"

A new window hovered in Dale's peripheral vision. Translucent blue text.

*STATUS: RESTRICTED.* *ACTION PERMITTED: OBSERVATION ONLY.* *ACTION PROHIBITED: ELIMINATION.* *PENALTY FOR VIOLATION: REVOCATION OF LICENSE.*

Dale stared at the words. Revocation meant he was just a man with a clipboard and a bad knee. It meant he couldn't stop what was coming out of the wall.

"Show me the job order," Dale said.

The man snapped his fingers.

The rubble shifted.

The massive concrete slab that had crushed the creature groaned, then lifted.

Not because of strength, but because of leverage. The creature wasn't strong.

It was slippery.

It had coated itself in a viscous, acidic slime that acted as a hydraulic lubricant against the weight of the debris.

A leg shot out. Then two.

The thing unfolded itself from under the concrete like a spider escaping a jar. It was a *Rattus profundus*, but wrong.

Its fur was matted with gray dust.

Its eyes were gone, replaced by pulsating sacs of bioluminescent fluid, which had burrowed. It had used the environment to break free.

Dale's Field Guide updated automatically.

*SPECIES: Rattus profundus (Variant: Sludge-Burrower).* *WEAKNESS UNIDENTIFIED.* *NOTE: High environmental adaptability. Resistant to blunt force trauma.*

The creature shook itself. Debris fell from its flanks. It scuttled toward the hallway opening, where screams were beginning to rise from the other side of the wall.

Dale's hand went to his belt. He reached for his spray canister.

His fingers locked.

The system flashed red.

*ERROR: PROTOCOL LOCKED.*

The canister didn't open. The safety mechanism, which was usually manual, had become digital. It refused to actuate.

Dale tried to pull the pin. It wouldn't budge.

The Authority's restriction wasn't just a suggestion; it was a physical lock on his equipment.

"You can't stop it," the red-stamped man said. "You are a witness. Observe. Document."

Dale looked at the creature. It was moving fast. It was heading for the stairwell.

There were families living in the apartments above, and he knew the floor plans. He had inspected them three months ago.

He had noted the lack of fire doors.

He had noted the single exit point.

If that thing reached the stairs, it would have access to twelve units. Fourteen people.

"Document," Dale muttered.

He pulled out his clipboard.

The man smiled.

"Good.

Write it down.

Note the time of emergence.

Note the behavior.

"

Dale clicked his pen. The click was loud in the silence.

He wrote: *Subject exhibits high mobility. Bypassed structural containment via lubrication.*

He looked at the creature. It paused, sensing the movement. The bioluminescent sacs on its face blinked. It was hunting by sight.

Dale looked at the ceiling. The support beams were exposed. The fire suppression system, damaged in the initial collapse, was dripping a steady, rhythmic *drip-drip-drip* onto the floor near the creature.

He looked at the water. He looked at the creature.

He looked at the red-stamped man.

"You said I can't fight," Dale said.

"You can't eliminate," the man corrected. "You can't deploy combat protocols. You can't use weapons."

"I'm not using a weapon," Dale said. "I'm using infrastructure."

Dale walked toward the wall. Not toward the creature. Toward the exposed junction box.

The red-stamped man's smile faltered.

"Kowalski."

Dale ignored him. He opened his clipboard. He didn't write. He tore out a page.

He crumpled the paper into a tight ball, and he threw it.

The paper ball hit the creature's face.

It shrieked. A high, piercing sound that shattered the quiet.

The creature lunged. Not at Dale. At the paper.

Dale had anticipated this. *Rattus profundus* were scavengers. They reacted to movement. They reacted to noise.

But more importantly, they reacted to foreign objects that interfered with their sensory organs. The paper had blocked its sight.

The creature scrambled toward Dale, confused, blind for a split second.

Dale sidestepped, and he didn't attack. He reached past the creature and grabbed the exposed copper wire of the junction box.

It was live. The building's power had been cut, but the backup generators were kicking in. The system hummed.

Dale pulled the wire free. Sparks showered.

He didn't touch the creature. He didn't try to electrocute it. That would be combat. That would be a protocol violation.

Instead, he threw the live wire onto the wet floor.

The puddle from the fire suppression system expanded. The water pooled around the creature's legs.

The current hit the water.

The creature convulsed.

It didn't die. It spasmed. The electricity caused its muscles to lock, its legs to jerk violently.

It fell over, sliding in the slime.

*ACTION DETECTED: ENVIRONMENTAL MODIFICATION.* *STATUS: PERMITTED.* *NOTE: This is not elimination. This is hazard mitigation.*

The text flashed green.

Dale stood there, breathing hard. His knuckles were white. He hadn't killed it. He had just made it fall.

But in that moment of paralysis, the creature was vulnerable.

Dale kicked a piece of rebar from the rubble. It clattered across the floor.

He didn't pick it up. He didn't swing it.

He pointed at the ceiling above the creature.

"Look up," Dale said to the red-stamped man.

The man looked.

Above the creature, the fire suppression main line was hanging by a single bolt. It was stressed. It was leaking. And right above it, a chunk of drywall had shifted, blocking the sprinkler head.

Dale had noted this in his initial inspection. *Code Violation: Obstruction of emergency egress and fire suppression. Severity: Critical.*

If the sprinkler head wasn't obstructed, the pressure from the backup system would blow it out.

If it blew out, the pressure would shatter the drywall.

If the drywall shattered, the debris would fall on the creature, which was a chain reaction. A cascade failure.

Dale picked up the rebar. He didn't throw it. He used it to jam the gap between the drywall and the sprinkler head.

The pressure built up.

The red-stamped man's eyes widened.

"What are you doing?"

"Correcting a code violation," Dale said.

The drywall groaned.

The creature, still twitching from the electric shock, looked up. It saw the obstruction. It tried to bite the rebar.

The pressure exceeded the material limit.

The sprinkler head burst.

Water exploded outward. The drywall didn't just crack; it disintegrated. A sheet of gypsum and plaster crashed down, right on top of the creature.

It wasn't a beam. It wasn't a crushing blow. It was a containment breach.

The creature was buried again. But this time, it was buried in wet, heavy plaster.

Dale watched the dust settle, and he waited for the text.

*INFESTATION NEUTRALIZED?*

No.

*CREATURE BURIED. STATUS: UNKNOWN.*

Dale frowned. "I need a verification."

The red-stamped man stepped forward. He looked annoyed.

"You are playing games, Kowalski. The Authority does not reward improvisation."

"The Authority rewards thoroughness," Dale said. "I identified the obstruction. I removed the obstruction. The system corrected itself."

He walked over to the pile of wet plaster. He didn't use his hands. He used the tip of his pen.

He prodded the mound.

Nothing moved, and he prodded again.

A sound. Not a shriek. A gurgle.

The creature was alive. It was suffocating.

Dale looked at the water pooling on the floor. It was mixing with the plaster, creating a thick, gray sludge.

He looked at the red-stamped man.

"Where is the secondary nest?" Dale asked.

The man blinked.

"What?"

"The broodmother. You said something else was coming. You said it would eat everyone."

Dale pointed to the sludge.

"This thing is small. It's a scout. Where is the mother?"

The man's smile vanished. He looked at the ceiling. Then at the walls.

"The Rift," the man said. "It's not here. It's in the walls."

Dale's stomach turned. He knew that feeling. The contamination anxiety. The need to seal the perimeter.

"If the nest is in the walls," Dale said, "then this corridor is a conduit."

He looked at the other survivors. They were huddled in the shadows, terrified. They were waiting for a hero. They were waiting for someone with a sword.

Dale was waiting for a hose.

"Give me access to the building's water main," Dale said.

"You're restricted," the man said. "You can't deploy fluid protocols."

"I don't need to deploy a protocol," Dale said. "I need to open a valve."

The man stared at him.

"If I open the valve," Dale continued, "I flood the corridor. The water will mix with the plaster sludge.

It will create a quicksand effect, and the creature is slippery, yes. But it's also heavy. If it gets trapped in a wet, settling substrate, it can't use its leverage.

"

"That's not a kill," the man said.

"It's a containment," Dale said. "It buys time. And if it's trapped, I can identify the nest's location by tracing the burrow tunnels through the water flow."

The man's eyes narrowed. He was calculating the risk. The Authority didn't like unpredictable outcomes. But they liked results.

"You have ten seconds to decide," the man said.

Dale didn't decide. He ran.

He sprinted toward the utility closet at the end of the hall. His legs burned. The floor was slick.

Behind him, the pile of plaster shifted. A black leg punched through the gray mess. Then another.

The creature was rising.

Dale slammed his shoulder into the closet door. It was locked.

He pulled his keycard. He swiped.

*ACCESS DENIED.*

*LICENSE RESTRICTED.*

"Damn it," Dale muttered.

He turned to the red-stamped man.

"Bypass the lock."

"The Authority," the man said, "does not intervene in local infrastructure."

"Then I'm out of options," Dale said.

He looked at the creature. It was pulling itself free. It was angry. It was hungry.

He looked at the survivors. They were screaming now.

He looked at his clipboard, and he had one entry in his Field Guide. *Rattus profundus.*

He had no weapons. He had no powers. He had a pen and a clipboard.

And he had a loophole.

The System rules stated: *Identify, document, isolate, eliminate, verify.*

He had failed to eliminate. But he had isolated.

The rule also stated: *Cross-contamination penalty applies if a protocol is misapplied.*

Dale didn't have a protocol. He had a job.

He pulled out his phone. It was dead, cracked, useless for communication.

But it had a flashlight, and he turned on the flashlight.

He shined it directly into the creature's bioluminescent eyes.

The creature screamed.

It wasn't a physical attack. It was sensory overload. The creature relied on its own light to navigate in the dark, damp burrows. Blinding it was a disorientation tactic.

Dale ran, and he ran toward the elevator.

The doors were open. The shaft was dark.

He jumped in.

He hit the button for the basement.

The doors closed.

Inside the elevator, Dale leaned against the wall. His heart hammered against his ribs.

The red-stamped man appeared in the corner of the elevator. He was standing there, smiling again.

"You ran," the man said.

"I'm relocating the problem," Dale said.

"You're running away."

"I'm going to the water main," Dale said. "I'm going to flood the building."

The man's smile faded.

"You'll drown the residents."

"No," Dale said. "I'll flood the basement. The nest is in the foundation. If I flood the foundation, the broodmother will surface."

"And then you kill it?"

"And then I verify," Dale said.

The elevator dinged.

Basement.

The doors opened, and the basement was dark. Wet.

And it was crawling.

Hundreds of them. Black, slick shapes moving in the dark.

Dale stepped out, and he didn't hesitate. He pulled the fire alarm pull station on the wall.

The alarm blared. A deafening, rhythmic scream.

The creatures froze.

They hated the noise.

Dale walked to the center of the room. He opened his clipboard.

He wrote: *Infestation Level: Critical.*

He looked at the water main valve across the room, and he looked at the red-stamped man.

"Watch," Dale said.

He walked toward the valve.

The creatures moved. They were confused by the alarm, but they were hungry. They were closing in.

Dale reached the valve. He gripped the wheel.

It was rusted. Stuck.

He pulled. It didn't move.

He pulled harder. His muscles strained.

The creatures were ten feet away.

Five feet.

Dale gritted his teeth. He used his body weight. He leaned back.

The wheel turned.

A *crack* echoed through the room.

The valve opened.

Water roared out.

It wasn't a trickle. It was a torrent. The pressure from the municipal line was immense.

The water hit the floor. It hit the creatures.

They shrieked.

But the water didn't wash them away.

It raised the level, and the floor was already flooded from the fire suppression leak. The new water added to it.

The creatures weren't drowning. They were floating.

Dale watched, horrified.

He had made a mistake, and he had assumed they couldn't swim.

They could.

The water lifted them. It gave them mobility. It turned the basement into a pool.

And in the pool, they were faster.

Dale backed up.

The red-stamped man stepped closer.

"You've made it worse," the man said. "You've turned a burrow into a lake."

Dale looked at the water. It was rising.

He looked at the ceiling. There was only one way out. The elevator.

But the elevator was above him.

And the creatures were surrounding him.

Dale looked at the water main pipe. The pipe that was leaking.

He looked at the electrical panel on the wall, and he looked at the red-stamped man.

"If I short the panel," Dale said, "the water conducts."

The man's eyes widened.

"You'll electrocute the building. You'll electrocute yourself."

"I have rubber soles," Dale said.

He pointed to his boots, and he reached for the panel.

The creatures lunged.

Dale slammed his hand on the breaker.

The lights went out.

Darkness.

And then, a blue arc.

A spark jumped from the panel to the water.

The water glowed.

The creatures convulsed.

Their bodies lit up like neon signs, and they spasmed. They died.

Or so it seemed.

Dale stood in the dark, his hand on the breaker. His fingers tingled.

He waited for the silence.

But the silence didn't come.

From the water, a sound emerged. A low, rumbling vibration.

The water bubbled.

Not from heat.

From something rising.

Something huge.

The surface of the water broke.

A claw emerged. Massive. Covered in barnacles and rot.

Then a head.

It wasn't a rat, which was a crustacean. A *Chelicerata profundus*.

It was the size of a van.

Dale stared.

He looked at his Field Guide.

*SPECIES: UNIDENTIFIED.* *WARNING: CROSS-CONTAMINATION RISK.*

He had used a rodent protocol on a crustacean.

The text flashed red.

*PROTOCOL ERROR.* *LICENSE SUSPENDED.*

Dale couldn't move.

The creature looked at him.

It didn't scream.

It spoke.

In a voice that sounded like grinding stones.

*"You broke the seal, Technician."*

The creature raised a claw.

And pointed at the red-stamped man.

*"He opened the door. You just cleaned it up."*

Dale looked at the man.

The man wasn't smiling anymore, and he was holding a stamp.

A new stamp.

Black ink.

He stamped the air.

*EVENT: ESCALATION.* *NEXT JOB: CLEAN THE MESS.*

The creature lunged.

Dale had one second to run.
