Chapter 170: One Sheet Left

# Chapter 170

The lock on the library door was a Grade 4 deadbolt, designed for a municipal building that hadn't seen a break-in since 1998. Dale didn't touch it.

His fingers, still tingling with the residual static of the System's transfer, hovered over the handle.

He could feel the metal's temperature through the leather of his gloves. It was warm. Too warm.

Heat radiated from the building's core, humming against the silence of the suburban street.

The purple line in the sky pulsed in rhythm with that hum, a rhythmic throb that made Dale's teeth ache. He checked his watch. 11:42 PM.

The next Rift window opened at 02:00 AM.

He had less than two and a half hours to find a woman who, according to his own memory, did not exist.

He pushed the door. It swung open with a smoothness that suggested the tumblers had already yielded, as if the building itself was inviting him in.

Inside, the air smelled of old paper and ozone. Not the dusty rot of neglect, but the sharp, electric tang of ionized oxygen.

Dale stepped onto the linoleum, his boots making a wet sound that echoed too in the vestibule.

He didn't look around, and he didn't wonder if anyone was inside. He walked to the circulation desk, pulling the clipboard from under his arm.

The paper on the clipboard was blank. The pen in his hand was a standard-issue ballpoint, blue ink.

**[LOCATING SUBJECT: VALERIA THORNE.]** **[STATUS: OFF-GRID.]** **[METHOD: ARCHIVAL TRACE.]**

The text floated in his peripheral vision, translucent and cold. Dale ignored it.

He opened the top drawer of the desk.

Locked. He didn't pick it. He didn't force it.

He reached into his utility belt and pulled out a small, unassuming device: a thermal imaging lens he used for detecting heat signatures in wall voids.

He held it up to the lock cylinder, and the metal was vibrating. Not from heat, but from frequency.

A new notification blinked in his vision, red and urgent.

**[WARNING: LOCAL REALITY FLUCTUATION DETECTED.]** **[CAUTION: DO NOT INTERACT WITH ANCHOR POINTS WITHOUT PROTOCOL GAMMA-7.]**

Dale lowered the lens.

Protocol Gamma-7. He had never heard of it. He had never studied it.

But his hand moved anyway, reaching for the small pouch at his hip that contained his emergency sealant foam. He sprayed a thin line of the sticky, expanding polymer along the seam of the drawer.

It hardened instantly, sealing the drawer shut.

Then he kicked the desk.

Not the lock.

The leg of the desk.

He struck the wood with the side of his boot, a precise, calculated blow aimed at the joint where the leg met the apron. The desk groaned.

The structure shifted. The locked drawer, deprived of its support, dropped downward on its hinges.

The lock mechanism snapped under the weight of its own misalignment.

Dale pulled the drawer open.

Inside was not paper. It was a slab of black obsidian, etched with glowing geometric patterns that hurt to look at. The obsidian pulsed with the same rhythm as the sky outside.

Dale stared at it. He didn't pick it up.

He knew better.

Objects that defied local physics were not evidence; they were contamination vectors. If he touched it, the System would flag the evidence as corrupted, invalidating the entire case file. The Thoroughness Scale would drop to zero.

He would fail the certification task before he even began it.

Instead, he reached into his bag and pulled out a pair of tongs and a sterile specimen jar. He clamped the tongs around the obsidian slab's edge, careful not to touch the glowing lines, and lifted it out. He placed it into the jar, sealing the lid with a rubber gasket.

**[EVIDENCE SECURED.]** **[CONTAMINATION RISK: 0%.]** **[ARCHIVAL TRACE INITIATED.]**

The jar glowed. The patterns on the obsidian shifted, rotating to form a map. Not a map of Ohio, but a map of the library's basement archives. A red dot pulsed in the center.

Valeria Thorne was downstairs.

Dale moved toward the elevator, then stopped. The elevator shaft was a vertical conduit for the Rift energy pouring in from the sky.

The air in the shaft shimmered, distorting the view of the cables.

He shook his head. Elevators were unreliable during dimensional breaches. The structural integrity of the cables could not be trusted.

He took the stairs, and the staircase was narrow, smelling of mildew and something sweeter, like rotting fruit. Dale climbed, one hand on the railing, the other gripping his clipboard.

He counted the steps.

Forty-eight down to the first basement level.

Forty-eight more to the second. He knew the building's layout by heart.

He had inspected it for asbestos in 2009 and for rodent entry points in 2014. The architecture was consistent.

At the bottom of the second flight, the darkness was absolute.

Dale clicked on his headlamp. The beam cut through the dust motes, revealing rows of shelving that stretched into the gloom.

The shelves were filled not with books, but with jars.

Thousands of them. Each jar contained a single object: a tooth, a fingerbone, a shard of glass, a piece of cloth.

This was not an archive of knowledge. It was an archive of containment.

A sound came from the end of the aisle. A wet, scraping noise. Like sandpaper on bone.

Dale froze, and he did not raise his weapon. He did not have a weapon.

He had a clipboard, a pen, and a jar of obsidian.

He reached into his pocket and pulled out a small, folded sheet of paper. It was a pre-printed form: *Incident Report - Unlicensed Biological Entity*.

He clicked his pen.

"Identify yourself," Dale said. His voice was steady, professional. It was the voice he used when a landlord tried to dispute a termite bond.

The scraping stopped.

From the shadows stepped a figure. It was humanoid, but wrong.

Its joints bent at impossible angles.

Its skin was translucent, revealing the dark, viscous fluid pumping beneath, which had no face, only a smooth, concave surface where features should be.

**[SPECIES UNKNOWN.]** **[FIELD GUIDE ENTRY: BLANK.]** **[ACTION REQUIRED: IDENTIFY OR RETREAT.]**

The creature tilted its head. The concave face rippled. A voice emerged, not from a mouth, but from the vibration of the air itself.

"Thorne... is... busy."

Dale wrote on the clipboard. *Subject exhibits non-vocal communication via air displacement. Potential species: Rift Mimicry. Status: Aggressive/Protective.*

"I am here for Valeria Thorne," Dale said. "Please step aside."

The creature stepped forward. The floorboards creaked. Dale didn't back up.

He watched the creature's feet. The toes splayed wide, gripping the wood.

The heel lifted.

A shift in weight distribution. It was preparing to lunge.

Dale calculated the distance. Twelve feet. The creature's stride length was long, but its center of gravity was high. Unstable.

He didn't attack. He waited.

The creature lunged.

Dale sidestepped.

Not a dodge, a shift. He stepped to the left, into the space between two shelving units.

The creature crashed into the shelves, shattering jars.

Glass rained down. The viscous fluid from the broken jars hissed on the floor, eating into the concrete.

Dale looked at the mess. He looked at the creature, now tangled in metal and broken glass.

**[INFRACTION: PROPERTY DAMAGE.]** **[PENALTY: -50 TREATMENT POINTS.]** **[CURRENT SCORE: 8,450.]**

He grimaced. That was a high score.

He couldn't afford to lose more.

The task required a minimum of 9,000 points to avoid erasure, and he had been banking on perfect execution. Now he was bleeding points.

The creature shrieked, a sound like tearing metal. It pulled itself free, shards of glass embedded in its translucent flesh. It raised a limb, ending in a claw that sparked with black electricity.

Dale looked at the shelves. The jars were labeled. Tiny, handwritten tags. *Specimen 409: Dimensional Leech.* *Specimen 410: Void Tick.*

He grabbed a jar from the nearest shelf. It was labeled *Specimen 412: Nullifier Spore*.

He didn't know what it did. But the Field Guide rule applied: *Identifying a weakness in your guide before combat grants a targeted damage bonus against that species.*

He didn't have a guide entry for the creature. But he had a guide entry for the jar's contents.

Dale smashed the jar against the ground.

A cloud of gray dust erupted. The creature inhaled it.

It didn't die. It stopped.

The black electricity on its claws flickered and died. The translucent skin turned opaque, gray and dusty. The creature convulsed, then collapsed, motionless.

**[SPECIMEN 412 INTERACTION DETECTED.]** **[INFERENCE: SUBJECT REQUIRES PURE DIMENSIONAL ENERGY TO SUSTAIN PHYSICAL FORM.]** **[NEW FIELD GUIDE ENTRY: RIFT MIMIC (NULLIFIED BY STATIC MATTER).]** **[BONUS APPLIED: +200 TREATMENT POINTS.]**

Dale stood over the body. He didn't celebrate. He walked to the end of the aisle, where a door stood ajar.

Light spilled from the crack. Warm, yellow light. Not the purple of the Rift. Human light.

He pushed the door open, and the room beyond was an office. Cluttered, chaotic, filled with papers that fluttered in a draft that didn't exist. And sitting at the desk, back to the door, was a woman.

She was wearing a lab coat stained with ink. Her hair was pulled back in a severe bun. She was typing on a mechanical typewriter, the keys clacking with aggressive precision.

"Valeria Thorne," Dale said.

The typing stopped, and she didn't turn around.

"You're late," she said. Her voice was tired, dry. "I expected you twenty minutes ago."

Dale stepped into the room. The air here was heavy, pressing against his eardrums. The obsidian jar in his hand grew hot.

"I was dealing with a nuisance," Dale said.

"You killed a Guardian," Valeria said, finally turning. Her eyes were dark, exhausted, and devoid of surprise. "That cost you points.

I can feel the System's disapproval. It's screaming in my head.

"

Dale walked to the desk. He placed the clipboard on the edge of the desk.

"Who are you?" Dale asked. "And why is the System using me to find you?"

Valeria picked up a cup of coffee. It was cold. She drank it anyway.

"I am the Auditor," she said.

"Or I was. I signed the expansion order for Sector 4 because I thought we could contain the breach. We were wrong.

The breach isn't a hole.

It's a valve. And it's opening wider because you're closing it too slowly.

"

Dale looked at her. He looked at the scars on his own hands, the tattoo he didn't recognize.

"The System said failure results in erasure," Dale said.

"It does," Valeria said.

"But not just yours. Everyone's. The entire county.

The entire timeline. If you don't seal the valve, the Rift becomes permanent.

The monsters won't be pests anymore.

They'll be the native fauna.

"

She stood up. She was taller than he realized.

She walked to the window. Outside, the purple line in the sky was no longer a line. It was a wound.

A tear in the fabric of the world, bleeding darkness.

"You have to go down," Valeria said.

"Down?" Dale asked.

"The library basement isn't the bottom," she said.

"It's the throat. The valve is beneath the foundation. You have to go into the source to close it.

But the source is guarded by something that doesn't have a Field Guide entry.

Something that doesn't exist in our reality until you look at it.

"

Dale looked at his clipboard. The paper was blank again.

"How do I fight it if I don't know what it is?" Dale asked.

Valeria smiled. It was a sad, thin smile.

"You don't fight it, Dale.

You document it. You write the report. The System rewards thoroughness.

It rewards the act of defining the undefined. If you can write the name of the thing in the dark, the System will recognize it.

And once it's recognized, it's vulnerable.

"

Dale picked up his clipboard. The pen in his hand felt heavy.

**[NEW OBJECTIVE: DESCEND TO SOURCE.]** **[THREAT LEVEL: UNKNOWN.]** **[RECOMMENDATION: EXTREME CAUTION.]**

Dale looked at Valeria.

"Why me?"

"Because you're the only one who sees the code violations," Valeria said.

"You see the cracks. You see the rot.

Everyone else sees a monster. You see a structural failure.

"

Dale nodded. He understood. A monster was an infestation. An infestation was a failure of maintenance.

He walked to the far wall of the office. There was a rug. He kicked it aside. Beneath it was a trapdoor, hidden by decades of dust and neglect.

He opened it.

Darkness rose from the hole. A smell of sulfur and ancient stone.

Dale checked his headlamp. Full battery.

He checked his clipboard.

Paper secure. He checked his pen. Ink full.

He looked at Valeria.

"Do you have a ladder?"

"No," she said. "You'll have to climb."

Dale grabbed the iron ring of the ladder rungs. They were rusted, flaking. He tested one. It held.

He descended.

The air grew colder.

The purple light from above faded, replaced by a bioluminescent green glow emanating from the walls of the shaft. The walls were not stone.

They were flesh.

Living, breathing tissue, pulsing with veins that carried a thick, black fluid.

Dale climbed down, rung by rung. His boots slipped on the wet metal.

He caught himself, muscles straining, and he didn't panic.

He adjusted his grip. He checked his footing.

At the bottom of the shaft, the floor was a circular chamber. The walls were lined with eyes. Hundreds of them. Embedded in the flesh, blinking slowly.

And in the center of the chamber, standing on a pedestal of bone, was a figure, which was Dale.

Or rather, it was Dale, but older. His hair was gray. His suit was torn. His clipboard was filled with thousands of pages.

The Other Dale looked up. His eyes were empty, white voids.

"You're early," the Other Dale said.

Dale stepped onto the floor of the chamber. The eyes in the walls turned to look at him.

**[ENTITY IDENTIFIED: ECHO OF FAILURE.]** **[STATUS: PARADOX CLASS.]** **[ACTION: ELIMINATE.]**

Dale raised his pen. He did not attack. He pointed it at the figure.

"What is your name?" Dale asked.

The Other Dale smiled. It was a smile of infinite pity.

"I am what you become when you run out of paper," the figure said.

The chamber shook. The ground cracked. From the cracks, things crawled.

Small, chitinous things. Fast.

Dale looked at his clipboard. He had one sheet left.

He wrote.
