Chapter 296: Begin Your Report

# Chapter 296

The nozzle sputtered. Thorne frowned at the sprayer in his hands, shaking it violently like a man trying to coax a dead car engine back to life. The rune-etched glass chamber remained stubbornly empty.

Dale didn't move. He kept his grip on the crowbar, though his other hand hovered near his utility belt. His eyes were fixed on the yellow hazmat suit. Specifically, on the intake valve.

"Malfunction," Dale said. Flat. Matter-of-fact.

Thorne looked up, sneering.

"Shut up, exterminator.

You don't know how this works.

These are Authority-grade discharge units. They don't fail.

"

"They do when the substrate is wrong." Dale pointed with his chin.

"You're using a Class-III solvent mix. High viscosity.

Designed for chitinous carapaces.

Acid Beetles, mostly. You're aiming it at me.

"

Thorne's smile tightened.

"And?"

"You're in a sewer main." Dale gestured to the rising gray water lapping at their waists. "This is an aquatic environment.

High pH.

Organic decay.

If you pull that trigger, the nozzle will clog in three seconds. The pressure buildup will rupture the tank.

You'll lose your certification rating. Maybe your hand.

"

The surrounding squad shifted uneasily. They looked at Thorne, then at the sprayer, then at Dale.

Thorne stepped closer, raising the weapon higher.

"I know what I'm doing, Dale.

I've been licensed for five years.

You're a glorified ant killer. Step aside. This infestation ends today.

"

"Infestation?" Dale tilted his head. "Who is the infestation? Me? Or you?"

"You're unregistered in this zone," Thorne said, his voice dropping to a dangerous whisper. "You crossed a dimensional boundary without filing a Form 14-B. That's a Class-A violation. We're here to terminate the anomaly."

The word *terminate* hung in the damp air. It wasn't a metaphor. Thorne meant it.

Dale looked at the red pen in his pocket, and the glow was gone, but the weight remained. He looked at the field around him.

Thirty agents.

All armed with modified sprayers, nets, and containment coils. All wearing the insignia of the Regional Licensing Authority.

"You're contaminating the site," Dale said.

Thorne blinked.

"What?"

"Look at your boots." Dale pointed down. The water was swirling around Thorne's heavy rubber soles.

"You walked through the upper sector.

The spire.

The ash. You're tracking spore-dust into the primary nest.

If you spray me, you'll aerosolize that dust. You'll trigger a cross-contamination penalty for every single one of your agents. Forty percent debuff.

Global."

A murmur rippled through the squad. A woman in an orange suit lowered her sprayer slightly.

"He's right," she said. Her voice was muffled by the helmet, but clear enough. "My Field Guide is already flagged for dimensional variance. If I deploy against a non-listed entity, I lose my multiplier."

Thorne's face went rigid. He looked at the woman, then back at Dale.

"Bluffing. You're trying to stall."

"I'm trying to save your Thoroughness Rating," Dale said.

He took a step forward. The water splashed.

"Check your log.

See the notification.

"

Thorne hesitated. He glanced at the side of his helmet, where a small HUD projected his current status.

His eyes widened. A red warning flashed: *PROTOCOL VIOLATION: CROSS-CONTAMINATION RISK HIGH.*

"You can't be serious," Thorne muttered.

"I'm always serious," Dale said. "That's why I'm still alive. And you're going to die."

The crowbar in Dale's hand shifted. He didn't swing it. He used it to hook a floating piece of debris—a chunk of rotted wood from the old duplex—pulling it toward him.

"Protocol Seven," Dale announced. "Isolation."

He threw the wood. It didn't hit Thorne. It hit the water between Thorne and the rest of the squad. A splash.

Thorne fired.

The sprayer roared. A jet of thick, green fluid erupted from the nozzle.

But Dale was already moving, and he didn't dodge away. He dove *under* the water.

The jet passed over his head. It hit the back wall of the sewer. The liquid hissed, eating into the concrete.

Dale surfaced, gasping, spitting out dirty water. He wasn't alone.

The water was churning. Something was moving beneath the surface.

Not a monster.

A shape.

Large. Bulbous.

Thorne raised his weapon again, aiming at the water.

"Don't let it surface!"

"It's already surfaced," Dale coughed. He wiped sludge from his eyes. "You just didn't see it because you weren't looking at the bottom."

The water erupted. A massive claw punched through the surface, smashing into Thorne's chest. The man flew backward, crashing into his own squad.

The agent screamed. The claw retracted.

Silence. Then, chaos.

Agents shouted. Sprayers fired blindly. Green, blue, and red jets of chemical death sprayed across the dark water. The water boiled with acid.

Dale stayed low. He kept his head below the waterline, using the debris as cover. He watched the water. He counted the ripples.

*One. Two. Three.*

A shape moved against the current. Fast. Silent.

"Target acquired," Dale whispered to himself.

He reached for his belt. Not a weapon. A marker. A standard-issue chalk marker, used for marking entry points.

He pulled the cap off.

Above him, the battle raged. Thorne was struggling to his feet, his chest plate cracked. He pointed at Dale.

"Get him! Don't let him tag the nest!"

Dale ignored him. He focused on the water. The shape was circling. It was waiting.

Dale threw the marker.

It wasn't a projectile. He threw it at a patch of foam on the water's surface. The marker hit the foam and sank.

Nothing happened.

Thorne laughed, coughing blood.

"You're throwing chalk at a Leviathan? You're insane."

"Field Guide entry," Dale said. Loudly. "New species. *Subterranean Leviathan.* Weakness: dorsal gill vents. Located two meters below the surface, posterior section."

The HUDs of every agent in the room flashed.

*NEW SPECIES IDENTIFIED.* *FIELD GUIDE UPDATED.* *TARGETED DAMAGE BONUS ACTIVE.*

The woman in orange looked at her HUD. She looked at Dale. She looked at the water.

"Two meters below," she said. "Posterior."

She adjusted her aim. She didn't aim at Dale. She aimed at the empty space ten feet to Dale's left.

She fired.

The jet of blue solvent hit the water. It didn't splash. It vanished.

A roar. Deep. Underwater. The water turned red.

The Leviathan thrashed. It breached the surface, revealing a sleek, armored back. Vents on its side pulsed, glowing red.

"Hit it again!" Thorne screamed. "Kill the exterminator!"

But the squad didn't listen. They were looking at their scores. The notification was clear: *THOROUGHNESS BONUS: +200% DAMAGE.*

They turned their weapons on the monster.

Dale scrambled backward, kicking off the wall. He needed distance. He needed air.

He climbed up onto a ledge, pulling himself out of the water. His suit was soaked. His lungs burned.

Thorne was still there. Bleeding. Shaking. He looked at Dale with pure hatred.

"You cheated," Thorne spat. "You gave them the data. You gave them the win."

"I gave them a protocol," Dale said. He checked his own HUD.

*JOB IN PROGRESS.* *STATUS: CONTAINMENT.* *AGENT: DALE KOWALSKI.* *THOROUGHNESS: 10.0.*

A perfect score, and the squad was tearing the Leviathan apart.

Chemical fire. Nets. They were efficient.

Brutal.

Professional.

Thorne stumbled toward Dale. He had a knife now. A small, sharp blade for cutting nets.

"This isn't over," Thorne said. He wiped blood from his chin. "You think you're safe? You think the Authority cares about a single job?"

Dale looked at the red pen in his pocket. He pulled it out. The ink was dried, but the nib was still sharp.

"The Authority cares about the backlog," Dale said. "And you're the bottleneck."

Thorne lunged.

Dale sidestepped, and he didn't strike. He grabbed Thorne's wrist and twisted. A simple joint lock.

Standard compliance maneuver.

Thorne dropped the knife. Dale kicked him in the knee. The man howled, collapsing into the water.

Dale stood over him. The Leviathan was dead. The squad was regrouping, looking at Dale.

The woman in orange walked over, and she pulled off her helmet.

She was young. Mid-twenties.

Her face was pale, but her eyes were steady.

"Who are you?" she asked.

Dale straightened his suit. He checked his clipboard.

It was gone, lost in the fall, and he felt a pang of anxiety. No clipboard. No report.

"Dale Kowalski," he said. "Class II."

The woman looked at his badge. Then she looked at the dead Leviathan.

"That's impossible," she said. "Class II can't identify Class V threats. The Field Guide doesn't have those entries."

Dale looked at the red pen.

"It does if you find the right loophole."

The woman frowned.

"What loophole?"

Dale didn't answer. He looked at the spire in the distance. The rift was still open. The sky was still broken.

"Your squad is contaminated," Dale said.

He pointed at the water.

"You all have spore-dust on your boots.

If you leave this sewer, you'll spread it to the surface.

You need to decontaminate. Now.

"

The woman looked at her boots. Thick black sludge.

"If we decontaminate here, we lose time," she said. "The Authority is expecting us at the checkpoint in ten minutes."

"If you don't decontaminate, you fail the inspection," Dale said. "And if you fail the inspection, you lose your licenses. All of you."

The squad froze. The Leviathan was dead. The immediate threat was gone. But the bureaucratic threat remained.

Thorne groaned in the water.

"Let them go. They're loyal."

"Loyalty doesn't pay fines," Dale said. He looked at the woman.

"Protocol 9.

Decontamination.

I can write the waiver if you stay for twenty minutes. But you have to let me lead the extraction.

"

The woman looked at Dale. looked at him.

"Why?" she asked.

"Because I know where the next nest is," Dale said. "And you don't."

She hesitated. Then she nodded.

"Lead the way, Dale."

Thorne screamed.

"Traitor!"

Dale ignored him. He turned and walked toward the dark tunnel leading deeper into the sewer. The squad followed.

Dale's hands were shaking. He hid them in his pockets.

He had lied, and he didn't know where the next nest was.

But he had noticed something. When he had identified the Leviathan, his Field Guide hadn't just updated. It had *merged*.

He pulled up his mental interface.

*FIELD GUIDE: MERGED.* *CURRENT ENTRY: DIMENSIONAL VERMIN (CLASS I).* *NEW DATA: THORNE, MARCUS (CLASS V).*

Dale stopped. He turned to look at Thorne.

Thorne was still in the water. But the water around him was changing. It wasn't just dirty.

It was *alive*. Black tendrils were crawling out of his pores.

His hazmat suit was splitting.

Thorne wasn't human anymore, and he was an entry in Dale's Field Guide.

A violation.

Dale felt the System click into place. A new protocol unlocked.

*PROTOCOL UNLOCKED: INTERNAL AUDIT.* *DESCRIPTION: TARGET MUST BE DOCUMENTED IN FIELD GUIDE BEFORE ELIMINATION. ELIMINATION REQUIRES WRITTEN REPORT.*

Dale looked at Thorne. Thorne looked at Dale. The man's eyes were gone. Replaced by black voids.

Thorne opened his mouth. A sound came out. Not a scream. A glitch.

"System... error..."

Dale raised his red pen.

"Report," Dale said. "Begin."
