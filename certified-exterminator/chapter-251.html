Chapter 251: Protocol One

# Chapter 251

Black lightning arced from the staff to the pavement, scorching the asphalt into a glassy crater. Dale didn't flinch. He kept his pen moving, scratching the final signature on Form 12-C with a flourish that looked less like writing and more like carving.

The Enforcer lowered the staff. The violet energy dissipated into a fine mist that smelled of ozone and burnt hair.

"Operator Kowalski," the Enforcer said. The voice was synthesized, layered with a harmonic distortion that vibrated in Dale's teeth. "You are in violation of Protocol 4-Alpha: Unsanctioned Engagement with Class-IX Assets."

Dale finished the signature. He capped the pen. He clipped the form to the clipboard.

"Protocol 4-Alpha applies to combatants," Dale said, not looking up. "I am a technician. I was performing a containment tag."

"A tag is a combat action."

"A tag is a data entry." Dale held up the clipboard. The page was saturated with monster blood, but the ink remained legible.

"I identified the specimen.

I isolated the entry point. I documented the threat. I did not attempt to eliminate it.

Elimination requires a Level V license.

I am a Level III. I know the difference.

"

The Enforcer stepped closer.

Its robes were woven from shifting geometric shapes that hurt to look at directly.

"The Authority does not recognize 'documentation' as a defense for interfering with a high-tier asset. The asset is now compromised.

You have contaminated the field.

"

"Section 12, Paragraph C," Dale recited, tapping the paper. "In the event of an Operator becoming a vector for contamination, immediate termination of the Operator is authorized to prevent spread. I am requesting that termination."

The Enforcer paused. The staff hummed, a low frequency that made Dale's vision blur at the edges.

"You are requesting your own death."

"I am requesting a sanitation zone.

I am the infestation.

You are the exterminator. Do your job, or file a violation report for incompetence.

I have a deadline on this job.

"

"You have no deadline. You have no license to operate in this sector."

"I have a deadline," Dale said. He pointed to the crater behind the Enforcer, where the portal had closed.

"The Rift is still open.

You just sealed the top. The bottom is still leaking. If you kill me, you leave the breach unsealed.

If you let me live, I can seal it.

But only if you grant me temporary Level V clearance for the duration of the seal.

"

The Enforcer tilted its head.

"Level V is reserved for licensed Exterminators. You are a pest control technician."

"I am a pest control technician who has never failed an inspection in fourteen years," Dale said.

"And I am currently standing next to a Class-IX entity that is still alive because you didn't kill it fast enough. Check your timestamps. You landed twenty-two seconds ago.

The creature is still moving.

"

The Enforcer looked down at the creature. The massive, chitinous horror was indeed twitching, its legs spasming against the pavement.

"Your identification was correct," the Enforcer conceded. "Rattus profundus, Variant Omega. Your Thoroughness Rating is 9.8. This is statistically improbable for a Level III."

"9.9," Dale corrected. "I deducted half a point for using a trash bag instead of a bio-hazard containment unit. It was a judgment call."

"A judgment call that resulted in a Class-IX asset being dragged into a residential zone."

"A judgment call that kept the asset contained within a five-foot radius while you were busy opening a portal across three time zones," Dale said.

His hand shook slightly, and he hid it behind the clipboard.

"I want to go home.

I want to finish this inspection. But I cannot leave the site until the breach is sealed. And I cannot seal the breach without a Level V protocol.

"

The Enforcer raised the staff again. The tip glowed with a terrifying intensity.

"Level V protocols require a bond. A neural link to the Authority's core.

You are human.

Your mind is not structured to hold the data of a Class-IX entity. It will burn out. You will die screaming.

"

"I have already died," Dale said flatly. "My insurance premiums went up when the Rift opened. That is a form of death. I am prepared for the next stage."

The Enforcer lowered the staff. It seemed to be calculating. The geometric patterns on its robe shifted, forming complex equations.

"There is another way," the Enforcer said. "A loophole in the licensing structure. Subsection 7-B: Emergency Deputization. A Level III operator may assume Level V privileges if a Level V operator is incapacitated or non-compliant."

"I don't know a Level V operator."

"You do not need to know them. You need to defeat them in a formal dispute resolution hearing. Right now."

Dale blinked. "A hearing?"

"The Authority values procedure above all else," the Enforcer said.

"If you can prove that your method is more thorough than the standard Level V protocol, you are deputized. The neural link is bypassed.

You operate via proxy authority.

The cost is the risk of the link itself.

"

"Proxy authority," Dale repeated. The word tasted like metal. "And if I lose?"

"You remain suspended. Your license is revoked. You return to spraying ants."

"And if I win?"

"You become a Certified Exterminator. Level V. You will be responsible for the entire Ohio Valley sector."

Dale looked at the clipboard. The blood on the paper was beginning to dry, flaking off in small, dark flakes. He looked at the Enforcer. He looked at the twitching monster.

"I accept," Dale said.

"Then speak," the Enforcer commanded. "State your case."

Dale straightened his spine. He clicked his pen.

"Standard Level V protocol involves direct engagement. High energy expenditure. High collateral damage. High risk of cross-contamination due to aggressive elimination tactics."

The Enforcer nodded.

"Correct."

"My protocol involves documentation first. Identification second.

Isolation third.

Elimination fourth.

Verification fifth. The elimination phase is delayed until the target is fully understood.

This reduces the likelihood of residual infestation by 84%.

"

"84% is an arbitrary number," the Enforcer said.

"It is a calculated risk assessment based on fourteen years of data. My Thoroughness Rating is 9.9. The average Level V rating is 7.2. I am more thorough than the average Level V by a margin of 2.7."

"Thoroughness is not the only metric," the Enforcer argued. "Efficiency is key. Time is a resource."

"Time is irrelevant if the job is not done," Dale said. He stepped forward, ignoring the electric crackle around the Enforcer.

"Look at the asset.

It is still alive. You arrived to terminate. You failed to terminate.

You are now here to debate, which is inefficiency. That is waste.

I sealed the perimeter with a trash bag and duct tape.

It held for six minutes. You opened a portal for twenty seconds. I want you to calculate the cost of that portal in mana units.

"

The Enforcer paused.

"The cost is... significant."

"Significant is not a number," Dale said. "Give me the number."

"40,000 mana units."

"And the cost of the trash bag?"

"0.004 mana units."

"0.004 against 40,000," Dale said. "That is a return on investment of ten million to one.

Your protocol is not just inefficient, which is fiscally irresponsible.

The Authority prides itself on resource management. You are wasting resources on overkill.

"

The Enforcer's staff dimmed. The geometric shapes on its robe slowed their rotation.

"You are exploiting the fiscal guidelines," the Enforcer said. It did not sound angry. It sounded intrigued.

"I am exploiting the logic," Dale said. "The System rewards thoroughness. You are rewarding speed.

Speed leads to error. Error leads to backlog.

Backlog leads to suspension.

You are currently suspended from performing your own job because you made a mistake. I am currently performing my job because I followed procedure.

"

"Procedure says you are unlicensed," the Enforcer countered.

"Procedure also says that in the absence of a licensed operator, a competent citizen may act as a deputy. I am the only competent person here. You are the only licensed person here. But you are incompetent."

The silence stretched. The monster gurgled, a wet, rattling sound.

The Enforcer raised the staff. Black lightning coiled around its arm.

"Case accepted," the Enforcer said.

"The Authority will render judgment. If you are found worthy, the proxy link will activate.

If you are found unworthy, the termination penalty will be executed immediately.

Do you understand the stakes?

"

"I understand," Dale said. He didn't look at the Enforcer.

He looked at the monster, and he raised his clipboard, holding it like a shield.

"I have a report to file."

The air around Dale changed. The ozone smell vanished, replaced by the scent of old paper and dust. A weight settled on his shoulders, heavier than gravity.

A text box appeared in his peripheral vision.

**PROXY AUTHORITY GRANTED.** **LEVEL V CLEARANCE ACTIVE.** **WARNING: NEURAL LINK STRESS IMMINENT.**

Dale gasped. A spike of white-hot pain drove into the base of his skull. It felt like an ice pick made of fire.

He dropped to one knee. The clipboard clattered to the pavement.

"Link establishing," the Enforcer said. Its voice was closer now. "Stabilize your mind. Do not resist the data."

"I am not resisting," Dale gritted out. His hands were shaking violently. "I am... processing."

"Processing," the Enforcer mocked.

"You are human. Your mind is fragile. The data from a Class-IX entity is vast.

It contains the memories of a billion years of evolution.

It will crush you.

"

Dale closed his eyes. He focused on the pain. He treated it like a code violation. *Identify. Isolate.*

*Identify:* The pain is incoming data. *Isolate:* The pain is not me. It is information.

He breathed in. He breathed out.

"Data intake at 12%," the Enforcer noted. "Pain threshold exceeded. Operator is destabilizing."

"Shut up," Dale whispered.

He opened his eyes, and the world had shifted. The air was thick with visible threads of energy. He could see the monster's nervous system, glowing green through its black chitin.

He could see the Rift's edges, fraying like old rope.

He could see the Enforcer, not as a being, but as a collection of permissions and restrictions.

"Link stability at 40%," the Enforcer said. "Operator is retaining coherence. This is... unexpected."

Dale stood up.

His legs felt like jelly, but they held, and he picked up the clipboard. The paper was blank now.

The blood had fallen away.

"I have the data," Dale said. His voice sounded distant, even to himself.

"You have the data," the Enforcer repeated. "Now eliminate the asset. Use Protocol 9: Disintegration."

Dale looked at the monster. He looked at the threads of energy connecting it to the ground.

"Protocol 9 is overkill," Dale said.

"It is the standard."

"It is sloppy." Dale raised his hand. The air around his fingers shimmered.

He didn't feel power, and he felt responsibility.

"I am using Protocol 3: Targeted Cessation."

The Enforcer stepped back.

"Protocol 3 requires precise anatomical knowledge. You do not have time to scan."

"I have the data," Dale said. "And I have my Field Guide."

He didn't open the physical guide. He opened the mental one. The entry for *Rattus profundus, Variant Omega* expanded in his mind. Weaknesses listed: Third thoracic ganglion, secondary heart, neural cluster at base of tail.

Dale pointed his finger. A thin, white beam of energy shot out. It was not a blast. It was a needle.

It struck the monster at the exact coordinate Dale had memorized.

The monster froze. Its legs stopped moving. Its eyes went dark.

It collapsed.

No explosion. No gore. Just a sudden, total cessation of biological function.

The Enforcer stared at the corpse. Then it stared at Dale.

"Efficiency rating: 10/10," the Enforcer said. "Zero collateral damage. Zero residual energy. Clean kill."

Dale didn't smile. He felt nauseous. The pain in his skull was fading, but the exhaustion was a physical weight. He leaned against the manhole cover.

"Job complete," Dale said. "File the report."

The Enforcer bowed. It was a stiff, jerky motion.

"Report filed. Operator Kowalski, you are now a Certified Exterminator, Level V."

A new notification popped up.

**CERTIFICATION LEVEL V ACHIEVED.** **NEW PROTOCOLS UNLOCKED.** **WARNING: SECTOR 4 BREACH DETECTED.**

Dale frowned. The monster was dead. The Rift was closed.

"Error," Dale said. "The asset is eliminated. The breach is sealed."

"The asset was a scout," the Enforcer said. "The infestation is not over."

Dale looked at the ground. The manhole cover was vibrating. Not from the monster. From below.

"How many?" Dale asked.

"Thousands," the Enforcer said. "The Variant Omega was a key. You turned it. You unlocked the next stage."

Dale looked at his hands. They were stained with black ichor. He looked at the Enforcer.

"I didn't turn anything," Dale said. "I just wrote the report."

"And the report," the Enforcer said, "was an invitation."

The manhole cover blew off.

A wave of smaller, faster creatures poured out. Not rats.

Something worse.

Something with too many legs and faces that looked like human children.

Dale raised his clipboard.

"Protocol 1: Identify," he said. "Document. Isolate."

The children screamed.

And they sounded like they were asking for help.
