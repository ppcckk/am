Chapter 244: Visa or Suspension

# Chapter 244

The mite on Dale's thumb twitched.

He didn't brush it off. He stared at it. The creature was translucent, barely larger than a grain of sand, but its legs were jointed with a precision that suggested engineering than biology. It raised two front legs, grooming its mouthparts, ignoring the bank vault behind it and the three humans staring at it.

"Stay still," Dale murmured, his voice flat.

Sarah raised her crossbow. The bolt tip glowed with a faint, unstable energy.

"It's a carrier.

Vance said so. We need to kill it before it breeds.

"

"Put the weapon down," Dale said, not looking up.

"You'll aerosolize it. You'll get particulates in your lungs, and you'll trigger a Level 4 Respiratory Hazard citation from the Authority.

That's a fine, a suspension of your license, and a mandatory retraining seminar.

Do you want to sit through six hours of PowerPoint on proper ventilation protocols?

"

Sarah lowered the crossbow, her knuckles white on the grip.

"It's eating the money."

"It's not eating the money," Dale corrected, pulling a small, sterile glass vial from his belt pouch. "It's metabolizing the gold to build a chitin lattice.

Look at the movement patterns, and they aren't hunting.

They're constructing.

"

He tilted his wrist. The mite hopped to his glove, then scurried onto the glass vial. Dale sealed the cap with a click.

**SUBJECT CAPTURED: DIMENSIONAL MITE (PROVISIONAL).** **STATUS: LIVE SPECIMEN.** **FIELD GUIDE ENTRY: PENDING IDENTIFICATION.**

"Vance," Dale said, standing up. His knees were still shaky, but his hands were steady. "Get the ledger.

The big one. The physical book they kept before the digital servers went dark.

"

Vance blinked.

"The ledger? Why?"

"Because the digital logs are corrupted. I can see the error flags in my peripheral vision. The Authority's interface is throwing a fit because the local network was fried by the Hollow Mouth's acid spray. We need a manual audit of the vault's contents to calculate the biomass-to-resource ratio."

"That's insane," Vance said, but he was already moving toward the heavy oak desk in the corner of the bank manager's office. "The building is still shaking."

"Let it shake. Structural integrity is a separate inspection category."

Dale walked to the center of the vault floor.

The thousands of mites had stopped moving. They formed a dense, black carpet that rippled like water. As Dale stepped closer, the surface of the carpet shifted.

A single line of mites broke away from the mass and marched toward him.

Then they stopped, and they formed a circle around Dale's boots. A perfect, geometric circle.

**NOTICE: SUBTLE BEHAVIORAL SHIFT DETECTED.** **INTERPRETATION: RECOGNITION OF DOMINANT PREDATOR.** **ACTION REQUIRED: DOCUMENT SOCIAL STRUCTURE.**

Dale pulled out his clipboard. He clicked his pen.

"Okay," he said, writing in the margin. *Subject exhibits collective consciousness. Potential hive-mind architecture. Note: No aggression observed. Subjects appear to be assessing threat level based on certification badge visibility.*

He tapped the Authority badge pinned to his chest. The holographic Class III insignia flared blue.

The circle of mites dispersed. They rushed back to the center of the vault, forming a tight pyramid that rose two feet into the air.

"They're bowing," Sarah whispered.

"They're calculating," Dale said. He looked at the pile of gold. "Vance, read me the last entry."

Vance flipped open the ledger. His hands trembled.

"October 12th.

Deposit of five hundred ounces of refined gold bullion. Verified by Manager Halloway.

"

"And the withdrawal?"

"There is no withdrawal recorded. The vault door was sealed."

Dale walked to the pyramid. He crouched.

The mites parted for him, revealing a core of molten gold at the base of the structure.

It wasn't melted by heat, which was melted by acid secreted from their mouths.

"They're refining it," Dale said. "They're breaking down the elemental structure. Why?"

"Energy," Vance said, joining him. "Maybe they use gold for conductivity? Like a battery?"

"Unlikely. Gold is an inert conductor. It doesn't react with biological acid." Dale narrowed his eyes.

"Unless the acid isn't biological. Unless it's alchemical."

He looked at his Field Guide. The entry for *Dimensional Vermin* was static. But a new, greyed-out line appeared below it.

**HYPOTHESIS: SUBJECTS ARE SYNTHESIZING A CATALYST.** **REQUIRED DATA: CHEMICAL COMPOSITION OF SECRETION.**

Dale needed a sample. He couldn't scrape the pyramid; the Authority would dock points for poor technique and potential cross-contamination. He needed a passive collection method.

He reached into his kit and pulled out a strip of silica gel desiccant tape. It was standard for moisture monitoring in crawlspaces. He peeled off the backing.

"Sarah, hold the light steady."

She raised her lantern. The beam hit the pyramid. The mites froze.

Dale pressed the sticky side of the tape against the base of the gold pile. He pulled it away. The tape was covered in a viscous, black slime.

**SAMPLE COLLECTED.** **ANALYZING...**

The interface paused. It seemed to hang, the text flickering.

"Come on," Dale muttered.

**ANALYSIS COMPLETE.** **SUBSTANCE IDENTIFIED: DIMENSIONAL MERCURY (UNSTABLE).** **PROPERTIES: VOLATILE. REACTS WITH ORGANIC MATTER TO CREATE STABILIZED ALLOY.**

Dale's stomach dropped.

Dimensional Mercury, which was a theoretical contaminant. It wasn't just poison; it was a reality anchor. If this stuff got into the groundwater, it wouldn't just kill the fish.

It would start to unravel the local spacetime continuity.

It would turn the suburb into a pocket dimension where physics was negotiable.

"They aren't building a nest," Dale said, his voice cold. "They're building a bomb."

"What?" Sarah asked.

"The gold.

They're mixing it with the mercury. The alloy stabilizes the mercury. It makes it solid.

It makes it safe to handle... until someone breaks it.

"

Dale looked at the pyramid, which was nearly finished.

Once the alloy hardened, the mites would scatter. They would take pieces of the bomb and hide them in walls, under floors, in the foundations of every house in the sector.

The Authority wouldn't detect the threat until the alloy was cracked. By then, the infestation would be everywhere.

"We have to stop them," Vance said. "Now. Before they finish."

"We can't use fire," Dale said. "Heat accelerates the reaction. We can't use acid; that's their medium. We need a neutralizing agent."

He scanned his kit. Insecticide?

No.

Traps? No. Bait?

No, and he looked at the ledger again. *Refined gold.*

"Vance, do you have any pure silver?"

Silver was a known neutralizer for dimensional mercury. It bonded with the mercury, pulling it out of the alloy and forming a stable, harmless powder. It was a trick from the advanced certification textbooks, Chapter 9, *Inorganic Contaminants of the Higher Planes.*

"I... I have my wedding ring," Vance said.

He pulled it off, which was a thick, heavy band. Sterling silver.

"Give it to me."

Vance handed it over. Dale held it up. The ring was small. Too small for the amount of mercury in the vault.

"It's not enough," Dale said. He ran the calculations in his head.

The mass of the gold pile was huge.

The ratio required for neutralization was 1:1, and he needed pounds of silver.

Sarah stepped forward. She unclipped her belt pouch and dumped its contents onto the floor. Two silver coins from a collection.

A silver flask. A silver locket.

"I have more," she said. "My car has a silver bumper. The house has silverware."

"We don't have time to go to the car," Dale said. He looked at the pyramid. The gold was turning grey. The reaction was accelerating.

He had one option. A loophole.

The Certification System awarded points for thoroughness. But it also had a clause for *Emergency Resource Allocation*. If a technician lacked the materials to complete a protocol, they could request an immediate requisition from the local Authority warehouse.

The catch: Requisitions were deducted from the technician's future point earnings. And if the requisition was denied, the technician was liable for the resulting damage.

Dale looked at his account. He had just leveled up to Class III. He had a surplus of points from the Hollow Mouth kill. But not enough to cover a requisition of this size.

He opened the Interface.

**REQUISITION REQUEST: PURE SILVER (15 KG).** **COST: 500 TREATMENT POINTS.** **CURRENT BALANCE: 120 POINTS.** **ERROR: INSUFFICIENT FUNDS.**

Dale stared at the error message. He couldn't afford the silver.

But there was another way. The System allowed for *Barter Transactions* within the sector. If a technician could provide a service to the Authority, they could exchange labor for materials.

What did the Authority need right now?

It needed data. It needed to know if this was an isolated incident or part of a larger pattern.

Dale looked at the mites. They were watching him. They were waiting.

He could submit a *Preliminary Hazard Assessment*. It was a low-risk, low-point action. But it took time. The alloy would solidify before he finished writing.

Or he could do something else, and he looked at the black speck on his thumb. The captured mite in the vial.

He could offer the Authority a live specimen. A *Class III Bio-Sample*.

The Authority paid handsomely for verified, live samples of new species. A Class III sample was worth a fortune in points. Enough to buy the silver ten times over.

But capturing a Class III sample was dangerous. And if the specimen died during transit, the points were voided.

Dale looked at the vial. The mite was still alive.

He looked at the pyramid. The grey alloy was forming a crust.

He made a decision, and he uncapped the vial.

He held it over the pyramid.

"Sarah," he said.

"When I drop this, I want you to shoot the top of the pyramid. Not with an arrow. With your crossbow bolt.

Aim for the center of mass.

"

"Why?"

"Because when I drop this sample, the hive mind will lose its leader. The chaos will create a venting point. The bolt will disrupt the alloy formation long enough for me to dump the silver."

"That's suicide," Sarah said.

"It's procedure," Dale said.

He dropped the vial.

The glass shattered on the gold. The mite in the vial shrieked, a sound like tearing paper.

The hive mind froze.

For a second, the entire swarm stopped. The pyramid wobbled.

Then it exploded.

Thousands of mites flew outward in a radial burst. They didn't attack. They scattered, trying to find cover, trying to re-establish a hierarchy.

"Shoot!" Dale yelled.

Sarah fired.

The bolt struck the center of the wobbling alloy. It punched through the soft, grey sludge, embedding itself in the floor.

The alloy cracked.

Dale threw Vance's ring and Sarah's silver items into the crack. He kicked the rest of the silver pile from the floor into the gap.

**REQUISITION OVERRIDE: LOCAL ASSETS UTILIZED.** **NEUTRALIZATION PROTOCOL INITIATED.**

The silver hit the mercury. The reaction was instant. The grey alloy hissed, bubbling and turning into a dull, white powder. The mites that touched the powder fell dead, their bodies desiccating in seconds.

The swarm slowed. Then stopped.

The silence in the vault was absolute.

Dale stood in the center of the white dust, breathing hard. His heart hammered against his ribs. He checked his interface.

**TREATMENT POINTS EARNED: 0.** **REASON: REQUISITION COST EXCEEDED BALANCE.** **DEBT ACCRUED: -380 POINTS.**

He owed the Authority, and he had saved the city from a dimensional bomb, and the Authority had charged him for the privilege.

"Dale?" Vance whispered. "Did we win?"

Dale looked at the white powder. He looked at the interface. The debt number pulsed in red.

"No," Dale said. "We just paid the first installment."

He turned to the vault door. It was still open. Beyond it, the bank lobby was quiet. Too quiet.

**NOTICE: NEW INFESTATION DETECTED.** **LOCATION: SECTOR 4, RESIDENTIAL ZONE.** **SPECIES: UNKNOWN.** **BEHAVIOR: ORGANIZED MIGRATION TOWARD SECTOR 4.**

Dale felt a chill that had nothing to do with the cold air of the vault.

The mites hadn't just been building a bomb. They had been a scout party. A vanguard.

And the main body was coming, and he looked at his negative balance. He looked at the open door.

"Sarah," Dale said, his voice flat. "Do you have a credit card?"

"I... what?"

"The Authority charges late fees," Dale said, clicking his pen. "And I don't think they accept Visa."
