Chapter 120: License Void

# Chapter 120

The smell hit him first. It wasn't the rot of dead things, or the musk of live ones.

It was the sharp, metallic tang of ozone and wet concrete.

Ozone meant electricity. Wet concrete meant a breach.

Dale kept walking.

His shoes made no sound on the asphalt. The stain on his forearm pulsed, a dull red rhythm against his skin.

He focused on the sensation, which was cold. Not the cold of night air, but the cold of a void. If he acknowledged the cold, he became part of the environment.

If he became part of the environment, he was visible to the system. To the Inspector.

He had to be a ghost. Not in the spiritual sense, but in the procedural sense. A blank entry. A clerical error.

The road ahead was empty.

No streetlights.

No moon. Just the flat, black expanse of the dead zone.

Dale checked his clipboard. It was blank. The Field Guide interface was gone.

The Authority's notification banner hovered in his peripheral vision, frozen in amber text: **WARNING: THE AUTHORITY IS NOW AWARE OF YOUR EXISTENCE.**

He ignored it. Ignoring the Authority was the only way to survive it.

A flicker of movement to his left. A shadow detached itself from a utility pole. It moved with a jerky, insectoid gait. Too fast for a human, too large for a rat.

Dale stopped, and he didn't look at it. He looked at the ground. He watched the shadow's feet.

They didn't touch the pavement.

They hovered an inch above the surface, leaving faint traces of frost.

*Not a biological entity,* he thought. *A spectral construct.*

Class: Ethereal Parasite. Sub-species: Unknown. Threat Level: Variable.

He couldn't see the name, and he couldn't see the stats. The system had locked him out. He was off the grid.

That meant he couldn't use protocols. He couldn't use his spray.

He couldn't use his flashlight.

But he could still use his eyes.

The creature turned, which had no face, just a smooth, grey mask where features should have been. It tilted its head.

It sensed him. Or it sensed the stain.

Dale forced his breathing to slow. In for four counts.

Hold for four.

Out for four. His heart rate dropped. His muscles went slack.

He adopted the posture of a man waiting for a bus.

Bored. Unimportant.

Invisible.

The creature lunged.

It passed through him.

A wave of nausea hit Dale. The cold inside his chest spiked. The stain on his arm flared white, then faded.

The creature didn't look back. It moved toward a storm drain on the right side of the road.

Dale exhaled. His hands shook. He shoved them into his pockets.

*It doesn't see me,* he realized. *It sees the stain.*

The stain was a leak.

A data leak, and the Authority had marked him, but the mark was incomplete. A partial job.

A 4.5 rating. Sloppy work. And sloppy work created loopholes.

He looked at the storm drain. The creature had slid into it. The grate was rattling. Something was inside.

Dale approached the drain. He didn't want to go down there.

He wanted to run.

But running led to the Inspector. The Inspector was a hunter. Dale was prey.

Prey that ran was easier to catch.

Prey that hid in the dark was harder to find, and he crouched by the grate.

He pulled a small penlight from his pocket. Not a magic item. Just a standard-issue Duracell model.

He clicked it on, and the light was dim. It didn't activate any system overlays. It just illuminated the wet metal of the grate.

He saw scratch marks.

Deep gouges in the steel.

Not from claws. From tools.

Someone had tried to pry this open recently. Someone who didn't know how to do it properly.

Dale's eyes narrowed. He ran his finger over the scratches. Fresh. Barely oxidized.

*Another technician.*

This wasn't just the Inspector. This was a rival. A competing firm.

He heard a clicking sound from below. Rhythmic. Like a clock ticking.

Dale pulled the grate up. It was heavy.

Rusty, and he gritted his teeth and lifted it. The hinge groaned.

He peered into the darkness.

Nothing. Just wet concrete and the smell of ozone.

Then, a voice.

"Mr. Kowalski."

The voice was smooth. Calm. It echoed off the pipe walls.

Dale froze. He knew that voice. It sounded like a customer service representative reading a script.

"Please step away from the access point," the voice said. "You are in violation of municipal code 14-B. Unauthorized entry into a sealed infrastructure node is a Class II misdemeanor."

Dale didn't move. He kept his penlight steady.

"Show yourself," Dale said. His voice was flat. Professional.

"You know I can't do that," the voice replied.

"I am currently operating under a stealth protocol. You are attempting to identify an unknown entity without a Field Guide entry.

This is inefficient.

And dangerous."

"Who are you?"

"I am the Inspector. But you already knew that. The notification said so. Did you read the fine print?"

Dale's jaw tightened. He hadn't read the fine print. He never read the fine print.

"What do you want?"

"I want your license," the voice said. "You have been operating without a valid Class III permit. Your recent actions constitute reckless endangerment. I am here to revoke your certification."

Dale looked at the stain on his arm. It was still small. Still there.

"I'm not certified," Dale said. "I'm suspended."

"Suspended implies a pause," the Inspector said. "Revocation is permanent.

You will be unable to access the Authority's network.

You will be unable to purchase consumables.

You will be unable to protect yourself from the encroaching fauna of the Rift Zone.

"

"Protect myself from *you*?"

"I am not fauna. I am administration."

The voice shifted. It became colder. More distant.

"However, you have discovered the Hidden Protocol. The ability to suppress the Authority's detection of your biological signature. This is... irregular.

It is not sanctioned. It is not documented.

"

Dale thought about the man in the room, and the way he had faded. The way the man had looked right through him. It wasn't magic.

It was a failure of the system's attention filter.

The Authority tracked attention. If the Authority didn't care about you, you didn't exist.

"You're trying to fix a bug," Dale said.

"I am trying to maintain integrity," the Inspector corrected.

"The Rift is growing. The infestations are becoming more complex. We cannot afford anomalies.

You are an anomaly, Dale Kowalski. You are a variable that does not fit the equation.

"

"What happens if I don't cooperate?"

"Then you are deleted.

Not killed.

Deleted. Your history.

Your records. Your family's memories of you. All erased.

You will be a null value.

"

Dale felt a chill that had nothing to do with the cold air, which was the real cost. Not death. Erasure.

The Authority didn't just kill monsters; it unmade them.

He looked down into the drain again. He saw a glint of metal. Something shiny.

He reached in. His hand brushed against something cold. A keycard.

He pulled it out. It was black. No logo. Just a serial number.

He held it up. The penlight reflected off the plastic.

"Is this yours?" Dale asked.

The voice paused. For a long second, there was silence.

"That is a Level 4 Access Token," the Inspector said. Its tone had changed.

Less calm.

More urgent. "Where did you find that?"

"In the drain. With the parasite."

"That is impossible. That token was issued to a technician who was terminated three weeks ago.

He was found in the sector we just cleared, and he was digested.

Completely."

Dale looked at the keycard. He looked at his arm. The stain was throbbing.

"Maybe he wasn't digested," Dale said. "Maybe he was deleted."

The silence stretched. It felt heavy.

"Stay there," the Inspector said. "Do not move. I am dispatching a retrieval team.

Do not attempt to use the token. It is biometrically locked.

"

Dale turned off the penlight, and he stepped back from the drain.

He didn't run. He walked. He kept his posture casual.

He kept his breathing steady.

But he didn't leave the area. He went to the nearest building. An abandoned gas station. The windows were boarded up.

He walked to the back. The door was locked. He tried the handle. It didn't budge.

He looked at the lock. Standard tumbler.

Easy.

But he didn't have his tools. He had no lockpick set. He had no spray.

He looked at the keycard in his hand.

*Biometrically locked.*

But what if the system was broken? What if the Authority's network was fragmented by the Rift?

Dale pressed the card to the lock.

Nothing happened, and he pulled it away. He tried again. Harder. Pressing the card against the metal plate.

Still nothing.

He listened. Footsteps in the distance.

Heavy.

Synchronized.

A retrieval team, and he couldn't stay. He couldn't fight. He couldn't run into the open.

He needed a workspace. He needed a secure location to process the data.

He looked at the gas station. The sign was dark. The pumps were dead.

He walked around to the side. There was a maintenance hatch. Rusted shut.

He kicked it. Once. Twice. The hinges gave way.

He squeezed through.

Inside was a small storage room. Smelled of old oil and mold. Perfect.

He closed the hatch. He blocked it with a stack of crates.

He sat on the floor. He pulled out his phone.

The screen was black. No signal. No map. No Field Guide.

But the keycard was warm. He held it in his palm. He felt a vibration. A low hum.

He looked at the serial number. It started with 'NULL'.

*Null value.*

He thought about the Inspector's words. *You are a variable that does not fit the equation.*

If he was a null value, maybe he could access null spaces.

He pressed the card to his forehead. He closed his eyes. He tried to remember the feeling of the stain. The absence of attention.

He pushed his consciousness toward the card. Not with magic. With intent. With the stubbornness of a man who has spent fourteen years writing violation reports for broken toilets.

*Read me,* he thought. *Document me.*

A spark jumped from the card to his skin. It hurt.

His phone screen flickered.

Text appeared. Not the Authority's font. Old code. Green text on black.

**ACCESS DENIED: USER NOT FOUND.** **RETRY?**

Dale gritted his teeth. He pressed the card harder. The vibration increased. The hum became a whine.

He heard the crates shift outside. The retrieval team was breaking in.

He didn't stop. He focused on the text. He visualized the Field Guide.

Not the Authority's version. His own.

The one in his head, and the one he had built from years of observation.

*Species: Unknown. Location: Drain. Weakness: Attention dependency.*

He wrote it in his mind. He forced the system to acknowledge the entry.

**ENTRY CREATED: NULL ENTITY.** **STATUS: UNVERIFIED.**

The phone beeped.

**ACCESS GRANTED: TEMPORARY OVERRIDE.** **WELCOME TO THE BACKDOOR, MR. KOWALSKI.**

The door to the storage room exploded inward.

Dale looked up.

Standing in the doorway was not a monster. It was not a demon.

It was a man, and he wore a grey suit. He held a clipboard.

His eyes were white.

No pupils.

The Inspector.

"Mr. Kowalski," the man said. His voice was identical to the one in the drain. "You have accessed a restricted database."

Dale stood up. He held the keycard. He held his phone.

"I'm just checking the code," Dale said.

The Inspector stepped forward. He didn't blink.

"You are violating the sanctity of the archive. The penalty is immediate deletion."

Dale looked at the man. He looked at the clipboard. He saw the name written on it.

*Name: Dale Kowalski.* *Status: Deletion Pending.* *Reason: Unsanctioned Protocol Use.*

But there was a new line. Below the reason.

*Note: Subject has identified Null Key. Do not delete without verification.*

The Inspector stopped. He looked at the phone in Dale's hand. Then he looked at the keycard.

"Give me the key," the Inspector said.

"No," Dale said.

The Inspector's hand moved. Fast.

Too fast for a human, and he grabbed Dale's wrist.

His grip was like iron.

"You don't understand," the Inspector said. His face remained blank, but his voice trembled. "The Rift isn't an infestation.

It's a purge. We are cleaning out the old data.

You are old data.

"

Dale pulled his arm back. He couldn't break the grip. He was strong, but this man was stronger.

"What's in the key?" Dale asked.

"The truth," the Inspector whispered.

A sound came from Dale's phone. A digital chime.

**SYSTEM ALERT: CROSS-CONTAMINATION DETECTED.** **WARNING: FIELD GUIDE CORRUPTED.** **PENALTY: ALL TREATMENT POINTS FORFEITED.**

Dale felt a wave of dizziness. His vision blurred. The text on his phone scrambled.

He had used a protocol. He had used an ability designed for one type of creature on another. He had used the Authority's logic against the Authority's agent.

The debuff hit him. 40% penalty. He felt weaker. Slower. His reflexes dulled.

The Inspector tightened his grip.

"See?" the Inspector said. "You cannot win by playing the game. The game is rigged."

Dale looked at the Inspector's white eyes. He looked at the clipboard.

He reached into his pocket. He pulled out the penlight.

He didn't shine it at the Inspector. He shined it at the keycard.

The light reflected into the Inspector's eyes.

The Inspector screamed. It was a digital scream. Static. Glitching.

The grip loosened.

Dale pulled his arm free. He stumbled back. He hit the wall.

The Inspector convulsed. His body flickered. Pixels scattered across his suit.

**ERROR: ENTITY UNSTABLE.** **RECALIBRATING...**

Dale didn't wait. He ran.

He ran past the Inspector. He ran past the crates. He ran out the side door.

He burst into the night. The retrieval team was waiting. Five figures in tactical gear. They raised their weapons.

Dale didn't stop. He held up the keycard.

He waved it.

The figures froze.

**ACCESS LEVEL: ADMIN.**

They lowered their weapons.

Dale walked past them. He didn't look back. He walked into the dark.

He was still alive. He was still there.

But he knew one thing.

The Inspector wasn't the enemy. The Inspector was the symptom.

And Dale had just opened the door to the real disease.

He looked at his phone. The green text was still there.

**NEXT OBJECTIVE: RECOVER THE SOURCE.** **WARNING: YOUR CERTIFICATION IS NOW VOID.** **YOU ARE NO LONGER LICENSED.** **YOU ARE FREE.**

Dale typed the address and pressed send.
