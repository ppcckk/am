Chapter 352: No Report No License

# Chapter 352

The floor of the courtroom was made of teeth.

Dale's boots sank into the jagged enamel, the sharp edges biting through his steel-toed soles. He didn't look up. He looked down, at the clipboard, at the pen. The ink flowed smoothly, black against white, ignoring the blood welling from his own fingertips.

*Violation 1: Improper Venue Selection.* *Violation 2: Lack of Proper Signage.*

The monsters surrounding him did not roar. They chittered. A hive-mind of chitin and wet fur, they pressed closer, their compound eyes reflecting the sterile, fluorescent glare of the Authority's judgment light. They were waiting for the verdict.

Dale finished the first line. He flipped the page.

It was blank, and he hated blank pages. They implied a lack of preparation.

Above him, the ceiling was a void of shifting geometric shapes. The Auditor hovered there, a silhouette in a suit that fit too perfectly, its face a smooth, featureless oval. The thing that had dragged Dale from Ohio was gone, replaced by this bureaucratic abstraction.

**"DEFENDANT SUBMITS NO DEFENSE,"** the Auditor intoned. The voice bypassed Dale's ears and vibrated directly in his molars. **"JUDGMENT PENDING."**

Dale ignored the voice, and he ignored the thousand eyes. He focused on the texture of the floor.

Bone.

Calcified. Porous. Not metal, not wood.

Organic material susceptible to fungal degradation.

*Entry 44-B: Ossuary Infestation.* *Weakness: Mycelial Network.*

He underlined it. Then he wrote, in large, block letters: **REMEDIAL ACTION REQUIRED.**

The Auditor tilted its head. **

"THIS IS A TRIAL, LICENSEE. NOT A SERVICE CALL."**

"Same difference," Dale muttered.

He clicked the pen again, and the sound was loud in the silence. Click.

Click. Click.

He wasn't writing a legal defense. He was writing a scope of work.

A creature lunged. A spider the size of a minivan, its legs clicking against the bone floor. It aimed for Dale's throat, mandibles dripping acid.

Dale didn't dodge. He stepped forward.

He reached into his belt, not for a weapon, but for a small glass vial he'd pocketed in the last chapter.

Fungicide. High concentration. Designed for wood rot, but effective on any carbon-based fungal bloom if applied directly to the substrate.

He smashed the vial on the floor between the spider's front legs.

The liquid hissed. White smoke rose. The spider shrieked, a sound like tearing metal, and recoiled.

**"SYSTEM ALERT: UNAUTHORIZED CHEMICAL APPLICATION,"** the Authority's voice boomed. **"CROSS-CONTAMINATION PENALTY IMMINENT."**

Dale felt the debuff hit him.

A wave of nausea. His vision blurred.

The Field Guide in his mind flickered, the entry for *Arachnida Profundus* turning a sickly, contaminated gray. 40% debuff.

His movements would be sluggish. His aim, poor.

But the spider was confused. It had expected a hero with a sword. It got a technician with a spray bottle.

Dale clicked his pen. *Incident Report: Subject attempted breach of containment perimeter. Neutralized via localized hazard suppression.*

He looked at the Auditor.

"You didn't issue a permit for this trial."

The Auditor paused. The geometric shapes above slowed their rotation.

**"THE AUTHORITY DOES NOT REQUIRE PERMITS FOR INTERNAL DISCIPLINARY ACTIONS."**

"Wrong," Dale said. His voice was steady, despite the shaking in his hands.

He pointed the pen at the ceiling.

"Article 7, Section 3 of the Municipal Licensing Code.

Any action taken by a licensee outside their primary service area requires a Temporary Variance Form. Form 7-B.

I never filed one.

"

Silence.

The monsters stopped chittering.

**"FORM 7-B IS RESERVED FOR STRUCTURAL MODIFICATIONS,"** the Auditor said.

"It's about location," Dale said. "You brought me here.

To a location not on my map.

You changed the parameters of my job without notifying me. That's a violation of my labor contract with the Authority.

"

The Auditor's featureless face seemed to lean forward. **

"YOU ARE CLAIMING JURISDICTIONAL ERROR?"**

"I'm claiming you're out of scope," Dale said. He tapped the clipboard. "And I know the scope because I wrote the manual."

This was a lie, and he hadn't written the manual. But he had read it.

Every line.

Every footnote. Especially the footnotes about how the Authority handled internal disputes.

The Auditor flickered. For a second, the suit looked less like a suit and more like a stack of unpaid bills.

**"THE DEFENDANT'S ARGUMENT IS NOTED. JUDGE INFESTATION WILL RULE ON THE MERITS."**

The floor shook.

The courtroom shifted. The bone floor cracked, revealing a churning sea of black, writhing bodies beneath.

Thousands of pests.

Roaches, beetles, larvae.

A living, breathing judge.

And from the depths, a voice rose. Not a voice. A chorus.

**"GUILTY."**

Dale felt the weight of it. The System was trying to force a conclusion. If the Judge found him guilty, his license would be revoked.

Revocation meant death in this world. Without a license, he was just a man.

And men were food, and he needed a loophole. A big one.

He looked at his Field Guide. The entries were corrupted by the chemical penalty, but one remained clear. *Rattus profundus*. The first thing he'd ever killed. The first thing he'd documented.

He wrote: **APPEAL BASED ON PRIOR PRECEDENT.**

He tore the page off the clipboard. He crumpled it. And then he did something insane.

He threw it at the Judge.

The paper ball didn't land. It was intercepted by a swarm of flying ants that burst from the churning mass. They devoured the paper.

Dale watched them eat it. And then he watched the ants choke.

The paper wasn't just paper. It was saturated with his own saliva, his own DNA, and the residual magic of the Authority's arbitration seal. To the System, it was a biological contaminant.

**"CONTAMINATION DETECTED IN JUDICIAL EVIDENCE,"** the Authority announced. **"TRIAL VOID. RECALIBRATION REQUIRED."**

The churning sea froze. The Auditor looked down, its suit rippling.

**"YOU HAVE BROKEN THE PROTOCOL, KOWALSKI."**

"I applied a standard protocol," Dale said. "Sanitize the evidence."

The Auditor didn't move. But the air pressure dropped. Dale's ears popped. The monsters took a step back.

Then, the Auditor raised a hand. A blade of pure light formed in its fingers.

**"THE TRIAL IS SUSPENDED. BUT THE ARREST STANDS."**

Dale saw the move coming. He'd seen it in a thousand inspection reports. *Sudden structural failure due to external pressure.*

He dropped to his knees. Not in submission. In search of a gap.

The bone floor was fractured from the Judge's shift. There was a crack, three inches wide, running beneath the Auditor's position. Dark. Deep.

Dale dove, and he hit the edge of the crack and slid into it. The blade of light sheared off his shoulder bag, severing the strap. He tumbled into darkness, the sound of tearing fabric fading into a wind rush.

He fell for three seconds. Then he hit water.

Cold. Stagnant. Smelling of decay.

Dale surfaced, gasping. He was in a sewer. A real one.

Not a magical construct.

Concrete pipes. Rusty rebar.

He pulled himself onto a ledge, his shoulder screaming in pain. He checked his clipboard.

It was wet. The ink was running.

But the complaint was still readable.

*Subject: Unlawful Detention.* *Status: Pending Arbitration.*

He wiped the page with his sleeve.

A notification pinged in his peripheral vision.

**SYSTEM UPDATE: LICENSEE STATUS - SUSPENDED.** **TREATMENT POINTS: 0.** **FIELD GUIDE: LOCKED.**

He was off the grid. No abilities. No stats. Just a man in a sewer.

But he was alive.

And he wasn't alone.

A light appeared at the far end of the tunnel. A flashlight beam, steady and professional.

Dale froze. He knew that light. He'd used one just like it for fourteen years.

A figure stepped into the beam.

Wearing a hazmat suit. Yellow. Faded.

The logo on the chest was a skull with a broom.

*Exterminators United.*

The figure walked closer.

The face behind the visor was young.

Too young. No more than twenty-five.

Sharp features. Eyes that scanned Dale not with fear, but with calculation.

"You're sloppy," the figure said. The voice was filtered through the respirator, hollow and cold.

Dale coughed, spitting out black water.

"I was in the middle of a dispute."

"You caused a dimensional rift in a residential zone," the figure said. "And you voided a Class-IX arbitration. Do you know what that costs?"

"I don't care about the cost. I care about the nest."

The figure stopped five feet away. It raised a tool.

Not a sword.

A sprayer. The tank was pressurized, humming with a low, dangerous frequency.

"The Authority sent me to clean up the mess," the figure said. "My name is Miller. I'm Level VI."

Dale's heart stopped. Level VI. He was Level IV. And suspended.

Miller tilted his head.

"You're the one who wrote the complaint?"

"I wrote the truth."

"Truth is inefficient," Miller said.

He adjusted the nozzle of his sprayer.

"The Authority doesn't want thoroughness, Kowalski.

It wants containment.

And you're a leak.

"

Dale looked at Miller. Looked at the sprayer. Looked at the narrow tunnel.

He had no weapons. No protocols. No Field Guide.

But he had knowledge. He knew the layout of the municipal sewage system in this county. He knew where the valves were. He knew where the backups were.

He knew that Miller, despite his higher level, was standing in the wrong spot.

Miller was standing on a grate.

Dale shifted his weight. His hand found a loose pipe fitting on the wall. Rusted. Brittle.

"If you're going to exterminate me," Dale said, "you need a permit."

Miller laughed. A dry, rasping sound.

"I'm the Authority now, Kowalski. I don't need permits."

"Then you're violating Article 12," Dale said. "Unauthorized use of chemical agents in a public utility."

Miller raised the sprayer.

"Goodbye, Dale."

Dale kicked the pipe fitting.

It snapped.

Water didn't burst out. Air did. A massive, pressurized vacuum from the main line, triggered by the rupture in the vacuum seal.

The grate beneath Miller's feet sucked open.

Miller didn't fall. He activated a protocol. A blue shield flared around him, anchoring him to the wall.

But the suction caught Dale, and he grabbed the pipe with one hand, the clipboard with the other. His boots lost traction. He was being dragged toward the grate. Toward the dark, churning flow of the city's waste.

Miller looked at him, safe on his shield.

"Pathetic."

Dale looked at the clipboard. The wet page. The running ink.

He tore off the top page. The one with the complaint.

He crumpled it.

And he jammed it into the crack in the pipe where the fitting had snapped.

The air pressure changed. The vacuum seal broke completely.

The suction reversed.

Water exploded out of the pipe, hitting Miller's shield.

It wasn't water. It was raw sewage, mixed with the alchemical runoff of the Authority's own dimensional filters.

Miller's shield flickered. It wasn't designed to handle organic decay. It was designed to handle energy attacks.

The shield shattered.

Miller screamed as the filth hit him. He slipped.

Dale didn't watch him fall. He climbed.

He pulled himself up the rusty ladder that hung from the broken pipe. His muscles burned. His shoulder throbbed.

He climbed out of the hole, into the main tunnel, and he was alone again. But not for long.

His vision cleared. The System notification returned.

**SYSTEM ALERT: LICENSEE DETECTED IN UNAUTHORIZED ZONE.** **PENALTY: -50 TP.** **CURRENT TP: 0.**

He was at zero. If he went negative, he died.

He checked his pocket. The pen was gone. The clipboard was gone.

All he had was his hands.

And then he heard it.

Not from the tunnel. From above.

A grinding sound. Heavy. Metallic.

He looked up at the ceiling of the sewer.

Something was cutting through the concrete.

Sparks fell like rain.

A shape emerged. Not a monster.

A tank.

A municipal service vehicle. Painted in the colors of the City Public Works.

The hatch opened.

Dale braced himself.

A rope ladder dropped down.

A voice floated down. Muffled. Familiar.

"Kowalski! You idiot! I saw the seismic spike! Are you alive?"

Dale looked at the rope. He looked at the tank.

It was Vane's tank.

But Vane wasn't supposed to be here. Vane was with the Resistance. Vane had tried to stop him.

Dale climbed up, and he reached the hatch. He pulled himself into the cab.

The cab was empty.

The engine was running.

The dashboard was covered in red lights.

And in the driver's seat, sat a man.

Not Vane, and the man was wearing a suit. But it was torn. Muddy. Covered in the same filth that had coated Miller.

The man looked at Dale. His eyes were wide. Terrified.

"They're coming," the man whispered. "All of them.

The Authority.

The Resistance.

The Monsters. Everyone is coming for the one who broke the trial.

"

Dale sat down. He closed the door.

He looked at the ignition key.

"Who are you?" Dale asked.

The man laughed. A broken, hysterical sound.

"I'm the guy you fired.

Three years ago. For stealing the company truck.

"

Dale remembered. Gary. The apprentice who'd been caught using the fuel for his personal van.

"Get out," Dale said.

"No," Gary said.

He turned the key.

"I'm driving. You're suspended.

You can't operate municipal vehicles.

I'm the only one who can.

"

Dale looked at the rearview mirror.

Behind them, the sewer tunnel was filling with light. Blue, green, red. Dozens of protocols activating.

And in the distance, the sound of thousands of wings.

Gary slammed the gear into drive.

"Hold on," Gary said. "We're not going to the Authority."

"Where are we going?" Dale asked.

Gary grinned. A wild, dangerous grin.

"We're going to the place where the bugs are the worst. The place where the Authority can't send its lawyers."

Gary floored the accelerator.

The tank lurched forward.

Dale grabbed the dashboard. He looked at the empty space where his clipboard used to be.

He didn't have a report, and he didn't have a license.

But he had a ride.

And for the first time in his life, Dale Kowalski didn't know what the next step was.

The tank hit the ramp.

They flew into the night.
