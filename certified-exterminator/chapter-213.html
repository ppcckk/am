Chapter 213: Audit of the Dead

# Chapter 213

The clipboard was gone.

Dale remembered holding it. He remembered the weight of the pen.

He remembered the specific, satisfying click of the latch on the back.

Now, only the strap remained, cutting into his wrist where the webbing had fused to the leather. His hands were pinned to his sides by strands of black silk that pulsed with a rhythmic, wet thrum. They smelled of copper and rot.

They smelled of old things.

Dale tried to inhale. The air was thick, viscous.

It coated his throat like syrup.

He coughed, a dry, hacking sound that echoed in the hollow space. The sound did not bounce back. It was absorbed.

Swallowed.

Panic tried to rise.

Dale pushed it down.

Panic was inefficient. Panic was a waste of calories.

He counted his breaths. One. Two.

Three. His heart hammered against his ribs, a frantic bird trapped in a cage of bone.

The web tightened.

The pressure increased. It was a slow squeeze, steady and inevitable. Like a hydraulic press.

Like a code violation being enforced.

His vision narrowed. The edges blurred into a static haze.

The darkness pressed in.

Not just the absence of light, but a presence. A weight.

*You are meat,* the voice whispered.

It was not a voice. It was a vibration in the silk. A frequency.

*Meat for the brood.*

Dale's fingers twitched. He needed to document.

He needed to identify.

The system demanded it.

Without identification, there was no weakness. Without weakness, there was no elimination.

Without elimination, there was death. Simple logic. Clean logic.

But the pen was gone. The clipboard was gone.

*License Suspended,* the red text had flashed. *Pending Revocation.*

The penalty was severe. Without a license, he was not a technician.

He was not a professional.

He was just another organism in the ecosystem. And the ecosystem did not forgive unauthorized access.

Dale closed his eyes.

He reached for the memory of the Field Guide. It was not physical.

It was neural.

A database etched into his mind by the Authority. He pulled up the entry for *Arachnidus imperator*. The Emperor Spider.

No, not Emperor. Something older.

Something that ate emperors.

*Unknown species. Classification: Predator Apex. Threat Level: Catastrophic.*

Useless.

The web tightened further.

A rib cracked. A sharp, bright pain flared in his chest.

Dale bit down on his tongue. The taste of iron flooded his mouth. Good.

Pain was data. Pain indicated proximity to structural failure.

He had minutes.

Maybe less.

He needed a workaround.

The System was rigid. It rewarded protocol.

But protocols were written for humans.

This creature was not human. It was a dimensional anomaly. It operated on different rules.

It fed on fear, on chaos, on the disorganization of the prey.

Dale stopped struggling.

He went limp.

The web responded instantly.

It loosened.

Just a fraction. Just enough for his lungs to expand.

A tiny gasp of air. Not enough to survive long, but enough to think.

The voice changed.

*Curious.*

*Prey usually struggles.*

Dale opened his eyes. He stared into the darkness above him.

He could not see anything.

But he could feel the vibration. The chittering was closer. Many legs.

Many eyes.

Approaching.

He did not speak.

He did not shout. He simply lay there. Still.

Silent. Contained.

He thought about the last inspection.

The duplex.

The German cockroaches. They did not fight the exterminator.

They hid. They waited. They adapted.

That was their survival strategy. Not aggression.

Avoidance.

Dale applied the same strategy. He became an object. An inanimate thing.

The web pulsed. The hunger receded. The creature was confused.

It had expected movement.

It had expected the electrical spikes of terror. It had received nothing.

Only static.

*What are you?* the voice asked.

Dale waited. He held his breath. He slowed his heart rate.

He forced his body into a state of dormancy. A hibernation.

A pause.

*Inspection,* Dale thought.

He projected the word. Not with his voice.

With his intent. With his professional stubbornness.

The web shuddered. The strands vibrated.

*Inspection?*

*Protocol?*

The confusion in the web was palpable. The creature was a hive mind.

A collective intelligence.

It processed data. It categorized. It sorted.

And here was a variable that did not fit the category *Prey*.

It was not *Food*. It was not *Threat*.

It was *Bureaucracy*.

And the Authority hated bureaucracy? No. The Authority *was* bureaucracy.

A crack in the logic. A loophole.

Dale focused on the sensation of the web. It was not just silk. It was a medium.

A conduit. If he could send a signal through it, he could communicate.

Not in words.

In status.

He pushed the concept of *Certification* into the vibration. He pushed the concept of *Class II Technician*. He pushed the concept of *Valid License*.

The web went slack. slack.

Dale fell.

He hit the ground. The web released him.

He rolled, gasping, coughing up black slime. He scrambled to his feet. His knees buckled.

He caught himself on a protrusion of rock. His hands were raw.

His skin was peeling where the silk had adhered.

He looked up.

The darkness was absolute. But something was there. A shape.

Massive.

Unfolding. Legs like pillars.

Eyes like stars.

But it was not attacking.

It was watching.

And it was waiting.

*Explain,* the voice said.

Dale wiped his mouth. He spat out a chunk of black clot. He straightened his tie.

It was ruined. But the knot was still centered.

"I am Dale Kowalski," he said.

His voice was hoarse.

It cracked. But it was steady.

"Class II Licensed Pest Control Technician. Ohio Municipal Authority. Permit Number 44-B."

Silence.

Then, a low rumble. The ground shook.

*Permit?*

*Valid?*

Dale pulled out his wallet. His hands shook.

He fumbled for the plastic card.

The laminated ID. It was intact. He held it up.

Even in the dark, he could feel its presence.

A small, hard rectangle of authority.

"The Authority recognizes this permit," Dale said. "It grants me the right to enter, inspect, and treat. Any organism that prevents this inspection is in violation of Ordinance 7-C: Obstruction of Regulatory Compliance."

The creature paused.

*Ordinance 7-C?*

"Subsection four," Dale continued.

He was improvising. He was making it up. But the System was listening.

The System loved rules. The System loved categories.

If he could categorize this monster as a *Violation*, he could treat it as a *Pest*.

"Non-compliant entities are subject to immediate remediation," Dale said. "Failure to comply results in escalation to Class III force."

The web rippled. The creature shifted.

*Escalation?*

"Yes."

*And what is Class III?*

Dale didn't know. He didn't have it. He was Level II.

But the creature didn't know that.

It was a creature of instinct, of hunger. It understood power.

It understood hierarchy. And it did not understand the specific details of Dale's license. It only understood the *concept* of authority.

It hesitated.

That hesitation was his chance.

Dale looked around. He was in a cave.

A vast, cavernous space.

The walls were lined with cocoons. Thousands of them. White sacs, pulsing with life.

He walked toward them.

The creature did not stop him. It watched.

Dale approached the nearest cocoon.

He reached out. His fingers touched the silk.

It was warm.

It was beating.

He pulled out his pen.

It was not his pen. It was a stick.

A piece of bone.

He didn't know where he had picked it up.

It didn't matter.

He wrote on the side of the cocoon.

*Species: Unknown.*

*Status: Infestation.*

*Action: Documentation.*

The System notification pinged.

**ACTION DETECTED: DOCUMENTATION ATTEMPT.**

**ERROR: NO FIELD GUIDE ENTRY FOR TARGET.**

**BONUS: +5 THOROUGHNESS FOR PROACTIVE DATA COLLECTION.**

Dale kept writing. He described the texture of the silk. The temperature.

The vibration frequency. He noted the location of the cocoons relative to the entrance.

He noted the presence of the large predator.

The predator watched.

*Why do you mark the young?* the voice asked.

"To identify the threat," Dale said. "To isolate the vector."

*Vector?*

"The source of the problem," Dale said. "The nest."

He turned to face the giant spider.

"You are not the nest," Dale said. "You are the manager."

The spider chittered. A low, confused sound.

*I am the Queen.*

"No," Dale said. "The Queen is in the walls. You are in the center.

That is poor design.

Poor flow. It creates bottlenecks.

It invites contamination."

Dale pointed to the entrance. The hole he had fallen through.

"Your entrance is unsecured. Unsealed. It allows unauthorized entry.

It allows the Authority to track you. It is a security failure."

The spider shifted. Its massive legs clicked against the stone.

*Security?*

"Protocol," Dale said. "If you do not secure your perimeter, the Authority will classify you as a breach. And breaches are not treated. They are erased."

The word *erased* hung in the air.

The spider recoiled. Just an inch. But an inch.

Dale pressed the advantage.

"I can help you," Dale said. "I can teach you to seal the entry points. I can teach you to hide the evidence. I can teach you to pass inspection."

The spider paused.

*Why?*

"Because I need to leave," Dale said. "And I cannot leave through a hole that is not sealed. It is a safety hazard. It is a code violation."

The spider considered this.

The cocoons pulsed. The hive mind churned.

*You would protect us?*

"I would certify you," Dale said. "If you let me go."

The spider moved closer.

Its breath washed over Dale.

Hot. Wet.

Smelling of decay.

*And if you fail?*

"Then you die," Dale said. "And I lose my license. And we both lose."

The spider reached out. A leg, thick as a tree trunk, hovered over Dale's head. It could crush him. It could unmake him.

But it did not.

It touched his clipboard.

Wait.

The clipboard was not there.

But the spider touched the spot where it had been.

*Show me,* the spider said.

Dale reached into his pocket. He pulled out the ID card. He held it out.

The spider took it.

It held the card in its claw. It examined it.

*Plastic,* the voice rumbled. *Fake.*

"Plastic is durable," Dale said. "It resists moisture. It resists rot. It is professional."

The spider dropped the card. It landed on the stone.

*You are strange, Technician.*

"I am thorough," Dale said.

*The Authority does not like thoroughness. It likes results.*

"It likes documentation," Dale corrected. "Results are subjective. Documentation is objective. And the Authority is an objective entity."

The spider clicked its mandibles.

*Prove it.*

"How?"

*Seal the entrance.*

Dale looked back at the hole. Far above. Tiny. A pinprick of light.

He looked at the web. The vast, sticky network that filled the cave.

He looked at the cocoons.

He looked at the spider.

"I need materials," Dale said.

*Silk,* the spider said. *We have silk.*

"No," Dale said. "Not silk.

Silk is a structure.

It is a trap. I need *barrier*. I need *sealant*.

I need something that blocks the dimensional resonance."

The spider tilted its head.

*What is resonance?*

Dale closed his eyes. He thought about the Rift.

He thought about the way the air had tasted when he fell.

Like ozone. Like burnt sugar.

"The air here is wrong," Dale said. "It vibrates at a frequency that hurts.

That attracts the Authority.

You must stop the vibration.

You must dampen the signal."

*How?*

"You must change the environment," Dale said. "You must introduce a foreign agent."

The spider chittered. *Foreign agent?*

"Acid," Dale said. "You are an acid spider. Your venom is not just for digestion.

It is a corrosive. It eats through reality.

Use it to etch the walls.

Use it to dissolve the connection to the Rift."

The spider looked at its own claws.

*We use acid to feed.*

"Then you must feed on the walls," Dale said. "Eat the stone. Eat the connection. Sever the tie."

The spider hesitated.

*That will weaken us.*

"It will hide you," Dale said. "A hidden infestation is a safe infestation. A visible one is a target. Which do you prefer?"

The spider looked at the cocoons. At its young.

*Safe,* the voice said.

"Then eat the walls," Dale said. "Start at the top. Near the entrance. Break the ring."

The spider moved.

It climbed the wall.

Its legs found purchase on the slick stone. It reached the edge of the hole.

It opened its mouth.

And it vomitted acid onto the rock.

The stone hissed. Smoke rose. The smell of sulfur filled the cave.

Dale watched.

He picked up his ID card. He put it back in his wallet.

He pulled out his pen. He wrote on the back of his hand.

*Protocol Initiated: Environmental Modification.*

*Objective: Concealment.*

*Status: In Progress.*

The System notification flashed.

**ACTION RECOGNIZED: ADAPTIVE STRATEGY.**

**THOROUGHNESS INCREASED: 10/10.**

**CERTIFICATION PROGRESS: 15%.**

Dale smiled. A small, tight smile.

He had worked the system. He had used the Authority's own bureaucracy against a monster. He had turned a survival situation into a compliance project.

But the spider was not done.

It finished the first line of acid. The stone dissolved.

A new hole opened in the wall.

A smaller hole.

A secret exit.

The spider turned to Dale.

*The entrance is sealed,* the voice said. *But the exit is open.*

Dale looked at the new hole. It was small. Too small for him.

*You said you would leave,* the spider said.

"I said I would leave through a sealed entrance," Dale said. "This is not sealed. This is a new infestation."

The spider chittered. *You are difficult.*

"I am thorough."

*Then close the new hole.*

Dale looked at the acid. He looked at the stone.

He didn't have acid.

He looked at the cocoons.

He walked toward them.

The spider watched.

Dale took one of the cocoons. It was small. The size of a football.

He held it. It was warm.

It was alive.

He walked back to the new hole.

He threw the cocoon into the hole.

The spider lunged.

*What are you doing?*

"Plugging it," Dale said.

The spider froze.

*That is my child.*

"It is a plug," Dale said. "It is biodegradable. It will decay.

It will seal the gap.

It is temporary. But it will buy you time."

The spider stared at Dale.

*You kill my young?*

"I use available resources," Dale said.

"In an emergency, containment is priority. The young are expendable. The nest is not.

That is protocol."

The spider's legs trembled.

*You are a monster, Technician.*

"No," Dale said. "I am a technician. And you are violating the rules of your own habitat. Fix it."

The spider looked at the cocoon in the hole. It looked at Dale.

It did not attack.

It turned back to the wall. And it eated the stone again.

Dale stood there. He watched. He documented. He wrote.

And then, he heard it.

A sound from above.

Not from the hole he had fallen through.

From the ceiling.

A scratching.

A tapping.

*Knock, knock.*

Dale looked up.

The spider looked up.

*Who is that?* the voice asked.

Dale didn't answer.

He looked at the ceiling.

The acid had eaten a hole. And through that hole, something was watching.

Not a spider.

Not a rat.

A face.

Pale. Human.

Smiling.

*Hello, Dale,* the face said.

It was his supervisor.

But his supervisor had died.

The face winked.

*The Authority is here to audit.*

Dale's blood ran cold.

The spider froze.

The cocoons stopped pulsing.

The silence was absolute.

And then, the face spoke again.

*Open the door.*
