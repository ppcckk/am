Chapter 391: Audit Failed Appeal

# Chapter 391

The air smelled of ozone and wet copper.

Dale floated in the void. The black slime pressed against his chest, warm and viscous.

It pulsed with a rhythm that matched his heartbeat, though his own heart hammered against his ribs.

He didn't struggle. Struggling wasted calories and oxygen, neither of which he had in abundance.

He checked his pockets. Empty.

The clipboard was gone.

"Technician Kowalski."

The voice didn't come from the air. It vibrated through the slime, through the skin, into the bone. It sounded like a printer jamming.

Dale blinked. Dust and mucus coated his eyelashes. He forced them open.

The eyes were everywhere. Dozens of them. White, glowing, pupil-less. They stared with the flat indifference of a security camera.

"State your certification level," the voice boomed.

Dale swallowed. His throat tasted like ash.

"Class Two," he croaked. "Licensed Pest Control Technician. Ohio State Board."

"Incorrect."

The slime tightened. Ribs creaked.

"Your field guide is empty. Your protocols are unverified. Your presence here constitutes an unauthorized entry into a Class Nine infestation zone. You are not licensed for this environment."

Dale gritted his teeth, and he looked for a weak point. A seam.

A joint.

Anything.

"I found the entry point," Dale said. "I sealed it with rebar and fire."

"Fire is not a sealing agent. It is an accelerant. You increased the biomass by forty percent."

The pressure on his chest doubled. His vision blurred at the edges.

"Correct the error," the voice demanded. "Document the species.

Identify the vector.

Eliminate the primary host.

Or be purged as part of the infestation.

"

Dale's fingers twitched. He couldn't move. He couldn't breathe.

*Think.*

The Authority didn't want a hero. It wanted a technician.

He closed his eyes. He ignored the crushing weight. He ignored the fear that screamed in the back of his skull.

He thought about the tunnel. The smooth, organic walls. The hand.

The arm. The mass.

*Rattus profundus.* Dimensional Vermin.

No. Too simple. The Authority had called it a Class Nine. That was higher than anything he'd faced.

He remembered the smell. Not just copper. Sweetness. Rotting fruit and sulfur.

*Mycopteris abyssalis.*

The name surfaced in his mind, unbidden.

A fungal-analogous parasite. Common in deep-earth excavation sites. Rare.

Dangerous.

"Identify," Dale whispered.

The voice paused.

"State the species."

Dale coughed. Black phlegm splattered against the slime.

"*Mycopteris abyssalis*," he said. "Fungal-analogous colony host. Vector: subterranean moisture and organic decay. Weakness: desiccation and UV radiation."

The pressure eased slightly.

"Partial credit," the voice said. "The species is correct. The weakness is incomplete. You are still inside the host."

"I know," Dale said.

The slime shifted. A tendril curled around his neck. It felt like a wet rope.

"You have failed the practical exam," the voice stated. "However, the audit is ongoing.

You have been granted provisional access to the Field Guide interface.

You have one minute to populate the entry. If you fail, your license is revoked. Permanently.

"

Dale's hands moved. He couldn't see them, but he felt them.

He reached into the air. His fingers brushed against something cold. Metallic.

A clipboard.

It hovered in the darkness, translucent and blue. A holographic overlay projected by the Authority.

Dale grabbed it, and the surface was smooth. No paper. Just light.

"Begin documentation," the voice said.

Dale's mind raced. He needed to fill out the form. Field Guide Entry.

*Species Name:* Already filled. *Habitat:* Deep earth. High humidity. *Diet:* Organic matter. Human tissue. *Reproduction:* Spore dispersal via tunnel networks.

He wrote it. Fast. His hands shook, but the system accepted the input.

*Weakness:* Desiccation. UV.

*Vulnerability:* Core cluster.

Dale paused. The core cluster. Where was it?

The hand had connected to the mass. The mass filled the tunnel. The eyes were on the mass.

But the arm.., and the arm was the conduit.

Dale looked up. The eyes were watching him.

But the mass pulsed.

A rhythm.

A pump.

The arm wasn't just holding him. It was feeding.

"The host is not the mass," Dale said aloud.

The eyes blinked.

"The host is the connection," Dale said. "The arm is the vascular system. Cut the arm, starve the mass."

"Insufficient data," the voice said. "Prove it."

Dale looked at the clipboard. He added a note.

*Structural Analysis:* The mass lacks independent circulation. It relies on the upper limb for nutrient transport from the surface entry point. Severing the upper limb disrupts homeostasis.

He hit submit.

The clipboard flashed red.

*Rejected. Insufficient evidence.*

Dale slammed his fist against the holographic surface. It rippled.

"Show me," Dale said. "Show me the anatomy."

The voice didn't answer.

Instead, the slime around him changed. It became transparent.

Dale gasped, and he could see inside the mass. Veins of black liquid pumped through the tissue. They led directly to the arm. To the hand holding him.

He saw the heart. A small, shriveled node near the wrist.

It wasn't the mass. It wasn't the eyes.

It was the hand.

"The hand is the heart," Dale said.

The transparency faded. The slime returned, opaque and crushing.

"Data accepted," the voice said. "Provisional entry updated. Weakness: Primary Node (Wrist). Protocol: Severance."

Dale looked around. He had no knife. No saw.

He looked at the rebar. It was still there, embedded in the mass, holding the hand.

But he was holding the hand, and he couldn't cut it. He was attached to it.

He needed leverage.

"I need a tool," Dale said.

"Tools are part of the kit," the voice said. "Your kit was lost."

"I have my body," Dale said. "I have gravity."

The voice remained silent.

Dale looked up. The tunnel ceiling was high above. He couldn't see it, but he could feel the pull.

He wrapped his legs around the wrist. He squeezed.

The muscle beneath the chitin twitched, and he pulled himself down.

The eyes turned toward him.

"Technician," the voice warned. "Excessive force may result in collateral damage to your skeletal structure."

"I don't care," Dale said.

He hung from the wrist. His weight pulled the arm down. The mass shuddered.

The veins pulsed faster.

Dale gritted his teeth. He felt the bone in his spine compress.

He pulled harder.

The wrist bent.

The chitin cracked.

A green fluid sprayed from the break. It hit Dale's face. It burned.

He didn't flinch. He kept pulling.

The crack widened. The muscle fibers snapped with the sound of wet rope.

The arm detached.

The mass screamed. A low, subsonic vibration that rattled Dale's teeth.

The eyes went dark.

The slime lost its cohesion. It liquefied.

Dale fell, and he hit the bottom of the tunnel. The ground was soft. Mushy.

He lay there, gasping. The arm was gone. Severed. Dangling above him in the darkness, twitching.

The mass above collapsed. The eyes extinguished.

Silence.

Then, a notification pinged.

*Protocol Complete: Severance.* *Points Awarded: 450.* *Thoroughness Rating: 3.1.* *Note: Excessive collateral damage. Field Guide entry marked 'Draft'.*

Dale rolled over. He vomited black bile.

"Draft," he muttered. "Just draft."

He wiped his mouth. His hands were raw. His back ached.

He sat up, and the tunnel was still dark. But the air was moving. A breeze.

Fresh air.

He looked up. The hole he had entered was still there, but the mass had blocked it.

He had to climb, and he crawled toward the exit. The sludge slowed him. It felt like walking through molasses.

He reached the rebar. The hand was gone. The rebar stuck out of the ceiling.

Dale grabbed it, and he pulled himself up. His muscles screamed.

He climbed.

Higher.

The tunnel narrowed.

The light returned, and he burst out of the earth.

He landed in the crater.

The drone was still there. Hovering. Its beams flickered.

A voice spoke over the drone's speakers.

"Kowalski?"

Dale looked up. He was covered in black slime. His clothes were torn. One shoe was missing.

A figure approached. A man in a hazmat suit. Yellow. Clean.

The man stopped ten feet away.

"You're alive," the man said. He sounded surprised.

Dale pulled himself up. He swayed.

"Who are you?" Dale asked.

The man reached into his belt. He pulled out a scanner.

"I'm Auditor Vance," the man said. "Licensing Authority, Regional Division."

Vance held up the scanner. It beeped. Red lights flashed.

"Your license is under probation," Vance said. "You violated Protocol 7-C. Unauthorized entry. Improper containment."

"I saved the site," Dale said. "The host is dead."

"The host is *partially* dead," Vance corrected. "You severed the limb. You did not terminate the organism. The root system remains."

Vance pointed to the ground, and the soil was bubbling. Small, white tendrils pushed up from the dirt.

Spores.

"You have created a secondary infestation," Vance said. "By severing the host, you triggered a stress response. The colony is dispersing."

Dale looked at the ground. The tendrils were multiplying. Fast.

"I need to treat them," Dale said.

"You don't have a kit," Vance said. "You don't have a license for Class Nine. You don't have authority."

"I have a clipboard," Dale said. He reached for his back pocket.

It was empty.

"No," Vance said. "You have a debt. The Authority does not grant free passes. You are now in arrears."

Vance held out a hand.

"Give me your Field Guide access token."

Dale stared at him.

"What?"

"The token. The digital key that allows you to view and edit your guide. Surrender it, and I will cover the clean-up. Refuse, and I will revoke your license and detain you for reckless endangerment."

Dale's hand went to his pocket. He felt for the small, metallic chip that had been embedded in his wrist during his initial certification.

It was gone.

Vance smiled. A thin, cruel smile.

"I see you've lost it," Vance said. "That's convenient. But it doesn't absolve you."

Vance stepped closer.

"Tell me where the token is, Kowalski. And tell me what you saw inside."

Dale looked at the spores. They were spreading. Reaching toward the treeline. Toward the suburban houses.

"If I tell you," Dale said, "will you treat the spores?"

"That depends on your cooperation," Vance said.

Dale looked at the drone. It was recording.

He looked at Vance, and the man was waiting.

Dale's jaw tightened.

He thought about the eyes. The Authority logo on the palm.

*Your field guide is incomplete.*

He thought about the Thoroughness Rating. 3.1.

Sloppy work, and he thought about the spores.

If he didn't act, the neighborhood would be overrun.

If he acted, he might lose his license.

Without a license, he couldn't fight. He couldn't protect. He couldn't even spray for ants.

He was nothing.

"I don't have it," Dale said.

Vance's expression hardened.

"Then you are unauthorized."

Vance tapped his earpiece.

"Unit Four, move in. Secure the technician."

The drone dipped lower. Its beams charged.

Dale looked at the ground. The spores were inches from his feet.

He had no tools. No weapon. No license.

But he had a habit, and he had a protocol.

Dale dropped to his knees.

He didn't fight Vance. He didn't run.

He dug.

His bare hands tore into the earth. He pulled up fistfuls of dirt and spores.

He piled them in front of him.

Vance watched. Confused.

"What are you doing?"

"Isolation," Dale said. "Standard containment. Physical barrier."

He dug faster. His nails broke. Blood mixed with the black slime.

He formed a trench. A moat.

The spores hit the dirt. They stopped.

Vance lowered his scanner.

"You're creating a breach in the soil structure," Vance said. "This is illegal."

"It's a firebreak," Dale said. He kept digging.

The spores crept around the edge of the trench.

Dale scooped more dirt. He threw it into the moat.

He was building a wall.

Vance watched. The drone hovered closer.

"You're wasting energy," Vance said. "The spores are airborne. They'll go over the wall."

Dale stopped. He looked up.

He reached into his mouth. He pulled out a small, hard object.

It was the rebar splinter. He had swallowed it. Or maybe it had been lodged in his gum.

He spat it out, which was a shard of rusted iron.

He held it up.

"Metal," Dale said. "Conductive."

Vance frowned.

"What?"

"The drone," Dale said. "It's firing ion beams. Charged particles."

Dale looked at the drone.

"Charged particles are attracted to conductors."

Dale threw the splinter.

It wasn't a good throw. It wobbled.

But it hit the drone's sensor array.

The drone flickered.

"Stop," Vance said. "That's destruction of Authority property."

Dale stood up. He grabbed the edge of the trench.

"The spores are biological," Dale said. "They respond to electrical fields. Like neurons."

He looked at the drone.

"Overload the emitter."

Vance's eyes widened.

"Kowalski, don't."

Dale looked at the spores. They were pulsing.

He looked at the drone, and he looked at Vance.

"Class Nine," Dale said. "Requires Class Nine containment. Or a Class Nine accident."

Dale stepped into the trench, and he raised his arms.

"Overload," he said.

The drone fired.

The beam hit Dale's chest.

White light engulfed the crater.

Vance screamed.

The spores ignited, and the world went white.

When the light faded, Dale was gone.

The crater was empty.

Only the drone remained, smoking.

And on the ground, where Dale had stood, a single item lay.

A clipboard.

Solid. Real.

Vance walked toward it. His boots crunched on the scorched earth.

He picked it up, and he opened it.

The pages were blank.

Except for one line, written in fresh, wet blood.

*Audit Failed. Appeal Filed.*

Vance stared at the words, and he looked up at the sky.

The Rift was wider now.

Something was pulling itself through.
