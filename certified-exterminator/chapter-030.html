Chapter 30

The Field Guide rejected his fourth entry.

Dale stared at the notification. Source sample required for isolation documentation. Source sample required. Source sample required. He'd been trying to log the NEXUS boundary for eleven minutes and every attempt bounced back with the same error.

He set his clipboard down. Picked it up. Set it down again.

The black soil pulsed at its edges like a slow heartbeat. The countdown sat at one hour and thirty-nine minutes. Voss sat on an overturned crate twenty feet back, arms crossed, watching Dale with the particular stillness of someone who had already decided they were going to die and was now waiting to find out when.

"It's not accepting my readings," Dale said.

"Because it's not real?"

"Because I'm missing a data point." He flipped through his Field Guide entries. Cross-referenced the NEXUS classification against the isolation protocol checklist. Isolate. That was the problem. He'd identified it. He'd documented it. But isolation required a source sample. A physical piece of the infestation removed from the main body and logged within the treatment zone perimeter.

He looked at the NEXUS. Looked at the soil. Looked at the black surface that rippled when he shone his flashlight on it.

"I need to go in there."

Voss stood up. "No."

"I need a sample. A piece of the substrate. Something I can log as a source specimen."

"That thing is Level IX."

"The sample doesn't have to be big."

"It doesn't matter if it's the size of a grain of rice. You cross that line and you're inside its territory."

Dale knew that. He knew what territory meant in the Authority's system. It meant the organism's response radius. Anything that entered without proper containment protocol was treated as prey. He'd seen it happen to the Class IV beetle infestation in Chapter Twelve. A woman had stepped past the yellow tape because she wanted to check on her dog. The Authority logged her as "uncontained biomass." The beetles processed her in under four seconds.

He needed a sample. Without a sample, his isolation documentation was incomplete. Incomplete documentation meant his case was weak. Weak case meant the Inspector would rule the NEXUS a removal target.

Removal target meant the Authority would authorize a Class IX intervention. Dale didn't know what that looked like. He suspected it looked like everything within a quarter mile being erased from the dimensional layer.

He picked up his clipboard again. Started writing.

*Provisional Treatment Authorization. Filed under Protocol 7-C. Imminent structural threat. Technician requests temporary authority to treat target as active infestation pending completion of isolation documentation.*

He'd never filed a PTA before. He'd read about them in the Authority's reference manual during the written exam prep. They were designed for situations like termite damage to load-bearing walls. A technician who identified a structural threat could request emergency treatment authority before completing the full protocol cycle. The Authority granted it if the threat was immediate and the technician could demonstrate competence.

The notification appeared.

PTA REQUEST RECEIVED. EVALUATING.

Dale held his breath.

PTA GRANTED. CONDITIONS: (1) Technician must be physically present within treatment zone for duration of authorization. (2) Authorization expires in thirty minutes. (3) Failure to complete isolation documentation within window results in automatic PTA revocation and Thoroughness penalty of -2.0.

Thirty minutes. He had thirty minutes to get a sample, log it, and complete his isolation documentation. And the Inspector was coming in forty-five.

He looked at Voss.

"I'm going in."

"You're not."

"Watch me."

He pulled his gloves up. Checked the seals at the wrists. Pulled his N95 mask over his face and pressed the metal strip flat against his nose. Checked his boot laces. Double-knotted them. This was the part that took time. This was the part that made people think he was insane.

He didn't care. Protocol was protocol.

The NEXUS edge was about six feet from where he stood. The black soil didn't look like soil. It looked like something that had been poured into the shape of soil. It was too uniform. Too still. Real dirt had texture, had variation, had the random architecture of decay. This was smooth. Flat. Like a parking lot made of ash.

Dale stepped to the boundary. His boot touched the black surface and the Field Guide pulsed.

TREATMENT ZONE ENTERED. PTA ACTIVE. TIMER: 29:47.

The air changed. Not temperature. Not smell. Something else. A pressure behind his eyes. A low hum that wasn't sound so much as the absence of other sounds.

He crouched. Pulled a specimen bag from his belt. Opened it. Held it flat against the surface.

Nothing happened.

He pressed the bag down. The surface yielded slightly, like wet clay. The bag stuck. He pulled it up. A thin layer of black material clung to the plastic.

He sealed the bag. Held it up to his flashlight. The material inside writhed. Not like a living thing. Like a liquid that was trying to remember how to be solid.

He logged it.

SOURCE SAMPLE ACQUIRED. LOGGING.

The Field Guide pulsed green.

ISOLATION DOCUMENTATION COMPLETE. NEXUS: FULL PROTOCOL CYCLE ACTIVE.

He stood. His knees ached. He walked back toward the boundary line and the Field Guide pulsed again.

ISOLATION COMPLETE. TREATMENT ZONE NOW ACTIVE. ALL TREATMENT PROTOCOLS AVAILABLE FOR THIS TARGET.

Dale felt something he hadn't felt in a while. The specific relief of a form being complete. Every box checked. Every signature in place. The infestation was contained. The documentation was sound. He had a case.

He made it back to Voss's side of the boundary line and checked the timer. Twenty-three minutes left on the PTA. The Inspector's arrival window was now thirty minutes away.

Then the ground shook.

Not the slow pulse of the NEXUS. A sharp jolt, like something heavy had been set down nearby. Voss grabbed his arm.

"There."

Dale looked. The air about fifty yards east of the NEXUS was folding. Not tearing. Folding. Like someone was pressing the space between dimensions flat and creasing it along a line. The crease widened. Something stepped through.

It was a man. Or it was shaped like a man. It wore a grey suit that fit wrong, like it had been tailored for a slightly different geometry. Its face was correct. Symmetrical. Appropriate features in the right places. But the proportions were off by fractions. The eyes were set a millimeter too far apart. The jaw was half a degree too narrow. The smile it wore had one too many teeth.

The Field Guide opened automatically.

UNLICENSED ENTITY DETECTED. CROSS-REFERENCE: NONE. CLASSIFICATION: PENDING.

The entity walked toward them. Each step was precise. Not human-precise. The kind of precise that meant it had never been clumsy a day in its existence.

"Technician Kowalski."

Dale's pen stopped moving.

"You filed a contested removal request."

"Yes."

"You also filed a Provisional Treatment Authorization."

"Yes."

The entity stopped ten feet away. It looked at the NEXUS. It looked at Dale's clipboard. It looked at the specimen bag still clipped to his belt.

"Your documentation is complete."

"It is."

"That is unusual."

"I'm thorough."

The entity's too-wide smile widened.

"Technician Kowalski. I am The Inspector. I am here to evaluate the validity of your contested removal request and your PTA filing. I will be conducting a field audit."

"Understood."

"Your PTA expires in twenty-one minutes. Your audit window is fifteen minutes. These windows overlap. I will be observing your treatment protocol in real time. If your treatment is valid, your objection stands. The NEXUS remains under your authority."

"And if it's not?"

"Then your objection is denied. Your PTA is revoked. Your certification is reviewed for disciplinary action."

"Reviewed how?"

The Inspector tilted its head. The motion was too smooth. Like a camera on a gimbal.

"Certification review for a Class IX infestation typically results in revocation."

Dale felt the words land in his chest like a physical weight. Revocation. Not suspension. Not a fine. Revocation. Fourteen years of work. Every inspection report. Every violation code. Every frass trail and entry point and containment protocol. Gone.

"You have twenty-one minutes to demonstrate that your treatment protocol is appropriate for a Level IX target." The Inspector sat down on the ground. Cross-legged. Like a child watching a demonstration. "Begin."

Dale looked at the NEXUS. He looked at his Field Guide. He looked at the timer.

He opened his protocol toolkit. The Authority's interface responded, showing available treatments for the NEXUS. He scrolled through them. Baiting. Exclusion. Chemical treatment. Fumigation. Nest destruction.

Every option was designed for organisms. Creatures. Things with biology that could be targeted.

The NEXUS wasn't a creature. He'd known that from the start. It was a breach. A hole. A violation of dimensional code. You couldn't bait a hole. You couldn't fumigate a hole. You couldn't put a trap on a hole.

He stared at the options. None of them fit.

The Inspector watched.

Dale's pen moved. He wrote in his Field Guide. Not a protocol. A question.

*Is the NEXUS a creature or a violation?*

The Field Guide pulsed.

CLASSIFICATION: STRUCTURAL BREACH VIOLATION (SBV). NOT A CREATURE. NOT AN ORGANISM. NOT SUBJECT TO BIOLOGICAL TREATMENT PROTOCOLS.

Dale stared at the words.

Structural Breach Violation. He'd never encountered that classification. He'd never even heard of it. But the Authority had logged it. It was in his Field Guide now. A new entry. A new violation type.

And it meant something he hadn't considered.

He looked at the Inspector.

"I need a different protocol."

The Inspector's smile didn't change. "Specify."

"If this is a structural violation, I need a structural treatment. Not a biological one."

"Structural treatments require a Class VII certification or higher."

"I know."

"You are Class IV."

"I know."

"Then you cannot treat this target."

Dale looked at the NEXUS. At the timer. At the Inspector's too-wide face.

He looked at his Field Guide. At the SBV entry. At the protocol options listed under it.

There was one. Just one.

SEAL.

Not a treatment. A sealing protocol. The kind used for structural entry points. The kind Dale had used ten thousand times on kitchen foundations and basement walls and the tiny cracks where the outside world leaked in.

He could do that. He'd done that a thousand times. It wasn't a Class VII protocol. It was a Class II protocol. Basic. Fundamental. The kind of thing he'd been doing since his first year on the job.

But the Authority had listed it under the SBV entry. Which meant it was valid. Which meant he was authorized.

He opened his toolkit. Selected SEAL.

The notification appeared.

SEAL PROTOCOL SELECTED. REQUIRES: (1) Treatment zone fully documented. (2) Source sample logged. (3) Boundary marked with Authority-grade sealant.

Dale had all three.

He pulled the sealant from his kit. The Authority had issued it to him three chapters ago. He'd never used it. It looked like caulking. It smelled like nothing. It was supposed to work on dimensional boundaries the same way silicone worked on kitchen tile.

He started at the NEXUS edge. Applied the sealant in a continuous bead. Pressed it flat with his gloved thumb. Moved to the next section.

The Inspector watched.

The black soil resisted. The sealant didn't stick the first time. It slid off like water off wax. Dale wiped it. Applied again. Pressed harder. The sealant bit.

He moved along the edge. One meter. Two. Three. The timer counted down. Fifteen minutes. Twelve. Nine.

The NEXUS pulsed faster. The hum behind his eyes grew louder. Something was pushing against the boundary from the other side. Testing it. Looking for a gap.

Dale kept working. He'd done this ten thousand times. You don't rush a seal. You don't cut corners. You lay the bead, press it flat, check for gaps, and move on. Slow. Steady. Complete.

Six minutes. He was halfway around the perimeter.

Three minutes. Three-quarters of the way.

The NEXUS shuddered. A section of already-sealed boundary cracked. The sealant split. Black material seeped through.

Dale didn't stop. He went back. Reapplied. Pressed. Checked.

One minute.

He was on the last section. The final two feet of perimeter. The NEXUS was surging now. The hum was a roar. The ground around him was vibrating. Voss was shouting something he couldn't hear.

He laid the bead. Pressed it.

The sealant held.

The hum stopped.

The Field Guide pulsed.

SEAL COMPLETE. SBV CONTAINED. TREATMENT VALID.

The Inspector stood. It looked at the sealed perimeter. It looked at Dale. It looked at the timer, which read zero.

"Your objection is sustained."

Dale exhaled.

"However."

The word landed like a hand on his shoulder.

"Your PTA expired before your seal was complete. You exceeded the authorization window by four seconds."

Dale's stomach dropped.

"Under Authority regulation 14.7, any treatment completed outside its authorization window is classified as an unauthorized procedure."

"I finished it. It's sealed. It's done."

"The procedure was unauthorized."

The Inspector pulled something from its grey suit. A card. Small. White. It held it up.

"Your certification is suspended pending disciplinary review. You have forty-eight hours to report to the nearest Authority office for an in-person hearing. Failure to appear results in permanent revocation."

It dropped the card on the ground.

"Also." The Inspector looked at the sealed NEXUS. "That seal will hold for approximately seventy-two hours. After that, the breach will resume expanding. The seal is a temporary measure. It requires a permanent fix."

"Which requires what?"

"A Class VII structural protocol. Which you cannot perform. Which no one in this county can perform."

The Inspector turned. Walked toward the fold in the air.

"Your Thoroughness Rating remains 9.2. You are still the only person in this county qualified for the next tier of threats." It paused. "You just aren't qualified for this one."

It stepped through the fold. The air closed.

Dale stood in the silence. The sealed NEXUS sat behind him. A black disc ringed with white caulk.

Voss walked over. Looked at the seal. Looked at Dale.

"Seventy-two hours."

"Yeah."

"Then we need to find someone who can do a Class VII structural protocol."

Dale looked at the card on the ground. Picked it up. Read it.

*REPORT TO AUTHORITY OFFICE. 48 HOURS. FAILURE TO APPEAR = REVOCATION.*

Forty-eight hours to report for his hearing. Seventy-two hours before the seal failed.

He looked at the NEXUS. He looked at the card.

He started walking.
