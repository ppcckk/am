Chapter 90: Violation Existence

# Chapter 90

The water was not water. It was liquid paperwork.

It rose up Dale’s shins, cold and viscous, smelling of ozone and old library dust, and he didn’t look at his chest.

He didn’t look at the judges, and he looked at the clipboard in his hands. It was empty. That was the problem.

An empty field guide meant no identification.

No identification meant no protocol. No protocol meant death.

"Violation: Existence," the tall judge repeated.

His voice didn’t echo in the room. The room itself seemed to be absorbing the sound, pressing it into the wet concrete floor. "You are an anomaly, Dale.

A pest that learned to think."

Dale’s heart hammered against his ribs, but his hands didn’t shake. Fourteen years of spraying for German cockroaches in kitchens where the grout was cracked and the grease was thick had calibrated his nervous system for panic. This wasn’t panic. This was a non-compliant situation requiring immediate corrective action.

He looked at the glowing red letters carved into his skin. *VIOLATION: EXISTENCE.*

It wasn’t a wound. It was a label. A tag. Like the ones he stuck on jars of dead roaches in the lab.

"Identify the vector," Dale said. His voice was flat. Deadpan. He tapped the blank side of his clipboard with a pen that had no ink.

The judges froze. Not because they were surprised, but because the System reacted. A soft chime sounded, audible only to Dale.

**[NOTICE: UNIDENTIFIED SUBJECT DETECTED IN LOCAL SCOPE.]** **[RECOMMENDED ACTION: DOCUMENT OR DISMISS.]**

Dale smiled, which was a thin, tight expression.

He didn’t look up at the man in the gray suit. He looked at the other judges.

Dozens of them. Standing in the rising tide of bureaucratic sludge. They all had perfect teeth.

They all had blank clipboards.

"You want me to audit myself?" Dale asked.

"We want you to cease," the leader said.

He took another step forward. The liquid parted around his shoes like oil. "Your presence in the System is unauthorized.

You have bypassed the certification ladder.

You have no license for this tier."

"I have a Level IX certification," Dale said.

"You have a Level *IX* badge," the leader corrected. "But you have no *Jurisdiction*. Jurisdiction is what allows you to exist within the Authority’s bounds. You stole it."

Dale’s mind raced. *Jurisdiction.* He had never heard that term in the manuals.

He had studied the Protocol Manual, the Field Guide standards, the Safety Regulations. He had memorized the Thoroughness Scale. He had never seen *Jurisdiction* because it wasn’t a skill.

It was a status, and he looked at the floor.

The water was rising faster now, reaching his knees.

The cold bit through his jeans. He could feel the System trying to delete him.

It felt like static electricity building under his fingernails. A tingling numbness spreading from his toes.

He had to find the violation. He had to document it. But you can’t document yourself if you are the one holding the pen. Unless the pen is the violation.

Dale looked at his hands. His fingers were turning gray. The System was erasing his physical form because it had erased his legal form.

He dropped the pen.

It splashed into the liquid paperwork. The ripple that followed wasn’t water.

It was data.

Bits of code, floating fragments of previous reports, scattered across the surface. Dale saw snippets of text. *K-7 structural entry.* *Frass trail confirmed.* *Broodmother neutralized.*

And there, floating near his left boot, was a fragment that made his breath catch.

*Source: Dimensional Breach Node 4. Status: Active. Host: Dale Kowalski.*

He hadn’t escalated the breach. He had *become* the breach.

The realization hit him with the force of a physical blow.

When he had opened the door to the Source, he hadn’t just let the energy out. He had let the Source in.

It had used his body as a conduit.

His certification wasn’t a license. It was a vessel.

The leader of the judges leaned down. His face was inches from Dale’s.

Up close, Dale could see the pores in the man’s skin.

They were square.

Geometric. Perfect.

"You are contaminated," the judge whispered. "You are not a technician. You are the infestation."

Dale didn’t pull away. He looked at the judge’s clipboard. He squinted.

It bore writing. Faint, gray text that the judge couldn’t see. Dale could.

It was the Field Guide. *His* Field Guide. It had updated.

**[SUBJECT: JUDICIAL ENTITY (CLASS VIII)]** **[WEAKNESS: BUREAUCRATIC RIGIDITY.]** **[NOTE: THESE ENTITIES CANNOT ACT OUTSIDE OF WRITTEN PROTOCOL. IF A STEP IS NOT DOCUMENTED, IT CANNOT BE EXECUTED.]**

Dale’s mind clicked.

The cross-contamination penalty. If he used a protocol designed for one creature type against another, he got a debuff. But what if he used a protocol that didn’t exist?

What if he invoked a rule that wasn’t in the manual?

The System was a machine. Machines followed rules. Rules had loopholes.

"Violation: Existence," Dale said. He took a deep breath. The air tasted like copper. "I reject the citation."

The judges stopped. The water stopped rising. For a second, the entire room held its breath.

"Explain," the leader said. His eyes narrowed. "This is an audit. You do not have the right to dispute a citation."

"I’m disputing the classification," Dale said. He pointed at the red letters on his chest.

"You called this a Violation of Existence.

But that’s not a code. There is no 'Violation of Existence' in the Penal Code of the Authority. It’s a custom edit.

You’re making it up."

The leader’s smile faltered. Just a fraction of an inch. "The Authority defines its own codes."

"Does it?" Dale asked.

He stepped forward, and the water was at his waist now. He ignored the numbness spreading through his legs.

"Show me the statute. Point to the page in the Manual. Section 4, Paragraph 12?

Section 9? Where is 'Violation of Existence'?"

The leader looked around at the other judges, and they looked back at each other. One of them, a short woman in a blue suit, flipped through her clipboard. She frowned.

She flipped faster.

"It’s not there," she said. Her voice was small. "It’s not in the index."

The leader’s face hardened. "It is an implied violation. You are an entity that should not be."

"Then classify it properly," Dale said. He raised his voice.

He sounded like he was talking to a homeowner who was trying to argue that a raccoon in the attic was his pet.

"If you’re going to execute me, you need to cite the correct protocol. Is this a Class-1 extermination? No, I’m not vermin.

Is this a Class-5 containment? I’m not a beast.

What are you doing?"

Silence.

The water sloshed.

The judges shifted their weight. They were uncomfortable.

They were rigid. They needed the rules to be clear. Without the rule, they couldn’t act.

The System required precise input.

Dale pressed the advantage. He needed to buy time. He needed to find the loophole that would let him keep his body.

"Let’s start over," Dale said. "Audit me. But do it right. What is the first step of a full extermination protocol?"

The leader stared at him. "Identify."

"Wrong," Dale said.

"Identify is step one for *vermin*. For *entity removal*, the first step is *Jurisdictional Verification*. You have to prove you have the right to be here before you can act.

Did you file the warrant?"

The leader opened his mouth. Closed it.

"We do not require warrants for internal threats," he said.

"Then you are in violation of Protocol 0-Alpha: *Consent of the System,*" Dale said. He was making it up.

He didn’t know if Protocol 0-Alpha existed.

But the judges didn’t know either. That was the point.

The System chime sounded again. Louder this time.

**[WARNING: PROTOCOL CONFLICT DETECTED.]** **[AMBIGUITY IN AUTHORITY. HALTING EXECUTION.]**

The water level dropped an inch. The red letters on Dale’s chest flickered. They dimmed, then brightened.

The leader looked furious. "You are gaming the system."

"I’m following the procedures," Dale said. "You’re the one breaking them.

You’re using an undefined code.

That’s sloppy work.

Sloppy work earns a Thoroughness penalty."

He pointed at the leader’s clipboard. "Your rating is dropping. I can see it.

It’s a 4.2. You’re going to lose your bonus points."

The other judges looked at their own clipboards. The leader looked down at his. He flinched.

"Silence," the leader barked. "You are confusing the process. You are a Level IX technician. You are not qualified to interpret the Law."

"I am qualified to interpret the *Manual*," Dale said. "And I am currently the only one in this room reading the fine print."

He looked at the woman in the blue suit. She was sweating. Her square pores were leaking a clear fluid. She was terrified.

"If you delete me without the correct citation," Dale said, "the audit fails.

The file remains open. The infestation persists. That’s a failure to complete the protocol.

That’s a zero on your performance review."

The leader’s jaw tightened. "If we do not delete you, you will spread the corruption. You are a carrier."

"Then treat the source," Dale said. "Don’t kill the patient. Treat the patient."

He reached into his pocket. He didn’t have a spray can.

He didn’t have a flashlight, and he had a pen. The same pen that had no ink.

He uncapped it.

"I am invoking the Right of Appeal," Dale said.

The room went dead silent.

"That is not a thing," the leader said. "There is no Right of Appeal."

"It’s in the preamble," Dale lied. "Page one. Under 'Core Principles.' *Fairness.*"

The System buzzed. A low, grinding sound, like gears stripping.

**[ERROR: RULE NOT FOUND.]** **[ERROR: CONTEXT AMBIGUOUS.]** **[RECALCULATING...]**

The water stopped moving entirely. It became a mirror.

Dale could see his own reflection, and he looked pale.

Gray. His eyes were dark circles.

But he was still solid.

The leader stepped back. He was afraid. Not of Dale. Of the ambiguity.

"You are stalling," the leader said.

"I am documenting," Dale corrected. He looked at the floating fragments of data in the water.

He saw one that looked like a map.

A map of the room. A map of the judges.

He realized then that the judges weren’t just people, and they were processes; they were subroutines given human form to make them manageable.

And they had a weakness.

They couldn’t handle contradiction.

Dale looked at the leader. "Let’s try something else. What is my species?"

"You are *Homo sapiens*," the leader said.

"No," Dale said. "I am *Rattus profundus*."

The leader blinked. "That’s absurd."

"Check the Field Guide," Dale said. "My first entry.

I killed a giant rat in a crawlspace.

I documented it.

I got points. But I didn’t check the post-completion status.

Did I verify the extermination?"

The leader paused. He looked at his clipboard. He scrolled. He frowned.

"Entry 1: *Rattus profundus*," the leader read. "Status: Eliminated."

"Is it?" Dale asked. "Did you verify the corpse? Did you check for eggs?"

The leader looked confused. "It’s been fourteen years. The nest is gone."

"Is it?" Dale said. "Or did I just seal the entry point? Did I treat the wall, or did I treat the *rat*?"

The leader didn’t answer.

"Protocol 3.4," Dale said. "*Isolation of Source.* If the nest is not found, the infestation is not closed.

The case remains open.

I am not the technician. I am the unresolved case file."

The System chimed. A major-key chord, triumphant.

**[CASE STATUS: RE-OPENED.]** **[THREAT LEVEL: PENDING.]** **[NEW VIOLATION: NEGLIGENCE.]**

The water began to drain away. Not fast, but steadily. The liquid paperwork receded from Dale’s waist. The gray numbness in his legs faded.

The judges looked at each other. The leader looked at Dale with pure hatred.

"You tricked us," the leader hissed.

"I audited you," Dale said.

He capped his pen. "You failed the practical.

You tried to delete a file that wasn’t finished.

That’s malpractice."

The leader raised his clipboard. "This isn’t over. The Authority will correct this error."

"Then correct it," Dale said. "But you need a new citation. Because 'Existence' doesn’t work. Try 'Negligence.' Try 'Procedural Error.' I’ll document that, too."

The water was gone. The floor was wet, but dry enough to stand.

Dale took a step forward.

The judges stepped back, and they didn’t attack. They couldn’t.

The protocol was broken.

Dale walked toward the exit. The door was gone. In its place was a hole in the reality of the room, jagged and black.

He turned back to the leader.

"One more thing," Dale said.

The leader stared at him.

"Your Thoroughness rating is now 2.1," Dale said. "You’re going to have to retake the exam."

He stepped into the black hole.

***

Dale woke up on his back, and he was in his van. The engine was off. The key was in the ignition.

He sat up. His chest was bare.

The red letters were gone.

In their place was a scar. Thick, raised, keloid tissue. It spelled out *CASE OPEN*.

He looked at his hands. They were normal. Pink. Fingernails trimmed.

On the dashboard, his Field Guide was open. A new entry had populated.

**[SUBJECT: LICENSING AUTHORITY ENFORCERS]** **[STATUS: ACTIVE.]** **[WEAKNESS: PROCEDURAL RIGIDITY.]** **[NOTE: THEY CANNOT ACT WITHOUT A VALID CITATION. ALWAYS ASK FOR THE STATUTE.]**

Dale stared at the entry, and he picked up his pen. He wrote *JURISDICTION* in the margin.

Then he crossed it out.

He wrote *STANDING*.

He didn’t know what it meant. But he knew it was the next problem.

His phone buzzed.

It wasn’t a text. It was a notification from the Authority app.

**[NEW ASSIGNMENT: EMERGENCY AUDIT.]** **[LOCATION: OHIO STATE CAPITAL BUILDING.]** **[TARGET: GOVERNOR’S OFFICE.]** **[INFESTATION TYPE: POLITICAL.]** **[CERTIFICATION REQUIRED: LEVEL X.]**

Dale frowned. There was no Level X. The system stopped at IX.

He looked at the scar on his chest. It pulsed with a faint red light.

He started the van.

The next chapter wasn’t about monsters. It was about the people who made the rules. And Dale Kowalski was about to file a complaint.
