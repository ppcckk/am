Chapter 232: Suspect in the Sludge

# Chapter 232

The truck engine coughed, sputtered, and died.

Dale didn't panic. He didn't slam the steering wheel.

He simply checked the fuel gauge, then the battery terminals, because panic was an unauthorized emotion that slowed down diagnostic procedures.

The city skyline loomed ahead, jagged teeth of concrete against a bruised purple sky. Lights flickered in the windows of the Water Treatment Plant. Not the steady hum of industrial machinery, but the erratic pulse of a nervous system in shock.

The drone hovered near the passenger window, its optical sensors zooming in on the main gate.

"Gate sealed," the drone reported. "Industrial-grade locks. No keycard access."

Dale unbuckled his seatbelt. "I don't need a key. I need an entry point."

He stepped out into the pre-dawn chill.

The air smelled of chlorine, rust, and something sweet, like rotting fruit. That was the parasite.

It thrived in moisture.

It loved the pipes.

Walking to the service entrance, Dale pulled a roll of duct tape from his belt. It wasn't magic.

It was high-tensile, weather-resistant, municipal-grade adhesive.

He applied three strips across the electronic keypad, creating a makeshift Faraday cage.

Not for electricity, but for signal. The Authority's protocols relied on networked verification.

If the network was compromised by Gary's sloppy work, the local systems would default to analog failsafes.

Analog was predictable. Analog was safe.

A heavy metallic clank echoed from inside the facility. Something large moved in the darkness beyond the chain-link fence.

"Biological containment unit active," the drone noted. "Signs of unauthorized modification."

Dale tightened his gloves. "Modification means deviation. Deviation means contamination."

He approached the fence. The lock was a standard padlock, rusted at the shackle.

A hero would smash it.

A soldier would cut it with bolt cutters they didn't have. Dale examined the hasp. It was a Class-2 security fixture, notorious for a weak pin in the lower loop.

He inserted a thin screwdriver into the gap, twisted, and felt the pin give way.

The gate swung open with a groan.

Inside, the main pump hall was flooded. Not with water, but with a viscous, gray sludge that bubbled against the concrete walls.

The water treatment vats, usually sterile stainless steel, were coated in a pulsating membrane.

It looked like mold, but it moved with intent. Threads of it reached out, connecting the vats to the control panels.

Dale pulled out his Field Guide. He flipped to the entry for *Dimensional Vermin (Rattus profundus)*. Then he turned to the blank page labeled *Unidentified Aquatic Parasite (Provisional)*.

He didn't have a name for it. He didn't have a weakness listed. But he had a protocol for *Unknown Infestations*.

*Protocol 4-B: Isolate, Sample, Hypothesize.*

"Drone, scan for thermal signatures," Dale ordered. "Differentiate between the sludge and the machinery."

The drone emitted a low hum. "Thermal output is uniform. The sludge and the pumps are at the same temperature. They are merging."

Dale's jaw tightened. Merging meant integration. If the parasite integrated with the mechanical systems, it could control the water pressure. It could push itself into every tap in the city.

A voice echoed from the far end of the hall. It was wet, gurgling, but intelligible.

"Hey! You're late!"

Dale walked toward the voice. His boots crunched on the sludge.

It resisted, sticky and heavy, but he kept his stride even.

He wasn't running.

Running triggered the predator response in some species. Walking triggered the territorial response in others.

But this wasn't a territorial animal. It was a system failure.

At the center of the hall, standing atop a raised platform, was a man.

He was wearing a hazmat suit that had been modified with duct tape and scrap metal. The visor was cracked. One arm was replaced with a crude prosthetic made of pipe fittings and a rusted engine block. He held a wrench in one hand and a clipboard in the other.

Gary.

The man looked up, his eyes wide behind the cracked visor. He didn't look like a monster. He looked like a tired, desperate technician who had given up on doing things the right way because the right way took too long.

"Kowalski," Gary said, his voice distorted by the suit's failing audio speakers. "You're here to audit me? Save it. I'm already done."

Dale stopped ten feet away. "Done? The city is waking up, Gary. People are making coffee."

"Let them," Gary spat. "I sealed the main valve.

The pressure is holding.

The city gets water. The problem is contained."

"Contained?" Dale pointed to the pulsating membranes on the vats.

"You didn't eliminate the source. You just dammed it.

That's not containment.

That's a pressure buildup waiting to burst."

Gary stepped down from the platform. The sludge oozed around his boots.

"The Authority doesn't care about the source, Dale.

They care about the output.

Clean water. No monsters.

I achieved that. Fast. Efficient."

"And the Thoroughness Rating?"

Gary laughed, a harsh, barking sound. "Who cares about the Rating?

It's just points.

I'm keeping people alive. You're polishing your resume."

Dale didn't answer. He looked at the control panel behind Gary. The lights were blinking red, but the pressure gauge was green. A lie.

"Protocol 7-C," Dale muttered. "Bypass safety overrides. Direct manual injection."

He pulled a canister from his belt.

Not a spray. A concentrated enzymatic cleaner, Class-IV, designed for breaking down organic bio-films in industrial pipes.

It was expensive.

It was rare. And it was strictly forbidden for use on living tissue without a Level-V waiver.

Dale didn't have a Level-V waiver.

But he had a Level-III license. And he had a Field Guide entry that was half-written.

*Hypothesis: The parasite is not an animal. It is a colony organism. It functions as a single neural network. Disrupt the network, and the colony collapses.*

"Drone," Dale said. "Record my actions. Tag this as an Emergency Deviation Protocol."

"Warning," the drone chimed. "Using Class-IV enzymatic cleaner on *Dimensional Vermin* constitutes a Cross-Contamination Event. Penalty: 40% debuff to all subsequent protocols for 24 hours. Field Guide entry corrupted."

"Affirmative," Dale said.

He threw the canister at the base of the main pump.

Gary lunged. His rusted prosthetic arm swung the wrench.

It caught Dale on the shoulder, spinning him into the sludge.

Pain flared, hot and bright, but Dale didn't drop his clipboard.

He rolled, came up on one knee, and saw the canister shatter.

Green foam hissed across the floor. It didn't smell like bleach. It smelled like old blood and copper.

The sludge screamed.

Not a vocal scream. A vibration. The entire hall shook.

The membranes on the vats tore apart, revealing writhing, eyeless bodies beneath. They were massive, bloated things, fused with the metal pipes.

They weren't rats.

They were something else. Something that had eaten the rats and grown in their skin.

"Stop!" Gary yelled, firing a taser at Dale. The prongs hit Dale's chest, sending a jolt through his nervous system. Dale's muscles locked. He fell back, gasping, his vision blurring.

Gary stood over him, panting.

"You idiot. You triggered the colony defense response.

Now the pressure will blow.

The whole city will flood with this filth."

Dale coughed. His hand twitched.

He could feel the debuff kicking in.

His Field Guide was flickering in his mind, the text scrambled. *Entry Corrupted.

Identification Failed. Weakness Unknown.*

He couldn't see the weakness.

But he could see Gary.

Gary was standing on the main pressure valve. The valve that held the city's water back. The valve that Gary had "sealed."

Dale looked at the valve. It was covered in the same green foam. But the foam wasn't breaking it down.

The foam was *curing* it. The enzyme was reacting with the parasite's exoskeleton, hardening it.

Gary hadn't sealed the valve. He had turned it into a bomb.

"Dale," the drone whispered in his ear. "Pressure critical. 180 PSI. 190 PSI."

Gary smiled, though his face was pale beneath the visor. "See? I'm fixing it. I'm making it strong."

Dale struggled to his feet.

His legs felt heavy, like they were moving through tar. The cross-contamination debuff wasn't just a number. It was physical.

His coordination was shot.

He looked at Gary. Then he looked at the wrench in Gary's hand.

Gary had used a wrench to tighten the valve. A wrench was a tool for metal. But the parasite was organic. Tightening it too hard would crack the seal.

Dale didn't have a wrench. He had a clipboard.

He reached into his belt pouch and pulled out a heavy, steel-reinforced pen. Not for writing. For prying.

"Gary," Dale said, his voice flat. "Your seal is failing."

Gary looked down at the valve. A hairline fracture appeared in the hardened green crust. A jet of black water sprayed out, hitting Gary in the chest. He stumbled back, screaming.

The fracture spread.

Dale didn't run to the valve. He didn't try to fix it. He walked toward the control panel.

The panel was alive. Cables snaked into the panel, pulsing with the parasite's energy. To stop the pressure, Dale didn't need to close the valve. He needed to open the release.

But the release was locked.

"Drone," Dale said. "Bypass the digital lock. Use the analog override."

"Analog override is behind the panel. Access requires removing the faceplate."

Gary was on the floor, clutching his chest. The black water was eating his hazmat suit.

The parasite was inside.

He was becoming part of it. His eyes were changing, the pupils widening, the iris dissolving.

"Kill it," Gary gurgled, his voice becoming multiple voices. "Kill it all."

Dale ignored him. He pried the faceplate off the control panel with his steel pen. The metal groaned. Inside, a complex tangle of wires and valves.

He saw it. A small, red valve handle. Manual release.

He grabbed it. It was hot. Burningly hot.

Dale turned it.

Nothing happened.

"Seized," the drone said.

Dale gritted his teeth. He used his foot to kick the valve, trying to loosen the rust. His boot slipped on the sludge. He fell, his head slamming against the panel.

Blood dripped into his eyes.

The pressure gauge hit 210 PSI. The pipes shook. The building groaned.

"Emergency," Dale muttered. "Protocol 9-Z. Sacrifice of Equipment."

He pulled the steel pen from the panel. He jammed it into the gap between the valve handle and its housing. He twisted with all his strength.

The pen bent. It snapped.

But the handle turned.

A loud *hiss* erupted from the pipes. A cloud of steam and black sludge blasted out of the side of the building, shooting out into the night air.

The pressure dropped.

190 PSI.

180 PSI.

150 PSI.

The shaking stopped.

Dale lay on the floor, breathing hard. His suit was torn.

His skin was raw.

The debuff was still active.

He couldn't feel his left hand.

Gary was silent. The parasite had consumed him. His body was still, fused with the sludge.

The drone landed on Dale's chest. "Infestation contained. Localized. No spread to municipal supply."

Dale closed his eyes. "Rating?"

"Thoroughness Rating: 6.4."

Dale sat up. 6.4. A passing grade. Barely.

"Reason for deduction?" he asked.

"Unauthorized use of Class-IV chemicals. Cross-contamination. Incomplete documentation of secondary species. Failure to neutralize rival technician."

Dale looked at Gary's body. The face was gone. The visor was cracked. But the clipboard was still there.

He crawled over to it. He picked up the clipboard.

There was a report on it. Handwritten. Messy.

*Status: Contained. Notes: The bugs are smart. They're learning. They're watching us.*

And below that, a sketch.

It was a diagram of the city's water grid. But it wasn't just the grid. It showed lines connecting the water pipes to the electrical grid.

To the sewage system. To the gas lines.

And in the center, a symbol.

Not a bug.

A hand.

Dale stared at the symbol. He had seen it before. On the broodmother's shell. On the drone's chassis.

"Drone," Dale said, his voice quiet. "Is the Authority a single entity?"

"Affirmative."

"Then why is Gary's sketch showing the Authority's infrastructure as part of the infestation?"

The drone paused. For the first time, it didn't answer immediately.

"Processing," it said.

The lights in the plant flickered.

Then, a new notification appeared in Dale's peripheral vision. It wasn't blue.

It was red.

**ALERT: LICENSING AUTHORITY COMPROMISED.**

**SUBJECT: DALE KOWALSKI.**

**STATUS: QUARANTINE INITIATED.**

Dale looked at the door. The main exit was sliding shut. A steel blast door, descending from the ceiling.

He wasn't the technician anymore.

He was the suspect.

He grabbed his clipboard. He grabbed his wrench.

He ran for the service exit.

The door slammed shut with a finality that shook the floor.

Trapped.

And inside the dark, from the sludge, a voice whispered.

"Welcome to the nest, Dale."
