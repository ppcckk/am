Chapter 139: Request Denied

# Chapter 139

Dale snapped the carbon paper against the clipboard and tore off the top sheet. Dale pressed harder. The cheap plastic housing of his clipboard cracked under the pressure, but the form remained intact.

He wrote the date, and he wrote the time. He wrote the location: Suburban Residence, Basement Stairwell.

Above him, the thing wearing Marcus's face shrieked again. It wasn't a human sound. It was the wet, tearing noise of vocal cords being stretched beyond their biological tolerance.

Dale didn't look up. He looked at the blank space on the form labeled *Incident Classification*.

He paused.

Most exterminators would call this a Class IV Apparition. Standard haunting protocol involved burning sage and filing a noise complaint. But this wasn't a haunting. This was an impersonation.

The system rules were clear, but the application was ambiguous. An Auditor wasn't listed in the standard Field Guide. It was a glitch in the Authority's logic, a creature that fed on the gap between what was recorded and what was true.

Dale flipped to the back of his Field Guide. The pages were thick, laminated paper. He found the blank slot where *Species: Auditor* should be.

He hadn't filled it in yet.

The stairs creaked. A footstep. Then another. Heavy, wet slapping sounds, like bare feet stepping through mud.

"Marcus?" Dale called out.

His voice was flat, devoid of the terror tightening his throat.

"This is Dale Kowalski.

Licensed Technician, Class IV.

Please state your identity for the record.

"

Silence.

Then, a laugh. It sounded like Marcus's laugh, but pitched an octave too high.

"I'm here, Dale. I'm right here. Why are you writing? Why are you always writing?"

Dale didn't answer. He moved to the next line: *Subject Behavior*.

*Vocal mimicry. Emotional manipulation. Physical deformation consistent with grief trauma.*

He underlined *grief trauma*.

The system pinged in his peripheral vision. A translucent blue box appeared, hovering over the ash-covered floor.

**NOTICE: FIELD GUIDE ENTRY INCOMPLETE.** **IDENTIFICATION CONFIDENCE: 64%.** **TARGETED DAMAGE BONUS: UNAVAILABLE.**

If he couldn't identify it, he couldn't hurt it. The Auditor fed on uncertainty. The more doubt Dale showed, the stronger the creature became.

Dale needed to be certain. He needed to write it down as fact, not as observation.

He stood up. His knees popped.

The basement was cold, but sweat prickled his neck, and he kept his clipboard held at chest height, shielding his heart.

A professional habit. You protect the paperwork.

You protect the proof.

"Come up," the thing said. It was closer now.

Top of the stairs.

"Come up and see my face. It's perfect."

Dale took a step forward. He didn't raise his weapon. He raised his pen.

"I require visual confirmation," Dale said. "Please remain stationary."

"I'm not stationary.

I'm moving toward you. I'm coming to fix you, Dale.

You're broken.

You're just a clipboard with a pulse.

"

The thing stepped into the light of Dale's flashlight, which was Marcus.

Not the Marcus who had run away. Not the Marcus who had cried for his son.

This Marcus was smooth.

Too smooth.

His skin had no pores, no scars, no hair follicles. It looked like wax poured over a mold.

His eyes were wide, black, and unblinking. His mouth was too large, stretching from ear to ear in a smile that showed too many teeth.

But the clothes were wrong. The thing wore Marcus's jacket, but it was inside out. The tags stuck out. A code violation.

Dale's eyes flicked to the tag. *Size M. Made in Vietnam.*

Marcus was a size XL.

The discrepancy hit Dale like a physical blow. The Auditor hadn't just copied Marcus's face. It had copied the *idea* of Marcus. It had filled in the gaps with generic human details, making mistakes a real person wouldn't make.

*Weakness 3: Detail Inconsistency.*

Dale wrote it down. Fast. His hand shook, but the words were legible.

*Inconsistency: Garment fit and manufacturing origin do not match subject profile.*

The system pinged again.

**IDENTIFICATION CONFIDENCE: 89%.** **TARGETED DAMAGE BONUS: ACTIVE.** **PROTOCOL UNLOCKED: ADMINISTRATIVE REJECTION.**

A new option appeared in Dale's mental interface. It wasn't a spell. It wasn't a sword strike. It was a button.

*Reject.*

He could reject the creature's claim to existence. He could declare it invalid because its paperwork didn't match the subject's file.

But the button was grayed out.

**ERROR: INSUFFICIENT CERTIFICATION LEVEL FOR ADMINISTRATIVE REJECTION.** **LEVEL III PROTOCOL REQUIRED. CURRENT LEVEL: IV (LOCAL AUTHORITY ONLY).** **RESTRICTION: CANNOT REJECT A LOCAL ENTITY WITHOUT WRITTEN JUSTIFICATION FROM THE LICENSING AUTHORITY.**

Dale stared at the message. The Authority was still in charge.

Even in his own basement, he was bound by their rules.

He couldn't just delete the monster. He had to process it.

He had to file a variance request.

"Hey, Marcus," Dale said.

He kept his voice steady. He walked up the first step.

"Your jacket is inside out.

And it's the wrong size.

"

The creature's smile faltered. Just a fraction. The wax skin rippled.

"What?"

"You're wearing a size Medium," Dale said. "Marcus is an XL.

The tag says Vietnam.

Marcus buys his clothes at the sporting goods store on Main.

He's allergic to synthetic blends.

"

The creature looked down at its own chest. It touched the tag. Its fingers were long, spindly. Too many joints.

"I... I bought it new," the creature said. Its voice wavered. The joy in its tone curdled into confusion.

"You're lying," Dale said. He took another step up. The creature backed down.

"And you're wearing a tag that doesn't belong to you. That's a violation of dress code.

And it's a violation of identity.

"

The creature's eyes narrowed. The black pupils shrank to pinpricks.

"You're just a technician," it hissed. "You don't get to decide what's real."

"I decide what's *documented*," Dale said.

He reached into his belt. Not for a spray can. Not for a shotgun.

He pulled out a red stamp. *VOID*, which was a prop. A toy. Something he kept in his bag for when he needed to mark a job as canceled due to client cancellation.

It was useless.

It had no power.

But the system didn't know that.

The system recognized the *intent*. It recognized the *protocol*.

Dale slammed the red stamp against the creature's forehead.

There was no impact. The stamp passed through the wax skin like smoke.

But the creature flinched.

It screamed. A high, thin shriek that shattered the lightbulb hanging from the ceiling.

**PROTOCOL INITIATED: ADMINISTRATIVE REJECTION (IMPROVISED).** **STATUS: PENDING REVIEW.** **DAMAGE APPLIED: 0.** **EFFECT: CONFUSION.**

The creature wasn't dead. It wasn't even hurt. But it was *confused*.

It looked at Dale. It looked at the stamp. It looked at its own hands.

"This isn't right," it whispered. "The paperwork..."

"The paperwork is the truth," Dale said. He backed down the stairs. Slowly. Keeping the clipboard raised.

The Auditor stumbled. It tripped over its own feet. It fell onto the landing, limbs twisting in unnatural angles. It tried to stand, but it couldn't remember how legs worked.

Grief was a heavy thing. Bureaucracy was heavier.

Dale reached the basement floor. He didn't stop. He walked to the far corner, where the fuse box was.

He needed to cut the power.

Without light, the Auditor couldn't reflect. It needed a witness. It needed someone to believe it was Marcus. If Dale turned off the lights, the creature would have to choose between maintaining the illusion or revealing its true form.

He yanked the main breaker.

Darkness.

The screaming started again. But this time, it wasn't joyful. It was panicked.

"No! Don't look away! Don't stop looking!"

Dale closed his eyes. He counted to ten.

One. Two. Three.

He could hear the wet sounds of something trying to pull itself together. The sound of bones resetting.

Four. Five. Six.

He opened his eyes, and the basement was still dark. But a faint blue glow emanated from his clipboard. The form he had written on was glowing.

*Incident Report: Submitted.*

A new notification appeared.

**JOB COMPLETE? NO.** **STATUS: PENDING.** **REASON: SUBJECT ESCAPED LOCAL JURISDICTION.**

Dale frowned. Escaped? The creature was on the stairs. It couldn't have left the house.

Unless, and he looked at the stairs. The Auditor was gone.

The landing was empty.

But the wall at the top of the stairs was rippling. Like water. Like a curtain.

Something was pulling itself through the plaster.

And then, a voice. Not the Auditor's voice.

Marcus's voice.

Real Marcus.

"Dale?"

Dale spun around.

The door to the basement stairs was open. The front door of the house was open.

Marcus stood in the doorway, silhouetted by the moonlight. He was covered in ash. He was bleeding from a cut on his forehead. He looked terrified.

He wasn't alone.

Behind him stood three figures in gray suits. They held clipboards. Their faces were blank, smooth like the Auditor's, but their posture was rigid.

Dale felt his stomach drop.

The Auditor hadn't escaped. It had *filed a complaint*.

And the Authority had sent inspectors.

Marcus looked at Dale. His eyes were wide, wet, and full of hope.

"Did you kill it?" Marcus asked. "Is my son back?"

Dale looked at the inspectors. They didn't move. They didn't speak. They just watched.

One of them raised a pen.

Dale looked at his own clipboard. The form was still glowing.

*Status: Pending.*

He realized the trap.

The Auditor wasn't just a monster. It was a *bait*. It had triggered a review process.

And now, the Authority was here to audit *him*.

If the inspectors found that Dale had used an improvised protocol, if they found that he had acted outside his certification level...

They wouldn't just kill the monster, and they would revoke his license.

And if his license was revoked, the Auditor could wear his face next.

Dale looked at Marcus. He looked at the blank-faced men.

He raised his pen.

"I have a variance request," Dale said. His voice shook, but he forced it out. "I need to speak to a supervisor."

The lead inspector tilted his head.

"Request denied," the inspector said. Its voice was the sound of a fax machine jamming.

They started walking down the stairs.

Dale backed away. He hit the wall.

There was nowhere left to go.
