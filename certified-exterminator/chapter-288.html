Chapter 288: License Torn

# Chapter 288

The tunnel air tasted of copper and wet rot.

Kaelen Vane pressed his back against the damp concrete, his breath hitching in his throat. He watched the man ahead of him. Dale Kowalski did not run. He walked with a steady, rhythmic gait that sounded absurdly calm against the backdrop of the facility's dying screams.

Vane had worked the black markets of the Undercity for six years. He had seen men break under the pressure of the Authority's audits. He had seen hunters freeze when their Field Guide entries corrupted. He had never seen a man walk into a purge zone with a clipboard.

Kowalski's hand hovered over his belt. Not on a weapon. On a pen.

Vane's fingers tightened around the hilt of his own jagged blade. He wanted to scream at the pest control technician. *Run, you idiot. The scanners are locking on.*

But Kowalski stopped.

The technician tilted his head, listening to the siren's shriek. It was no longer a hum. It was a warning tone, sharp and discordant.

Kowalski pulled a crumpled piece of paper from his pocket. It was a receipt from a hardware store, dated three days ago. He unfolded it with precise, trembling fingers. He held it up to the dim emergency light, aligning the edges with the tunnel's curvature.

Vane squinted.

The paper wasn't a receipt. It was a schematic. A hand-drawn map of the ventilation shafts above them.

Kowalski traced a line with his pen. He muttered something unintelligible.

Then he looked at Vane.

His eyes were not afraid. They were annoyed.

"Stand where I tell you," Kowalski said. His voice was flat, devoid of panic. "If you move, you trigger the motion sensors in the ceiling grid. I need thirty seconds to clear the sector."

Vane opened his mouth to protest. The words died in his throat.

Kowalski reached into his vest, and he didn't pull out a grenade. He pulled out a canister of industrial adhesive sealant.

He sprayed it onto the floor, forming a perfect square around his feet.

Then he sprayed another square around Vane's boots.

"Stay in the box," Kowalski ordered.

A scanner drone dropped from the ceiling. It was a sleek, black sphere, bristling with red lenses. It hovered above them, spinning. The siren screamed louder, vibrating in Vane's teeth.

The drone's lens focused on Kowalski.

A red beam swept over him. Then it paused.

Kowalski raised his clipboard. On the front, taped securely, was a photograph. It was blurry, taken with a shaky hand, but it showed a creature. Specifically, it showed a *Rattus profundus* broodmother, dead and decomposing, pinned to a wall with a staple gun.

Under the photo, Kowalski had written in bold marker: **SPECIMEN LOGGED. SECTOR ISOLATED. NO FURTHER TREATMENT REQUIRED.**

The drone scanned the photo.

It scanned the clipboard.

It scanned the adhesive squares on the floor.

The siren cut out.

Silence crashed into the tunnel, heavier than the noise.

The drone's red light turned green.

**STATUS: COMPLIANT.**

Kowalski let out a breath he seemed to have been holding for an hour. He stepped out of the adhesive square. The adhesive was already hardening. He kicked it loose, leaving a perfect rectangle of glue on the concrete.

"Come," Kowalski said. "We have four minutes before the manual override kicks in. The Authority doesn't like automated compliance."

Vane stared at the drone as it retracted into the ceiling. He looked at the glue. He looked at the man.

"You used the Thoroughness Scale against them," Vane whispered.

Kowalski didn't look up. He was checking his watch.

"Protocol requires verification of containment before elimination can be finalized.

The scanner sees a contained sector.

It assumes the threat is neutralized. It doesn't check for the threat's continued existence.

It checks for the *report* of its end.

"

"That's a loophole," Vane said.

"It's a filing error," Kowalski corrected. "And I am a meticulous filer."

They moved. Kowalski led, his steps measured.

Vane followed, his heart hammering against his ribs.

The tunnel sloped upward. The air grew drier, smelling of ozone and dust.

A new notification flashed in Kowalski's peripheral vision. Vane couldn't see it, but he saw Kowalski flinch. A muscle in the technician's jaw twitched.

"What is it?" Vane asked.

"Penalty applied," Kowalski muttered. "-500 TP for loss of documentation. My certification is in freefall."

"Does that matter?"

"It means if I fail to identify the next thing we find, I don't get points. I get a citation. And citations lead to revocation."

"Then we get out."

"We don't get out until we file the incident report."

Vane wanted to hit him, and he wanted to drag him by the collar. But he followed.

Because Kowalski knew where the exits were.

And because the drone had stopped tracking them.

They reached a junction. Three tunnels branched off.

Left was dark and wet.

Right was blocked by rubble.

Center was brightly lit by emergency strips.

Kowalski stopped. He pulled a small notebook from his vest. He flipped to a fresh page. He wrote.

"What are you doing?" Vane asked, scanning the shadows. "We're being chased."

"I am noting the environmental conditions for the file," Kowalski said. "Humidity: high. Lighting: emergency only.

Structural integrity: compromised. This is relevant data.

"

"There's a monster in the left tunnel," Vane said, pointing his blade. "I can smell it."

Kowalski didn't look up.

"Describe it."

Vane blinked. "What?"

"Size. Color. Number of legs. Behavior."

Vane hesitated, and he glanced at the left tunnel. Something shifted in the darkness. A wet slapping sound.

"It's big.

Low to the ground. Too many legs.

Black."

Kowalski scribbled.

"Multi-limbed. Terrestrial.

Aggressive. Likely a Class IV Arachnid variant. *Araneus dimensional*.

"

"I didn't say that."

"You didn't have to. You described the morphology. I have the entry. It's in my Field Guide."

"Then why are you writing it down?"

"Because the System requires on-page confirmation. If I don't write it, it didn't happen. And if it didn't happen, I can't claim the points. And if I can't claim the points, I can't buy the new protocols I need to survive the next ten minutes."

The slapping sound grew louder. Closer.

Vane raised his sword.

"I'm going to kill it."

"Don't," Kowalski said sharply.

"Why?"

"Because if you kill it without me documenting it first, you create a cross-contamination event.

My Field Guide entry will be flagged as 'Unverified Combat.' I lose access to targeted damage bonuses for that species for twenty-four hours.

I need that bonus. The Rift is expanding.

I need every advantage.

"

Vane looked at the darkness. The thing was emerging. It was vast, a mass of chitin and shadow, with dozens of eyes reflecting the emergency lights.

"I don't care about your bonuses," Vane hissed. "It's going to eat us."

Kowalski looked at Vane. His expression was cold.

"If I don't have the bonus, I can't pierce its carapace.

You'll break your sword. I'll die. And you'll be alone in a facility that wants you dead.

"

Vane lowered his sword an inch.

"Fine. Document it."

Kowalski pulled out a stylus. He tapped the screen of a battered tablet he had scavenged earlier. He pointed it at the monster.

**IDENTIFYING... TARGET LOCKED.** **SPECIES: Araneus dimensional (Common Name: Rift Spider).** **WEAKNESS: Ventral ganglia cluster.** **TARGETED DAMAGE BONUS: +45%.**

The spider lunged.

It moved with terrifying speed, a blur of legs and venom. Vane shouted, swinging his blade. The sword struck the carapace. It bounced off, sparking.

Kowalski didn't flinch, and he stepped forward. He raised his hand.

He didn't hold a weapon.

He held a small, modified spray nozzle.

"Protocol: Chemical Disruption," Kowalski said.

He fired.

A cloud of grey mist erupted from the nozzle. It wasn't poison. It was a desiccant, designed to dry out soft tissue. It hit the spider's face.

The monster shrieked. Its legs tangled. It stumbled.

Vane saw the opening. He thrust his sword into the gap Kowalski had created. The blade slid in easily, protected by the System's targeted bonus. It sank into the soft underbelly.

The spider collapsed.

Kowalski didn't cheer. He walked over to the corpse.

He pulled out his tablet, and he took a photo.

He uploaded it.

**TREATMENT COMPLETED.** **TP AWARDED: 150.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 8.4.**

"We're moving," Kowalski said.

Vane wiped spider blood off his blade.

"You just saved our lives with a bug spray."

"It's a certified exterminant," Kowalski said. "It's not a bug spray. It's a tool. And we still have to find that printer."

They ran, and the facility was changing. The walls were breathing. Not metaphorically. The concrete rippled, expanding and contracting like lungs.

Kowalski stopped at a door. It was marked **ARCHIVE STORAGE 4**.

"The printer is in there," Kowalski said. "Old model. Thermal transfer.

It doesn't need ink. It needs paper.

"

Vane looked at the door. It was sealed with a heavy steel bar.

"Locked."

Kowalski pulled out a lockpick set. He worked the mechanism with trembling hands. His fingers were clumsy. He was sweating profusely.

Vane watched him.

"You're shaking."

"The air quality is degrading," Kowalski said. "Particulate matter is increasing. It's affecting my fine motor skills. I need to wear a respirator."

"We don't have a respirator."

Kowalski stopped picking. He looked at Vane.

"I do."

He reached into his vest, and he pulled out a half-mask respirator. It was dirty, stained with grime. He put it on.

He checked the seals.

"Better," he said.

He finished the pick. The lock clicked.

The door swung open.

Inside, the room was small. Shelves lined the walls, filled with metal boxes. In the center stood a large, boxy printer. It was covered in dust.

Kowalski rushed to it. He hit the power button. The lights flickered.

"Come on," he muttered.

Vane stood guard at the door. He could hear the siren starting again. Faintly. Getting louder.

"Kowalski," Vane said. "Hurry."

Kowalski pulled a stack of paper from his pocket. It was his inspection report. Dozens of pages. He fed them into the printer tray.

He pressed **PRINT**.

The printer groaned. A sheet of paper emerged. Then another.

Kowalski snatched them. He flipped through the pages. They were blank.

He frowned. He pressed **PRINT** again.

Blank pages.

"Paper jam," Vane said. "Fix it."

Kowalski opened the printer cover. He reached inside. His hand moved slowly. Too slowly.

Vane turned. The hallway was empty. But the shadows were moving.

"Kowalski," Vane said. "What is wrong with you?"

Kowalski didn't answer. He was staring at the printer's circuit board.

"Printer isn't jammed," Kowalski said. His voice was distorted by the respirator mask. "It's not connected."

"Connected to what?"

"To the network. The Authority's network."

Vane felt a chill.

"Why would it be connected?"

Kowalski turned around. His eyes were wide behind the goggles.

"Because it's not a printer," Kowalski said. "It's a terminal."

A green light flashed on the machine.

**CONNECTION ESTABLISHED.** **UPLOADING...**

Vane looked at the screen. Text was scrolling.

**SUBJECT: DALE KOWALSKI.** **DATA UPLOAD COMPLETE.** **FILE: TERMINAL.**

Kowalski stepped back. He looked at Vane.

"They didn't lose my file," Kowalski said. "They were waiting for me to upload it myself."

"Upload what?"

"My data."

Kowalski pointed at the screen. A progress bar was filling.

"It's my certification history. My Field Guide. My Thoroughness Rating. It's all of me."

Vane stared at the screen.

"Why did you print it?"

"I didn't," Kowalski said. "The machine did. I just fed it the blank pages. The machine filled them."

The progress bar hit 100%.

**STATUS: TERMINAL CONFIRMED.** **ACTION: PURGE PROTOCOL OMEGA INITIATED.**

The floor beneath them vibrated. The shelves of metal boxes rattled.

Kowalski looked at his hands. The hum in his bones was louder now. It was a roar.

"I'm not a technician," Kowalski said. "I'm a beacon."

Vane grabbed Kowalski's arm.

"We have to go. Now."

Kowalski didn't move. He watched the screen.

Another line of text appeared.

**NEW ENTRY DETECTED IN FIELD GUIDE.** **SPECIES: The Source.** **WEAKNESS: Unknown.** **TARGETED DAMAGE BONUS: 0%.**

Kowalski looked up. His face was pale.

"I can't kill myself," he said.

The door behind them slammed shut. The steel bar slid into place.

Vane pounded on the door.

"Open it! Open it!"

Kowalski stood still. He looked at the printer. Then he looked at Vane.

"There's a way," Kowalski said. "But it violates every protocol I have."

"What is it?"

"I have to change my job."

Vane stopped pounding. He turned.

"What?"

Kowalski reached into his vest. He pulled out his Level II license. He held it up.

"I need to resign."

The lights in the room turned red.

**WARNING: UNAUTHORIZED ACTION DETECTED.**

Kowalski tore the license in half.

The screen flashed.
