Chapter 364: Auditor Behind Him

# Chapter 364

The chime didn't fade. It vibrated in Dale's molars, a low-frequency hum that felt less like sound and more like the building settling after a foundation shift.

**AUDIT INITIATED.** **SUBJECT: KOWALSKI, DALE.** **VIOLATION: UNAUTHORIZED DEBT TRANSFER (CLASS IX).** **PENALTY: IMMEDIATE SUSPENSION OF TREATMENT PROTOCOLS.**

The text burned in the corner of his vision, red and jagged. Not the usual polite grey of standard notifications. This was emergency red. The kind used for biohazards or structural collapses.

Dale didn't look up. He was already moving.

The darkness of the Rift wasn't empty. It was dense, viscous, like air thickened with gelatin.

He could feel the pressure against his eardrums, a popping sensation that mirrored the static in his head.

His flashlight beam cut through the gloom, illuminating not rock or soil, but something organic. Membranous walls stretched across the void, pulsing with a faint, bioluminescent vein.

This wasn't a tunnel. It was a stomach.

Behind him, Jenkins stumbled. Her boot caught on a ridge of chitinous material, and she let out a sharp gasp, the sound swallowed instantly by the damp air.

"Stay low," Dale said, his voice flat. "Don't touch the walls. The mucosa is acidic."

"It's alive?" Jenkins whispered. She sounded younger than she had five minutes ago. The transfer had drained something from her, a physical exhaustion that sagged her shoulders.

"It's a containment unit," Dale corrected. "And it's leaking. If I touch that wall, my gloves degrade in four seconds. If you touch it, you lose the hand."

He pulled his clipboard from his belt. The paper inside was dry, protected by a plastic sleeve. He clicked his pen. The click was loud in the silence.

**DOCUMENTATION PROTOCOL: ACTIVE.** **FIELD GUIDE ENTRY: THE INSPECTOR GENERAL (PENDING).**

He couldn't fight this, and he couldn't spray it. The suspension notice was clear: no protocols, no weapons, no targeted damage bonuses.

The Authority had stripped him of his tools.

He was just a man with a clipboard in a hole in the world.

But the Authority was also a bureaucracy. And bureaucracies feared paperwork more than monsters.

"What are you doing?" Jenkins asked. She was staring at the clipboard, her eyes wide. "Why are you writing?"

"Establishing a paper trail," Dale said. "If they're auditing me, they need to see I was performing my duties. If I'm just standing there, I'm guilty. If I'm inspecting, I'm compliant."

He stepped forward, boots squelching in the muck. The air smelled of ozone and old blood.

Above them, the eyeless face in the clouds watched. It didn't blink. It didn't move. It just listened.

A second notification appeared.

**NOTICE OF INSPECTION.** **AUDITOR: UNIT 7-ALPHA.** **ETA: 00:00:10.**

Ten seconds.

Dale's hand moved quickly. He didn't draw a weapon. He drew a form.

It was a **Temporary Hazard Abatement Permit**, Form 114-B. A piece of paper he'd pulled from his kit during the last job, unused, blank. He scribbled in the header: *Immediate Environmental Stabilization.*

He filled in the location: *Rift Sector 4, Substrate Layer.* He filled in the hazard: *Structural Instability / Membrane Degradation.* He filled in the action taken: *Visual Assessment and Perimeter Marking.*

It was a lie. A technicality.

But the Authority operated on rules, not truths.

If he marked the perimeter, he was managing the site.

If he was managing the site, he wasn't abandoning his post.

"Jenkins," he said, not looking back. "Stand exactly where you are. Don't move your feet. Don't breathe loud."

"Dale, the thing is here!"

The ground shook. Not a tremor, but a shift. The membranous walls rippled. From the darkness ahead, a shape detached itself from the ceiling.

It wasn't a monster in the traditional sense. It was a construct of geometry and light, vaguely humanoid but wrong in proportion. Long limbs, no face, and a body made of shifting, translucent plates that clicked together like a roach's exoskeleton.

**UNIT 7-ALPHA.** **CLASS IX AUDITOR.** **STATUS: ACTIVE.**

The Auditor didn't walk. It flowed over the ground, its plates sliding over the organic floor without making a sound. It stopped ten feet from Dale.

It tilted its head. Or the plates that served as a head.

**QUERY: EXPLAIN THE TRANSFER.**

Dale held up the clipboard. His hand didn't shake. It hadn't shaken when he'd sealed a bedbug infestation in a tenement with black mold, and it wouldn't shake now.

"Transfer was necessitated by imminent threat to local personnel," Dale said. He kept his tone professional. Neutral.

Like he was explaining why he'd had to use a heavier concentration of pesticide on a resistant strain.

"Class IX entity presence detected prior to standard quarantine protocols.

Subject Jenkins was exposed to high-level dimensional resonance.

To prevent total cognitive collapse in a civilian, liability was transferred to the licensed technician.

"

The Auditor hovered. Its plates shifted, forming a pattern that looked almost like a frown.

**ASSESSMENT: NON-COMPLIANT.**

"Compliance requires Form 114-B," Dale said, tapping the paper.

"Environmental hazard assessment prior to intervention. I am assessing.

I am documenting.

The transfer is a secondary action taken to preserve the integrity of the investigation.

"

The Auditor remained silent. The silence stretched, thick and suffocating. Jenkins pressed a hand to her chest, her face pale. She could feel the connection to Dale, the phantom weight of his heartbeat syncing with her own.

The Auditor moved closer. One of its long, spindly arms extended, not toward Dale, but toward the clipboard.

**REVIEWING DOCUMENTATION.**

Dale watched it. He didn't flinch.

He knew what it was looking for.

Missing signatures?

Incorrect dates? He had filled it out with the precision of a man who had spent fourteen years avoiding citations for sloppy work.

The Auditor scanned the page. Then it looked at Dale.

**FORM VALIDITY: ACCEPTABLE.**

A small green checkmark appeared in Dale's vision.

**PROTOCOLS REMAIN SUSPENDED.** **BUT: INSPECTION RIGHTS GRANTED.**

Dale let out a breath he didn't realize he'd been holding. It wasn't a victory. It was a stay of execution.

"Inspection rights," Dale repeated. "Does that mean I can see the source?"

The Auditor tilted its head again.

**YOU MAY OBSERVE. YOU MAY NOT INTERVENE.**

Dale nodded. "Understood."

He turned to the wall behind him. The membranous tissue was pulsing faster now. He pulled a small UV light from his belt.

It wasn't a weapon. It was a diagnostic tool.

He shone it on the wall, and the acid reaction was immediate.

The light revealed intricate patterns beneath the surface. Not random. Structured.

Like a blueprint.

"Jenkins," Dale said. "Look at this."

She stepped forward, despite his warning. She couldn't help it. The curiosity was a hook in her gut.

"It's… wiring?" she asked.

"Neural pathways," Dale said. "This isn't just a hole in the ground.

It's a server farm.

The Rift isn't a breach. It's a data stream.

"

He looked at the Auditor.

"You're not here to kill the monster," Dale said. "You're here to delete the data."

The Auditor didn't respond. It just watched.

Dale scanned further down the wall. There, embedded in the organic matter, was a small, metallic box. It hummed with a familiar frequency. The same frequency as the Authority's notification chimes.

A node.

And next to it, etched into the membrane, were numbers. Coordinates. But not for Earth.

*SECTOR 9. THE HIVE MIND'S NURSERY.*

Dale's mind raced. The Inspector General wasn't just an entity.

It was a symptom, and the Rift was feeding off the Authority's own licensing network.

Every time Dale filed a report, every time he earned points, he was sending data back to the Authority. And the Authority was using that data to map the Rift.

But someone was corrupting the data.

"Someone is feeding it false positives," Dale muttered.

Jenkins looked at him.

"What?"

"If I get a false positive, I lose points. But if the Authority gets a false positive, they send an Auditor. If an Auditor arrives at a non-threat, they delete the substrate. They erase the local reality to prevent contamination."

He looked up at the Auditor.

"You're not auditing me," Dale said. "You're auditing the system. And you found a bug."

The Auditor's plates clicked rapidly. A sound of distress. Or anger.

**WARNING: SYSTEM INTEGRITY COMPROMISED.** **ACTION: PURGE SECTOR.**

The ground beneath them dissolved. Not melt. Dissolve.

Into white noise. Into static.

Jenkins screamed. The connection between them spiked, a surge of pain that dropped her to her knees.

Dale grabbed her arm. Hard.

"Don't fight it," he said. "It's a memory wipe. If you resist, you get deleted."

"What do I do?" she cried.

Dale looked at his clipboard. Then he looked at the blank back page.

He flipped it over.

On the back of Form 114-B, there was a footnote. A tiny line of text that most technicians never bothered to read because they were too busy dealing with ants and roaches.

*In cases of systemic paradox, the Technician may invoke Clause 7: The Right to Contest.*

Dale hadn't known the clause existed. But the Authority, in its infinite bureaucratic wisdom, had printed it.

He clicked his pen.

**CONTEST FORM: INITIATED.**

He wrote quickly.

*CHALLENGE: The presence of Class IX Auditor constitutes a threat to the stability of the inspection site. Requesting immediate withdrawal of Auditor Unit 7-Alpha pending re-evaluation of Sector 4.*

It was a gamble. A stupid, dangerous gamble. But the Authority loved procedure. And it hated being told it was wrong.

He held the paper up.

The Auditor froze. The dissolving ground stopped.

The eyeless face in the sky blinked. Once.

**RECEIVING CONTEST.** **PROCESSING...**

The silence was louder than the chime.

Dale watched the Auditor. He could see the plates shifting, recalculating. He was betting his life, and Jenkins's life, on the fact that the Authority cared more about following the rules than saving the world.

**CONTEST DENIED.**

The word hung in the air.

The ground dissolved again. Faster this time. The static roared, a wall of white nothing approaching them.

Jenkins looked at Dale. Her eyes were wide with terror.

"Dale..."

Dale didn't look at her. He looked at the Auditor.

"Denied?" he said. His voice was calm. Too calm.

He reached into his pocket. He didn't have a weapon. He didn't have a spray.

He had a receipt.

A receipt from the last job. A job he'd done in Cleveland. A Class-4 termite infestation. He'd kept it because he liked the way the paper felt.

He ripped the receipt in half, and he held up the two halves.

**CLAUSE 12: APPEAL OF DENIAL.**

The Auditor's plates shattered. Literally shattered. Small shards of light exploded outward, embedding in the walls.

**ERROR. ERROR. PARADOX DETECTED.**

The white noise stopped, and the Auditor was frozen, stuck in a loop of its own logic.

Dale stepped forward. He walked past the frozen machine. He walked past the static.

He walked to the metallic node in the wall, and he pulled a pen from his pocket and jammed it into the node.

Not to destroy it. To bridge it.

He connected his clipboard's wire (yes, the clipboard had a wire, a data port for syncing with Authority servers) to the pen.

**UPLOADING DATA...**

He wasn't uploading his report, and he was uploading the Auditor's error code.

**DATA UPLOAD COMPLETE.** **AUDITOR UNIT 7-ALPHA: DECOMMISSIONED.**

The Auditor slumped. Its plates fell to the ground with a clatter, inert.

The static faded. The walls stopped dissolving. The eyeless face in the sky vanished, replaced by normal, Ohio sky.

Jenkins sat on the ground, gasping. She looked at Dale.

"What did you do?" she whispered.

Dale pulled the pen from the node. It was hot. Burnt.

"I filed a complaint," he said.

He looked at his Field Guide.

**NEW ENTRY: THE INSPECTOR GENERAL.** **WEAKNESS: BUREAUCRATIC RIGIDITY.**

A new notification appeared.

**CERTIFICATION LEVEL: IX.** **STATUS: UNLOCKED.** **WARNING: YOU ARE NOW ON THE AUTHORITY'S INTERNAL BLACKLIST.**

Dale smiled. It was a thin, tight smile.

"Welcome to the big leagues, Jenkins," he said.

From the darkness of the Rift, a new sound emerged. Not a chime.

A voice.

"Mr. Kowalski," the voice said. It sounded like Jenkins's father.

Dale froze.

"Please report to the Central Processing Center," the voice continued. "Your license is under review."

Jenkins scrambled up.

"Dale, that's my dad's voice."

"It's not your dad," Dale said.

He looked into the dark.

"It's the next Auditor."

And it was already standing behind him.

The next Auditor was already standing behind him.
