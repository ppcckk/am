Chapter 268: Not Clean Anymore

# Chapter 268

The perimeter fence was humming. Not with electricity, but with the low, thrumming vibration of containment fields that had been dialed to maximum output for six hours straight.

Outside the yellow tape, a crowd of onlookers pressed against the barricade, their faces illuminated by the blue glow of their phones.

They weren't looking at the hole in the ground. They were looking at the man standing on the edge of it.

Marcus Vane, Head of Tactical Response for the Local Authority, adjusted his tie.

He didn't like Kowalski. He didn't like the way the technician stood there, clipboard in hand, looking less like a hero and more like a building inspector assessing a cracked foundation.

The media drones hovered higher, broadcasting the scene to three counties, and the comments were already rolling in, a cascade of skepticism and awe.

"Look at him," Vane muttered to his deputy, Sarah. "He's not even holding a weapon."

"He has a spray can," Sarah said, squinting through her binoculars. "And a flashlight. And that clipboard."

"It's a breach protocol," Vane said, though his voice lacked conviction. "Kowalski has a Level II license. He's authorized for containment, not combat. If that thing gets out, we're liable."

"The thing," Sarah pointed a gloved finger at the crawlspace entrance, "is already out. Look at the shadow."

On the ground, a pool of darkness was spreading. It wasn't static.

It pulsed, rippling outward like oil on water, eating the light from the floodlights.

The crowd murmured, a collective intake of breath.

The air grew cold, a sharp drop that raised gooseflesh on Vane's arms despite the summer humidity.

Kowalski moved. He didn't run. He walked with the deliberate, measured pace of a man walking into a room where he expected to find mold.

He stepped over the yellow tape, not bothering to lift it, simply stepping over it as if it were a line drawn on pavement. He approached the pool of shadow.

Vane grabbed his radio.

"Team Alpha, move in on my mark. Do not engage until the shadow stabilizes."

"Sir," the radio crackled, "we're getting interference. The Geiger counters are screaming, but they're not picking up radiation. They're picking up… static."

"It's the Rift bleed," Vane said. "Get your people ready. Kowalski is a liability. If he fails, we take the shot."

Down below, Kowalski stopped five feet from the edge of the crawlspace, and the claw that had grabbed him was gone. The box, that impossible metal coffin, lay on its side, its seam wide open. Inside was not a monster, but a void.

A darkness so deep it hurt to look at.

Kowalski opened his Field Guide. The pages were fluttering in a wind that didn't exist. The ink on the page for the Unidentified Pre-Rift Entity was gone. In its place was a jagged, black smudge that looked like a burn mark.

*Field Guide Corrupted. 23:59:59 remaining.*

The timer ticked down. Dale watched the seconds bleed away. He had less than a minute to identify the threat before the debuff fully settled, locking him out of his primary tools. But the System notification had flashed again, a second line of text appearing in red, pulsing with urgency.

**LOOT DETECTED: RESIDUAL TRACE OF PRE-RIFT ANOMALY.** **ACTION: SAMPLE.**

Sample. The word hung in Dale's mind.

You didn't sample a monster.

You sampled a substrate. A nest. A breeding ground.

The shadow surged forward. It wasn't attacking; it was expanding.

It reached toward Dale's legs, seeking purchase.

Dale didn't dodge, and he raised his left hand, holding the clipboard, and angled it. The shadow hit the cardboard.

The material didn't tear. It absorbed.

A notification chimed in Dale's ear. Not a sound, but a sensation, like a toothache.

**DOCUMENTATION COMPLETE: 15%.** **FIELD GUIDE ENTRY: PRE-RIFT ANOMALY (PARTIAL).** **WEAKNESS: UNKNOWN.**

Dale gritted his teeth. Fifteen percent wasn't enough.

He needed more, and he needed the shape of the thing. He looked at the floor of the crawlspace. The shadow was leaving residue.

A fine, grey powder that smelled of ozone and old blood.

He reached into his pocket and pulled out a sterile swab. His hands were steady now.

The shaking had stopped, replaced by the cold clarity of procedure.

He knelt, the knee pads clicking against the concrete. He extended the swab into the grey powder.

The shadow recoiled. A high-pitched whine, audible to everyone on the surface, erupted from the box.

Up on the surface, Marcus Vane flinched.

"What did he do?"

"He touched it," Sarah said, her voice trembling. "He's sampling the decay."

"Pull him back!" Vane shouted into the radio. "Team Alpha, breach and secure! Kowalski, get away from the hole! That's an order!"

Dale heard the order. He heard the heavy boots of the tactical team kicking through the side door of the duplex.

He heard the shouts of men preparing to neutralize a threat they couldn't see.

But he didn't move, and he was writing.

He couldn't see the Field Guide, but he could feel the words forming in his mind, translating the sensory data into the rigid syntax of the Authority.

*Substance: Non-Newtonian shadow matter.* *Behavior: Repels organic contact, adheres to inert surfaces.* *Vulnerability: Structural integrity relies on a cohesive lattice. Disruption causes collapse.*

The swab came back clean. But the shadow was reacting to the absence of the sample. It was confused. It was searching for the connection he had just severed.

Dale stood up. He turned his back to the box. This was a violation of every survival instinct in his DNA, but it was correct according to protocol.

If you don't treat the nest, the problem comes back. The nest was the box.

The shadow was just the symptom.

"Hey!" a voice boomed. A tactical officer, face masked, leveled a rifle at Dale's chest. "Step away from the breach!"

Dale didn't step away. He walked toward the officer. He held up the clipboard, the back of which was now stained with grey powder.

"Do not fire," Dale said, his voice flat, carrying the weight of a municipal inspector denying a permit. "You'll contaminate the site. And you'll miss the point."

"The point is you're dead if you don't move," the officer growled.

"The point," Dale said, pointing to the grey stain on the clipboard, "is that the substrate is reactive to light. Specifically, high-lumen tactical flashes. You have them. I don't."

The officer hesitated. The shadow behind Dale was rising, a towering figure of darkness that had no face, only a suggestion of a head. It was reaching for the officer's rifle.

Dale didn't look at it. He looked at his watch.

**FIELD GUIDE CORRUPTION EXPIRED IN: 00:00:03.**

Three. Two. One.

The System chimed, a bright, clear note that cut through the tension.

**FIELD GUIDE RESTORED.** **ENTRY UPDATED: PRE-RIFT ANOMALY.** **WEAKNESS IDENTIFIED: PHOTON DENSIFICATION.** **BONUS: +40% DAMAGE VIA LIGHT-BASED PROTOCOLS.**

Dale spun around. The shadow lashed out, a whip of darkness aimed at his neck. He didn't duck. He raised his clipboard.

"Flashbang," Dale commanded.

The tactical officer, trained to follow direct orders from senior Authority agents, reacted on instinct. He unclipped a stun grenade from his vest and tossed it. It landed next to the box.

Dale didn't wait for the explosion. He pulled a flare from his utility belt—a standard issue for pest control technicians working in low-light conditions—and struck it against the concrete.

The magnesium flare sputtered to life, burning with a blinding, white-hot intensity. Dale thrust it into the center of the shadow's mass.

The effect was instantaneous. The shadow didn't scream. It crystallized.

The black mist turned into shards of obsidian glass, shattering into a thousand pieces that clattered onto the concrete floor.

The box, deprived of its animating force, slumped to the side, dead metal.

Silence rushed back into the room. The hum of the containment fields outside dropped to a low idle.

The tactical officer lowered his rifle. He looked at Dale, then at the shards of shadow, then at the flare still burning in Dale's hand.

"You just…" the officer started.

"Protocol 7-B," Dale said, extinguishing the flare and dropping it into a metal bucket he pulled from his belt. "Neutralize the vector. Document the remains. Verify the area."

He opened his Field Guide. The page for the Pre-Rift Anomaly was now fully populated. But there was a new line, written in gold ink, a color Dale had never seen before.

**TREATMENT POINTS AWARDED: 1,500.** **RANK UP: CERTIFICATION LEVEL III.** **NEW PROTOCOL UNLOCKED: QUARANTINE SEAL.**

Dale stared at the text.

Level III. He had skipped Level II entirely. The Authority didn't allow that.

Unless…

Unless the job had been deemed a Critical Infestation Event.

"Kowalski!" Marcus Vane's voice rang out from the entrance. The Head of Tactical Response stepped into the crawlspace, his boots crunching on the shards of shadow glass. He looked pale. "You just bypassed the entire tactical team."

"I followed the protocol," Dale said, closing his Field Guide. "The threat was identified. The weakness was documented. The elimination was verified."

"You gambled with public safety," Vane said, his voice tight. "That thing could have gone airborne. You could have spread it to the neighborhood."

"It didn't," Dale said.

He pulled out a small, empty glass vial from his pocket.

He had taken a sample of the grey powder earlier. He held it up.

"I have a sample. It's stable. It's not spreading.

It's inert."

Vane stared at the vial. Then he looked at the shards on the floor. He looked at the crowd outside, who were now cheering, unaware of how close they had come to annihilation.

"You're lucky," Vane said. "You're lucky."

"I'm not lucky," Dale said. He tucked the vial into his pocket. "I'm thorough. There's a difference."

He turned to leave, and he needed to file the report. The Authority would be watching. The bonus points were nice, but the Thoroughness Rating was the real currency.

He had to make sure this job didn't look like a fluke.

He had to make sure they knew he knew what he was doing.

As he walked up the stairs, the crowd parted. People were filming him. Someone shouted, "Is he a wizard?"

Dale ignored them. He reached the surface, blinking in the sudden sunlight. The heat was oppressive. The air smelled of burnt plastic and ozone.

His phone buzzed. It wasn't the Authority's notification system. It was his personal cell. A number he didn't recognize.

He answered it.

"Mr. Kowalski," a smooth, unfamiliar voice said. "This is the Regional Licensing Authority. We've been monitoring your activity."

Dale stopped walking. He held the phone to his ear, his other hand gripping his clipboard so hard the cardboard bent.

"We need to discuss your recent rank advancement," the voice continued. "It appears you have unlocked a protocol that is currently classified as Restricted. Protocol 7-B is not authorized for Level II technicians. Or Class III, for that matter."

Dale felt a cold knot form in his stomach. He looked back at the house. The door to the crawlspace was still open. The black shards were still on the floor.

"I used a standard flare," Dale said. "It's municipal equipment."

"It's not what matters," the voice said.

"It's what you did *before* the flare. You sampled the anomaly. You identified a weakness that doesn't exist in any public database.

Where did you get that information, Mr. Kowalski?

"

Dale's blood ran cold, and he hadn't told anyone.

The System had given him the weakness based on the sample. But the Authority didn't know that.

They thought he had prior knowledge. They thought he was hiding something.

"I deduced it," Dale said.

"You have twenty-four hours," the voice said, its tone dropping, becoming dangerous. "Report to the Central District Office.

Bring your Field Guide.

Do not attempt to delete any entries. If you do, we will revoke your license and arrest you for unauthorized possession of Pre-Rift intelligence.

"

The line went dead.

Dale stood on the sidewalk, and the crowd was still there, but the cheering had died down. They were looking at him with suspicion now.

The magic trick was over.

The monster was dead. But the bureaucrat was still alive.

He looked at his Field Guide. The gold text for Level III pulsed in his vision. **QUARANTINE SEAL.**

He hadn't used it yet. But he had unlocked it. And now, because he had unlocked it, he was a target.

A black sedan pulled up to the curb. The engine was silent. The windows were tinted so dark they looked like mirrors. The door opened.

A woman stepped out. She wore a suit that cost more than Dale's house. She held a briefcase in one hand and a tablet in the other. She walked past the crowd, ignoring their shouts, her eyes fixed on Dale.

"Mr. Kowalski," she said. "My name is Agent Corliss. You're under audit."

Dale looked at her. Then he looked at the crawlspace. Then he looked at the vial in his pocket, the one containing the grey powder that had just killed a god-like entity.

He pulled the vial out. He unscrewed the cap.

Agent Corliss stopped. Her hand hovered over her belt, where a weapon was likely hidden.

"Don't," she said.

Dale poured the grey powder into his palm. He didn't drop it. He let it sit there, a small mound of dust that shimmered with an inner light.

"I didn't steal this," Dale said. "I harvested it.

As part of my job.

And now I have a problem.

Because my Field Guide is still open. And the next page is blank.

"

Agent Corliss's eyes narrowed.

"What next page?"

Dale looked at the empty space in his mind, the slot for the next creature. The System was waiting. The Authority was watching. And the Rift above Ohio was still open, still dripping, still hungry.

"The page for whatever comes next," Dale said. "And I think I already know what it is."

He closed his hand, clenching the powder into a fist. The grey dust seeped into his skin, burning cold.

**SYSTEM ALERT: CONTAMINATION DETECTED ON PERSONNEL.** **INITIATING BIO-HAZARD PROTOCOL: 3… 2… 1…**

Dale looked at Agent Corliss. He smiled, a dry, thin expression that didn't reach his eyes.

"You might want to call the sanitation department," he said. "I'm not clean anymore."
