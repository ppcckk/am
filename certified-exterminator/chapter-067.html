Chapter 67: Self-Referential Artifact

# Chapter 67

The room smelled like a duplex in Westerville, which meant the Authority had built it from memory. The linoleum was wrong. The baseboards were wrong. Dale knew because he had inspected forty-three units in the Westerville corridor and none of them had this particular configuration of water staining beneath the kitchen sink.

He knew because he had written the correction report, and he stood in the center of the living room with his clipboard. His boots were clean.

His coveralls were clean.

He had not touched anything in this room, and he intended to keep it that way until he had identified the specimen, documented its entry point, isolated the infestation zone, eliminated the organism, and verified the treatment. Full protocol. Five steps.

No shortcuts, and the notification had appeared three minutes ago.

LEVEL IX EXAMINATION: COMMENCED. SPECIMEN IS PRESENT. IDENTIFY IT.

That was the entire instruction. No location marker.

No species hint.

No timer. Just the word IDENTIFY and the room, which looked like a house but was not a house, because houses did not have a ceiling that was also a floor.

Dale looked up, and the ceiling was a floor. Someone was walking above him.

The footsteps were wrong.

Too heavy for the joists.

Too evenly spaced. Like a metronome calibrated to the rhythm of a heart that had no business beating inside a ceiling.

He looked at the clipboard. His Field Guide was populated. Forty-seven entries.

Every creature he had ever properly identified, documented, and treated. He scrolled through them.

Rattus profundus.

Acid beetles. The broodmother. The Class III specimen he had negated in the previous examination.

The dimensional mites.

The wall-crawlers. The thing in the storm drain that had a face.

None of them matched the footsteps above him, and he looked at the walls.

He looked at the floor. He looked at the gap beneath the front door, which was sealed with a weatherstrip that had been installed incorrectly. The gap was too wide.

The weatherstrip was the wrong gauge. Someone had installed a 3/8-inch strip in a 1/2-inch gap, which meant the seal was decorative than functional.

He wrote it down.

K-14.

Improper weatherstrip installation. Not a violation class he had ever used before.

He had to invent the code, which meant the Authority was watching him invent it, which meant the Authority expected him to invent it.

The footsteps stopped.

Silence. Then a sound from the kitchen.

A wet click, like a tongue against a palate.

Then another. Then a third, faster. The rhythm of feeding.

Dale moved toward the kitchen. He did not run. Running disturbed the air column and made it harder to locate the source of a feeding sound. He walked at a pace that kept his breathing steady and his clipboard level.

The kitchen was the same wrong linoleum, and the sink was dripping. The cabinet beneath the sink was open, and the water staining inside it was wrong for the configuration of the pipes.

The pipes were intact.

The stain was not from a leak.

He crouched. He put his flashlight on the stain.

It was frass.

Not the frass of Rattus profundus, which was dark and pellet-shaped. Not the frass of acid beetles, which was crystalline and smelled like vinegar.

This frass was pale, fibrous, and arranged in a pattern, which was not scattered.

It was written.

Dale stared at it. The frass formed letters. Small ones.

He could read them if he got close enough. He got close enough.

The letters said: NOT.

The same word. The word he had written. The word that had negated a Class III specimen. The word that had created a new violation class.

He pulled back, and he looked at the frass pattern again. It was not a instance of the word. It was the word repeated, stacked, layered, forming a structure.

A nest.

A nest made of negation.

His Field Guide flickered.

NEW ENTRY DETECTED: Species unidentified. Source: self-referential documentation artifact.

Self-referential. The creature was made of his own paperwork. His own writing. The word NOT had become a material, and the material had become a nest, and the nest had become a specimen.

He wrote: Self-referential documentation artifact.

Origin: operator error. Kowalski, Dale. Certification Level IV.

Date of creation: prior examination.

The Field Guide accepted the entry. A progress bar appeared beside it. IDENTIFICATION: 100%. DOCUMENTATION: IN PROGRESS.

He had identified it, and he was documenting it.

But documentation of a self-referential artifact created more of the artifact. The frass was growing while he wrote.

The nest was expanding in response to his description of it. Every word he added made it larger.

He stopped writing.

The frass stopped growing, and he looked at the ceiling. The footsteps resumed.

The thing above him was walking in the same rhythm as the frass below him.

The ceiling and the floor were the same surface. The creature was the room. The room was the creature.

The documentation was the organism, and he looked at his clipboard. He looked at the entry he had written. He looked at the frass, which had grown another layer while he was thinking.

The system rewarded documentation. The system rewarded identification.

The system rewarded thoroughness.

And the creature was made of documentation. Which meant the system was feeding it.

Dale set the clipboard on the counter. He picked up his pen. He did not write.

He opened the Field Guide entry he had just created. He looked at the IDENTIFICATION line.

He looked at the DOCUMENTATION line, and he looked at the ISOLATION line, which was empty.

He looked at the ELIMINATION line, which was empty. He looked at the VERIFICATION line, which was empty.

Full protocol. Five steps. Identify, document, isolate, eliminate, verify.

He had identified it. He had begun documenting it. The documentation was feeding it. He could not document it.

But the system required documentation for Treatment Points. He could not earn points. But if he could not earn points, he could not pass the examination.

But if he could not pass the examination, he could not advance.

But if he could not advance, the creature would continue to grow, because the creature was made of his own progress.

He was trapped in a loop. The system's own rules had created the problem.

Dale picked up the clipboard. He turned to a blank page. He did not write a Field Guide entry. He wrote a code violation report.

K-14: Improper weatherstrip installation. 3/8-inch strip in 1/2-inch gap. Recommend replacement with 1/2-inch strip. Note: gap is functional entry point for unidentified organism. Recommend sealing all entry points before treatment.

He wrote it in the format of a building inspection.

Not a Field Guide entry. Not a treatment protocol.

A code violation.

The kind of report he had been writing for fourteen years. The kind of report that triggered a maintenance work order, not a combat protocol.

The Field Guide did not react. The frass did not grow.

He kept writing. He described the water staining.

He described the wrong linoleum, and he described the ceiling that was a floor.

He described the footsteps. He described the feeding sounds.

All of it in the language of building inspection. Structural deficiency. Improper installation.

Inadequate ventilation. Code non-compliance.

He did not call it a creature. He did not call it an infestation. He called it a structural problem with a contributing biological factor.

The frass stopped growing.

The footsteps stopped, and the room went quiet.

His phone buzzed.

DISPATCH: Kowalski.

You have filed a correction than a treatment. The Authority recognizes this as a valid approach. But you have not eliminated the specimen.

You have only reclassified it.

The creature still exists. It is now a code violation.

You must resolve the violation.

Dale looked at the frass. It was still there. The nest was still there.

But it was no longer growing. It had stopped because he had stopped calling it a creature.

It was a gap.

A structural gap.

A gap that allowed entry, and he picked up the clipboard. He wrote: K-14 resolution. Seal gap with 1/2-inch weatherstrip.

Remove all biological residue. Verify seal integrity.

He looked at the frass. He looked at the gap beneath the front door. He looked at the ceiling, which was still a floor.

He could not seal the ceiling, and the ceiling was above him. The ceiling was the creature. The creature was the room.

The room was a house that was not a house, built by the Authority from a memory of a house that was wrong.

He could not seal the ceiling. But he could seal the entry point. The gap beneath the door. The 3/8-inch weatherstrip in a 1/2-inch gap.

He walked to the front door. He crouched.

He pulled the weatherstrip free, which came off easily, and it had been installed without adhesive, which was itself a code violation. He noted it.

He looked at the gap. The gap was half an inch.

Through the gap, he could see the ground outside.

The ground was not a yard, and the ground was the same wrong linoleum, extending outward into a space that had no walls.

He reached into his kit. He found a 1/2-inch weatherstrip. He had been carrying it for three weeks because he had a job in Dublin that needed one and he kept forgetting to install it.

He pressed it into the gap. It fit.

The seal was tight.

The room shuddered.

The frass started drying out. Not the slow drying of a negated specimen. Fast drying.

Like paper left in the sun.

The nest collapsed inward, losing structure, losing cohesion, losing the pattern of the word NOT.

His Field Guide updated.

ENTRY: Self-referential documentation artifact.

STATUS: RECLASSIFIED. TREATMENT: STRUCTURAL CORRECTION. TREATMENT POINTS: 40.

THOROUGHNESS RATING: 7.8.

7.8, and he had never scored below 8.0.

The Authority had docked him because he had not eliminated the creature. He had sealed the gap.

The creature had starved. That was not elimination. That was starvation.

But it worked.

His phone buzzed again.

DISPATCH: Kowalski. You have passed the Level IX Examination.

He stared at the screen. He was Level IV. He had just passed a Level IX Examination.

DISPATCH (cont.): You have passed the examination by demonstrating that the examination was invalid, and the specimen was not a Class IX threat.

It was a documentation error.

You reclassified it as a code violation and resolved it through structural correction; the Authority acknowledges this as a novel application of Protocol 0.

Protocol 0. There was no Protocol 0. The protocols started at I.

DISPATCH (cont.): Protocol 0 is not a protocol.

Protocol 0 is the recognition that some infestations are not biological. They are administrative.

They are caused by errors in the system itself.

Protocol 0 has no Treatment Points. Protocol 0 has no certification level. Protocol 0 has no protocol.

Protocol 0 is the act of filing a correction report when the system has produced a violation that the system cannot resolve.

Dale looked at his clipboard. He looked at the code violation report. K-14. Improper weatherstrip installation.

He looked at the frass, which was now just dust.

His phone buzzed one final time.

DISPATCH: Kowalski, and the specimen was not generated by the Authority.

The specimen was generated by you. The word NOT was not a negation.

It was a seed. You planted it in the Field Guide. The Field Guide grew it.

The system fed it. You have been growing a self-referential artifact for three examinations without knowing it.

He looked at the dust.

DISPATCH (cont.): This is not your first Level IX Examination. It is your fourth. You have passed three others. You do not remember them.

Dale looked at his clipboard. He looked at the forty-seven entries in his Field Guide. He counted them. He counted them again.

Forty-eight.

Forty-eight entries were present. He had forty-seven when he walked into the room.

He looked at the new entry, which was at the bottom of the list. He had not written it. He had not identified it.

He had not documented it.

ENTRY: Kowalski, Dale. Species: self-referential documentation artifact.

STATUS: ACTIVE.

TEMPLATE: COMPLETE. NOTE: THIS ENTRY HAS BEEN PRESENT IN YOUR FIELD GUIDE SINCE CHAPTER 1. YOU HAVE NEVER IDENTIFIED YOURSELF.
