Chapter 375: Triggered Ombudsman

# Chapter 375

The audit did not arrive with trumpets. It arrived with the sound of a filing cabinet slamming shut in an empty office building.

Dale froze, his pen hovering over the waiver. The floor beneath him trembled, not from the grinding gears of the engine, but from the vibration of a stamp hitting paper somewhere in the deep architecture of reality. The cracks in the chamber walls widened, leaking a grey, viscous fluid that smelled of ozone and stale coffee.

Vane recoiled. The ink that formed his body rippled, losing its cohesion. He looked at the paper Dale had signed, then at the cracks spreading across the ceiling.

"You triggered the Ombudsman," Vane whispered. His voice lacked its usual baritone confidence. It sounded like dry leaves skittering on pavement. "Do you understand what an Ombudsman is, Dale?"

Dale capped his pen. He placed it in his breast pocket with the precision of a surgeon placing a scalpel.

"An internal review body," Dale said. He didn't look at Vane.

He looked at the grey fluid oozing from the ceiling.

It dripped onto the burning technicians. The fire hissed and died, replaced by a cold, gray fog.

"They check for procedural violations.

Specifically, unauthorized resource diversion.

"

"It's not a review body," Vane snapped. He stepped back, his feet dissolving into the ink pool.

"It's a predator.

The Authority doesn't have employees. It has functions. And when a function breaks, the correction mechanism doesn't fix it.

It consumes it.

"

Above them, the Rift pulsed.

The grey fluid coalesced into shapes.

Not humanoid. Not animal.

They were geometric nightmares, jagged angles of static and silence that moved with jerky, stop-motion unnaturalness.

Dale's Field Guide flashed in his peripheral vision. The entry for *Rattus profundus* was still there, but a new line was populating, scrolling rapidly as his system struggled to categorize the entities emerging from the cracks.

**UNKNOWN ENTITY TYPE: CORRECTIVE AGENT.** **THREAT LEVEL: NULL.** **PROTOCOL: NOT APPLICABLE.**

"Null?" Dale muttered. He pulled out his clipboard.

His hands were steady, and they always were, even when the world was ending. It was the only part of him that remained compliant with safety standards.

"If the threat level is null, the protocol should be observation and documentation.

But you can't document something that doesn't exist in the taxonomy.

"

Vane laughed, a harsh, barking sound.

"That's the point.

The Ombudsman erases.

It identifies errors and deletes the source. You filed a complaint against the system. The system is auditing itself.

And you, Dale, are the error.

"

One of the geometric entities dropped from the ceiling. It landed on the central gear assembly of the engine. The metal didn't bend; it simply ceased to be metal. It became a void, a hole in the world shaped like a jagged spike.

Dale moved. He didn't run. He walked to the nearest technician, a woman whose legs were fused into the ink pipes. Her face was frozen in a scream that had been silenced by the cold fog.

"Ma'am," Dale said, kneeling. He pulled a marker from his pocket. "Stay still. I need to verify your status for the record."

"Don't!" Vane shouted. "If you touch her, the Ombudsman will flag you as an accessory to the infestation!"

Dale ignored him. He clicked the marker. He drew a small circle on the technician's forehead. Then another on her chest.

**Field Guide Update: Status Check - Vital Signs Compromised. Containment Protocol: Immediate.**

He wasn't trying to save her, and he knew he couldn't.

The Authority's rules were clear: once a resource is integrated into the engine, it is no longer a living subject. It is infrastructure.

But infrastructure had to be inventoried.

The Ombudsman turned. Its lack of eyes was more unsettling than having them. It focused on Dale. The air around him grew heavy, pressing against his eardrums.

**Violation Detected: Unauthorized Interaction with Infrastructure.** **Penalty: 40% Cross-Contamination Debuff.** **Effect: Field Guide Entry Corrupted for 24 Hours.**

A warning flashed red in Dale's vision. The text of his Field Guide scrambled, letters swapping places, numbers bleeding into words. His ability to identify weaknesses in monsters was gone. Blind.

He stood up. He faced the entity.

"You're late," Dale said.

The Ombudsman tilted its head. A sound like a printer jamming echoed through the chamber.

Dale reached into his belt. He didn't grab a weapon. He grabbed a sample jar. A simple, glass vial with a rubber stopper, used for collecting frass and urine samples in residential inspections.

Vane stared. "What are you doing?"

"Collecting evidence," Dale said.

He lunged. Not at the Ombudsman. At the grey fluid pooling around the base of the engine. The Ombudsman reacted instantly, a spike of void-form shooting toward Dale's chest.

Dale sidestepped. The spike punched a hole through his shoulder guard.

The pain was sharp, electric, and immediate, and he didn't flinch. He slammed the jar into the pool of grey fluid.

The fluid resisted. It was thick, like mercury. Dale had to jam his thumb against the stopper, forcing the vacuum seal to break. He poured the sample into the jar.

The Ombudsman shrieked. The sound was the digital distortion of a file being corrupted.

**Evidence Collected.** **Field Guide Entry: CORRECTIVE AGENT.** **Weakness: Procedural Paradox.**

Dale's vision cleared for a split second, then blurred again. The debuff was active, but the data had been captured. He had identified the creature's nature before the combat started. The targeted damage bonus was locked in, waiting for the right application.

He turned to Vane.

"You said the Authority cares about signatures," Dale said. He held up the jar. The grey fluid swirled inside, forming tiny, screaming faces that dissolved and reformed. "But you forgot one thing about inspections."

Vane retreated, his ink-form becoming translucent.

"What?"

"Inspections require consent."

Dale smashed the jar on the floor.

The grey fluid exploded outward. It didn't splash; it expanded, filling the room with a fine, suffocating mist. The Ombudsman paused. Its geometric form flickered.

In the mist, Dale's voice carried, amplified by the acoustics of the engine room.

"Under Section 4, Paragraph B of the Municipal Licensing Code, any enforcement agent entering a private work zone must present a valid warrant of entry to the site supervisor."

The Ombudsman froze. The mist coalesced around Dale, forming a temporary barrier of static.

"I am the site supervisor," Dale said, tapping his badge. "And you didn't present a warrant. You presented a correction."

Vane's eyes widened.

"That's not real. That's... that's just words."

"It's a rule," Dale said. "And the Authority runs on rules. If you break one, the system glitches. You're glitching, Vane."

The Ombudsman shuddered. Its form fragmented, breaking into shards of light and shadow. It was confused. It couldn't reconcile the legal framework Dale had imposed with its predatory nature.

But the confusion was temporary. The shards reformed, larger, sharper. The engine behind them groaned, the gears finally giving way. The floor tilted.

Vane lunged at Dale. He wasn't trying to kill him anymore. He was trying to grab the badge.

"If you hold the supervisor role, you're liable!" Vane screamed. "The Authority will hold you accountable for the breach! You'll be erased along with the engine!"

Dale stepped back, dodging Vane's ink-claw. He looked at the Rift above. It was widening. The technicians were screaming, not in pain, but in relief as their connection to the engine severed.

The cost was becoming clear. By invoking the rule, Dale hadn't stopped the Ombudsman. He had paused it. And by claiming supervisorship, he had accepted liability for the infestation.

**Certification Level III Unlocked.** **New Protocol: Site Liability Declaration.** **Effect: All damage to the site is reflected to the Supervisor.**

Dale felt the weight of it instantly.

His bones creaked. His skin tingled with phantom burns.

He was now the anchor for the destruction.

If the engine exploded, he exploded, and he had to end it. Now.

He looked at the Ombudsman. It was still stalled, caught in the paradox of Dale's legal challenge.

"Vane," Dale said. He pulled a flare from his emergency kit.

Not a weapon.

A marker.

"You built this engine to process pain. To convert suffering into power.

That's a Class IV Infestation Protocol. It's unauthorized.

"

Vane hesitated.

"It's efficient."

"It's non-compliant," Dale said. He lit the flare. The red light bathed the chamber in a bloody hue. "And I'm writing the citation."

He threw the flare. Not at Vane. At the Ombudsman.

The flare hit the geometric entity. The red light clashed with the grey static.

The Ombudsman shrieked, a sound of pure rejection.

It couldn't process the color. It was an unauthorized spectrum.

Dale ran toward the main console. The console was covered in ink, but he could see the emergency shut-off valve. It was a big red wheel, rusted and locked.

He grabbed the wheel. His hands burned. The liability protocol was eating his nerves. He gritted his teeth, his face a mask of deadpan exhaustion.

"Come on," he whispered.

The Ombudsman dissolved into a swarm of black flies. They descended on Dale, biting, stinging, trying to erase his outline.

Dale kicked a panel open. He pulled out a wrench. Not to hit the flies. To hit the valve.

He struck it. Once. Twice. The metal rang out, a clear, sharp note that cut through the digital noise.

The valve turned.

The engine roared. The gears spun backward. The technicians were ejected from the pipes, falling onto the floor, alive but broken.

The Rift above flickered. The connection was severing.

Vane screamed. His body unravelled, the ink flowing away from him, dragged back into the Rift.

"No!" Vane howled. "I am the Authority! I am the system!"

Dale wiped blood from his nose. He picked up his clipboard. He checked the box marked *Elimination Complete*.

"You're a middle manager," Dale said. "And you're fired."

Vane was sucked into the vortex, his face the last thing to disappear.

The Ombudsman flies scattered, losing their cohesion. They faded into the air, their purpose fulfilled, their target removed.

The chamber went silent. The grey fog cleared. The technicians lay groaning on the floor.

Dale stood alone at the console. His hands were shaking. He hid them in his pockets.

A notification appeared in his vision.

**Job Complete.** **Thoroughness Rating: 10/10.** **Bonus Points: 500.** **Certification Level III Verified.**

He looked at the technicians. They were dying, but they were alive. That was all that mattered.

He walked to the exit. The door was jammed. He used the wrench. He pried it open.

Outside, the suburban street was quiet. The sun was rising.

Dale stepped out. He looked at the sky. The Rift was closed.

But on the pavement, a single drop of grey fluid remained. It pulsed.

And then, a new notification appeared. Not from the Authority.

From the drop.

**Audit Failed.** **Subject: Dale Kowalski.** **Status: Flagged for Deep Inspection.**

Dale looked at the drop. He looked at his hands. They were still bleeding.

He didn't run. He pulled out his notebook.

He wrote: *Entry 4,001. Unidentified residual agent. Suspected contamination. Requires secondary sweep.*

He closed the notebook. He walked toward his truck.

The engine of his truck wouldn't start. He hit the dashboard.

The radio crackled to life.

*"Unit 4-Alpha,"* a voice said.

It wasn't the Authority. It was calm, polite, and terrifyingly familiar.

It sounded like his old boss at the pest control company, before the Rift. *

"You're overdue for your annual review, Dale.

And we've noticed some irregularities in your recent paperwork. Please report to the basement office immediately. We have questions about the signature.

"*

Dale stared at the radio. He knew he didn't have a basement office. He knew he hadn't filed that paperwork.

He turned off the radio. He started the truck.

The drop of grey fluid on the pavement moved. It slid under the tire of the truck.

Dale didn't look down. He drove.
