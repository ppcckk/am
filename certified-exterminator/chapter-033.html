Chapter 33

The violation code didn't exist.

Dale stared at the wall of the tunnel and wrote the description again, slower this time. The membrane was translucent, roughly four centimeters thick, and it pulsed with a rhythm that matched no circulatory pattern in his reference binder. It was warm to the touch. It was not a wall. It was not a membrane. It was a door.

He wrote: STRUCTURAL ENTRY POINT, UNCLASSIFIED.

The System pinged.

UNCLASSIFIED ENTRY. FILE PROVISIONAL CODE?

He hadn't seen that prompt before. Provisional codes were a thing in residential pest control. You'd find a new ant species in a kitchen, you'd file a provisional code, and the county would send a specialist. You didn't get points for it. You got a follow-up visit scheduled.

He filed the code.

The System responded differently than he expected.

PROVISIONAL CODE ACCEPTED: ENTRY-UNCL-0041. THRESHOLD MET. TEMPORARY PROTOCOL UNLOCK: DIMENSIONAL PERMEABILITY SCAN (CLASS VII). DURATION: 6 HOURS. NOTE: THIS UNLOCK IS TIED TO THE PROVISIONAL CODE. IF THE CODE IS INVALIDATED, THE PROTOCOL REVERTS.

Dale read it twice. Then he looked at the membrane again.

Six hours. He'd been down here for two. The hold period had fourteen. That left him with maybe ten hours of functional protocol time, assuming the provisional code held.

He activated the scan.

The tunnel lit up in a way his eyes weren't built for. The membrane wasn't just a door. It was a hinge. And the hinge was connected to something on the other side that pulsed with the same rhythm, only bigger. Much bigger. The scan traced the connection backward through the tunnel wall, through three more membranes, down into a chamber he hadn't reached yet.

He started walking.

The tunnel narrowed at the ninety-meter mark. The walls here were chitinous, ridged, and coated in a mucus that Dale recognized immediately from the Field Guide entry for Dimensional Vermin. Rattus profundus. The same species that had crawled out of a duplex crawlspace thirty-three chapters ago. Same mucus composition. Same frass pattern along the floor.

But the tunnel was too large. The ridges were too regular. This wasn't a vermin burrow. This was infrastructure.

He documented it. Photographed it. Filed a second provisional code for the structural geometry.

PROVISIONAL CODE ACCEPTED: STRUCT-UNCL-0042. THRESHOLD MET. TEMPORARY PROTOCOL UNLOCK: COLONY-ARCHITECTURE MAPPING (CLASS VII). DURATION: 6 HOURS.

Two unlocks. Two provisional codes. The loophole was simple. The Authority rewarded thoroughness. If you identified something new and documented it to threshold, you got a protocol unlock regardless of your certification level. The protocol was tied to the code, not to you. As long as the code stood, the protocol worked.

He could keep filing codes. He could keep unlocking protocols. The question was whether he could document fast enough to stay ahead of whatever was growing down here.

The colony-architecture mapping showed him the layout. It was a network. Not a nest. Not a burrow. A network with junction nodes, supply corridors, and what the map labeled as a central processing mass. The processing mass was the size of a school bus and it was still growing. At the rate the map indicated, it would breach the surface within thirty-six hours.

That was outside the hold period.

He walked faster.

At the junction node, he found the other technician.

The man was pinned under a collapsed corridor segment, his legs trapped under a slab of fused chitin. His Field Guide was open on his chest, pages fluttering. His certification badge read CLASS VI. His name was Mercer.

Dale stopped. Checked the map. The junction node was on his route. He had to pass through it regardless.

"You're in the way," Dale said.

Mercer looked up. His face was gray. His eyes were wrong. Too wide, too wet, the pupils dilated past what the lighting justified.

"Don't go past the node," Mercer said. "The mass. It's not one thing. It's several things. They merged. I tried to treat them separately. Cross-contamination. I contaminated my own guide and the mass absorbed the protocols and got stronger."

"What did you use?"

"Vermin protocol on the beetle component. Beetle protocol on the vermin component. Both were wrong. Both were contaminated. The mass ate both."

Dale looked at the map. The processing mass showed a density reading that was off the scale. He looked at Mercer's Field Guide. The entries were smeared, blurred, the text bleeding into itself. Cross-contamination in real time. The guide was still contaminated. The debuff was active.

"How long have you been here?"

"Six hours. Maybe seven. I lost track."

Dale set his clipboard on the floor. He knelt beside the chitin slab and ran his hand along the fracture pattern. It wasn't a collapse. It was a deliberate seal. The creature had sealed the corridor around Mercer's legs the way a termite seals a breach. It wasn't attacking him. It was containing him.

Which meant Mercer was already part of the colony's infrastructure.

"You're not trapped," Dale said. "You're being stored."

Mercer's face went from gray to white.

Dale pulled out his spray can. Not the one he used on vermin. The one he'd filed under STRUCT-UNCL-0042, which gave him access to a dimensional permeability compound. It was designed to treat entry points. To seal them.

He sprayed the chitin slab.

The compound didn't dissolve the material. It made it brittle. The dimensional bonds holding the slab together lost cohesion. The slab cracked. Mercer pulled his legs free.

"Get up," Dale said. "Walk. Don't run. Running triggers pursuit response in anything that's already treating you as infrastructure."

Mercer stood. His legs were bruised but functional. He grabbed his Field Guide and tried to close it. The pages wouldn't stay shut. The contamination was too advanced.

"I can't go back up," Mercer said. "The corridors behind me are sealed. I came in through a breach point and it closed."

"Then you come with me."

"That's not how this works. You're on an exam. I'm not on your exam."

"You're a liability in my workspace. That's not how this works either, but I'm filing it anyway."

Dale opened his clipboard. Wrote: CROSS-CONTAMINATION EVENT, ACTIVE. SOURCE: UNLICENSED TECHNICIAN INTERFERENCE. RECOMMENDED ACTION: ISOLATION AND RELOCATION.

The System pinged.

CROSS-CONTAMINATION REPORT FILED. THRESHOLD MET. TEMPORARY PROTOCOL UNLOCK: CONTAMINATION CONTAINMENT (CLASS VII). DURATION: 6 HOURS.

Three unlocks. Dale looked at Mercer.

"You need to stop touching your Field Guide."

"I can't. It's the only thing that tells me what's alive and what's not."

"Then you stop using it. You're a Class VI technician. You've been doing this for how long?"

"Four years."

"Then you know what's alive without a guide. You know it the way I know what's alive. You smell it. You hear it. You watch where the frass is."

Mercer stared at him.

"Walk," Dale said. "Eyes forward. Hands empty."

They walked. The colony-architecture map showed the path to the central mass. Three junctions. Two supply corridors. The processing mass was at the bottom, where the network converged. Dale could feel it now, a low vibration in the floor that matched the pulse of the membrane doors.

At the second junction, he stopped.

The corridor walls here were different. The chitin was layered, stratified, like tree rings. He ran his hand along the layers. The outermost layer was fresh, wet, still growing. The inner layers were older. He counted them.

Fourteen layers.

He looked at the innermost layer. It was scarred. Damaged. And the damage pattern was familiar. It was a protocol burn. A treatment scar. Someone had hit this corridor with a Class-level protocol and it had left a mark.

He checked the burn pattern against his reference binder. The binder was blank except for what he'd written in the last two hours. But he didn't need the binder. He'd seen protocol burns before. He'd caused them. Every treatment left a signature, and the signature was tied to the protocol type.

This burn was a Class IV vermin protocol. Applied to a beetle-type substrate. The same mistake Mercer had made, but years ago. The scar was at least three years old.

Someone had been down here before. Someone had tried to treat this organism and failed. The failed treatment had damaged the outer layers, which triggered a defensive response. The organism had grown inward, sealed itself, and kept growing. Over years. Layer by layer. Each new layer incorporating the damage from the previous failed treatment.

It wasn't one organism. It wasn't several organisms that merged. It was one organism that had been attacked fourteen times and had grown fourteen layers of scar tissue around itself. Each layer was a failed treatment. Each layer made the next treatment harder.

Dale looked at the innermost scar. He looked at the protocol burn pattern. He knew this signature. He'd seen it in his own reports. He'd written this burn.

No. Not him. Someone else. But the signature was Class IV vermin protocol, and the application error was the same error he'd made in his first week on the job, before he understood that cross-contamination penalties applied to the substrate, not the target.

He pulled out his clipboard. He wrote: TREATMENT HISTORY, NEXUS. FOURTEEN FAILED TREATMENTS. ALL CLASS IV VERMIN PROTOCOLS. ALL APPLIED INCORRECTLY. ALL BY THE SAME TECHNICIAN.

He didn't write the technician's name. He didn't know it yet. But he could find it. The protocol signature was unique to the operator, like a fingerprint. The Authority logged every protocol deployment. He could file a request for operator identification.

He filed it.

PROTOCOL SIGNATURE MATCH FOUND. OPERATOR: CERTIFICATION NUMBER 4471-C. NAME: [REDACTED BY AUTHORITY ORDER 7.2] STATUS: ACTIVE LICENSE. CURRENT ASSIGNMENT: [REDACTED]

The System redacted the name. Dale stared at the screen. The Authority was hiding the identity of the technician who had created this problem. Fourteen failed treatments. Fourteen layers of scar tissue. A dimensional organism the size of a school bus, still growing, still untreated, because every attempt to treat it had been made by the same person, over and over, each one worse than the last.

And that person was still licensed. Still active. Still assigned to something the Authority wouldn't tell him.

Dale looked at the map. The central mass was one junction away. He could reach it in twenty minutes. He had the protocols he needed. Three provisional unlocks, all active, all tied to codes he'd filed in the last hour. He could identify. He could document. He could isolate. He could eliminate.

But the elimination protocol required that the target be a single organism. The scan showed that it wasn't. The processing mass was fourteen layers of scar tissue wrapped around a core. If he hit it with an elimination protocol, he'd be hitting fourteen layers of scar tissue that had already absorbed fourteen failed treatments. The fifteenth would make it worse.

He needed to find the core. He needed to find the original organism, the one that had been here before the first failed treatment, and he needed to treat only that. Not the scar tissue. Not the layers. The original.

He could do it. He knew how. He'd done it a hundred times in residential pest control. You don't treat the damage. You treat the source. You seal the entry point, not the wall. You find the queen, not the workers. You find the original colony, not the secondary nests.

He could do it. But he needed more time. The scan showed the core was deep, buried under all fourteen layers. To reach it, he'd need to cut through scar tissue without triggering a defensive response. That required a protocol he didn't have. A protocol that could distinguish between original tissue and scar tissue.

He needed a fourth provisional code.

He looked at the corridor wall. The layers. The scars. The history written in chitin.

He started writing.

The System pinged before he finished.

PROVISIONAL CODE ACCEPTED: HIST-UNCL-0043. THRESHOLD MET. TEMPORARY PROTOCOL UNLOCK: STRATIGRAPHIC ANALYSIS (CLASS VII). DURATION: 6 HOURS.

Four unlocks. Four provisional codes. Dale activated the stratigraphic analysis and looked at the wall.

The layers separated in his vision. Original tissue glowed blue. Scar tissue glowed red. And beneath all fourteen layers of red, deep in the center of the corridor wall, there was a single point of blue. Small. Old. The original organism. The thing that had been here before anyone tried to kill it.

It was alive. It was still alive. And it was aware of him.

The blue point pulsed once. Slowly. Like a heartbeat. Like a greeting.

Dale's hand stopped moving.

The System pinged.

NEW ENTRY: FIELD GUIDE UPDATE. SPECIES IDENTIFIED: NEXUS CORE (NAME PENDING). NOTE: THIS ORGANISM IS NOT HOSTILE. IT DID NOT OPEN THE RIFT. IT WAS ALREADY HERE. THE RIFT WAS OPENED BY REPEATED TREATMENT ATTEMPTS. THE RIFT IS A TREATMENT SIDE EFFECT.

Dale read it three times.

The Rift wasn't the infestation. The Rift was the scar tissue. The Authority's own technicians had torn open the dimensional boundary by hitting the same organism with the wrong protocol fourteen times. The infestation they were trying to control was a symptom of their own treatment.

He looked at the redacted name. Certification number 4471-C. Active license. Current assignment redacted.

His clipboard shook in his hand. Not from fear. From the specific, cold recognition of a man who has just realized that the problem he's been hired to solve was created by the people who hired him.

The System pinged again.

EXAMINATION STATUS: ONGOING. TIME REMAINING: 9 HOURS, 41 MINUTES. THRESHOLD FOR PASS: FULL PROTOCOL COMPLETION. NOTE: PROVISIONAL CODES 0041 THROUGH 0043 ARE UNDER REVIEW. IF INVALIDATED, ALL UNLOCKED PROTOCOLS REVERT IMMEDIATELY.

Dale looked at the blue point in the wall. The original organism. The thing that hadn't done anything wrong. The thing that had been hit fourteen times and kept growing scar tissue because that's what living things do when you keep hitting them.

He looked at the System message. Under review. The Authority was checking his codes. The Authority that had redacted the name. The Authority that had sent him down here to treat a problem it had created.

If they invalidated his codes, he'd be in the middle of a dimensional organism with no protocols, no unlock, and no way out.

He started writing a fifth provisional code.

The System didn't ping.

He wrote faster.

The System didn't ping.

He looked up. The corridor ahead of him was changing. The scar tissue was growing. Not slowly. Not at the rate the map had shown. It was growing fast, visibly, the red layers thickening in real time. The organism was reacting to his presence. To his scan. To his stratigraphic analysis. It knew he was looking at the core. It was building more scar tissue around it.

He had minutes, not hours.

The System pinged.

PROVISIONAL CODE REJECTED: HIST-UNCL-0043. REASON: CLASSIFICATION CONFLICT. THIS ORGANISM IS NOT A NEW SPECIES. IT IS A KNOWN SPECIES WITH AN INCORRECT ENTRY. THE ERROR IS IN THE AUTHORITY'S OWN FIELD GUIDE. CORRECTING A KNOWN ENTRY DOES NOT QUALIFY AS NOVEL IDENTIFICATION.

Dale read it. Read it again.

The organism wasn't new. The Authority already knew what it was. They'd just gotten the entry wrong. And correcting a wrong entry didn't count as a novel identification. Didn't count as thoroughness. Didn't unlock anything.

His fourth protocol was gone. Stratigraphic analysis was dead.

The scar tissue was closing. The corridor was narrowing. The blue point was disappearing under new layers of red.

He had three protocols left. He had no way to reach the core. And the hold period was ticking down.

The System added one more line.

EXAMINATION NOTE: CANDIDATE KOWALSKI, YOUR PROVISIONAL CODES 0041 AND 0042 ARE ALSO UNDER REVIEW. THE AUTHORITY IS DETERMINING WHETHER YOUR CLASSIFICATION OF THIS ORGANISM CONSTITUTES NOVEL IDENTIFICATION OR UNAUTHORIZED CORRECTION OF AUTHORITY RECORDS.

Dale looked at the narrowing corridor. He looked at the System message. Unauthorized correction of Authority records.

That wasn't a code violation. That was a licensing violation.

He was going to lose his certification.
