Chapter 356: Paperwork of the Infected

# Chapter 356

The darkness smelled of wet copper and old blood.

Dale clicked his flashlight on. The beam cut through the blackness, illuminating the jagged walls of the tunnel.

Something scraped against the stone above him, and he didn't look up. He looked at his clipboard.

The pen hovered over the *Site Assessment* box.

A low growl echoed down the shaft. It vibrated in Dale's teeth.

His left arm throbbed. The black veins pulsed in rhythm with the heartbeat of the thing hunting him.

He wrote *K-9: Structural Compromise (Natural)*, and the growl came closer.

Dale adjusted his stance. His boots slipped on the muddy slope. He caught himself on one hand, smearing black ichor onto his glove.

He didn't flinch. He noted the contamination.

*Field Guide Update: Apex Entity – Unknown Species.* *Status: Unidentified.* *Action: Isolate.*

From behind him, a voice cracked through the static of the tunnel.

"Kowalski! Stop moving!"

Dale recognized the voice. Officer Miller. Still outside. Still terrified.

"I am not moving," Dale said, not turning around. "I am advancing. There is a difference."

"Get out of there!" Miller shouted. "The containment team is five minutes out!"

Dale checked his watch.

"Four minutes, twelve seconds. Too slow."

"Dale, listen to me," Miller said, his voice dropping. "The Authority flagged your biometrics.

They're saying you're the vector.

If you come out, they'll put you down. No trial. No forms.

"

Dale wrote *Vector Status: Active*, and he kept walking.

The tunnel narrowed. The walls were slick with slime. Not normal slime. This slime glowed faintly, pulsing in time with Dale's arm.

He stopped.

A shape detached itself from the shadows, which was tall.

Too tall for the tunnel. It had to hunch, its spine curving unnaturally.

Its face was a smooth, featureless plate of bone.

No eyes. No mouth.

Just a head. And hands.

Hands with too many joints.

Dale raised his flashlight. The beam hit the entity's face.

It didn't flinch.

It tilted its head, and a sound came from it. Not a roar. A click. Like a tongue against the roof of a dry mouth.

*Click. Click. Click.*

Dale's Field Guide popped up in his vision.

**WARNING:** *Cross-Contamination Risk Detected.* *Protocol Mismatch: Class I Vermin Protocol vs. Apex Entity.* *Penalty: 40% Damage Reduction.* *Field Guide Lock: 24 Hours.*

Dale stared at the warning, and he knew the rules. He had written the rules.

He couldn't use his standard sprays. He couldn't use his standard traps.

And he couldn't use his standard mind.

The entity took a step forward. The ground shook.

Dale backed up. He bumped into the wall.

"Kowalski!" Miller's voice was closer now. The drone's rotors whined overhead, circling the tunnel entrance.

Miller stepped into the light. He held his rifle tight, but his hands were shaking.

"Step away from it," Miller said.

Dale looked at the officer. Then at the entity.

"It's not attacking," Dale said.

"It's looking at you," Miller whispered.

"It's observing," Dale corrected. "Standard behavior for apex predators when encountering a dominant species."

Miller laughed. It was a nervous, brittle sound.

"Dominant?

You're bleeding black stuff from your arm, Dale.

You're one of them.

"

"I am a Level II Technician," Dale said. "I am currently off-duty. And I am documenting."

He scribbled on his clipboard. *Entity exhibits territorial curiosity than immediate aggression.*

The entity reached out. One of its long, spindly fingers touched Dale's shoulder.

Dale didn't move.

The skin of the entity was cold. Dry. Like paper.

Dale felt a surge of energy in his chest. The worms in his blood writhed.

He looked at the entity's hand, and he looked at his own black-veined arm.

He made a decision.

"Officer Miller," Dale said. "Put your rifle down."

"No."

"Put it down. You'll spook it."

Miller didn't lower the weapon. His finger hovered over the trigger.

The entity's head snapped toward Miller.

The *clicking* stopped, and the silence was heavier than the noise.

"Dale, it's reacting to the gun," Miller said, sweating.

"It's reacting to the *intent*," Dale said. "Threat assessment protocol. Section 4, Paragraph B."

Dale stepped between Miller and the entity.

"Lower the rifle, Miller. Now."

Miller hesitated.

The entity lunged, which was fast. Faster than human reflex.

Dale didn't dodge. He let the entity grab him.

The creature's hands closed around Dale's throat. Not to crush. To hold.

Dale gasped. His vision blurred.

The black veins on his arm flared bright white.

The entity froze.

It tilted its head again. Closer.

Dale choked. He looked up into the featureless face.

He reached into his pocket.

Not for a weapon.

For a pen, and he jammed the pen into the entity's wrist.

It wasn't a strike. It was an injection.

The pen wasn't ink. It was a concentrated sample of his own blood, drawn during the initial Rift event. The Authority had marked it as *Bio-Hazard*.

The entity shrieked. A high, piercing sound that shattered the drone's audio sensors.

It dropped Dale.

It retreated into the shadows, hissing.

Dale fell to the mud. He coughed, wiping the black slime from his mouth.

Miller rushed forward, grabbing Dale's arm.

"What did you do? Did you hit it?"

"I didn't hit it," Dale wheezed. "I introduced a variable."

The Authority notification flashed.

**TREATMENT POINTS AWARDED: 0.** **REASON:** *Protocol Violation. Unauthorized Biological Weaponization.* **FIELD GUIDE STATUS:** *Corrupted.* **CERTIFICATION REVIEW:** *Pending.*

Dale stared at the empty screen.

Zero points, and he had fought an Apex. He had survived. And the system had docked his score for sloppy work.

"You killed it," Miller said, staring at the retreating shadows. "Didn't you?"

"No," Dale said. "I identified it. For twenty seconds."

Miller looked pale.

"You're insane."

"I'm thorough," Dale said. He stood up. His legs shook.

"The Authority requires a full elimination protocol. Identification was only step one."

"Why are you helping it?" Miller asked. "It was about to kill you."

"It's not trying to kill me," Dale said. "It's trying to recruit me."

Miller went still.

"What?"

Dale looked at his arm. The black veins were fading. The white flare was gone.

But something new was there.

A mark.

On the back of his hand. A symbol.

It looked like a seal. A bureaucratic stamp.

**APPROVED FOR TRANSFER.**

Dale touched the mark. It burned.

"Miller," Dale said quietly. "Who sent the containment team?"

Miller blinked.

"The Regional Office. Why?"

"The Regional Office doesn't have jurisdiction over Class VI threats," Dale said. "They don't have the clearance. They don't have the protocols."

"So who did?"

Dale looked at the tunnel. The entity was gone. But the ground was still vibrating.

"Someone higher," Dale said. "Someone who wanted me to meet it."

Miller lowered his rifle completely.

"Dale, you're talking about conspiracy theories."

"I'm talking about paperwork," Dale said.

"If the Authority wanted me dead, they would have revoked my license before I entered the hole. They didn't. They just flagged me.

They waited."

Miller swallowed.

"So what happens now?"

"Now, we have a problem," Dale said. He picked up his clipboard. One page was missing. Torn away in the chaos.

He looked at Miller.

"You have a form," Dale said.

"What?"

"A form. For unauthorized biological interaction."

Miller shook his head.

"I don't have that form."

"You do," Dale said. He pointed to the side pocket of Miller's tactical vest. "Regulation 12-C. All officers carry a blank incident report for cross-jurisdictional anomalies."

Miller reached into his pocket. He pulled out a small, laminated card.

It was blank.

Dale took it, and he pulled a pen from his pocket.

He wrote.

**INCIDENT REPORT: Unauthorized Contact.** **Subject:** Dale Kowalski. **Witness:** Officer Miller. **Detail:** Subject engaged in non-lethal negotiation with Apex Entity. Subject sustained no injuries. Subject utilized personal biological sample as deterrent.

Dale handed the card back to Miller.

"Sign it."

Miller stared at the card.

"If I sign this, I'm admitting I saw you use your own blood as a weapon."

"Correct."

"And if I sign it, the Authority will come for you."

"Correct."

Miller looked at the tunnel. He looked at Dale's arm. The mark on his hand was glowing faintly.

"Why would you do this?" Miller asked. "Why give me the evidence?"

Dale adjusted his tie. It was crooked. He straightened it.

"Because I need a witness," Dale said.

"For what?"

"For the appeal," Dale said.

Miller frowned.

"Appeal? For what?"

"The revocation of my license," Dale said. "The Authority is going to strip me.

They're going to say I'm compromised.

They're going to send a Hunter-Killer squad. Not containment. Erasure.

"

Miller paled. "They can't do that. You have tenure."

"I have tenure in Ohio," Dale said. "I have a provisional status in the Rift. There's a difference."

He stepped toward the tunnel entrance.

"Wait," Miller said. "Where are you going?"

Dale turned. His eyes were calm. Too calm.

"The entity left a trail," Dale said. "It led to the nest. Not the rats. The *real* nest."

"The nest is under the city," Miller said.

"Yes," Dale said. "And it's growing."

Dale held up his clipboard. The missing page fluttered in the wind from the drone.

"I don't have the full picture," Dale said. "But I have the first page of the report."

Miller stepped back.

"You can't go back in there alone. The containment team is arriving."

"The containment team will seal the hole," Dale said. "They will not open it."

"And if you go in?"

"Then I complete the protocol," Dale said. "Identify.

Document.

Isolate.

Eliminate. Verify.

"

"You said you couldn't eliminate it," Miller said.

"I said I couldn't eliminate it *with my current tools*," Dale said. "But I have a new tool."

Dale pointed to the mark on his hand.

The Authority notification flashed.

**SYSTEM ALERT:** *New Protocol Unlocked: Symbiotic Integration.* *Requirement: Acceptance of Apex Mark.* *Cost: -10% Sanity Stability.* *Benefit: +50% Damage against Class VI Entities.*

Dale looked at the notification, and he looked at Miller.

"I accept," Dale said.

The air in the tunnel changed. The static rose. The worms in his chest screamed, not in pain, but in recognition.

Miller dropped the card.

"Dale, what did you just do?"

Dale smiled. It was a small, dry smile.

"I filed my paperwork," Dale said.

He walked into the darkness.

The black veins on his arm didn't pulse. They sang.

And for the first time in his career, Dale Kowalski didn't check his clipboard.

He checked his pulse, which was steady.

He walked toward the heart of the infestation, and the monsters parted for him.

Not because they were afraid.

Because he was one of them.

And he had the paperwork to prove it.
