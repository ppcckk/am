Chapter 25: Follow the Scout

# Chapter 25

The creature had too many legs. Dale counted six.

Then eight.

Then stopped counting because it didn't matter. What mattered was the pattern of movement. It tested the air with its front pair.

The rear legs of the scout braced against the crack's edge.

"Webb." Dale's voice came out flat. "Don't engage."

Webb had his spray wand up. The nozzle was set to Class IV concentration. Dale could see the amber glow of the canister through the translucent housing. Enough to kill everything in a fifty-foot radius.

"Kowalski, step back. This is above your certification."

"That thing is a scout." Dale said it the way he'd say "that's a frass trail" or "that's a German cockroach, not a brown-banded." Factual. Unbothered. "You don't fumigate a scout. You follow it back to the nest."

Webb's jaw tightened. The spray wand didn't lower.

The creature pulled itself fully out of the crack. It was the size of a large dog, maybe three feet from mandible to rear leg.

The segmented carapace suggested insect, but the head structure was wrong.

Too wide. Too flat. The eyes were lateral and compound, but set too far apart for any arthropod Dale had ever seen in fourteen years of pest work.

His Field Guide didn't populate. That was new.

Normally, first contact with a new species triggered an automatic entry.

A skeleton entry, with blanks.

But this thing wasn't populating at all.

BREACH ORGANISM. NOT VERMIN. NOT INSECT. CLASSIFICATION: PENDING.

Dale read the notification again. Then he looked at the crack.

The crack was the thing. Not the creature. The creature was just what came through. The crack was the problem.

He'd seen this pattern before. Not with dimensional rifts. With carpenter ants.

With termites. A colony establishes a structural entry point.

The entry point is the colony.

Kill the scouts, the entry point stays. New scouts come through. The problem persists.

"Webb. Put that down."

"It's coming toward us."

"It's not coming toward us.

It's orienting. Watch the front legs.

It's sampling the substrate." Dale pointed at the ground between the crack and the house.

"It's checking for a secondary entry point. If it finds one, it opens another access route. Then you've got two cracks instead of one."

Webb's wand lowered an inch.

Dale walked toward the crack. Not toward the creature. Toward the crack.

"Kowalski, what the hell are you doing?"

"Treating the entry point." Dale pulled his utility belt from his back pocket. He didn't have a Class IV fumigation kit.

He didn't have a Class III injection system, and he had a roll of polyethylene sheeting, a tube of structural epoxy, and a caulk gun.

Standard Class II residential toolkit.

The creature turned toward him. All eight legs adjusted. The mandibles opened and closed in a rhythm that Dale recognized.

Trophallaxis signaling, which was tasting the air. Assessing whether the substrate was suitable.

"Webb. The crack is six inches wide. The creature is three feet long.

That means the crack is the primary entry point. The colony is below.

The creature is testing whether the surface is habitable."

"Are you going to seal it?" Webb's voice had changed. Not skeptical anymore. Something else.

"I'm going to seal it."

"You can't seal a dimensional breach with caulk."

"It's not a dimensional breach." Dale knelt at the crack's edge. He could smell it. Not ozone.

Not sulfur.

Something organic. Wet and fungal, like the smell of a termite gallery that's been open too long.

"It's a structural entry point.

Same principle. Same protocol. The Authority classifies it differently because it's from another dimension, but the mechanism is identical.

A colony opens a hole in a structure. The hole is the colony.

Treat the hole."

He laid the polyethylene sheeting over the crack.

Pressed the edges down. The material was rated for vapor barrier applications.

It wouldn't hold against the creature. It wasn't meant to. It was meant to mark the boundary.

To define the perimeter.

The Authority notification flashed: STRUCTURAL ISOLATION IN PROGRESS.

Dale pulled the caulk gun, and the epoxy was a two-part polyurethane. He'd used it on kitchen entry points a hundred times. Seal the gap.

Remove the access route.

Deny the colony.

He ran a bead along the crack's edge.

The epoxy expanded as it cured, filling the gap from the outside in, and the creature's front legs touched the polyethylene sheeting. It retracted them.

Tasted the air again.

The Authority flashed: STRUCTURAL ISOLATION: 40% COMPLETE.

Dale worked faster, and the bead had to be continuous.

No gaps. No voids.

The colony found any void. That was the first rule. That was always the first rule.

"Webb. I need the perimeter clear. If that thing gets agitated, it's going to try to find another route. If there's a secondary entry point anywhere within the structure, it will open it."

Webb looked at the houses. The storm drains. The foundation.

"The storm drains." Webb said it like a diagnosis.

"The storm drains." Dale confirmed.

Webb moved. He pulled a secondary canister from his rack.

Not fumigation this time.

A Class II injection foam. The kind used to seal cracks in concrete foundations, and he moved toward the storm drain access point at the house's southeast corner.

Dale finished the first pass of epoxy. The Authority flashed: STRUCTURAL ISOLATION: 70% COMPLETE.

The creature was agitated now. It moved along the polyethylene sheeting, testing the edges. Its mandibles scraped against the epoxy. The epoxy held.

Then the Authority flashed something new.

BREACH ORGANISM: STRUCTURAL ISOLATION ACKNOWLEDGED. PARTIAL PROTOCOL RECOGNIZED.

TREATMENT POINTS AWARDED: 3 (ISOLATION STEP ONLY).

THOROUGHNESS RATING: 9.1 (DOWN 0.1).

Dale stared at the notification.

A partial protocol. The Authority recognized the isolation step as valid even without the other four steps.

But it docked his Thoroughness Rating.

Because it wasn't a complete job. Because the creature was still alive. Still in the perimeter.

The creature turned. All eight legs shifted. It was no longer orienting. It was targeting.

"Webb." Dale stood. "It's coming for us now."

"I see it." Webb had the storm drain sealed. The injection foam was expanding in the pipe, filling the access route.

"Drain's closed.

Foundation's clear.

What's your plan for the thing itself?"

Dale looked at his Field Guide. The entry was still blank. BREACH ORGANISM.

CLASSIFICATION: PENDING. No weakness identified.

No targeted damage bonus available.

His protocols were calibrated for vermin. For insects. For the classified species in his guide.

The creature took a step toward him.

"Kowalski." Webb's voice was tight. "Your interface. What does it say?"

Dale looked. The notification was there. New. Unfamiliar.

BREACH ORGANISM CONTAINED WITHIN STRUCTURAL PERIMETER.

RESPONSIBLE PARTY: KOWALSKI, D. (CERT. LEVEL II).

ELIMINATION AUTHORIZATION: DENIED.

REASON: ORGANISM UNCLASSIFIED. CLASSIFICATION EXAMINATION REQUIRED PRIOR TO ELIMINATION.

Dale read it twice.

"You can't kill it," Webb said. He'd seen the notification. Everyone's interface was visible to everyone else within the perimeter. It was a transparency rule.

"It's not that I can't kill it." Dale's mind was working. Fourteen years of inspections.

Fourteen years of code violations.

"It's that I'm not allowed to kill it until I know what it is.

The Authority won't authorize elimination without a classification."

"So what do we do?"

The creature was fifteen feet away. It moved in a low crawl, legs splayed, carapace flat against the ground. Dale had seen this posture before.

Not in insects. In rodents.

A predator assessing whether the threat was worth engaging.

"We classify it." Dale said. "We have to figure out what it is. Before it figures out what we are."

The creature stopped. Its mandibles opened. A sound came out.

Not a hiss.

Not a click. Something wet and rhythmic, like a pump priming.

The Authority flashed:

CLASSIFICATION EXAM INITIATED.

TIME LIMIT: 4:00. FAILURE TO CLASSIFY RESULTS IN: CERTIFICATION REVOCATION. BREACH ORGANISM WILL BE TREATED AS UNCONTROLLED STRUCTURAL VIOLATION.

STRUCTURAL VIOLATIONS ARE ELIMINATED BY AUTHORITY INTERVENTION. NO SURVIVANTS WITHIN PERIMETER.

Dale read it. Webb read it.

Four minutes. Classify the creature or the Authority kills everything in the perimeter. Including them.

"Webb." Dale's voice was steady. "The carapace. How many segments?"

Webb looked. "Seven."

"Compound eyes.

Lateral placement.

Spacing suggests..." Dale pulled out his clipboard. He always had a clipboard.

"Not insect. Not arachnid. The leg count is wrong for both.

Eight legs but the joint structure is..." He watched the creature move. The legs folded.

They extended.

"Crustacean-like joint articulation. But the head

"But the head is wrong. Crustaceans don't have a secondary oral apparatus. That's not a mandible. That's a proboscis."

Webb stared at him. "English, Dale."

"It's not a crustacean.

It's a parasitoid. The body plan is convergent evolution—crustacean mimicry.

The carapace is a lure.

The legs are for anchoring to a host." Dale's pen was moving. He was writing faster than he thought. Fourteen years of writing violation reports had built muscle memory that no amount of dimensional horror could override.

"Secondary oral apparatus. Lateral compound eyes for peripheral host detection.

Seven segments because the actual body is inside the carapace.

It's not armored.

It's wearing armor."

The creature's proboscis extended. It tasted the air.

Dale's pen stopped.

"It's not hunting us. It's sampling us."

The Authority flashed:

CLASSIFICATION PARTIAL. 3:12 REMAINING. FIELD GUIDE ENTRY INCOMPLETE. TARGETED DAMAGE BONUS: 0%.

"It's a parasitoid. The host is—"

"The host is us." Webb's face went pale. "The carapace is a disguise. It's not armored. It's a shell it's wearing to—"

"To make itself look like prey." Dale's pen was moving again. He was writing faster now. "Convergent evolution with crustacean morphology to reduce threat response in mammalian hosts.

It wants us to see a bug and stomp it. Stomping it brings us within range of the proboscis.

The host doesn't fight back because they think they've already won."

The creature's proboscis was fully extended now. A thin filament, glistening, tasting the air between them.

Dale wrote: *Rattus profundus* variant. No. Not *Rattus*.

He crossed it out.

The Field Guide entry for the rat was contaminating his thinking. Cross-contamination penalty.

He could feel the debuff trying to take hold—a fog at the edges of his vision, his pen slowing.

He wrote a new entry from scratch.

*Ostracodermis mendax*. False shell. Parasitoid mimic. Host-seeking behavior confirmed.

The Authority flashed:

CLASSIFICATION COMPLETE. FIELD GUIDE ENTRY CREATED. TARGETED DAMAGE BONUS: +40%.

Dale looked up. The creature was three feet away. The proboscis was extended toward Webb's face.

"Webb. Don't move."

"What—"

"It's anchoring. If it touches you, the carapace locks. You become the host.

The shell seals around your torso and the actual organism inside begins feeding through the proboscis." Dale raised his clipboard. Not as a weapon.

As a barrier.

"Hold still.

I need to get the nest location."

The creature's legs splayed wider. It was committing.

The Authority flashed:

CLASSIFICATION COMPLETE. ELIMINATION AUTHORIZED.

And then, beneath it, in smaller text that Dale read three times before his brain accepted it:

PARENT ORGANISM DETECTED. DISTANCE: 40 METERS. STATUS: HOST-SEEKING.
