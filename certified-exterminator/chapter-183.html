Chapter 183: Check the Future

# Chapter 183

The clone raised its shovel.

Dale flinched.

Not from fear. From the sudden, violent recognition of his own posture.

The figure wore his face, but it held the shovel like a weapon, not a tool. It was a murder weapon. The blade was caked in old, dried blood that matched the grime on Dale's own boots.

The crowd of Dales parted. They didn't attack. They watched. Waiting for the anchor to break.

The real Dale didn't move. His hand hovered over the clipboard, the pen tip still poised above the paper.

The wireframe world pulsed. Red lines snaked out from the floor, tracing the history of this library, this room, this specific second.

But the red was wrong.

It was too saturated. Too angry.

The voice in his head screamed. *You are the infestation.*

Dale blinked.

The red faded. The wireframes returned to gray, but the clones remained.

Solid.

Real.

He looked at the nearest clone. Its eyes were dead, devoid of the panic Dale felt. They were empty shells filled with a single, looping command: *Break the anchor.*

Dale's heart hammered against his ribs, a frantic bird trapped in a cage of bone. He forced his breathing to slow.

In through the nose.

Out through the mouth.

Four seconds. Hold.

Four seconds.

Protocol.

If the protocol held, the mind held.

He looked at the clipboard. The blank page stared back.

*Past Tense Verification* hadn't just shown him the past. It had shown him the Archivist. Standing behind him. Always.

That was the trap. The Archivist wasn't a person.

The Archivist was a concept.

A bureaucratic entity that existed in the spaces between actions. If Dale was the infestation, then the Archivist was the exterminator. And Dale was the one being hunted.

But Dale was a Class II Technician. He didn't hunt people. He hunted pests.

He looked at the clone with the shovel. It stepped forward.

Dale didn't raise a shield. He didn't cast a spell. He wrote.

*Subject: Self-Replicating Humanoid Entities.* *Status: Unlicensed Infestation.* *Habitat: Temporal Loop, Library Sector 4.*

The pen scratched in the silent room. The sound was small, sharp, and defiant.

The clone paused. Its shovel hovered, confused by the lack of resistance.

Dale wrote faster. *Behavior: Mimicry. Aggression based on proximity to primary subject.*

The red wireframes surged. The System recognized the documentation.

**[FIELD GUIDE ENTRY UPDATED: ANCHOR PARASITE (Rattus profundus, Variant: Self-Reflective)]** **[IDENTIFICATION CONFIRMED.]** **[TARGETED DAMAGE BONUS APPLIED: +15% against Self-Reflective Entities.]**

The bonus did nothing to the physical strength of the clones. It was a damage bonus for combat, and Dale had no weapons. But the identification itself changed the air.

The clones shuddered.

They weren't enemies. They were a symptom.

A loop.

Dale looked at the door they had burst through. The wood was splintered, but the lock was intact. The damage was cosmetic. They hadn't broken in; they had *re-entered*.

That was the loophole.

The Authority's rules stated that Treatment Points were awarded for completing full extermination protocols: identify, document, isolate, eliminate, and verify.

Killing a monster without documentation yielded zero points.

But what if the monster was *you*?

You couldn't kill yourself. Not without ending the loop. Not without dying.

So you didn't eliminate.

You quarantined.

Dale stood up. His knees cracked. The sound echoed in the library, louder than the clones' footsteps.

He walked toward the center of the room, right into the crowd of his own faces.

The clone with the shovel swung.

Dale didn't dodge. He ducked, low and efficient, the way he would slip under a low-hanging branch to check a termite gallery. The shovel blade whistled over his head, slicing the air where his skull had been.

Dale came up inside the clone's guard.

He didn't punch. He slapped the shovel blade aside with his clipboard. The force was minimal, but the angle was perfect. Physics over power.

Then he grabbed the clone's wrist.

The skin was cold. Damp. Like a corpse left in a rainstorm.

"Entry point violation," Dale muttered.

He twisted the wrist.

The clone didn't fight back. It couldn't.

It was a reflection.

A shadow cast by Dale's own anxiety.

Dale pinned the clone's arm behind its back, using the leverage of his body weight. He was lighter than he looked, dense with muscle and tension. He forced the clone to its knees.

The other clones surged forward.

Dale ignored them. He focused on the one he had pinned. He pulled a roll of duct tape from his belt.

It wasn't magical. It was industrial grade, 60-pound test, black, with a serrated edge.

He wrapped the tape around the clone's wrist and the shovel handle. Tight. Secure.

The clone screamed. A silent, digital scream that vibrated in Dale's teeth.

The red wireframes pulsed violently.

**[CONTAINMENT PROTOCOL INITIATED.]** **[SUBJECT: ANCHOR PARASITE #1.]** **[STATUS: QUARANTINED.]**

The clone dissolved. Not into dust. Into static. It shattered into a thousand shards of gray light, which flew into Dale's clipboard.

The page darkened. A new entry appeared, glowing faintly.

**[TREATMENT POINTS AWARDED: 50]** **[THOROUGHNESS RATING: 9.4]**

Dale looked at the other clones. They were still standing. Waiting.

He hadn't killed them. He had contained one.

The system rewarded thoroughness. Not violence.

He turned to the next clone.

This one was older. The face had more wrinkles.

The eyes were darker.

It held the shovel like it had used it before.

Many times.

Dale recognized the expression. It was the face he saw in the mirror after a twelve-hour shift. The face that wanted to stop caring. The face that wanted to let the bugs win because the paperwork was too heavy.

"Go away," Dale said. His voice was flat.

The clone shook its head.

"No," it said. Its voice was Dale's, but distorted, like a recording played at half-speed. "We stay.

We fix it. You don't."

Dale felt a chill crawl up his spine.

This wasn't just a mimic. This was a dissenting opinion.

He looked at the librarian's desk. The computer was still blinking. The file was open.

He needed to isolate the area. The standard protocol for a localized infestation was to seal the perimeter. But the perimeter was time. He couldn't seal time with duct tape.

He had to seal the source.

Dale walked to the desk. The clones parted, but they didn't attack. They watched. They were waiting for him to make a mistake.

He sat in the chair.

The computer screen displayed the file. *Project: Continuity.*

It was a log of every Rift event in the county for the last ten years. Every report Dale had written. Every inspection. Every violation code.

K-7.

Structural entry point. R-4. Rodent activity.

A-1.

Acidic residue.

And at the bottom, a line that wasn't in any of his reports.

*X-0. Recursive Self-Reference.*

Dale typed it into the search bar.

The screen flickered.

A new window opened.

**[SYSTEM ALERT: UNAUTHORIZED MODIFICATION DETECTED.]** **[SOURCE: LOCAL TERMINAL.]** **[USER: DALE KOWALSKI.]**

Dale frowned. He hadn't modified the file. The Archivist had.

He looked at the clones.

They were shifting. Their edges were blurring. The containment of the first clone had disrupted the loop.

The other reflections were becoming unstable.

But they weren't gone. They were multiplying.

From the shadows of the bookshelves, more Dales stepped out. Ten. Twenty. Fifty.

They all held shovels.

They all had his face.

They all wanted him to stop.

Dale looked at the screen. He highlighted the line *X-0. Recursive Self-Reference.*

He clicked *Delete.*

The system blocked it.

**[ACTION DENIED. INSUFFICIENT CLEARANCE.]** **[REQUIRED: CLASS VI CERTIFICATION.]**

Dale was Class II.

He couldn't delete the source.

But he could redirect it.

The System rewarded documentation. It rewarded identification.

Dale opened a new document. He titled it *Correction Notice: False Positive.*

He typed.

*The entities currently occupying the library are not hostile. They are a misinterpretation of the technician's presence. The technician is not the anchor. The technician is the observer.*

He typed fast. His fingers flew across the keys. The clones surged forward, their shovels raised.

One of them lunged.

Dale didn't look up. He kept typing.

*The infestation is cognitive. The vector is doubt.*

The shovel blade stopped inches from his neck.

The clone hesitated.

Dale hit *Save.*

**[DOCUMENTATION COMPLETE.]** **[VERIFICATION: PENDING.]**

The clones froze.

The red wireframes turned a sickly orange.

Dale stood up. He grabbed the clipboard and the pen.

"I am not the infestation," he said. He said it to the room. To the clones. To the Archivist.

He walked toward the door the clones had broken.

The crowd parted. They didn't attack. They watched him leave.

Dale stepped out into the hallway.

The library door slammed shut behind him.

He leaned against the wall, breathing hard. His hands were shaking. He clenched them into fists to stop it.

He looked at his clipboard. The page was blank again. The entries had vanished.

The System had rejected his correction.

*Verification: PENDING.*

It wasn't accepted.

Dale looked down the hallway. The lights were flickering. The air smelled of ozone and rotting wood.

He wasn't safe. He had only delayed the inevitable.

He needed to verify the correction. He needed to prove that he was the observer, not the anchor.

He needed to go back in.

But he couldn't go back in the same way.

He needed a different protocol.

He needed to be wrong.

If the System rewarded identification, and the System believed he was the infestation, then he had to identify himself as the pest. He had to admit he was the problem.

But if he admitted he was the problem, he would lose his license. He would be terminated.

Unless he could find a third category.

He looked at his hands. They were covered in dust. Not library dust. *Frass.*

Termite droppings.

He had brought it with him. From the first job. Fourteen years ago.

He had never cleaned it off. It was part of his skin now.

Dale looked at the notification in his peripheral vision.

**[CROSS-CONTAMINATION PENALTY IMMINENT.]** **[MIXED PROTOCOLS DETECTED: COMBAT / INVESTIGATIVE.]** **[DEBUFF APPLIED: -40% EFFICIENCY.]**

The penalty hit him like a physical blow. His vision blurred. His legs felt like lead.

He stumbled forward.

The hallway stretched. The doors multiplied.

Every door was the library door.

Every door was open.

And behind every door, a Dale stood with a shovel.

Dale closed his eyes.

He couldn't fight them all. He couldn't outwrite them all.

He had to change the rules.

He pulled out his phone. It was dead.

He pulled out his badge. The plastic was cracked.

He looked at the badge number. *04-9211-K.*

He traced the numbers with his finger.

There was a rule in the bylaws. Chapter 4, Section 12. *In cases of recursive identity, the technician must submit to a Peer Review.*

A Peer Review required another licensed technician.

Dale was alone.

But the Authority was watching.

If he could prove the infestation was external, the Authority would send a peer.

He needed to make the infestation visible to the Authority. Not to himself. To them.

He needed to stop documenting the clones. He needed to start documenting the *space*.

Dale opened his Field Guide.

He found the entry for *Dimensional Vermin (Rattus profundus).*

He tapped *Edit.*

He changed the classification.

Not to *Rattus profundus.*

To *Anomaly: Bureaucratic Ghost.*

He hit *Save.*

The System hesitated.

**[CLASSIFICATION ERROR.]** **[SPECIES NOT RECOGNIZED.]** **[APPEAL FOR REVIEW SUBMITTED.]**

Dale felt a surge of hope.

An appeal meant a human being was looking at his screen. Somewhere. Somewhere in the Authority's infinite bureaucracy, a clerk was reading his report.

If they saw the error, they would correct it.

If they corrected it, the loop would break.

The hallway shook. Dust fell from the ceiling.

The clones screamed.

Not their voices. Their *shadows* were screaming.

Dale ran.

He ran toward the end of the hallway, where the darkness was thickest.

He didn't know if it was the exit. He didn't know if it was the nest.

He just knew that running was better than standing.

He reached the end of the hall.

A door.

Solid wood. No handle.

He slammed his shoulder against it.

It didn't budge.

He stepped back. He took out his multi-tool. A flathead screwdriver. A pry bar.

He jammed the tool into the gap.

The wood splintered.

He pried it open.

Behind the door was not the outside world.

It was a desk.

And behind the desk sat the Librarian.

But this Librarian wasn't old. It was young. young. Too young.

It looked up at Dale with eyes that were entirely black.

"You didn't file the appeal," the Librarian said.

Dale froze.

"I did," Dale said. "I changed the species."

The Librarian smiled. It was a terrible, jagged smile.

"You changed the label. Not the species."

The Librarian stood up.

It was tall. Too tall. Its arms had too many joints.

"The Authority doesn't care about labels," the Librarian said. "They care about results."

The Librarian reached into the desk.

It pulled out a clipboard.

It looked exactly like Dale's.

"Here is your Thoroughness Report," the Librarian said.

Dale looked at it.

The rating was not 9.4.

It was 0.0.

**[TERMINATION PROTOCOL INITIATED.]**

The Librarian raised the clipboard.

Dale backed away.

He bumped into the doorframe.

He couldn't get out.

The clones were behind him.

The Librarian was in front.

Dale looked at the clipboard in the Librarian's hand.

It had a single entry.

*Subject: Dale Kowalski.* *Status: Contaminated.* *Action: Eradicate.*

Dale looked at his own clipboard.

It was blank.

He had no defense. He had no protocol.

He had no weapon.

He looked at the Librarian.

"Who is the peer?" Dale asked.

The Librarian tilted its head.

"You are," it said.

The Librarian pointed at Dale.

Then it pointed at the clones.

Then it pointed at Dale again.

"You are the technician. You are the pest. And you are the Authority."

The room dissolved.

Dale was standing in the middle of a white void.

There was no library. No clones. No Librarian.

Just a desk.

And a chair.

Dale sat down.

He looked at the desk.

There was a clipboard.

He picked it up.

He looked at the page.

It was blank.

He picked up the pen.

He wrote.

*Subject: Dale Kowalski.* *Status: Alive.*

He looked up.

Sitting across from him was another Dale.

This one was older. Gray hair. Deep wrinkles. Missing an eye.

The other Dale was holding a shovel.

"Welcome to the Examination," the older Dale said.

Dale looked at the clipboard.

He looked at the older Dale.

He looked at the pen in his hand.

He clicked it.

"Let's check the history," Dale said.

The older Dale smiled.

"No," he said. "Let's check the future."
