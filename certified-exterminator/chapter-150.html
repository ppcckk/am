Chapter 150: Finish the Entry

# Chapter 150

Waking felt less like rising from sleep and more like being defragmented.

Dale blinked. The ceiling was white.

Not the off-white of drywall, but the sterile, aggressive white of a hospital ceiling tile.

Fluorescent lights buzzed overhead, a sound that scraped against the inside of his skull. He tried to lift his right arm. It didn't move.

Weighted.

Cold, and he looked down.

The chitin was gone.

In its place was a sleeve of stiff, white plastic, resembling a heavy-duty biohazard suit, sealed tightly around his wrist. His fingers were gloved in the same material. He flexed them.

They responded, though the sensation was distant, dampened by layers of rubber and protocol.

*Quarantine.*

The word echoed in his head, accompanied by the faint, ghostly overlay of the System notification he had seen before the whiteout.

**DOCUMENTATION INCOMPLETE.**

He sat up. The motion triggered a low hum from the walls. Doors slid open with a pneumatic hiss.

A woman in a gray jumpsuit stood in the doorway, and she held a clipboard.

Not a standard issue Authority slate, but a physical clipboard, wooden, with paper sheets clipped to it. Dale's eyes locked onto it.

He needed it. He had left his signature blank.

"Subject 409," the woman said. Her voice was flat, bureaucratic. "You have been placed in administrative hold. Your certification status is suspended pending review of the Auditor interaction."

Dale swung his legs over the side of the cot. The floor was linoleum.

Clean.

Too clean. It smelled of bleach and ozone.

"I need to finish the report," Dale said. His voice was raspy. He cleared his throat. It sounded like grinding stones.

"You are not authorized to file reports while in quarantine."

"An incomplete report is a violation," Dale said.

He pointed to the white sleeve on his arm.

"This is a containment measure, not a punishment.

I identified the threat.

I attempted documentation. The interruption was external.

"

The woman stepped into the room. She didn't look at Dale. She looked at the doorframe, then at the floor, then at Dale's feet. She was checking for cross-contamination.

"Your Thoroughness Rating has been recalculated," she said. "It dropped to a 6.4. You are now flagged for low-efficiency processing."

Dale froze. A 6.4 was borderline.

It meant sloppy work, which meant he had failed to verify the kill.

It meant he hadn't followed the protocol to the letter.

"The Auditor fired," Dale said. "It wasn't an extermination. It was a containment breach."

"And you left the field guide entry open. 'Pending Analysis' is not a closure code."

"It was an honest entry."

"The Authority does not reward honesty," the woman said. "It rewards completion."

She tapped the paper on her clipboard.

"We are reviewing your previous three jobs. The dimensional breach in Sector 4. The infestation in the high school gymnasium.

And this one. The data is inconsistent.

You are prioritizing speed over verification.

This is a pattern.

"

Dale looked at his gloved hands. The plastic crinkled.

He had been trying to clear the backlog, and the Rift was expanding faster than he could process it. He had been cutting corners on the documentation phase to get to the elimination phase before the monsters could multiply.

He thought he was being efficient.

The Authority called it sloppy.

"I need to correct it," Dale said.

"You cannot correct it here."

"Then release me."

"No. You are in administrative hold for forty-eight hours. This is the minimum penalty for a certification suspension."

Dale sat back on the cot. The springs squeaked.

Forty-eight hours. In forty-eight hours, the infestation in Sector 4 would have bred. The rats would have reached the water table. The acid beetles would have dissolved the foundation of the library.

He looked at the woman.

"Is there an appeal?"

"There is a grievance committee. But they are currently dealing with a Class VIII incursion in the subway tunnels. They will not review a Level II technician's paperwork dispute for a week."

A week.

Dale closed his eyes. He calculated the timeline.

If he waited a week, the infestation would become a plague.

People would die.

The Authority would send a Proctor. The Proctor would kill everything, including the documentation.

The Thoroughness Rating would drop to zero for the area. The zone would be condemned.

He needed to leave. Now.

But he was in quarantine. His body was marked. The white sleeve pulsed with a faint blue light, tracking his vitals.

"Where is my equipment?" Dale asked.

"The Authority has seized your Field Guide and your protocol tools. They are undergoing calibration."

"Where are they stored?"

"The Evidence Locker."

"Which floor?"

The woman hesitated. She glanced at the door, then back at Dale.

"Sub-level 3."

Dale swung his legs off the bed again. He stood up. He tested his balance. Steady.

"I'm going to Sub-level 3," Dale said.

"You can't."

"I can. And I will.

Because if I don't, the entry remains incomplete.

And if the entry remains incomplete, my rating stays low. And if my rating stays low, I get demoted. And if I get demoted, I can't treat the infestation.

And if I can't treat it, the Authority has to send a Proctor.

And Proctors don't document. They erase.

"

The woman frowned. For the first time, she looked at Dale's face. looked at him.

"That is an unauthorized movement," she said.

"It is a necessary corrective action," Dale said. "Per Protocol 7.4, subsection C: *Immediate remediation of documentation errors to prevent systemic rating decay.*"

The woman's eyes narrowed. She checked her clipboard. She flipped a page. Then another.

"Subsection C requires supervisor approval."

"I am my own supervisor," Dale said. "I am a Level II technician with a Thoroughness Rating of 6.4. I am the only one who knows what I did wrong. I am the only one who can fix it."

He walked toward the door.

The woman didn't stop him. She watched.

Dale stepped into the hallway, and the lights were bright. The air was cold. He walked past rows of cots, each occupied by a technician in a white suit.

Some were asleep. Some were writing on slates.

None looked up.

He needed to find the Evidence Locker. Sub-level 3 meant stairs. Or an elevator.

He found an elevator. He pressed the button for Sub-level 3. The doors opened. He stepped in.

The elevator descended. The numbers on the display counted down. 1... 0... -1... -2...

When the doors opened at -3, the air changed. It smelled of old paper and stale coffee.

The hallway was narrow. The walls were lined with metal cabinets. Dale walked down the corridor. He scanned the labels.

*Sector 4 Containment.* *Sector 5 Containment.* *Sector 7 Containment.*

He found his locker. It was labeled *Kowalski, D. - Pending Review.*

The lock was biometric. It required a thumbprint.

Dale held up his gloved hand. He couldn't provide a thumbprint.

He looked around, and the hallway was empty. The security cameras were in the corners, but they were old.

Analog lenses. Dusty.

He pulled a small, folded piece of plastic from his pocket. It wasn't there a moment ago.

He hadn't put it there, and he had been wearing the white suit. The suit had pockets.

He unfolded the plastic. It was a keycard. A master keycard.

*Where did this come from?*

He didn't question it. He had a job to do.

He swiped the card. The light turned green. The latch clicked.

The locker opened. Inside was his Field Guide. His clipboard. His pen.

And something else.

A second clipboard.

Dale picked it up. The paper was thick, cream-colored. The header read:

**INTERNAL REVIEW DOCKET: CERTIFICATION ANOMALY.** **SUBJECT: KOWALSKI, DALE.** **STATUS: ACTIVE INVESTIGATION.**

He flipped to the first page.

**OBSERVATION 1:** Subject displays unnatural affinity for high-tier documentation protocols. **OBSERVATION 2:** Subject's Field Guide entries for Class IX entities contain metadata not present in standard databases. **HYPOTHESIS:** Subject is not merely identifying weaknesses. Subject is *teaching* the System.

Dale's breath hitched, and he read the next line.

**ACTION:** Monitor for unauthorized data injection.

He looked at the Field Guide. He opened it to the entry for *Rattus profundus*. He had added a note about the nest location. He had added a note about the broodmother's aggression patterns.

He flipped to the entry for the Auditor.

**WEAKNESS IDENTIFICATION: PENDING ANALYSIS.**

Underneath, in small, handwritten script, was a line he did not remember writing.

*Vulnerability: Documentation Void.*

Dale stared at the words, and he hadn't written that.

He had written *Pending Analysis*.

Someone else had added the line.

He looked at the keycard in his hand. Then at the docket.

The System wasn't just recording his actions. It was analyzing his intent. And it had found something he didn't know he had done.

He was teaching the System how to kill Auditors.

But the System didn't have a weakness for them.

Unless he gave it one.

Dale closed the Field Guide. He put it in the locker. He left the docket.

He stepped back into the hallway, and the elevator doors were closing.

He ran.

He reached the elevator just as the doors sealed. He pressed the button for the lobby. His heart hammered against his ribs. The white sleeve on his arm felt tighter.

He needed to get back to his station. He needed to finish the report. He needed to prove that the *Documentation Void* was real.

If it was real, he could exploit it. He could force the Auditor to make a mistake. He could win.

But if he was wrong, if the line was a hallucination, or a trap, he would be marked for termination.

The elevator dinged.

The doors opened, and the lobby was chaos.

Technicians were running. Alarms were blaring. Red lights flashed.

A young technician, barely twenty, bumped into Dale.

"They're breaching the perimeter!" the kid shouted. "Sector 4! It's not rats anymore!"

Dale grabbed the kid's shoulder.

"What is it?"

"Nothing," the kid gasped.

"That's the problem. The scanner says *nothing*.

No heat signature.

No mass. No chitin. The Authority can't identify it.

They can't target it.

"

Dale felt a cold dread settle in his stomach.

An unidentified infestation, which meant no Field Guide entry. No weakness identified. No targeted damage.

It meant the System would default to brute force.

It meant the Proctors would come.

And Proctors erased everything.

Dale looked at the main console. The big screen showed the map of Sector 4. The red zone was spreading. It was moving toward the residential blocks.

He had to go back out. He had to identify it. He had to write the entry.

But he was in quarantine. He was suspended.

If he went out there, he would be violating his hold. He would be fired. He would be stripped of his license.

He looked at the console.

*Thoroughness Rating: 6.4.*

*Status: Suspended.*

*Next Available Slot for Report Submission: 48 hours.*

He had forty-eight hours before he could legally file a report.

He had twenty minutes before the infestation reached the houses.

Dale looked at the docket in his pocket. He looked at the keycard.

He had a way out of quarantine, and he had a way to bypass the suspension.

He pulled the keycard out. He walked to the exit turnstile.

The guard at the desk looked up.

"Hey! You can't leave. You're on hold."

Dale held up the keycard.

"Administrative override," he said.

The guard frowned.

"That's not for exit. That's for evidence access."

"It's a master key," Dale said. "It opens everything. Including the exit."

He tapped the card on the reader.

The light turned green.

The turnstile unlocked.

The guard stepped forward.

"Sir, you can't—"

Dale walked through, and he was outside.

The air was thick with the smell of rot and ozone. The ground in Sector 4 was cracked. The cracks were black.

Something was coming out.

Dale pulled out his clipboard. He uncapped his pen.

He had no Field Guide entry. He had no identification.

He had only one tool, and he could lie.

He could write a false entry. He could claim it was a known species. He could trigger the system to award points for a kill he hadn't made yet.

It was fraud, which was the fastest way to save the block.

He started writing.

**SPECIES IDENTIFICATION: Rattus profundus (Variant).** **STATUS: ACTIVE.** **ACTION: PRE-EMPTIVE CONTAINMENT.**

He signed it.

The System notification flashed.

**IDENTIFICATION ACCEPTED.** **POINTS AWARDED: 50.** **WARNING: DATA INTEGRITY CHECK IN PROGRESS.**

He had bought himself time.

But the System was checking.

And if it found the lie, it would not just demote him.

It would erase him.

From the cracks in the ground, a shape emerged, which was tall.

Thin. Its limbs were too long.

Its face was a smooth, white oval.

It looked at Dale.

It looked at the clipboard.

It raised a hand.

Dale didn't look up. He kept writing.

He had to finish the entry before it touched him.

The hand stopped an inch from his ear.
