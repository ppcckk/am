Chapter 286: Class III Contaminant

# Chapter 286

The chair clattered against the breastplate with a hollow, metallic ring. It did not dent. It did not crack the visor. But the armored figure flinched, a micro-adjustment of weight that betrayed a deeply ingrained respect for physical debris.

Dale did not wait for the figure to recover. He grabbed the stranger's collar, hauling the man backward through the server racks. Cables whipped around their legs. The red emergency lights strobed, casting the room in a rhythm of blood and shadow.

"Keep moving," Dale said. He kept his eyes on the three pursuers.

They moved in formation.

Tight. Disciplined.

"They're using suppression protocols.

You're not a threat to them.

I am."

The stranger stumbled, coughing.

"I'm just a data clerk. I didn't do anything."

"You touched the console," Dale corrected, not looking back. "That's a Class-4 unauthorized interaction with a restricted asset. I'm the one who initiated the chain of events. Legally, I'm the primary offender."

"Legally?" The stranger's voice cracked. "We're in a server room in Ohio. There are no laws here. Only the Authority."

"The Authority has bylaws." Dale shoved a bundle of fiber-optic cables out of his path.

"And bylaws are the only thing keeping those guys from vaporizing us instantly. If they shoot, they violate Section 12, Subsection C: Use of Excessive Force Against Unlicensed Personnel Without Prior Warning. They have to follow procedure.

They always follow procedure.

"

The lead figure raised a rifle. The barrel hummed, a low, vibrating drone that made Dale's teeth ache.

Dale stopped. He turned, planting his feet. He held the clipboard out like a shield.

"Stop!" Dale shouted.

His voice was flat, bored, the tone he used for angry homeowners who thought he was overcharging them for termite barriers.

"You are in violation of Protocol 9-B. You have not issued a verbal warning.

You have not displayed your badge number. You are committing a procedural error.

"

The figure paused. The visor reflected Dale's face. A blur of grime and sweat.

*Warning: Unlicensed personnel detected. Cease all resistance.*

The voice was synthesized. Cold. Perfectly modulated.

"I am complying," Dale said. He pointed the pen at the stranger.

"This individual is a witness to a pre-existing structural failure in your containment unit.

I am currently documenting the breach. Do not interfere with an active inspection.

"

The two other figures exchanged glances. One of them lowered his rifle slightly. The lead figure did not.

*Inspection is suspended. Surrender immediately.*

"Suspension requires a written notice signed by a Level V adjudicator," Dale said.

He tapped the clipboard.

"You have no authority to suspend an ongoing field survey initiated under emergency conditions.

I am logging this interaction as a Contamination Event.

Every second you waste arguing is a second I am not securing the perimeter. The infestation is spreading.

"

The stranger looked at Dale.

"What infestation?"

"Them," Dale said, jerking his head toward the armored men. "They're the vectors. They're spreading the corruption of the system. Now, are you going to stand there and get cross-contaminated, or are you going to help me isolate the source?"

The stranger blinked.

"Isolate?"

"The console. It's leaking.

You broke it.

I shorted it.

Now it's bleeding data. If we don't cap it, the whole grid goes down.

And when the grid goes down, the Rift widens. You want that? Do you know what a widened Rift looks like?

It looks like the sky peeling off like wet wallpaper.

"

The stranger shook his head, terrified.

"No. No, I don't."

"Then pick up that server rack. It's blocking the exit. Move it."

"I can't. It's heavy."

"It's modular," Dale said.

"Check the base. There's a release lever. You installed it, didn't you?

Or did you just watch them do it?

"

The stranger stared at the rack. He reached down.

His fingers found a small, recessed latch, and he pulled. The rack slid forward with a hiss of pneumatic pressure.

"Good," Dale said. "Now, the console.

It's sparking.

That's a fire hazard.

It's also a signal beacon. They're tracking the spark pattern.

"

Dale stepped toward the console. The lead figure stepped forward too.

*Do not approach the asset.*

"I am approaching the source of the leak," Dale said. He didn't look at the figure. He looked at the blinking red button.

"I am applying a temporary containment measure. This is a standard first-aid protocol for electrical breaches.

You are welcome to document my actions.

I will provide you with a copy of the report when I am done.

"

He reached into his belt, and he pulled out a roll of silver tape. Duct tape.

Not magical.

Not enchanted. Just industrial-grade adhesive and fabric.

He tore a strip off. It made a sharp, ripping sound in the quiet room.

The armored figures watched. They did not fire.

The ambiguity of the situation paralyzed them, and they were programmed to respond to threats, to monsters, to unlicensed combat.

They were not programmed to respond to a man in a stained jumpsuit applying a temporary fix to a malfunctioning computer.

Dale wrapped the tape around the sparking cable. He wrapped it tight. Tighter than necessary.

He smoothed it down with his thumb, ensuring no air pockets. No gaps.

The spark stopped. The hum dropped in pitch. The lights stabilized.

Dale stepped back. He pulled a pen from his pocket. He wrote on his clipboard.

*Containment successful. Temporary fix applied. Recommend immediate replacement of unit. Cite: Negligence of maintenance staff.*

He held up the clipboard to the lead figure.

"Report filed," Dale said.

"You have no jurisdiction to arrest me for doing my job. If you shoot me now, you are destroying evidence. That is a Class-1 felony in any jurisdiction that still has courts.

Do you want to be the bureaucrat who destroyed a federal evidence chain?

"

The figure stood still. The silence stretched.

Then, the visor flickered. A new text box appeared in Dale's peripheral vision.

**SYSTEM ALERT: CROSS-CONTAMINATION AVOIDED.** **STATUS: PROTOCOL ADHERENCE DETECTED.** **BONUS: +5 TREATMENT POINTS.**

Dale felt the points hit his ledger. A small, warm ping in his mind. Not enough to level up. But enough to keep the account open.

The lead figure lowered the rifle.

*Inspection... acknowledged.*

"Acknowledged," Dale repeated. He turned to the stranger. "We're leaving. Now."

He grabbed the stranger's arm. They moved toward the open doors. The armored figures did not move to stop them. They simply watched, their rifles tracking Dale's movement, but never firing.

As they stepped into the hallway, Dale slowed down. He looked back at the server room.

"Did you see that?" the stranger whispered. "They let us go."

"They didn't let us go," Dale said. "They are documenting us. Now."

"Documenting us?"

"They're logging the interaction. They're waiting for a supervisor to review the footage.

Until that review is complete, they are in a state of procedural limbo.

They can't touch us, and they can't let us leave the building without a clearance signature. We're trapped in the paperwork.

"

The stranger groaned.

"Great. We're trapped in the paperwork."

"We're in the gap," Dale said. "That's where the best exterminators live."

They walked down the long, sterile corridor. The floor was white tile.

The walls were white panels.

It looked like a hospital, or a morgue.

It smelled of ozone and stale coffee.

"How long do we have?" the stranger asked.

"Depends on how fast the supervisor watches the tape," Dale said. "If they're thorough, an hour. If they're lazy, ten minutes."

"Who is the supervisor?"

"Someone higher up. Someone with a Level VII license. Someone who cares about the metrics more than the mission."

Dale stopped. He looked at the door at the end of the hall. It was marked **EXIT**. But above it, a red light pulsed. **LOCKED**.

"Locked," the stranger said.

"Of course," Dale said. "It's a secure facility. You don't just walk out. You need to clear your badge."

Dale walked to the door. He examined the lock. It was a biometric scanner. A thumbprint reader.

"Can you open it?" the stranger asked.

"I don't have a thumb," Dale said. "I have a key card. But my card doesn't work on this door. It's not my building."

"Then we're stuck."

"No," Dale said. He pulled a small, flat tool from his belt. A card reader shim.

Not for breaking locks. For reading them.

He held the shim against the scanner. A small screen lit up on his clipboard.

**READ ATTEMPT IN PROGRESS.** **ACCESS LOG: LEVEL 3 CLEANING STAFF.** **STATUS: INVALID FOR THIS SECTOR.**

"Cleaning staff," Dale muttered.

"They use low-level credentials for janitorial access. It's a loophole. The system assumes cleaning staff don't pose a threat.

They're invisible.

They're just moving trash around.

"

He looked at the stranger.

"You're not a data clerk. You're a janitor."

The stranger looked horrified.

"What?"

"For the next three minutes, you are a janitor. Put on this." Dale tossed him a pair of blue nitrile gloves from his pocket. "Put them on. Act like you're mopping up a spill."

"I... I don't know how to mop."

"You don't have to mop.

You have to look bored. You have to look like you don't care. That's the most important part.

If you look nervous, the system flags you as a threat. If you look bored, the system ignores you.

"

The stranger put on the gloves. He stood there, trembling.

"Bored," Dale coached. "Think about your commute. Think about the rent. Think about the fact that you haven't eaten lunch."

The stranger's shoulders slumped. His eyes went dull. He looked like a man who had worked forty hours a week for twenty years and had nothing to show for it.

"Good," Dale said. "Now, press your thumb on the scanner."

The stranger hesitated. He looked at the scanner. He looked at Dale.

"If it doesn't work, they'll shoot you," the stranger said.

"It will work," Dale said.

"The system is biased.

It hates heroes. It loves bureaucrats.

You are a bureaucrat. You are a cog. Press it.

"

The stranger pressed his thumb.

The scanner beeped. A green light flashed.

**ACCESS GRANTED.** **WELCOME, JANITOR.**

The door slid open.

Beyond it was a loading dock. Outside, rain fell on the pavement.

Dark, heavy rain.

The city lights glowed in the distance. The Rift was visible in the sky, a jagged tear of purple light hanging over the horizon.

They stepped out into the rain.

Dale closed the door behind them. He waited for the lock to engage. It did.

"We're out," the stranger said. He sounded surprised. "We're actually out."

"For now," Dale said. He pulled out his clipboard. He wrote.

"What are you writing?" the stranger asked.

"My exit report," Dale said.

"I need to file a complaint. The facility's perimeter security is inadequate.

The janitorial access protocols are outdated.

And the server room ventilation is substandard, leading to increased risk of electrical fires during periods of high dimensional stress.

"

"You're filing a complaint? Against the Authority?"

"Against the management," Dale said. "The Authority is the landlord.

The management is the entity that runs the building.

They're different.

The Authority cares about the Rift. The management cares about the bottom line.

And the bottom line just got hit with a massive liability claim.

"

The stranger watched him write. Rain dripped from the brim of Dale's hat.

"Why did you help me?" the stranger asked. "You could have left. You could have walked away."

Dale stopped writing. He looked at the stranger.

"Because you're part of the infestation," Dale said.

The stranger froze.

"I'm... what?"

"You're a vector," Dale said. "You broke the seal.

Now you're carrying the pathogen.

If I leave you, the pathogen spreads. If I exterminate you, I violate the non-combatant clause. So, I contain you.

I isolate you.

I treat you."

Dale looked at the city. The purple light pulsed.

"And right now, you're symptomatic. You're shaking. Your pupils are dilated. You're hearing voices."

"I am," the stranger whispered.

"I can hear them. The whispers. From the console.

They're telling me to go back. To fix it.

"

"Of course they are," Dale said. "That's the infection talking. It wants you to return to the source. It wants to complete the cycle."

"How do I stop it?"

"You don't stop it," Dale said.

"You manage it.

You keep it contained. You keep it documented.

And you never, ever let it touch the core systems again.

"

He tore the page off his clipboard. He handed it to the stranger.

"Here."

The stranger took the paper. He looked at it. It was a standard violation form. **K-7: Structural Entry Point Failure.**

"What is this?"

"It's your proof," Dale said. "If they come for you, you show them this.

It proves you were part of a documented incident.

It shifts the blame to the facility. It buys you time.

"

The stranger looked at the paper. Then he looked at Dale.

"What happens to me now?"

Dale looked at the rain, and he looked at the purple tear in the sky. He looked at the loading dock, where trucks were parked.

Trucks that belonged to the Authority.

Trucks that carried the tools of containment.

"Now?" Dale said. "Now we go find a place to wash your hands. Because you're still covered in my contamination."

The stranger looked down at his gloves. They were pristine.

"I didn't touch anything," he said.

Dale smiled. It was a small, tight smile.

"You touched the wire. You touched the console. You touched the concept of rebellion. That's a deeper contamination than grease."

He turned toward the parking lot.

"Walk," Dale said. "And don't talk. If you talk, you might invite something else in. And I'm out of duct tape."

The stranger followed him. His steps were unsteady. His eyes were fixed on the back of Dale's head.

Behind them, the door to the facility remained locked. But the camera above it zoomed in. The red light turned off.

A new message appeared on the monitor inside the server room.

**SUBJECT: DALE KOWALSKI.** **STATUS: ESCALATED.** **CLASSIFICATION: CLASS III CONTAMINANT.** **ACTION: DEPLOY PURGE TEAM.**

The message blinked once. Then it vanished.

The server room hummed. The console sparked again.

Somewhere in the facility, a siren wailed.
