Chapter 7

The creature's mandibles clicked twice. Dale wrote: "Mandibular nesting pattern: two sets, inner set retracted at rest, deploys in 0.4-second window." He counted the seconds in his head. One. Two. The inner set slid forward again. He wrote the timing.

Rick was backing toward the crawlspace entrance. "Dale."

"Keep your voice down."

"Dale, it's looking at you."

"It doesn't have eyes."

"It's looking at you."

Dale didn't look up from the Field Guide. The creature's body was segmented, but not cleanly. The segments were asymmetric, swollen on the left side, and the carapace had a wet sheen that suggested soft tissue underneath. Not a hard shell. A membrane.

He wrote: "Exoskeleton: incomplete. Suggests molting stage or transitional morphology. Carapace thickness: approximately 2mm on dorsal surface. Lateral surfaces: soft."

The creature took a step forward. Its legs were wrong. Too many joints. It moved like something that had been assembled from the wrong parts and then told to walk anyway.

Dale wrote: "Locomotion: irregular gait. Likely compensatory. Possible injury or incomplete development."

The Authority notification pulsed.

FIELD GUIDE ENTRY IN PROGRESS. COMBAT PROTOCOLS LOCKED UNTIL IDENTIFICATION COMPLETE.

"Almost got it," Dale muttered.

"Dale, I'm leaving."

"You can't leave. You're inside the containment perimeter."

Rick stopped. "What containment perimeter?"

Dale gestured at the crawlspace entrance with his pen. The trash bag and duct tape from the previous job were still there, stretched across the opening like a bad curtain. "That. You're on my side of it. The Authority flagged this space as a potential infestation zone. If you cross that line, you're a civilian in an active treatment area and I have to file a liability form."

Rick stared at him. Then he looked at the creature. Then back at Dale.

"You're filing a liability form."

"I'm filing it in my head right now. Don't make me do it for real."

The creature lunged.

Not at Dale. At the crawlspace wall. It hit the plywood with its shoulder and the wood cracked. Dale stepped back. His boot caught on a pipe and he nearly went down, but he kept the Field Guide up. Kept writing.

"Impact behavior: targets structural boundaries. Not prey. Suggests burrowing instinct."

The creature pulled its shoulder free. A chunk of plywood came with it. Behind the plywood, the insulation was exposed. And in the insulation, Dale saw something.

Frass.

Dark pellets, scattered in a line. Not fresh. Maybe three days old. But the pattern was unmistakable. He'd seen it a thousand times. German cockroach frass. Small, dark, cylindrical, deposited in a straight line along a surface edge.

He wrote: "Frass present in treatment area. Species: unknown. Size consistent with Blattodea order. Deposit pattern: linear, along structural boundary."

Then he stopped. Looked at the creature again. Looked at the frass.

The creature wasn't making the frass. It was walking toward it.

The Authority notification changed.

CROSS-CONTAMINATION RISK UPDATED. MULTIPLE SPECIES DETECTED IN TREATMENT AREA. FIELD GUIDE ENTRIES MAY INTERACT. REVIEW COMPLETE BEFORE ENGAGEMENT.

Dale looked at his Field Guide. Three entries now. The rat. The beetle from three chapters ago. And the new entry, still provisional, still incomplete.

The creature had stopped moving. It was at the wall. Its vertical mouth was open, and the nested mandibles were spread wide, and from inside the mouth came a sound like wet paper tearing.

Then it vomited.

Not onto the wall. Onto the frass. A viscous, amber fluid coated the pellets and the insulation and the wood behind them. The fluid steamed. The insulation began to dissolve.

Dale wrote: "Behavior: regurgitation onto frass deposits. Possible fertilization behavior. Possible vector mechanism."

The Authority notification flashed red.

RETROACTIVE CONTAMINATION DETECTED. FIELD GUIDE ENTRY: RATTUS PROFUNDUS (MUTAGENIC VARIANT). STATUS: INVALID. BASE ORGANISM HAS UNDERGONE MUTAGENIC ALTERATION VIA CURRENT SPECIES. PREVIOUS TREATMENT RECORDS UNDER REVIEW.

Dale read it twice.

His previous records. The rats. The broodmother. The beetle. All of it. Under review.

"Dale." Rick's voice was small. "What does that mean?"

"It means the Authority is checking whether my previous exterminations are still valid."

"Are they?"

"If this thing changed the rats after I killed them, then the nests I treated might not have been fully eliminated. The mutations could have spread to other colonies I didn't see."

Rick's face went white. "You're saying the rats might still be alive."

"I'm saying the nests might have reconstituted under a different genetic profile. Which means my Treatment Points could be revoked."

The Authority notification updated again.

THOROUGHNESS RATING RECALCULATION PENDING. CURRENT SCORE: 9.2. ESTIMATED REVISED SCORE: 6.4. CERTIFICATION LEVEL I AT RISK.

Dale felt something cold move through his chest. Not fear. Recognition. He'd seen this before. A customer who'd hired him to treat a kitchen, then gone and sprayed bleach under the sink, destroying the evidence of where the nest actually was. He'd have to re-inspect. Re-treat. Write a new report. Bill them again.

The Authority was doing the same thing to him. Except the Authority didn't bill. It revoked.

He needed to fix this. Now. Before the recalculation finalized.

He looked at the creature. Provisional entry. Incomplete identification. He couldn't use any protocol. He couldn't kill it. But he could contain it.

The Authority had a protocol for this. Dale had read it in the certification manual on page 47, under a section called Temporary Containment Hold. It was meant for situations where a technician needed to isolate an unidentified organism while awaiting a higher-level specialist. The hold froze all systemic effects for six hours. Including retroactive contamination.

The catch: you had to physically seal the organism in a space that met the Authority's definition of containment. Which meant no gaps larger than 2mm. Which meant he needed to seal a hole that was currently being widened by a creature the size of a large dog.

He looked at the crawlspace entrance. The trash bag. The duct tape. Not enough.

He looked at the plywood the creature had cracked. He looked at the pipe his boot had caught on.

"Rick."

"Yeah."

"Grab that pipe. The copper one. Hold it against the wall where the plywood is broken."

"What?"

"Against the wall. Now."

Rick grabbed the pipe. He held it against the broken section. Dale could see the creature's head pressing against the gap. The mandibles were working at the wood, pulling fibers free.

Dale grabbed the duct tape. He wrapped the pipe to the wall frame. One loop. Two. Three. The pipe held the plywood in place. Not sealed. Not even close. But it reduced the gap.

He grabbed the trash bag. He pulled it over the entire opening. He taped the edges to the floor joists. He taped the pipe to the bag. He taped the bag to the wall.

The creature pushed against the bag. The bag stretched. The tape held.

He pulled out his Field Guide. He added a line to the provisional entry: "Containment attempt: physical barrier, improvised. Gap reduction to approximately 4mm on primary face."

He wrote it as a containment log. Not a combat log. A containment log.

The Authority notification changed.

TEMPORARY CONTAINMENT HOLD: INITIATED. DURATION: 6 HOURS. RETROACTIVE CONTAMINATION: FROZEN. THOROUGHNESS RATING RECALCULATION: SUSPENDED.

Dale exhaled.

Rick was breathing hard. "Did you just trap it?"

"I contained it. There's a difference. Containment means I acknowledge it's still there and I'm responsible for it. Trapping implies I've solved the problem. I haven't solved anything."

"Then what did you do?"

"Bought six hours."

The Authority notification pulsed one more time.

TEMPORARY CONTAINMENT HOLD CONFIRMED. NOTE: HOLD IS INDIVIDUAL. APPLIES TO THIS TREATMENT AREA ONLY. ADDITIONAL MUTAGENIC VECTORS DETECTED IN SECTOR 7. COUNT: 14. RECOMMEND IMMEDIATE ESCALATION TO CLASS 3 LICENSING.

Dale read the notification. Fourteen more. In the same sector. The same neighborhood.

Then a second notification.

CLASS 3 LICENSING PREREQUISITE: CERTIFICATION LEVEL II. CURRENT LEVEL: I. EXAMINATION REQUIRED. WRITTEN COMPONENT: SCHEDULED FOR 72 HOURS FROM THIS NOTIFICATION. PRACTICAL COMPONENT: SCHEDULED FOR 72 HOURS FROM THIS NOTIFICATION. FAILURE TO PASS WILL RESULT IN PERMANENT INELIGIBILITY FOR CLASS 3 TREATMENT AUTHORIZATION.

Dale looked at Rick. Rick looked at Dale.

"Fourteen more of these things," Dale said. "And I have seventy-two hours to pass an exam that will let me kill them."

"Can you pass it?"

Dale looked at the Field Guide. Three entries. One provisional. One under review. One that might be revoked entirely.

"I need to study," he said.

"Study for what? A test?"

"For a test that the Authority is going to give me in three days, in which I have to identify, document, isolate, eliminate, and verify fourteen mutagenic vectors across a residential sector, while my previous work is under review and my certification level is one step below what the job requires."

Rick sat down on the crawlspace floor. "That's insane."

"It's a code violation."

Dale pulled out his clipboard. He'd been carrying it since the first job. The clipboard had a clip at the top and a metal ring at the bottom. He used it for reports. He used it for diagrams. He used it for the Field Guide entries when the system projection was inconvenient.

He started writing a study plan.

The Authority notification pulsed one final time.

NOTICE: UNLICENSED INDIVIDUAL DETECTED IN SECTOR 7. ACTIVE ENGAGEMENT WITH MUTAGENIC VECTORS. NO FIELD GUIDE ENTRY. NO CONTAINMENT PROTOCOL. NO DOCUMENTATION. CROSS-CONTAMINATION SPREAD RATE: ACCELERATING.

Dale looked up.

Someone else was out there. Someone who was fighting the creatures without documenting them. Without containing them. Without understanding what they were.

And every creature that person killed without a proper protocol was spreading the mutation faster.

He stood up. His knees popped. He was thirty-eight years old and he had a clipboard and a duct tape problem and seventy-two hours to become a Class 2 technician.

"Rick."

"Yeah."

"Who else is out there? Who's been fighting these things?"

Rick thought. "There's a guy. Name's something like... Vance. Big guy. Works security at the mall. He's been out there with a baseball bat since day two."

"Has he been killing them?"

"Killing them? He's been smashing them. Everywhere. In the streets, in the yards. He posted videos."

Dale closed his eyes.

A baseball bat. No documentation. No identification. No containment. Just impact.

The Authority's system didn't register impact as extermination. It registered impact as an unlicensed individual creating an uncontrolled dispersal event. Every creature Vance smashed was a creature whose contents were now spread across a surface area, and whose genetic material was now in the environment, and whose mutation vector was now uncontained.

Dale opened his Field Guide to a fresh page. He wrote: "Unlicensed intervention event. Sector 7. Subject: Vance (surname unknown). Method: blunt force. Estimated vector dispersal: HIGH. Estimated contamination radius: UNKNOWN."

He underlined UNKNOWN twice.

The Authority notification appeared.

UNLICENSED INTERVENTION NOTED. CROSS-CONTAMINATION SPREAD RATE: 400% ABOVE BASELINE. RECOMMEND IMMEDIATE TREATMENT AREA EXPANSION.

Dale looked at the crawlspace. The bag was still holding. The creature was still pushing. The pipe was still in place.

Six hours.

Fourteen vectors.

A man with a baseball bat who was making everything worse.

And a test in three days that would determine whether he was allowed to fix it.

He looked at Rick.

"Vance's videos. Where did he post them?"

"His phone. He's been live-streaming the whole thing."

Dale pulled out his phone. He found the account. He watched for ninety seconds.

Vance was in a backyard. He'd found a nest. A real nest, with eggs. And he was smashing it. Not the adults. The eggs. He was smashing the eggs with the bat and the fluid from the broken eggs was spraying across the grass, the fence, the siding of the house.

Dale watched the fluid hit the siding. He watched it steam. He watched the siding soften.

He looked at the timestamp. The video was from four hours ago.

He looked at the Authority notification. Fourteen vectors. Detected now.

The vectors weren't coming from the Rift.

They were coming from Vance.
