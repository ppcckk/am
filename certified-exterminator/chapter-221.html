Chapter 221: One Year Breach

# Chapter 221

Dale sprinted across the wet grass, the clipboard clutched to his chest like a shield.

The Auditor did not run. He walked with that same terrifying, fluid grace, his suit perfectly pressed despite the mud. Behind him, the crack in the patio widened. Something wet and chittering pushed through the earth, blind and hungry.

"Stop," the Auditor said. His voice wasn't loud, but it echoed inside Dale's skull, vibrating against his teeth. "You are in violation of Order 44-B. Destruction of Authority property carries a mandatory license suspension."

Dale didn't stop. He kept running toward the edge of the yard, where the tree line offered cover and, more importantly, a signal.

"Vane!" Dale shouted over his shoulder. "Where's the camera?"

"It's on the fence! I dropped it!"

Dale skidded to a halt, his boots sliding in the slurry of the lawn. He looked back.

The Auditor had stopped ten feet away.

The dark thing from the patio had emerged fully now. It was a segmented horror, six legs thick as tree trunks, clicking mandibles that dripped acidic slime onto the grass.

The Auditor raised a hand. The creature froze.

"Your documentation is incomplete," the Auditor said. "You failed to isolate the breach point before engaging the primary entity. That is a Class-3 infestation error. Punitive action is warranted."

Dale backed away, his eyes scanning the perimeter. He needed a weapon. He needed a protocol. He needed time.

"I didn't engage," Dale said, his breath coming in short, sharp bursts. "I identified. I documented. I just haven't filed the report yet."

"Ignorance of the filing deadline is no excuse." The Auditor's eyes were voids, black pits where human pupils should have been. "Surrender the clipboard. Submit to re-certification."

"No."

Dale reached into his pocket and pulled out a small, battery-powered spray bottle labeled *Insecticide*. He shook it. It was empty.

**SYSTEM: ITEM DEPLETED.**

He tossed it aside. It clattered against the concrete walkway.

"Vane," Dale muttered, keeping his eyes on the Auditor. "Talk to me. What's the status?"

Vane was crouched by the wooden fence, fumbling with a smartphone that had a cracked screen. "I'm trying to upload the last entry. But the signal is dead. The Auditor's presence is jamming the local network."

"Can you record offline?"

"Yes, but it won't sync until we're out of range. Dale, he's just standing there. He's not attacking. Why isn't he attacking?"

Dale watched the Auditor's hand twitch. The creature behind him took a step forward, then stopped.

"He can't," Dale said. "Not without a written order. That's the loophole."

Vane looked up, confused. "What loophole?"

Dale stepped closer to the creature, keeping a safe distance.

The stench of ammonia and rot washed over him. He recognized the scent profile.

High nitrogen.

Rapid decomposition.

"Look at his suit," Dale said, pointing at the Auditor's lapel. "No pin. No badge. No official Authority seal."

Vane squinted. "So?"

"If he's not wearing a badge, he's not acting under immediate administrative authority. He's acting as a rogue agent." Dale turned to the Auditor. "Prove you have jurisdiction."

The Auditor tilted his head. A slow, mocking smile returned. "My word is law within this grid."

"Prove it," Dale said. He tapped the clipboard.

"Section 12, Subsection D.

An auditor must present a valid Digital Credential to initiate a Revocation Protocol.

Until you do, you're just a guy in a suit threatening a licensed technician."

The air grew heavy. The silence stretched, thin and brittle.

The Auditor's black eyes narrowed. He reached into his jacket.

Dale tensed.

But the Auditor didn't pull out a weapon. He pulled out a tablet.

"Fine," the Auditor said. He tapped the screen. A holographic projection appeared in the air between them. It displayed Dale's name, his Certification Level, and a red stamp reading **PROVISIONAL SUSPENSION PENDING REVIEW**.

"See?" the Auditor said. "I have authority."

Dale felt his heart sink. The system recognized the command.

**SYSTEM: LICENSE SUSPENDED. PROTOCOLS DISABLED.**

Dale tried to call up his Field Guide. Nothing happened. The menu didn't appear.

His hands were just hands. No interface.

No bonuses.

No targeted damage.

Vane let out a whimper. "Dale."

The Auditor stepped forward.

"You attempted to destroy evidence. That is a felony-level infestation offense.

The creature will now be released to consume you.

It is a natural consequence of your negligence."

The dark thing chittered. It lowered its head, mandibles clicking.

Dale backed up until his heels hit the tree line. He had no spray. No traps. No interface.

He had only his knowledge.

He looked at the creature. looked at it. He ignored the size.

He ignored the horror.

He looked at the legs.

The segmentation. The way it moved.

"Vane," Dale said, his voice flat. "Don't look at it. Look at the ground."

"What?"

"Look at the ground where it walks."

Vane glanced down. The creature's legs were leaving deep, muddy prints. But there was something else.

A trail. A fine, white powder.

"Calcium carbonate," Dale said. "It's shedding. It's in a molting cycle."

"So?"

"It's vulnerable. It's soft."

The Auditor laughed.

A dry, rasping sound. "Even if it were, you have no tools. You have no license.

You are a civilian, Mr.

Kowalski. You have no right to act."

Dale looked at the Auditor. "I don't need a license to observe. And I don't need a license to speak."

Dale turned to the creature. He took a deep breath. He spoke, projecting his voice like he was addressing a tenant who had violated lease terms.

"Identification: *Arachnis molitor*. Common name: Molting Void Spider. Weakness: Unhardened chitin. Habitat: High humidity, calcium-poor soil."

The Auditor frowned. "Stop that."

Dale kept talking.

He pulled the pen from his pocket. He wrote on the clipboard. Not a report.

A note.

**SYSTEM NOTICE: FIELD GUIDE ENTRY UPDATED. WEAKNESS IDENTIFIED: SOFT CHITIN (PHASE 2).**

Dale's hands shook. The interface was gone, but the system was still watching. It was still logging.

"Vane," Dale said. "Did you hear that?"

Vane stared at Dale. "Hear what? The system isn't talking."

"It's not talking to you. It's talking to the audit log."

The Auditor's face twisted. "You are manipulating the record."

"I am documenting reality," Dale said. "And reality is that you are standing on a nest."

The Auditor looked down.

The ground beneath his expensive shoes had begun to bubble.

The white powder from the spider's legs mixed with the mud.

It wasn't just calcium. It was a pheromone trail.

A beacon.

"You triggered it," Dale said. "When you walked out there. You stepped in the frass trail. You're covered in it."

The Auditor's eyes widened. He looked at his own shoes. A fine, grey dust coated the leather.

"Get back!" the Auditor shouted to the spider.

The spider did not get back. It screamed. A high-pitched, psychic shriek that drove Dale to his knees.

The creature lunged. Not at Dale. At the Auditor.

The Auditor raised his tablet, trying to issue a stop command, but the creature was in a frenzy. It sensed the pheromones on the Auditor's suit. It sensed the intrusion.

Mandibles snapped shut around the Auditor's arm.

The sound was wet. Crunching. Bone giving way to cartilage.

The Auditor screamed. A human, unprofessional scream.

Dale didn't move. He watched.

The system flashed in the corner of his vision, faint and ghostly, because his license was suspended, but the core kernel still processed events.

**EVENT: THREAT NEUTRALIZED BY HOSTILE FORCE.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: N/A (UNLICENSED ACTION).** **POINTS AWARDED: 0.**

"Vane," Dale said. "Run."

Vane scrambled to his feet. "What about him?"

"He's the problem now. Let the infestation handle him."

They ran. They didn't look back. Behind them, the sound of tearing fabric and wet snapping continued for a few seconds, then was replaced by a low, satisfied chittering.

Dale and Vane burst through the tree line and onto the main road. The street was empty. The air was thick with the smell of ozone and rot.

Dale stopped, leaning against a mailbox. He checked his clipboard. The page he had ripped off was gone. But the rest of the entries remained.

"We need to report this," Vane said, panting. "We need to tell the Authority that the Auditor was rogue."

"No," Dale said. He wiped a smudge of mud from his cheek. "We tell them we were audited."

"They'll kill us."

"They'll fine us." Dale straightened his tie. "And they'll send someone else."

Vane stared at him. "Someone else?"

"An Auditor is expensive. They don't send them for Class-1 breaches.

They send them for systemic failures." Dale looked at the distant house, where the dark shape was still visible through the trees.

"That wasn't a breach, Vane. That was a test."

Vane paled. "A test?"

"The Authority doesn't just punish sloppiness. It punishes competence." Dale opened his Field Guide. The entry for the spider was there, glowing faintly. **WEAKNESS: SOFT CHITIN.**

But next to it, a new line appeared.

**NOTE: HOSTILE ENTITY (AUDITOR) EXHIBITED UNLICENSED AGGRESSION. CROSS-CONTAMINATION RISK: HIGH.**

Dale stared at the note.

"Vane," he said. "We didn't just fail the audit."

"What did we do?"

"We contaminated the field."

Before Vane could ask what that meant, a notification pinged. Not in the peripheral vision. Directly in Dale's mind.

**SYSTEM ALERT: LOCAL NODE COMPROMISED.** **INFESTATION SOURCE SHIFTED TO USER.**

Dale looked at his hands. His fingertips were turning grey. The white powder from the spider's legs was on his clipboard. It was on his tie.

It was in his lungs.

"Vane," Dale said, his voice calm, procedural. "Don't breathe."

Vane froze.

"It's not just in the air," Dale said.

He looked at the road ahead. The asphalt was bubbling.

The streetlights were flickering.

"It's in us."

A figure stepped out from the shadows of the next house. It wore a suit. A different suit. A blue one.

"Mr. Kowalski," the new figure said. "You have been flagged for Biological Contamination. Please remain stationary."

Dale didn't run. He couldn't. His legs were numb.

He looked at Vane. Vane was holding his breath, his face turning red.

Dale looked at the clipboard. He picked up his pen.

"Vane," Dale said. "Take the camera."

"Dale, my feet are stuck."

"I know."

Dale turned to the new Auditor. He raised the clipboard.

"Before you take me in," Dale said, "I need to file a hazard report. There's a breach in the containment zone. And I'm the only one who knows how to seal it."

The blue-suited Auditor paused. "You are contaminated. Your certification is revoked."

"Revoked isn't dead," Dale said. "And I'm not fighting. I'm working."

He tapped the pen against the clipboard.

**SYSTEM: EMERGENCY PROTOCOL UNLOCKED: QUARANTINE BREACH.** **COST: ONE YEAR OF CERTIFICATION PROGRESS.** **ACCEPT?**

Dale looked at the grey spreading up his arms. He looked at Vane, who was struggling to stand.

"Accept," Dale said.

The world went black.
