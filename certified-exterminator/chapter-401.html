Chapter 401: Licensing Code Clause

# Chapter 401

The pen scratched against the clipboard, loud in the silence.

Dale didn't look at the faceless thing. He looked at the ants.

Hundreds of them. Mandibles clicking in unison. A sound like rain on a tin roof, but heavier. Wet.

"Recruit," the faceless figure said. The voice wasn't a voice. It was a vibration in Dale's teeth.

Dale shifted his weight. His left arm hung dead at his side, frostbite cracking the skin of his forearm. He raised his right hand. The glass shard was still there, slick with ichor.

"I am a Level II technician," Dale said. "I do not recruit colonies. I exterminate them."

The Inspector stepped forward. Its suit was immaculate. No dust. No blood.

"You failed to eliminate," the Inspector said. "You disrupted. The Authority does not pay for disruption. It pays for closure."

"Clause 4, Section 12 of the Licensing Code," Dale said. "Inter-species conflict induced by environmental manipulation counts as passive elimination if the target population declines by forty percent within ten minutes."

The ants did not decline. They advanced.

A worker ant lunged. Dale sidestepped. He didn't swing the glass. He stepped over it.

The ant crashed into the wall where the frass trail was. The powder coated its legs. It stopped. It groomed its antennae.

The other ants followed the scent. They clustered around the first one. They eated it.

Dale watched the pile grow. He checked his watch. Seven minutes left.

"Look at the data," Dale said. "It's working."

The Inspector tilted its head. The smooth white oval had no eyes, but Dale felt the judgment.

"Is it?" the Inspector asked.

"The biomass is decreasing," Dale said. "They are consuming their own.

That is a valid control strategy.

Integrated Pest Management. I used the environment. I didn't use a protocol.

So no cross-contamination penalty.

"

The faceless figure lowered the clipboard.

"You are citing code to survive a threat assessment," the Inspector said. "This is not bravery. This is paperwork."

"Bravery is for people who don't have a Thoroughness Rating," Dale said. "I have a nine-point-two. I keep it."

From the shadows, Margaret emerged. She was bleeding from a cut above her eye. Her uniform was torn. She held a rusted pipe like a club.

"Dale," she said. Her voice was tight. "Stop talking. They're circling."

"They are not circling," Dale said. "They are regrouping based on new chemical signals. The frass is a marker. They are marking the corpse."

Margaret looked at the ants. Then at Dale.

"You're insane," she said. "You're using their own pheromones to turn them into a cannibalistic frenzy."

"It's efficient," Dale said.

"It's horrifying," Margaret said.

"The Authority doesn't care about horror. It cares about results."

The faceless figure raised the pen again.

"Time is expiring," the Inspector said.

Dale looked at the pile of ants. It was shrinking.

The workers were eating the soldiers, and the soldiers were eating the drones. The hierarchy was collapsing under the weight of confused chemical signals.

He had thirty seconds left.

"Accept the passive elimination," Dale said. "Award the points.

I need the certification upgrade for the Rift breach in Sector Four, and the broodmother there is a Class IV.

I can't handle it with a Class II toolkit.

"

The Inspector paused.

"You are attempting to leverage a combat anomaly to bypass the practical exam requirements," the Inspector said.

"I am attempting to demonstrate that my methods are valid," Dale said. "My Field Guide is accurate. My protocols are compliant.

I am not a hero. I am a technician.

Hire me, or watch me get eaten by a colony I just destabilized.

"

Margaret stepped closer. She put her hand on Dale's shoulder. Her grip was firm.

"He's right," Margaret said. "The ants are dying. Look at the ground.

The biomass is dropping.

If you kill him now, you lose a qualified technician. If you let him live, he fixes the Rift.

"

The Inspector looked at Margaret.

"You are also unlicensed," the Inspector said. "You are interfering with the assessment."

"I am a witness," Margaret said. "I saw the frass. I saw the confusion. I saw the numbers drop."

The Inspector turned back to Dale.

"The Authority recognizes the efficacy of your method. However, the method is irregular. It lacks documentation prior to engagement."

"I documented it now," Dale said. "I am writing it in my head. I will transcribe it in the report."

"We do not accept mental transcriptions," the Inspector said.

Dale reached into his vest. He pulled out a small, battered notebook. It was stained with coffee and old bug splatter.

He tore out a page, and he held it up.

"Here is the observation log," Dale said.

"Timestamps. Species behavior. Environmental triggers.

I am submitting it for retroactive validation.

"

The Inspector looked at the paper.

"Retroactive validation is denied," the Inspector said. "Protocol violation."

"Then what?" Dale asked. "I don't have a weapon. I don't have a spell. I have a piece of paper and a dying colony."

The faceless figure stepped forward. It placed the clipboard on the floor.

"Sign it," the figure said.

Dale looked down. The paper was blank.

"Sign what?" Dale asked.

"The Recruit Contract," the Inspector said.

Dale's breath hitched. Just once.

"If I sign this," Dale said, "I am no longer a technician."

"You are an agent," the Inspector said. "The Authority requires intermediaries. Your methods are... unconventional. They require a license that exists outside the municipal framework."

Margaret grabbed Dale's arm.

"Don't," she whispered.

"This is how they get you.

They don't hire exterminators. They make them part of the system.

And the system eats people.

"

Dale looked at the ants. The pile was gone. Only husks remained.

He looked at the Rift. The air was shimmering.

More things were coming.

Not ants this time. Something larger. Something with teeth.

"I need to treat the breach," Dale said.

"Sign," the Inspector said. "Or we classify you as a contaminant. And we remove you."

Dale picked up the pen. The pen was cold. It hummed.

He looked at Margaret, and she shook her head. Slowly.

Dale signed.

The ink was black. It burned into the paper.

The System notification flashed in Dale's peripheral vision.

**STATUS CHANGE: UNLICENSED -> PROVISIONAL AGENT.** **NEW PROTOCOL UNLOCKED: CHAIN OF COMMAND.** **PENALTY APPLIED: SOCIAL ISOLATION DEBUFF (-50% cooperation from non-Authority entities).**

Margaret stepped back.

Her eyes widened. She looked at Dale, but she didn't see him. She saw something else.

"Dale?" she said. Her voice was distant.

"I'm here," Dale said.

She shook her head. She raised her pipe.

"Get away from me," she said.

Dale looked at the Inspector.

"What did you do?"

"Chain of Command," the Inspector said. "As a Provisional Agent, you are no longer one of them. You are one of us. The social contract is severed."

Margaret swung the pipe.

Dale ducked. The pipe hit the wall. Plaster dust rained down.

"Margaret, it's me," Dale said. "Dale Kowalski. Class II. Fourteen years in pest control."

Margaret didn't listen. She swung again. Her eyes were blank.

The faceless figure pointed at Margaret.

"Non-compliant civilian," the Inspector said. "Remove."

Two ants crawled out of the dust. They were different. Larger. They had red markings on their backs.

They moved toward Margaret.

"Stop them," Dale said. "They're attacking her."

"They are removing a threat," the Inspector said.

"They are civilians!" Dale shouted.

"They are contaminants," the Inspector said. "Provisional Agents do not protect contaminants. They secure the perimeter."

Dale ran toward Margaret. He threw himself between her and the ants.

The ants stopped. They looked at him.

He was an Agent. They recognized him.

Margaret stumbled back. She looked at the ants. She looked at Dale.

"You're one of them," she said. "You're not Dale anymore."

"I am Dale," Dale said. "I'm just licensed."

The ants clicked. They turned. They went back to the shadows.

The Inspector picked up the clipboard.

"Job complete," the Inspector said. "Sector Four is next. The broodmother is a Class IV. You have ten minutes to travel."

"I need gear," Dale said. "I need a spray tank. I need a mask."

"You have the Chain of Command," the Inspector said. "The Authority provides."

A crate dropped from the ceiling. It landed with a thud.

Dale opened it.

Inside was not a spray tank. It was a jar.

A jar containing a single, pulsating eye.

"Use this," the Inspector said. "It is a Lure. It mimics the queen's pheromones. It will attract the broodmother."

Dale looked at the eye. It blinked.

"It's alive," Dale said.

"It is a tool," the Inspector said. "Do not anthropomorphize the equipment. That is a code violation."

Dale closed the jar. He put it in his pocket.

He looked at Margaret. She was backing away. She was terrified of him.

"I can fix this," Dale said. "I can get my license back. I can get rid of this debuff."

"You can't," Margaret said. "You signed it. You chose the Authority."

"I chose survival," Dale said.

"You chose power," Margaret said.

She turned and ran. She ran into the dark.

Dale watched her go.

The Inspector watched him.

"Feel bad?" the Inspector asked.

"I feel like I have a job to do," Dale said.

"Good," the Inspector said. "The broodmother is in the subway tunnels. It has eaten three trains. It is breeding."

Dale started walking. His steps were heavy. His left arm throbbed.

"Wait," Dale said.

The Inspector turned.

"Who is Margaret?" Dale asked. "Why did she know the code?"

The Inspector paused.

"She was a Provisional Agent," the Inspector said. "Before you."

Dale stopped.

"She failed?" Dale asked.

"She retired," the Inspector said. "Or so she thought."

Dale looked at the jar in his pocket. The eye was pressing against the glass.

"She didn't retire," Dale said. "She hid."

"Hiding is a form of failure," the Inspector said. "And failures are removed."

Dale walked faster, and the tunnel entrance was ahead. The air smelled of rust and rot.

He pulled out his clipboard. He wrote one word.

*RETRIEVE.*

He didn't know what he was retrieving. He didn't know if he could save Margaret.

But he knew that if he didn't treat the broodmother, the infestation would spread. And if the infestation spread, he would lose his Thoroughness Rating.

And if he lost his rating, he would be removed, and he walked into the dark.

The jar in his pocket pulsed. It got warmer.

Something in the tunnel clicked.

Dale adjusted his grip on the glass shard, and he didn't have a spray tank. He didn't have a mask.

He had a eye. And a debt.

And eight chapters to pay it off.
