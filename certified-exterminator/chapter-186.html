Chapter 186: Licensed to Descend

# Chapter 186

The chittering stopped.

It didn't fade. It cut off.

A hard, digital silence that felt heavier than the humidity pressing down on the mill district.

Dale Kowalski kept walking, but his pace slowed. He didn't look up. He looked down at the pavement.

Cracks. New ones. Jagged fissures running parallel to the foundation of the nearest row house.

He stepped over a puddle of oily sludge.

The smell hit him first. Copper and rot.

Not the usual sewer gas.

This was biological waste from something that didn't digest like a mammal.

A woman stood on the porch of number 42. She was holding a broom.

Not a weapon.

A broom.

Her knuckles were white.

"Did you hear that?" she asked. Her voice was high, thin.

Dale stopped. He adjusted the strap of his tank. He didn't look at her face. He looked at the siding.

"Ma'am," Dale said. His voice was flat. "Are you reporting a noise complaint or an infestation?"

"I said, did you hear that?"

"I heard it. It's gone now."

"It's not gone. It's inside."

She pointed the broom at her front door. The wood was warped. The paint was bubbling.

"I tried to call the city," she said. "They said there's no ordinance for 'ground noise'."

"There is," Dale said. He pulled a clipboard from his vest. "Ordinance 14-B.

Subsurface Acoustic Disturbance. Class 1 nuisance if intermittent.

Class 2 if continuous.

This looks like a Class 3 precursor."

He stepped onto the porch. He didn't wait for an invitation. He tapped the doorframe with his pen.

"Is this your primary residence?"

"Yes. I live here. With my kids."

"Good. I need to inspect the perimeter before I can issue a citation. Or a treatment order. Whichever applies."

"You can't just come in here."

Dale held up his badge. It was laminated. Standard issue.

"I have a permit. Signed by the Authority. You're welcome to call the dispatch line to verify, but I'd prefer you don't. I'm on a clock."

The woman hesitated. Then she stepped back.

Dale entered. The air inside was stale. Thick. He checked his Field Guide on his wrist display.

*Corporate Parasite: Contaminated.*

The warning flashed red. A 40% debuff to identification.

He ignored it. He relied on his eyes.

The hallway was narrow. Carpet stained with dark patches. Not blood. Slime.

At the end of the hall, the floorboards were buckling. A mound of dirt rose from beneath the rug.

Dale knelt. He didn't touch it. He used a probe from his belt.

A click.

The mound twitched.

"Hey!"

The voice came from the street. A man in a high-vis vest. A city surveyor. He was holding a tablet.

"Kowalski!"

Dale didn't stand up. He kept his probe steady.

"Surveyor," Dale said. "You're trespassing on a active treatment zone."

"That's my zone too," the man shouted.

He walked up the steps. He was sweating.

"I got a report of structural compromise.

Foundation shift."

"Dale," the woman said. "Is that safe?"

"It's not safe," Dale said. He didn't look at her. "But it's contained."

The surveyor reached the porch. He looked at the mound. He looked at Dale.

"What is that?"

"Excretion," Dale said.

"Excretion? From what?"

Dale stood up. He wiped the probe on a rag.

"From a burrowing organism. Likely *Rattus profundus* variant. Or something worse. My guide is glitched, so I can't be sure."

The surveyor frowned. "Glitched? What does that mean?"

"Cross-contamination. I treated a corporate entity last week. Used the wrong protocol. Now my data on similar bio-structures is corrupted."

The surveyor laughed. A nervous, sharp sound.

"Corrupted data. Right.

Look, Dale.

I'm not here to argue philosophy.

I'm here because the city wants to condemn this block. They're talking about tearing it down.

Bulldozing everything."

Dale froze.

"Condemn?"

"Yeah. The Rift activity is spiking.

The Authority is overwhelmed.

The city council says if we can't identify the threat by noon, they pull the plug on the infrastructure. Gas, water, power. They'll seal the block."

"That's not extermination," Dale said. "That's abandonment."

"It's containment," the surveyor said. "By force."

Dale looked at the mound. The chittering started again. Low. Rhythmic.

"If they cut the power, the moisture control fails," Dale said.

"The soil dries out. The organisms go dormant.

They don't die.

They wait."

"And when the power comes back?"

"They wake up. Hungrier."

The surveyor checked his tablet. "Look. I can give you two hours. If you don't provide a verified identification and a containment plan, I file the condemnation order."

Dale looked at his watch.

"I have one hour and forty-five minutes of lunch break left," Dale said. "But I'm willing to waive the lunch."

The woman in the hallway stepped forward.

"You're not going to kill it?"

"I'm going to document it," Dale said. "Then I'm going to eliminate it. Then I'm going to verify it. Then I'm going to write the report."

"That's a lot of steps for a rat," the surveyor said.

"It's not a rat," Dale said.

He pulled out his spray tank. He checked the nozzle setting.

"Step back."

The surveyor took two steps. The woman stayed put.

Dale aimed at the mound.

He didn't spray. He waited.

The mound shifted. A pale, segmented limb pushed through the dirt. Then another.

Dale's Field Guide flashed.

*Warning: Identification Confidence: 15%. Target: Unknown.*

The debuff.

Dale closed his eyes for a second. He opened them.

"It's a Broodmother," Dale said. "Secondary caste. Not the primary. The primary is deeper."

"How do you know?" the surveyor asked.

"The chittering pattern. It's a distress call. It's calling for workers."

Dale sprayed a mist. Not the acid. The pheromone disruptor.

The limb retracted. The mound stopped moving.

Silence.

"Did that work?" the woman asked.

"It bought me ten seconds," Dale said. "I need to find the nest."

"You can't dig up the floor," the surveyor said. "Structural integrity."

"I don't need to dig. I need to bait it."

Dale pulled a small, sealed container from his belt. It contained a synthetic lure. High protein. High scent.

"Ma'am, do you have a vacuum?"

The woman blinked. "A vacuum?"

"Industrial. With a HEPA filter."

"I... I have a Dyson."

"Will do. Go get it."

She ran to the closet.

The surveyor watched Dale. "You're using a Dyson to fight a monster?"

"I'm using it to remove the evidence," Dale said. "I need a sample. Clean. Untainted."

"You're contaminating the crime scene."

"I'm preventing secondary infestation," Dale said. "If the spores spread, the whole block is unsalvageable. Condemnation is the only option then. I'm trying to save the buildings."

The woman returned. She held the vacuum.

"Plug it in," Dale said. "I'll direct."

He pointed to the mound.

"Suck the top layer. Slowly. Don't agitate the soil."

The vacuum roared. The woman guided it. Dale watched the suction nozzle.

Dirt flew up. Then, something else. A web of white filaments.

"Stop," Dale said.

The woman lifted the vacuum.

The filaments hung in the air, vibrating.

Dale pulled a glass slide from his kit. He touched it to the filaments. They snapped onto the glass.

He held the slide up to the light from the window.

The surveyor leaned in. "What is that?"

"Mycelium," Dale said. "Fungal network. Connected to the rodent system. Symbiotic."

The Field Guide updated.

*Target Identified: Fungal-Vermin Hybrid.* *Weakness: Spore Dispersal.*

The debuff lifted slightly. Confidence rose to 60%.

Dale didn't celebrate. He wrote it down.

"Fungal-Vermin Hybrid," he said aloud. "Class 4. Requires Class III treatment protocol. I have Class II."

The surveyor's face went pale.

"Class III? You can't do that. You're not certified."

"I can improvise," Dale said.

"Improvising Class III protocols is a violation. It voids your license."

"Not if I document the hazard first," Dale said. "I'm requesting an emergency variance. Authority, are you listening?"

Nothing happened.

The Authority didn't talk. It just graded.

Dale tapped the side of his head.

"Log entry. Requesting variance for immediate treatment of Class 4 Fungal-Vermin Hybrid in residential zone. Justification: Structural preservation. Risk of systemic spread."

A pause.

Then, text appeared in his vision.

*[Variance Denied. Protocol Mismatch. Penalty: -20% Treatment Points.]*

Dale stared at the text.

He looked at the surveyor.

"They're not helping me," Dale said.

"Who?"

"The Authority. They're letting you condemn the block."

The surveyor looked at the tablet. "The order is queued. It goes live in five minutes unless I cancel it."

Dale looked at the mound. The filaments were growing again. Faster now.

"If I don't kill the broodmother, the spores release," Dale said. "If I don't kill the broodmother, the city loses this block. If the city loses this block, the Rift widens."

"How do you know the Rift widens?" the surveyor asked.

"Because untreated infestations act as anchors," Dale said. "The Rift pulls things through. The more things come through, the wider the tear."

"That's theory."

"That's protocol."

Dale looked at the woman.

"Turn off the vacuum."

She did.

Dale walked to the center of the hallway.

"I need to go deeper," he said.

"Deeper?" the surveyor asked.

"Into the floor. The nest is below the subfloor. If I go down there, I can use the acid spray. But the acid will damage the joists."

"So you break the house to save it?"

"I break the nest to save the house."

The surveyor checked his watch. "Three minutes."

Dale looked at the Field Guide.

*Fungal-Vermin Hybrid.* *Weakness: Spore Dispersal.* *Vulnerability: Thermal Shock.*

Thermal shock.

Dale reached into his tank. He pulled out a canister of compressed CO2. Usually for freezing roaches.

Not enough for a broodmother.

He looked at the woman.

"Do you have a blowtorch?"

The woman shook her head.

"A hair dryer?"

The surveyor pointed to the bathroom. "Maybe. Old one."

Dale ran.

He came back ten seconds later. He held a battered hair dryer.

He plugged it in. He aimed it at the mound.

"Turn it to high," Dale said.

The woman cranked the dial.

The air grew hot. The filaments writhed.

Dale sprayed the CO2.

The cold hit the heat.

The filaments snapped. They shattered like glass.

A scream. Not human. High-pitched. Shattering.

The mound burst.

Black ichor sprayed the walls. The woman screamed. Dale didn't flinch. He stepped back.

The mound was gone.

In its place was a hole. Dark. Wet.

Something moved in the darkness.

Dale's Field Guide flashed.

*Target: Primary Broodmother.* *Level: 5.* *Status: Aggressive.*

The debuff was back.

*Identification Confidence: 10%.*

Dale looked at the surveyor.

"It's not dead," Dale said.

"I see that," the surveyor said. He was backing away. "Kowalski. The order is live."

"Give me one minute."

"I can't. The system is automated."

Dale looked at the hole.

He looked at his spray tank. Empty.

He looked at the CO2 canister. Empty.

He looked at the hair dryer. The motor was smoking.

He looked at the woman. She was crying.

Dale Kowalski pulled out his pen.

He opened his clipboard.

He wrote:

*Job Site: Residential Mill House.* *Hazard: Active Class 5 Infestation.* *Action: Unlicensed Improvisation.*

He looked at the surveyor.

"Cancel the condemnation order."

"I can't!"

"Then I'm going to do something that voids my license," Dale said.

The surveyor stared at him.

"What are you going to do?"

Dale pointed to the hole.

"I'm going to fill it."

"With what?"

"With concrete."

The surveyor blinked. "You don't have concrete."

"I have the foundation," Dale said. "And I have the Authority's rules."

He tapped his clipboard.

"Rule 4: Structural Integrity is paramount. If I seal the breach with a permanent barrier, the infestation is contained. Not eliminated. Contained."

"That's not extermination," the surveyor said.

"It's isolation," Dale said. "And isolation is a valid protocol for Class 5 threats when elimination is impossible."

The Authority text appeared.

*[Isolation Protocol Accepted. Verification Required. Time Limit: 10 Minutes.]*

Dale looked at the surveyor.

"Call the city. Get me a concrete truck. Fast."

The surveyor dialed. His hands were shaking.

"You're betting your license on a truck that might not make it," the surveyor said.

"I'm betting on the fact that they love paperwork," Dale said.

The phone rang.

The surveyor held it out.

"City Infrastructure. They're asking why you're requesting a pour for a residential home."

Dale took the phone.

"Because I'm sealing a dimensional breach," Dale said. "And if you don't send the truck, I'm writing you up for non-compliance with Ordinance 14-B, Section 3, Paragraph 2: Failure to Provide Immediate Material Support for Licensed Technicians."

He hung up.

The surveyor looked at the phone.

"They're coming," the surveyor said. "Fifteen minutes."

Dale looked at the hole.

Something was climbing out. A claw. Black and chitinous.

Dale stepped forward.

He didn't have a weapon. He didn't have a spell.

He had a clipboard.

He held it up.

"Identify yourself," Dale said.

The claw froze.

Dale wrote.

*Subject: Unidentified. Behavior: Confused.*

The claw hesitated.

Dale smiled. A dry, thin smile.

"Stay there," he said. "I'm documenting you."

The claw pulled back.

Dale didn't run. He sat on the porch steps. He opened his Field Guide.

He drawed.

The surveyor watched him.

"You're really doing that?"

"Identification is the first step," Dale said. "If I don't know what it is, I can't contain it properly."

The claw emerged again. It looked at the clipboard. It tilted its head.

It didn't attack.

It waited.

Dale wrote.

*Note: Subject responds to administrative presence.*

The surveyor's phone buzzed.

"Truck is here," the surveyor said. "But it's not concrete."

Dale looked up.

"What is it?"

"It's a tanker. Water main repair."

Dale stared at him.

"Water?"

"They couldn't find the concrete mixer. It's at the other site."

Dale looked at the hole.

If he poured water, he would drown the broodmother.

But he would also ruin the foundation.

And he would kill the woman's house.

The Authority text appeared.

*[Decision Point: Eliminate or Contain?]* *[Warning: Improper Containment Method Results in Total Job Failure.]*

Dale looked at the tanker. He looked at the claw.

He looked at the surveyor.

"Get the hose," Dale said.

"You're going to flood the house?"

"I'm going to induce a flood event," Dale said. "To trigger a defensive response."

"What response?"

Dale pointed to the claw.

"It's waiting for me to act. If I act, it reacts. If I flood it, it will try to escape."

"Where will it go?"

Dale looked at the sewer grate in the street.

"Down," Dale said.

The surveyor grabbed the hose.

"You're sending it into the city sewers."

"I'm moving the infestation to a larger, untreated zone," Dale said. "Where the Authority can deal with it."

"That's... that's not ethical."

"It's efficient," Dale said. "And I'm not charging you for it."

He stood up. He walked to the sewer grate. He lifted it.

The smell was worse.

Dale looked at the hole in the floor.

"Pull the lever," Dale said.

The surveyor hesitated.

"Do it," Dale said.

The surveyor pulled.

Water erupted from the hydrant. It sprayed into the house.

The claw shrieked. It dove into the hole.

The water rushed after it.

Dale watched the water flow. It disappeared into the dark.

The house groaned. The foundation cracked further.

The Authority text appeared.

*[Containment Failed. Infestation Relocated to Municipal Sewer System.]* *[Thoroughness Rating: 2/10.]* *[New Violation: Unauthorized Relocation of Class 5 Threat.]* *[Penalty: Certification Level Downgrade to Class II.]* *[Lockout: 72 Hours from all Level III Protocols.]*

Dale read the text.

He closed his eyes.

He opened them.

He looked at the surveyor.

"It's done," Dale said.

"It's a disaster," the surveyor said.

"It's a job," Dale said.

He turned to the woman. She was sitting on the couch, soaked.

"I'm sorry," Dale said. "I'll file the compensation claim."

"You destroyed my house," she whispered.

"I saved the block," Dale said.

He walked out.

The surveyor followed.

"Kowalski. You're downgraded.

You can't use the acid.

You can't use the thermal shock.

You're back to basics."

"I know," Dale said.

"The Rift is widening," the surveyor said. "Something big is coming. And you're locked out of the high-level gear."

Dale looked at the sky. The purple tear was pulsing.

"Then I'll find a new loophole," Dale said.

He walked to his truck. He opened the door.

His phone buzzed.

It wasn't the Authority.

It was a text from an unknown number.

*Meet me at the old cannery. Bring the guide. We need to talk about the sewers.*

Dale stared at the screen.

He typed: *Who is this?*

The reply came instantly.

*I'm the reason you're still licensed.*

Dale looked at the sewer grate.

Something was moving down there.

Not rats.

Something larger.

He got in the truck.

He didn't drive to the cannery.

He drove to the sewer.
