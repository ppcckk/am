Chapter 359: Retrieve Host Asset

# Chapter 359

The pen tip snapped against the clipboard's edge.

Dale didn't flinch. He just clicked the barrel twice until the ink flowed again.

Darkness pressed against his eyes, thick and viscous. It smelled of sulfur and old blood.

The tunnel walls pulsed, a slow, rhythmic contraction that pushed air out of porous vents in the stone.

Warm air. Breathed air.

Miller hung from the broken ladder rungs above, his boots kicking uselessly at the sludge pile.

"Stop moving," Dale said, keeping his voice level. "The vibration triggers the peristalsis."

Miller froze.

Above them, the metal grate had fused shut. Not rusted. Fused. The edges had melted together like wax.

Dale looked at the blank page of his clipboard. He wrote: *Location: Subterranean. Status: Sealed. Hazard Level: Unknown.*

The Authority's interface flickered in his peripheral vision, a translucent blue overlay that hovered in the void.

**SYSTEM NOTICE:** **Environment Analysis: Organic Tunnel Network.** **Classification: Biomechanical Host.** **Threat Type: Internal Infestation.** **Protocol Required: Ventilation Audit and Pathogen Containment.**

Dale ignored the threat classification. He focused on the audit.

He clicked his flashlight.

The beam cut through the gloom, illuminating the walls. They weren't stone.

They were chitin.

A pale, translucent material that rippled with muscle fibers beneath the surface. Capillaries of black fluid ran through them, pumping a low-grade hum that vibrated in Dale's teeth.

"Miller," Dale called up. "Do you see any light sources on the ceiling? Any vents?"

"Nothing but slime," Miller wheezed. "Dale, my arm is going numb."

"Hold on. I'm establishing the perimeter."

Dale walked ten steps forward. He stopped.

The floor was spongy, and he dug his steel-toed boot in, testing the traction.

It held.

He pulled out his Field Guide. The page for *The Nexus* was empty, but the previous entry for *Dimensional Vermin* was highlighted in red.

*Cross-Contamination Warning: Using Class-1 extermination protocols on Class-9 biological structures may result in host immune response.*

Dale grimaced. He couldn't spray. Not yet.

The environment was too reactive. If he introduced chemicals now, the walls would likely convulse, crushing them both.

He needed a different approach, and he tapped the side of the clipboard.

"Authority. Request for Environmental Data. Specifics: Airflow, pressure differentials, and heat signatures within a five-meter radius."

The system lagged. The blue text shimmered.

**PROCESSING...** **DATA UNAVAILABLE. SENSOR CALIBRATION ERROR.**

Dale frowned. The system was blind down here.

That was the loophole.

The Authority tracked infestations by monitoring the disruption of local mana fields.

But the Nexus wasn't an infestation. It was the house. If Dale treated the walls as part of the structure than a pest, the system wouldn't register him as an aggressor.

He could move freely, and he had to become part of the infrastructure.

"Miller," Dale said. "I'm going to need you to stop breathing."

Miller choked.

"What?"

"Shallow breaths. Through your nose.

We're creating a pressure differential.

If you exhale forcefully, the walls react. Watch.

"

Dale held his hand up. He took a deep breath and exhaled sharply.

The wall three feet away twitched. A spasm. A section of chitin peeled back, revealing a cavity filled with writhing, worm-like larvae.

Miller gagged.

Dale didn't look at the larvae. He looked at the cavity.

"See that?" Dale asked. "Ventilation port.

Blocked.

That's why the air is stale.

That's why the pressure is building. We're in a blocked bronchial tree.

"

"Great," Miller said. "So we're in a giant lung."

"Exactly. And lungs need oxygen. If we clear the blockage, the pressure normalizes. The exit might open."

"Or the host might cough us out into the bloodstream," Miller said.

"Better than being digested in the stomach," Dale replied. "Protocol 4-B: Clear the obstruction. Use mechanical extraction only. No chemicals."

Dale approached the cavity. The larvae writhed, their bodies slick and pale. They were blind, seeking heat.

He pulled a pair of heavy-duty pliers from his tool belt. He'd grabbed them from his kit before falling.

"Stay back," Dale said. "I need clean workspace."

Dale extended the pliers. He didn't grab the larvae. He grabbed the edge of the chitin flap that covered the vent.

The material was tough. It resisted.

Dale applied torque. His biceps burned. He gritted his teeth, focusing on the leverage.

This wasn't combat. This was maintenance.

The chitin tore. A jagged strip peeled away.

The vent opened.

A rush of air blasted out, carrying a cloud of spores.

Dale turned his head away, shielding his face with his arm.

"Mask up!" Dale shouted.

Miller fumbled for his bandana, tying it over his nose.

Dale coughed once, then stabilized. The air in the tunnel shifted. The pulsing slowed. The rhythmic thumping of the walls became smoother, less agitated.

**SYSTEM NOTIFICATION:** **VENTILATION IMPROVED.** **HOST AGITATION DECREASED BY 15%.** **TREATMENT POINTS AWARDED: 5.** **NOTE: This action was not classified as 'Extermination.' It was classified as 'Structural Repair.' Points reduced due to incomplete elimination of parasitic load.**

Dale stared at the notification. *Structural Repair.*

It was a new category. The system didn't know how to score him when he was fixing the monster.

"Five points," Dale muttered. "Pathetic. But it keeps the host calm."

He looked at the vent. The larvae were spilling out, confused by the sudden airflow.

Dale swept the pliers through them, crushing them against the tunnel wall.

One, two, three. Efficient. Quick.

No wasted motion, and he didn't enjoy it. He checked the ground. No frass trails. No egg sacs nearby.

"Clear," Dale said.

Miller was shaking.

"We can't stay here, Dale. The air is changing. It smells... sweeter."

Dale paused. He sniffed the air.

Miller was right. The sulfur was gone. Replaced by a cloying, floral scent. Like rotting lilies.

He checked his Field Guide. The entry for *The Nexus* updated automatically.

*Species: The Nexus.* *Stage: Respiratory Distress.* *Note: Host is attempting to flush intruders. Do not provoke further.*

A deep rumble echoed through the tunnel. Not from the walls. From below.

Dale looked down. The sludge pile he'd landed on was shifting. It wasn't just sludge. It was a nest.

The chitin floor beneath his feet rippled.

"Move," Dale said. "Back toward the ladder. Now."

Miller scrambled up the remaining rungs, his fingers slipping on the wet metal.

Dale backed away from the nest, keeping his pliers raised.

The floor split open.

A limb shot up. Not an arm.

A root.

Thick, pale, and covered in hook-like thorns. It slammed into the spot where Dale had been standing.

The impact sent a shockwave through the tunnel. The walls convulsed violently.

Miller lost his grip. He fell.

Dale lunged, catching Miller's belt with one hand. The weight nearly pulled Dale's arm from its socket.

"Let go!" Miller screamed.

Dale didn't let go. He planted his feet, bracing against the chitin wall. The root-limb was rising, seeking the next heat source.

Dale looked at the limb. It was connected to the main body of the Nexus.

Cutting it would trigger a systemic immune response.

A full-body convulsion, and they would be crushed.

But leaving it alone meant the limb would grab Miller.

Dale made a calculation.

He couldn't kill it. He couldn't cut it.

He had to *sedate* it, and he reached into his pocket. He didn't have a sedative. He had a roll of industrial-strength duct tape and a can of compressed air used for cleaning vents.

It wasn't a weapon. It was a gag.

Dale jammed the nozzle of the compressed air can into the joint of the root-limb, where the fibers were densest. He pulled the trigger.

A blast of freezing gas hissed into the tissue.

The root shuddered. The thorns curled inward. The limb slowed, its movement becoming sluggish and uncoordinated.

Dale swung Miller up. The man dangled, gasping, his eyes wide.

"Dale, my leg," Miller whispered.

Dale looked. A thorn had pierced Miller's calf.

It wasn't bleeding. The wound was sealed. A white, fibrous web had grown over the entry point, knitting the flesh together.

Dale stared at the web.

"Did it heal you?" Dale asked.

"It... it stopped the pain," Miller said, his voice trembling. "But it feels cold."

Dale looked at his Field Guide. A new line appeared under *The Nexus.*

*Symptom: Symbiotic Integration.* *Note: The host does not just consume. It incorporates. Do not allow tissue contact.*

The realization hit Dale like a physical blow.

The Nexus wasn't trying to kill them. It was trying to *use* them.

Miller was already becoming part of the infrastructure.

"We need to get him out," Dale said, his voice tight. "Now. Before the integration reaches the nervous system."

He hauled Miller up the last few rungs. The metal groaned under the strain.

Above them, the fused grate remained sealed.

Dale looked at the ceiling. He needed a way out.

He looked at the vent he'd cleared. The airflow was steady now. Strong.

"Miller, hold on," Dale said.

He climbed up the ladder, dragging Miller with him. They reached the top. The grate was still shut.

Dale examined the metal. It was hot.

He pressed his hand against it. It burned.

"The host is heating the area to cook us," Dale realized.

He needed to create a pressure differential strong enough to blow the grate open. Or melt it.

He couldn't melt it, and he had to blow it.

Dale looked at the vent below. The airflow was rising.

"Miller, do you have your lighter?"

Miller nodded, his face pale.

"Light the compressed air canister," Dale said. "Not the nozzle. The valve."

"That's suicide," Miller said.

"It's a controlled explosion," Dale said. "The gas will expand. The pressure will break the seal.

If you're not ready, you'll be deafened. If you are, you'll be out.

"

Miller looked at him.

"And us?"

"We'll be behind the blast radius. The tunnel curves left. It should deflect the shock."

It was a lie. The tunnel didn't curve. It was straight.

But Miller didn't know that. Miller was panicking. And panicked men followed orders.

Dale helped Miller position himself.

"Cup your hands around the valve. Strike the flint. Pull the pin on the canister.

Throw it away from us.

"

Miller's hands shook. He struck the lighter.

The spark jumped.

The canister hissed, then exploded, and the shockwave was silent in the vacuum, but the force was immense. The air in the tunnel turned into a solid wall of pressure.

Dale threw himself flat on the ladder, covering Miller with his body.

The grate above shattered.

Not melted. Shattered. The force blew the fused edges apart, sending shards of metal raining down into the tunnel below.

Sunlight.

Blinding, harsh sunlight.

The hole in the sewer was open.

Dale dragged Miller up. They climbed the final rungs, bursting out into the alleyway.

They collapsed on the wet pavement, gasping, coughing.

The sewer cover lay broken, twisted metal ringing against the asphalt.

Dale rolled onto his back. He stared at the sky. It was blue. Normal.

He checked his Field Guide.

**STATUS UPDATE:** **SUBTERRANEAN INFESTATION MITIGATED.** **HOST: NEUTRALIZED (TEMPORARILY).** **TREATMENT POINTS: 10.** **CERTIFICATION PROGRESS: 45%.**

Dale closed the guide, and he sat up. Miller was checking his leg.

The white webbing was gone.

The wound was bleeding normally now. Red, human blood.

"Did it work?" Miller asked.

"For now," Dale said.

He stood up. He brushed the sludge off his uniform. He straightened his clipboard.

A figure stood at the end of the alley.

It wasn't a monster, which was a man. Dressed in a pristine white suit. No tie. No badge.

He held a clipboard identical to Dale's.

The man looked at Dale. His eyes were entirely black. No whites. No irises.

**SYSTEM NOTIFICATION:** **RIVAL LICENSEE DETECTED.** **NAME: Agent Silas Vane.** **CERTIFICATION LEVEL: IV.** **SPECIALIZATION: Rapid Suppression.**

Dale stiffened.

Level IV. Two levels above him.

Vane stepped forward. He didn't speak. He just watched Dale.

Then, Vane raised his clipboard, and he pointed it at Dale.

A notification flashed in Dale's vision.

**CHALLENGE RECEIVED.** **SUBJECT: Dale Kowalski.** **REASON: Unauthorized Structural Modification of Nexus Site.** **PENALTY: License Suspension.** **ACCEPT?**

Dale looked at Vane. Then at the sewer. Then at his own clipboard.

He had just bitten the Authority. And now the Authority had sent a lawyer.

Dale uncapped his pen, and he didn't click *Accept*.

He didn't click *Deny*.

He wrote a new line on his report.

*Violation Code: O-1 (Obstruction of Justice).* *Perpetrator: Agent Vane.* *Status: Contested.*

Vane tilted his head. The black eyes narrowed.

For the first time, the man smiled. It was a thin, razor-sharp thing.

"You're good," Vane said. His voice sounded like grinding stones. "But you're sloppy."

Dale clicked his pen.

"I'm thorough," Dale said. "And you're in my workspace."

He raised his pliers.

Vane raised his hand. A blade of shadow materialized in his grip.

The alley darkened. The streetlights flickered and died.

And from the sewer, a sound emerged. Not a rumble.

A giggle.

High-pitched. Childlike.

*"Help me,"* it whispered. *"I'm trapped in the walls."*

Miller scrambled to his feet.

"Dale. That voice."

Dale froze, which was the voice of the tenant from the duplex. The one who had been dragged into the walls in Chapter 1.

She had been dead for years.

The giggle came again. Closer. From the sewer. From the Nexus.

Dale looked at Vane.

Vane looked at the sewer.

Then Vane looked back at Dale.

"She's not a ghost," Vane said. "She's a key."

Dale's blood ran cold.

A key.

To what?

Vane stepped back, lowering his shadow blade.

"The exam starts now," Vane said. "Find her. Or lose your license. Permanently."

Vane dissolved into smoke, drifting up into the night sky.

Dale stood alone in the alley.

The sewer grate gaped open. Dark. Waiting.

From the depths, the voice called out again.

*"Dale... please. I remember you."*

Dale looked at his clipboard. The page was full. He needed a new one.

He ripped off the top sheet, and he wrote: *Next Step: Retrieve Host Asset.*

He stepped toward the hole.

The voice stopped giggling.
