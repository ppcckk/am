Chapter 52: Recalled Field Guide

# Chapter 52

The Authority ink hit the page like a stamp.

Dale watched it dry. Fourteen years of writing reports had taught him the difference between ink that was still wet and ink that had set.

This was set, and the letters were permanent. The species name was there. He could read it.

Not Rattus.

Not any Linnaean genus. The Authority ink gave it its own designation.

A regulatory code.

The kind Dale used for structural violations.

K-7. Structural entry point.

He'd used that code before. Fourteen years ago.

A duplex in Dayton.

He'd used it for a rat.

Now the system was using it for the Authority itself.

The operator's Field Guide pulsed again. Then it went flat. Not dark.

Flat. Like a phone losing its carrier signal.

"Your guide is down," Dale said.

"It's not down.

It's being recalled." She held it up. The page she'd been reading was gone. Not blank.

Gone, and the paper was still there. The ink was still there.

But the information was being pulled out of it like a thread from a seam.

Around the parking lot, other guides were doing the same thing. Dale could see them. Phones, tablets, actual paper guides. All of them losing their entries.

The operator's face went pale. "Every technician. The Authority is pulling the guides."

"Not pulling. Recalling. There's a difference.

Pulling is when you take something away. Recalling is when the system identifies a defect and requests the return of the unit."

"That's the same thing."

"It's not. Recalled units can be reissued. Pulled units are destroyed."

She stared at him. "You're reading the system language right now."

"I'm reading the system language. It's what I do."

He looked down at his own Field Guide.

His entry was still there.

Rattus profundus. Acid beetles.

The broodmother. The dimensional vermin he'd documented over the last fifty chapters of this mess. All still there.

His guide wasn't being recalled.

Because his guide was the one that had written the entry, and the Authority was recalling every guide that contained its information. But it couldn't recall the guide that had created the recall order. That would be a circular dependency.

The system didn't process circular dependencies.

Dale knew this as well as he knew the difference between a German cockroach and a brown-banded cockroach. Not intellectually.

Structurally, and the system had rules. He knew the rules.

The operator's guide went fully dark. She dropped it like it was hot.

"Class V," she said. "They said Class V."

The air three meters behind her split open. Not a Rift.

Something cleaner.

A seam.

The kind of opening that appeared when the system was doing maintenance. No sound.

No light. Just a gap in the parking lot's geometry, and a figure stepping through it.

The Class V operator was tall. Wore a suit that looked like it had been ironed by a machine that understood ironing at a molecular level. Held no weapon. Held a clipboard.

Dale felt something click in his chest. Not recognition.

Professional kinship.

The clipboard. The posture. The figure scanned the parking lot, just as Dale scanned a kitchen.

Looking for entry points.

Looking for frass trails. Looking for nests.

The Class V operator spoke. Voice like a form letter.

"Licensed technicians in this sector.

You are now classified as symbiotic treatment agents of a Class IX infestation entity. Protocol S-7 applies. You will be isolated, treated, and reclassified.

Remain stationary."

The operator beside Dale started to run.

Dale didn't. He watched the Class V operator's clipboard. He watched the figure's movement. Watched the protocol sequence.

Identify.

Document.

Isolate. Eliminate.

Verify.

S-7. Symbiotic treatment.

The protocol for agents that were part of an infestation but not the infestation itself.

Dale had read about S-7 in the protocol manual. It was the least violent protocol in the system. It didn't eliminate.

It separated.

It cut the symbiotic link and reclassified the agent as a standalone entity.

The Class V operator reached the running technician.

Didn't touch them. Held up the clipboard. The technician froze mid-stride, like a dog hearing a command.

Then they sat down. Sat down on the asphalt.

Eyes open.

Breathing.

But not moving.

"Isolation," Dale said. "Not elimination. They're isolating."

The operator looked at him. "How do you know?"

"Protocol S-7. Symbiotic agent treatment. Isolate, treat, reclassify.

No elimination step. That's the whole point.

Symbiotic agents aren't the problem.

They're the symptom."

The Class V operator turned toward Dale, and the clipboard was raised. Dale could see the entries forming on it.

His name.

His certification level. His Thoroughness Rating. 7.7. It had been the highest in the county.

Maybe the highest in the state.

The operator's eyes moved across the clipboard. Stopped.

"Your guide is not flagged."

"No."

"Why?"

"Because I wrote the entry. You can't recall the document that issued the recall order. Circular dependency. The system doesn't process those."

The operator was quiet for three seconds. In operator time, that was probably a lifetime.

"You understand the system better than any Class V I've encountered."

"I understand the system the way I understand a cockroach infestation. It's not about intelligence. It's about pattern recognition and following the trail."

The operator lowered the clipboard. "Protocol S-7 does not apply to you."

"I know."

"You are not a symbiotic agent.

You are the entity that identified the symbiotic relationship.

In system terms, you are..." The operator paused. Looked at the clipboard again.

"You are the frass trail."

Dale blinked. "The what."

"Frass. Insect droppings.

The evidence that a nest exists.

You documented the Authority. Your Field Guide is the frass trail. The system doesn't treat frass.

It follows it."

The operator stepped closer. "The Authority is not the nest."

Dale felt cold. Not metaphorical cold. The kind that comes when you realize you've been treating the wrong pest for the entire job.

"Then what is?"

The operator didn't answer. The operator looked at Dale's Field Guide.

Then at the parking lot.

Then at the sky. The sky was wrong. Not Rift-wrong.

Wrong in a subtler way. The kind of wrong that Dale would have noticed if he'd been looking at a wall instead of a sky.

A seam.

A gap in the geometry.

Like a door that had been installed in the wrong place.

"The Authority is the nest's immune system," the operator said. "The system that protects the nest from detection. You documented the immune system.

The system is now treating the immune system. Which means the nest is no longer protected."

Dale looked at his Field Guide.

The entry he'd written. The Authority ink. It was still there.

Still set.

But below it, new ink was forming. Slow.

Like a stain spreading through paper.

A new entry.

Not the Authority. Something else. Something that had been hiding behind the Authority for as long as the Authority had existed.

The new entry had a name. Dale read it.

He didn't recognize it. He didn't recognize the taxonomy. He'd never seen a creature classified this way.

Not in fourteen years of pest control. Not in fifty chapters of the system.

The entry read:

Infestation type: Regulatory substrate.

Classification: Class IX.

Status: Active. Nesting.

Weakness: Undetermined.

Undetermined.

Dale had never seen "Undetermined" in a Field Guide entry. Every entry he'd ever documented had a weakness.

Every pest had a weakness, which was the whole point. That was the entire reason the system existed. You identify the weakness, you exploit it, you exterminate.

But this entry had no weakness.

The operator saw his face. "What does it say?"

"Undetermined."

The operator's suit rippled.

The molecular ironing. The form-letter voice.

Gone.

It was replaced by something Dale had seen in fourteen years of inspections. The face of a man who'd just found out that the thing in the walls was bigger than the walls.

"The system is trying to determine the weakness," the operator said. "It's trying to write it."

"Then we wait."

"No." The operator's clipboard was changing. Entries appearing and disappearing.

The Class V operator was being reclassified.

In real time, and the symbiotic link was being cut. The operator was becoming a standalone entity.

Which meant the operator was losing access to the Authority's protocols.

"I'm losing Class V," the operator said. "If the Authority is being treated, I lose my protocols. I lose my clearance. I lose everything."

"How long?"

"Minutes."

Dale looked at the new entry. Undetermined.

No weakness.

A Class IX infestation with no known weakness, and every licensed technician on the planet was about to lose their tools.

He looked at his Field Guide.

His entries. All still there.

Because he was the frass trail.

The system couldn't touch him. But his entries were all for creatures that no longer existed. Rattus profundus.

Acid beetles. The broodmother.

All of them were Authority agents

All of them were Authority agents.

The thought didn't land like a revelation. It landed like a code violation. The kind you write down because the math doesn't add up and the numbers are the only thing you trust.

"Rattus profundus," Dale said. "You created those."

The operator didn't answer, and the clipboard was empty now. Blank. The molecular ironing had stopped.

The suit was just a suit. The man inside it was just a man, and his hands were shaking, and Dale recognized that too. Fourteen years of watching homeowners realize the exterminator wasn't going to fix the problem because the problem was the house.

"The Field Guide is still populated," Dale said. He looked at his entries.

Still there.

Still readable. But the weakness lines were gone. Not "Undetermined." Gone.

As though they had never been written.

He'd been writing them. For fourteen chapters of his life.

Every weakness he'd identified.

Every targeted damage bonus. Every protocol that had worked. They hadn't been discoveries.

They'd been permissions. The Authority had been letting him see the weaknesses because the Authority wanted the creatures dead.

And now the Authority was being exterminated.

Dale looked at the ceiling. At the crack that hadn't been there an hour ago. At the light coming through it, which was not sunlight, which was not any light he could name, which was the color of a violation code that didn't exist yet.

"The system is being treated," he said. "By something that has a protocol for it."

"Yes."

"Then that thing has a Field Guide entry for the Authority."

The operator nodded.

"And if it has a Field Guide entry, it has a weakness."

"Yes."

Dale pulled out his clipboard.

His real clipboard.

The one with the paper on it. The one that had nothing to do with the system, nothing to do with Treatment Points or Certification Levels or the Authority's bureaucratic architecture.

Just paper. Just a pen. Just a man who'd been writing things down for fourteen years because writing things down was how you made a problem smaller than yourself.

He wrote: *Infestation type: Authority. Classification: Class IX. Status: Being treated. Weakness: Undetermined.*

He looked at the operator. "You said the system is trying to write the weakness."

"Yes."

"It's not writing it in the Field Guide."

The operator's face went still.

"It's writing it in whoever's holding the clipboard."

Dale looked down at his own clipboard. At the words he'd just written.

And beneath them, in handwriting that was not his, in ink that was not ink, a second line was forming.

Slowly. Like a technician filling out a form they didn't want to fill out.

*Weakness: The technician who wrote the report.*

The pen in his hand went cold.
