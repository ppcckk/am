Chapter 219: Zoning Violation

# Chapter 219

The crowbar connected with the thing's knee.

Not the head. Not the chest. The knee.

Bone snapped with a wet, dry crunch. The creature shrieked, a sound like tearing metal, and stumbled sideways.

Its legs were too long, folding under its own weight.

Dale didn't stop. He didn't check his footing. He swung again, aiming for the other knee.

**SYSTEM NOTICE: IMPROVISED WEAPON DEPLOYED.** **CLASS: NON-CERTIFIED.** **EFFICIENCY: 0%.** **TREATMENT POINTS AWARDED: 0.**

The notification flickered in the corner of his vision, red and mocking.

Dale ignored it. He knew the rules.

Killing without documentation yielded zero points.

Killing with the wrong protocol yielded a debuff. Killing with a crowbar yielded nothing but exhaustion and a broken tool.

But it bought time.

The creature crashed into a patio chair. Plastic legs splintered.

It scrambled back, claws scraping against the concrete.

It didn't look angry.

It looked confused. A Class 5 entity, expecting resistance from a warrior, now faced a man in a stained polo shirt treating its limbs like structural failures in a drywall installation.

"Vane," Dale said, breathless. "I need a camera."

She was already moving. Not toward the monster. Toward the house.

"Too late," she said. "Perimeter breach is already logged. The Authority flagged us the moment it crossed the fence line. We're in a live audit."

Dale wiped sweat from his forehead. The crowbar bent at the hook. Useless. He dropped it.

"Then we document," Dale said. "Fast."

He pulled out his phone. The screen was cracked, but the Authority's interface overlaid the display, crisp and bureaucratic. He opened the Field Guide.

It was empty. No entry for this specimen.

No weakness identified.

No treatment protocol unlocked.

The creature rose. Its faceless mask turned toward him. Mandibles clicked.

**WARNING: BIOLOGICAL SIGNATURE UNKNOWN.** **SUGGESTION: RETREAT.**

Dale tapped the screen. *Identify.*

A loading bar spun. A simple, circular progress icon. It crawled forward at 1%.

The creature lunged.

Dale sidestepped.

He didn't run; he pivoted, using the patio table as a pivot point. The creature's claw slashed the air where his head had been.

It missed by an inch.

Dale felt the wind of the strike on his cheek. He didn't flinch. He watched the claw retract.

"Vane, give me a descriptor," Dale said, still circling. "Chitin color? Limb ratio? Eye placement?"

"It's white," Vane yelled from the porch. She had a tablet out now, recording.

"Smooth.

No texture.

And it smells like ozone and rotting eggs."

"Ozone and eggs," Dale muttered. He typed it into the identification field.

The progress bar jumped to 4%.

The creature shrieked. It wasn't pain. It was offense.

Being described was being contained. It swarmed forward, limbs blurring.

Dale dove behind the propane tank for his grill.

The creature slammed into the metal, denting the surface.

*Thump. Thump. Thump.*

The tank leaked gas.

Dale stared at the leak.

A small hiss. A perfect opportunity.

And a catastrophic safety violation.

If he ignited it, he could kill the creature. But the fire would void his license. Uncontrolled combustion in a residential zone?

Immediate suspension. Maybe revocation.

He closed his eyes for half a second.

*Protocol 4-B: Containment via localized pressure disruption.*

He didn't have the tools. But he had the tank.

"Vane," Dale said, voice flat. "Don't light anything."

The creature pulled back, its limbs unfolding again. It prepared to leap onto the tank.

**IDENTIFICATION PROGRESS: 7%.** **ESTIMATED TIME TO COMPLETE: 45 SECONDS.**

Forty-five seconds. He had to survive forty-five seconds of a Class 5 melee without dying, without running, and without using a banned substance.

Dale looked at the patio furniture. A wicker chair. A glass table. A flower pot.

He grabbed the flower pot. Terracotta. Heavy.

He threw it.

Not at the creature. At the ground in front of it.

The pot shattered.

Soil and shards scattered.

The creature hesitated. Not because of the damage.

Because of the pattern. Dale knew that Class 5 entities, like many high-tier vermin, relied on sensory input to locate prey. Disrupting the ground texture confused their proprioception.

The creature's left foot slipped in the wet dirt. It stumbled.

**IDENTIFICATION PROGRESS: 12%.**

"Nice shot," Vane said, her voice tight.

"It wasn't a shot," Dale said. "It was a disturbance. Keep talking. What else does it do?"

"It clicks," Vane said. "Before it strikes. Like a warning. Or a targeting mechanism."

"Targeting," Dale repeated. He stepped out from behind the tank.

The creature heard him. It snapped its head toward the sound. Mandibles flared.

Dale raised his hands. Empty.

"Look at me," he said. Calm.

Professional.

"I am the inspector. I am documenting you."

The creature paused. The clicking slowed.

Was it fear? No. Confusion. It had never encountered a predator that announced itself as a bureaucrat.

**IDENTIFICATION PROGRESS: 18%.** **DATA POINT ADDED: VOCALIZATION PRECEDES STRIKE.**

Dale stepped closer. One step. Two steps.

The creature tensed.

Dale stopped.

He pulled out his clipboard. Actual paper.

From his back pocket.

He clicked the pen.

He wrote.

*Subject: Unknown Class 5.* *Location: Residential patio, 412 Maple Drive.* *Time of Encounter: 18:42.* *Behavioral Note: Exhibits predatory stance but hesitates upon direct address.*

The creature watched him write. It tilted its head.

**IDENTIFICATION PROGRESS: 25%.**

"Why is it stopping?" Vane asked, whispering.

"It's waiting for the next input," Dale said, not looking up. "It's analyzing the threat level. It doesn't understand me. It's trying to categorize me."

"Category: Victim?"

"Category: Unknown," Dale corrected. "And unknowns are risky.

It's holding back.

Good.

That means it's thinking. Thinking is slow."

The creature clicked again. Louder this time. It took a step forward.

Dale didn't retreat. He wrote faster.

*Behavioral Note: Aggression triggered by movement, not proximity. Stationary subject may be perceived as non-threat.*

He stood perfectly still.

The creature froze. Its claw hovered inches from Dale's face. He could smell the ozone.

It was sharp, metallic, burning his nostrils. He could see the pores in its chitin, or what passed for pores.

Tiny, black pits.

**IDENTIFICATION PROGRESS: 33%.** **NEW DATA POINT: CHITIN POROSITY.**

Dale's eyes widened. Porosity.

That was the weakness.

Not armor. Not muscle. The pores.

They were sensory organs.

If he could block them, he could blind it.

He looked at Vane.

"Vinegar," he said.

"What?"

"Acid.

Household. White vinegar. It's in the kitchen.

Go."

Vane didn't argue. She ran inside.

Dale remained frozen.

The creature's claw trembled.

It was confused. Why was the prey not fleeing?

Why was it writing?

**IDENTIFICATION PROGRESS: 40%.**

The creature leaned in. It wanted to see the clipboard. Curiosity. A flaw in its programming.

Dale held his breath. He could feel the heat of its body. It was cold-blooded, but the friction of its movement generated warmth.

*Identify.*

He typed the new data point into the guide.

*Weakness: Sensory Pores.*

The bar spiked to 50%.

**SYSTEM NOTICE: PRELIMINARY FIELD GUIDE ENTRY CREATED.** **STATUS: INCOMPLETE.** **TREATMENT POINTS: 0.** **NOTE: ENTRY REQUIRES VERIFICATION VIA ELIMINATION.**

Halfway there. But the guide was incomplete. He couldn't use a targeted protocol yet. He had to kill it manually to verify the weakness.

The creature lunged.

Dale dropped to the ground. The claw slashed over his head.

He rolled, coming up on one knee.

The crowbar was too far. The grill was too far.

He looked at the propane tank. The leak was still hissing.

Vinegar wasn't coming fast enough.

He needed to block the pores.

He looked at the shattered flower pot. Soil. Dust.

He grabbed a handful of dry dirt from the broken pot's contents.

The creature turned to strike him again. It raised both arms.

Dale threw the dirt.

Not at its face. At the mask.

The dust cloud enveloped the creature's head. The tiny black pits were clogged.

The creature shrieked.

A sound of pure distress. It flailed, swinging wildly.

It couldn't see.

It couldn't hear through the dust.

**SYSTEM NOTICE: SENSORY DEPRIVATION ACHIEVED.** **TARGET ACCURACY: 0%.** **THREAT LEVEL: CLASS 5 (IMPaired).**

Dale didn't wait. He grabbed the crowbar.

He didn't swing at the body. He swung at the ground.

He hit the patio tile. Hard. The vibration traveled up the creature's legs. It was blind, but it could feel the ground.

Dale hit the tile again. And again.

The creature stumbled, disoriented by the vibrations and the blocked senses. It swung blindly, smashing a chair to pieces.

Vane came running out. A bottle of white vinegar in her hand.

"Here!" she shouted.

Dale took it. He uncapped it.

He poured the vinegar directly onto the mask.

The dust washed away. The liquid ran into the pores.

The creature convulsed.

**SYSTEM NOTICE: CHEMICAL IRRITANT APPLIED.** **TARGET STAGGERED.** **VERIFICATION IN PROGRESS...**

Dale stepped in. The crowbar hooked around the creature's neck.

He didn't pull.

He pushed.

He drove the metal into the soft tissue beneath the mask, right where the pores clustered.

The creature spasmed. It fell.

Dale pinned it. He used his foot to hold its shoulder down. He raised the crowbar.

He didn't swing wildly. He targeted the center of the mask. The largest cluster of pores.

One strike.

Two strikes.

Three.

The creature went limp.

**SYSTEM NOTICE: SPECIMEN NEUTRALIZED.** **VERIFICATION COMPLETE.** **FIELD GUIDE ENTRY: "THE SILENT WATCHER" CREATED.** **WEAKNESS: SENSORY PORES (VULNERABLE TO PARTICULATE BLOCKAGE AND ACIDIC IRRITANTS).** **TREATMENT POINTS AWARDED: 150.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 6.4.**

Dale sat back on his heels. He was shaking. Not from fear. From adrenaline dump.

He looked at the notification.

6.4.

Sloppy. He had used improvised tools. He had violated safety protocols (propane tank near combat zone). He had failed to secure the perimeter properly.

But he had identified the weakness. He had verified the kill.

He pulled out his pen. He wrote in his physical report, ignoring the digital notification.

*Incident Report: Unlicensed Entity, Class 5.* *Method: Chemical/Particulate Disruption of Sensory Organs. Mechanical Termination of Central Nervous Cluster.* *Notes: Subject exhibited curiosity-based hesitation. Recommend deploying distraction protocols for similar entities.*

He signed it.

Vane stood over him, breathing hard. She looked at the corpse.

"150 points," she said. "That's more than you've earned in the last month."

Dale looked at the rating. 6.4.

"It's not enough," he said.

"It's progress, Dale."

"No. Look at the report." He pointed to the digital log. "It flagged the propane leak.

It flagged the lack of PPE. It flagged the use of an uncertified weapon."

"Who cares? You're alive."

"I'm certified Class II," Dale said. "I need a 9.0 average to qualify for the next tier of threats. My current average is 7.1. A 6.4 drops me to 6.9."

Vane frowned. "So?"

"So, I'm getting demoted."

Dale stood up. He wiped his hands on his pants. He looked at the house. The front door was open.

"Vane," he said. "Did we seal the entry point?"

Vane's face went pale.

The ground outside the patio. The crack that had widened when the creature leaped.

It hadn't closed.

And it was growing.

Something else was coming up.

Not a creature.

A hand.

Human.

No. Not human. Too many fingers. Long, spindly fingers, pale as bone.

It gripped the edge of the patio slab.

**SYSTEM NOTICE: NEW INFESTATION DETECTED.** **SEVERITY: CLASS 7.** **STATUS: ESCALATING.**

Dale looked at his phone. His certification progress bar was flashing red.

**DEMOTION IMMINENT.** **ACTION REQUIRED: COMPLETE MAJOR INCIDENT REPORT WITHIN 10 MINUTES TO AVOID REVOCATION.**

He looked at the hand pulling itself up.

He looked at his empty clipboard.

He looked at Vane.

"I need a witness," Dale said. "And I need a stamp."

"For what?"

"For the permit application," Dale said. "Because this isn't an infestation anymore. It's a zoning violation."

The hand pulled up another limb. Then another.

A head emerged. It had no face. Just the same white, smooth mask as the first one. But bigger.

And it was wearing a suit.
