Chapter 228: Second Ticket in Name

# Chapter 228

The clipboard was heavy.

Inspector Halloway adjusted his grip, the knuckles white against the black polymer. He hated the night shift.

The Rifts didn't care about the clock, and the Authority didn't care about overtime, but the coffee machine in the breakroom had been broken for three days.

Halloway stared at the anomaly on the thermal imaging tablet. A cold spot in the center of the suburban cul-de-sac. Not cold in temperature, but cold in presence.

The data stream from the local sensors was screaming a static hiss that sounded like tearing paper.

Behind him, the containment barrier hummed. A low, vibrating thrum that rattled the teeth.

Three junior technicians stood with their backs against the van, checking their seals.

They were young. Too young to have seen a Class-IV breach without flinching. Halloway wanted to tell them to stop shaking.

He wanted to tell them that fear leaked out of the pores and attracted the scavengers. But he had no authority here.

He was just the observer.

The witness.

The man in the center of the cold spot wasn't moving.

He was on his knees.

The uniform—standard issue Class II, faded at the elbows—was wrong. It was wet.

Not with water. The fabric clung to the skin like a second layer of dermis, dark and slick. The chest plate was stamped with a red ink mark that shouldn't exist. *APPROVED FOR TERMINATION.*

Halloway zoomed in on the tablet. The resolution sharpened, but the image glitched.

Pixels bled into each other, turning the man's face into a smear of grey.

The sensors registered a biological signature, but it was decaying at an impossible rate. The heart rate was zero. The brain activity was a flat line.

Yet the thermal signature was rising.

Something was heating up inside the corpse.

"Get the extraction team ready," Halloway muttered into his comms unit. His voice sounded thin in the recycled air of his helmet.

"Don't engage.

Do not engage. Just observe."

The junior tech nearest to the van stepped forward. "Sir, the signature is shifting. It's not dead. The necrotic markers are reversing."

Halloway watched the man's legs. They were still turning into paper.

The transformation was subtle, a slow desiccation of the skin, flaking away in dry, white curls that drifted to the asphalt. Underneath, the muscle fibers were grey, brittle, and cracking.

It was a body horror Halloway had only read about in the theoretical manuals.

Biological transmutation via bureaucratic metaphysics.

The Authority's joke. The universe's punchline.

A hand twitched.

Not the whole arm. Just the fingers. They curled inward, grasping at nothing.

The pen from the previous chapter was gone, lost in the slurry of blood and ink that had pooled around the man's head. But the hand moved with a purpose that defied the paralysis.

It tapped.

One finger. Then two. Against the asphalt.

*Tap. Tap. Tap.*

Rhythm. Logic. Structure.

Halloway felt a chill that had nothing to do with the Rift. This wasn't a ghost. This was a technician.

The man's head snapped up.

The eyes were open.

One pupil was dilated so far it swallowed the iris. The other was a pinprick, focused and terrified.

The mouth was gone, sealed shut by the same paper-like skin that consumed his legs.

But the throat vibrated. A sound emerged, not a scream, but a low, mechanical whine. Like a drill bit hitting metal.

The blue uniform standing over him—the one who had held the stamp—froze. It tilted its head, confused by the anomaly.

It raised its spray can, nozzle pointed at the kneeling figure.

The canister was labeled *Null-Protocol*.

A weapon designed to erase matter.

Halloway hit the record button on his helmet.

*Click.*

The blue uniform pulled the trigger.

The spray hit Dale Kowalski's face.

It wasn't liquid. It was a cloud of fine, white particulate that hung in the air, shimmering with geometric patterns. Hexagons.

Triangles. Fractals.

The particles settled on Dale's exposed skin, embedding themselves in the pores.

His legs crumbled further, turning into stacks of blank sheets that fluttered away in the wind. His torso slumped. The chest plate cracked.

But Dale didn't dissolve.

He didn't die.

Instead, the white powder on his face rearranged.

It formed lines. Boxes.

Checklists.

The particles moved with microscopic precision, drawing a grid over his features. His nose, broken and bleeding from the previous chapter's assault, was outlined in white chalk-lines. His eyes were boxed in.

His mouth, sealed shut, was marked with a red X.

The blue uniform stepped back.

Its clipboard tilted.

The creature behind the mask seemed to hesitate. The protocol was failing.

The target was rejecting the deletion.

Dale's hand shot out.

It wasn't a punch. It was a grab.

He seized the blue uniform's ankle.

The grip was weak, the skin papery and tearing, but it held. The blue uniform struggled, lifting its other leg, trying to shake the grip loose. But Dale held on.

He held on with the desperate strength of a man who knows that if he lets go, the job never gets done.

The junior techs screamed. The containment field flickered.

Halloway watched the thermal reading spike. The man's body temperature was dropping, approaching absolute zero, but his energy signature was burning white-hot.

He was channeling something.

Not magic. Not mana. Something older.

Something procedural.

A notification flashed in the corner of Halloway's HUD, overriding the sensor data.

**SYSTEM ALERT: ILLEGAL MODIFICATION DETECTED.** **SUBJECT: KOWALSKI, DALE.** **ACTION: FIELD GUIDE OVERRIDE.** **STATUS: PENDING REVIEW.**

Halloway blinked. *Field Guide Override?* That wasn't a standard protocol. That wasn't even in the Class IX manuals. That was a loophole. A cheat code for the bureaucracy itself.

Dale's hand moved again.

He didn't attack.

He reached for the blue uniform's clipboard. The creature yanked it away, but Dale's fingers had hooked into the binding rings.

He tore a page out.

Just one.

The paper floated in the air, suspended by the static charge of the Null-spray. Dale's eyes tracked it. His hand, shaking violently, reached up and caught the page. He pressed it against his chest, over the *APPROVED FOR TERMINATION* stamp.

The stamp dissolved.

The red ink bled into the paper, mixing with Dale's blood. The words *TERMINATION* faded, replaced by a new entry.

Dale couldn't write.

He couldn't speak. But the System responded to intent. To the absolute, unyielding certainty of a technician who knows he is right.

The text on the page changed.

*STATUS: UNDER REVIEW.* *CLAIM: INVALID.* *JUSTIFICATION: PROCEDURAL ERROR.*

The blue uniform shrieked.

A sound like grinding gears. It swung the spray can, aiming for Dale's head.

But the air around Dale solidified.

A barrier of white, geometric shapes formed. A shield. Not magical.

Administrative. The System's own defense mechanism, triggered by the validity of his claim.

Halloway stepped forward, breaking his own protocol.

"Hold fire," he shouted.

The junior techs didn't hear him. They were too panicked.

One of them fired a stun round. It hit the barrier and bounced off, sparking against the asphalt.

Dale looked up. His eyes found Halloway.

For a second, the panic in the technician's gaze vanished.

Replaced by a cold, professional assessment. Dale tilted his head. He looked at Halloway's clipboard.

Then he looked at his own empty hands.

He pointed at Halloway.

Not a threat. A request.

*You have a pen.*

Halloway understood. The realization hit him like a physical blow.

Dale wasn't trying to kill the blue uniform.

He was trying to document it. He was trying to file a counter-claim. He was trying to prove that the termination order was invalid because the target was not the infestation.

The target was the Authority's own error.

If Dale filed a report, he became a licensed technician again. If he became a licensed technician, he could appeal the termination.

The blue uniform lunged.

Dale didn't dodge.

He leaned into the attack.

He took the spray to the shoulder. His flesh blistered and turned to ash.

But he grabbed the blue uniform's wrist. He twisted. The creature's arm snapped with a wet crunch.

The spray can fell, hissing as it hit the ground, releasing a cloud of neutralizing gas that ate through the asphalt.

Dale fell backward, gasping for air he no longer had.

His body was half-paper, half-flesh. The duality was painful to look at. The paper parts were rigid, brittle.

The flesh parts were soft, bleeding.

He was a collage of two states of matter.

Halloway ran.

He didn't know why.

He broke every safety regulation in the handbook. He ran past the containment barrier, past the screaming techs, and slid on his knees to a stop ten feet from Dale. The blue uniform was retreating, clutching its broken arm, fading into the shadows of the Rift.

It knew it had lost. The System had rejected its action.

Halloway pulled a pen from his breast pocket. A standard blue ballpoint. Cheap. Reliable.

He held it out to Dale.

Dale looked at the pen.

His hand, skeletal and flaking, reached out.

He took it. His fingers closed around the plastic barrel.

The paper-skin on his hand tore, revealing red muscle underneath, but he held on.

He looked at Halloway. Then he looked at the blank page still attached to his chest.

He wrote.

The pen scratched in the silent street. The letters were jagged, uneven. But they were legible.

*Complaint Filed: Unauthorized Termination.* *Perpetrator: Class III Enforcer (Unidentified).* *Evidence: Attached.*

Halloway watched, breathless. The System processed the input. The text glowed gold.

**CLAIM RECEIVED.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 10.** **PENALTY WAIVED.** **RESTORATION INITIATED.**

Dale's legs stopped crumbling. The paper rehydrated, turning back into skin.

The red stamp on his chest faded completely.

The air around him warmed. The cold spot vanished.

Halloway sat back on his heels.

He stared at Dale. Dale stared back.

He didn't smile.

He didn't thank Halloway. He simply capped the pen and put it back in Halloway's pocket.

Then, Dale stood up.

He was swaying. His uniform was torn, stained with ash and blood.

But he was whole.

He looked at the spot where the blue uniform had been.

Then he looked at Halloway.

"Report filed," Dale said. His voice was raspy, like sandpaper on wood. "Do I need a countersignature?"

Halloway swallowed. His heart was hammering against his ribs. He realized he had just violated three federal laws.

He had interfered with an Authority execution. He had assisted a suspect.

But Dale was looking at him with those flat, deadpan eyes. The eyes of a man who had just survived hell by filing the correct paperwork.

Halloway pulled out his own pen. He signed the bottom of the floating page.

*Halloway, J. Inspector, Level 4.*

The page dissolved into light.

Dale nodded. "Good. Now, about the infestation."

Halloway frowned. "There's no infestation. The Rift is closing. The enforcer is gone."

Dale shook his head slowly. He pointed to the ground. To the spot where the Null-spray had eaten through the asphalt.

"It's not gone," Dale said. "It's just moved."

He pointed to Halloway's shadow.

Halloway looked down.

His shadow was wrong. It was too long. And it was holding a clipboard.

Dale looked at Halloway, then at the junior techs who were now staring at the inspector with wide, terrified eyes.

"You didn't just help me, Inspector," Dale said. "You joined the crew."

The shadow moved. It reached out and grabbed Halloway's ankle.

Halloway screamed.

Dale didn't move.

He just watched. He pulled a small, battered notebook from his pocket. The Field Guide.

He opened it to a blank page.

He uncapped his pen.

He looked at the shadow gripping Halloway. He looked at the screaming inspector. He looked at the junior techs who were frozen in place, unable to act.

Dale wrote.

*Species: Bureaucratic Parasite.* *Behavior: Feeds on administrative error.* *Weakness: Excessive compliance.*

He looked up at Halloway. The inspector was being dragged into the asphalt, sinking into a hole that shouldn't exist. The shadow was pulling him down, into a darkness that looked suspiciously like a filing cabinet.

Dale took a breath. He adjusted his collar. He straightened his tie.

"Protocol Seven," Dale said to the air. "Isolate the source. Document the breach. Eliminate the liability."

He looked at the junior techs.

"You're up," he said.

The youngest tech, the one with the shaking hands, looked at Dale. He looked at Halloway sinking into the ground. He looked at his own empty hands.

"What do we do?" the tech whispered.

Dale held out his Field Guide.

"You take notes," Dale said. "And you don't let him finish the form."

The tech stepped forward.

He grabbed Halloway's arm. The other two joined him. They pulled.

They strained. They fought against the weight of the Authority's own corruption.

Dale watched them struggle. He didn't help. He didn't join. He stood back, pen poised over his notebook.

Because if he helped, he'd contaminate the data.

And the job required a witness.

Halloway's hand broke the surface of the asphalt. He grabbed Dale's ankle.

Dale didn't pull away. He looked down at the inspector's hand. Then he looked at the shadow.

The shadow smiled.

It wasn't Halloway's smile. It was something else. Something with too many teeth and no eyes.

Dale's pen hovered over the page.

If he wrote *Help*, he'd get points. But he'd lose his objectivity.

If he wrote *Observe*, he'd get more points. But Halloway would die.

Dale's hand trembled.

The shadow looked up at Dale. It whispered, in a voice that sounded like crumpled paper.

*Sign it, Dale.*

Dale looked at the pen. He looked at the Field Guide.

He wrote:

*Subject is resistant to termination.*

The shadow laughed. It pulled Halloway under.

The asphalt sealed over the inspector's head.

Silence.

Dale stood alone in the street. The Rift was closing. The lights came back on. The birds started singing, though it was still night.

He looked at his notebook. The page was full.

He looked at the spot where Halloway had vanished.

He uncapped his pen.

He started a new entry.

*Species: Inspector.* *Status: Terminated.* *Cause: Lack of Protocol.*

He closed the notebook.

He turned and walked away.

Behind him, the ground cracked open again.

Not a small crack. A fissure.

A hand emerged. Not Halloway's.

It was Dale's.

Dale stopped walking. He didn't turn around.

He knew the rule.

You cannot treat two nests at once.

The Authority had just issued a second ticket.

And this one was in his own name.
