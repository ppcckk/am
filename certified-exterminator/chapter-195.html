Chapter 195: Loophole of Containment

# Chapter 195

The ground did not open; it unzipped.

A seam of violet light split the asphalt, widening with the slow, wet sound of a zipper being pulled apart by unseen fingers. The air pressure dropped so that Dale's ears popped. His Field Guide flickered in the corner of his vision, a persistent orange border that pulsed in time with his heartbeat.

*CLASS-IX BREACH DETECTED.* *PROTOCOL: CONTAINMENT PRIORITIZED.* *WARNING: UNLICENSED PARTICIPANT PRESENT.*

Dale ignored the warning. He looked at Vane.

She stood rigid, her rifle raised, her knuckles white against the polymer grip.

Her eyes were fixed on the seam, not on him. That was good. If she looked at him, she would see the black ichor weeping from his pores, the way his skin had begun to bubble and harden into chitin.

He could feel the hive mind buzzing in his teeth, a low-frequency hum that tasted like copper and old blood.

"Keep writing," Dale said. His voice sounded like gravel grinding in a mixer. "If you stop, the Authority marks it as non-compliant."

Vane's pen scratched against her notebook. The sound was small, insignificant against the roaring void, but it was the only thing anchoring them to reality.

The breach widened. From the violet seam, shapes pulled themselves out.

They were not rats, and they were not beetles. They were geometric impossibilities, shards of obsidian glass that moved with the fluid grace of eels. *Shard-Worms*. A new species.

Dale's Field Guide updated instantly.

*SPECIES: SHARD-WORM (FRAGMENTED REALITY VARIANT).* *STATUS: UNVERIFIED.* *WEAKNESS: UNKNOWN.*

"Unknown weakness," Dale muttered. He reached for his clipboard.

The paper was already damp with ichor, but he held it steady.

"Standard protocol requires identification before elimination.

We can't spray what we don't understand.

"

Vane lowered the rifle slightly, her eyes darting to the approaching horde. There were dozens of them, sliding over the cracked earth, their bodies refracting the violet light into blinding rainbows.

"They're coming," she said.

"No," Dale corrected. "They're *emerging*. There is a difference.

One implies intent. The other implies biology.

"

He stepped forward.

The chitin on his legs clicked against the asphalt. It was a loud, dry sound, like a beetle's carapace. The hive mind surged, eager to attack, but Dale forced it down.

He had to follow the protocol.

If he attacked now, he would contaminate the sample. If he contaminated the sample, he lost his Thoroughness Rating.

And if he lost his rating, the Authority would revoke his license.

Without a license, he was just another casualty.

"Vane," Dale said, not looking back. "Record the emergence pattern. Note the color spectrum. Is it pure violet, or is there a gradient?"

"Gradient," Vane said, her voice trembling. "Deep purple at the base, fading to pink at the tips. Like... like a bruise."

Dale paused. A bruise. That was a metaphor.

Metaphors were inefficient, but they were often accurate. He watched the Shard-Worms slide past each other, their bodies intersecting without collision.

They were phase-shifted, and they existed out of sync with local space.

"Phase-shifted entities," Dale said. "Standard pesticides won't work. You can't poison something that isn't fully there."

"So what do we do?" Vane asked. She looked at him then, looked at him.

Her face was pale, sweat beading on her forehead. She saw the black fluid pooling in the hollows of his collarbone. She didn't scream.

She just wrote it down.

"We isolate," Dale said.

He reached into his utility belt and pulled out a canister.

It wasn't spray. It was a vacuum sealant, thick and viscous, designed for sealing cracks in dimensional foundations. It was a Class-II tool, usually used for minor tears in the fabric of suburban basements.

Using it on a Class-9 breach was reckless, which was a violation of Protocol 4-B: *Do not use containment tools on offensive entities.*

But Dale saw the loophole.

The Authority rewarded thoroughness.

It did not reward bravery. It rewarded procedure. If he could document the isolation, he could get the points.

And if he got the points, he might survive the audit.

He uncapped the sealant. The smell was acrid, like burning hair and ozone.

"Move left," Dale commanded.

Vane hesitated.

"The hive is behind us."

"The hive is irrelevant. The breach is the problem. Move left. Create a perimeter."

She moved, which was a small movement, a shuffle of boots on gravel, but it broke the symmetry of their standing.

The Shard-Worms paused. Their heads, if they had heads, tilted.

They were reacting to the change in spatial dynamics.

Dale stepped forward, directly into the path of the nearest creature. It lunged, a blur of violet glass. He didn't dodge. He let it hit him.

The impact was cold. Not painful, but deeply unsettling.

The Shard-Worm passed through his torso, leaving a trail of freezing air and crystalline dust.

Dale didn't flinch. He kept his clipboard steady.

"Documenting contact," he said aloud, ensuring the Authority's audio sensors picked up the words. "Entity passes through biological host. No physical damage sustained. Confirms phase-shift hypothesis."

Vane stared at him, her mouth open.

"You're insane."

"I'm certified," Dale corrected.

He sprayed the sealant.

Not at the worms. At the ground around them.

A ring of thick, black goo encircled them, rising in the air and hardening instantly into a translucent barrier.

The Shard-Worms hit the barrier and bounced off, unable to penetrate the solidified reality anchor.

*ISOLATION COMPLETE.* *AWARDING 50 TREATMENT POINTS.* *FIELD GUIDE UPDATED: SHARD-WORM WEAKNESS IDENTIFIED – SPATIAL ANCHORING.*

The notification flashed in Dale's vision. Green text. Clean. Efficient.

But the cost was immediate. The sealant was drawing energy from Dale's body.

He felt a sharp tug in his chest, a hook pulling at his soul.

The Authority was auditing him in real-time, and they were checking his biometrics. They were comparing his stress levels to the required baseline for a Class-9 operation.

His stress levels were too low.

That was a problem. The Authority expected fear. Or at least, a physiological response consistent with danger. By remaining calm, by treating the apocalypse as a code violation, Dale was flagging himself as *anomalous*.

*ANOMALY DETECTED.* *COMPLIANCE CHECK FAILED.* *PENALTY: -10 TREATMENT POINTS.* *REVIEW FLAGGED FOR INTERVENTION.*

Dale felt a cold spike in his gut. He had made a mistake. He had been too professional.

The system wanted him to panic, to fight, to *feel*. It didn't know what to do with a man who saw a monster and saw a filing number.

"Vane," Dale said, his voice dropping. "Read the violation code. Now."

"What?"

"OSHA Code 12-B. Unauthorized entry into a hazardous zone. Read it."

Vane frowned, but she obeyed.

"OSHA Code 12-B. Unauthorized entry..."

"Stop."

The world jerked. The violet light of the breach flickered. The Shard-Worms froze, mid-slither. The silence that followed was heavier than the noise had been.

Dale looked down at his hands. The black ichor was spreading faster, climbing up his wrists. He could feel the hive mind pressing against the inside of his skull, begging to be let out, begging to destroy the barrier, begging to consume the light.

He wasn't just fighting the monsters. He was fighting the system's expectation of him as a victim.

And he was losing.

The barrier of sealant cracked.

Not because the worms were strong enough to break it, but because Dale's will was slipping. The chitinous plates on his skin were spreading to his neck, his jaw. He could taste the hive's hunger on his tongue.

It wanted to speak.

It wanted to tell him that he was already one of them.

"Write it down," Dale whispered. "Write down that I am refusing the intervention."

Vane's pen hovered over the paper.

"Dale, your eyes..."

"I am refusing the intervention," he said, louder. The Authority's voice echoed in his mind, a cold, bureaucratic chorus.

*SUBJECT IS NON-COMPLIANT.* *SUBJECT IS UNDER EXTERNAL INFLUENCE.* *INITIATING PURGE PROTOCOL.*

The sky above them turned white.

Not the white of clouds, but the white of a blank page. A beam of pure, erasing light coalesced in the center of the breach. It wasn't aimed at the worms.

It was aimed at Dale, and he had triggered the system's fail-safe. By refusing to panic, he had become a threat to the narrative of the audit. The Authority wanted a hero, or a victim. It did not want a technician who questioned the premise of the test.

Vane raised her rifle. Not at Dale. At the sky.

"Stop!" she shouted. "He's documenting! He's doing the job!"

The light didn't stop. It intensified.

Dale looked at Vane, and he smiled, a grotesque stretching of hardened skin.

He realized what was happening. The Authority wasn't just auditing the breach.

It was auditing *him*. And he had failed. He had been too thorough, too cold, too right.

But there was one thing he had missed. One variable.

Vane, and she was an unlicensed assistant. Unlicensed participants were not subject to the same compliance algorithms as certified technicians. They were variables. They were chaos.

If Vane acted, the algorithm would break.

"Dale," Vane said, her voice steady now. "I'm not writing anymore."

She dropped the pen.

It hit the asphalt with a clatter that sounded like a gunshot.

The white light stuttered. The beam faltered.

The system could not process an unlicensed participant's refusal to document.

It created a logic loop. A null pointer exception in the fabric of reality.

Dale felt the hive mind recoil. The Authority's attention shifted from his compliance to the glitch. The purge beam dissipated into harmless sparks.

The breach remained open. The Shard-Worms resumed their slithering, confused but alive.

Dale slumped against the sealant barrier, the chitin receding from his face.

He was exhausted. The cost of the glitch had been immense.

He could feel his memories blurring, the edges of his identity fraying.

He didn't remember his mother's face. He didn't remember the color of his first car. He only remembered the protocol.

Vane picked up the pen. Her hand was shaking.

"We're not done," she said.

"No," Dale agreed. He looked at the breach. The violet light was pulsing faster now, rhythmic, like a heart.

"We're not fighting the breach," Dale said. "We're filing a complaint."

Vane looked at him, confused.

"What?"

"Every extermination job ends with a report," Dale said. He pulled a fresh sheet of paper from his clipboard, despite the fact that he had no more paper left.

The paper appeared, manifested from his sheer will to document.

"We need to submit a formal grievance to the Rift.

We need to prove that this infestation is a violation of the Interdimensional Zoning Ordinance.

"

"That's insane," Vane said.

"It's procedural," Dale corrected. "And it's the only way to get the Authority to give us the Class-X tools we need to close this hole."

Vane looked at the approaching horde of glass creatures. She looked at the glitching sky. She looked at Dale, whose eyes were now entirely black, with no whites, no irises, just voids where a soul used to be.

She sat down on the asphalt. She opened her notebook to a fresh page.

"Okay," she said. "What's the code?"

Dale stood up. The hive mind screamed in his head, a chorus of millions demanding blood. He pushed it down, packing it into a neat, labeled box in the back of his mind.

"Code K-99," he said. "Unauthorized dimensional expansion. Subsection C: Failure to obtain proper permits for reality alteration."

He pointed to the center of the breach, where the violet light was thickest.

"Start with the date," Dale said. "And list the submitter as 'Dale Kowalski, Class II, acting under duress.'"

Vane wrote.

The Shard-Worms lunged.

Dale didn't move. He didn't spray. He didn't fight. He stood still, holding his clipboard like a shield, and waited for the Authority to read the violation.

Because if they didn't read it, they would have to kill him.

And if they had to kill him, they would have to admit that the system was broken.

The first Shard-Worm hit his chest. It passed through him, leaving a trail of ice.

Dale didn't flinch.

He just kept looking at the page, waiting for the ink to dry, waiting for the stamp of approval that would either save his life or erase him from existence.

"Write faster," he whispered. "They're reading over your shoulder."

Vane's pen scratched furiously. The sound was the only thing keeping the world from ending.

And then, from the depths of the violet light, a voice answered. It was not a monster's growl. It was the crisp, dry tone of a government auditor.

*QUERY: WHY ARE YOU FILED UNDER CODE K-99?* *NOTE: CODE K-99 IS FOR ADMINISTRATIVE ERRORS, NOT EXISTENTIAL THREATS.* *RECOMMENDATION: RESUBMIT UNDER CLASS I. FAILURE TO DO SO WILL RESULT IN IMMEDIATE TERMINATION OF SUBJECT.*

Dale smiled. It was a terrifying expression, all teeth and shadow.

He looked at Vane.

"Change it," he said. "Change it to Code X-01."

Vane looked up, terrified.

"I don't know what that is."

"It's the code for 'The Authority is Wrong,'" Dale said. "And I'm about to prove it."

He took a step toward the breach. The hive mind surged forward, no longer a burden, but a weapon. He had found the loophole.

The Authority didn't care about heroes. It cared about rules.

And he had just invented a new one.

"Write it down," Dale commanded. "Every word."

The ground beneath him gave way.
