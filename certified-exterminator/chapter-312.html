Chapter 312: Command the Shadows

# Chapter 312

The click was louder than the gunshot.

Dale didn't flinch. He didn't blink. The muzzle flash from the woman's sidearm illuminated the dust motes dancing in the stagnant air of the containment chamber, but the bullet had already passed through the space where his throat used to be. It struck the reinforced glass of the observation booth behind him, spiderwebbing the surface with white fractures.

He looked down at his own hands. They were trembling, not from fear, but from the vibration of the chittering that now filled his skull.

The *Symbiotic Insight* wasn't a gift, which was a frequency. And right now, the frequency was screaming.

The woman with the sidearm, a tactical officer Dale hadn't seen before, lowered her weapon.

Her face was a mask of professional confusion. She checked her earpiece, then looked at Dale.

She didn't shout anymore, and she tilted her head, listening.

Dale tried to form the word *Stop*. His tongue hit the roof of his mouth, but the sound that emerged was a wet, resonant *thrum*. It bounced off the concrete walls, creating a standing wave that made the officer's eyes water.

*Click-clack.*

The officer stepped back, her hand hovering over her radio.

"Command, this is Unit Seven.

The subject is emitting sonic pulses.

It's not English.

It's... it's not human.

"

Dale grabbed his clipboard. The paper was blank. The symbols he had written moments ago had vanished, consumed by the biological rewrite of his vocal cords.

But the pen was still warm. He pressed the nib against the cardboard backing, trying to write.

His hand spasmed.

The pen skidded across the surface, carving a jagged line that looked less like writing and more like a claw mark.

He couldn't speak. He couldn't write. But he could *see*.

The air in the room shimmered. Not with heat, but with intent. To Dale's newly altered perception, the darkness in the corner of the room wasn't empty. It was teeming with threads of gold light, thick and sticky, connecting the shadows to the concrete floor.

*Host Integration,* the System had warned.

The threads pulsed.

Dale felt the rhythm in his own heart. It was a hive heartbeat.

Slow.

Heavy. Patient.

The officer raised her rifle again.

"Step away from the glass, Kowalski. Identify yourself."

Dale ignored her. He turned toward the corner where the shadows pooled. A new notification flashed in his peripheral vision, overlaid on his retina in a font that looked like dripping ink.

**WARNING: VOCAL CENTER DEGRADATION AT 15%.** **CURRENT STATUS: PHASE 1 (PRE-CHITIN).** **NEXT PHASE IN 00:58:30.**

Fifty-eight minutes. That was how long he had before his larynx fused into a carapace. Before he lost the ability to speak to a human being. Before the Authority classified him as *Vermin* than *Technician*.

He needed to file the report. He needed to document the infestation.

If he didn't, the Authority would default to termination.

Protocol 9-Alpha: Unlicensed Biomechanical Hybrid.

Immediate purge.

Dale moved.

His legs felt heavy, the joints stiffening as if greased with oil. He walked toward the center of the room, his gait uneven. The officer tracked him, her finger white-knuckled on the trigger.

"Don't make me do this," she said. Her voice was tight. "I have orders."

Dale raised his clipboard. He held it out like a shield.

The officer hesitated. It was a reflex. Even in a combat zone, the sight of a clipboard triggered a bureaucratic pause. Dale exploited it.

He slammed the clipboard onto the metal table in the center of the room. The sound was sharp, final.

He picked up the pen again. He couldn't write English. He couldn't write numbers.

But he could draw. He drew the shape of the thing he saw in the corner.

A circle.

Eight legs. A segmented abdomen. The specific curvature of the mandibles.

The System recognized the drawing instantly.

**FIELD GUIDE ENTRY UPDATED: SPECIES UNKNOWN.** **STATUS: ID CONFIRMED VIA SYMBIOTIC RESONANCE.** **NEW DATA POINT: WEAKNESS EXPOSED.**

The gold threads in the corner snapped taut. The shadows recoiled.

A chittering sound erupted from the darkness.

It wasn't loud, but it was everywhere. It vibrated in Dale's teeth.

From the shadows, a creature emerged, which was no larger than a house cat, but it moved with the fluidity of mercury. Its exoskeleton was matte black, absorbing the light from the overhead LEDs. Its eyes were not eyes, but clusters of bioluminescent nodes.

The officer shouted something. Dale didn't hear the words.

He heard the *intent* behind them.

Threat.

Containment.

The creature lunged.

Dale didn't dodge. He didn't have the flexibility anymore. His body was becoming rigid, calcifying. He let the creature hit him.

It slammed into his chest, knocking the wind out of him. But it didn't bite. It pressed against his sternum, its legs gripping his shirt. It was trying to interface.

Dale gasped. The air tasted like copper and ozone.

Inside his mind, the creature's consciousness brushed against his own. It wasn't a monster. It was a worker.

A drone. And it was confused.

It sensed the host—the human shell—but it also sensed the *other*.

The symbiote. It didn't know whether to attack or submit.

Dale grabbed the creature's thorax. His fingers dug into the chitin. It was warm. Purring.

He looked at the officer.

Her eyes were wide. She wasn't looking at the creature.

She was looking at Dale.

At the black veins spreading up his neck. At the way his pupils had slit vertically.

"Command," she whispered. "I think he's gone."

Dale shook his head. He wanted to scream *No*.

He wanted to shout *I am still here*.

But his vocal cords had locked.

The muscles in his throat spasmed, producing only a low, guttural hum.

The officer raised her rifle.

Dale had seconds. The creature in his hands was struggling, its legs kicking. If he killed it now, he would lose the only source of *Symbiotic Insight*.

He would be blind to the next wave. He would be deaf to the hive.

But if he let it go, it would flee into the walls.

And the Authority would never find it. The infestation would remain active. His Thoroughness Rating would plummet.

His license would be revoked.

There was only one option.

Dale looked at the officer. He pointed at her rifle. Then he pointed at the creature. Then he pointed at his own chest.

*Shoot it,* his eyes said. *Shoot me.*

The officer frowned. She lowered the rifle slightly.

"What are you doing?"

Dale couldn't explain. He couldn't use the *Isolate* protocol because the creature was already attached to him. He couldn't use *Eliminate* because the creature was part of the host.

He had to use the loophole. The one the System hadn't explicitly banned, but had never encouraged.

*Documentation.*

He grabbed the pen again. With his free hand, he grabbed the creature's leg. He held it steady against the clipboard.

He didn't try to write a description. He tried to write the *sound*.

He opened his mouth. He forced air through his degrading vocal cords. He aimed not for words, but for resonance. He pushed the *hum* from his chest into the pen, into the paper.

The pen vibrated. The ink splattered.

But the System saw it.

**ACTION LOGGED: SONIC DOCUMENTATION.** **METHOD: DIRECT NEURAL TRANSCRIPTION.** **CLASSIFICATION: CLASS II PROTOCOL (ADAPTED).** **PENALTY: VOCAL DEGRADATION ACCELERATED. TIME TO PHASE 2: 00:15:00.**

The gold threads in the room lit up like fiber optics. The creature in Dale's grip went still.

It wasn't dead, which was *recorded*. It was pinned in the System's database, identified by its own song.

**INFESTATION ISOLATED.** **POINT TOTAL: +450.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING ADJUSTED: 9.4.**

The creature's bioluminescent nodes dimmed. It slumped against Dale's chest, heavy and limp.

Not dead, but dormant, and the System had accepted the documentation.

The threat was neutralized by law, not by force.

The officer lowered her rifle. She looked at the clipboard. The page was covered in jagged, ink-blot patterns. To her, it looked like a seizure victim's scribbles.

But Dale saw the metadata. He saw the timestamp. He saw the approval stamp from the Authority, glowing faintly in the corner of his vision.

He had done it. He had exterminated the threat without killing the host. He had saved his license.

He had also signed his own death warrant.

Fifteen minutes. That was all he had left before his tongue dissolved into chitin. Before he could no longer form the words *I am human*. Before the Authority's automated systems flagged him as non-compliant.

Dale dropped the creature. It slid off his chest and hit the floor with a soft *thump*. It didn't move.

It didn't need to. It was logged.

He turned to the officer, and he needed to leave. He needed to get to a quiet place. A place where he could write, even if he couldn't speak.

He needed to find the next nest.

He walked toward the door. His movements were jerky now. His knees locked. The stiffness in his joints was spreading to his spine.

The officer blocked his path.

"Stay there. Medical team is en route."

Dale stopped. He looked at her.

He raised his hands, and they were turning gray. The skin on his knuckles was hardening, taking on a texture like bark.

He pointed at the door. Then he pointed at his watch.

*Time,* he signaled. *Running out.*

The officer shook her head.

"I can't let you leave. You're contaminated."

Dale felt a surge of panic. Not for himself.

For the job.

For the backlog.

If he was detained, the next Rift event would go untreated. The ants would come.

The beetles. The broodmothers.

He needed a distraction. He needed to break the protocol.

He looked at the observation glass. The bullet had cracked it.

He walked to the glass. He placed his hand against it. The grayness of his skin pressed against the pane. He could feel the structural integrity failing.

*Violation,* he thought. *Code 4-Bravo: Structural Compromise.*

He pushed.

The glass didn't shatter. It bowed.

Dale leaned his full weight against it, his new, hardened bones taking the strain.

He was using his own body as a tool. A lever.

The frame groaned. The metal warps.

The officer shouted, "Back off!"

Dale didn't back off. He pushed harder. He used the *Contamination* penalty against the environment. The System flagged his action, but it didn't stop him.

*Cross-contamination penalty applied,* a red text flashed. *40% debuff to physical strength.*

He didn't care. He had 60% of his strength left. That was enough.

The glass exploded outward.

Shards rained down onto the corridor beyond.

Dale stumbled through, cutting his arms on the jagged edges. The blood that welled up was dark.

Too dark.

It didn't drip. It oozed, thick and viscous, like resin.

He was out of the containment chamber. He was in the hallway.

The officer screamed something into her radio. Alarms blared. Red lights flashed along the walls.

Dale ran. Or tried to.

His legs were buckling, and the grayness had reached his thighs.

He could feel the hive-mind of the dormant creature in the room, a faint whisper in the back of his brain. It wasn't gone.

It was waiting.

He needed to find a terminal. A public computer. A place where he could type.

He couldn't speak. He couldn't write with a pen. But he could type.

He spotted a security kiosk halfway down the hall. It was a standard municipal interface. Touchscreen. Keyboard.

He reached it. His fingers were stiff, clumsy. He hammered the keys.

*HELP,* he typed. *LICENSE HOLDER. DALE KOWALSKI. CLASS II.*

The screen flashed. *ACCESS DENIED.*

*UNAUTHORIZED BIOLOGICAL SIGNATURE DETECTED.*

Dale stared at the screen. His own body had been flagged. The System knew he was changing. It was protecting itself from him.

He tried again.

*REPORT. INFESTATION. SECTOR 4.*

*ACCESS DENIED.*

He slammed his fist against the kiosk. The screen cracked.

*ACCESS DENIED.*

He couldn't log in. He couldn't file the report. He was locked out of his own career.

Then, a new notification appeared. Not in his vision. On the screen.

**SYSTEM OVERRIDE ACCEPTED.** **SOURCE: ADMINISTRATIVE ACCESS.** **USER: DIRECTOR VANCE.** **MESSAGE: DO NOT ENGAGE. CONTAINMENT PROTOCOL GAMMA INITIATED.**

Director Vance. The head of the Local Authority. The man who had signed Dale's Level II license three years ago. The man who had laughed when Dale asked for better hazard pay.

Dale looked at the screen.

*Why?* he typed, his fingers slipping on the plastic keys, and the response was instant.

**YOU ARE NOT A LICENSE HOLDER ANYMORE.** **YOU ARE A HOST.** **TERMINATION ORDER: IMMEDIATE.**

The hallway doors at the far end slammed shut. The blast shields came down. Dale was trapped.

He turned around. The officer was still in the containment chamber, but the door was sealed. She couldn't reach him.

But something else could.

From the vents above, a hiss of gas filled the air. Not sleep gas. Not knockout gas.

*Defoliant.*

A Class 4 chemical agent. Used for rapid biomass breakdown.

Dale held his breath. He looked at his hands.

The grayness had reached his elbows, and he could feel his bones reshaping. The pain was gone, replaced by a numbness that was worse.

He looked at the kiosk. He looked at the vent.

He had one move left.

He grabbed the kiosk. He ripped the cord from the wall. Sparks flew. He dragged the heavy unit across the floor, toward the nearest window.

The gas was filling the room. Thick, white clouds rolled over the floor.

Dale climbed onto the kiosk. He smashed the window with his head. The glass shattered.

He climbed out, and he was on the fire escape. The night air hit him. Cold. Clean.

He looked down. The street below was empty. Silent.

Then, he heard it.

Not from the building. From the ground.

A clicking. Thousands of clicks. Rising from the sewers, from the cracks in the pavement, from the walls of the houses across the street.

The Authority hadn't just sent gas. They had opened the gates.

The Rift wasn't leaking anymore. It was flooding.

Dale gripped the railing. His body was turning into armor. His mind was turning into a hive.

He looked at the street. The shadows were moving.

He opened his mouth. He tried to scream.

What came out was a command.

*Join,* he clicked.

And the shadows answered.
