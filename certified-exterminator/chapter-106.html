Chapter 106: New Entry Foreman

# Chapter 106

The grate was hot.

Not warm. Hot.

Enough to blister the soles of Dale's boots even through the thick rubber tread.

He stepped back, the metal frame of the access hatch groaning under the shift of weight. Steam hissed from the gap, smelling of sulfur and old copper.

"Don't touch it," Dale said.

His voice was flat, procedural. He pointed the flashlight at the seam where the steam escaped.

"That's not water.

That's coolant. Or blood. It's viscous."

Behind him, Deputy Miller lowered his rifle. The barrel wavered. "It's a monster, Kowalski. Look at it."

The thing inside the plant wasn't a monster. It was a shape.

A geometry of wet stone and pulsing muscle, wedged into the primary filtration chamber.

It had no eyes.

It had no mouth. It had hands.

Dozens of them, flat and gray, pressing against the concrete walls, holding the structure together.

"No," Dale said, clicking his pen. The click echoed in the humid air.

"It's a load-bearing entity.

You shoot it, the ceiling comes down. We die. The town's water supply gets contaminated with Class VI biomass.

You want a news cycle on that?"

Miller swallowed. "The Authority said 'Do not document.'"

"The Authority said the *job* is to engage," Dale corrected, not looking up. "The instruction is a constraint, not a permission slip. I don't know what that means yet. But I know what happens if you fire that 5.56 round into a pressurized filtration unit."

A second figure stepped out of the shadows. Not a cop. A man in a crisp, charcoal suit, holding a tablet that glowed with a faint, blue light. He looked like he belonged in a boardroom, not a municipal sewage plant at 2:00 AM.

"Dale Kowalski," the man said. "Certification Level VI. Thoroughness Rating 9.4. You're slowing down the timeline."

Dale kept writing. *Hazard: Structural Instability. Cause: Entity integration. Mitigation: Containment only.*

"You're not on the roster," Dale said.

"I'm the Inspector General. My name is Vance. And you are violating Directive 9."

Vance stepped closer. The air around him shimmered, a subtle distortion of heat and light.

Dale didn't flinch.

He'd seen the shimmer before. Usually, it meant the person was holding a high-level protocol item, or they were out of sync with local reality.

"Directive 9 prohibits active recording of Class VI entities," Vance said. "Your Field Guide is already populated.

You know what this is.

You don't need to write it down.

Writing it down creates a tether. It anchors the entity to human perception.

If you document it, you make it real. If you make it real, it can't be unmade."

Dale stopped writing. He looked at Vance. Then he looked at the tablet. The screen was blank.

"Is that why the page was blank earlier?" Dale asked.

"It's why the page will always be blank for this job," Vance said. "We need it gone.

Not killed.

Gone. Disassociated from the physical plane. Your spray, your sealant, your little clipboard—those are anchors.

They define the problem.

We need to stop defining it."

Miller looked between the two men. "So we just… walk away?"

"No," Dale said. "We work."

Dale turned back to the hatch.

He pulled a canister from his belt. Standard industrial degreaser, modified with a localized dampening field. He didn't spray the creature.

He sprayed the seam of the hatch.

The foam hissed. It turned yellow, then gray, then dissolved into a thick, oily sludge.

"Vance," Dale said. "Your tablet. Is it recording?"

"It's monitoring," Vance said. "Why?"

"Because if I don't write it down, I can't prove I did it.

If I can't prove I did it, the Authority won't pay me.

And if the Authority doesn't pay me, I don't get the next certification. If I don't get the next certification, I can't handle Class VII.

And if I can't handle Class VII, the next thing that crawls out of the Rift eats the county."

Vance's jaw tightened. "That's petty, Kowalski."

"That's the system," Dale said. "You're trying to cheat the economy of effort.

You want the result without the labor.

I don't work like that. I charge by the hour and the hazard class."

The creature shifted. A low groan vibrated through the concrete floor. Miller took a step back, his rifle raising again.

"Stop moving it," Dale snapped. "If it moves, the sealant cracks. If the sealant cracks, the pressure release blows the roof off. Stand still."

Miller froze.

Vance looked at Dale with something akin to disgust. "You're a technician, not a hero. You're worried about paperwork while the world ends."

"The world ends every Tuesday," Dale said. "I fix it on Wednesday. Today is Tuesday. I'm fixing it."

He pulled a second tool from his pack.

Not a weapon. A sensor.

A simple, handheld device used to detect methane leaks.

He held it near the steam. The needle jumped.

"Methane," Dale said. "And something else.

Ionizing radiation.

Low level, but steady.

That's the signature of the Rift bleed. The entity is feeding on the radiation, not the water."

"Then kill the source," Vance said. "Cut the power to the plant."

"No," Dale said. "The plant is grounded. Cutting the power grounds the entity to the earth.

It'll spread. Like a root system."

Dale looked at the steam again. He tapped his pen against his thigh. *Tap. Tap. Tap.*

He was thinking.

The instruction was *Do not document*. But the penalty for cross-contamination was severe. If he used a protocol meant for a gas leak on a sentient entity, he'd debuff himself.

And if he used a protocol meant for a sentient entity on a gas leak, he'd contaminate his guide.

There was a third option. One that didn't exist in the manual.

"Miller," Dale said. "Do you have a piece of paper? Not your notebook. Just a scrap."

Miller looked confused. "Uh. Receipt? From the donut shop?"

"Give it to me."

Miller fumbled in his pocket and handed over a crumpled napkin. It smelled of sugar and coffee.

Dale unfolded it. He didn't write.

He drew.

A circle. A line. A simple schematic of the filtration chamber.

Vance stepped forward. "What are you doing?"

"Drafting," Dale said.

"You're documenting," Vance said. His voice rose. "I told you—"

"I'm not writing words," Dale said. "I'm drawing shapes.

Words anchor meaning.

Shapes anchor form.

If the Authority's logic is that documentation creates a tether, then I'm creating a blueprint. A plan.

Not a record."

Vance hesitated. The blue light on his tablet flickered.

"Is that… allowed?" Miller asked.

"The rulebook says *document*," Dale said. "It doesn't define the medium.

And it doesn't define the intent.

I'm not recording the event for history. I'm recording the geometry for remediation. It's engineering, not journalism."

Vance stared at the napkin.

Then he looked at the creature. The groaning had stopped.

The hands were still pressing against the walls, but the rhythm was different.

Slower.

"Fine," Vance said. "Do it. But if it comes back, it's on your head."

"It's on my head anyway," Dale said. "I'm the guy with the clipboard."

Dale turned to the hatch. He didn't spray.

He didn't seal.

He reached into his pack and pulled out a small, black box.

A frequency jammer, usually used for interfering with insect pheromone trails.

He set it on the metal frame.

"Miller, stand back. Ten feet. Now."

Miller moved.

Vance didn't move. He watched.

Dale pressed a button on the box. A high-pitched whine filled the air. It wasn't loud, but it vibrated in the teeth. The steam from the hatch changed direction, swirling into a tight vortex.

The creature in the chamber reacted. The hands twitched.

"Protocol: Sonic Disorientation," Dale murmured. "Target: Nervous system resonance. Not physical harm."

He adjusted the dial on the box. The pitch rose.

Vance's face went pale. "That's a Class IV protocol. You can't use that on a Class VI.

It's a mismatch. You'll contaminate your guide."

"It's not a protocol for the creature," Dale said.

"It's a protocol for the *air*. I'm changing the medium the sound travels through. The creature isn't hearing it.

The concrete is."

The concrete groaned. A crack appeared in the wall above the hatch. Not a structural failure. A release.

The steam stopped.

The vortex collapsed.

The hands on the wall relaxed. They didn't fall.

They simply… stopped pressing.

The tension in the room vanished. The ionizing radiation reading on Dale's sensor dropped to zero.

The entity didn't die. It didn't disappear. It went dormant.

Dale clicked the box off.

Silence. Heavy, dripping silence.

Vance looked at the tablet. The blue light was steady.

"Did you kill it?" Miller asked.

"No," Dale said. He picked up the napkin. He folded it neatly. "I put it to sleep."

He looked at Vance. "Where did you get this job? The Authority doesn't send Inspectors to Class VI sites. They send Exterminators."

Vance straightened his suit jacket. "I'm not here for the job, Kowalski. I'm here for the audit."

"The audit?"

"You've been leveling up too fast," Vance said. "Your Thoroughness Rating is unnatural.

You're balancing speed and quality perfectly.

That's not human.

That's systemic."

Dale felt a cold prickle at the base of his neck. He didn't touch it. He kept his hand on the napkin.

"I'm thorough," Dale said.

"You're efficient," Vance said. "And efficiency is suspicious. The Authority rewards thoroughness, but it punishes curiosity.

You've been asking questions about the Rift. You've been looking at the patterns.

You're starting to see the grid."

"I see a kitchen infestation," Dale said. "I see a water leak. I see a violation code."

"Do you?" Vance stepped closer. "Because the Foreman isn't a creature, Dale. It's a function.

And the Authority isn't a bureaucracy.

It's a containment field. And you're breaching it."

Dale looked at the hatch.

The sleep was temporary. He knew that. The frequency jammer only worked for ten minutes.

After that, the resonance would return, and the creature would wake up angrier.

He had to write it down. He had to file the report. But if he wrote it down, Vance might be right. The tether might snap back.

Unless.

Dale looked at the blank page of his clipboard. He looked at the napkin in his hand.

He had a new loophole.

He pulled out his pen.

"I'm going to write the report," Dale said.

Vance's eyes narrowed. "No."

"I'm going to write the report," Dale repeated. "But I'm not going to write it in the Field Guide. I'm going to write it in the *Incident Log*."

The Incident Log was a separate database.

It was used for non-combat accidents.

Car accidents. Slip and falls.

Structural failures.

If he filed this as a structural failure, not a monster kill, the System wouldn't register it as an extermination. No points. No guide entry. No tether.

But it would still count as a completed job for the *municipal* license.

It was a technicality. A gray-area exploit.

Vance stared at him. "You're trying to trick the System."

"I'm trying to pay my taxes," Dale said.

He wrote.

*Incident: 404-B. Cause: Seismic shift in filtration infrastructure. Action: Temporary vibration dampening. Status: Resolved pending long-term reinforcement.*

He signed it.

The notification appeared.

*Job Complete.* *Points Awarded: 0.* *Certification Progress: 62% (Unchanged).* *Field Guide Update: Denied.*

But then, a second notification. Smaller. In a different font.

*Municipal Code Violation: Clear.* *License Renewal: Approved.* *Authority Note: Clever. But noted.*

Dale looked up. Vance was smiling. It wasn't a nice smile.

"You just flagged yourself," Vance said. "The Authority doesn't like clever. It likes predictable."

"I'm predictable," Dale said. "I'm a pest control guy. I fix problems. If fixing them means lying about what they are, I'll lie."

Vance walked away, his heels clicking on the wet concrete. "Enjoy your downtime, Kowalski. The Foreman doesn't sleep for long. And neither do we."

He disappeared into the shadows of the plant.

Miller looked at Dale. "Did we win?"

"We filed paperwork," Dale said.

"Is that winning?"

Dale looked at the hatch. The steam was starting to return. Slowly.

"No," Dale said. "It's buying time."

He checked his watch. Eight minutes left on the jammer.

He pulled out a new can of sealant. Heavy-duty. Industrial strength.

Miller raised his rifle. "What are you doing?"

"Sealing the gap," Dale said. "Before it wakes up."

"You're going to fight it?"

"No," Dale said. "I'm going to lock it in."

The ground rumbled. A deep, resonant thrum that vibrated in Dale's chest. The hands inside the chamber pressed harder.

Dale sprayed. The foam hit the crack. It hardened instantly, turning into a rock-like barrier.

"Miller," Dale said. "Get the truck. We're moving the perimeter tape."

"Why?"

"Because the next thing isn't in the plant," Dale said. He pointed to the ceiling.

The crack in the concrete was spreading.

Not down. Up.

Into the town's water mains.

"Where are you going?" Miller shouted over the rising rumble.

Dale didn't answer. He was looking at his Field Guide. The entry for The Architect was still there. *Status: Exterminated.*

But below it, a new line was forming. Faint. Glitchy.

*New Entry: The Foreman.* *Status: Active.* *Weakness: None.* *Note: Do not engage.*

Dale closed the guide.

He picked up his clipboard.

He started running.
