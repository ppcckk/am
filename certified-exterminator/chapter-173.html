Chapter 173: Bureaucracy of the Void

# Chapter 173

The junior agent's pen hovered over the asphalt.

A drop of ink fell. It hit the pavement, blooming into a black star against the gray concrete.

The Agent in the trench coat did not blink, and he watched the ink spread with the detached curiosity of a scientist observing a mold culture. Behind him, the other agents formed a semicircle, their clipboards held tight against their chests like shields. They were not looking at the void.

They were looking at the man dangling over it.

Dale Kowalski hung by his fingertips.

His fingernails were white. Then pink. Then bleeding.

The blackness below churned, a slow, viscous movement that defied the physics of soil and rock. It smelled of ozone and old paper. It smelled of things that had been filed away and forgotten.

"Recommendation: Immediate Purge," the Agent repeated. His voice was dry, devoid of urgency. "Technician Kowalski has failed to maintain structural integrity of his own containment zone. This constitutes a Class 4 Safety Violation."

One of the junior agents stepped forward. He raised his pen. The tip glowed with a faint, amber light. A standard cancellation protocol.

"Wait," Dale said.

His voice was thin, scraped raw by the cold rising from the abyss.

The Agent tilted his head.

"You are requesting a stay of execution?"

"I am requesting a pause in processing," Dale corrected. "Check your Field Guide. Page 412, subsection C, paragraph three. Regarding Unstable Substrate."

The junior agent hesitated. His pen lowered an inch.

The lead Agent frowned. The infinite pity in his eyes shifted, just slightly, to irritation.

"You cannot cite a guideline that does not exist in your current certification tier."

"It exists," Dale said. He gritted his teeth as a tendon in his forearm screamed.

"It's in the Master Technician's Appendix.

I checked it out from the Regional Library before the breach. I have the digital receipt.

"

Silence.

The streetlights flickered. The wind died.

The Agent looked at the void. Then he looked at Dale.

"Prove it," the Agent said.

Dale didn't answer with words. He answered with a protocol.

He couldn't see the guide. He didn't need to. He had memorized the cross-referenced index for Unstable Substrates during his downtime in Chapter 140, while waiting for the Authority to process his renewal fees.

*Unstable Substrates are not voids,* the manual read. *They are compressed narratives. To purge them is to rewrite history without editing the source material. This causes recursive debt.*

If the agents used a Purge Protocol here, they wouldn't just kill Dale. They would erase the event that created the sinkhole. But the sinkhole was tied to the Agent's own presence. If the event was erased, the Agent's authority to be there was erased.

It was a paradox loop.

The Agent knew this. The junior agents did not.

"Standard Purge," Dale said, his voice gaining strength as the logic locked into place. "If you use a Level 3 Cancellation on a substrate linked to a Level 7 Authority figure, the feedback loop will invalidate your current license. You will become unlicensed. And unlicensed entities are subject to immediate termination by the Authority."

The junior agent lowered his pen completely.

The lead Agent's smile vanished.

For the first time since the Rift opened, the Agent looked like a bureaucrat who had realized he was buried in paperwork he couldn't fix.

"You are bluffing," the Agent said.

"Check the Thoroughness Rating on my last job," Dale said. "9.8. I don't miss loopholes. I document them."

The Agent stared at him. The blackness below pulsed. A tentacle of shadow rose, not to grab Dale, but to inspect the clipboard in the Agent's hand. It licked the paper.

The Agent flinched.

"Cease Purge," the Agent said.

The glow on the junior agents' pens faded.

Dale did not celebrate. He did not smile.

He focused on the pain in his fingers, and the cold was still climbing.

The bone-clipboard below was still there, the *Terminated* stamp still visible.

But the ink on the paper below hadn't dried.

That was the clue.

If he was dead, the paperwork would be complete. The stamp would be permanent. It wasn't. It was wet.

Someone was still writing.

"Pull me up," Dale said.

The Agent did not move. He was staring at his own clipboard. The lines on the paper were shifting. Rewriting themselves.

"Who is writing?" the Agent whispered.

"The one who filed the complaint," Dale said. "Check the timestamp on the violation. It wasn't filed by the Authority. It was filed by a competitor."

The Agent looked down at Dale. His face was pale.

"Get him out," the Agent snapped.

Two junior agents rushed forward. They grabbed Dale's arms. They hauled him up with clumsy, frantic strength.

Dale collapsed onto the pavement, gasping. The cold receded, leaving his joints aching and his hands numb.

He rolled over, clutching his chest. He looked at the sinkhole. The blackness was receding, folding in on itself like a closing eye. The bone clipboard remained, floating in the air for a second before dissolving into ash.

Dale sat up. He wiped the blood from his fingers onto his pants.

"Status?" the Agent asked. He was already writing. His pen moved fast, scratching furious sounds against the paper.

Dale looked at his HUD.

**System Alert: Anomaly Neutralized.** **Cause: Regulatory Paradox.** **Treatment Points Awarded: 0.** **Reason: Incomplete Protocol. Documentation Missing.**

Dale closed his eyes.

Zero points. He had saved his life, but he hadn't finished the job. The Authority didn't pay for survival.

It paid for completion.

"Where is the other Agent?" Dale asked.

The lead Agent didn't look up.

"Gone. The violation was filed under a proxy ID. Untraceable."

"It wasn't untraceable," Dale said. He stood up. His legs shook, but he locked his knees. "Check the ink."

The Agent paused.

"What?"

"The ink," Dale said. He pointed to the drop that had fallen earlier.

"Standard Authority ink is cyan-based.

It evaporates in three seconds. That drop is spreading. It's oil-based.

"

The Agent looked at the black star on the pavement. It was still wet. It was still spreading.

"Oil-based," the Agent murmured. "That's a Class I artifact. Used in the Pre-Rift era for legal contracts."

"Someone filed a real-world legal complaint," Dale said. "Against the Rift.

Against us.

They're trying to shut down the Authority's operation in this district through bureaucratic obstruction.

The sinkhole wasn't an attack. It was a subpoena.

"

The Agent's eyes widened, which was new. The Authority handled monsters. It handled infestations. It did not handle lawyers.

Unless the lawyers were monsters.

Dale felt a chill that had nothing to do with the void. He reached for his clipboard. It was gone.

Lost in the fall. He grabbed the junior agent's clipboard instead.

The surface was warm.

"Name of the filer," Dale said.

"It's redacted," the Agent said. "But the proxy ID matches a local firm."

"Which one?"

The Agent hesitated.

"Kowalski, Kowalski, and Sons."

Dale's head snapped up.

"My father?"

"No," the Agent said. "Your brother. Greg."

Dale stared. Greg. The one who had left the family business to go into municipal zoning law. The one who thought pest control was beneath them.

"Why would Greg file a complaint against the Authority?"

"He's not filing against the Authority," Dale said. The realization hit him like a physical blow. "He's filing against *me*.

He's trying to prove I'm operating out of jurisdiction.

If the Authority is deemed illegitimate in this district, my certification is void. My licenses are void.

I become an unlicensed exterminator.

And unlicensed exterminators...

"

"...are exterminated," the Agent finished.

Dale looked at his hands. The blood was drying.

He felt a surge of rage, hot and sharp, but he swallowed it.

He needed to think, and he needed to work the problem.

This wasn't a monster fight. It was a paperwork war.

And Dale Kowalski was a professional.

"Reset the scene," Dale said.

The Agent looked at him.

"Excuse me?"

"Reset the scene," Dale repeated. "The sinkhole is a violation of municipal code 14-B: Unpermitted Excavation. If we document it as a natural disaster, it's a liability issue.

If we document it as a Rift event, it's an Authority issue. But if we document it as a *structural failure caused by prior infestation*, then it's a pest control issue.

"

The Agent frowned.

"That's a stretch."

"It's protocol," Dale said.

"Check my Field Guide. Entry 44: *Subterranean Structural Parasites*. They don't just eat wood.

They eat foundations.

If we claim the sinkhole was caused by a pre-existing infestation that I failed to document, I'm negligent. That's bad.

But if we claim the infestation was *caused* by the Agent's Purge Protocol, then the Agent is liable.

"

Dale pointed at the junior agents.

"Your pens are charged with Purge energy. You tried to use it.

If I can prove that the attempt triggered the structural collapse, the fault lies with the Authority, not the technician. My negligence is excused by the hazardous conditions imposed by the employer.

"

The Agent stared at Dale. The junior agents stared at Dale.

The silence stretched.

"You want to falsify the report," the Agent said.

"No," Dale said.

"I want to *interpret* the report.

I am filing an Objection to Cancellation. I am citing the hazard of the Purge Protocol itself.

I am requesting a re-evaluation of the Thoroughness Rating based on extenuating circumstances.

"

The Agent looked at his own clipboard. The lines were still moving. The wet ink was still spreading.

If he didn't agree, the complaint from Greg would stand. If he agreed, the Authority kept its jurisdiction, but it admitted fault for the structural damage.

It was a lose-lose for the Authority. But it was a win for Dale.

"If I approve this," the Agent said slowly, "you are admitting you caused the damage."

"I am admitting I failed to prevent it," Dale corrected. "There is a difference.

One is malice, and the other is incompetence. The Authority tolerates incompetence. It does not tolerate malice.

"

The Agent lifted his pen. He hovered it over the paper.

Dale held his breath.

The pen touched the paper.

The ink flowed. Black. Oil-based.

The ground beneath them shook.

A low rumble, deep in the earth. Not a sinkhole opening.

Something moving.

Something large.

The Agent's pen stopped.

"New entry," the Agent said. His voice was flat.

"Subject: Greg Kowalski.

Status: Active Threat.

Classification: Bureaucratic Parasite.

"

Dale blinked. "What?"

"The complaint wasn't filed by Greg," the Agent said. "It was filed by something *wearing* Greg's signature. Look at the ink."

Dale looked down at the wet black star on the pavement, and the oil was moving. It was forming shapes. Letters.

*HELP ME.*

Dale backed away.

"Greg is inside," Dale said. "He's not the lawyer. He's the host."

The Agent's face went blank.

"A Bureaucratic Parasite? That's a Class 9 entity.

It consumes legal structures. It eats contracts.

It feeds on liability.

"

"It's feeding on us," Dale said. "Every time we write a report, we feed it. Every time we document a violation, we give it power. It's using our own system against us."

The junior agents raised their pens again. The amber glow returned.

"Don't," Dale shouted. "If you use Purge, you're filing a cancellation notice. That's food. It will eat your license."

The agents froze.

Dale looked at the ink. It was spreading faster now. It was crawling up the legs of the junior agents' pants.

"How do you kill it?" the Agent asked.

Dale's mind raced. He scanned his mental index of the Field Guide. There was no entry for Bureaucratic Parasites.

He had never encountered one, and he had never even heard of one.

But he knew how to kill lawyers.

Not with spray. Not with traps.

With discovery.

"You don't kill it," Dale said. "You audit it."

He reached into his pocket, and he pulled out a small, folded piece of paper. His personal inspection log. The one he kept off the grid.

The one that proved he had been following protocol since Chapter 1.

"Give me a pen," Dale said.

The Agent handed him one.

Dale knelt on the pavement. He wrote.

He didn't write a report. He wrote a subpoena.

He wrote for the Authority to appear for questioning regarding the origin of the ink. He wrote for the source of the oil-based contract. He wrote for Greg Kowalski to present himself for examination.

He wrote with fast, precise strokes. His hand didn't shake. The fear was there, but it was locked in a box, labeled *Handle with Care*.

The ink on the ground stopped spreading. It shuddered.

The shadow beneath the streetlights writhed. A sound rose from the blackness, not a roar, but a whisper. A million pages turning at once.

The Agent watched. The junior agents watched.

Dale wrote the final line.

*Signed: Dale Kowalski, Certified Exterminator, Class II.*

He lifted the pen.

The ink on the ground vanished, and the sinkhole was gone. The pavement was whole.

The bone clipboard was gone.

The only thing left was the smell of ozone and the silence of the street.

The Agent looked at Dale. He looked at the pen in Dale's hand.

"You served it," the Agent said.

"I served notice," Dale said. "It's bound by its own rules. It can't eat what it can't legally acquire. I just made the acquisition process painful enough to stop."

The Agent nodded slowly, and he picked up his clipboard.

He tore off the page with the wet ink. He held it up.

The paper was blank.

"Thoroughness Rating updated," the Agent said. "9.9."

Dale exhaled. He leaned against a lamppost. His legs felt like water.

"Is Greg safe?"

"We don't know," the Agent said. "But he is no longer an active threat. The parasite has retreated to its nest."

"Where is the nest?"

The Agent smiled. It was a cold smile.

"It's in the courthouse," the Agent said. "And it's growing."

Dale looked up at the sky. The Rift was still open above the city. But now, he saw something else.

A line of black ink, hanging in the air. Like a spider's thread. It led from the Rift, down to the downtown area. To the county records office.

Dale tightened his grip on the pen, and he wasn't just an exterminator anymore. He was a process server.

And he had a date with the biggest pest in the city.

He looked at the Agent.

"I'm filing a Class 9 Request for Assistance," Dale said. "I need backup. And I need a bigger clipboard."

The Agent handed him his own.

"Welcome to the big leagues, Technician," the Agent said.

Dale opened the clipboard. The first page was blank.

But as he looked at it, words appeared.

Not in ink. In blood.

*Next Target: The Clerk.*

Dale closed the board. He looked at the junior agents.

"Let's go," he said. "We have paperwork to do."
