Chapter 311: Becoming the Infestation

# Chapter 311

The scalpel hovered three millimeters above the ulnar artery, trembling in sync with the clicking beneath the skin. It was a wet, chitinous sound, like sandpaper dragged across dry bone. Dale didn't blink. His breath hitched in short, shallow pulls, the only movement in his face besides the rapid dilation of his pupils.

The blade touched down. A bead of blood welled up, dark and viscous.

"Stop."

The voice cut through the sterile hum of the office. Not Vane. Not the Authority.

Dale froze. The scalpel remained pressed against his wrist, the tip sinking just enough to break the epidermis.

He didn't pull back, and he didn't look up. He watched the blood welling around the steel, counting the seconds.

"Step away from the surgical field," the voice said, sharper this time. "That is a Class-4 biohazard violation if you puncture the dermal layer without containment protocols."

Dale withdrew the blade. He placed it on the stainless-steel tray with a soft *clink*.

"Who authorized entry?" Dale asked, his voice flat.

The figure stepped into the light, and she wore the charcoal suit of a Senior Auditor, but the lapel pin was wrong. It wasn't the Authority's eye.

It was a stylized scorpion, its tail curled around a quill.

The mark of the Bureau of Occupational Safety and Compliance, a regulatory body that had existed alongside the Authority for centuries, usually to keep the Authority from burning down the world.

"Officer Mara Kline," she said, flashing a badge that shimmered with holographic text. "BOSAC. I'm here to shut you down, Mr. Kowalski."

Dale wiped the single drop of blood from his wrist with a sterile gauze pad. He didn't wince. The clicking in his arm had paused.

"I have a provisional license," Dale said, not looking at her. He picked up his clipboard. "Review required. Twenty-four hours."

"Your provisional license is for *extermination*," Kline said, circling the desk. Her heels clicked on the linoleum, a rhythm that mirrored the thing under Dale's skin.

"It is not for *surgery*.

It is not for *internal containment*.

You are currently harboring a Level-6 dimensional entity within a Class-1 biological vessel. That is not a job.

That is a public health emergency.

"

"The entity is sealed," Dale said. "In a box. A conceptual box."

Kline laughed. It was a dry, humorless sound.

"There is no such thing as a conceptual box, Dale.

There are only containment fields, chemical flushes, and incineration. You have chosen the fourth option: infection.

"

She pulled a tablet from her jacket. The screen glowed with a red warning banner.

"Section 9, Paragraph 4 of the Inter-Agency Accord," Kline read aloud. "Any technician who introduces a non-native biological agent into their own system without immediate quarantine and decontamination is stripped of all certifications. Permanently. And the agent is destroyed."

"The agent is part of the infestation," Dale said. "If I destroy it, I lose the evidence. The Thoroughness Rating drops to zero. I fail the review."

Kline stopped walking. She leaned against the desk, crossing her arms.

"You're worried about a rating? You're worried about a *score* when there is a parasite eating your nerve endings?"

"Protocol dictates documentation before elimination," Dale said.

"If I cut it out now, I can't prove what it was. I can't prove *why* it was there.

The Authority needs the 'Why'.

The BOSAC cares about the 'How', but the Authority cares about the pattern. If I fail to identify the vector, the next one comes back in six weeks. Just like the ants.

"

"The ants don't click," Kline said. "The ants don't try to pupate in your carpal tunnel."

Dale looked at her. For the first time, he let the fear show. Not in his face, but in the way his left hand twitched, fingers curling into a claw.

"It's not trying to pupate," Dale said quietly. "It's trying to hatch.

The residual energy from the Rift event triggered a metamorphic cycle.

It's dormant until it finds a host.

I am the host."

Kline's expression softened, just for a fraction of a second. Then it hardened again.

"Then you need to be evacuated. We have a containment team en route. They will sedate you, open you up, and remove the organ tissue that's been colonized.

You'll lose the hand, Dale. Maybe the arm.

But you'll live.

"

"I can't afford to lose the hand," Dale said. "I need to write reports. I need to sign off on inspections."

"You need to survive."

"I need to finish the job."

Kline shook her head.

"This isn't a job. This is a mistake. And I'm here to correct it before it becomes a liability for the Bureau."

She reached into her pocket and pulled out a small, silver canister. A chemical flush agent. It was designed to dissolve organic matter at the cellular level. If she sprayed it into Dale's wound, the insect—and the tissue it was embedded in—would liquefy.

"Give me the scalpel," Kline said, holding out her hand.

Dale looked at the canister. He looked at his wrist. The clicking started again, faster this time. *Click-click-click-click.* The translucent outline of the insect shifted, pressing against the inside of his skin.

"If you use that," Dale said, "you contaminate the sample. The Field Guide entry will be flagged as 'Unverified' for twenty-four hours. You said it yourself. If another technician identifies it differently, my license is revoked."

"So you want to gamble with your life for a field guide entry?"

"I want to follow protocol," Dale said. "The Authority rewards thoroughness. You reward safety.

But the Authority is the one that pays the bills.

And right now, the Authority is watching.

"

Kline raised an eyebrow.

"How do you know that?"

"Because the notification appeared in my peripheral vision before you entered the room," Dale said.

He tapped his temple.

"UNLICENSED SURGICAL ATTEMPT DETECTED. WITNESS: BOSAC AUDITOR.

ACTION: HOLD FOR REVIEW.

"

Kline went still. The color drained from her face.

"You... you can't invoke the Witness Clause without a formal request."

"I didn't invoke it," Dale said.

"The System did.

It's a passive observation. If you take action against me while I am under provisional review for a different infestation, you are interfering with an active Authority case.

That's a conflict of interest. That's a violation of the Inter-Agency Accord Section 2, Paragraph 1: *Non-Interference with Active Certification Tracks.*

"

Kline stared at him. Her hand tightened around the canister.

"You're bluffing."

"Check your tablet," Dale said. "Look at the timestamp. Look at the jurisdiction flag."

Kline looked down. Her eyes widened. The screen flickered, then displayed a new line of text in bold red.

**INTER-FEDERAL CONFLICT DETECTED.** **HOLD ALL ACTIONS.** **ESCALATE TO JOINT COMMITTEE.**

She looked up, anger flashing in her eyes.

"You tricked the System."

"I used the System," Dale corrected. "You tried to bypass protocol.

I enforced it.

Now, if you want to cut me open, you have to wait for the Joint Committee to rule. Which takes three to five business days.

"

"Three days," Kline hissed. "The parasite won't wait three days."

"It might," Dale said.

"It's adapting. It's quiet now.

It's learning me.

If I stay calm, if I keep my heart rate down, if I maintain a sterile environment, the metabolic rate of the entity drops. It enters a state of suspended animation. Like a pupa.

"

"You're betting on biology to save you from a regulator?"

"I'm betting on the fact that the Authority wants a clean kill, not a messy surgery," Dale said. "If you kill it, you destroy the evidence of the Rift's nature.

The Authority needs to know how these things enter our dimension.

They need the *source*.

I am the source.

"

Kline stepped forward, her face inches from his.

"You're insane. You're letting a monster crawl inside you so you can write a better report."

"It's not a monster," Dale said. "It's a pest. And pests need to be studied before they are poisoned."

The door to the office burst open.

Two men in tactical gear rushed in. They carried heavy rifles, not standard issue, but modified with resonance dampeners. Behind them walked a woman in a white lab coat, her expression bored.

"Kline," the lead tactical officer barked. "Stand down."

Kline didn't move.

"He's a liability. He's harboring a Class-6 bio-agent."

"Stand. Down," the officer repeated. He pointed his rifle at Kline's chest.

"The Authority has issued a temporary injunction. Mr.

Kowalski's condition is now classified as 'Active Case Study.' Any interference by BOSAC personnel will result in immediate suspension.

"

Kline looked at the officer, then at Dale. She spat on the floor.

"You have twenty-four hours," she said, pointing a finger at Dale. "I will be watching. If that thing gets out, if it spreads, I will burn this entire district to the ground. And you will be in the ashes."

She turned and walked out. The tactical team followed, leaving Dale alone with the woman in the lab coat.

The woman approached the desk. She didn't look at Dale. She looked at the clipboard.

"Name?" she asked.

"Dale Kowalski."

"Certification Level?"

"Level III Provisional. Pending review."

"And the entity?"

"Unidentified. Dimensional origin. Likely *Arthropoda profundi* variant."

The woman nodded. She pulled a scanner from her bag and waved it over Dale's wrist. The device beeped, a low, mournful tone.

"Metabolic rate is stable," she said. "But the neural integration is rising. It's connecting to your motor cortex."

Dale's hand spasmed. His fingers jerked upward, curling into a fist that wasn't his.

"Can you stop it?" he asked.

"No," she said. "But you can slow it down. You need to reduce stress.

You need to complete the documentation.

The more you write, the more the System recognizes the pattern. The more it recognizes the pattern, the more control it gives you.

"

Dale looked at his hand. It was shaking. The translucent outline under his skin was pulsing, matching the beat of his heart.

"What kind of documentation?" Dale asked.

The woman handed him a fresh clipboard. It was heavier than his usual one. It smelled of ozone and old blood.

"You need to map the infestation," she said.

"Not just the insect. The *room*. The *building*.

The *psychological impact* on the occupants. The Authority doesn't just want the species.

They want the *context*.

"

"Why?"

"Because the next one won't be in a wall," the woman said. "It will be in a person. And if you can't identify the vector before it jumps, you won't just lose the job. You'll lose your humanity."

Dale looked at the clipboard. The first line was pre-filled: *SUBJECT: DALE KOWALSKI. STATUS: CONTAINER.*

He picked up his pen. The clicking in his wrist grew louder. *Click-click-click.*

He wrote.

*Entry 1: Structural Integrity.*

The words flowed onto the page, neat and precise.

But as he wrote, he felt a sensation in his chest. A warmth.

Not from the blood, but from something else. Something expanding.

He paused. He looked at his reflection in the darkened window.

His eyes were changing. The pupils were splitting. Not vertically, like a goat, but horizontally, like a compound eye.

The woman in the lab coat didn't notice. She was typing on her tablet.

Dale swallowed. He forced the pen to move.

*Entry 2: Host Integration.*

The clicking stopped.

Silence.

For the first time since the Rift opened, the silence was absolute.

Dale held his breath. He waited for the pain. He waited for the movement.

Nothing.

Then, a new notification appeared in his peripheral vision. It wasn't red. It wasn't blue.

It was gold.

**NEW SYSTEM MECHANIC UNLOCKED: SYMBIOTIC INSIGHT.** **CONDITION: VOLUNTARY HOSTING.** **BENEFIT: FIELD GUIDE ENTRIES FOR ALL DIMENSIONAL ARTHROPODS ARE NOW INSTANTANEOUS.** **PENALTY: LOSS OF HUMAN LANGUAGE CENTER IN 48 HOURS.**

Dale stared at the text. The words swam before his eyes.

Loss of human language, and he looked at the clipboard. The words he had just written were dissolving. Fading from black to gray, then to nothing.

He tried to speak, to ask the woman a question, but his mouth only formed shapes. No sound came out.

The woman looked up. She frowned.

"Mr. Kowalski?" she said. "Are you okay?"

Dale opened his mouth. He tried to say *Yes*.

What came out was a series of clicks.

*Click.*

The woman's face went pale. She backed away from the desk, her hand reaching for her sidearm.

"Containment breach!" she shouted. "Subject is non-verbal! Prepare for termination!"

Dale looked at his hands. They were still holding the pen.

The pen was still writing.

But he wasn't writing English. He was writing symbols. Complex, jagged, beautiful symbols that meant nothing to him, but everything to the thing inside him.

And for the first time, he understood them, and he didn't need to fight the infestation.

He needed to become it.
