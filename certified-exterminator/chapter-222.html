Chapter 222: Counter Claim

# Chapter 222

The darkness wasn't black. It was static.

A low-frequency hum vibrated in Dale's molars. He blinked.

The grey powder coating his lungs was gone, replaced by the sterile, recycled air of a containment pod.

His limbs were pinned to a steel table by magnetic clamps. Above him, a translucent panel displayed his vitals. Heart rate: 110.

Oxygen saturation: 88%.

Toxicity level: Critical.

Panic flared in Dale's chest, hot and sharp. He forced it down, compartmentalizing it the way he handled a customer screaming about bedbugs. Fear was irrelevant. Protocol was everything.

He tried to sit up. The clamps held fast.

"Mr. Kowalski."

The voice came from behind the glass. Dale turned his head, straining against the neck restraints.

Vane stood on the other side of the partition, wearing a hazmat suit that looked three sizes too small.

Her face was obscured by a respirator, but her eyes were wide, darting around the lab. Beside her stood a figure in a crisp blue suit. Not the Auditor from the street.

This one had a tablet instead of a clipboard, and his posture suggested he had never lifted a spray can in his life.

"Status report," Dale croaked. His throat felt lined with sandpaper.

"You are under administrative quarantine," the blue-suited man said.

He didn't look up from his tablet.

"Your certification has been revoked pending a review of the Incident Report. You triggered an Emergency Protocol without proper authorization.

That is a Class A violation."

Dale looked at his hands. The grey taint had receded to his fingertips, forming intricate, branching patterns like frost on a window.

The Quorum Protocol cost him a year of progress.

A year of identifying *Rattus profundus* and *Arachnida voids*. He had spent the last eighteen months grinding for that tier. Losing it meant dropping back to Class II.

His treatment points would be frozen.

His Field Guide entries would remain locked.

"I sealed the breach," Dale said.

"You contaminated yourself," the man replied. "That is a breach of safety regulation 4.1-B. Self-Containment Failure. The Authority does not recognize heroic suicide as valid extermination."

"It wasn't suicide. It was isolation."

"Semantics. Your license is suspended. Do not touch the glass."

Dale ignored the warning. He looked at Vane. She was shaking her head, tapping at her wrist comm. She was trying to send a signal, but the pod blocked it.

"Vane," Dale said, his voice flat. "How many minutes since the spider exploded?"

"Four," she muffled through the respirator.

"Four minutes. The aerosol density in Sector 4 is still climbing. If it spreads to the sewer junction, the infestation vector expands by three hundred percent."

The blue-suited man finally looked up. "That is not your concern, Mr. Kowalski. You are no longer cleared for active duty."

"My concern is the backlog," Dale said. "You have a Thoroughness Rating of 1.2 in this sector. You're losing points for every untreated nest within a five-mile radius. I can fix it."

"No."

The word was absolute. The man tapped his tablet, and the magnetic clamps tightened. A warning light pulsed red above Dale's head. **VIOLATION DETECTED. PENALTY INCREASED.**

Dale stared at the ceiling.

He felt the familiar itch of contamination anxiety, the urge to check the seals on his suit, to verify the air filters. He couldn't move. He couldn't spray.

He could only think.

*Think.*

The Authority rewarded thoroughness. Thoroughness required documentation. Documentation required access to the Field Guide.

Dale closed his eyes. He didn't pray. He visualized the interface.

**SYSTEM: FIELD GUIDE.**

The menu hovered in his mind's eye, greyed out. Locked.

**SYSTEM: CERTIFICATION STATUS.**

Level II (Revoked). Points: 0. Next Tier: Level III (Locked).

He scanned the error codes. One line flickered in the corner of his vision.

**QUARANTINE PROTOCOL ACTIVE. BIOLOGICAL CONTAMINANT DETECTED IN LOCAL NETWORK.**

Dale opened his eyes. He looked at the blue-suited man.

"Your tablet," Dale said. "It's connected to the local Authority network, correct?"

"Standard issue. Why?"

"Because the quarantine protocol doesn't just lock me out. It locks the network. Anything I touched is flagged. But anything *connected* to me is also flagged for inspection."

The man frowned. "That is a security feature, not a bug."

"It's a vulnerability," Dale said.

"The spider's venom acts as a data carrier.

It's not just poison. It's code.

I inhaled it. It's in my blood. And my blood is now a node."

The man's fingers stopped typing. He looked at the vitals monitor.

The toxicity graph was spiking again.

But the spike wasn't chaotic. It was rhythmic. A pulse.

"Get the medic," the man said.

"No medics," Dale said.

"Medics will try to flush me. That will release the payload into the municipal water grid.

You'll have an infestation in the drinking supply.

Class 5. Uncontrollable."

The man went pale. "What do you want?"

"Give me the tablet."

"Absolutely not."

"I'll die in ten minutes. The payload releases.

Your sector goes dark.

Your Thoroughness Rating drops to zero.

You get demoted to sanitation duty."

The blue-suited man swallowed. His eyes flicked to Vane, then back to Dale. He hated this.

He hated the risk. But he hated the Authority's metrics more.

Dale saw the hesitation. It was a crack. He pushed.

"I can identify the payload.

I have the Field Guide entry in my head. I can type it into your system. It will register as a documented hazard, not an infestation event.

That keeps your rating intact."

"You're bluffing," the man said.

"I'm a technician," Dale said. "We don't bluff. We document."

The man hesitated for three seconds. Then he slid the tablet across the metal table. It stopped at the edge of the clamps.

Dale stretched his right arm. The muscle burned. The grey veins in his forearm pulsed in time with his heartbeat. He reached for the tablet.

His fingers brushed the screen.

The moment he touched it, the System interface flared.

**ACCESS GRANTED: LOCAL NETWORK (TEMPORARY).** **WARNING: CROSS-CONTAMINATION RISK HIGH.**

Dale typed. His fingers were clumsy, numb.

He didn't write a report.

He didn't write a justification. He wrote a command string he had memorized from a Class 9 violation code he'd seen once, on a job in Cleveland.

**SUBMIT: HAZARD IDENTIFICATION.** **SPECIES: ARACHNIDA VOIDS (SUB-CLASS: DATA-PARASITE).** **STATUS: CONTAINED.**

He hit enter.

The blue-suited man watched the screen. "You're submitting a field entry from inside a quarantine pod. That's illegal."

"It's effective," Dale said.

The screen flashed green.

**ENTRY RECEIVED. VERIFICATION PENDING.** **THOROUGHNESS BONUS: +50 POINTS.**

The magnetic clamps released.

Dale slumped forward, gasping. Air rushed into his lungs.

The grey patterns on his skin faded, retreating into his pores.

The System had accepted the data.

It had recognized the contaminant as a documented entity, not an active threat. By classifying it, he had neutralized its "infestation" status.

He pulled himself up, leaning heavily on the table. His legs shook.

Vane rushed to the glass. "Dale!"

The blue-suited man was staring at his tablet, his mouth open. "This… this is a loophole. I've never seen a hazard classified via biological ingestion."

"It's not a loophole," Dale said, wiping sweat from his forehead. "It's a procedure. You just didn't know the code."

He grabbed the tablet. He didn't hand it back.

"I'm going to Sector 4," Dale said.

"You have no certification," the man said, snapping out of his trance. "You have no weapons. You are a liability."

"I have the Field Guide," Dale said. "And I have the source."

He pointed to his own chest.

"The spider was a drone. The queen is still out there. If I walk out of here with the payload in my system, I'm a walking beacon. She'll come to me."

The man stepped back. "You're bait."

"I'm a trap," Dale corrected. "But I need a containment field. Something that can hold a Class 4 dimensional breach without collapsing."

The man shook his head. "We don't have equipment for that. The Authority provides standard suppression kits. Not containment."

"Not from the Authority," Dale said. He looked at Vane. "From the contractors."

Vane froze. "The private sector? Dale, they'll kill you for the bounty. They'll dissect you for the payload."

"They'll pay us to keep you alive," Dale said. "Because if you die, the payload spreads. They need the data more than they need the body."

The blue-suited man looked at the tablet in Dale's hand. The green light on the screen pulsed. The Authority's algorithm had accepted Dale's submission. The penalty for the emergency protocol had been waived, converted into a "Research Contribution" bonus.

It was a technicality. A bureaucratic accident. And Dale had exploited it with the precision of a man who had spent fourteen years finding the one loose screw in a door hinge.

"Go," the man said, his voice quiet. "But if you breach the perimeter without a permit, I will revoke your access permanently. And I will bury you in the paperwork."

Dale nodded. He tapped the tablet one last time.

**SYSTEM: EMERGENCY PROTOCOL REVOKED.** **COST: 0 POINTS.** **GAIN: 50 POINTS (TEMPORARY).**

The grey on his skin vanished completely. He felt light, hollowed out.

He walked to the door. Vane was already outside, waiting for him with a fresh canister of suppressant foam and a spare pair of gloves. She looked terrified.

"You're insane," she said.

"I'm efficient," Dale said. He took the gloves. They were too big.

He adjusted the straps until they fit his wrists like a vice. "Let's go."

They stepped out into the corridor. The lights were buzzing. The air smelled of ozone and burnt sugar.

Dale checked his Field Guide. The entry for *Arachnida voids* was updated. A new weakness had been added: **Sensitivity to Digital Noise.**

He looked at Vane.

"We need to make a lot of noise," Dale said.

"Like what?"

"Like a glitch."

He raised the tablet. He didn't call the police. He didn't call the Authority.

He dialed the number for *Vermin Solutions LLC*, a private extermination firm known for using experimental sonic weaponry to drive pests into traps. They operated on a commission basis. They didn't care about laws. They cared about volume.

The line connected.

"Vermin Solutions," a voice crackled.

"I have a Class 4 breach in Sector 4," Dale said. "The target is a dimensional arachnid. It's vulnerable to high-frequency interference."

"Do you have a license?" the voice asked.

Dale looked at the blue-suited man, who was watching from the observation window. The man's face was unreadable.

"I have a report," Dale said. "And I have the bug."

He hung up.

The phone vibrated. A payment confirmation.

*Vermin Solutions* had accepted the job.

But the message that followed wasn't from the company.

It was from the Authority.

**ALERT: UNAUTHORIZED COMMUNICATION DETECTED.** **SOURCE: USER.** **ACTION: PURGE SECTOR.**

The lights in the corridor died.

Emergency reds slammed on. The floor vibrated. A deep, grinding sound echoed from the walls, like gears turning in the earth.

Vane grabbed Dale's arm. "What did you do?"

"I escalated," Dale said. He looked at the tablet. The screen was cracking. The green light was turning black.

The System didn't like being used as a weapon. And the Authority didn't like being ignored.

The wall at the end of the hallway split open. Not a crack. A mouth.

Chitin plates unfolded from the concrete. Eyes, dozens of them, blinked into existence. They weren't looking at Dale. They were looking at the tablet.

The spider had found the signal.

Dale raised the canister of suppressant. He had no license. He had no backup. He had a contract with a private firm that would betray him the second the money ran out.

And he had one minute before the Authority's purge drones arrived to level the block.

He looked at Vane.

"Run," he said.

"No."

"Run."

He stepped forward, directly into the mouth of the wall.

The spider shrieked, a sound of grinding metal and breaking bones.

Dale sprayed.

The white foam hit the eyes. The creature recoiled, thrashing against the concrete.

Dale didn't stop. He walked into the nest.

The darkness swallowed him.

And somewhere, in the high offices of the Authority, a man in a blue suit watched the monitor go black.

He picked up his phone.

"Cancel the contract," he whispered. "And initiate the purge."

But the screen on his desk flickered.

**ERROR: PURGE CANCELLED.** **REASON: USER DALE KOWALSKI HAS FILED A COUNTER-CLAIM.**

The man stared at the text.

Dale hadn't just filed a report. He had filed a grievance.

For unsafe working conditions.

And he had named the Authority as the liable party.
