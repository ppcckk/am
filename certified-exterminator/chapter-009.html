Chapter 9: Unarmed and Assigned

# Chapter 9

The spray can was gone. It had bounced off the creature's skull and skittered under the bed, and Dale watched it go with the specific frustration of a man who knew he'd just thrown away the only tool he had that fit in his hand.

The creature was four and a half feet from him. It had stopped walking. Its head tilted at an angle that suggested curiosity, or something approximating it, and its mouth opened to reveal too many teeth arranged in a pattern that Dale's brain kept trying to resolve into something recognizable and kept failing to do so.

He was thinking about the notification.

CERTIFICATION HOLD: 72 HOURS, which meant no protocols. No Field Guide access.

No Treatment Points.

It did not, he realized, mean he was unarmed. To the Authority, this meant he was unarmed. The Authority tracked treatment.

It tracked elimination.

It tracked the specific sequence of actions that constituted a completed extermination protocol, which did not track a trash bag.

Dale reached behind him, felt the bed frame, and kept moving backward until his spine hit the wall. The creature took another step. Its hands hung at its sides, fingers too long, joints bending in directions that were technically possible but shouldn't have been comfortable.

"Vance," Dale said.

His voice was flat. He'd used this voice on angry tenants who wanted to argue about lease violations. "I need you to leave the room.

Close the door behind you. Do not lock it."

Vance was at the door. His phone was still up. His face was the color of old milk.

"Are you serious?"

"Close the door."

Vance closed the door. The click of the latch was loud in the silence.

Dale was alone with the creature, and he looked at the room.

It was a bedroom.

Standard suburban bedroom. Carpet, drywall, a closet, a window with blinds.

The window was closed. The screen was intact. The door was closed.

The room had four sealed boundaries.

Four sealed boundaries.

Dale moved, and he crossed the room in three steps, not toward the creature but toward the closet.

He opened it. Empty. A rod hanger, a shower curtain ring, a bottle of Febreze.

He grabbed the hanger and the Febreze and closed the closet.

The creature watched him. It didn't attack. It watched with a wrong attention, like a predator assessing if something was prey.

Dale went to the window. He checked the blinds.

He checked the screen.

Intact. He pulled the blinds down and the creature flinched. Not from the darkness.

From the sound. The blinds made a rattle, and the creature's head snapped toward the window like a dog hearing a doorbell.

Dale noted this, and he didn't write it down.

He couldn't. His Field Guide was corrupted.

But he noted it in the other place, the place fourteen years of inspections had built in his memory. He filed it where he filed everything: the mental cabinet labeled ENVIRONMENTAL SENSITIVITIES.

The creature took another step. Dale stepped back.

He was near the bed again, and he looked down. The spray can was under there. He could see it.

The creature was three feet away.

Dale dropped to his knees. He reached under the bed. The spray can was there. He grabbed it.

The creature lunged.

Dale didn't spray. He threw the can.

Not at the creature's head this time.

At the window. The can hit the glass and bounced off. The glass didn't break.

The screen didn't tear. The can fell to the carpet with a soft thud.

But the sound.

The impact.

The creature recoiled. Not much.

A half-step. Its hands came up, covering its ears, and its mouth opened wide and made a sound like a nail dragged across a chalkboard.

Dale was already moving. He grabbed the bed frame and pushed.

The bed was a queen, which was heavy. He wasn't strong. But he'd moved furniture during inspections when he needed to check baseboards, and he knew how to distribute weight, how to use the floor, how to find the angle that made mass work for you instead of against you.

The bed slid two feet.

It hit the wall. It was now positioned across the space between the creature and the door.

Not a wall.

A barricade. Imperfect. Gaps on either side.

But the creature would have to step over it or go around it, and either path took time.

Dale went to the door. He opened it. The hallway was empty. Vance was there, still holding his phone, still recording.

"Get the duct tape from my kit," Dale said. "The wide roll. And the plastic sheeting. The heavy gauge."

Vance moved.

Dale closed the door, and he looked back at the room.

The creature was standing behind the bed. It was looking at the bed.

It was looking at Dale. It made a sound, low and wet, and Dale understood it the way he understood a rodent chattering at a barrier. Not language.

Frustration. The sound of something that wants to get through and can't yet figure out how.

He had maybe two minutes. Maybe less. The creature was intelligent enough to try to go around the bed. It was already shifting its weight.

Vance came back.

Duct tape. Plastic sheeting. Dale took both.

He tore the plastic sheeting with his hands, no time for cutting, and he taped it over the door frame from the outside.

Not sealing the room. Sealing the door.

He taped the edges, the top, the bottom, and he used the entire roll. He pressed every seam flat with his thumb, as if checking a caulked seal for gaps.

The plastic wasn't strong. It wouldn't hold forever. But it would hold for ten minutes. Maybe fifteen.

"Vance. The window in the next room. Is it open?"

"No. I checked."

"Good. Close the closet doors in this room. All of them. Any room on this floor."

Vance ran.

Dale stood in the hallway. He could hear the creature behind the plastic.

A scraping sound.

The sound of long fingers testing the seal.

The plastic bulged once, twice, then went still.

Dale pulled out his phone. He opened the Authority interface. His Field Guide entry was greyed out, marked CORRUPTED.

His certification status was HOLD. His Treatment Point balance was frozen.

But the interface had other tabs. He scrolled. He found the one he was looking for.

PREVENTION ACTIONS LOG.

He typed:

SUBJECT: Class IV entity (unidentified, pending post-hold documentation).

LOCATION: 4417 Maple Ridge Dr, Unit B, second floor bedroom. ACTION: Physical exclusion via temporary barrier (plastic sheeting, adhesive tape, furniture barricade). STATUS: Contained.

NOT TREATED, and he submitted it.

The Authority responded in four seconds.

PREVENTION ACTION LOGGED. NO CERTIFICATION PENALTY. NO POINT AWARD. CONTAINMENT NOTED.

Dale stared at the screen.

Prevention. Not treatment.

The Authority tracked extermination protocols.

It tracked elimination. But exclusion, sanitation, habitat modification, these were the other half of pest control. The half that didn't kill anything.

The half that just made the environment wrong for the pest.

He hadn't broken the rules. He'd worked around them. The certification hold blocked treatment. It didn't block a roll of duct tape.

"Vance."

Vance appeared at the end of the hallway. His face was better. He'd stopped recording. He looked like a man who'd decided that whatever Dale was doing, it was working.

"Did you call anyone else?" Dale asked.

"911. They said to evacuate."

"Did you?"

"No."

"Good. Don't. The containment is holding. I need to check the other rooms for entry points before I leave."

Vance blinked. "You're checking entry points. There's a monster in the bedroom."

"There's a contained pest in the bedroom. I'm checking the structure."

Dale walked to the stairs, and he went down.

He checked the kitchen. The baseboards were clean.

No frass. No droppings. No gnaw marks.

He checked the crawlspace access under the stairs. The seal was intact.

He checked the exterior doors.

All closed. All sealed.

He went back upstairs, and he checked the hallway closet. Empty.

He checked the bathroom.

The vent cover was loose. He pulled it off. Behind it, the ductwork was clear.

No nesting material. No eggs.

No sign that the creature had come through here, which meant it came from above. The attic. The Rift was above.

He went back to the bedroom door. The plastic was still holding. The scraping had stopped. The room was quiet.

Dale pressed his ear to the door. He listened. Nothing.

He checked his phone. The Authority interface updated.

NEW ENTRY: FIELD COMPLIANCE REVIEW. Your unauthorized treatment attempt has been flagged for on-site verification, and a Field Compliance Officer has been dispatched to your location. ETA: 6 minutes.

Dale read it twice. He looked at the plastic-covered door. He looked at the hallway. He looked at Vance, who was standing at the top of the stairs with his hands in his pockets.

"Vance. Do you have a clipboard?"

"No."

"Find one."

Vance disappeared into a room. Dale heard drawers opening. He heard cursing. He heard a drawer close.

Vance came back with a clipboard. A real one. Metal clip, lined paper, slightly bent at the corner.

"Where did you find that?"

"It was on the counter. I think it's a church thing. 'First Baptist Potluck RSVP.'"

Dale took it. He flipped to a blank page. He started writing.

He wrote the containment log.

Time of containment. Materials used.

Structural assessment. Entry point analysis. He wrote it like any other inspection report.

Date, time, address, findings, recommendations. He wrote: RECOMMEND IMMEDIATE ATTIC INSPECTION AND EXCLUSION.

RECOMMEND STRUCTURAL SEALING OF ALL ROOF PENETRATIONS.

RECOMMEND NO FURTHER OCCUPATION OF UNIT B UNTIL EXCLUSION COMPLETE.

He was on the last line when the front door opened.

Dale heard it. He heard footsteps. Heavy boots on hardwood. The specific cadence of someone who expected to be in charge.

He walked to the stairs. He went down.

The man in the kitchen was tall.

Mid-forties. Lean.

He wore a grey coverall with a badge on the chest, and the badge was a different color than Dale's. Silver instead of bronze. He had a Field Guide on his belt.

It was thick. Well-worn.

He had a tool at his hip that Dale didn't recognize.

It looked like a spray wand but thicker, with a brass nozzle and a pressure gauge.

The man looked at Dale. He looked at the clipboard. He looked at the plastic-covered door visible at the top of the stairs.

"Dale Kowalski." Not a question.

"Yes."

"You're on a certification hold."

"Yes."

"You attempted unauthorized treatment on a Class IV entity."

"I attempted treatment with a non-protocol tool. It was not authorized. I accept the penalty."

The man nodded slowly. He pulled a device from his belt.

It looked like a phone but thicker. He tapped it. He looked at the screen.

He looked at Dale.

"Containment via physical exclusion.

Logged. No penalty. Clever." The word was flat.

Not praise.

Assessment. "You understand that containment is not extermination.

The Authority requires elimination."

"I understand. I'm not certified for Class IV."

"You're not certified for Class III either. You're a Level I technician."

"Yes."

The man put the device away. He looked at the stairs. He looked at the plastic.

"I'm going to treat the entity."

Dale said nothing.

The man pulled the spray wand from his hip. He adjusted the nozzle. The pressure gauge ticked. He started up the stairs.

Dale followed. He kept his distance. The man reached the plastic-covered door.

He didn't remove the plastic. He aimed the wand at the gap between the door and the frame.

He fired.

A stream of something green and viscous shot through the gap. It hit the plastic and ran down it. It hit the carpet. The carpet hissed.

The creature screamed.

Not the chalkboard sound from before. A real scream.

High, sustained, human-adjacent. The plastic bulged outward. Something hit the other side.

The tape tore at one corner.

The man fired again. The green stream hit the door itself. The wood began to smoke.

Dale watched the green stream hit the wall. He watched it run down the drywall. He watched it soak into the carpet. He watched it spread.

"Sir," Dale said. "That's a Class II solvent. You're using a Class II treatment protocol on a Class IV entity."

The man didn't look at him. He fired again. The creature screamed again. The plastic tore further.

"That's a cross-contamination event," Dale said. "The protocol is wrong for the species. You're going to get a debuff and corrupt your own Field Guide entry."

The man fired one more time.

Then he stopped. He looked at his Field Guide. It was shaking.

The pages were turning themselves, fluttering like something trapped inside.

His face changed. He looked at Dale.

"How did you know that?"

"Because I've been doing this for fourteen years. And you're using a beetle protocol on something that isn't a beetle."

The man's Field Guide went still. The pages stopped. A notification appeared in the air between them, visible to both of them.

CROSS-CONTAMINATION EVENT LOGGED. FIELD GUIDE ENTRY CORRUPTED: 48 HOURS. TREATMENT PROTOCOL DEBUFF: 40% EFFECTIVENESS REDUCTION FOR 72 HOURS.

The man stared at the notification. He stared at his Field Guide. He stared at the plastic door, which was now sagging, the creature behind it making a sound like something trying to decide whether to come through.

He looked at Dale. His expression was complicated.

"You're a Level I."

"Yes."

"You identified the protocol mismatch before I did."

"I didn't need to. I could see the nozzle.

Class II wands have a fixed aperture.

Class IV requires a variable-flow applicator. You were using the wrong tool."

The man looked at the door. The plastic was failing.

The creature was pushing through, and he looked at his wand.

It was still green, still loaded, still wrong.

"Can you help?" the man asked.

Dale looked at the door. He looked at the creature's shadow through the sagging plastic. He looked at his clipboard.

He looked at his certification status. HOLD. 68 hours remaining.

He could not treat. He could not eliminate. He could not earn points.

But he could contain. He could exclude. He could modify the environment.

"Give me your wand," Dale said.

The man handed it over.

Dale looked at it.

Class II. Fixed aperture. Green solvent.

Wrong for this species.

But it wasn't the treatment that mattered. It was the delivery.

He pulled the nozzle off the wand, and he put it in his pocket; aimed the bare wand at the floor in front of the door. He fired.

The green stream hit the carpet. It hissed.

It soaked in.

The creature's shadow shifted.

It pulled back from the door. It didn't like the smell.

It didn't like the chemical. It was moving away from the treated area.

Dale fired again. A line across the carpet.

A barrier.

The creature tested it, pulled back, tested again. It was a chemical exclusion barrier. Not a treatment.

Not a kill.

A fence, and he fired again.

Another line. He was building a wall of solvent on the floor. The creature couldn't cross it.

It was pacing behind the door, trapped between the plastic and the chemical barrier.

Dale turned off the wand. He handed it back to the man.

"Remove the plastic from the outside.

Seal the door.

The entity will stay in the room. It won't cross the barrier.

It'll exhaust itself against the door."

The man stared at him. Then he moved.

He went to the door, and he pulled the plastic; sealed the door with a different adhesive from his belt. He pressed it flat.

The creature hit the door once. Then again. Then it stopped.

The man turned to Dale.

"That wasn't treatment."

"No."

"That was exclusion."

"Yes."

The man was quiet for a moment. Then he pulled out his device. He typed something. He looked at Dale.

"I'm filing a competency note. Cross-certification request. You're being recommended for a Level II practical exam."

The cold went through his chest and stayed there. Not excitement. Something harder to name.

"The exam requires treatment protocols," Dale said. "I'm on hold."

"The hold expires in sixty-eight hours. The exam window opens tomorrow morning."

Dale looked at the door, and the creature was silent behind it. Contained.

Not eliminated.

Not documented. Not treated. Just... Excluded.

Kept in a room. A problem deferred.

He looked at the man.

Silver badge.

Thick Field Guide. Level IV clearance.

A man who could kill things Dale couldn't touch.

"What's your name?" Dale asked.

"Reeves."

"Reeves. What's the Thoroughness Rating on that treatment?"

Reeves looked at his device. He looked at the notification. He looked at the door.

"Three point one."

Dale nodded. He wrote on his clipboard.

He wrote the time, and he wrote the address. He wrote: STRUCTURAL EXCLUSION IN PROGRESS. TREATMENT PENDING CERTIFICATION CLEARANCE.

RECOMMEND RE-INSPECTION WITHIN 72 HOURS.

He tore the page off. He handed it to Reeves.

Reeves took it. He looked at it. He looked at Dale like he was seeing him for the first time.

"You're going to fail the practical if you keep doing this."

"Doing what?"

"Containing instead of killing."

Dale looked at the door. The creature was quiet.

It was waiting, which was patient. It had all the time in the world, and Dale had sixty-eight hours before he could touch it with anything that counted.

"Maybe," Dale said.

Reeves left. His boots went down the stairs. The front door opened and closed.

Dale stood in the hallway. He looked at the door. He looked at his clipboard. He looked at his phone.

The Authority notification was new.

FIELD COMPLIANCE REVIEW: COMPLETE. FINDING: NO FURTHER ACTION. NOTE: PREVENTION ACTION DEMONSTRATES PROFESSIONAL COMPETENCY OUTSIDE CERTIFICATION PARAMETERS. FLAGGED FOR EXAMINER REVIEW.

And below it, a second notification.

EXAMINER REVIEW: ACCEPTED. PRACTICAL EXAMINATION RESCHEDULED.

DATE: TOMORROW.

LOCATION: 4417 MAPLE RIDGE DR.

SUBJECT: THE ENTITY BEHIND THE DOOR.

Dale looked at the door. The creature was still there. He had sixty-eight hours on his hold. The exam was in fourteen hours.

He could not treat it. He could not document it. He could not earn a point from it.

And the Authority was going to make him do it anyway.

They had already assigned him the address.
