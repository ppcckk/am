Chapter 185: Cognitive Sanitation Protocol

# Chapter 185

The fluorescent lights in the breakroom flickered, buzzing with the frequency of a dying transformer. Dale Kowalski didn't look up.

He kept his eyes on the laminated card in his hand, turning it over and over between thumb and forefinger.

The plastic was warm. Too warm.

*Violation Notice: Code 44-B. Unauthorized Interference with Private Remediation Assets.*

The penalty wasn't a fine. It wasn't a suspension. The text at the bottom of the card, written in a font that shifted when Dale tried to read it directly, specified a "Cognitive Sanitation Protocol."

He knew what that meant. In municipal pest control, sanitation meant cleaning up the mess so the next tenant could move in. In the Authority's lexicon, it meant wiping the offender's memory of the last forty-eight hours.

Dale pocketed the card. He stood up. His knees popped, loud in the silence of the room.

Across the table, Marcus, the regional supervisor for the Aeternum branch, was typing on a tablet. He wore the same yellow suit as the men who had chased Dale through the library, but his collar was open, revealing a neck tattooed with swirling, geometric patterns that hurt to look at.

"You're making this difficult, Dale," Marcus said.

He didn't look up from his screen. "We have a contract with the city.

We have the permits.

You have a clipboard and a god complex."

"I have a license," Dale said. His voice was flat, stripped of any inflection that might betray his rising heart rate.

"Class II.

Valid through next fiscal year.

Code 44 applies to unlicensed contractors interfering with licensed operations. I am not unlicensed."

Marcus finally looked up. His eyes were bloodshot, the pupils dilated to black saucers. "The Authority revoked your status ten minutes ago.

Digital audit. You failed the integrity check."

Dale checked his peripheral vision. No notification. No red text. No system alert.

This was new.

For years, the Authority had been a benevolent, if bureaucratic, god.

It tracked his points, graded his reports, and awarded his upgrades. It never lied. It never omitted.

If Dale's certification was revoked, there would be a notification.

A stark, white box telling him he was now a civilian.

The absence of the notification was the violation.

"Show me," Dale said.

"Show you what?"

"The revocation notice."

Marcus laughed.

It was a wet, ugly sound. "You don't need to see it to feel it. That's how the Authority works.

It's about trust. You've been sloppy, Dale.

Your Thoroughness Rating dropped to a 4.1 after the library incident.

Do you know what a 4.1 means?

It means you're a liability. It means you're a vector."

A vector. A carrier of disease. The insult landed with the precision of a hammer.

Dale looked at the door. It was locked. The electronic lock glowed red.

He looked at Marcus. "Where is the infestation you're treating?"

"Residential Sector 4. The old mill houses."

"That's my territory."

"Not anymore." Marcus tapped his tablet. "We're deploying a Class-4 Thermal Dispersal. We're going to burn the nests out.

Simple. Efficient."

"Thermal Dispersal doesn't work on *Rattus profundus* larvae," Dale said.

The words came out before he could stop them. It was professional pride, a reflex as ingrained as breathing. "They're subterranean.

They burrow into the foundation concrete.

Heat just drives them deeper. You'll miss the broodmother.

The infestation will return in six weeks, but this time they'll be heat-resistant."

Marcus smiled. It wasn't a nice smile. "That's not our problem.

The city pays us to remove the visible threats. The Authority doesn't care about long-term efficacy.

It cares about compliance.

And you, Dale, are out of compliance."

Dale felt a cold knot form in his stomach. This wasn't just a corporate takeover.

This was a system exploit. Aeternum was using the Authority's love of paperwork to bury real infestations under layers of bureaucratic red tape. They were exterminating the symptoms, not the cause, and charging the city for the privilege.

And the Authority was letting them do it.

Unless Dale could prove otherwise.

He looked at his Field Guide. It lay on the table. He hadn't opened it since the library. He reached for it.

Marcus moved faster than he should have been able to. He slammed his hand over the book. "Don't touch the evidence."

"It's not evidence. It's a field guide."

"It's a restricted document. Access requires Level IV clearance."

Dale looked at Marcus's hand. He looked at the tattoo on Marcus's neck. The geometric patterns were moving, shifting like oil on water.

*Corporate Parasite.*

The entry he had written in the library.

*Weakness: Public Perception.*

Dale needed to make this public. He needed to break the closed loop.

He looked around the breakroom. There were no windows. No phones. Just the lock, the tablet, and the book.

Dale picked up his pen.

He didn't try to hit Marcus. That was a combat move, and Dale wasn't built for combat. He was built for documentation.

He clicked the pen.

"Marcus," Dale said. "Do you have a current Field Guide entry for *Rattus profundus*?"

Marcus blinked. "Why would I?"

"Because if you don't, your Thermal Dispersal protocol is invalid. Under Section 8, Clause C, you cannot deploy a species-specific protocol without a verified identification in your personal guide."

Marcus sneered. "We use the central database. We don't carry paper books."

"The Authority doesn't recognize central database entries as 'personal verification' for on-site deployment," Dale said. He recited the rule from memory. It was one of the few obscure loopholes he had memorized during his Level II exam prep, expecting it might never be useful.

He was wrong.

"If you deploy without a personal, handwritten entry in your Field Guide, the Authority considers the treatment 'unverified.' Unverified treatments yield zero Treatment Points for the operator, and they generate a Contamination Flag on the location."

Marcus paused. His fingers hovered over his tablet.

"If you flag the location," Dale continued, his voice steady, "the city cannot pay you for the job. They have to wait for a certified exterminator to verify the clean-up. And since your certification is under review, you can't verify it yourself."

It was a bluff. Mostly.

The Authority did require verification. But it usually allowed digital signatures.

But the Authority was rigid. It loved forms. It loved paper trails. If Dale could create a discrepancy in the digital record, the Authority's automated systems might default to the strictest interpretation of the rules.

And Dale had one card to play.

He opened his Field Guide.

Marcus tried to stop him, but Dale had already flipped the page. He ignored Marcus's hand, pushing it aside with a strength he didn't know he possessed. The black oil under his nails smeared on the page.

He wrote.

*Subject: Aeternum Pest Solutions.* *Action: Unauthorized Deployment of Class-4 Protocol.* *Status: Unverified.* *Contamination Flag: Active.*

As soon as the ink hit the paper, the air in the room changed.

The humming of the lights stopped.

The lock on the door clicked. Green.

Dale didn't run. He waited.

The door swung open.

Two Authority Drones hovered in the hallway. They weren't the friendly, glowing orbs that had granted Dale his first points. These were angular, black, and bristling with sensors. They scanned the room, their red lasers sweeping over Marcus, then over Dale.

Marcus stepped back, his face draining of color. "Wait. The central database—"

The lead Drone spoke. Its voice was the same dry, procedural tone as the notifications Dale had lived with for a decade.

"[AUDIT INITIATED. DISCREPANCY DETECTED BETWEEN LOCAL FIELD LOG AND CENTRAL DATABASE. LOCAL LOG TAKES PRECEDENCE.]"

Marcus looked at Dale. "You can't do this. You're a Level II. You can't challenge a Level IV."

"I'm not challenging him," Dale said. He looked at the Drone. "I'm flagging an anomaly."

"[ANOMALY ACCEPTED. AETERNUM OPERATOR STATUS: PENDING REVIEW. KOWALSKI STATUS: MAINTAINED.]"

The Drones turned toward Marcus.

"[OPERATOR MARCUS: PREPARE FOR INTERROGATION.]"

Marcus scrambled backward, knocking over a chair. "This is a mistake! The contract—"

"[CONTRACT SUSPENDED PENDING VERIFICATION.]"

The Drones advanced. Not with violence, but with the inexorable weight of bureaucracy. Marcus was surrounded by their sensors, his tablet confiscated, his suit scanned for contaminants.

Dale stood up. He picked up his clipboard.

He walked past the Drones. They parted for him.

He walked out of the breakroom, down the hallway, and into the main office. The other Aeternum employees stared.

Some were packing their bags.

Some were arguing on phones. The hierarchy was collapsing in real-time, the digital leash cut by a single handwritten note.

Dale walked to the front entrance. He pushed the glass door open.

The sun was bright. Too bright.

He stepped out onto the sidewalk. He looked at his hands. The black oil was still there, under the nails.

He looked at the city. Sector 4 was three blocks away. The mill houses.

If Aeternum was suspended, the infestation was untreated. The broodmother was still there, burying her eggs in the foundations.

Dale checked his watch. He had a lunch break. He had a permit.

He started walking.

His phone buzzed in his pocket. He pulled it out.

It wasn't a text. It was a notification from the Authority.

*[SYSTEM UPDATE: CROSS-CONTAMINATION PROTOCOL ACTIVATED.]*

Dale stopped.

He read the text.

*[Your Field Guide entry for 'Corporate Parasite' has been flagged as Non-Standard. Future interactions with Aeternum personnel will incur a 40% debuff to all identification attempts.]*

He had bought himself a delay. He had stalled the corporate takeover. But he had contaminated his own guide.

If he wanted to fight Aeternum again, he would be handicapped. He would be slower. Less precise.

And they would know it.

He looked up at the sky. The Rift above the city was larger than it had been yesterday. The tear in the fabric of reality was widening, the edges bleeding purple light.

Something was coming through. Something bigger than rats.

Dale clicked his pen. He opened his Field Guide to a fresh page.

He wrote:

*Next Threat: Unknown.* *Preparation: Required.*

He looked at the pen in his hand. It was trembling.

Not from fear.

From anticipation.

He turned the corner, heading toward the mill houses. The ground beneath his feet was soft. It gave way slightly, like loose soil.

He could hear it.

A low, chittering sound. Coming from inside the earth.

Dale Kowalski adjusted his clipboard. He checked his spray tank. It was full.

He runned.
