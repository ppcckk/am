Chapter 38: Posterior Tapping

# Chapter 38

The surveyor's head pressed against the joist and the wood groaned like a door in a windstorm. Dale noted the sound. Flat iron head, matte black carapace, segmented thorax with six visible legs and two that weren't legs. The two at the rear ended in flat, chitinous plates that scraped against the crawlspace floor in a rhythmic pattern.

Tapping. That was the tapping.

He wrote it down. BEHAVIOR: Rhythmic substrate percussion via posterior appendages. Purpose: unknown. Frequency: approximately 2.3 seconds between strikes.

The surveyor stopped tapping. It rotated its head toward him.

The flat iron shape caught his flashlight beam and reflected nothing.

Not a point of light came back. The head was a matte void.

Dale stepped back, and he clicked his flashlight off. Then on again.

The surveyor hadn't moved.

Its head was still oriented toward where he'd been standing.

He wrote: SENSORY: Possibly photophobic. Possibly not. Inconclusive. Requires controlled light test.

His clipboard buzzed. Question fourteen. He ignored it. He had seventy-three minutes left on the practical timer, and the Authority's entry for HIST-0041-A-4 was still three-quarters empty.

He moved to the east side of the house. The crawlspace was accessible from three points: the hatch he'd entered from, a secondary access panel on the north wall, and a gap where the foundation met the grade. He'd noted all three in his inspection three weeks ago, and the gap he'd flagged as a K-7 structural entry point.

The surveyor was between the hatch and the gap. It had created a tunnel in the crawlspace floor.

Not a tunnel in dirt.

A tunnel in the concrete slab, and the edges were smooth, glassy, like something had melted the material than broken it.

Dale knelt at the tunnel's edge. He ran his gloved finger along the lip. The material was warm.

Not hot. Warm, like a wall that had been in sunlight.

He wrote: SUBSTRATE ALTERATION: Concrete softened and displaced without thermal signature consistent with heat application. Mechanism: unknown. Possibly dimensional displacement than physical erosion.

His clipboard buzzed. Question fifteen. He glanced at it.

QUESTION 15: In the context of dimensional surveyor behavior, what does the term "satellite colony" refer to?

He didn't know. He wrote: UNKNOWN. See practical observation.

That was an honest answer. The Authority accepted honest answers. They gave you partial credit. They gave you nothing for guesses.

He went back to the hatch, and the surveyor had resumed tapping. The rhythm was the same. Two point three seconds.

But now he noticed something else.

The tapping wasn't random. It was directional.

The posterior plates struck the floor at an angle, and the angle was toward the gap in the foundation.

Toward the entry point he'd flagged and not sealed.

Dale pulled out his phone. He pulled up the photo he'd taken three weeks ago. The gap.

Four inches wide, six inches long. A clean line where the concrete foundation met the soil grade.

In his report he'd written: K-7.

Structural entry point.

Recommend sealing with polyurethane foam and steel mesh, and he hadn't sealed it. He'd written the recommendation. He'd filed the paperwork. He'd done his job.

Now something was tapping at that gap from the inside, and the surveyor in the crawlspace was tapping in the same rhythm.

Two creatures. One rhythm. One signal.

Dale uncapped his pen. He wrote: BEHAVIOR UPDATE: Posterior percussion is communicative. Signal relay between individuals.

At least two surveyors present in this structure. The second individual is located at the K-7 entry point.

The first individual (HIST-0041-A-4) is acting as a relay or forward unit.

His clipboard flashed. The Authority's entry updated. BEHAVIOR: [PARTIAL].

The system accepted his observation, which was incomplete, but it was his.

He looked at the surveyor.

It was still tapping. Still oriented toward the gap. It hadn't attacked him.

It hadn't moved toward him at all. It was doing a job.

Dale understood that, and he understood jobs.

A surveyor wasn't a threat. A surveyor was a worker.

It was doing what it was designed to do. It was creating an entry point.

He thought about termites. Specifically, subterranean termites. The kind that built mud tubes from the soil to the foundation. The kind that sent out scout workers to find cellulose, then relayed the location back to the colony through drumming signals.

The surveyor was a scout. The thing at the gap was the colony. Or another scout. Or both.

He needed to isolate the colony. Not the scout. The colony.

The Authority's protocol said: isolate. In previous encounters, isolation meant physical containment.

A trash bag.

A sealed crawlspace. A perimeter.

But this wasn't a physical problem. This was a dimensional problem. The surveyor was creating a hole in space. Isolating it meant sealing the hole.

He pulled out his kit. He pulled out the polyurethane foam canister he always carried for K-7 violations. Standard issue. Two pounds of expanding foam rated for concrete gaps up to six inches.

He crawled toward the gap.

The surveyor's tapping intensified as he moved. The rhythm sped up.

One point eight seconds.

One point five. It was warning the other one.

Dale moved faster, and he reached the gap. The outside air was cold.

The soil beyond was dark.

And in the dark, something was pushing against the gap from the outside.

A flat, chitinous plate. The same posterior appendage type.

Pressing. Expanding the gap by fractions of a millimeter with each push.

He sprayed the foam. The canister hissed.

Polyurethane expanded outward and inward simultaneously, filling the gap, pressing against the chitinous plate, hardening in seconds.

The plate stopped pushing. The tapping stopped.

His clipboard flashed.

ISOLATION COMPLETE: HIST-0041-A-4 entry point sealed. Dimensional signature contained. Protocol step 3/5 fulfilled.

He blinked. The Authority had accepted foam; it had accepted a K-7 sealant as a valid isolation method for a dimensional surveyor.

He wrote: ISOLATION METHOD: Polyurethane foam (standard K-7 application). Accepted by Authority as dimensional sealant. Implication: dimensional entry points behave as structural violations under Authority classification, and the same materials that seal a termite entry point can seal a dimensional breach.

His clipboard flashed again. This time it wasn't a question. It was a notification.

FIELD GUIDE UPDATE: HIST-0041-A-4 (Dimensional Surveyor, Large). WEAKNESS IDENTIFIED: Entry point dependency. Sealing creates isolation without elimination. Targeted damage bonus available: +30% against this species when attacking the creature itself while its entry point is sealed.

Dale stared at the notification, and he understood what it meant. He'd found the weakness.

Not a physical weakness.

A behavioral one. The surveyor depended on its entry point. Seal the hole, and the creature loses its dimensional anchor.

He crawled back to the surveyor. It was still in the crawlspace.

Still oriented toward the now-sealed gap.

Its tapping had stopped, which was motionless. Confused.

A worker whose communication channel had been cut.

Dale pulled out his spray can. Not the foam.

The other can.

The one he used for Class-1 dimensional vermin. A chemical compound the Authority had provided at Certification Level III. It was rated for soft-bodied organisms.

The surveyor was not soft-bodied, and he checked his Field Guide. HIST-0041-A-4.

CARAPACE: Matte black, chitinous, density unknown.

The spray wouldn't work. He needed something else.

He looked at the surveyor's body. Six legs.

Two posterior plates.

A flat iron head, and the thorax segments were visible between the legs, and they were softer than the carapace. Slightly.

A fraction of a millimeter less dense, and he could tell by how the flashlight beam reflected differently off those segments.

He uncapped his pen. He wrote: WEAKNESS ADDENDUM: Intersegmental membranes at thoracic junctions exhibit reduced carapace density. Estimated 15-20% thinner than primary exoskeleton.

His clipboard flashed.

WEAKNESS RECORDED. Targeted damage bonus updated: +45% against HIST-0041-A-4 when targeting intersegmental membranes.

Forty-five percent. That was significant.

But he still needed a delivery method.

His spray was chemical. His knife was physical. Neither was optimized for intersegmental membranes on a creature the size of a large dog.

He looked around the crawlspace. The tunnel the surveyor had created in the concrete. The glassy edges. Warm to the touch.

He pulled out his utility knife, and he pressed it against the tunnel wall. The material was soft.

Not soft like foam.

Soft like warm wax. It gave under the blade and reformed when he pulled away.

Dimensional material. Not concrete. The surveyor had replaced the concrete with something else. Something that was soft to a knife but warm to the touch.

He wrote: SUBSTRATE ALTERATION UPDATE: Surveyor replaces native material with dimensional medium. Medium is soft to physical cutting. Medium is warm. Medium reforms after cutting.

He thought about the protocol. Eliminate.

He needed to eliminate the surveyor, and the Authority gave him a +45% damage bonus for targeting the intersegmental membranes.

But he had no weapon optimized for that.

Unless.

He looked at the tunnel. The dimensional medium. It was soft.

It reformed. It was warm.

He looked at the surveyor. It was sitting in the tunnel. Its body was in contact with the medium on all sides. Its intersegmental membranes were pressed against the warm, soft, reforming material.

He looked at his spray can. The chemical compound for Class-1 dimensional vermin. It was rated for soft-bodied organisms. It worked by disrupting cellular cohesion.

The intersegmental membranes were soft. They were in contact with the medium. If he could get the spray into the medium, it would contact the membranes directly.

He sprayed the tunnel wall.

The chemical hissed as it hit the dimensional medium. The medium absorbed it. The surface darkened.

The surveyor's body twitched.

Its thorax segments contracted. The intersegmental membranes where they met the medium turned a pale, sickly gray.

It was working, and the medium was acting as a delivery vehicle; he was spraying the tunnel, not the creature. The creature was in contact with the tunnel.

The chemical was reaching the membranes through the medium.

He sprayed again.

And again.

The surveyor's legs started to fold. Its head dropped.

The tapping plates scraped against the floor one last time, then stopped, and the surveyor went still.

His clipboard flashed.

ELIMINATION CONFIRMED: HIST-0041-A-4. Protocol step 4/5 fulfilled.

He wrote: ELIMINATION METHOD: Indirect chemical application via dimensional medium substrate. Medium acts as contact surface for intersegmental membranes. Chemical disrupts membrane cohesion through medium contact.

His clipboard flashed.

METHOD NOTED. Field Guide entry updated. New protocol variant unlocked: SUBSTRATE DELIVERY (Dimensional Medium).

Dale sat back on his heels. He looked at the dead surveyor. He looked at the sealed gap. He looked at his clipboard.

Protocol step 5: verify, and he crawled to the gap. He checked the foam seal.

It was intact.

The polyurethane had hardened fully. No dimensional signature leaking through. He pressed his palm against it.

Nothing.

No warmth. No vibration.

He crawled back to the surveyor, and he checked its body. No movement. No tapping.

He wrote: VERIFICATION: Specimen confirmed dead. Entry point sealed.

No secondary dimensional signatures detected in structure.

His clipboard flashed.

PROTOCOL COMPLETE. HIST-0041-A-4. Thoroughness Rating: 9.4. Treatment Points awarded: 340.

He looked at the number.

Three hundred and forty. That was more than he'd ever earned from a elimination.

The substrate delivery method had added points. The Authority rewarded creative protocol adaptation.

His clipboard buzzed. Question sixteen.

Then seventeen.

Then eighteen. The written exam was still running. He'd been doing it in parallel with the practical.

The Authority allowed it.

He'd been answering questions between crawlspace movements, and he stood up. He crawled out of the crawlspace. He closed the hatch. He walked to the front of the house.

The street was still empty.

The Authority's perimeter was still active. The sky was the wrong color. It had been the wrong color for two hours now.

A bruised purple at the edges, like a bruise on skin.

He pulled out his phone, and he checked his certification status.

Certification Level IV. He'd earned enough points from the practical to advance.

A notification from the Authority hung at the edge of his vision.

CERTIFICATION LEVEL IV CONFIRMED. New protocol unlocked: NEST ASSESSMENT. You are now authorized to assess and document dimensional colony structures.

Dale read the notification. He read it again.

NEST ASSESSMENT.

He'd never been authorized to assess a nest before. He'd always been authorized to eliminate individual organisms.

His clipboard buzzed. Question nineteen.

QUESTION 19: You have eliminated a satellite surveyor and sealed its entry point, and the satellite was in communication with a primary colony. Using your Field Guide entry for HIST-0041-A-4 and your knowledge of the signal relay, identify

QUESTION 19: You have eliminated a satellite surveyor and sealed its entry point, and the satellite was in communication with a primary colony. Using your Field Guide entry for HIST-0041-A-4 and your knowledge of the signal relay, identify the likely response vector of the primary colony and the appropriate containment adjustment for the sealed entry point.

Dale stared at the question.

The signal relay. The surveyor had been broadcasting on a frequency that the Field Guide had flagged as non-standard—not a distress call, not a mating signal.

A ping.

A confirmation pulse, and he thought about the crawlspace. The way the surveyor had been positioned.

Not foraging.

Not nesting.

It had been facing the wall. Facing the entry point.

Facing something on the other side, and he typed:

The surveyor was not a forager. It was a monitoring unit.

Its elimination removes the colony's ability to assess the integrity of its own boundary.

The sealed entry point should be treated as a compromised seal, not a closed one. The primary colony will attempt re-establishment of communication through the same substrate channel within 48-72 hours. Recommend: secondary seal with substrate-resistant barrier.

Do not treat entry point as resolved.

Treat as active monitoring station, and he hit submit.

His clipboard flashed.

EXAM COMPLETE.

Score: 19/20. Written component passed. Practical component passed.

Certification Level IV advancement confirmed, and he exhaled. He hadn't realized he'd been holding his breath since question fourteen.

He looked at the house.

The sealed crawlspace.

The entry point he'd patched with expanding foam and a sheet of galvanized steel. A standard closure.

Protocol-compliant.

His clipboard buzzed one more time. Not a notification.

A new entry in his Field Guide.

Auto-populated. He hadn't identified this species. The Authority had added it without his input.

HIST-0041-A-PRIME.

Classification: Containment Organism. Not infestation.

Not pest.

Function: barrier maintenance. Threat level: N/A. Note: Do not eliminate.

Dale read it twice. He looked at the crawlspace hatch. He looked at the galvanized steel. He looked at the expanding foam.

He'd sealed the lock.

Not the door.

The sky changed color again. Not purple this time. Something closer to the color of a wall before it cracks.

His clipboard buzzed. A new job. A new notification. The kind he'd never seen before.

INFESTATION RECLASSIFIED. CONTAINMENT BREACH DETECTED. ALL UNITS.
