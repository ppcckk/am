Chapter 321: Blank Field Guide

# Chapter 321

The tunnel mouth breathed.

A wet, rhythmic exhalation pushed warm, sulfur-tinged air against Dale's boots. The sound was not biological in any standard sense; it was hydraulic. A pressurized system failing to contain its own volume.

Dale adjusted his respirator. The straps dug into his hair, a familiar pressure that usually soothed his anxiety.

Today, it felt like a vice, and he checked his clipboard. The paper was dry, tucked inside a waterproof sleeve. He clicked his pen.

**PERMIT 14-C ACTIVE.** **TARGET: GEODE-SHARD (UNCLASSIFIED).** **STATUS: CONTAINMENT IMMINENT.**

He stepped forward.

The light from his headlamp cut through the gloom, illuminating the jagged teeth of the cavern entrance. To his left, a support beam had splintered. Not from impact. From growth.

A hand clamped onto his shoulder. Hard.

Dale didn't flinch. He didn't spin. He simply shifted his weight, breaking the grip with a professional's indifference to amateur contact.

"You're out of uniform, Kowalski."

The voice was gravel dragged over glass. Dale turned.

Standing in the shadows of the support pillar was Silas Vane. The man wore a suit that cost more than Dale's truck, tailored to hide the bulk of the sub-dermal armor plating beneath. His eyes were replaced with optical lenses that whirred softly, focusing on Dale with predatory precision.

"Permit is in hand," Dale said, holding up the clipboard. "Solo operation clause. You're a violation, Mr. Vane."

Vane laughed, which was a dry, hacking sound.

"I'm not here to fight the beast, Dale.

I'm here to watch you die.

The Auditor wanted a spectacle. I'm providing the audience.

"

"The permit says no support units."

"Support implies assistance," Vane stepped closer, his boots crunching on loose shale. "I am an observer.

A witness to the inevitable.

Your Thoroughness Rating is 9.2, but your survival instinct is apparently zero.

You drove into a Class-IV breach zone with a clipboard and a roll of tape. That's not bravery.

That's malpractice.

"

Dale looked past him, into the dark throat of the cave.

"If you're an observer, you need a permit. Do you have one?"

Vane's lenses narrowed.

"I have a license to kill. The Authority grants that to anyone who clears the backlog."

"That's an interpretation," Dale said flatly. "It's a sloppy interpretation. And sloppy work contaminates the field."

He tapped his pen against the clipboard. Once. Twice.

"Step back."

Vane didn't move.

"Or?"

"Or I file a cross-contamination complaint before I even enter the zone. If I deploy a protocol against a target that doesn't match my Field Guide entry, I get debuffed. But if I document that *you* are obstructing a licensed operation, you get a citation. And the Authority hates citations more than it hates monsters."

Vane stiffened. The whirring in his eyes stopped.

"You wouldn't."

"Watch me." Dale opened a new page in the Guide. He didn't look at it.

He wrote from memory. **OBSTRUCTION OF LICENSED TECHNICIAN.

CLASS: HUMAN. BEHAVIOR: INTIMIDATION. REMEDY: ADMINISTRATIVE REPORT.**

The system notification pinged in Dale's vision, sharp and clear.

**FIELD GUIDE ENTRY ADDED: OBSTRUCTOR (HOMO SAPIENS, VARIANT BUREAUCRATIC).** **WEAKNESS: EGO. TARGETED DAMAGE BONUS APPLIED TO SOCIAL CONFLICT.**

Vane blinked. The arrogance drained from his posture, replaced by confusion.

"What did you just do?"

"Documented the hazard," Dale said.

"Now step back. Or I'll treat you as an infestation.

And I don't use spray on humans.

I use paperwork. It's slower. It hurts more.

"

Vane hesitated. Then, with a sneer that didn't reach his eyes, he stepped aside.

"Go ahead. Make a mess of it."

Dale walked past him. He did not look back.

The tunnel narrowed. The air grew thicker, smelling of ozone and copper. The walls here were not stone. They were crystalline, pulsing with a faint, bruised purple light.

Ahead, the cave opened into a vast chamber.

In the center stood the Geode-Shard, which was twelve feet tall. A jagged formation of translucent mineral that hummed with a low-frequency vibration.

Inside the crystal, suspended in a viscous blue fluid, was a shape.

It looked like a heart.

Or a fist. Or something with too many joints.

Dale stopped. He raised his binoculars.

"Identify," he muttered.

He opened his Field Guide. The entry for Geode-Shard was blank. It had not been seen before. The blur he had felt earlier was gone, replaced by a void.

**UNKNOWN SPECIES.** **PROTOCOL REQUIRED: IDENTIFICATION.**

He couldn't fight it. He couldn't contain it. He had to know what it was first.

A voice crackled in his earpiece. Static. Then a voice, smooth and cold.

"Dale. I'm seeing your feed. You're standing still."

The Auditor.

Dale pressed the transmit button on his collar.

"I'm observing. You told me the Auditor was watching. I'm making sure the record is clean."

"The record is irrelevant. The job is the job. Kill it."

"Kill it without documentation yields zero points," Dale said. "And zero points means zero certification progress. I'm not here to kill. I'm here to exterminate."

Silence on the line.

"Your permit expires in twenty minutes," the Auditor said. "Then the crystal breaches. The fluid spreads.

The city gets a new neighbor. Good luck, Dale.

"

The channel closed.

Dale lowered the binoculars, and he looked at the crystal. The blue fluid pulsed. *Thump. Thump.

Thump.*

He checked his tools.

Spray canister: Empty. Duct tape: Full roll. Clipboard: Full. Pen: Full.

He walked to the edge of the pool surrounding the crystal. The ground was slick with condensation. He slipped on a patch of moss, catching himself on a stalactite. His hand left a smear of green slime.

He looked at the slime, and he looked at the crystal.

He looked at his Field Guide.

Something clicked.

The crystal wasn't growing. It was *bleeding*.

The purple light wasn't a glow, which was a membrane. A containment field failing under pressure from the inside.

The shape inside wasn't a monster. It was a seed.

Dale's breath hitched. He flipped pages in the Guide. *Plant-based infestations. Root systems. Mycelial networks.*

He found an entry for *Mycelial Parasite*. Class II.

But this wasn't a parasite. It was a host.

He pulled out a sample vial from his belt. He uncapped it.

"Don't breathe," he whispered to himself.

He stepped onto a protruding crystal shard. He extended the vial. A single drop of the blue fluid fell into the glass.

The vial clouded instantly. The liquid inside turned a violent, boiling red.

Dale dropped the vial. It shattered on the stone floor.

The red stain sizzled. It ate into the rock.

**SPECIMEN ANALYZED.** **CLASSIFICATION: DIMENSIONAL MIMIC (PHASE II).** **WEAKNESS: CHLORINE-BASED OXIDATION.** **NOTE: THIS SPECIES FAKE-DEATH TO PRESERVE NEST. DO NOT ENGAGE IN COMBAT. DO NOT ATTEMPT TO KILL THE HOST.**

Dale stared at the message.

Fake-death?

He looked at the crystal. The hum stopped. The pulse stopped. The crystal went dark.

It looked dead.

If he tried to smash it, the system would register it as 'killed'. But if it was faking death, the nest would trigger. The entire cavern would collapse. The city above would fall into a hole.

The permit said: *IF THE SPECIMEN IS DESTROYED, THE PERMIT IS VOIDED.*

Dale understood now. The Authority didn't want him to kill it. They wanted him to *prove* it was dead. Or prove it wasn't.

They wanted the verification, and he pulled out his pen. He wrote in the Field Guide.

**STATUS: FALSE POSITIVE.** **ACTION: QUARANTINE.**

But he had no containment unit. He had a truck. And a truck wasn't rated for dimensional mimics.

He needed a solvent, and he looked at his spray canister. Empty.

He looked at his first aid kit. Isopropyl alcohol. Not chlorine.

He looked at the pool. The water.

He looked at the crystal, and the silence was absolute.

Then, a crack.

Tiny. Sharp. Like ice breaking.

A fissure appeared in the crystal's surface. A thin line of red light leaked out.

Dale backed away.

The crystal wasn't dead. It was waking up.

And it knew he was there, and a voice came from the tunnel entrance. Not the Auditor. Not Vane.

A young woman. She wore a janitor's uniform. She held a mop.

"Dale?"

He turned. It was Sarah, the night-shift cleaner at the municipal building. She looked terrified.

"You shouldn't be here," she whispered. "The water... it's talking."

Dale looked at the pool. The surface was rippling. Not from wind. From below.

"Get back," Dale said. His voice was steady, but his hands were shaking. He pressed the pen hard against the clipboard.

"It's not a monster," Sarah said. "It's a leak. The pipes are leaking."

"Pipes?"

"The plumbing in the building. It runs under here.

The basement.

The foundation.

It's all connected.

"

Dale's mind raced. The city's infrastructure. The water table.

The dimensional rift wasn't just in the cave. It was in the pipes.

The mimic had latched onto the city's water supply.

If he treated the crystal, he treated the source. But he needed a chemical agent.

Chlorine.

Where did he have chlorine?

He looked at Sarah.

"Do you have bleach?"

Sarah blinked.

"I... I have a bucket.

With bleach.

For the floors.

"

Dale stared at her.

"How much?"

"Half a gallon. Industrial strength."

Dale smiled. It was a thin, grim smile.

"Bring it."

Sarah moved to run, but she slipped on the moss. She fell. The bucket slid across the stone.

The red light from the crystal flared.

The blue fluid in the pool boiled.

A shape rose from the water. Not the heart. A hand. Made of glass and shadow.

Dale raised his clipboard like a shield.

"Put on your gloves, Sarah," he said. "Protocol requires PPE."

She scrambled up, shaking.

"I don't have gloves!"

"Use your apron. Cover your face."

The hand reached for her.

Dale stepped between them. He didn't have a weapon. He had a clipboard.

He slammed the clipboard into the crystal shard protruding from the pool.

**PROTOCOL: ISOLATION.** **ACTION: SEVER ENTRY POINT.**

The shard cracked. But the hand didn't recoil. It grabbed Dale's collar.

The cold was instant.

It burned. Not heat. Cold.

Absolute, void-like cold.

Dale's vision blurred. The System notification flooded his eyes, red and screaming.

**CONTAMINATION DETECTED.** **CROSS-CONTAMINATION PENALTY IMMINENT.** **FIELD GUIDE CORRUPTED FOR 24 HOURS.**

He couldn't use the Guide. He couldn't use the protocols.

But he remembered the weakness.

Chlorine, and he grabbed Sarah's bucket. It was inches away.

The hand tightened. His neck creaked.

Dale looked at Vane, who was still at the tunnel entrance, watching. Laughing.

Dale looked at the hand, and he didn't pull it off. He pulled the bucket toward him.

He kicked the bucket. It slid across the wet stone, right into the pool.

The bleach mixed with the blue fluid.

The reaction was instantaneous.

White smoke exploded from the water. The hand shrieked. A sound like metal tearing.

The crystal shattered. Not broke. *Shattered*. Into a million pieces.

The red light died.

The hand dissolved into sludge.

Dale fell back, gasping, and he clutched his throat.

His skin was gray. Frostbite?

No. Necrosis.

The System notification appeared, slow and painful.

**SPECIMEN DESTROYED VIA CHEMICAL DISRUPTION.** **DOCUMENTATION INCOMPLETE.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 4.1.**

4.1.

Dale stared at the number, and he had survived. But he had failed.

He had destroyed the nest, but he hadn't documented it properly.

The Guide was corrupted. The points were meager.

And worse.

A new notification.

**PENALTY ASSESSED.** **CERTIFICATION LEVEL: DEMOTED TO CLASS II (PROVISIONAL).** **LICENSE SUSPENDED FOR 72 HOURS.**

He looked at Sarah. She was crying, but she was alive.

He looked at Vane. Vane was no longer laughing. He was looking at his wrist. His sub-dermal armor was sparking.

Vane looked up. His eyes were no longer lenses. They were organic. And they were wide with terror.

"What did you do?" Vane whispered.

Dale tried to speak. His throat was raw. He managed a croak.

"I treated the problem."

"You broke the crystal," Vane said. "The containment was inside it."

"No," Dale said. He pointed to the floor.

Where the red sludge had been, a new crack had formed. Smaller than the first. But glowing.

Blue light.

The city's plumbing.

"You didn't kill it," Dale rasped. "You woke it up."

The ground beneath them rumbled. Not from the cave. From above.

From the city streets.

**ALERT: INFESTATION SPREADING.** **LOCATION: MUNICIPAL WATER SUPPLY.** **CLASSIFICATION: CLASS IX (DIMENSIONAL MIMIC).** **CERTIFICATION REQUIRED: CLASS IX (MASTER).**

Dale looked at his license. It was flashing red.

He looked at Sarah.

"Get in the truck," he said.

"We can't leave," Sarah said. "The water—"

"The water is the problem," Dale said. "And I need a bigger spray tank."

He stood up. His legs felt like lead.

Vane stepped forward.

"You're suspended. You can't operate."

Dale pulled the permit from his pocket. He tore it in half.

"Permit 14-C is void," Dale said. "But I'm still licensed."

"For 72 hours, you're not."

"Not if I file an appeal," Dale said. He looked at Vane. "The Authority rewards thoroughness. I'm not done."

He walked toward the truck. The ground shook harder. A pipe burst in the distance, spraying black water into the air.

The water fell on Vane.

Vane screamed.

Dale didn't look back. He got into the truck. He started the engine.

He checked his mirrors, and he checked his gas gauge.

He opened his Field Guide. The pages were blank. Corrupted.

He had no data. No weaknesses. No protocols.

He had a truck. A pen. And a city full of pipes.

He put the truck in gear, and he drove toward the city.

And for the first time in his career, Dale Kowalski didn't write a report. He just drove.
