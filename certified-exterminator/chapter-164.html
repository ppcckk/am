Chapter 164: The Blank Contract

# Chapter 164

The blade bit into the first layer of necrotic tissue.

It didn't cut. It slid.

The flesh of the Corrupted Node offered no resistance, parting like wet clay under a thumb.

Dale adjusted his wrist, keeping his elbow tucked, his stance wide enough to absorb the shifting gravity of the chamber. His breathing was shallow, controlled. In through the nose, out through the mouth.

Standard protocol for high-stress manual dexterity.

A notification pinged in his peripheral vision. Sharp. Urgent. Red.

**[PROTOCOL ERROR: INCISION DEPTH EXCEEDS AUTHORIZED LIMIT.]** **[CROSS-CONTAMINATION RISK: 12%]** **[PENALTY PENDING: -50 TP]**

Dale froze. The scalpel hovered, suspended in the gelatinous gap he had just created.

Behind him, the Auditor watched. The man's posture was relaxed, arms crossed, one leg resting on a pile of rubble that used to be a municipal library.

He wasn't breathing.

He wasn't blinking. He was waiting for Dale to fail.

"You're going too deep," Dale said. His voice remained flat, stripped of panic.

He didn't look up.

"This isn't a tumor.

It's a structural anomaly. The tissue density is variable."

"Cut," the Auditor said. The word was a command, not a suggestion.

"If I cut deeper, I breach the membrane," Dale replied. He clicked his pen. Not to write on paper, but to trigger the Field Guide's diagnostic overlay.

"I need to identify the substrate first. You can't treat what you haven't documented."

The Auditor's smile didn't waver.

"The Authority does not require you to understand the anatomy, Technician. It requires the elimination of the threat. Speed is a factor in the Thoroughness Rating.

Hesitation is not."

Dale tightened his grip. The scalpel's handle was slick with condensation.

He could feel the vibration of the beast's heartbeat through the metal.

A low, thrumming rhythm. Like a heavy generator running in a basement.

He pulled the blade out. A thick, black fluid oozed from the incision. It hissed when it hit the floor.

Dale stepped back. He reached into his utility belt, bypassing the spare spray cans and pulling out a sample vial. He dipped the scalpel tip into the fluid. The black liquid clung to the steel.

**[SAMPLE COLLECTED.]** **[ANALYZING...]** **[WARNING: UNKNOWN BIOLOGICAL AGENT.]** **[FIELD GUIDE UPDATE PENDING.]**

"This isn't organic," Dale muttered. He held the vial up to the green light emanating from the creature's core.

The fluid didn't reflect the light.

It absorbed it.

"It's dimensional residue. Solidified."

Marla made a sound from the corner. A choked gasp. She was crouched behind a support pillar, her hands covering her mouth. Her eyes were wide, fixed on the black pool spreading beneath the creature's leg.

"Don't touch it," Dale said. He didn't look at her. He kept his eyes on the wound.

"And don't breathe it. Ventilation is compromised in this sector."

The Auditor tilted his head. "You are deviating from the surgical protocol. The Authority expects a clean excision. You are conducting a lab analysis."

"I'm conducting a risk assessment," Dale corrected.

He pulled a small notepad from his pocket. The paper was yellowed, stained with coffee from a break three chapters ago. He wrote down the fluid's viscosity, its color, and the temperature differential.

"The Field Guide entry for *Corrupted Node* is blank.

I can't apply the targeted damage bonus if I don't know what it is. If I guess, and I guess wrong, I get the cross-contamination penalty.

And if I get the penalty, my certification level drops.

Do you know what happens if my level drops?"

The Auditor's eyes narrowed slightly. "You lose access to Class-V protocols."

"I lose my license," Dale said. He wrote: *Residue pH: Neutral. Volatility: High.* "And without a license, I'm just a guy with a knife. And you know what happens to unlicensed personnel in infested zones."

The Auditor stepped forward. The sound of his boots on the debris was soft, deliberate.

"You are manipulating the system, Kowalski.

The Authority sees all inputs.

You are delaying the extermination to protect your metrics."

"I'm protecting the job," Dale said. He turned to face the Auditor. "You gave me a scalpel.

You didn't give me a scanner. I'm using what I have.

If you want speed, you should have given me a flamethrower.

You didn't. You gave me a tool for precision. Precision requires data."

He dipped the scalpel into the wound again.

This time, he didn't cut. He probed.

He felt the resistance of the inner layers.

Hard. Fibrous. Not muscle.

Something else.

**[IDENTIFICATION COMPLETE.]** **[SPECIES: CORRUPTED NODE (SUBSTRATE: DIMENSIONAL CALCIFICATION)]** **[WEAKNESS: STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY AT FRACTURE POINTS.]** **[TARGETED DAMAGE BONUS UNLOCKED: +40% AGAINST CALCIFIED TISSUE.]**

Dale exhaled. The air tasted metallic.

"Got it," he said.

He drove the scalpel into the fracture point he had identified.

The blade sank in, hitting bone-like material.

The creature shrieked. A sound of tearing metal and wet meat.

The green light flared, blindingly bright.

Dale twisted the handle.

The creature convulsed. Its massive leg buckled. The entire mound of flesh shifted, sliding off the platform it had been anchored to.

The Auditor didn't move. He watched the creature fall. He watched Dale pull the scalpel free, wiping the black slime onto his apron.

"Good," the Auditor said. "But the infection is still active. The node is disconnected from the main mass, but the root system remains. You must extract the root."

Dale looked at the gaping hole in the creature's belly. Inside, pulsing roots of black and green light spread into the floor, into the walls, into the foundation of the building. They were everywhere. A web of corruption.

"I can't do that," Dale said. He sheathed the scalpel.

"This is a structural infestation.

The roots are integrated into the infrastructure. If I pull them, the building collapses. If the building collapses, we lose the containment field."

"Then you are failing," the Auditor said.

His voice dropped an octave. The air in the room grew colder.

Frost formed on the edges of the rubble.

"The Authority requires a full protocol completion. Identify. Document.

Isolate. Eliminate.

Verify.

You have not eliminated."

"I have documented," Dale said. He pointed to his notepad.

"I have isolated the primary mass. But elimination is not feasible without destroying the site. That's a code violation.

Section 4, Paragraph C: *Do not compromise structural integrity during pest control operations.*"

The Auditor walked closer. He was ten feet away now. Dale could smell ozone and old blood.

"The Authority supersedes local municipal codes," the Auditor said. "Your license is granted by us. Your protocols are defined by us. And right now, your protocol dictates that you pull the root."

Marla stood up. She walked into the center of the room, ignoring the black slime pooling around her boots. She stopped between Dale and the Auditor.

"He's right," she said.

Her voice shook, but her chin was high. "If he pulls that root, the Rift destabilizes. The Authority knows this.

That's why you gave him a scalpel instead of a bomb.

You want him to fail. You want him to destroy the containment so you can reset the sector."

The Auditor looked at Marla. He didn't blink. "The technician is the one responsible for the outcome. The witness is irrelevant to the metrics."

"I'm not a witness," Marla said. "I'm a client. And I'm refusing service."

The Auditor smiled.

It was a thin, cruel expression. "Service is not optional. You are in an active infestation zone.

By standing here, you are obstructing a licensed professional. That is a violation of Authority Decree 114."

A new window popped up in Dale's vision.

**[ALERT: CLIENT INTERFERENCE DETECTED.]** **[ACTION REQUIRED: ISOLATE CLIENT OR CONTINUE TREATMENT.]** **[OPTION 1: RELOCATE CLIENT TO SAFE ZONE.]** **[OPTION 2: CONTINUE TREATMENT (CLIENT SAFETY NOT GUARANTEED).]**

Dale looked at the options. He looked at Marla. She was staring at him, pleading with her eyes. *Don't let him make me choose.*

If he moved her, he broke protocol. If he ignored her, he risked her life. And if she died, the job review would be a 1. A zero, almost.

Dale tapped the air. He dismissed the menu.

He turned back to the creature. He picked up a heavy chunk of concrete debris. It weighed maybe thirty pounds. He held it in his left hand.

"Marla," Dale said. "Stay behind me. Don't move."

He walked toward the creature's leg. The roots were thick as tree trunks, glowing with that sickly green light. He raised the concrete block.

The Auditor stepped forward. "What are you doing? That is not a treatment tool."

Dale slammed the concrete block down on the nearest root.

The root shattered.

Green ichor sprayed outward.

Dale didn't flinch.

He swung again. And again.

He wasn't cutting. He was smashing. He was breaking the connection between the root and the floor.

The creature screamed. The ground shook. Dust rained down from the ceiling.

**[PROTOCOL ERROR: IMPROPER TOOL USE.]** **[CROSS-CONTAMINATION RISK: 45%]** **[PENALTY: -100 TP]**

Dale ignored the notification. He kept swinging. He broke the second root. The third.

The Auditor's face hardened. "Stop."

"I'm eliminating the infestation," Dale said. He was sweating. His arms burned. "I'm just using a different method."

"It's sloppy," the Auditor said. "It's violent. It's unprofessional."

"It's effective," Dale grunted. He raised the block for the final root. The main one. The one connected to the creature's heart.

Marla grabbed his arm. "Dale, stop!"

He hesitated. The block was heavy. His muscles trembled.

The Auditor raised a hand. A shield of black energy formed around him. "You will kill her."

The creature thrashed. One of its massive, fleshy limbs swung toward Marla. It was too fast. She couldn't dodge.

Dale dropped the concrete block.

He lunged.

He didn't grab Marla. He grabbed the creature's limb. He used his weight, his leverage, and the momentum of the swing to redirect the impact. The limb slammed into the wall next to him, cracking the masonry.

Dale slid across the floor, pulling Marla with him. They crashed into the base of the support pillar.

The creature roared. It was free. The roots were broken. The containment field flickered, then died.

The green light expanded. It washed over the room. It touched the walls, the floor, the Auditor.

The Auditor didn't flinch. He stepped back into the shadows. The darkness swallowed him.

"Job incomplete," the voice echoed from everywhere and nowhere. "Thoroughness Rating: Pending. Penalty for unauthorized structural damage applied."

Dale lay on the floor, gasping for air. The green light filled his vision. It felt warm. Comforting.

His Field Guide updated.

**[STATUS: CRITICAL INFESTATION SPREADING.]** **[NEW MECHANIC UNLOCKED: LOCALIZED REALITY STABILIZATION (LEVEL I).]** **[COST: 500 TP.]** **[CURRENT TP BALANCE: 120.]**

Dale looked at his balance. Then he looked at the spreading light. Then he looked at Marla, who was staring at the ceiling as the cracks glowed from within.

He stood up. His legs shook.

He opened his Field Guide. He typed a query into the search bar.

*How to buy TP.*

The system flashed a red error.

**[CANNOT PURCHASE TP. TP IS EARNED THROUGH VERIFIED TREATMENT.]**

Dale looked at the Auditor's empty spot.

He knew what he had to do. He had to finish the job. But there was no creature left to kill. The source was gone, replaced by the spreading infection.

He pulled out his pen.

He didn't write a report.

He wrote a request for a Class-IX intervention.

The system blinked.

**[REQUEST DENIED: INSUFFICIENT CLEARANCE.]**

The green light reached his boots. It eated the rubber soles.

Marla grabbed his hand. "Run."

Dale looked at the light. He looked at the exit.

He stayed still.

He clicked his pen again.

"I'm not running," Dale said. "I'm filing an appeal."

The floor beneath him cracked open. Not from the creature. From the Auditor.

A hand reached out of the darkness. It held not a scalpel, but a license. A physical, paper license.

"Sign it," the Auditor's voice whispered, close to Dale's ear. "Sign it, and the Authority will stabilize the sector. But you will be bound to us. Forever."

Marla pulled him back. "Don't!"

Dale looked at the paper. It was blank.

He took a breath.

He signed.
