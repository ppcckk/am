Chapter 386: File the Report

# Chapter 386

The latex gloves snapped against the man's wrists.

It was a small sound, sharp and final, like a bone breaking in a quiet room.

Dale didn't flinch. He didn't blink.

He just tightened his grip on the blue notebook until the plastic cover creaked under the pressure of his knuckles.

The man in the black suit took another step forward. His shoes were polished to a mirror shine, reflecting the dust motes dancing in the beam of Dale's flashlight.

"Put it down, Kowalski," the suit said.

Dale looked at the empty air where his System notification had been. Then he looked at the suit.

"Where is your identification badge?" Dale asked.

The suit paused. The smile didn't fade, but it tightened at the corners.

"I don't wear them. They're messy."

"OSHA regulation 1910.132," Dale said, his voice flat. "Requires employer-provided personal protective equipment for anyone entering a controlled hazard zone. You're in a Class X containment breach. You're in my workspace."

"Your workspace," the suit repeated, tasting the words. "You're a pest control technician. Not a regulator."

"I'm a certified professional with Level IX clearance, or I was, ten seconds ago." Dale tapped the notebook against his thigh. "And I have documented evidence that you are contaminating an active site without proper decontamination protocols. That's a violation."

"Immunity," the suit said softly. "Means I'm above the code."

"Nobody is above the code."

"Try again."

The man raised his hand again.

The air warped, a visual distortion that made Dale's eyes water. It wasn't magic.

It was pressure.

The atmosphere in the doorway condensed, crushing the oxygen out of the space between them. Dale felt his ears pop.

Martinez, huddled behind a pile of drywall debris, made a strangled noise.

Dale didn't move. He watched the distortion.

He counted the seconds.

One.

Two. Three.

At four, the pressure wave hit.

Dale dropped. He didn't run. He didn't dive.

He simply let gravity do the work, folding his body low and rolling toward the support beam behind him. The air crackled where he had been standing, shattering a pane of broken glass into dust.

Dale hit the floor hard. His shoulder slammed against the concrete. Pain flared, bright and hot, but he ignored it. He scrambled to his feet, boots skidding on the grit.

"Stay down!" Dale shouted to Martinez.

The suit stepped over the threshold. The distortion field expanded, pushing the dust away from his body in a perfect sphere. He looked untouched. Untouchable.

"You're resisting a regulatory order," the suit said. "This escalates the penalty. We don't revoke certifications for minor infractions. We revoke them for insubordination."

Dale leaned against the beam, and he was breathing hard. He pulled a pen from his pocket. It was a cheap ballpoint, blue ink.

He uncapped it.

"You're not a regulator," Dale said.

The suit stopped.

"Excuse me?"

"Regulators wear vests. They carry clipboards with carbon copies.

They don't use reality-warping gauntlets.

" Dale clicked the pen.

"You're a cleaner. A fixer. Someone paid to make sure the evidence doesn't get filed."

The suit's expression shifted. The polite mask slipped, revealing something colder underneath.

"You're clever, Kowalski. Too clever for your own good."

"Thoroughness," Dale said. "It's my job."

"It's your funeral."

The suit raised a finger. The air around Dale's head vibrated.

A high-pitched whine, like a dentist's drill amplified a thousand times, filled the room.

Dale felt the pressure build against his sinuses.

His vision blurred.

Martinez screamed.

"Dale!"

Dale didn't look at Martinez. He looked at the suit.

He remembered the rules.

*Cross-contamination penalty: deploying a protocol designed for one creature type against another inflicts a 40% debuff and contaminates your Field Guide entry for 24 hours.*

The System was offline. The Field Guide was dark.

But the rules of physics weren't.

Dale looked at the notebook in his left hand. He looked at the pen in his right.

He wasn't using a protocol. He was using evidence.

"Hey," Dale said, loud and clear.

The suit tilted his head. The whining stopped. Just for a second. Curiosity.

"What?"

"You're wearing latex," Dale said. "Standard nitrile blend, powder-free, blue. Class 1 sensitivity risk."

The suit looked down at his gloves.

"Standard issue."

"Is it?" Dale took a step forward.

"Stay back."

"I'm not fighting you," Dale said. "I'm auditing you."

He swung the notebook. Not at the man. At the air.

The blue cover slapped against the invisible barrier of the pressure field. The impact didn't break it. But the paper edge caught the distortion.

A sound like tearing silk ripped through the room.

The barrier didn't shatter. It *leaked*.

A stream of cold, gray vapor poured out from the tear, swirling around Dale's arm. It wasn't magic. It was displaced air. Pure, pressurized vacuum.

The suit stumbled back. His eyes widened.

"What did you do?"

"I found a weak point," Dale said. "Pressure fields have structural integrity. You're holding the shape with your focus. If you disrupt the perimeter with a high-velocity impact, you create a vacuum cavity."

"That's not possible."

"Physics is real even if the System is gone," Dale said. He pulled a folded piece of paper from his back pocket. It was a carbon copy of the initial inspection report from Chapter 1. The paper was thin, brittle, and old.

He threw it.

The paper sailed through the air, caught in the turbulent flow of the leaking vacuum. It didn't fly straight. It spiraled, whipping around the man's head.

The suit swatted at it.

Dale lunged, and he didn't attack the man. He attacked the floor.

He kicked a loose piece of rebar that had been protruding from the broken concrete. The metal rod skittered across the ground, sliding directly under the suit's polished shoe.

The man's foot caught.

He didn't fall. He stumbled. The perfection of his stance broke.

And that was the mistake, and the pressure field was tied to his stability. His balance. His focus.

When he stumbled, the field rippled. The vacuum leak widened.

Dale grabbed the rebar. It was cold, heavy, and sharp.

He didn't swing it like a sword. He held it like a crowbar. A tool.

"You're violating containment," Dale said, his voice shaking with exertion. "You're spreading contaminants."

The suit regained his balance. His face was twisted now. The smooth mask was gone.

"You're going to die for a piece of paper, Dale."

"It's not just paper," Dale said. "It's the log."

He swung the rebar.

The suit raised his hand to block. The pressure field flared, trying to stop the metal. But Dale wasn't trying to hit the suit. He was trying to hit the *notebook*.

He slammed the rebar against the blue cover, driving the pen through the binding, stabbing the pages inside.

Ink exploded.

Blue ink sprayed everywhere. On the suit's white shirt. On his latex gloves. On the polished shoes.

The suit recoiled. Not from pain. From disgust.

"Ink," the suit hissed. "You're staining the asset."

"Contamination," Dale said, panting. "You're contaminated. You can't touch anything now. Not if you want to keep your record clean."

The suit looked at his hands. The blue ink was spreading on the latex. He tried to peel the glove off, but the pressure field made his movements clumsy. He pulled at the glove, but it was stuck to the skin.

Dale saw the weakness, and the suit was prioritizing cleanliness over combat. He was more afraid of the stain than the attacker.

Dale grabbed a shard of glass from the floor. He held it out, not as a weapon, but as a mirror. He angled it so the light from his flashlight reflected directly into the suit's eyes.

The suit flinched. He turned his head away, shielding his face from the glare.

That was all Dale needed, and he didn't run. He walked. He walked toward the pile of debris where Martinez was cowering.

"Move," Dale said. "Now."

Martinez scrambled up.

"What are you doing? He's going to kill us."

"He's busy cleaning," Dale said.

They moved to the far side of the room, behind a collapsed section of the ceiling.

Dale pressed his back against the cold concrete. He checked his hands. They were shaking.

He hid them in his pockets, and the suit was still in the center of the room, pulling at his gloves, muttering about protocols and cleanup. He wasn't attacking. He was cleaning.

"He's stuck," Martinez whispered. "Why isn't he coming?"

"Because he's a perfectionist," Dale said. "He can't engage with a target if he's compromised. He needs to be sterile before he can strike."

"So he's afraid of us?"

"He's afraid of the mess."

Martinez stared at him.

"That's insane."

"That's bureaucracy."

Dale looked at the notebook. The pen had punctured the cover, but the core documents were still there.

The carbon copies.

The photos. The timestamps.

He had the evidence. But he had no way to transmit it.

The System was down.

The Authority couldn't see it.

The world couldn't see it.

Unless.

Dale looked at the suit. The man had finally gotten one glove off. He was holding it up, examining the inside of the latex with a magnifying glass he had produced from his pocket. He was checking for residue.

He was documenting his own contamination.

Dale's heart skipped a beat, and he reached into his pocket and pulled out a small, crumpled photograph. It was a picture of the broodmother, taken in the crawlspace years ago. It was blurry, poorly lit, but it showed the creature.

He held it up.

"Hey!" Dale shouted.

The suit looked up.

"What do you want?"

"I'm filing a complaint," Dale said.

"You're too late."

"I'm not filing it with you," Dale said. He looked at Martinez. "Martinez, hold this."

He handed Martinez the notebook. Then he held the photograph up high.

"I'm filing it with the *other* thing," Dale said.

The suit froze.

"There is no other thing."

"Yes, there is."

Dale stepped out from behind the debris. He walked into the open room, right into the pressure zone. The vacuum sucked at his clothes, tearing at his jacket. He ignored it.

He held the photograph out toward the man.

"You're not the only one watching," Dale said.

The suit's eyes narrowed. He looked at the photo. Then he looked at Dale.

"Who?" the suit asked.

Dale pointed to the shadows in the corner of the room. The dark, unlit corner where the rebar had fallen.

Something moved there.

It wasn't a person. It was a shape. A silhouette of something that had too many legs and too much height.

The suit turned.

The vacuum field collapsed.

The suit dropped to his knees, clutching his chest. The air rushed back into the room with a roar, blowing dust and debris everywhere.

Dale fell forward, gasping. The pressure released so that his eardrums rang.

He scrambled up, looking at the corner, and the shape was gone.

The suit was on the floor, wheezing. He looked up at Dale, fear in his eyes. Real fear. Not the polite terror of a bureaucrat, but the primal panic of a prey animal.

"You called it," the suit whispered.

"No," Dale said. "I just didn't deny it."

The suit scrambled backward, crab-walking away from Dale.

"You can't control it. You don't have the license."

"I don't need the license," Dale said. "I have the bait."

He looked at the photograph in his hand. Then he looked at the empty corner.

He knew what lived there. He didn't know what it was, but he knew it hated being documented. He knew it hated being seen.

And he knew that the man in the black suit was terrified of it.

Dale took a step toward the suit.

"Stay down," Dale said. "Don't move. Don't clean. Don't look away."

The suit shook his head.

"You're making a mistake."

"I'm making a record," Dale said.

He turned to Martinez.

"Grab the notebook. We're leaving."

"Leaving?" Martinez asked. "We can't leave him!"

"He's not our problem anymore," Dale said. He looked at the corner again. The shadows were shifting. Swirling.

Something was coming out.

It wasn't a monster. It was a concept. A violation of the rules so severe that the System itself was reacting.

Dale felt a tug in his mind. A faint, static hum.

**SYSTEM REBOOTING...**

**ERROR: UNAUTHORIZED ENTITY DETECTED.**

**PROTOCOL OVERRIDE: MANUAL AUDIT REQUIRED.**

Dale smiled. It was a grim, tired smile.

He looked at the suit.

"You wanted my certification revoked?" Dale said. "Congratulations. You broke the seal."

He turned and ran.

The shadows lengthened, stretching toward the man on the floor.

Dale didn't look back. He grabbed Martinez and pulled them toward the exit.

Behind them, a sound echoed through the ruined building. It wasn't a scream. It was a voice.

*"Identify."*

Then another voice, smoother, colder.

*"Isolate."*

Dale burst out of the building into the cold night air. The rain was falling hard now, washing the dust off his coat. He gasped for breath, his lungs burning.

Martinez collapsed on the wet pavement, vomiting.

Dale stood over him, watching the door.

The door creaked.

A hand reached out. It wasn't the suit's hand. It was larger.

Paler. With too many joints.

Dale backed away.

The System notification flashed in his peripheral vision, weak and flickering.

**CERTIFICATION STATUS: SUSPENDED.** **NEW HIERARCHY DETECTED.** **WELCOME TO LEVEL X.**

Dale looked at the notebook in Martinez's shaking hands, and he looked at the door.

He knew he had to go back in.

Not to save the suit.

To document the thing coming out.

Because if he didn't, it would never stop.

And the Authority wasn't going to help him, and they were waiting for him to file the report.

The Authority would not wait for him forever.
