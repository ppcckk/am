Chapter 223: Pending Legal Injunction

# Chapter 223

Marissa Vane watched the monitor.

The screen displayed a single, blinking red cursor against a void of static. No audio feed. No visual. Just the flatline of a connection that should have been severed by the Authority's purge command.

Her fingers hovered over the keyboard. The command line for the demolition of Sector 4 was typed, verified, and ready to execute.

It was standard procedure.

When a technician went rogue, you erased the site. You erased the evidence. You erased the technician.

But the command wouldn't take.

*ERROR: JURISDICTIONAL CONFLICT.*

*ERROR: PENDING LEGAL INJUNCTION.*

*ERROR: CLAIMANT ACTIVE.*

Vane stared at the text.

She had worked for the Regional Oversight Committee for six years. In that time, she had authorized the erasure of seventeen technicians who had lost their minds to the rifts.

None of them had filed a legal injunction.

None of them had invoked the Occupational Safety Act of the Pre-Dimensional Era.

The file on her desk listed Dale Kowalski. Certification Level IV. Thoroughness Rating: 9.4. A man who had once cited a Class-III eldritch horror for improper waste disposal.

She opened the live telemetry logs. They were corrupted, jumping with noise, but one data stream remained clear. It wasn't combat data. It was a timestamp.

*02:14:33. Claim filed.*

*02:14:35. Purge suspended.*

*02:15:01. Technician status: ACTIVE.*

*02:15:01. Technician location: UNKNOWN.*

Vane sat back. Her chair creaked in the silence of the control room.

The blue suits in the upper tiers were watching.

She could feel their eyes on her neck, heavy and cold.

If she didn't resolve this, the purge drones would launch on their own. They would level the block.

They would kill the technician. And they would kill the claimant.

A dead claimant meant the injunction expired. The file would close.

She typed a new command.

*DEPLOY LOCAL UNIT. CLASS: HAZARD CONTAINMENT. TARGET: KOWALSKI, DALE.*

*STATUS: TERMINAL.*

*NOTE: NON-LETHAL FORCE AUTHORIZED FOR ASSESSMENT.*

She hesitated. Then she hit Enter.

The drone fleet outside the window adjusted its vectors. They didn't drop. They descended.

***

Darkness pressed against Dale's chest. It was thick, viscous, and smelled of copper and old blood.

He didn't scream. Screaming wasted oxygen.

He counted.

One. Two. Three.

His flashlight was off. The battery had died in the hallway. He relied on the glow of his Field Guide interface, a faint blue outline hovering in his peripheral vision. It was barely visible, dimmed by the lack of signal, but it was there.

The spider was gone. Or rather, it had dissolved into the walls. The concrete here wasn't concrete. It was a lattice of chitin and wet gristle, pulsing with a low, rhythmic thrum.

Dale adjusted his grip on the suppressant canister.

The nozzle was clogged with mucus. He wiped it on his sleeve, a reflex, then checked the pressure gauge.

The needle hovered in the yellow.

Risky.

He had no license. The Authority had revoked it the moment he walked into the nest.

This was technically a trespassing violation, not a extermination call.

If he killed anything here, he earned zero points.

If he died, he earned nothing.

But he had filed the grievance.

That was the loophole. The Authority couldn't kill him while the claim was active. They couldn't purge the block.

They had to process the claim. And processing required an on-site assessment.

Which meant they were sending someone.

Dale moved forward.

His boots squelched in the slime. The tunnel branched left. He took the left.

It led to a chamber.

The chamber was vast. The ceiling was lost in shadow.

The floor was a carpet of egg sacs, translucent and trembling.

Hundreds of them. Maybe thousands.

In the center stood a figure.

It wasn't a monster. It was a man in a hazmat suit, white and bulky, holding a scanner.

The scanner beeped. A steady, annoying tone.

Dale froze. He knew that suit. Class-IV Hazard Assessment. A bureaucrat.

The figure turned. The helmet's faceplate was reflective, obscuring the eyes.

It raised a hand.

Not in threat.

In greeting.

"Mr. Kowalski," the voice came through the suit's external speakers. It was filtered, metallic, and bored. "You have created a significant administrative burden."

Dale didn't lower the canister. "I created a safety hazard. You created the liability."

"Semantics." The figure stepped closer. It moved with the grace of someone who had never run from anything. "My name is Agent Halloway. I am here to assess the validity of your claim regarding unsafe working conditions."

"Conditions are fine," Dale said. "The environment is hostile. The management is negligent."

Halloway tilted their head. "Define negligent."

"Failure to isolate the broodmother. Failure to provide containment barriers. Failure to warn the public." Dale pointed the nozzle at the egg sacs.

"That's a Class-9 violation. It requires immediate remediation.

Which I am not allowed to do because you're here."

Halloway looked at the sacs. The scanner whirred louder. "The Authority deems this a localized infestation. Not a systemic failure."

"It's a systemic failure," Dale said. "The rift is widening. Look at the substrate."

He kicked the floor. The chitin cracked, revealing dark, fibrous roots beneath. These weren't just spider webs.

They were mycelial networks.

Fungal.

"Cross-contamination," Dale said.

"Spiders and fungi. Symbiotic. You treat one, the other adapts.

You treat neither, the block collapses. That's the claim."

Halloway paused. The scanner stopped beeping.

"You're claiming the Authority's standard protocol is flawed."

"I'm claiming the Authority's standard protocol is outdated."

"That's a bold statement for a man without a license."

"I have a Thoroughness Rating of 9.4.

I have documented this infestation for three days.

You sent me here with a contract that expires in twenty minutes. I'm not the problem.

I'm the symptom."

Halloway was silent. The reflective faceplate showed Dale's own distorted face.

Pale.

Exhausted. Alive.

"Prove it," Halloway said.

"Prove what?"

"That the symbiosis is real. That the spiders are not just hunting. That they are farming."

Dale looked at the sacs. He looked at the canister. He looked at Halloway.

"If I prove it, do I get my license back?"

"You get your claim processed."

"And if I don't prove it?"

Halloway raised a hand. A small, black drone detached from their shoulder. It hovered, red eye blinking.

"Then I terminate the claim. And the purge drones resume their descent."

Dale checked his watch. Twelve minutes until the claim expired. Twelve minutes until the block was erased.

He had one shot. He needed to trigger a reaction that showed the link between the spiders and the fungus. He couldn't shoot them. Shooting would be an unauthorized use of force, voiding the claim.

He had to document it.

He raised his tablet. The screen was cracked, but the camera worked.

"Recording," Dale said. "Field observation. Subject: Symbiotic relationship between *Rattus profundus* variants and fungal substrate."

He walked toward the nearest egg sac.

Halloway didn't move. They watched.

Dale reached into his belt.

He didn't pull a weapon. He pulled a sample jar.

It was sterile glass.

He labeled it with a marker.

*Sample 1: Egg Sac Membrane.*

He uncapped the jar.

The membrane of the egg sac twitched. A thin, white thread emerged from the sac, reaching toward Dale's hand. It wasn't attacking. It was probing.

Dale held his breath.

The thread touched the glass. It dissolved into the jar, merging with the air inside.

"Documentation complete," Dale said.

He pointed the tablet at the floor. "Observe the substrate."

He kicked the floor again. This time, harder.

The crack spread. A pulse traveled through the network. The egg sacs vibrated in unison. A low hum rose from the ground.

The threads from the sacs extended. They wrapped around each other. They formed a web of fungal filaments that pulsed with light.

"It's a hive," Dale said. "Not a nest.

A hive.

The spiders provide the protein.

The fungus provides the structure. The Authority's protocol treats them as separate entities.

That's why the infestations keep coming back. You're not killing the host. You're feeding it."

Halloway stepped forward. The scanner beeped rapidly. Red lights flashed on the chest plate.

"Anomaly detected," Halloway said. "Biological linkage confirmed."

"Confirmed," Dale said. "Now assess the liability."

Halloway looked at the data. The screen on their chest turned green.

"Claim validity: High."

Dale exhaled. His shoulders dropped.

"Does that mean the purge stops?"

"The purge is suspended pending further investigation," Halloway said. "You are to remain in this chamber. Do not engage further threats. Do not leave the site."

"And my license?"

"Reinstated. Provisionally. For the duration of the investigation."

Dale looked at the tablet. The screen flickered.

*SYSTEM NOTIFICATION: CLAIM UPHELD.* *TREATMENT POINTS AWARDED: 0 (DOCUMENTATION ONLY).* *STATUS: UNDER REVIEW.*

Zero points. Of course.

He hadn't killed anything.

He hadn't exterminated. He had just watched.

But he was alive. And the block was still standing.

Halloway turned to leave.

"Wait," Dale said.

Halloway stopped. "What?"

"The rift isn't just leaking monsters. It's leaking data."

Halloway turned back. "Explain."

Dale pointed to the fungal web. "It's reacting to the tablet. Look."

The threads on the floor were pulsing in a rhythm.

Short. Short.

Long.

Short.

Binary.

Halloway froze.

"It's not just a fungus," Dale said. "It's a server.

The Authority's signal is leaking into the rift.

The monsters are learning.

They're adapting to our protocols in real-time."

Halloway's voice went flat. "That is impossible."

"The cross-contamination penalty," Dale said. "It's not just a debuff. It's a feedback loop.

Every time we use a protocol, the rift learns it. Next time, the monsters are immune."

Halloway looked at the tablet. Then at the ceiling. Above them, through the layers of concrete and chitin, Dale could hear the distant whine of drones circling.

"They're not waiting to see if you're lying," Halloway said. "They're waiting to see if you're right."

"If I'm right, what happens?"

Halloway didn't answer. They pulled a radio from their belt.

"Command," Halloway said. "Do not launch the purge. Repeat, do not launch the purge. We have a Class-10 Information Hazard."

Static crackled.

A voice came back. Cold. Bureaucratic.

"Negative, Agent Halloway. The purge is mandatory for Information Hazards. The risk of data leak exceeds acceptable parameters."

Dale felt a chill that had nothing to do with the dampness.

"They'll destroy the data by destroying the site," Dale said.

"They have already scheduled the execution," Halloway said. "Ten minutes."

Dale looked at the tablet. The binary rhythm was speeding up. The fungus was learning faster now.

He looked at Halloway.

"You have a license to exterminate," Dale said.

"I have a license to contain," Halloway corrected.

"Containment is failing. Look at the walls."

The chitin was spreading. It was climbing the scanner on Halloway's chest.

Halloway looked down. The white suit was turning gray.

"If I exterminate it," Dale said, "the data dies. If you contain it, the data spreads. There is no third option."

Halloway looked at Dale. For the first time, the faceplate reflected something human. Fear.

"Unless," Dale said, "we upload it."

"Upload it to what?"

"To the Authority."

Halloway stared at him. "You want to send the monster's data back into the source?"

"Force a system error. Crash the licensing server. The purge protocols are tied to the server. If the server goes down, the drones stop."

Halloway shook their head. "That is treason."

"That is a code violation," Dale said. "Section 4, Paragraph B: Prohibition of Data Hoarding in Critical Systems. You're hoarding the rift's intelligence. That's why it's getting smarter."

Halloway's hand trembled.

"If I do this," Halloway said, "I lose my career."

"You lose your life," Dale said. "Look at your chest."

The fungal threads had breached the suit. They were inside the chest plate. Halloway screamed, a muffled, garbled sound.

They fell to their knees.

Dale grabbed the tablet. He didn't have admin access. He didn't have a key.

But he had a grievance.

And grievances triggered automated workflows.

He opened the Claim Portal.

*ADD EVIDENCE.*

He uploaded the video of the binary pulse. He uploaded the scan of the symbiotic link. He uploaded the audio of the Authority's order to purge despite the hazard.

He added a new field.

*REQUEST: SYSTEM DIAGNOSTIC.*

*JUSTIFICATION: DATA INTEGRITY VIOLATION.*

He hit Submit.

The tablet grew hot in his hand.

Halloway was convulsing. The suit was cracking. The black liquid seeped out.

"Kowalski!" Halloway gasped. "What did you do?"

Dale watched the upload bar. 40%. 50%.

"I filed a complaint," Dale said.

The floor shook.

Above them, the ceiling of the chamber glowed. Not with light. With code.

Lines of green text scrolled across the concrete, burning themselves into the stone.

*ERROR: LOOP DETECTED.* *ERROR: PARADOX.* *SYSTEM REBOOTING.*

The drones outside stopped circling. Their lights went dark. They fell, silent and heavy, into the streets below.

Silence returned to the chamber.

Halloway lay still. The fungal threads receded, retreating into the floor.

Dale stood up. His legs shook.

The tablet pinged.

**CLAIM RESOLVED.** **LIABILITY: ASSIGNED TO CENTRAL AUTHORITY.** **COMPENSATION: NONE.** **NEW STATUS: WHISTLEBLOWER.**

Dale looked at the text.

Whistleblower.

He hadn't saved the block.

He had just made the Authority angry. And now, every single system in the city was offline. The licensing servers were down.

The drones were dead.

The protocols were reset.

For the first time in history, there were no rules.

No certifications. No Treatment Points. No Field Guide updates.

Dale looked at Halloway's body. Then at the darkness stretching out behind him, into the deeper tunnels of the rift.

He had no license. He had no backup. He had no protection.

And the monsters were still there.

But they didn't know what to do next.

Dale picked up Halloway's scanner. It was dead.

He picked up his own suppressant canister. It was empty.

He picked up a piece of chitin from the floor. It was sharp.

He walked toward the tunnel.

The darkness didn't move. It waited.

Dale took out his marker. He wrote on the wall, in big, block letters.

*WORK IN PROGRESS.*

Then he stepped into the dark.
