Chapter 250: Termination Requested

# Chapter 250

The hand punched through the foam. It was small, pale, and covered in a viscous, translucent slime that smelled of ozone and rotting lilies.

Dale didn't flinch. He didn't recoil. He leaned forward, his nose inches from the writhing digits, and squinted.

"Juvenile," he muttered, scribbling on the clipboard. "Sub-adult stage.

Likely Molting Phase Three.

Note the lack of chitinous hardening on the phalanges. Indicates recent emergence from a pupal sac.

"

The drone hovering above him emitted a low, dissonant hum.

Its optical lens zoomed in, the aperture contracting with a mechanical click. To the Authority's sensors, Dale was not a man standing on the edge of a precipice.

He was a technician conducting an on-site assessment of an active hazard.

The drones were programmed to assist, but they were bound by the same rigid bureaucracy that governed the infestations. They could not engage a target that was currently categorized as

"Unverified."

Verification required data. Data required documentation.

Dale pressed the ballpoint pen against the paper. The ink bled through the thin sheet, dark and immediate.

**SPECIES ID:** *Caveat Incognita*. **PHASE:** Larval/Transitional. **THREAT LEVEL:** Unassessed. **ACTION:** Immediate quarantine protocol initiation.

The small hand froze. It hung suspended in the air, fingers splayed. The creature on the other side of the wall let out a shriek that sounded like grinding metal.

Behind Dale, Halloway scrambled back, slipping on the wet concrete of the manhole rim. His boots skidded, sending a cascade of gravel into the pit below.

"Dale, move!" Halloway screamed. His voice cracked, high and jagged.

"It's pulling more of them through!

Look at the wall!

It's splitting!

"

To Dale's left, the grey foam was indeed fracturing. Cracks spiderwebbed across the surface, glowing with a faint, pulsating violet light. The structural integrity of the containment barrier was failing.

The Authority had provided the foam, but they had not provided the manpower to maintain it. That was the loophole.

That was the leverage.

Dale ignored the screaming man. He focused on the hand.

"Subject is exhibiting non-compliant behavior," Dale said, his voice flat and carrying the practiced neutrality of a man reading a meter reading. "Attempted breach of containment vector. Per Protocol 7-B, unauthorized contact with an unlicensed operative constitutes a Class-4 Contamination Event."

The drone dipped lower. The hum increased in pitch.

Dale held up the clipboard. The form, **12-C**, was still glowing white, but the **PENDING** status had shifted to **UNDER REVIEW**.

"If you want the sanitation crew, you need to prove this isn't a false alarm," Dale said, addressing the drone directly. "I am documenting the infestation. The penalty for filing a false sanitation request is revocation of my license.

The penalty for ignoring a confirmed infestation is... well, I don't know.

But I'd rather be safe than sorry.

"

He turned to Halloway.

"Put your hands on your head. Now."

Halloway blinked, confused.

"What?"

"Hands.

Head. Or I list you as an obstruction agent. That adds a mandatory fifteen-minute hold on my grievance processing.

Do you want to wait fifteen minutes while that thing drags you into the dark?

"

Halloway stared at the hole in the wall. Another hand punched through, larger this time. Then a third. The foam groaned, a deep, resonant sound like a cello string snapping under tension.

Halloway put his hands on his head. He looked terrified, but he stood still.

Dale nodded. He turned back to the drone.

"Obstruction resolved. Proceed with verification."

The drone's lens shifted from red to amber. It scanned the area. The sensors swept over the hole, measuring the density of the biomass. The readings appeared in Dale's peripheral vision, translucent text overlaying his sight.

**BIOMASS DENSITY: 40%.** **CONTAINMENT INTEGRITY: 60%.** **RECOMMENDATION: ESCALATE TO CLASS-2 TREATMENT.**

Dale frowned. Class-2 meant fire. Class-2 meant burning the site. It meant burning Halloway.

"No," Dale said. "Request denied. This is a Class-1 nuisance infestation. Misclassification detected."

The drone hovered, confused. The algorithm didn't like contradictions.

"Look at the frass trail," Dale said, pointing with his pen.

He pointed to a smear of black, oily substance running down the side of the manhole.

"Classic *Rattus profundus* marker. But look at the size.

These are juveniles. Juveniles don't attack. They disperse.

If you burn the site now, you scatter the nest. They'll move to the next district.

You'll have a systemic infestation, not a localized one.

"

This was a lie. A technical, bureaucratic lie. But it was the right lie.

Dale had spent fourteen years dealing with rats. He knew that if you nuked a nest, the survivors scattered. If you poisoned the nest, you killed them all. But if you blocked the entry point and applied a slow-acting bait, they died in place.

The Authority valued thoroughness. Burning was fast. But it was sloppy.

The drone paused. The amber light flickered.

**CALCULATING...**

From the hole, a sound emerged. Not a shriek this time. A gurgle. Wet and heavy.

Something massive pushed through the foam. Not a hand. A head.

Or what passed for one, which was a bulbous, eyeless mass of flesh, studded with sensory pits that twitched violently. It was the size of a basketball.

It had no mouth, but it had a row of jagged, chitinous plates along its underside that acted as a jaw.

It pulled itself halfway out. The foam tore away in strips, revealing the wet, glistening skin beneath.

Halloway whimpered. He couldn't help it. The air around the creature distorted, a heat haze of pure malice.

Dale didn't look at the creature. He looked at the drone.

"I am initiating a localized isolation protocol," Dale announced. "I am sealing the secondary entry points. This requires three minutes.

During this time, the infestation is contained. If you deploy Class-2 now, you violate the Containment Prioritization Act, Section 14.

"

The drone's lens widened.

**VIOLATION CHECK: RUNNING.**

Dale moved, and he didn't run toward the monster.

He didn't run away from it.

He moved to the edge of the manhole, where the concrete lip was cracked. He pulled a roll of industrial-strength duct tape from his belt.

It was the only tool he had left. His spray can was empty. His Field Guide was gone.

His armor was gone, and he had tape. He had a pen. And he had the Authority's obsession with procedure.

He slapped a strip of tape across the largest crack in the manhole wall.

Then another. Then another. He moved with the mechanical efficiency of a man who had sealed thousands of entry points.

He ignored the monster, and he ignored Halloway. He ignored the drones.

He was sealing the breach.

The creature let out a roar.

A sound that vibrated in Dale's teeth. It slammed a massive, clawed arm against the wall, trying to push itself free. The concrete cracked further.

Dust rained down on Dale's shoulders.

Dale taped over the dust.

"You are interfering with my workspace," Dale said, not looking up. "Cease movement or I will log this as non-compliant behavior from an unregistered entity."

The creature paused. It tilted its head. The sensory pits turned toward him.

It didn't understand language.

But it understood intent.

It understood that Dale wasn't attacking it. It was *categorizing* it.

And for some reason, that confused the beast's predatory drive. Predators attacked threats. They ignored non-threats.

Dale was not a threat. Dale was a paperwork clerk.

The drone hovered closer.

**WAIT TIME: 47 HOURS, 58 MINUTES.**

Dale looked at the timer. He looked at the monster. He looked at Halloway, who was shaking, tears streaming down his face.

"I can't hold this for two days," Halloway whispered.

"You don't have to," Dale said. He finished the last piece of tape. He stepped back.

He held up the clipboard.

"I am invoking Emergency Clause 9: Resource Reallocation for Immediate Hazard Mitigation."

The drone froze.

**CLAUSE 9: AUTHORITIES MAY REASSIGN PERSONNEL TO ACTIVE SITES IN LIEU OF WAITING PERIODS IF THREAT IS CONFIRMED CLASS-1 AND IMMEDIATE.**

"But you said it was Class-1," the drone's automated voice buzzed, sounding almost offended.

"I said it was a Class-1 *nuisance* infestation," Dale corrected. "The *presence* of the nuisance is confirmed.

The *extent* is pending.

Therefore, by definition, I require immediate assistance to complete the assessment. If I die before completing the assessment, the infestation remains unverified. The Authority's Thoroughness Score drops.

You know how they hate unverified kills.

"

The drone's lens turned red, then green, then red again. It was processing the logic. The logic was sound. The loophole was real.

**REQUEST: DISPATCH LOCAL SANITATION UNIT. ETA: 15 MINUTES.**

Halloway slumped against the wall.

"Fifteen minutes?"

Fifteen minutes was an eternity. The creature was still trying to push through the wall.

Its claws were gouging deep furrows into the concrete, and the foam was gone. The barrier was failing.

Dale looked at his tape. It was holding. Barely.

"Stay on your hands," Dale said. "Don't move. Don't speak. Don't breathe too loud."

"What do we do?" Halloway asked.

"We wait," Dale said. "And we observe."

He pulled out his pen. He wrote another line.

**OBSERVATION: SUBJECT EXHIBITS HIGH RESISTANCE TO MECHANICAL CONTAINMENT. SUGGESTS HIGH-LEVEL CHITIN. POSSIBLE LINK TO HIGHER TIER SPECIES.**

This was the gamble. By noting the link to a higher tier, Dale was flagging the infestation for a higher certification review. He was raising the stakes. He was making the Authority care.

If the Authority saw this as a gateway to a Class-3 or Class-4 threat, they wouldn't just send a sanitation crew. They would send a containment team.

Specialized.

Fast.

Lethal.

But it also meant the Authority would want to study the creature alive.

The creature pulled itself further out. It was half-way through the hole now. It was ugly.

It was wrong. Its limbs were too long, its joints bending in directions that made Dale's eyes water.

Dale stepped closer, and he was too close. Protocol required a minimum five-meter standoff for unverified threats. He was at one meter.

Halloway gasped.

"Dale, get back!"

Dale didn't move. He reached out with his pen.

He touched the creature's sensory pit, which was warm. It pulsed.

Dale wrote.

**TACTILE SAMPLE: WARM.

PULSING. NO CHITIN ON SURFACE. SOFT TISSUE EXPOSED.

WEAKNESS IDENTIFIED: SOFT TISSUE.**

The Field Guide was gone. He couldn't use the bonus. But he could document it. He could prove it existed.

The creature shrieked. It swiped at him.

Dale sidestepped. The claw missed his head by an inch. It carved a gouge in the concrete where Dale had been standing.

"Aggression registered," Dale said, breathing hard. "Logging as defensive response to handling. Not predatory. Defensive."

He pointed at Halloway.

"Run to the ladder. Now."

"I can't leave you!"

"Go!"

Halloway scrambled toward the rusted ladder on the far side of the manhole. The creature turned its eyeless head toward the movement.

Dale was alone, and he looked at the drone.

"Sanitation Unit ETA?"

**MINUTES: 12.**

Dale looked at the creature. It was pulling itself free.

It was going to be out.

Fully out. And when it was out, it would kill him.

He had no weapons. No protocols. No armor.

He had a clipboard, and he looked at the edge of the manhole. The concrete lip was sharp. Broken. jagged.

He looked at the creature's exposed underbelly. The soft tissue.

He looked at his pen.

It was a cheap, plastic ballpoint. The tip was sharp.

It wasn't a weapon. It was a tool.

Dale gripped the pen. He tightened his grip until his knuckles turned white.

"Protocol 12-A: Point of Entry Treatment," Dale whispered.

He didn't have the protocol. But he had the mindset.

The creature lunged.

Dale didn't dodge. He stepped *into* the lunge. He jammed the pen into the soft, pulsating tissue of the creature's underbelly.

It wasn't enough to kill it. It wasn't even enough to hurt it, really. It was a pinprick. A nuisance.

But it was a *treatment*.

The creature froze. The pen was in its flesh. Dale's hand was in its flesh.

He was attached to it.

**CONTAMINATION WARNING: BIOLOGICAL CONTACT DETECTED.** **CROSS-CONTAMINATION RISK: HIGH.**

The drone's lens turned a violent, flashing red.

**DETECTION: UNLICENSED COMBAT ENGAGEMENT.** **PENALTY: IMMEDIATE SUSPENSION.**

Dale smiled. It was a small, tight, terrible smile.

He wasn't fighting. He was tagging.

And if he was tagged, the Authority had to deal with him. They couldn't let an unlicensed operator contaminate a high-tier asset. They had to clean him up.

Which meant they had to come here. Now.

The creature roared, thrashing wildly. Dale held on.

He wrapped his arms around the creature's limb, using his own body as a restraint.

He was no longer a technician, and he was part of the infestation.

"Come and get me," Dale said to the sky.

The ground shook. Not from the creature.

From above.

The clouds parted. A sound like thunder rolled across the sky, but it wasn't thunder. It was the sound of a portal opening. A massive, swirling vortex of violet energy, right over the manhole.

And from the vortex, a shadow descended. Not a drone.

A figure. Tall. Robed. Holding a staff that crackled with black electricity.

The Authority's Enforcer.

Dale looked up, pen still jammed in the monster, blood dripping from his hand onto the clipboard.

He looked at the Enforcer, and he looked at his clipboard.

And he started writing again.

**FORM 12-C: REQUEST FOR EMERGENCY SANITATION ZONE.** **ADDENDUM: OPERATOR COMPROMISED. REQUESTING TERMINATION OF ALL LIVING ENTITIES IN SECTOR 4.**

He signed it.

The Enforcer landed. The ground cracked.

Dale looked at the monster. He looked at the Enforcer.

"You're late," Dale said. "But you're exactly where I want you."

The Enforcer raised its staff.

And Dale closed his eyes.
