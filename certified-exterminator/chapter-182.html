Chapter 182: You Are the Infestation

# Chapter 182

The gloved hand hovered inches from Dale's chin.

Refusal wasn't an option. Compliance wasn't guaranteed. The stranger's smile didn't waver, but his eyes did. They flickered, a rapid, mechanical stutter that suggested the face on top was slipping over the machinery beneath.

Dale didn't take the hand, and he stepped back. Not away, but sideways.

A professional maneuver.

Creating distance to establish a perimeter. The stranger's grip closed on empty air.

"Protocol violation," the stranger repeated, his voice flat. No anger. Just data entry.

Dale's briefcase snapped open at his feet. Not by his hand. By the wind that kicked up around them, swirling with the scent of ozone and wet dog. The Field Guide lay exposed on the pavement. **[ENTRY: THE ARCHIVIST]** pulsed with a dull, crimson light.

*Recursion.*

The word burned in Dale's mind. It wasn't just a weakness. It was a trapdoor.

"Identify," Dale muttered. His hands were shaking, but not from fear. From the adrenaline dump of a near-miss with a Class-V threat he hadn't even seen coming. He dropped to one knee, not to pray, but to check the pavement.

The stranger tilted his head.

"You are non-compliant, Technician Kowalski."

"I am assessing the scene," Dale said.

He pulled his pen from his pocket. The ink-hand incident had left a phantom ache in his fingers, but the pen remained.

He held it like a scalpel.

"And you are obstructing the line of sight. Please move to the left, three feet. Standard safety buffer."

The stranger didn't move. The crowd on the sidewalk had stopped.

Not screaming.

Just watching.

Their faces were blank, smooth, featureless. Like mannequins left out in the rain.

Dale's breath hitched. *Mannequins.*

He looked closer. The skin wasn't plastic. It was paper. Thin, pressed, layered paper.

The stranger took a step forward. The air rippled. The pavement beneath Dale's knees softened, turning into gray sludge. Ink.

Dale scrambled back, kicking off the dissolving concrete. He landed on dry asphalt. The sludge chased him, bubbling with black bubbles.

"Stop," the stranger said. "The exam requires cooperation."

"The exam requires a controlled environment," Dale snapped. He stood up, brushing dirt off his khakis. "You are contaminating the site.

I am logging this as an environmental hazard. Do you have a permit for localized reality degradation?

"

The stranger's smile vanished. His face cracked. A spiderweb of fissures ran from his eyes to his jaw. White light leaked from the cracks.

"Insufficient," the stranger said. "You are insufficient."

The figure lunged.

Not physically. The space between them folded. One second, ten feet.

The next, zero.

Dale didn't dodge. He didn't have time. He did the only thing he knew how to do when the threat was too fast to see. He documented it.

He slammed his pen onto the Field Guide.

**[ACTION: QUICK SKETCH]**

The System responded. A blur of motion froze for a microsecond in Dale's vision. The stranger's lunge was caught in a wireframe overlay. A red circle appeared around the stranger's left shoulder joint.

**[WEAKNESS IDENTIFIED: STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY (LEFT SHOULDER)]**

Dale didn't attack. He pointed.

"Left shoulder," he said. Loud. Clear. "Compromised."

The stranger froze. The white light in his cracks flickered. The fold in space snapped back. The stranger stumbled, clutching his left arm.

Dale grabbed his briefcase. He didn't run.

He walked.

Fast, purposeful strides. The kind you use when you've just found a massive hole in the foundation and you need to get the client out before the floor gives way.

Behind him, the stranger screamed. A sound like tearing metal.

"Recursion," Dale muttered to himself. "Of course."

The Archivist wasn't an entity. It was a mirror.

A reflection of the examiner.

Or rather, a reflection of the *idea* of the examiner.

If Dale accepted the stranger's authority, he became part of the loop. He became the next iteration.

By challenging the stranger's structural integrity, he had broken the mirror.

The black veins on his wrist were throbbing now, hot and angry. The contamination was spreading. He could feel it moving up his forearm, seeking the heart.

If it reached his chest, he wouldn't just fail the exam. He'd be erased.

He turned the corner onto Main Street, and the city was still loud, but the sound was wrong. It was a loop. Car engines revving in a perfect circle.

Birds singing the same four notes.

He needed a sanctuary. A place with boundaries. Walls. Doors.

The municipal library was two blocks away. It had been condemned last week due to a Class-2 fungal bloom. The doors were chained, but the System recognized public infrastructure as neutral ground unless actively infested.

Dale checked his watch. 2:14 PM, and he had four minutes before the local reality stabilized. If he was still in the open, the Archivist would find him again. The

"next" iteration would be waiting.

He sprinted.

His lungs burned. The blackness in his veins pulsed in time with his heartbeat. *Thump-thump. Thump-thump.*

A figure stepped out from an alley. Another Dale. This one wore a hazmat suit. The visor was cracked.

Dale skidded to a halt.

"Don't," the figure said. Its voice was Dale's, but distorted. Deep. "We are all trying to survive."

Dale looked at the figure. No Field Guide. No clipboard. Just a shovel.

"You're not an infestation," Dale said, his voice tight. "You're a symptom."

The figure raised the shovel.

Dale didn't fight. He threw his clipboard.

It wasn't a weapon. It was a distraction. The clipboard flew, hitting the figure in the chest. The paper slid across the plastic surface with a wet *splat*.

The figure paused to look at it.

Dale ran, and he could hear the figure chasing him. Heavy boots. The scrape of the shovel on pavement.

He reached the library. The chains were rusted.

He didn't bother unlocking them.

He slammed his shoulder into the door. It was locked.

He pulled out his multi-tool. A simple thing. Screwdriver, wire cutter, lock pick.

The footsteps were ten feet away.

*Click.*

The lock disengaged.

Dale shoved the door open and threw himself inside. He slammed it shut behind him and jammed a chair under the handle.

Silence, and the library was dark. Smelled of mildew and old paper. The fungal bloom had been treated, but the spores lingered in the air.

Dale leaned against the door, gasping. The black veins on his arm had stopped spreading. They were retreating, pulling back toward his wrist.

A message appeared in his peripheral vision.

**[THREAT: LOCAL. STATUS: CONTAINED.]** **[THREAT: GLOBAL. STATUS: ACTIVE.]**

He looked at his Field Guide. The entry for **THE ARCHIVIST** was no longer red. It was gray.

**[STATUS: DORMANT]** **[NOTE: RECURSION LOOP BROKEN VIA STRUCTURAL CRITIQUE.]** **[NEW MECHANIC UNLOCKED: ECHO TRACING]**

Dale stared at the text. *Echo Tracing?*

A sound came from the back of the room. A slow, rhythmic tapping.

Dale turned, and a librarian stood in the shadows. Tall. Thin. Wearing glasses that reflected no light.

"You are late for your appointment," the librarian said.

Dale checked his pocket watch. He didn't have an appointment.

"Who sent you?" Dale asked.

"The Authority does not send," the librarian said. "The Authority *is*. You are here because the next level requires you to understand the cost of thoroughness."

The librarian raised a hand. In it was a file. A single, manila folder.

"Take it," the librarian said. "Read it. Then decide if you want to keep your certification."

Dale didn't move. The air in the library felt thick.

Heavy.

The dust motes in the air weren't dust, and they were tiny, floating faces. Screaming silently.

"What is the cost?" Dale asked.

"The Archivist was not a monster," the librarian said. "It was a record. A memory of every technician who failed the practical exam.

Every one of them is still there. Waiting.

"

Dale felt a chill crawl up his spine. The figure in the alley. The man with the shovel.

"They are not waiting for the next victim," the librarian continued. "They are waiting for the next *copy*."

Dale looked at his hands, and the black veins were gone. But under his skin, he could see the faint outline of text. Script.

Tiny, scrolling lines of code running beneath his flesh.

"You have contaminated yourself with the data," the librarian said. "You are now partially indexed."

Dale swallowed. His throat felt dry.

"What is in the file?"

"The list of people you have saved," the librarian said. "And the list of people who died because you stopped writing the report."

Dale froze.

"That's not how the System works," Dale said. "Points are awarded for completion. Not for outcomes."

"Correct," the librarian said. "But the Authority keeps a ledger.

A true ledger.

You think you are a technician, Dale Kowalski. But you are becoming a historian. And historians remember everything.

"

The librarian held out the folder.

"If you open it, you will see the names. If you do not open it, you will forget them.

But you will also lose your Certification Level.

You will be demoted to Class I.

You will not be able to touch the capital infestation.

"

Dale looked at the folder. It was thin. It weighed nothing.

He thought of the broodmother in the crawlspace. He thought of the acid beetles. He thought of the woman who had screamed as the rat dragged her into the wall.

He had saved her. He had written the report. He had gotten the points.

But had he been thorough?

He had identified the species. He had isolated the area. He had eliminated the threat.

Had he verified the survivors?

He hadn't checked the neighbors. He had assumed they were safe.

The librarian waited.

Dale reached for the folder. His fingers hovered over the tab.

"If I open it," Dale said, "do I have to save them?"

"No," the librarian said. "You just have to acknowledge that you can't."

The weight of the words hit Dale harder than the physical impact of the stranger's punch.

He grabbed the folder, and he didn't open it.

He tucked it into his briefcase.

"I will read it later," Dale said. "I am currently on a clock. The Class-V infestation is in the capital. I have a travel time of ninety minutes."

The librarian smiled. It was a sad, tired smile.

"You chose the burden," the librarian said. "That is not a failure. That is a certification."

The System chimed.

**[CERTIFICATION LEVEL: II] [NEW PROTOCOL: PAST TENSE VERIFICATION]** [DESCRIPTION: Allows the technician to review the last 24 hours of a target's movement history, at the cost of 50% Thoroughness Rating for the current job.]**

Dale stared at the notification.

*Past Tense Verification.*

He could see where the enemy had been. Not where they were.

It wasn't a combat buff. It was an investigative tool.

It meant he had to be right about the past to be right about the present.

One mistake in the history, and the future was blind.

The front door burst open.

Not the one Dale had locked. The one on the other side of the library.

The man with the shovel walked in, and he wasn't alone. Behind him, a dozen more figures. All with Dale's face. All with shovels.

The librarian vanished.

The lights flickered.

The man with the shovel pointed at Dale.

"He opened the file," the man said. "He is the anchor. If we break him, the loop resets."

Dale looked at his briefcase. The folder was inside.

He looked at the crowd of Dale-Clones, and he didn't have a weapon. He didn't have a protocol for fighting his own reflections.

But he had a clipboard.

And he had a pen.

And he had a new protocol that let him know exactly where they had come from.

Dale clicked his pen.

"Let's check the history," Dale said.

He slammed his hand against the floor.

**[ACTIVATING: PAST TENSE VERIFICATION]**

The world dissolved into wireframes.

But this time, the wireframes weren't gray, and they were blood red.

And the history he saw wasn't just the past twenty-four hours.

It was the last ten years.

And in every single memory, the Archivist was already standing behind him.

Dale spun around.

Nothing there.

But in his mind, a voice whispered.

*You are not the technician.*

*You are the infestation.*
