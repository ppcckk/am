Chapter 303: Filing a Counter-Complaint

# Chapter 303

The flame did not explode.

That was the first mistake everyone else made in their heads. They expected a fireball. A cinematic bloom of orange and heat that would singe the eyebrows off a hero and incinerate the villain in a single, dramatic stroke of narrative convenience.

The flame crawled. It moved with the sticky, reluctant patience of spilled motor oil.

It licked the puddle of natural gas pooling beneath the rusted iron legs of the boiler, fed by the hissing wound in the main line.

It touched the ash-thing's shadow. The shadow receded, pulling back from the light, but the creature itself remained rooted to the spot, its form composed of compacted soot and old grief.

Sarah Miller stood frozen near the doorway, her flashlight beam trembling in her hand. She watched Dale Kowalski crouch on one knee, his back to her, facing the monster. She saw him take a deep, shaky breath. She saw his hands, usually so steady when holding a clipboard, shake as he adjusted his grip on the heavy pipe wrench.

To her, he looked like a man trying to hold back a tide with a spoon.

She wanted to scream, and she wanted to tackle him, drag him out, run until her lungs burned. But her feet were lead.

The Authority's warning had been loud enough to rattle her teeth. *Audit.

Permanently.* She didn't know what that meant. She only knew that Miller, unconscious in the corner, wasn't breathing fast enough.

Dale didn't look at her. He didn't look at Miller. He looked at the ash-thing.

"Target acquired," Dale muttered. His voice was flat, stripped of the panic that vibrated in Sarah's chest.

The ash-thing's head tilted. Miller's face, stretched tight over a skull of charred wood and bone, twitched. The eyes, hollow pits of blue flame, blinked once.

*"One minute,"* the thing rasped. The voice sounded like grinding gravel.

Dale pulled a small notebook from his inner pocket. Not a hero's journal. A water-stained, spiral-bound field notebook with a red cover. He flipped it open with his thumb.

Sarah saw him write. Right there, on the floor of the basement, with the gas hissing and the monster waiting.

*Species: Ash-Wraith Variant. Entry Point: Residential Heating System. Primary Vulnerability: Oxygen deprivation. Secondary Vulnerability: Structural Integrity of Host.*

He scribbled a code: **K-4: Improper Ventilation.**

He underlined it twice.

"Is he crazy?" Sarah whispered to the empty air. "He's taking notes."

Dale stood up. He tucked the notebook away. He looked at the gas line. The pressure gauge on the boiler was cracked, the needle buried in the red.

"Protocol 7," Dale said aloud. "Requires controlled ventilation. If I seal the room, I create a bomb. If I leave it open, the thing escapes."

He looked at the ash-thing.

"You have thirty seconds."

The thing lunged.

Dale didn't dodge. He stepped *into* the lunge. He swung the wrench not at the creature, but at the steam release valve on the side of the boiler. It was old, painted over with layers of lead-free enamel that had chipped away to reveal brittle iron.

The wrench connected. Metal shrieked against metal. The valve sheared off.

A jet of scalding steam erupted, filling the immediate area with a white, opaque fog.

The ash-thing recoiled, its form losing cohesion in the sudden humidity. Steam was water vapor. Water was the enemy of fire, and fire was the engine of the ash-thing's rage.

Dale backed away, coughing, shielding his face with his forearm. He watched the gauge. The pressure was dropping, but the gas was still leaking. The flame was still there, a small, orange tongue dancing in the corner.

He had to finish it. Not with fire. With air.

"Sarah," Dale yelled through the steam. "The vent cap on the east wall. Kick it off."

"It's locked!"

"Kick it harder!"

Sarah moved. Instinct, not logic, drove her.

She ran to the wall, raised her boot, and slammed it into the rusted grate covering the return air duct.

The metal groaned, buckled, and snapped loose.

The grate fell with a clang.

Fresh air rushed in.

The gas flame died instantly.

The ash-thing roared. It wasn't a sound of pain. It was the sound of suffocation. Its form unravelled, the soot particles scattering into the vacuum created by the sudden pressure differential.

*"You... cheat..."* the thing gurgled.

"It's not cheating," Dale said, walking toward the dissolving mass. He held out his hand, not in a fighting stance, but in a demanding posture. "It's compliance.

You're violating the fire code. I'm correcting the violation.

"

He reached into his belt and pulled out a small canister of compressed nitrogen. A tool he carried for clearing blockages in HVAC systems.

He sprayed the canister into the center of the ash-thing's chest.

The cold blast hit the core. The blue flames in its eyes sputtered. The soot froze, turning into a brittle, gray lattice.

Dale took a step forward. He raised the wrench.

"Documenting elimination," he said.

He brought the wrench down.

The ash-thing shattered. It didn't bleed. It broke apart into a cloud of fine, gray dust that settled on the concrete floor like snow.

Silence returned to the basement.

Sarah stood by the broken vent, her chest heaving. She looked at the pile of ash. She looked at Dale. He was wiping his hands on his trousers, checking his nails for contamination.

"Did we win?" she asked.

Dale looked at the floating text in his peripheral vision.

**INFESTATION ELIMINATED.** **THOROUGHNESS RATING: 8.4.** **TREATMENT POINTS AWARDED: 150.** **FIELD GUIDE UPDATE: Ash-Wraith Variant added.**

He frowned.

"Eight point four," Dale said. "That's low."

"Why?"

"Because I used a secondary protocol. I didn't fully isolate the nest before I engaged. The gas line was the nest. I treated the symptom, not the root."

Sarah walked over to Miller. She checked his pulse. It was weak, but steady. She looked up at Dale.

"We're alive," she said. "Does the rating matter?"

Dale looked at the pile of ash. Then he looked at the broken boiler.

"The Authority doesn't just want us dead, Sarah. It wants us *thorough*."

He picked up his clipboard. A piece of it had snapped off in the scuffle. He taped it back together with a strip of duct tape he ripped from his belt.

"We need to find the source," Dale said.

"What source?"

"The thing had Miller's face. That means it wasn't just a random spawn. It was *recruited*."

Sarah stared at him.

"Recruited by what?"

Dale didn't answer, and he walked to the corner where Miller lay. He knelt beside him. He didn't touch the man.

He used the tip of his pen to lift the collar of Miller's shirt.

There, on the back of Miller's neck, was a mark.

It wasn't a bite. It wasn't a claw wound.

It was a stamp. A perfect, circular seal burned into the skin. The design was intricate, geometric, and alien.

Dale recognized it, and he had seen it before. On the eviction notice for the building he'd inspected last week. The one he'd cited for 'unauthorized dimensional anchoring.'

"It's a lien," Dale said.

"A what?"

"A legal claim. The Authority doesn't just kill monsters, Sarah. It *forecloses* on them."

He looked up, his eyes hard.

"Miller is collateral. And we're next."

***

The drive home was quiet. The suburban streets of Dayton were empty, illuminated by the flickering glow of streetlights that had been shorted out by the Rift event. The power grid was unstable.

Dale drove. Sarah sat in the passenger seat, clutching her seatbelt.

Miller was in the back, strapped into his own seat, unconscious but stable.

The paramedics had taken the initial call, but they couldn't handle the

"psychotic episode" without police escort, and the police were overwhelmed. Dale had insisted on driving him to the county hospital himself. It was faster.

It was cleaner, and he kept one hand on the wheel. The other rested on the dashboard, fingers tapping a rhythmic pattern against the plastic.

*Tap-tap-tap.*

A code. A rhythm. A habit.

Sarah watched him, and she had seen men fight monsters.

She had seen soldiers. She had seen people run.

She had never seen a man treat a demon like a broken water heater.

"Who are you?" she asked. Her voice was small.

Dale didn't look at her.

"I'm a technician. Level II. Certified in Structural Extermination and Dimensional Remediation."

"Is that... a real job?"

"It's a license," Dale said. "Just like being a plumber. Or an electrician. Or a dentist."

He pulled into the driveway of his house. It was a modest ranch-style home, well-kept. The lawn was mowed. The gutters were clear.

"Get the stretcher from the trunk," Dale said.

Sarah opened the door. The air outside was cold, carrying the scent of ozone and wet earth. She grabbed the medical stretcher from the back of Dale's van.

Dale went to the front door. He unlocked it.

The house was dark. No lights.

He stepped inside.

The smell hit him first. Not smoke. Not gas.

Rot.

Sweet, cloying rot, and he clicked on the flashlight. The beam cut through the darkness of the living room.

The furniture was covered in a fine, gray dust. The same dust from the basement.

Dale walked to the kitchen. The sink was overflowing.

Not with water.

With sludge. Black, viscous fluid that pulsed slowly.

He walked to the bedroom, and the bed was made. Perfectly made. Hospital corners.

But the pillow was wet.

Dale lifted the pillow.

Underneath, pressed into the mattress, was a child's drawing.

It was a crayon drawing of a house. And outside the house, a figure. A tall, thin figure made of lines and scratches. It was holding a clipboard.

Dale felt a cold spike in his gut. He hadn't seen this drawing before.

He pulled out his Field Guide. He flipped to the entry for Ash-Wraith Variant.

The entry was changing. The text was rewriting itself, glowing with a faint red hue.

**STATUS: CONTAMINATED.** **CAUSE: CROSS-CONTAMINATION VIA UNLICENSED ENTITY.** **PENALTY: -40% EFFECTIVENESS FOR 24 HOURS.** **WARNING: SOURCE IDENTIFIED. LOCALIZED BREACH DETECTED IN RESIDENCE.**

Dale dropped the Field Guide, and he looked at the crayon drawing.

He looked at the sludge in the kitchen.

He looked at the window.

Outside, the streetlights flickered and died.

From the backyard, a sound emerged. A wet, slapping sound. Like wet leather hitting pavement.

Dale turned to the hallway.

"Sarah," he whispered. "Get the children. Now."

Sarah was in the hallway, holding the stretcher. She looked pale.

"The kids are asleep," she said.

Dale grabbed her arm. His grip was tight. Too tight.

"No," Dale said. "The kids aren't asleep."

He pointed to the door of the twins' room, and the door was open.

And the room was full of ash.

Not the dry, dead ash from the boiler.

Wet, living ash.

And standing in the center of the room, holding two sleeping dolls, was a figure, which had Miller's face.

But it was taller. And it was wearing Dale's work boots.

The figure looked at Dale.

It smiled.

*"Audit initiated,"* it said. *"Subject: Dale Kowalski. Violation: Failure to secure perimeter. Penalty: Asset Forfeiture."*

The figure raised its hand.

In its hand was Dale's clipboard.

And on the clipboard was a new citation.

**VIOLATION: FAILURE TO REPORT SECONDARY INFESTATION.**

Dale looked at the figure. He looked at Sarah. He looked at his own tools, still in his belt.

He had no wrench. He had no gas. He had no nitrogen.

He had only his Field Guide. And it was contaminated.

The figure took a step forward.

Dale stepped back, and he reached into his pocket.

He didn't have a weapon.

He had a pen.

He uncapped it.

The figure lunged.

Dale didn't fight. He wrote.

He wrote on the air itself. The ink hung there, floating, glowing red.

**APPEAL FILED.**

The figure paused.

The air around Dale crackled. The Authority was listening.

But the figure didn't stop. It smiled wider. Its jaw unhinged.

*"Appeal denied,"* it said. *"Reason: Subject is unlicenced for Class III entities."*

Dale looked at the floating text.

**CERTIFICATION LEVEL I.** **REQUIRED LEVEL: III.**

He looked at the figure, and he looked at his children's dolls.

He looked at the pen in his hand.

And then, Dale Kowalski did something that no hero in this world had ever done.

He didn't attack.

He filed a counter-complaint.
