Chapter 247: License Expired

# Chapter 247

The claw descended.

Dale didn't flinch. He stepped sideways, a pivot he'd perfected dodging swinging garage doors in tight suburban garages. The chitin scraped the pavement where his boots had been, gouging a trench in the asphalt.

Vance screamed. The water jet wavered, spraying mud and oil across the street.

"Steady!" Dale barked, his voice flat, cutting through the panic. "Aim for the legs.

Not the face.

You're not hunting a deer, Vance. You're hosing down a sewer blockage.

"

The spider-monster hauled itself fully onto the street, which was massive. The legs alone stood taller than the streetlamp.

The human mask stretched over its carapace, the features sliding with each movement.

A left eye twitched. The right eye remained fixed on Dale's clipboard.

**WARNING: LICENSE EXPIRED.** **STATUS: UNLICENSED.** **ACTION: CEASE IMMEDIATELY OR FACE ARREST.**

Red text flashed across Dale's retina. The System wasn't joking. Without points, he was just a man with a pen.

"Renewal fees are due," Dale repeated, clicking the pen again. It made no sound. The clicker was broken. He clicked it a third time, purely out of habit.

The creature lunged.

Dale rolled behind the overturned police cruiser. The car crumpled like foil under the impact of the claw. Metal shrieked. Glass shattered.

"Sarah!" Dale shouted into the radio strapped to his chest. "Log the species. *Arachnidae profundus*.

Sub-species: *Persona*.

Note the vocalization.

Note the mask material. Is it biological or synthetic?

"

Sarah's voice crackled, breathless.

"Biological! It's shedding! It's pulling skin!"

"Good. That's a molting vulnerability. If we can catch it mid-shed, the exoskeleton is soft. We can penetrate the chitin with standard Class II tools."

Vance was firing the hose blindly. The water hit the creature's legs, slicking the ground. The Hosts—the smaller, chittering parasites that had swarmed the streets—slipped and fell. They clawed at the wet pavement, trying to regain purchase.

"Stop spraying the legs!" Dale yelled. "You're contaminating the Field Guide! If you use water on a *Persona*-type, the entry flags for water resistance. You'll get a debuff if we try fire next."

Vance looked at the nozzle, confused.

"But it's big!"

"It's a violation of protocol! Stop wasting resources!"

Dale pulled a small flare from his belt. Not for light. For documentation.

**ACTION: MARKER DEPLOYMENT.** **COST: 0 POINTS.** **EFFECT: CREATES TEMPORARY TARGET ZONE FOR ALLIED ASSETS.**

He threw the flare. It sputtered to life, burning with a harsh, red magnesium glow. It landed near the creature's front left leg.

The spider-monster hissed. It recognized the light. It associated it with previous exterminators. It hated it.

It raised its other claw to crush the flare.

Now.

"Vance! The leg! The joint above the knee! Hit the joint!"

Vance adjusted the nozzle. The water jet, pressurized to a level that could strip paint, slammed into the chitinous joint.

The creature shrieked. The sound wasn't a roar. It was the sound of a microphone feedback loop amplified through a thousand throats. It was painful to listen to.

Dale scrambled out from behind the wrecked car. He wasn't running away. He was running toward the manhole.

He needed the nest, and he knew the rule. *Eliminate the source.* Killing the adult was temporary. The eggs below were the problem.

But he had no points. He had no protocols. He had only his hands, his clipboard, and the broken pen.

He slid down the muddy slope of the manhole. The smell hit him first. Rot.

Copper. Old blood.

He pulled his flashlight. The beam cut through the darkness below.

Hundreds of eggs clung to the walls of the shaft. Pulsing. Translucent. Each one held a tiny, folded leg.

**FIELD GUIDE UPDATE: ARACHNIDAE PROFUNDUS (PERSONA).** **WEAKNESS IDENTIFIED: EGG SAC STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY.** **BONUS DAMAGE: +15% TO EGG SAC.**

The bonus was tiny. Useless without a way to break them.

Dale looked around. The shaft was narrow. No room for the hose. No room for the Hosts to swarm.

But there were pipes. Old, rusted utility lines running down the shaft.

He looked up.

The spider-monster was still distracted, its legs flailing at the flare and the water jet. Its mask was slipping further. The skin was tearing.

It was molting.

"Sarah!" Dale shouted up. "Do we have any structural demolition charges in the truck? The small stuff? For breaking concrete pads?"

"We have two charges for the parking meter foundation!"

"Bring them down. Tie them to the main water main. The one running under the street."

"That will flood the whole block!"

"Better flooded than eaten. Go!"

Dale didn't wait. He climbed back up. The mud sucked at his boots. He emerged into the night.

The spider-monster had crushed the flare. It was turning toward the manhole. It sensed the movement below.

It smiled again. The mask's mouth stretched too wide, revealing rows of needle-teeth behind the human lips.

"Technician," it said, its voice gurgling. "You are non-compliant."

"I am compliant with Section 4, Paragraph 12 of the Municipal Code," Dale said. He held up his clipboard. "Unapproved structural alteration of the subsurface. Immediate remediation required."

The creature paused. It tilted its head. The mask's eyes widened.

It didn't understand. It couldn't.

It was a predator, not a bureaucrat.

It relied on fear. Dale offered procedure.

That was the gap.

Vance was struggling with the hose. The pressure was dropping. The water tank was low.

"Dale, it's coming! The Hosts are fleeing! They smell the blood!"

The chittering swarm was scattering into the sewers, leaving the giant spider alone.

"Good," Dale said. "Let them go. They're not the target."

He took two steps toward the creature.

Vance froze. "Dale, what are you doing?"

"Finishing the inspection."

Dale pulled the clipboard from under his arm. He opened the back cover. Inside, taped to the board, was a small, silver disk. A Class I Contamination Tag.

It was useless for damage. It was designed to mark a hazard zone for other workers.

But the System had a loophole.

**ITEM: CLASS I CONTAMINATION TAG.** **FUNCTION: MARKS AREA AS HAZARDOUS.** **SYSTEM INTERACTION: IF MARKED AREA IS SUBSTANTIALLY DAMAGED, THE TAG TRIGGERS A SAFETY SHUTDOWN OF ALL ACTIVE PROTOCOLS IN THE ZONE.**

Dale had zero active protocols.

The spider-monster had a passive aura. A *Fear Field*. It caused the Hosts to panic. It caused humans to freeze.

It was a protocol. An unlicensed one.

If Dale marked the spider's location as hazardous, and then damaged that location, the System would force the spider to stop using its Fear Field to avoid a

"Safety Violation."

It was a bureaucratic nightmare. It was also his only shot.

"Vance. Stop spraying."

"What?"

"Stop spraying! Let me get close!"

Vance hesitated. Then, he let go of the nozzle. The water died.

The spider-monster took a step forward. It raised its claw.

Dale walked forward.

The creature loomed over him. The mask smelled of rot and old perfume.

"You are small," it said. "Your kind is small."

"Size is irrelevant to code compliance," Dale said.

He reached up, and the claw came down.

Dale didn't dodge. He stepped *into* the shadow of the claw. He pressed the silver tag against the creature's leg.

**ACTION: MARK HAZARD.** **TARGET: LOCALIZED ZONE.** **COST: 1 POINT (DEBT).**

The tag stuck. It glowed red.

The spider-monster paused. It looked at its leg. It looked at Dale.

It felt the System's eye. It felt the sudden, crushing weight of the Authority's attention.

For a moment, the Fear Field flickered. The air felt lighter.

Dale slammed his clipboard into the tag. Hard.

**ACTION: IMPACT.** **DAMAGE: 0.** **STATUS: VIOLATION DETECTED.**

The System screamed.

**CRITICAL ERROR: HAZARD MARKER DAMAGED WITHOUT SAFETY PROTOCOLS.** **PENALTY: IMMEDIATE SUSPENSION OF ALL COMBAT ABILITIES IN ZONE.**

The spider-monster shrieked. Not in pain.

In confusion.

Its legs locked up.

Its claws froze mid-air. The Fear Field collapsed.

The Hosts stopped fleeing. They stopped freezing. They looked around, blinking.

Vance stared. "What did you do?"

"I filed a complaint," Dale said.

The creature tried to move. It couldn't. It was trapped in a System-enforced timeout. A safety pause.

It had three seconds.

Dale pulled a fire axe from his belt. He'd taken it from the truck earlier, thinking it might be useful for prying open windows.

He swung it.

Not at the head.

At the joint Vance had softened with water.

The axe bit into the wet, molting chitin. It cracked.

The spider-monster convulsed. The freeze didn't stop physical impact. It only stopped *abilities*.

Dale swung again. And again.

The leg gave way.

The creature crashed to the asphalt. It tried to crawl. Its limbs were deadweight.

Dale didn't stop. He climbed onto the creature's back. He crawled toward the mask.

Sarah was running toward him, holding a crowbar.

"Dale! The charges! They're here!"

"Set them on the manhole rim!" Dale yelled. "I need a clear shot at the eyes!"

He stood on the creature's thorax. He balanced on the slick chitin. He looked down at the mask.

The human face was tearing off completely. Underneath, a cluster of black orbs stared up at him.

"You cannot kill me," the creature whispered. "I am the Rift."

"No," Dale said. "You're an infestation."

He raised the axe, and he brought it down on the central eye.

Black fluid sprayed out.

The creature spasmed.

Dale stepped back.

"Sarah! Now!"

Sarah dropped the charges. They were small, orange cylinders. She pulled the pins.

She ran.

Dale ran.

Vance ran, and the explosion was muffled. It wasn't a fireball. It was a concussive thump that shook the ground.

The manhole cover, already gone, didn't matter. The explosion collapsed the shaft inward. The concrete rim shattered. The earth buckled.

The spider-monster, paralyzed by the System's safety pause, couldn't escape the collapse. It was dragged down into the hole by the shifting earth.

It screamed. The sound was cut short by a shower of dirt and debris.

Silence.

Dust hung in the air.

Dale stood in the middle of the street. His suit was covered in black slime. His clipboard was splattered.

He looked at the System notification.

**INFESTATION NEUTRALIZED.** **CERTIFICATION LEVEL IV THREAT: CONTAINED.** **REWARD: 0 POINTS.** **NOTE: LICENSE STILL EXPIRED.** **PENALTY FOR UNLICENSED COMBAT: FINE OF 500 CREDITS.**

Dale stared at the text, and he was broke. He was suspended. And he had saved the block.

Vance slumped against the water truck. He was shaking.

"Did we win?"

"We treated the problem," Dale said. He walked over to the edge of the collapsed manhole. He looked down into the darkness.

The shaft was plugged with rubble. The spider was gone.

But the dust was settling. And in the dust, something was moving.

Small things.

Not rats. Not beetles.

Spiders.

Dozens of them. Tiny.

No bigger than a coin, and they poured out of the cracks in the asphalt. They swarmed the rubble pile.

They didn't attack. They didn't flee.

They formed a pattern.

A shape.

In the center of the swarm, a single larger spider sat. It had no mask. It had no human face.

It had Dale's face.

A tiny, chitinous Dale.

It clicked its legs.

**NEW FIELD GUIDE ENTRY: CLONE PROGENY.** **THREAT LEVEL: UNKNOWN.** **STATUS: AWAITING IDENTIFICATION.**

Dale felt a cold prickle at the base of his neck. His hands went still.

He looked at the tiny spider. It looked back.

It held a tiny, chitinous clipboard.

Dale dropped his pen, and he picked it up.

He clicked it.

It worked.

A small green light blinked on the side.

**LICENSE RENEWAL:** **DEBT OVERDUE.** **COLLECTOR DISPATCHED.**

Dale looked up.

The air above the street shimmered. Not with a Rift. Not with a portal.

With a door.

A wooden door, floating in mid-air. It was old. Oak. Heavy.

The handle turned.

Someone opened it from the other side.

A hand reached out. A human hand. But the fingers were too long. The nails were black.

It grabbed the doorframe.

And pulled.

The door swung open.

Standing in the doorway was a figure in a suit. A sharp, grey suit. A tie with a geometric pattern.

It looked like an accountant.

It looked like a bureaucrat.

It smiled, which was the same face as the spider-monster's mask.

The Authority.

Dale took a step back.

The figure stepped out of the door. The door vanished behind it.

It looked at Dale. It looked at the destroyed street. It looked at the collapsed manhole.

It checked a watch on its wrist.

"Technician Kowalski," it said. Its voice was smooth. Calm. Terrifying.

"You've incurred a debt," it said.

Dale swallowed.

"I filed the paperwork."

"You filed the wrong paperwork," the figure said. "And now, you owe us a life."

The figure raised a hand.

The tiny spiders swarmed up Dale's legs, and they weren't attacking.

They were tagging him.

**CONTAMINATION DETECTED.** **SUBJECT: DALE KOWALSKI.** **STATUS: ASSET OF THE AUTHORITY.**

Dale tried to run.

His legs wouldn't move.

The spiders tightened around his ankles.

The figure in the grey suit walked toward him. It pulled a pen from its pocket.

It was identical to Dale's.

"Sign here," it said.

It pointed to a blank line in the air.

A contract.

Floating in the void.

Dale looked at the pen. He looked at the contract. He looked at the thousands of tiny spiders climbing up his pants.

He reached for the pen.

His hand shook, and he took it.

And the chapter ended with his thumb hovering over the signature line, the ink dripping onto the floating paper.

But the ink wasn't his.
